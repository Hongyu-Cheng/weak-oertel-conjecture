## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(a_i\in\mathbb Z\) and \(L_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) for \(1\le i\le n\), and let
\(D=\prod_{i=1}^n\{a_i,a_i+1,\ldots,a_i+L_i\}\).
For any \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), give \(D\) its original strictly positive probability law
\(p_{\mathbf x}=e^{\gamma+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}/\sum_{\mathbf y\in D}e^{\gamma+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf y}\).
Define actual depth using every containing closed halfspace:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf x)
&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf x\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf y\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf y},\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exactly \(\tau_p\) and \(M_3\) of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, and \(M_3(p)=\mathcal M_3(p)\) in the notation of accepted fact `0ea9778d4ccdcc84`. The ambient \(N\) of the former fact and ambient \(m\) of the latter fact are both \(n\) here. Allowing the whole-space closed trace or restricting to proper ambient halfspaces gives the same depth. In intrinsic rank zero the depth is one.

Put \(b_{\mathrm{bin}}=\#\{i:L_i=1\}\), \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}=\#\{i:L_i\ge2\}\), and \(R=b_{\mathrm{bin}}+m_{\mathrm{nb}}=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D)\). In particular, \(b_{\mathrm{bin}}\) is not the occupied-parity count \(b\) of `3bcbf21f33edf564`; \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}\) is the nonbinary count \(m\) in the assigned task. Use the accepted constants of `f35126e13cfd3cb5`, with the explicit aliases \(\alpha=(8/27)^{3/2}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{score}}=92/9\) for that fact's \(\alpha\) and \(C\). Set
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}=\log(8\alpha)>0\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm{box}}=\tfrac18e^{-23/\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}}\).
The requested moment coefficient \(\kappa\) can therefore be chosen as \(\kappa_{\mathrm{box}}\), independently of all dimensions, lengths, translations, and affine scores. More precisely,
\[
M_3(p)
\ge \frac18\alpha^{m_{\mathrm{nb}}}8^{-b_{\mathrm{bin}}}e^{-3\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{score}}m_{\mathrm{nb}}}}
\ge \kappa_{\mathrm{box}}8^{-R}
\ge \kappa_{\mathrm{box}}8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}=0\), there is also the exact formula
\[
M_3(p)
=\prod_{\{i:L_i=1\}}\frac{1+r_i^4}{(1+r_i)^4}
\ge 8^{-R},\qquad r_i=e^{-|\ell_i|},
\tag{2}
\]
where an empty product equals one. Thus the all-frozen case and every binary dimension are covered with absolute constants.

## Proof

First transport the original law by a coordinate reflection and translation. For each \(i\), let \(\varepsilon_i=-1\) and \(c_i=a_i+L_i\) if \(\ell_i>0\); otherwise let \(\varepsilon_i=1\) and \(c_i=a_i\). Put
\(\Phi(\mathbf j)=\mathbf c+\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n)\mathbf j\)
and \(E=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,L_i\}\). This is an invertible ambient affine map and an integer-lattice bijection, with \(\Phi(E)=D\). Write \(s_i=|\ell_i|\), \(r_i=e^{-s_i}\), and \(Z_i=\sum_{h=0}^{L_i}r_i^h\). Define \(p_i(j)=r_i^j/Z_i\) for \(0\le j\le L_i\). The exact probability alias is
\[
\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}:=p_{\Phi(\mathbf j)}
=\prod_{i=1}^np_i(j_i),\qquad \mathbf j\in E.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(\gamma+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\Phi(\mathbf j)=\gamma+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf c-\sum_i s_i j_i\), so its constant prefactor cancels in the original normalizer. No conditioning or change of an individual original probability occurs in (3).

A halfspace with normal \(\mathbf u\) pulls back under \(\Phi\) to a halfspace with normal \(\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_i)\mathbf u\). This is a bijection between containing closed cap traces, so the exact depth and moment aliases are \(\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)=\tau_p(\Phi(\mathbf j))\) and \(M_3(\widetilde p)=M_3(p)\). For frozen coordinates, the cap transport of accepted fact `0ea9778d4ccdcc84` identifies ambient and intrinsic depth: an ambient cap restricts either to a containing cap in the active coordinates or to the whole intrinsic support, and every intrinsic cap extends to an ambient one by zero normal components in frozen coordinates. The same original probabilities are used in either chart. When \(R=0\), every containing trace includes the sole point and its depth is one.

The support \(E\) is complete, and the function
\(\mathbf t\mapsto e^{-\sum_i s_i t_i}\mathbf1_{\prod_i[0,L_i]}(\mathbf t)/\prod_i Z_i\)
is a globally log-concave extension of \(\widetilde p\), including when some intervals are frozen. Thus the hypotheses of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564` hold. We use its oriented reflection energy and containing-cap inequality (16)--(17), with the exact alias
\[
S_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)
=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\min\{\widetilde p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},\widetilde p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\},
\qquad
\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)
\ge\frac{S_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)+\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}}2
\ge\frac12S_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j).
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(\widetilde p\) is extended by zero outside \(E\). To check all containing caps explicitly, if a closed halfspace contains \(\mathbf j\), it contains \(\mathbf j\) itself and at least one endpoint of each pair \(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v\). Otherwise the two strict violations of its defining affine inequality would average to a violation at \(\mathbf j\). Each nonzero pair occurs twice in the oriented sum, whereas the central atom occurs once. This proves (4), including boundary equality and every ambient cap normal, rather than only a selected cap.

For each coordinate and root, put \(b_i(j)=\min\{j,L_i-j\}\) and \(N_i(j)=2b_i(j)+1\). The oriented displacements whose two endpoints belong to \(E\) are exactly \(\prod_i\{-b_i(j_i),\ldots,b_i(j_i)\}\). Conditional on a fixed root \(\mathbf j\), let \(V_i\) be independent and uniform on these coordinate displacement intervals. These auxiliary displacement probabilities do not replace the original root probabilities. For every admissible displacement \(\mathbf v\) with both endpoints in \(E\), log-affinity in (3) gives the first identity below; summing over exactly those displacements gives the second:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min\{\widetilde p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},\widetilde p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\}
&=\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}e^{-|\sum_i s_i v_i|},\\
S_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)
&=\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}\,
\mathbb E[e^{-|\sum_i s_iV_i|}\mid\mathbf j],
\qquad
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}:=\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}\prod_iN_i(j_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The exact coordinate alias for `f35126e13cfd3cb5` is \(b_i(j)=b_j\), \(N_i(j)=N_j=k_j\), and \(N_i(j)p_i(j)=\mathsf H_j\) after substituting its \(L=L_i\), \(r=r_i\), and \(s=s_i=-\log r_i\). Thus \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=\prod_i\mathsf H_{j_i}\) uses oriented counts including zero displacement.

Every \(V_i\) is centered and has second moment \(b_i(j_i)(b_i(j_i)+1)/3\), by the finite sum of squares on \(\{-b_i,\ldots,b_i\}\). Independence eliminates all cross terms. Define the conditional score variance
\(v(\mathbf j)=\sum_i s_i^2b_i(j_i)(b_i(j_i)+1)/3\).
Cauchy--Schwarz and Jensen for \(t\mapsto e^{-t}\) yield
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-|\sum_i s_iV_i|}\mid\mathbf j]
\ge e^{-\mathbb E[|\sum_i s_iV_i|\mid\mathbf j]}
\ge e^{-\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(L_i=1\) or \(L_i=0\), one has \(b_i(j_i)=0\) at every root, so those coordinates contribute exactly zero to \(v\), regardless of \(s_i\).

Define the finite factors \(F_i=\sum_{j=0}^{L_i}p_i(j)^4N_i(j)^3\) and the auxiliary coordinate laws \(Q_i(j)=p_i(j)^4N_i(j)^3/F_i\). All factors are positive, and \(Q=\bigotimes_iQ_i\) is a probability law. For \(L_i\ge2\) or \(L_i=1\), these are exactly the finite original factor \(F_{L_i}(r_i)\) and finite auxiliary law \(Q_{L_i}\) of accepted fact `f35126e13cfd3cb5`, with the unchanged rate \(s_i=-\log r_i\). For \(L_i=0\), \(p_i(0)=N_i(0)=F_i=Q_i(0)=1\). The accepted finite bounds give
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_i&\ge\alpha&& (L_i\ge2),\\
F_i&=\frac{1+r_i^4}{(1+r_i)^4}\ge\frac18&& (L_i=1),\\
\mathbb E_Qv(\mathbf J)
&=\sum_i\mathbb E_{Q_i}\frac{s_i^2b_i(J_i)(b_i(J_i)+1)}3
\le C_{\mathrm{score}}m_{\mathrm{nb}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The last inequality includes \(r_i=1\), when \(s_i=0\), and arbitrarily tilted binary or frozen coordinates. Only the \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}\) nonbinary indices can have a nonzero summand.

Now cube (4)--(6), multiply by the original root probability \(\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}\), and sum. By (3) and (5), \(\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^3=\prod_i p_i(j_i)^4N_i(j_i)^3\). Consequently the normalization is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)=M_3(\widetilde p)
&\ge\frac18\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}
\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^3e^{-3\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}}\\
&=\frac18\left(\prod_iF_i\right)
\mathbb E_Qe^{-3\sqrt{v(\mathbf J)}}\\
&\ge\frac18\left(\prod_iF_i\right)
e^{-3\mathbb E_Q\sqrt{v(\mathbf J)}}\\
&\ge\frac18\left(\prod_iF_i\right)
e^{-3\sqrt{\mathbb E_Qv(\mathbf J)}}\\
&\ge\frac18\alpha^{m_{\mathrm{nb}}}8^{-b_{\mathrm{bin}}}e^{-3\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{score}}m_{\mathrm{nb}}}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The first Jensen step uses the convex function \(t\mapsto e^{-3t}\); the next step uses Cauchy--Schwarz and the fact that the exponential decreases in its argument. The auxiliary law \(Q\) occurs solely as an exact rewrite of the displayed original sum. In particular, there is one original root-mass factor and three original depth-mass factors, giving fourth powers, with no hidden conditional-mass power or new moment interpretation. Equations (7)--(8) pay score attenuation only \(e^{-3\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{score}}m_{\mathrm{nb}}}}\), independently of \(b_{\mathrm{bin}}\) and of the number of frozen coordinates.

Since \(R=m_{\mathrm{nb}}+b_{\mathrm{bin}}\), the last line of (8) equals
\(\tfrac18 8^{-R}\exp(\lambda_{\mathrm{box}} m_{\mathrm{nb}}-\sqrt{92m_{\mathrm{nb}}})\).
The strict inequality \(\alpha>1/8\) is part of accepted fact `f35126e13cfd3cb5`, so \(\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}>0\). The following square completion is valid for every real \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}\ge0\), including \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}=0\):
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{box}} m_{\mathrm{nb}}-\sqrt{92m_{\mathrm{nb}}}
=\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}\left(\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{nb}}}-\frac{\sqrt{92}}{2\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}}\right)^2
-\frac{23}{\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}}
\ge-\frac{23}{\lambda_{\mathrm{box}}}.
\tag{9}
\]
This gives the middle inequality of (1). As \(R\le n\), it also gives the ambient-dimensional bound in (1). The coefficient \(\kappa_{\mathrm{box}}\) is positive and absolute.

Finally suppose \(m_{\mathrm{nb}}=0\). If \(R>0\), each root \(\mathbf j\in E\) can be isolated by a containing closed cap: set \(u_i=1\) when \(L_i=1,j_i=1\), set \(u_i=-1\) when \(L_i=1,j_i=0\), and set \(u_i=0\) when \(L_i=0\). For every other \(\mathbf k\in E\), \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf k-\mathbf j)<0\), while the root gives equality. Thus \(\{\mathbf t:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf t-\mathbf j)\ge0\}\) is a proper closed halfspace with support trace \(\{\mathbf j\}\). Every containing cap also includes the root, so \(\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf j)=\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}\). If \(R=0\), the same equality is one. Therefore
\(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}\widetilde p_{\mathbf j}^4=\prod_i\sum_{j=0}^{L_i}p_i(j)^4\).
Frozen factors are one; binary factors are exactly those in (2), and their lower bound \(1/8\) is supplied by accepted fact `f35126e13cfd3cb5`. This proves (2) and completes every boundary and rank-zero case.
