## Statement

Let $C$ be a compact convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, and let a nonvertical plane through $Y=(z_0,x_0)$ cut the interior of its positive-volume own fiber. With unit continuous normal $b$, rotate the continuous coordinates so $b=e_d$; then the plane is $x_d=x_{0d}-a\cdot(z-z_0)$. The shear $(z,x',x_d)\mapsto(z,x',x_d+a\cdot(z-z_0)-x_{0d})$ identifies its section with a convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d-1}$, with the integer base unchanged and exact preservation of each fiber's $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ section measure. The full induced section measure agrees with its restriction to positive original $d$-fibers exactly when the original $d$-null fibers contribute no $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ section measure. If, for every positive original fiber $K_z$, its planar section $L_z$ satisfies $\mathcal H^{d-1}(L_z\cap\partial K_z)=0$, the restriction is the section measure giving the first derivative of cap mass under parallel shifts.

It is possible for a regular global minimizing cut to have its derivative section centered at the mark but its full intrinsic mixed section centered elsewhere. In $n=1,d=2$, take the hull of the unit disk at base zero and the horizontal segment $[-1,1]\times\{0\}$ at base one. The cap $x_2\ge0$ through the disk center is regular for the actual area measure and globally minimizes the containing cap mass. Its derivative section has ambient centroid $(0,0,0)$, but the full induced length section has base centroid $1/2$.

## Proof

After the rotation the displayed shear takes the cut plane to $x_d=0$, fixes the base $z$, and acts on each fixed-base slice by a translation. Thus it preserves $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ on that slice, also for $d=1$ when the section measure is counting measure. There is no ambient-plane Jacobian in the sum of these measures. There are points of $C$ on both sides because the plane cuts the interior of the own fiber. Mixing each slightly with an ambient interior point produces ambient interior points of both signs. Their segment therefore meets the plane in the ambient interior of $C$, proving that the section has dimension $n+d-1$. The induced full measure is the sum over all integer section fibers. Restrict it to the positive original $d$-fibers. The omitted contributions are nonnegative and disjoint by base, so equality of the two measures is equivalent to the stated null condition. Under the additional section regularity, in each positive fiber the indicator of $K_z$ on nearby parallel planes converges to the indicator on the given plane for almost every transverse coordinate, since only boundary points can disrupt convergence. The indicators have bounded support. Dominated convergence, also with any continuous test function, gives continuity of the slice integrals there. By Fubini, moving the retained threshold by $t$ subtracts the integral of the slice integrals between $0$ and $t$. Therefore the first derivative is minus the stated restricted section integral. The zero-volume fibers contribute identically zero to the original cap mass.

In the example the lifted disk and segment generate a full-dimensional body with exactly the two named integer fibers; at the extremes no point from the other base enters the section. The original mixed area is supported on the disk alone. Moving a containing halfspace parallel to the center can only reduce its mass; at the center a nonvertical trace cuts the disk into halves, and a pure-base cap retains the full disk. Thus the named cap attains raw depth $\pi/2$. Its only positive-area fiber trace cuts the disk properly, with just the two endpoints on the disk boundary, so it is regular for area differentiation. The derivative length measure is the central diameter, centered at the mark. The original null fiber lies wholly in the horizontal plane and contributes a second length-two segment at base one to the full section. Equal lengths at bases zero and one give base centroid $1/2$.
