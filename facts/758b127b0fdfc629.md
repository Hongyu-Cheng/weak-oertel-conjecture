## Statement

Let $K$ be the unit disk in $\mathbb R^2$, fix $a>0$, and sample its first coordinate on the affine lattice with consecutive sites $-1+\varepsilon$ and $1-a\varepsilon$. For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ these are its only sites in $[-1,1]$. The normalized sum of the lengths of the sampled vertical fibers converges weakly, as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, to the law on $(-1,0),(1,0)$ with weights $1/(1+\sqrt a)$ and $\sqrt a/(1+\sqrt a)$, respectively. The limit lattice is $-1+2\mathbb Z$, whose disk trace has equal weights under normalized counting measure.

## Proof

The lattice spacing is $2-(a+1)\varepsilon>0$. The previous and next sites tend to $-3$ and $3$, respectively, so eventually they are outside the disk's first-coordinate range. For each vertical fiber at $z$ the length is $2\sqrt{1-z^2}$. Thus the two lengths are $2\sqrt{2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2}$ and $2\sqrt{2a\varepsilon-a^2\varepsilon^2}$, in a ratio tending to $1:\sqrt a$. The two segments shrink to the indicated tangent points. Against any bounded continuous test function, each segment's uniform average tends to the function's value at the corresponding tangent point. Multiplication by the limiting length fractions proves weak convergence. The limit lattice has exactly those two tangent sites in the disk, each of counting mass one. For $a\ne1$ the normalized weights differ from the counting law.
