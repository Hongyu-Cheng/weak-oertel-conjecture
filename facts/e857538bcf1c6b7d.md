## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(R>0\), and let \(B\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) satisfy \(|B_{li}|\le1\). Put \(|x|=(|x_1|,\ldots,|x_n|)\), \(a(x)=R-\sum_i|x_i|\), and \(c_B(x)=B|x|\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_B&=\left\{(x,y):\sum_i|x_i|\le R,\quad
c_B(x)-a(x)\mathbf1_d\le y\le c_B(x)+a(x)\mathbf1_d\right\},\\
S_B&=C_B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
D_+=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i|z_i|<R\right\},\\
a_z&=R-\sum_i|z_i|,\qquad
C_{B,z}=c_B(z)+[-a_z,a_z]^d,\\
w_z&=(2a_z)^d,\qquad W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z,\qquad p_z=w_z/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

All vector inequalities are coordinatewise. The depth \(h_{S_B}\) is the depth in the locked problem, with the actual mixed volume \(\mathcal H_d(S_B)=W>0\).

If \(d<n\), there exists a point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S_B\), fixed before every containing closed halfspace, with
\(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge(1/64)2^{-n}W\).
More precisely, if \(n\ge32\) and \(p_{0_n}<1/2\), the fixed point
\(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,B\mu)\), where \(\mu=\mathbb E_p|Z|\), satisfies
\(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge W/(4096n^3)\ge2^{-n}W\).
Here \(Z\) has exactly the original law \(p\), rather than an auxiliary radial law.

For completeness, retain the exact constant of accepted fact `43a45f629e754bbb`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0&=64\cdot10^{10},\\
\delta&=\frac{e^{-4}}{96}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{225}{2}e^8-60\sqrt6\,e^6\right),\\
c_0&=\min\!\left\{\frac\delta4,
\frac{1}{24\sqrt{15}(\log2)(N_0+5)},\frac1{2e}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then for every \(n,d,R,B\) in the statement, without any order restriction on \(n,d\), there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S_B\) with
\(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c_0 2^{-n}W\).
This conclusion concerns this specified cube class, not arbitrary convex bodies.

## Proof

First check the body and the actual probability law. For each row \(l\), the upper constraint is
\(y_l\le R-\sum_i(1-B_{li})|x_i|\), and the lower constraint is
\(y_l\ge-R+\sum_i(1+B_{li})|x_i|\).
All coefficients in both sums are nonnegative. The upper right side is concave and the lower right side is convex, so their hypographs and epigraphs are convex. The base constraint is convex as well. All constraints are closed and imply \(|x_i|\le R\) and \(-R\le y_l\le R\); thus \(C_B\) is compact. They are all strict at \((0_n,0_d)\), so continuity gives an ambient open neighborhood in \(C_B\). Hence it is a full-dimensional convex body.

Its positive integer fibers are exactly the stated cubes with \(z\in D_+\), and their actual volumes are \(w_z=(2a_z)^d\). A fiber with \(\sum_i|z_i|=R\) is a singleton and has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume. Other integer fibers are empty. The support is finite, and \(0_n\in D_+\) has volume \((2R)^d>0\). It follows directly from the mixed-volume definition that \(W=\mathcal H_d(S_B)>0\).

Let \(Z\) have the original law \(p_z\). Independently, let \(U\) be uniform on \([-1/2,1/2]^d\), and set
\(Y=B|Z|+2a_ZU\).
Conditional on \(Z=z\), this is uniform on the actual fiber \(C_{B,z}\). Therefore for every measurable ambient set \(A\),
\(\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in A)=\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap A)/W\).
Boundary fibers have zero mass on both sides. All probabilities below use this original law.

Assume now \(d<n\), so \(n\ge2\). If \(p_{0_n}\ge1/2\), fix \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,0_d)\). Every ambient closed halfspace containing this point has, on the origin fiber, either the entire cube or a containing closed fiber halfspace. A cube is centrally symmetric about zero; reflection shows that every such fiber halfspace has volume at least \(w_{0_n}/2\). Thus \(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge w_{0_n}/2\ge W/4\ge2^{-n}W\). The whole ambient space also satisfies this estimate. Hence suppose below that \(p_{0_n}<1/2\).

We first reprove an exact first-moment shell inequality, valid for all positive integers \(n,d\), with no hypothesis \(d\ge n\). Write \(L=\sum_i|Z_i|\), and let \(A_n(s)\) count the signed lattice vectors of \(\ell_1\) norm \(s\), for integers \(s\ge0\). Write \(t_+=\max\{t,0\}\). The one-coordinate formal counting series is \(M(t)=(1+t)/(1-t)\), and \(\sum_sA_n(s)t^s=M(t)^n\), so differentiation gives

\[
\sum_{s\ge0}sA_n(s)t^s
=\frac{2nt}{1-t^2}\sum_{s\ge0}A_n(s)t^s.
\]

For \(b>0\), the function \(f(u)=(b-u)_+^d\) on \([0,\infty)\) is convex because \(d\ge1\). Its midpoint inequality on \([2j,2j+2]\), summed over \(j\ge0\), gives
\(2\sum_{j\ge0}(b-2j-1)_+^d\le\int_0^\infty(b-u)_+^d\,du=b^{d+1}/(d+1)\).
Using the coefficient identity in the preceding display yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\ge0}sA_n(s)(R-s)_+^d
&=2n\sum_{r\ge0}A_n(r)
\sum_{j\ge0}(R-r-2j-1)_+^d\\
&\le\frac n{d+1}\sum_{r\ge0}A_n(r)(R-r)_+^{d+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The terms with \(r\ge R\) vanish, and every surviving summand is finite in number. A shell at \(s=R\), when present, contributes zero. Dividing by \(\sum_s A_n(s)(R-s)_+^d\) proves
\(\mathbb E_pL\le[n/(d+1)]\mathbb E_pa_Z\).
Since \(L+a_Z=R\), it follows that
\(\mathbb E_pa_Z\ge[(d+1)/(n+d+1)]R\).
In the present regime \(d<n\), the numerator is at least \(2\) and the denominator is at most \(2n\). Consequently
\(\mathbb E_pa_Z\ge R/n\), and Jensen's inequality gives
\(\mathbb E_pa_Z^2\ge R^2/n^2\).
This derivation is an independent proof of the needed shell estimate, not an application outside a predecessor's quantifiers.

We next prove
\(m_2:=\mathbb E_p|Z_1|^2\ge R^2/(512n^3)\)
under \(p_{0_n}<1/2\). The coordinate law is exchangeable. If \(R<16n\), the event \(Z\ne0_n\) has probability greater than \(1/2\), and the integer inequality \(\sum_i|Z_i|^2\ge1\) holds on that event. Thus \(nm_2>1/2\), which implies the claimed bound since \(R^2<256n^2\).

For \(R\ge16n\), let \(N(t)=|\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|z\|_1\le t\}|\). The unit cubes centered at these lattice points have disjoint interiors. Their union is contained in the \(\ell_1\) ball of radius \(t+n/2\); if \(t\ge n/2\), it contains the \(\ell_1\) ball of radius \(t-n/2\), by rounding each coordinate to a nearest integer. The volume of the \(\ell_1\) ball of radius \(r\ge0\) is \(2^nr^n/n!\), obtained by dividing into its \(2^n\) orthants and successively integrating the nonnegative simplex. Therefore
\(N(t)\le2^n(t+n/2)^n/n!\), and
\(N(t)\ge2^n(t-n/2)^n/n!\) for \(t\ge n/2\).
The points with \(\|z\|_1\le R/2\) all belong to \(D_+\) and have \(a_z\ge R/2\). The points with \(\|z\|_1<R/8\) have \(a_z\le R\). Cancelling the common factor \(2^d\) in the original weights gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
p(L<R/8)
&\le\frac{N(R/8)R^d}{N(R/2)(R/2)^d}\\
&\le2^d\left(\frac{R/8+n/2}{R/2-n/2}\right)^n
\le\frac{2^d}{3^n}
\le\left(\frac23\right)^n\le\frac23.
\end{aligned}
\]

The penultimate comparison uses \(d<n\). The ratio bound uses \(n/2\le R/32\), so the numerator is at most \(5R/32\) and the denominator at least \(15R/32\). Thus \(p(L\ge R/8)\ge1/3\), giving \(\mathbb E_pL^2\ge R^2/192\). Pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz and exchangeability give \(\mathbb E_pL^2\le n\mathbb E_p\sum_i|Z_i|^2=n^2m_2\). Hence \(m_2\ge R^2/(192n^2)\ge R^2/(512n^3)\), as required. Both radius branches are exact; no large-radius approximation is used.

Put \(\mu=\mathbb E_p|Z|\). The original law is invariant under every coordinate sign change. Hence \(\mathbb EZ=0_n\), and \(\mathbb EY=B\mu\). Also
\(|(B\mu)_l|\le\sum_i\mu_i=\mathbb E_pL<R\).
Thus the actual mean \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,B\mu)\) belongs to the origin fiber and to \(S_B\). Fix this point now, before any halfspace is chosen.

For arbitrary ambient coefficients \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), define the centered scalar projection
\(F=\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-B\mu)\).
Set \(A=\alpha\cdot Z\) and \(Q=\beta\cdot(Y-B\mu)\), so \(F=A+Q\), and both terms have mean zero. Sign symmetry gives \(\mathbb EZ_iZ_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\), and therefore
\(\operatorname{Var}A=m_2\|\alpha\|_2^2\).
It also gives \(\mathbb E[Z_i(|Z_j|-\mu_j)]=0\) for all \(i,j\). The conditional cube noise is centered, so \(\operatorname{Cov}(A,Q)=0\).
Since the independent coordinates of \(U\) have variance \(1/12\), conditioning on \(Z\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}Q
&=\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\beta\cdot B(|Z|-\mu)\bigr)
+\frac{\mathbb E_pa_Z^2}{3}\|\beta\|_2^2,\\
\sigma^2:=\mathbb EF^2
&=m_2\|\alpha\|_2^2+\operatorname{Var}Q
\ge m_2\|\alpha\|_2^2+\frac{R^2}{3n^2}\|\beta\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular \(\sigma^2>0\) for every nonzero ambient normal. The actual support satisfies \(\|Z\|_2\le L<R\), and each coordinate of \(Y\), as well as each coordinate of \(B\mu\), lies in \([-R,R]\). Thus pointwise

\[
\begin{aligned}
F^2&\le2A^2+2Q^2
\le2R^2\|\alpha\|_2^2+8R^2d\|\beta\|_2^2\\
&\le1024n^3m_2\|\alpha\|_2^2
+24dn^2\operatorname{Var}Q
\le1024n^3\sigma^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the first term uses the proved lower bound on \(m_2\), and the second uses \(\operatorname{Var}Q\ge R^2\|\beta\|_2^2/(3n^2)\). The final comparison uses \(d<n\). Multiplying by \(F^2\) and integrating proves
\(\mathbb EF^4\le1024n^3\sigma^4\).

We give the scalar cap argument explicitly. A centered scalar variable \(F\) with positive variance \(\sigma^2\) and \(\mathbb EF^4\le\kappa\sigma^4\) satisfies, by Hölder's inequality,
\(\sigma^2\le(\mathbb E|F|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|F|^4)^{1/3}\), and hence \(\mathbb E|F|\ge\sigma/\sqrt\kappa\).
Centering gives \(\mathbb EF_+=\mathbb E|F|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\((\mathbb EF_+)^2\le\mathbb P(F>0)\mathbb EF_+^2\le\mathbb P(F>0)\sigma^2\).
Therefore \(\mathbb P(F\ge0)\ge\mathbb P(F>0)\ge1/(4\kappa)\).
With \(\kappa=1024n^3\), every proper closed halfspace through the fixed actual mean has original probability at least \(1/(4096n^3)\).
Every closed halfspace containing that mean contains its parallel closed halfspace through the mean. The original-law identity for \((Z,Y)\) therefore proves
\(h_{S_B}((0_n,B\mu))\ge W/(4096n^3)\).
The full ambient space also satisfies this inequality. This proves the stated polynomial bound for all \(d<n\) under \(p_{0_n}<1/2\).

For \(n\ge32\), one has \(2^n\ge4096n^3\). Indeed, at \(n=32\) the right side is \(2^{27}\), and the left side is \(2^{32}\). For successive integers \(n\ge32\), the factor by which \(n^3\) increases is at most \((33/32)^3<2\), since \(33^3=35937<65536=2\cdot32^3\); the factor for \(2^n\) is exactly \(2\). Induction proves the inequality. Together with the heavy-origin branch, this establishes \(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge2^{-n}W\) whenever \(n\ge32\) and \(d<n\).

It remains to patch \(n<32\). We use only the arbitrary-selector theorem, equation (8), of accepted fact `ae8bb1ec375ace78`. Its body \(C\) is our \(C_B\), its fiber \(C_z\) is our \(C_{B,z}\), and its \(S\) and depth are our \(S_B\) and \(h_{S_B}\). The quantity denoted \(a_z\) in that fact's centroid specialization is the actual fiber volume \(w_z\) here; our \(a_z\) is instead the cube radius. In its arbitrary-selector theorem take \(I=J=D_+\), \(q_z=c_B(z)\), and its selector weight \(w_z\) equal to our actual weight divided by two. Its total selector weight is therefore \(T=W/2\). These weights are permitted because a centrally symmetric cube gives absolute fiber depth at its centroid at least half its actual volume. Take its affine map \(s\) to be the constant zero map. Its residual span rank \(r_s\) is at most \(d\), and its support affine dimension \(\rho\) is at most \(n\). Equation (8) supplies a point in \(S_B\), fixed before all containing halfspaces, with
\(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge W/[2^{\rho+1}(r_s+1)]\ge W/[2^{n+1}(d+1)]\).
Since \(d<n<32\), one has \(d+1\le31<32\), so this is at least \((1/64)2^{-n}W\). This application selects an actual point, rather than relying only on an unattained supremum of depths.

The two branches prove the whole \(d<n\) assertion with the absolute constant \(1/64\). Finally, accepted fact `43a45f629e754bbb` applies to exactly our body, actual fiber volumes, and depth when \(d\ge n\), with the unchanged constant \(c_0\) displayed in the statement. We use it only within that order restriction. Its \(\delta\) satisfies \(0<\delta<1/96\), so \(c_0\le\delta/4<1/384<1/64\). The newly proved complementary branch therefore also satisfies its \(c_0\) bound. This proves the entire stated cube class with the same absolute \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\). All radius arguments kept the exact \(R\), including integral radii, and all witnesses are chosen before every containing halfspace.
