## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge1$, finite nonempty lattice-convex $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ and $z_0\in P\cap\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}P$. Let depth mean the maximum, over actual lifted fiber points, of the infimum of summed $d$-volume of containing closed ambient halfspaces, divided by total summed $d$-volume. If a compatible family over exactly $P$ of positive total volume has depth less than $\alpha$, then for some finite $N$ this depth attains its minimum $\beta_N<\alpha$ in the central-unit-volume shared-plane class of the preceding lemma. At a minimizing family with null fibers their removal preserves the measure and maximum depth and gives a proper lattice-convex subprojection. Otherwise all fibers are positive and arbitrary small shared coefficient changes are actual competitors after restoring central unit volume.

## Proof

Take a finite net inside each original fiber, including a point at every site and $d+1$ affinely independent points at each positive site. Form the polytope given by the hull of the lifted nets. Its actual fibers lie between the hulls of the respective nets and the original fibers, so they converge simultaneously in Hausdorff distance as the mesh tends to zero. Their projection is exactly $P$, and their central fiber has positive volume by the interior barycentric weights in 2fadf089fb53a6e3. Depth continuity from cac63cc5f166ba86 preserves strict depth below $\alpha$ for some such polytope. The continuity proof there uses a common ball, positive limiting mass and moving marks, so it applies before normalizing these uniformly bounded approximants.

The polytope has full relative dimension in $\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d$ because the central ball supplies the $d$ vertical directions and its base spans $\operatorname{aff}(P)$. Take every nonvertical facet row in a relative halfspace description, extending the base-affine terms to $\mathbb R^n$. The vertical facet rows hold throughout the projection and need no place in the shared list. All sampled fibers are represented by the same finite list, so choose $N$ equal to its length. Normalize the central volume. More generally a map $(z,x)\mapsto(z,Ax+Bz+b)$ with invertible $A$ preserves exact compatibility and the common plane bound: substitution transforms all rows with $n_f$ replaced by $A^{-T}n_f\ne0$. It multiplies all fiber and cap masses by the same $|\det A|$ and preserves normalized depth.

Take a minimizing sequence for that $N$. Apply the determinant-one bounded normalization of 2fadf089fb53a6e3 to put all fibers in a common ball. By the compactness argument of cac63cc5f166ba86, a simultaneous subsequence has nonempty compatible limits over exactly $P$, with central volume one, and its depths converge. These compactness and continuity statements apply to the stated relative-interior setting; the full-base-rank condition of that fact's last conclusion concerns full ambient dimension of the hull. The shared-plane closure lemma keeps the limit in the same $N$ class, proving attainment. If null fibers are present, 55a57b1cf55d0c87 applies to the compatible limit and its summed $d$-volume to give the claimed deletion and proper subprojection. Otherwise the all-positive part of shared-plane closure gives the permissible variations.
