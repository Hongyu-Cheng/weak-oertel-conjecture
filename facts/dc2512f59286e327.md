## Statement

Fix \(D:=20000\) and \(M:=10000\). There is an explicit sequence of compact full-dimensional convex polytopes
\[
C_k\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+d_k},\qquad d_k:=Dk,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\]
whose integer-coordinate roofs are nonnegative affine functions. Put
\[
\Lambda_k:=\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^{d_k},\qquad
S_k:=C_k\cap\Lambda_k,\qquad
W_k:=\mathcal H_{d_k}(S_k)>0.
\]
Each \(C_k\) has only finitely many nonempty lattice fibers. For \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^k\) and \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^{d_k}\), define the same-continuous-fiber reflected count
\[
R_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf y)
:=
\#\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^k:
(\mathbf I,\mathbf y),(2\mathbf J-\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C_k\}.
\]
For a nonnegative integrable function \(q\) on \(\mathbb R^{d_k}\), define
\[
\tau_q(\mathbf y)
:=
\inf\left\{\int_Gq:
G\subseteq\mathbb R^{d_k}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf y\in G
\right\}.
\]
Then, for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^k}\int_{\mathbb R^{d_k}}R_{\mathbf J}
<
\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^k2^{-k}W_k,
\tag{1}
\]
and, simultaneously for every measurable probability density \(g\) on \(\mathbb R^{d_k}\) and every \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^k\\0<\alpha<1\\g\ge0,\ \int g=1}}
\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{d_k}}R_{\mathbf J}^{\alpha}g^{1-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}
<
\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^k2^{-k}W_k,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^k,\ \mathbf y\in\mathbb R^{d_k}}
\tau_{R_{\mathbf J}}(\mathbf y)
<
\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^k2^{-k}W_k.
\tag{3}
\]
The reference density and exponent in (2) may depend arbitrarily on \(k\), \(C_k\), and \(\mathbf J\). Consequently, for every absolute \(c_1>0\), all three possible fixed-center reflection certificates are smaller than \(c_1 2^{-k}W_k\) for all sufficiently large \(k\).

In contrast, each of these same bodies has a point \(\mathbf p_k^\star\in S_k\) such that its actual mixed halfspace depth satisfies
\[
h_{S_k}(\mathbf p_k^\star)
\ge
\frac1e\left(\frac{1499}{1501}\right)^kW_k.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{h_{S_k}(\mathbf p_k^\star)}{2^{-k}W_k}
\ge
\frac1e\left(\frac{2998}{1501}\right)^k,
\tag{5}
\]
so this sequence satisfies, rather than contradicts, the conclusion of the locked problem for every proposed \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\).

## Proof

Define, for \(0\le t\le6\),
\[
a(t):=t,
\qquad
b(t):=\frac{6-t}{20},
\qquad
r_j(t):=2[\min\{a(t)+j,b(t)-j\}]_+
\quad(j\in\mathbb R).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(r_j(t)\) is exactly the length of the intersection of the interval \([-a(t),b(t)]\) with its reflection through \(j\). Its total unreflected scalar mass under \(e^{-t}dt\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{\rm e}
&:=\int_0^6(a(t)+b(t))e^{-t}\,dt\\
&=\frac{19}{20}(1-7e^{-6})+\frac3{10}(1-e^{-6})
=\frac54-\frac{139}{20}e^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

We first prove the uniform scalar estimates
\[
\sup_{j\in\mathbb R}\int_0^6r_j(t)e^{-t}\,dt
\le\frac{2433}{4000},
\qquad
W_{\rm e}\ge\frac{9861}{8000}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(j\ge0\), \(r_j(t)\le2b(t)\), and hence
\[
\int_0^6r_j(t)e^{-t}\,dt
\le\frac1{10}\int_0^6(6-t)e^{-t}\,dt
=\frac12+\frac1{10}e^{-6}.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(j=-s\) and \(s\ge3\),
\[
r_{-s}(t)\le2(t-s)_+,
\qquad
\int_0^6r_{-s}(t)e^{-t}\,dt
\le2e^{-s}\le2e^{-3}.
\tag{10}
\]
It remains to take \(j=-s\) with \(0\le s\le3\). The two terms in the minimum in (6) cross at
\[
t_0=\frac{6+40s}{21}.
\]
Integrating on \([s,t_0]\) and \([t_0,6]\) gives the exact identity
\[
\int_0^6r_{-s}(t)e^{-t}\,dt
=2\left[e^{-s}-\frac{21}{20}e^{-(6+40s)/21}
-\left(s-\frac1{20}\right)e^{-6}\right].
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore this integral is at most
\[
2\left[e^{-s}-\frac{21}{20}e^{-(6+40s)/21}\right]
+\frac1{10}e^{-6}.
\tag{12}
\]
The derivative of the first term in (12) has the sign of
\(2e^{-(6+19s)/21}-1\). It changes sign once, at
\[
s_*=\frac{21\log2-6}{19}\in(0,3),
\]
so the maximum of that term on \([0,3]\) is
\[
\frac{19}{20}\exp\left(\frac{6-21\log2}{19}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
Here are exact rational checks for the three scalar estimates used next:
\[
2\left(\frac13+\frac1{81}+\frac1{1215}\right)-\frac{69}{100}
=\frac{73}{24300}>0,
\tag{14}
\]
and the positive-term Taylor series give
\[
\sum_{q=0}^4\frac{(849/1900)^q}{q!}-\frac{25}{16}
=\frac{73727924667}{104256800000000}>0,
\tag{15}
\]
\[
\sum_{q=0}^8\frac{3^q}{q!}-20
=\frac{41}{4480}>0.
\tag{16}
\]
The identity
\(\log2=2\sum_{q\ge0}3^{-(2q+1)}/(2q+1)\) and (14) imply
\(\log2>69/100\). Equation (15) implies
\(e^{-849/1900}<16/25\), while (16) implies \(e^{-6}<1/400\) and \(2e^{-3}<1/10\). Consequently, (9), (10), and (12)-(13) are all bounded by
\[
\frac{19}{20}\frac{16}{25}+\frac1{4000}
=\frac{2433}{4000}.
\]
The second part of (8) follows from (7) and \(e^{-6}<1/400\):
\[
W_{\rm e}\ge\frac54-\frac{139}{8000}
=\frac{9861}{8000}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{\sup_j\int_0^6r_j(t)e^{-t}\,dt}{W_{\rm e}}
\le\frac{4866}{9861}<\frac12,
\tag{17}
\]
where the exact margin in the last inequality is
\(1/2-4866/9861=43/6574\).

We now realize this weighted calculation by uniform volume on a finite-dimensional polytope. Let
\[
T_D:=\left\{(t,\mathbf z)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{D-1}:
0\le t\le6,\ z_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{D-1}z_i\le D-t\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
This is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope. Its \(\mathbf z\)-section at \(t\) has volume
\[
\frac{(D-t)^{D-1}}{(D-1)!}
=c_Dg_D(t),
\qquad
c_D:=\frac{D^{D-1}}{(D-1)!},
\qquad
g_D(t):=\left(1-\frac tD\right)^{D-1}.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(u=t/D\), the elementary inequalities
\[
\log(1-u)\le-u,
\qquad
\log(1-u)\ge-u-\frac{u^2}{2(1-u)}
\]
give, throughout \(0\le t\le6\),
\[
-\frac{18}{19994}
\le \log g_D(t)+t
\le\frac3{10000}.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, in the lower estimate one drops the positive term \(t/D\) and uses \(t^2\le36\). Since \(e^{-v}\ge1-v\) for \(v\ge0\), and \(e^v\le(1-v)^{-1}\) for \(0\le v<1\), (20) yields the convenient pointwise bounds
\[
\frac{999}{1000}e^{-t}
\le g_D(t)
\le\frac{1001}{1000}e^{-t}.
\tag{21}
\]
For the lower bound, \(1-18/19994>999/1000\); for the upper bound,
\((1-3/10000)^{-1}<1001/1000\), so no decimal approximation is being used.

Put
\[
W_{\rm c}:=\int_0^6(a+b)g_D\,dt,
\qquad
V_{\rm c}:=\int_0^6g_D\,dt.
\]
Equations (8), (17), and (21) imply
\[
\frac{\sup_j\int_0^6r_jg_D\,dt}{W_{\rm c}}
\le
\frac{1001}{999}\frac{4866}{9861}
=\frac{1623622}{3283713}
<\frac{989}{2000},
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
W_{\rm c}\ge\frac{999}{1000}\frac{9861}{8000},
\qquad
V_{\rm c}\le\frac{1001}{1000}.
\tag{23}
\]
The exact margin in (22) is
\[
\frac{989}{2000}-\frac{1623622}{3283713}
=\frac{348157}{6567426000}>0.
\tag{24}
\]

Define the one-integer-coordinate polytope
\[
C_0:=\left\{(x,t,\mathbf z):(t,\mathbf z)\in T_D,
\ -Mt\le x\le1+\frac M{20}(6-t)\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]
It is compact, convex, and full-dimensional. In canonical asymmetric-roof notation,
\[
\ell^-(t,\mathbf z):=Mt,
\qquad
\ell^+(t,\mathbf z):=\frac M{20}(6-t),
\]
so the fiber is \(-\ell^-\le x\le1+\ell^+\). Both roofs are nonnegative and affine on \(T_D\). Only finitely many integers occur in its bounded \(x\)-projection.

Fix \(t\), and abbreviate
\[
A(t):=\lfloor Mt\rfloor,
\qquad
B(t):=\lfloor Mb(t)\rfloor.
\]
The lattice fiber is exactly
\[
\{-A(t),-A(t)+1,\ldots,B(t)+1\},
\]
and hence its exact cardinality is
\[
N_M(t)=A(t)+B(t)+2
=\lfloor Ma(t)\rfloor+\lfloor Mb(t)\rfloor+2.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(J\in\mathbb Z\), closed-endpoint intersection gives the exact reflected census
\[
R_{J,M}(t)
=\left[
\min\{B(t)+1,2J+A(t)\}
-\max\{-A(t),2J-B(t)-1\}+1
\right]_+.
\tag{27}
\]
The actual integer interval is the set of integer points of
\([-Ma(t),Mb(t)+1]\). Its intersection with its reflection through \(J\) has at most its real length plus one integer points. If the right endpoint in that real interval is moved from \(Mb(t)\) to \(Mb(t)+1\), the upper endpoint of the overlap can increase by at most one and the lower endpoint can decrease by at most one. Since the unextended real overlap has length \(Mr_{J/M}(t)\), (27) therefore yields, for every \(J,t\),
\[
R_{J,M}(t)\le Mr_{J/M}(t)+3.
\tag{28}
\]
Also (26), using \(\lfloor u\rfloor\ge u-1\), gives
\[
N_M(t)\ge M(a(t)+b(t)).
\tag{29}
\]

Let \(S_0=C_0\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^D)\), and let
\(W_0:=\mathcal H_D(S_0)\). Fubini-Tonelli and (19), (26) give the exact full original mass identity
\[
W_0=c_D\int_0^6N_M(t)g_D(t)\,dt,
\tag{30}
\]
not the mass of a cap, erosion, or reflected subset. From (23), (28)-(30),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sup_{J\in\mathbb Z}\int_{T_D}R_{J,M}}{W_0}
&\le
\frac{1623622}{3283713}
+\frac{3\cdot1001\cdot8000}{999\,M\,9861}\\
&<\frac{989}{2000}+\frac1{4000}
=\frac{1979}{4000}
<\frac{99}{200}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
The two final strict comparisons are exact:
\[
\frac1{4000}
-\frac{3\cdot1001\cdot8000}{999\cdot10000\cdot9861}
=\frac{80513}{13134852000}>0,
\qquad
\frac{99}{200}-\frac{1979}{4000}=\frac1{4000}>0.
\tag{32}
\]

For \(k\ge1\), take the Cartesian power \(C_0^k\) and permute coordinates so that its \(k\) integer coordinates come first. Explicitly,
\[
C_k:=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_k):
(x_i,\mathbf y_i)\in C_0\ (1\le i\le k)\right\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{Dk}.
\tag{33}
\]
This is again a compact full-dimensional convex polytope with the asserted affine roofs. Its full original mixed mass tensorizes exactly:
\[
W_k=W_0^k.
\tag{34}
\]
Writing \(\mathbf y_i=(t_i,\mathbf z_i)\), the reflected count also tensorizes pointwise:
\[
R_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_k)
=\prod_{i=1}^kR_{J_i,M}(t_i).
\tag{35}
\]
Thus (31), Tonelli, and (34)-(35) give
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J}\int R_{\mathbf J}
<\left(\frac{99}{200}\right)^kW_k
=\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^k2^{-k}W_k,
\]
which is (1).

Let now \(0<\alpha<1\) and let \(g\) be any measurable probability density on \(\mathbb R^{d_k}\). Holder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(1/\alpha\) and \(1/(1-\alpha)\) gives, for every \(\mathbf J\),
\[
\int R_{\mathbf J}^{\alpha}g^{1-\alpha}
\le
\left(\int R_{\mathbf J}\right)^\alpha
\left(\int g\right)^{1-\alpha}
=\left(\int R_{\mathbf J}\right)^\alpha.
\tag{36}
\]
The bound is uniform in \(\alpha\), \(g\), and \(\mathbf J\), even when all are selected after seeing the body. Taking the \(1/\alpha\) power and applying (1) proves (2). For any \(\mathbf y\), every halfspace integral of \(R_{\mathbf J}\) is at most its total integral, and hence
\(\tau_{R_{\mathbf J}}(\mathbf y)\le\int R_{\mathbf J}\); this proves (3).

If \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^{d_k}\) is any measurable cap of finite positive volume and \(g=\mathbf1_K/|K|\), then the left side of (2) is exactly
\[
\frac{\left(\int_KR_{\mathbf J}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{|K|^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}}.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus (2) includes simultaneous roof-threshold caps, references supported on adaptively chosen caps, and every body-dependent choice of exponent. Since \((99/100)^k\to0\), (1)-(3) have the claimed consequence for every \(c_1>0\).

It remains to establish the actual-depth calibration on these same bodies. Define
\[
E_0:=\left\{(x,t,\mathbf z):(t,\mathbf z)\in T_D,
\ -Mt+\frac32\le x\le\frac M{20}(6-t)-\frac12\right\},
\qquad
E_k:=E_0^k,
\tag{38}
\]
again with the integer coordinates permuted first. The continuous \(x\)-length in an \(E_0\)-fiber is \(M(a+b)-2\), while (26) also gives \(N_M(t)\le M(a+b)+2\). Since
\(M(a(t)+b(t))\ge3000\) on \([0,6]\),
\[
M(a+b)-2
-\frac{1499}{1501}\bigl(M(a+b)+2\bigr)
=\frac{2M(a+b)-6000}{1501}\ge0.
\tag{39}
\]
Integrating this pointwise inequality and tensorizing yields
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{k+d_k}(E_k)
\ge\left(\frac{1499}{1501}\right)^kW_k.
\tag{40}
\]
The set \(E_k\) is a compact convex polytope, and its fiber widths are at least \(2998\), so it is full-dimensional and hence is a convex body.

We now apply accepted fact \(a4e3dbc02619d36f\) with its symbols matched as follows. Its integer dimension is \(n=k\), its continuous dimension is \(d=d_k\), its \(C\) is the present \(C_k\), and
\[
P:=[-1,1]^k\times\{\mathbf0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{d_k},
\qquad \mathbf a=\mathbf0,\qquad R=1.
\]
Thus its parameter \(\alpha=3/(2R)\) is \(3/2\), and (38) gives the exact Minkowski identity
\[
E_k+\frac32P=C_k.
\tag{41}
\]
All of that fact's hypotheses hold. Its accepted transfer, whose rounding mechanism is already contained in its accepted dependency closure, supplies a fixed translate
\(D_k=E_k+(3/2)\mathbf w\) and its centroid \(\mathbf p_k^\star\in S_k\), chosen independently of a halfspace, such that
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{k+d_k}(D_k\cap H)
\le\mathcal H_{d_k}(S_k\cap H)
\tag{42}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\).

The uniform probability density on the convex body \(D_k\) is log-concave, has finite first moment, and has centroid \(\mathbf p_k^\star\). The continuous log-concave centroid halfspace inequality recorded and verified in accepted fact \(5bd39b316ef3c194\) therefore gives, for every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p_k^\star\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{k+d_k}(D_k\cap H)
\ge\frac1e\operatorname{vol}_{k+d_k}(D_k)
=\frac1e\operatorname{vol}_{k+d_k}(E_k).
\tag{43}
\]
Combining (40), (42), and (43), and then taking the infimum over all closed ambient halfspaces containing \(\mathbf p_k^\star\), proves (4). Dividing by \(2^{-k}W_k\) proves (5). Finally,
\(1499/1501>1/2\), so (4) is strictly larger than
\(c_0 2^{-k}W_k\) for every \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\). Hence the construction is a counterexample only to the fixed-center same-continuous-fiber reflection certificate, and it does not disprove the locked target.
