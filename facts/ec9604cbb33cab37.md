## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite complete sampled lattice base of a compact compatible family of positive $d$-fibers $K_z$, and let $\sigma$ be their summed surface measure with each regular point marked by its actual continuous outward unit normal. At an own-interior source $Y=(p,y)$, use the ordinary optimal denominator $P_y(x)=1+a_y\cdot(x-y)>0$ on $K_p$, its energy $E_y=\int_{K_p}P_y^{-d-1}\,dx$, and $h_y(n)=h_{K_p}(n)-n\cdot y$. Suppose for every unit $n$ there is a slope $a_n$ with
$
h_{K_p}(n)-h_{K_z}(n)-a_n\cdot(z-p)>0\qquad(z\in P\setminus\{p\}).
$
There is a jointly continuous, globally finite nonnegative field $\Phi_p(n,z,x)$, convex in the physical point for each $n$, equal to $h_y(n)P_y(x)^{-d}$ at the actual own marked boundary and zero on every other measured boundary. It gives an actual compatible inward velocity field, has $\int\Phi_p\,d\sigma=dE_y$, and for every prescribed probability menu $W_p$ of outward-smoothed through-source caps with nonnegative buffer has $\int\Phi_p W_p\,d\sigma\ge\gamma_d dE_y$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Zero width means the closed step; a pure-base cap through the source has own bit one.

The strict gap holds if $p$ is an exposed vertex of $\operatorname{conv}P$, or if the positive fibers are sampled from a strictly convex compact ambient body. It can also be created by adding balls of radii $t(R-\|z\|^2)>0$ to any finite compatible family, with $t>0$ and $R>\max_{z\in P}\|z\|^2$.

For $k$ sources satisfying the gap there is one field $\Phi$ of total integral $k$ such that $\int\Phi W_i\,d\sigma\ge\gamma_d$ for every individual prescribed profile. If a positive boundary law $A$ dominates $(\sum_i\omega_i W_i)\sigma$ for probabilities $\omega_i$ and satisfies $\int\Phi\,dA\le B_*\int\Phi\,d\sigma$ for every admissible field, then $B_*\ge\gamma_d/k$. For actual deepest groups in the positive-width uniform outward ramp with covariance norm, continuous concave nonnegative buffer, and an attaining nonvertical positive-coarea cap at every deepest mark, this gives $B_*\ge\gamma_d/2^n$. The required law domination is a separate hypothesis.

## Proof

If $P=\{p\}$, use the field below with no isolation term. Otherwise, continuity of the finitely many support functions makes the slope chosen at one normal still work with a positive gap on a neighborhood. A finite such cover of the normal sphere and a continuous partition of unity give a bounded continuous slope $a(n)$ and a common $\eta>0$ valid at every other site. The function
$
g_n(z,x)=h_{K_p}(n)-n\cdot x-a(n)\cdot(z-p)
$
is affine in the physical point, has value zero on the actual own face with normal $n$, and is at least $\eta$ everywhere on every other sampled fiber.

To extend the desired field globally, choose $0<l\le P_y\le u$ on $K_p$ and put
$
J(s)=\max\{0,\sup_{l\le t\le u}[t^{-d}-dt^{-d-1}(s-t)]\}.
$
It is finite continuous convex and equals $s^{-d}$ on $[l,u]$, by the tangent inequalities there. Hence $F(n,x)=h_y(n)J(P_y(x))$ is jointly continuous and convex in the physical point for fixed $n$. The field $[F(n,x)-Tg_n(z,x)]_+$ has the required exact traces for any $T$ greater than the maximum of $F/\eta$ on the other marked bundles (or the whole compact sphere times their fibers). In the singleton case take $F$. At a regular own boundary point use its actual outer normal $n$, so $g_n=0$; no equality at other pairs of normals and points is claimed. In particular 8820984cf6a6942e realizes this exact field as inward velocity on the actual fibers, since its global continuity and physical convexity are the required hypotheses.

If $p$ is exposed, choose $a_0$ such that $a_0\cdot(z-p)<0$ at every other base; a sufficiently large multiple of $a_0$ works uniformly over the sphere.
