## Statement

There are independent random variables $X,E,Y$ with $X$ a sum of $25$ independent unit-rate exponentials, $E$ a unit-rate exponential, and $Y\ge0$ with $\mathbb EY=24$, for which $\mathbb EX=\mathbb E(E+Y)=25$ yet $\Pr(X\ge E+Y)<4/9$. Thus the comparison $\Pr(X\ge E+Y)\ge4/9$ cannot follow from retaining a fixed negative unit exponential and allowing an arbitrary independent nonnegative residual law with only its mean specified.

## Proof

Take $Y=0$ with probability $2/5$ and $Y=40$ with probability $3/5$. Then $\mathbb EY=24$ and
$$
\Pr(X\ge E+Y)\le \frac25+\frac35\Pr(X\ge40).
$$
The gamma moment generating function gives, by Markov's inequality at $3/8$,
$$
\Pr(X\ge40)\le e^{-15}(8/5)^{25}<(3/8)^{15}(8/5)^{25}<2/27.
$$
For the first strict inequality, $e>8/3$ follows from the partial sum of its power series through $1/4!$. The second is exactly $3^{18}8^{10}=415989582513831936<596046447753906250=2\cdot5^{25}$. The probability is therefore strictly less than $2/5+(3/5)(2/27)=4/9$. This residual law has a positive atom at zero despite its positive mean. A sum of independent exponentials with nonnegative coefficients and positive mean is almost surely positive, since at least one coefficient is positive.
