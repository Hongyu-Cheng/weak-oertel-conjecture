## Statement

Let finitely many fixed positive compact polytopes $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ at sampled integer bases carry summed volume $\mu$ of mass $M$. Fix a count of closed affine caps with unit continuous normals. For feasible $0<\tau<1$ let $v(\tau)$ be the attained minimum of their total hinge over covers of the actual fibers with cap masses at most $\tau M$. Then $v$ is right-continuous at every feasible budget. If $[a,b]$ lies in the interior feasible domain, and $r_i(t)$ are the sensitivity-normalized cap-budget multipliers at regular budgets, then
$$
v(a)-v(b)=M\int_a^b\sum_i r_i(t)\,dt+\sum_{a<t\le b}[v(t-)-v(t)].
$$
Only finitely many terms of the sum are nonzero. The integrand is specified almost everywhere by $\sum_i r_i(t)=-v'(t)/M$, independently of the choice of stress.

## Proof

The fixed-actual-fiber theorem d83edebb7d5b8852 gives attainment, monotonicity, finitely many exceptional interior budgets and the stated identity for the sum of multipliers. For a feasible $\tau$ and $\tau_k\downarrow\tau$, choose attaining covers of hinge at most $v(\tau)$. Every fiber lies in some ball $RB_d$ and has positive volume. For a cap's affine threshold $t_{iz}$ on that fiber the bound on its hinge implies $t_{iz}\ge-R-v(\tau)/|K_z|$. Therefore the affine trace compactification 0a73ba4a9c7b24d1, applied successively to the finitely many indices, produces finite limiting caps with every threshold limit either finite or $+\infty$. For base rank zero the same result is obtained by embedding the singleton at $0\in\mathbb Z$. Each fixed point is covered by one recurring index and stays covered by the closed limiting trace. Cap masses converge and are at most $\tau M$ at the limit. On finite trace coordinates the hinge integrands converge uniformly; on the diverging ones they are eventually identically zero and the limiting trace is empty. Thus the limiting cover has hinge $\lim_k v(\tau_k)$. Feasibility gives $v(\tau)\le\lim_kv(\tau_k)$, and monotonicity gives the reverse inequality.

On each open interval between the finitely many exceptional points the value is $C^1$ and nonincreasing. Integrate $-v'$ on interior subintervals and use the one-sided limits of this monotone finite function. This gives a finite improper integral at the endpoints. Summing over the intervals and using right continuity gives the displayed identity, with the left-limit loss at a jump counted precisely when $a<t\le b$. The sensitivity identity then replaces $-v'$ almost everywhere, so individual multipliers require no measurable selection.
