## Statement

Use the thirteen integer bases $p_0,\ldots,p_{12}$ and weights $w_0,\ldots,w_{12}$ of 280a179b646688e0, in its displayed order. For every $d\ge1$ and every compatible family of full-dimensional convex fibers with these exact $d$-volumes, the actual centroid at $p_7=(1,0,1)$ has normalized mixed closed-halfspace depth at least $\Gamma_d/7+3/1300$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. The original mass is one, so this is also the raw bound.

## Proof

The table and census in 280a179b646688e0 give total mass one, seven occupied parities, and the actual anchor
$$
p_7=(1,0,1)=\frac{p_{12}+p_1+p_9}{3},
\qquad (p_{12},p_1,p_9)=((2,0,1),(0,-2,0),(1,2,2)).
$$
Here $v=w_7=58/455$ and $(b_1,b_2,b_3)=(w_{12},w_1,w_9)=(58/455,1179/14560,13/168)$. At the centroid of $K_{p_7}$ the full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d pays at least $\Gamma_d v$ from the anchor in every containing cap, including when its trace is the whole anchor.

Suppose one containing closed cap captures at most $1/100$ in total from $p_{12},p_1,p_9$. Then the three strict complements have positive volumes at least $b_j-1/100$. Their weighted Minkowski midpoint with weights $1/3$ lies in the strict complement at $p_7$ by compatibility and affinity. Brunn–Minkowski on compact inner exhaustions, followed by arithmetic-geometric mean, yields
$$
[(1-\Gamma_d)v]^{1/d}\ \ge\ \frac13\sum_{j=1}^3(b_j-1/100)^{1/d}
\ \ge\ \left[\prod_{j=1}^3(b_j-1/100)\right]^{1/(3d)} .
$$
No cut-status assumption is needed, since the positive lower volumes make the compact subcuts nonempty, even for a purely base normal. But direct rational multiplication gives
$$
\prod_{j=1}^3(b_j-1/100)-(7v/11)^3
=\frac{1348713982727}{48144144048000000}>0 .
$$
Also $\Gamma_d>4/11$ because $(1+1/d)^d<\sum_{j\ge0}1/j!\le8/3+5/96=87/32<11/4$, where the tail from $j=4$ is bounded by $(1/24)\sum_{r\ge0}5^{-r}=5/96$. Thus the preceding root inequality is impossible. Taking the infimum of the original cap masses leaves at least $\Gamma_d v+1/100$. Since $v=1/7-1/65$ and $\Gamma_d\le1/2$ by the first two binomial terms, this is at least $\Gamma_d/7+3/1300$.
