## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $d\ge1$, with positive mixed $d$-volume $M$. Let $D(Y)$ be its normalized depth over all closed ambient halfspaces. Let $Y_e=(z_e,x_e)$ be sampled points of positive depth, with $z_0<z_1$ integers. For every integer $p$ strictly between $z_0$ and $z_1$, let $t=(p-z_0)/(z_1-z_0)$. Then
$$
D((1-t)Y_0+tY_1)>\min\{D(Y_0),D(Y_1)\}.
$$
In particular the globally maximizing integer bases form a singleton or a pair of adjacent integers. Supporting sections of positive $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measure are allowed, including supporting endpoints when $d=1$.

## Proof

The actual fibers are finite at integer bases and compatible by convexity. Fact 55a57b1cf55d0c87 puts both endpoints in the interiors of their fibers and gives positive depth at any own-fiber interior mark. Fact 1ec728c26c11b368 gives a nonvertical attaining cap through the intermediate point, which belongs to the mixed set by convexity and integrality. Halfspace depth is quasiconcave because a halfspace containing a strict convex combination contains at least one endpoint. Suppose equality holds in this lower bound. Fact 06476fb81821acf7, applied to the raw depths $M D$, makes the same cap $H$ a minimizing cap through both endpoints. Thus it has proper cuts there. Normalize its continuous normal $u$ to norm one and write its margin as $L(z,X)=u\cdot X+a z-b$.

The positive integer fibers are consecutive, since Minkowski interpolation between two positive-volume fibers gives positive volume at intermediate bases. Truncate $C$ to the interval from the first to the last positive integer base. This leaves all masses and the endpoints unchanged, and every real fiber in the new projection has positive volume, by interpolation of the two extreme positive fibers. The boundary section of $H$ in this truncated body is convex. Its fiber at real $z$ is isometric to the slice in $u^\perp$ under the affine parametrization $(z,\xi)\mapsto(z,\xi+(b-a z)u)$. For integer $z$, let $s_z=\mathcal H^{d-1}(C_z\cap\{L(z,\cdot)=0\})$ in the truncated body. For $d\ge2$, Brunn–Minkowski in $u^\perp$ gives concavity of $s_z^{1/(d-1)}$ on the real interval with positive section measure. It follows by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality that the sampled positive weights are log-concave, with no gaps. For $d=1$ a nonempty section is one point, so the weights are one on a consecutive interval.

Every interior real base of the projection of this section has a proper cut. Indeed, $z\mapsto\min_{C_z}L(z,\cdot)$ is convex and $z\mapsto\max_{C_z}L(z,\cdot)$ concave. They have strict signs at $z_0$, whose section is proper. At any distinct interior base of the section projection, extend the ray from $z_0$ a little beyond the base inside this projection. At the farther point the minimum is nonpositive and the maximum nonnegative, so interpolation gives strict signs at the intermediate base. The properness at $z_0$ was already established. Only the extreme bases of the section projection can have supporting sections. Consequently only the first and last positive sampled weights may be supporting.

At $Y_e$ vary the tilt by adding $t(z-z_e)$ to $L$. Both one-sided cap-mass derivatives exist by the proper, full-side and empty-side slice calculation in the proof of fact b319e7dcda1486e9. Its positive-volume, compact convex fiber hypotheses hold after truncation, the cap minimizes at both own-interior marks, and the continuous normal has unit length. On a proper slice both derivatives contribute $(z-z_e)s_z$, on a supporting slice one contributes zero and the other $(z-z_e)s_z$, with the order depending on side and sign. A disjoint or zero-measure slice contributes zero. The cap mass has a minimum at $t=0$, so its left derivative is nonpositive and its right derivative is nonnegative. A convex combination is zero. Using those same combination coefficients for each slice supplies weights $s'_{z,e}$ equal to $s_z$ on proper slices and in $[0,s_z]$ on supporting slices, with $\sum_z(z-z_e)s'_{z,e}=0$. The total weight stays positive because the two endpoint slices are proper. Thus both $z_0$ and $z_1$ are means of the same log-concave positive sequence with only its two extreme weights trimmed. There are at least three positive sampled weights because the proper slices extend throughout the interval between $z_0$ and $z_1$, which contains $p$. The endpoint-trimming lemma bounds the span of these means by one. This contradicts $z_1-z_0\ge2$ and proves strict interpolation.

By facts 1ec728c26c11b368 and 55a57b1cf55d0c87, a global maximum exists and is positive. If two maximizing integer bases differ by at least two, apply the proved inequality to their marks at an intermediate integer base to contradict maximality. The conclusion about the set of bases follows.
