## Statement

Let $f,g$ be nonnegative concave upper semicontinuous functions on respective compact intervals $I,J$ of positive lengths, each with integral one and first moment zero. Form the symmetric planar section bodies $E_f=\{(s,y):s\in I,\ |y|\le f(s)/2\}$ and $E_g$ analogously. Fix $\alpha\in\mathbb R$ and put
$$
J_\alpha(f,g)
=\left|\left(\tfrac12(E_f+E_g)\right)\cap\{s\ge0\}\right|
 +\int_\alpha^\infty f(s)\,ds+\int_{-\alpha}^\infty g(s)\,ds,
$$
where bars denote ordinary planar area, and extend each profile by zero only for the tail integrals.

If the profiles are piecewise affine on prescribed finite knots including the support endpoints and the feasible height set is nonempty, then $J_\alpha$ attains a minimum over those heights. A minimizer can be chosen with at most $2+z\le6$ affine pieces in each profile, where $z$ is its total number of zero endpoint heights. If all four endpoint heights are prescribed, a minimizer can be chosen with at most six pieces in each profile whenever this prescribed problem is feasible.

For arbitrary profiles as above, there exist profiles with the same respective support intervals, masses and first moments, each with at most six nonvertical affine graph pieces, whose $J_\alpha$ is no larger. The resulting closed profile endpoint values may differ from those of the input.

## Proof

The bodies are compact, convex, symmetric transversely, of area one and centroid zero. They realize the actual functional: put $E_f$ and $E_g$ at base $-1$ and $1$ and take their lifted convex hull. Its only integer fibers are these bodies and their exact Minkowski average at base $0$. The cap $s+\alpha z\ge0$ has the three displayed contributions, with nonempty endpoint sections retained even when their length is zero.

For fixed knots, the nonnegative height vectors satisfying the concavity inequalities and the four mass and moment equations form a compact polytope. Every knot has a positive coefficient in its trapezoidal mass equation, so all heights are bounded. Partition into finitely many closed cells by the pairwise cross-profile slope comparisons. In each cell the order of upper roof edges of the Minkowski sum is fixed, merging in decreasing slope order. Indeed the upper sum height at any axial coordinate is the maximum of the sum of the two upper heights with those coordinates summing to the prescribed coordinate. Measured from the two left endpoints, the increase in height is obtained by allocating the axial displacement to roof segments in decreasing slope order. Choosing these segments in this order respects the prefix order within each profile and maximizes the increase by the exchange inequality for any larger unfilled slope. Parallel ties merely insert collinear vertices. Thus each upper vertex of the Minkowski average has an abscissa equal to the average of a fixed pair of knot abscissae and an ordinate affine in the heights. This also holds for the upper left endpoint, including positive endpoint heights. By symmetry and integration over fixed axial subintervals, the portion over $s\ge0$ has area affine in the heights. Cutting a segment at zero uses a fixed interpolation fraction. Each of the other two tails is linear in the heights because its knots and cut are fixed. Consequently $J_\alpha$ is affine on each closed cell and attains its global minimum at a vertex of some cell.

At that vertex merge each run of collinear segments. If the two resulting profiles have $m,r$ pieces, the perturbations satisfying the within-profile active slope equalities have dimension $m+r+2$, corresponding to their retained vertex heights. A nonnegative concave positive-mass profile is positive at each interior knot by convex interpolation between a positive-height point and the endpoints. Thus only the $z$ zero endpoint heights can contribute active nonnegativity equations. The two masses and two moments impose at most four equations. After merging, the slopes within each profile are strictly decreasing, so the distinct active cross-profile equalities match a slope from one profile to at most one slope from the other, contributing at most $\min(m,r)$ equations. If the resulting lower bound on the dimension of allowable perturbations, $m+r+2-z-4-\min(m,r)$, were positive, a sufficiently small nonzero perturbation in either direction would satisfy every active equation and preserve all the finitely many strict inequalities. This would contradict vertexhood. Therefore $\max(m,r)\le2+z\le6$. With prescribed endpoint heights, imposing all four endpoint equations gives $\max(m,r)\le6$ by the same count.

Now allow arbitrary profiles. Concavity gives interior continuity, and concavity against a fixed interior point bounds the one-sided lower limit at each support endpoint from below by its endpoint height. Upper semicontinuity supplies the reverse upper bound. Interpolating increasingly fine knots therefore gives uniform convergence on each original support interval. Divide each interpolated height function by its integral and translate its axial coordinate by minus its own mean. This restores unit mass and zero moment, keeps the support width fixed and translates it by an amount tending to zero. The normalized bodies converge in Hausdorff distance to the two originals, and the three halfplane areas in the functional converge at the same fixed $\alpha$. Indeed Minkowski addition is Hausdorff-continuous, and for bounded convex bodies under Hausdorff convergence, the indicators converge off the limiting boundary (by the support inequalities at interior points and distance at exterior points). Boundaries and the cutting lines have zero planar area.

Apply the fixed-knot result to each normalized pair, preserving its two support intervals. Its replacements have heights at most $2/w$ on an interval of width $w$, since integrating the two chords from a maximizing point to the endpoints gives at least $w/2$ times the maximal height. Each upper graph is thus described by at most seven ordered vertices in a common compact rectangle, and the section body is the convex hull of these vertices and their lower reflections. Pad by repetition and take a subsequence on which all vertices of both profiles converge. Their convex hulls with the reflected vertices converge in Hausdorff distance. The limit bodies retain the original support intervals and have area one and zero first moments by bounded convergence of the indicators (if a limit had empty interior, it would have area zero). Their upper graphs have at most six nonvertical edges. The lower reflected points cannot add upper vertices because each lies at or below its upper counterpart with the same axial coordinate. Coalescing axial coordinates may create vertical support faces at the endpoints; their lower points do not increase the number of nonvertical graph edges. The closed limit bodies therefore give admissible replacement profiles. Convergence of the three halfplane areas, together with the finite minimization inequalities, gives the desired inequality for $J_\alpha$.
