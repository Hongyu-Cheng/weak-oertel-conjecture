## Statement

For every \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
D_n:=\{0,1\}^n=[0,1]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\]
If \(T\subseteq[0,1]^n\) is a simplex whose vertices belong to \(D_n\),
then
\[
T\cap\mathbb Z^n=\operatorname{vert}(T),
\qquad
|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\le n+1.
\]
The simplex
\[
T_n:=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_n\}
\]
attains equality. Hence the largest fraction of \(D_n\) captured by one
such simplex is exactly
\[
\frac{n+1}{2^n},
\]
and this fraction tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\).

Moreover, let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a \(d\)-dimensional convex body with
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)=v>0\), and set
\[
C_n:=[0,1]^n\times K,
\qquad
S_n:=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
Then
\[
W:=\mathcal H_d(S_n)=2^n v,
\]
and every \(p\in S_n\) satisfies
\[
h_{S_n}(p)\le v=2^{-n}W.
\]
This upper bound is witnessed by a proper vertical halfspace whose
intersection with \(S_n\) is exactly the base fiber containing \(p\).

Finally, for the constant concave profile \(\rho\equiv1\) on
\([0,1]^n\),
\[
\max_{t>0}t^d\#\{z\in D_n:\rho(z)\ge t\}
=\sum_{z\in D_n}\rho(z)^d
=2^n,
\]
and the maximum on the left is attained at \(t=1\).

## Proof

### Exact lattice-point retention and attainment

Let \(V:=\operatorname{vert}(T)\). Since \(T\subseteq[0,1]^n\), every
integer point of \(T\) belongs to \(D_n\). Fix any \(x\in D_n\) and
consider the linear functional
\[
L_x(u):=\sum_{i=1}^n(2x_i-1)u_i.
\]
For every \(y\in D_n\), coordinatewise calculation gives
\[
L_x(x)-L_x(y)
=\sum_{i:x_i=1}(1-y_i)+\sum_{i:x_i=0}y_i
=d_H(x,y),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(d_H\) denotes Hamming distance. Thus \(x\) is the unique
maximizer of \(L_x\) over \(D_n\), and in particular over the whole cube
vertex set.

Suppose that \(x\in T\cap\mathbb Z^n\) but \(x\notin V\). Write \(x\)
as a convex combination of vertices of \(T\):
\[
x=\sum_{q\in V}\lambda_q q,
\qquad
\lambda_q\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{q\in V}\lambda_q=1.
\]
Every \(q\in V\) differs from \(x\), so (1) gives
\(L_x(q)<L_x(x)\). Applying \(L_x\) to the convex combination yields
\[
L_x(x)=\sum_{q\in V}\lambda_qL_x(q)<L_x(x),
\]
a contradiction. Therefore every integer point of \(T\) is one of its
vertices. The reverse inclusion holds because \(V\subseteq D_n\), so
\[
T\cap\mathbb Z^n=V.
\tag{2}
\]
A simplex in \(\mathbb R^n\) has at most \(n+1\) vertices, which proves
the upper bound.

The points \(0,e_1,\ldots,e_n\) are affinely independent and belong to
\(D_n\). Their convex hull \(T_n\) lies in \([0,1]^n\), and (2) gives
\[
T_n\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_n\}.
\]
Thus \(T_n\) contains exactly \(n+1\) points of \(D_n\), proving
attainment and the exact fraction \((n+1)/2^n\). If
\(a_n:=(n+1)/2^n\), then for every \(n\ge2\),
\[
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{n+2}{2n+2}\le\frac23.
\]
Hence \(a_n\le a_2(2/3)^{n-2}\) for \(n\ge2\), so \(a_n\to0\).

### Proper vertical isolation and exact mixed mass

Because \([0,1]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_n\), the mixed set is the disjoint
union
\[
S_n=\bigcup_{y\in D_n}(\{y\}\times K).
\]
Each fiber is isometric to \(K\), and the \(2^n\) fibers are pairwise
disjoint. Finite additivity of \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure gives
\[
W=\mathcal H_d(S_n)
=\sum_{y\in D_n}\mathcal H_d(\{y\}\times K)
=2^n v.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix \(p=(z,u)\in S_n\), so \(z\in D_n\) and \(u\in K\). Define the
affine functional on the base by
\[
\Phi_z(x):=\sum_{i=1}^n(2z_i-1)(x_i-z_i).
\]
For every cube vertex \(y\in D_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_z(y)
&=\sum_{i:z_i=1}(y_i-1)-\sum_{i:z_i=0}y_i\\
&=-\#\{i:y_i\ne z_i\}
=-d_H(y,z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(\Phi_z(z)=0\), while \(\Phi_z(y)<0\) for every other
cube vertex \(y\ne z\).

Now set
\[
H_z:=\{(x,w)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:\Phi_z(x)\ge0\}.
\]
The normal vector of this halfspace is
\((2z_1-1,\ldots,2z_n-1,0,\ldots,0)\), which is nonzero because
\(n>0\). Thus \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace. It is vertical
because its defining inequality is independent of \(w\), and its
boundary contains \(p\) because \(\Phi_z(z)=0\). Equation (4) gives
\[
H_z\cap S_n=\{z\}\times K.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathcal H_d(H_z\cap S_n)=v=2^{-n}W
\]
by (3). Since mixed halfspace depth is the infimum of this intersection
mass over halfspaces containing \(p\), (5) proves
\[
h_{S_n}(p)\le v=2^{-n}W.
\]
The construction applies to every \(p\in S_n\).

### Lossless constant-profile level selection

For \(\rho\equiv1\) and \(t>0\), one has exactly
\[
\{z\in D_n:\rho(z)\ge t\}
=
\begin{cases}
D_n,&0<t\le1,\\
\varnothing,&t>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\[
t^d\#\{z\in D_n:\rho(z)\ge t\}
=
\begin{cases}
2^nt^d,&0<t\le1,\\
0,&t>1.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(d>0\), the first branch in (6) is at most \(2^n\), with
equality at the included endpoint \(t=1\). On the other hand,
\[
\sum_{z\in D_n}\rho(z)^d
=\sum_{z\in D_n}1
=2^n.
\]
This proves the exact identity and its attainment.

Because \((n+1)/2^n\to0\), no dimension-free positive constant can
lower-bound the fraction of every cube level set captured by one lattice
simplex with vertices in that level set. This is the only no-go
conclusion. It does not rule out a coupled level-packet argument, a
target-scale simplex method, or weak Oertel. Indeed, a one-vertex
simplex already retains
\[
1=2^{-n}|D_n|,
\]
so the cube obstruction is compatible with the target \(2^{-n}\) scale.
The product calculation likewise gives the upper calibration
\(2^{-n}W\), not a failure of any lower bound by
\(c\,2^{-n}W\) with \(0<c<1/e\). This packet makes no final-target
claim.
