## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a finite nonempty set in convex position. For each $z\in P$ let $K_z$ be an interval of length $w_z>0$, and use the summed fiber length $\mu$ on $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}\{z\}\times K_z$. Put $M=\sum_z w_z$ and $W=\max_z w_z$. Give a nonvertical cap with affine margin $L(z,x)=a\cdot z+ux-b$, $u\ne0$, the hinge $\int_S L_+\,d\mu/(W|u|)$, the support normalization for $D=[-W,W]$.

A finite closed nonvertical cap cover with mass at most $\tau M$ per cap exists exactly when $\tau M\ge W/2$. In that case the minimum summed hinge over all finite covers of this budget is $\sum_z w_z^2/(4W)$, attained by two isolated midpoint caps per fiber.

## Proof

A cap containing the midpoint of an interval of length $W$ contains at least half of it, proving the necessary budget. To build a cover, at each base $z$ let $s_z$ be the midpoint of $K_z$ and choose an affine $q_z$ with $q_z(z)=0$ and strictly positive values at all other bases, possible by vertex exposure. Extend each margin $\pm(x-s_z)$ by subtracting a sufficiently large multiple of $q_z$ to miss all other compact fibers strictly. The resulting $2|P|$ caps cover and each has mass $w_z/2\le W/2$. The pair's unnormalized hinge is $\int_{K_z}|x-s_z|\,dx=w_z^2/4$.

For the lower bound rescale each continuous normal to sign $+1$ or $-1$, which divides its original hinge numerator by $|u|$. On $[l,r]=K_z$ keep the least threshold $t$ of positive-sign caps $x\ge t$ and the greatest threshold $s$ of negative-sign caps $x\le s$, omitting an orientation if absent. Their union is the original coverage and discarded integrals are nonnegative. If a retained cap contains the whole interval, its unnormalized integral is at least $(r-l)^2/2$. This includes a cover with just one orientation. Otherwise both orientations are present and coverage forces $l<t\le s<r$: if either retained halfline were empty on the interval or there were a gap between them, the entire interval could not be covered. Their summed integral is
$$
\frac{(r-t)^2+(s-l)^2}{2}
\ \ge\ \frac{(r-t)^2+(t-l)^2}{2}
\ \ge\ \frac{(r-l)^2}{4}.
$$
Divide the bound by $W$ and sum over the actual fibers. The isolated midpoint pairs attain equality, proving the stated minimum for arbitrary finite cap count.
