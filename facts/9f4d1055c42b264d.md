## Statement

On $\Delta_3=\{u\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4:\sum_j u_j=1\}$ let $U$ be uniform, $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$, and $\phi_3(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$. For $x=(270,26,636,819)/900$ the unique minimizing threshold is the proper threshold $t=331/900$. It strictly captures vertices $1,2$ and the actual section centroid is
$$
b=(1171/3294,1561/4758,98/549,2953/21411).
$$
Only $b_1>1/3$. The Hessian of $\phi_3$ at $x$ is negative semidefinite with kernel $\operatorname{span}\{x\}$.

## Proof

For positive $p,q,r,s$ with $p\ne q$, the normalized volume of the cap with raw margins $(p,q,-r,-s)$ is, by 1462fa9ce6ced54e,
$$
P(p,q,-r,-s)=\frac{p^3/[(p+r)(p+s)]-q^3/[(q+r)(q+s)]}{p-q}.
$$
The same cap probability is obtained with independent unit exponentials in place of $U$, by normalizing their sum. At $C=(1,5,-5,-8)$ let $g=\nabla P(C)$ and $H=\nabla^2P(C)$, with derivatives in the four signed margins. Differentiating the displayed rational function (changing the sign of each $r$ or $s$ derivative) gives
$$
P(C)=\frac{331}{1404},\qquad \frac{\mathbf 1^\top g}{3}=\frac{61}{1404},\qquad
\frac{g}{\mathbf 1^\top g}=(1171/3294,1561/4758,98/549,2953/21411).
$$
The last vector is the section centroid by the coarea derivative in 1462fa9ce6ced54e. Its first coordinate exceeds $1/3$ by $73/3294$; the second is smaller by $25/4758$, and the last two are smaller as well.

At $t=331/900$ the actual margin is $(61/900)C$. It has two positive and two negative entries and $0<\min_j x_j<t<\max_j x_j<1$. Degree-zero homogeneity gives $F_x(t)=P(C)$ and $F_x'(t)=(900/61)\mathbf1^\top g$, so $tF_x'(t)=3F_x(t)$. As in d2ba4e3cbd6eb730, $F_x^{1/3}$ is concave between the extreme heights. Its tangent line at $t$ is $\tau\mapsto F_x(t)^{1/3}\tau/t$, so $t$ minimizes $\tau/F_x(\tau)^{1/3}$ there. The line remains above the CDF cube root for $\tau\ge\max_j x_j$ since the latter is then one. The same fact gives uniqueness.

The tangent vectors $v=(g_2,-g_1,0,0)$ and $w=(0,0,g_4,-g_3)$ belong to $g^\perp$. Differentiating the same rational expression yields the matrix of $H$ on $(v,w)$:
$$
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
298246999/303084016455168 &114611287/454626024682752\\
114611287/454626024682752 &1004887915/681939037024128
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det Q=\frac{23880754822693393}{17223735193236852561853857792}>0.
$$
Thus $Q$ is positive definite. The three vectors $v,w,C$ span $g^\perp$: $C$ belongs to it by homogeneity, whereas the $g$-weighted sum of its first two entries is positive, unlike any combination of $v,w$. Homogeneity also gives $HC=-g$, so $C$ is a bilinear kernel vector of the restriction to $g^\perp$. Consequently that restriction is positive semidefinite with exactly the line $\operatorname{span}\{C\}$ as kernel. The same holds for the threshold-one margins $\widehat C=(61/331)C$ by positive rescaling.

Use 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9 for the normalized positive row $x/t$. Decompose a direction as $v_0+s_0\mathbf 1$, where $v_0$ is tangent to the gradient of $P$ at $\widehat C$. The optimized Hessian is a strictly negative constant times the sum of the tangent cap form on $v_0$ and the nonnegative Schur square. The first term vanishes exactly when $v_0=a\widehat C$. Since $\widehat H\widehat C=-\widehat g$ and $\mathbf1^\top\widehat g=3P$, the Schur square then vanishes exactly at $s_0=-a$. Thus the only kernel direction is the line of $\widehat C-\mathbf1=-x/t$. Degree-one homogeneity of capacity rescales the Hessian by a positive factor at $x$, preserving this conclusion.
