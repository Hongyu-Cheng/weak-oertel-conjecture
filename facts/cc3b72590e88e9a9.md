## Statement

Keep the locked mixed set \(S\), arithmetic dimension \(N\), and all locked notation unchanged. The core spatial rank is \(k\); an auxiliary minimizing rate is \(s_\star(U)\), and \(\sigma^b\) is the conditional imbalance second moment, exactly as in accepted 30375b78b4633f77, 35319f07df377c73, and b90bdf64e7944ce3. No scalar rate denotes a spatial rank.

For every integer \(\ell\ge2400\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_0&=\ell^2,&m_1&=\left\lceil e^{2\ell/3}\ell^2\right\rceil,&
m_2&=\left\lceil e^{4\ell/3}\ell^2\right\rceil,\\
k&=m_0+m_1+m_2,&M&=2^{20k}k^2,&H&=\lfloor6M/5\rfloor.
\end{aligned}
$$
There are \(m_0,m_1,m_2\) core coordinates with second costs \(10M,\ell M,2\ell M\), respectively. Every core coordinate has \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,2B_i-1)\) on \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\), with unit original site multiplicities. Set \(s_{\rm bad}=\log(1+1/M)\). There is a unique positive \(\beta\) solving \(M(e^\beta-1)=1-e^{-H\beta}\); write \(r=e^{-\beta}\).

Here are all scalar definitions used in the tests. For \(\lambda\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{i,\lambda}&=\lbrace h\in\mathbb Z:\lambda+h,\lambda-h\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\rbrace,\\
e_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]-g_i(\lambda),\\
b_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)-g_i(\lambda-h)],\\
K_{s,i}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}}
 e^{-(2s/3)[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]},\\
H_{s,i}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}}e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)},\\
P_i(s)&=\sum_{\lambda=0}^3K_{s,i}(\lambda)^3,&
Z_i(s)&=\sum_{x=0}^3e^{-s g_i(x)},&
\delta_i(s)&=\log\frac{8P_i(s)}{Z_i(s)^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(J_i\) have probabilities \(K_{s,i}(\lambda)^3/P_i(s)\), and, conditionally on \(J_i=\lambda\), let \(V_i\) have probabilities \(e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)}/H_{s,i}(\lambda)\). Thus \(\sigma^b_{s,i}(\lambda)=\mathbb E[b_{i,\lambda}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=\lambda]\). Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_i(s)&=\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)+e_{i,J_i}(V_i)],\\
R_i(s)&=s^2\left(1+\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)^2+e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2]\right),\\
\mu(s)&=\sum_i\mu_i(s),\qquad V_s=\sum_iR_i(s),\\
L(s)&=40\sqrt{\sum_i\left(1+\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)^2+e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2]\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The finite-sum definition specifies \(L(0)\); for \(s>0\), \(L(s)=40\sqrt{V_s}/s\). These are exactly the full-prefix scalar quantities of accepted 30375b78b4633f77, 35319f07df377c73, and b90bdf64e7944ce3, with their \(C_{4,i}=2B_i-1\), no change of moment normalization, and their cap replaced by the particular core cap \(U\) below.

For every integer \(U\ge0\), define the complete original core law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)&=\sum_i g_i(x_i),\\
A_{{\rm core},U}&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t'):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t'\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)\le t'\le U\rbrace,\\
Z_{\rm core}(U)&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},U}}r^{t'},\qquad
p_{\rm core}^{(U)}(\mathbf x,t')=r^{t'}/Z_{\rm core}(U).
\end{aligned}
$$
Set \(Z_{\rm core}(U)=0\) for negative integers \(U\). Write \(f_t^{(U)}=\sum_{v=t}^U r^v\) for \(0\le t\le U\), let \(s_{\max}=12\log2\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_U(s)&=\max_{0\le t\le U,\ t\in\mathbb Z}
 [\log(f_t^{(U)}/f_0^{(U)})+st],\\
\mathcal J_U(s)&=\mathop{\rm argmax}_{0\le t\le U,\ t\in\mathbb Z}
 [\log(f_t^{(U)}/f_0^{(U)})+st],\\
\Psi_U(s)&=\mathcal F_U(s)+\tfrac14\sum_i\log P_i(s)-\int_0^sL(y)\,dy
 \qquad(0\le s\le s_{\max}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Every \(\Psi_U\) has a unique minimizing rate \(s_\star(U)\in(0,s_{\max}]\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
t_0&=\lfloor\mu(s_{\rm bad})+L(s_{\rm bad})\rfloor,&
U_{\rm bad}&=t_0+H,&
I_\ell&=\lbrace U_{\rm bad},\ldots,U_{\rm bad}+M\rbrace,\\
s_g&=\log(1+19/(20M)),&
T&=\lceil\mu(s_g)+L(s_g)\rceil+20M+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(I_\ell\subseteq\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace\), the full-cap core passes the surplus test strictly,
$$
0<s_\star(T)<s_{\max},\qquad
\sum_i\delta_i(s_\star(T))>256\sqrt{V_{s_\star(T)}}+4s_\star(T),
\tag{1}
$$
and every \(U\in I_\ell\) satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
0<(1-1/(10\ell))s_{\rm bad}&<s_\star(U)\le s_{\rm bad}<s_{\max},\\
\sum_i\delta_i(s_\star(U))&<256\sqrt{V_{s_\star(U)}}+4s_\star(U),\\
\min\mathcal J_U(s_\star(U))&>U/2,\\
\beta&<3s_{\rm bad}/8<3s_\star(U)/4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

To specify the original residual marginal, put \(\pi=r/(1+r)\), \(\bar U=U_{\rm bad}+M/2\), and \(a_\ell=\lfloor(T-\bar U)/\pi\rfloor\). Append \(a_\ell\) binary coordinates of cost \((0,1)\). On the complete original support
$$
A=\lbrace(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ \mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{a_\ell},\ t\in\mathbb Z,
\ \Gamma_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)+|\mathbf y|\le t\le T\rbrace,
$$
let \(p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)=r^t/Z\), where \(Z=\sum_A r^t\), and set \(U=T-|\mathbf Y|\). Then
$$
\mathbb P_p(U\notin I_\ell)
\le\frac{410\ell}{2^{18k}k}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3}
$$
In particular, let \(\mathcal G\subseteq\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace\) consist exactly of cutoffs passing at least one of these three predicates:

1. \(\sum_i\delta_i(s_\star(U))\ge256\sqrt{V_{s_\star(U)}}+4s_\star(U)\).
2. \(s_\star(U)<s_{\max}\) and some \(t\in\mathcal J_U(s_\star(U))\) has \(t\le U/2\).
3. \(\beta\ge3s_\star(U)/4\).

These are precisely the surplus predicate used in accepted 35319f07df377c73, the low-height hypothesis of accepted 30375b78b4633f77, and the tilt hypothesis of accepted 35319f07df377c73. The union here includes no other sufficient conditions or accepted facts. We have \(T\in\mathcal G\), \(I_\ell\cap\mathcal G=\varnothing\), and \(\mathbb P_p(U\in\mathcal G)\to0\). The original marginal is the one appearing in accepted 8668ed0d436b9cba, with the explicit map given in the proof.

## Proof

All ranks and costs are finite integers, with \(k\ge\ell^2\), \(M\ge100\), \(M\ge10\ell\), and \(2k/M^2<1\). The costs are sorted and at least two. Interpolating a core cost has successive slopes \(1,B_i-1,B_i-1\), so it is convex. Binary costs are linear. Consequently the core and appended supports are complete lattice supports of compact convex epigraphs over their respective boxes, with unit multiplicities and the stated original log-affine weights. Their zero atoms ensure positive normalizers. No change to a mixed set or arithmetic dimension is being made.

First verify the low original tilt. Let \(G(x)=M(e^x-1)+e^{-Hx}-1\). It has \(G(0)=0\), \(G'(0)=M-H<0\), and \(G''(x)>0\). Since \(H/M\ge6/5-1/M\), at \(x=1/(4M)\) we have
$$
G(x)\le\frac{100}{399}
 +1-\frac{119}{400}+\frac12\left(\frac{119}{400}\right)^2-1<0.
$$
Here \(M(e^x-1)\le Mx/(1-x)\), \(H/(4M)\ge119/400\), and \(e^{-z}\le1-z+z^2/2\) for \(z\ge0\) give the bound. At \(x=1/(3M)\), the inequalities \(M(e^x-1)>1/3\), \(H/(3M)\le2/5\), and
\(e^{-2/5}\ge1-2/5+(2/5)^2/2-(2/5)^3/6>2/3\)
give \(G(x)>0\). Strict convexity therefore gives a unique positive zero and
$$
\frac1{4M}<\beta<\frac1{3M},\qquad
1-\frac1{2M}\le Ms_{\rm bad}<1,\qquad
\beta<\frac38s_{\rm bad}.
\tag{4}
$$
The logarithm bounds in (4) follow by integrating \(1/(1+x)\) or by its alternating quadratic bound. For the last inequality, \(Ms_{\rm bad}\ge199/200>8/9\).

We use accepted b90bdf64e7944ce3 with precisely the full-prefix scalar objects in the statement, core cap \(U\), tilt \(\beta\), and fourth costs \(2B_i-1\). It proves that \(\mu,L\) are strictly decreasing on finite nonnegative rates, that every \(\Psi_U\) is strictly convex, and that its unique minimum belongs to \((0,s_{\max}]\). It also gives, at each interior rate, the one-sided derivative identities
$$
\Psi'_{U,-}(s)=\min\mathcal J_U(s)-\mu(s)-L(s),\qquad
\Psi'_{U,+}(s)=\max\mathcal J_U(s)-\mu(s)-L(s).
\tag{5}
$$
Thus all minimizing rates below are global, with no assumption of an ensemble equivalence. The individual scalar estimates (6) in accepted 30375b78b4633f77, with identical scalar kernels and moments, also give
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\delta_i(s)\ge0,\qquad R_i(s)<256\quad(0<s\le s_{\max}),\\
&\delta_i(s)\ge(s^2+e^{-2sB_i/3})/1024,\qquad
R_i(s)\le2s^2+128(sB_i)^2e^{-2sB_i/3}
\quad(0<s\le1,\ sB_i\ge24).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
These are individual-coordinate assertions, so their use imposes no cost-group restriction.

Here are finite-sum estimates sufficient for the failed interval. Put \(s_-=(1-1/(10\ell))s_{\rm bad}\). For \(s\in[s_-,s_{\rm bad}]\), we have \(sM>3/4\), \(s<1/M\), and \(1-Ms\le1/(10\ell)+1/(2M)\). If \(a=1,2\) and the coordinate has \(B=a\ell M\), put \(q_a=e^{-2a\ell Ms/3}\). The defining ceilings give
$$
\ell^2\le m_aq_a
\le\ell^2\exp\left(\frac a{15}+\frac{a\ell}{3M}\right)+1
\le\ell^2e^{1/5}+1<\frac32\ell^2.
\tag{7}
$$
The last inequality uses \(e^{1/5}<5/4\) and \(\ell\ge2400\). In particular \(q_a<1/100\).

For an individual minimal-fourth coordinate of cost \(B\), write \(x=e^{-4s/3}\), \(v=sB\), \(q=e^{-2v/3}\). Directly from the definitions,
$$
K_{s,i}(1)=x+2q,\quad K_{s,i}(2)=3e^{-4sB/3},\quad
P_i(s)=1+(x+2q)^3+27q^6+e^{4s}q^{12}.
\tag{8}
$$
All tuple energies are nonnegative, so \(P_i(s)\le P_i(0)=56\); since \(Z_i(s)\ge1\), every cheap coordinate has \(\delta_i(s)\le\log448<7\). For each expensive coordinate in this interval, \(x^2>3/4\), \(P_i<4\), and comparison with \(P_{\rm bin}=1+e^{-4s}\) gives
$$
P_i-P_{\rm bin}
=6x^2q+12xq^2+8q^3+27q^6+e^{4s}q^{12}\le8q.
$$
For example \(x\le1\), \(q<1/100\), and \(e^{4s}<2\) make the coefficient after division by \(q\) at most
\(6+12/100+8/10000+27/100^5+2/100^{11}<8\).
The binary surplus satisfies
\(\delta_{\rm bin}(s)=\log\cosh(2s)-4\log\cosh(s/2)\le2s^2\), because \(\log\cosh z\le z^2/2\). Since \(Z_i(s)\ge1+e^{-s}\) and \(\log(1+z)\le z\), we obtain \(\delta_i(s)\le2s^2+8q\). Summing this, (7), and the cheap bound gives
$$
\sum_i\delta_i(s)<7\ell^2+2k/M^2+24\ell^2<32\ell^2.
\tag{9}
$$

The joint root/displacement probability from the very same finite scalar law is
\(\Pr(J_i=1,|V_i|=1)=2q(x+2q)^2/P_i>3q/32\).
Each of these displacements has imbalance square \(B^2/4\). Hence \(R_i(s)>3v^2q/128\). For the two expensive groups, (7) and \(Ms>3/4\) yield
$$
V_s>\frac3{128}\frac9{16}(1+4)\ell^4
=\frac{135}{2048}\ell^4>\frac{\ell^4}{16},\qquad
256\sqrt{V_s}>64\ell^2.
\tag{10}
$$
This proves strict surplus failure throughout the rate interval. It also implies
\(L(s_{\rm bad})>10M\ell^2\), and therefore
$$
t_0>10M\ell^2-1>H+M+2.
\tag{11}
$$

To prove the lower rate bound as the cap varies, we need a quantitative derivative. Expand \(K_{s,i}(\lambda)^3\) into ordered triples of displacements; the tuple energy is
\(\Xi=4g_i(\lambda)+(4/3)\sum_{j=1}^3e_{i,\lambda}(h_j)\).
Their probabilities are \(e^{-s\Xi}/P_i(s)\). Their mean energy is \(4\mu_i(s)\), so differentiation gives \(-\mu_i'(s)=\operatorname{Var}(\Xi)/4\). The root-zero tuple has probability \(1/P_i>1/4\). Root-one tuples with exactly one nonzero slot have total probability \(6x^2q/P_i>9q/8\) and energy \(4+(2/3)(B-2)\ge2B/3\). The variance pair formula between these two groups gives \(-\mu_i'(s)>B^2q/32\). Applying just the \(m_1\) group and (7), and integrating over \([s_-,s_{\rm bad}]\), proves
$$
\mu(s_-)-\mu(s_{\rm bad})
>\frac{\ell^4M^2}{32}\frac{s_{\rm bad}}{10\ell}
\ge\frac{3M\ell^3}{1280}>2M.
\tag{12}
$$
Since \(L\) decreases, (12) also holds with \(\mu\) replaced by \(\mu+L\).

For the geometric height sequence, the defining equation of \(\beta\) gives, whenever \(U-t=H\),
$$
\frac{f_t^{(U)}}{f_{t+1}^{(U)}}
=1+\frac{e^\beta-1}{1-r^H}=1+\frac1M=e^{s_{\rm bad}}.
\tag{13}
$$
These ratios strictly increase with \(t\). Thus, if \(U=U_{\rm bad}+v_0\) with integer \(0\le v_0\le M\), the exact active set at \(s_{\rm bad}\) is \(\lbrace t_0+v_0,t_0+v_0+1\rbrace\). All its heights are allowed, since \(t_0\ge0\) and \(H\ge1\). At the smaller rate \(s_-\), monotonicity of maximizing slopes of a maximum of affine functions gives \(\max\mathcal J_U(s_-)\le t_0+v_0\). Equations (5) and (12) therefore give \(\Psi'_{U,+}(s_-)<0\). For \(v_0\ge1\), the left derivative at \(s_{\rm bad}\) is nonnegative. For \(v_0=0\), (5) and the definition of \(t_0\) bracket zero between its two derivatives. Strict convexity gives
$$
s_-<s_\star(U)\le s_{\rm bad}<s_{\max}.
\tag{14}
$$
Together with (9)-(10), this proves the surplus failure in (2).

Strict increase of the height ratios also says that at any rate the active set is one integer or two consecutive integers. At the interior minimizing rate, (5) brackets \(\mu+L\) between the smallest and largest active heights. Consequently
\(\min\mathcal J_U(s_\star(U))\ge\mu(s_\star(U))+L(s_\star(U))-1\ge t_0-1\).
By (11) and \(U\le t_0+H+M\), this is greater than \(U/2\). Finally \(s_\star(U)>s_->s_{\rm bad}/2\), which, together with (4), gives \(\beta<3s_{\rm bad}/8<3s_\star(U)/4\). This proves all of (2), including failure of the other two named tests at every cutoff in \(I_\ell\).

We next verify success at the stated larger full cap. On the entire interval \(\beta\le s\le s_g\), each expensive coordinate has \(24<sB_i\le2\ell\). Put \(Q=m_1e^{-2s\ell M/3}+m_2e^{-4s\ell M/3}\), which is an auxiliary activity sum, not an original probability. Since \(s_gM<19/20\), we have \(Q\ge\ell^2e^{\ell/30}\). From (6),
$$
\sum_i\delta_i(s)\ge Q/1024,\qquad
V_s<256\ell^2+2k/M^2+512\ell^2Q<513\ell^2Q.
\tag{15}
$$
For the last strict inequality, \(Q\ge\ell^2e^{\ell/30}>257\) and \(2k/M^2<1\) suffice. Now \(\sqrt{513}<23\) and
\(\sqrt Q\ge\ell e^{\ell/60}\ge\ell e^{40}>2^{40}\ell\).
Thus \(Q/1024>8192\ell\sqrt Q>5888\ell\sqrt Q+4s\), while (15) gives \(256\sqrt{V_s}<5888\ell\sqrt Q\). The full surplus strictly succeeds at every rate in \([\beta,s_g]\).

It remains to put the own full-cap saddle in that interval. At \(s=\beta\), the sequence \(f_t^{(T)}e^{\beta t}=(1-r^{T-t+1})/(1-r)\) strictly decreases with \(t\), so \(\mathcal J_T(\beta)=\lbrace0\rbrace\) and \(\Psi'_{T,+}(\beta)<0\). At height \(t=T-20M\), (4) gives
$$
\frac{f_t^{(T)}}{f_{t+1}^{(T)}}
=1+\frac{e^\beta-1}{1-r^{20M}}
<1+\frac{34}{99M}<1+\frac{19}{20M}=e^{s_g}.
$$
Indeed \(e^\beta-1<1/(3M-1)<34/(100M)\), and \(r^{20M}<e^{-5}<1/100\). Therefore every active height at \(s_g\) is at least \(T-20M+1\), which exceeds \(\mu(s_g)+L(s_g)\) by the definition of \(T\). Equation (5) gives \(\Psi'_{T,-}(s_g)>0\), and strict convexity proves \(\beta<s_\star(T)<s_g\). This establishes (1).

For use in the original marginal calculation, we record
$$
T>U_{\rm bad}+18M,\qquad T\le6\ell kM.
\tag{16}
$$
The first inequality follows from \(s_g<s_{\rm bad}\) and strict decrease of \(\mu+L\), since the added \(20M+1\) exceeds \(H+18M\). For the second, \(\mu(s_g)\le4\ell Mk\), because \(g_i(J_i)+e_{i,J_i}(V_i)\) is an average of two endpoint energies, each less than \(4\ell M\). Also \(s_g>9/(10M)\), and (6) gives \(V_{s_g}<256k\), hence \(L(s_g)<800M\sqrt k\le\ell kM\). The ceiling and \(20M+1\) cost at most \(20M+2\le\ell kM\), proving (16). In particular \(I_\ell\) lies below \(T\) and \(a_\ell\) is a positive integer.

Finally verify the original marginal with its exact core truncation. Since \(\pi>1/3\), we have \(a_\ell\le3T\le18\ell kM\). Let \(\mathbb P_{\rm prod}\) be the auxiliary law of independent Bernoulli \(\pi\) tail coordinates. Under this law,
\(\mathbb E_{\rm prod}U\in[\bar U,\bar U+1)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_{\rm prod}(U)\le a_\ell/4\). Its mean has distance at least \(M/2-1\ge M/3\) from either endpoint of the real interval \([U_{\rm bad},U_{\rm bad}+M]\). Hence Chebyshev gives
$$
\epsilon_\ell:=\mathbb P_{\rm prod}(U\notin I_\ell)
\le\frac{9a_\ell}{4M^2}\le\frac{41\ell k}{M}<\frac12.
\tag{17}
$$

Put \(f_0(\infty)=1/(1-r)\). At every integer \(U\), including negative ones, \(Z_{\rm core}(U)\le4^k f_0(\infty)\), since each of the \(4^k\) spatial sites contributes at most a full height sum. On \(I_\ell\) the zero spatial site gives the lower bound
$$
Z_{\rm core}(U)\ge f_0^{(U)}
=f_0(\infty)(1-r^{U+1})>\frac15f_0(\infty).
\tag{18}
$$
Here \(U\ge H\ge M\), \(\beta>1/(4M)\), and \(e^{1/4}>5/4\) give \(r^{U+1}<e^{-1/4}<4/5\). This lower bound uses just one original spatial site. It assumes neither a quarter of an infinite core normalizer nor any local comparison of nearby core normalizers.

For a fixed binary vector \(\mathbf y\), the exact change of variable \(t'=t-|\mathbf y|\) shows that its original unnormalized mass is \(r^{|\mathbf y|}Z_{\rm core}(T-|\mathbf y|)\). Since \(r^{|\mathbf y|}/(1+r)^{a_\ell}\) is its Bernoulli product probability, the original marginal is exactly that product law reweighted by \(Z_{\rm core}(U)\). Thus (17)-(18) yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_p(U\notin I_\ell)
&=\frac{\mathbb E_{\rm prod}[Z_{\rm core}(U)\mathbf1_{U\notin I_\ell}]}
 {\mathbb E_{\rm prod}Z_{\rm core}(U)}\\
&\le\frac{5\,4^k\epsilon_\ell}{1-\epsilon_\ell}
\le\frac{410\,4^k\ell k}{M}
=\frac{410\ell}{2^{18k}k}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
The limit follows already from \(k\ge\ell^2\). Formula (19) includes infeasible negative residual cutoffs through \(Z_{\rm core}(U)=0\); no product-law probability is substituted for an original-law probability.

For completeness, the original marginal in (19) is also exactly the marginal (3) in accepted 8668ed0d436b9cba: map its core rank to \(k\), tail count to \(a_\ell\), \(D_{\rm core}\) to \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k\), \(h_{\rm core}\) to \(\Gamma_{\rm core}\), and \(q_{\mathbf x}\) to one, retaining its \(T,\beta,Z_{\rm core}(U),Z,p\). Realize each binary tail as its three-site potential \((0,1,T+1)\); every third site is infeasible, so both complete supports and original normalizers are identical. The predecessor's symbol \(s\) for the tail count is thereby mapped to \(a_\ell\), never to the present auxiliary rate. Its weighted fourth-root bound remains valid; the present assertion concerns only the particular cutoff set \(\mathcal G\).

Equation (1) puts \(T\) in \(\mathcal G\). Equation (2) makes \(I_\ell\) disjoint from each of its three defining predicates. Equation (19) then proves \(\mathbb P_p(U\in\mathcal G)\to0\). No failure of an actual moment bound follows from failure of these sufficient tests. This completes the claimed counterexample to the stated three-test selection shortcut.
