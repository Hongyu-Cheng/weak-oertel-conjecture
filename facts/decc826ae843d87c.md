## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf_{\substack{
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
p\in H}}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}.
\tag{0}
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(C_z\neq\varnothing\) and \(x\in C_z\), put
\[
\delta_z(x):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\), an affine map
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\) with
\(s(z)\in C_z\) for \(z\in E\), and an anchor
\[
\bar z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\]
put
\[
D_E:=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2.
\]
If \(q(E):=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)>0\), define
\[
\alpha_{\bar z}(E,s):=
\inf_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(E)\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
\bar z\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in D_E\cap Q}\delta_z(s(z)).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(q(E)=0\), necessarily \(D_E=\{\bar z\}\), and define
\[
\alpha_{\bar z}(E,s):=\delta_{\bar z}(s(\bar z)).
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
h_S((\bar z,s(\bar z)))\geq\alpha_{\bar z}(E,s)
\tag{4}
\]
for every such pair.  This includes proper and whole ambient halfspaces,
closed boundaries, vertical ambient halfspaces, and an identically-zero
pullback to \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\).

The following explicit rank bounds hold.

1.  In rank zero, let \(E=\{\bar z\}\), and choose
\(x_{\bar z}\in C_{\bar z}\) maximizing (1), as supplied by accepted fact
`4e868f774de77608`.  With \(s(\bar z)=x_{\bar z}\),
\[
\alpha_{\bar z}(E,s)
=\max_{x\in C_{\bar z}}\delta_{\bar z}(x).
\tag{5}
\]

2.  In rank one, let \(v_0,v_m\in\mathbb Z^2\) be distinct, write
\[
v_m-v_0=m u
\]
with \(m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(u\in\mathbb Z^2\) primitive, and let
\[
\bar z=v_{k_\ast}:=v_0+k_\ast u
\qquad(0\leq k_\ast\leq m).
\]
Choose \(x_i\in C_{v_i}\) for \(i\in\{0,m\}\), put
\[
t_i:=\delta_{v_i}(x_i),
\]
and let \(s\) be the affine interpolant with
\(s(v_i)=x_i\).  For \(0\leq k\leq m\), define
\[
g_k:=
\left(
\left(1-\frac{k}{m}\right)t_0^{1/d}
+\frac{k}{m}t_m^{1/d}
\right)^d.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_{\bar z}(\{v_0,v_m\},s)
\geq
\min\left\{
\sum_{k=0}^{k_\ast}g_k,\
\sum_{k=k_\ast}^{m}g_k
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

3.  In rank two, let \(v_1,v_2,v_3\in\mathbb Z^2\) be affinely
independent and suppose that their centroid
\[
\bar z:=\frac{v_1+v_2+v_3}{3}
\tag{8}
\]
belongs to \(\mathbb Z^2\).  Put
\[
T:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},
\]
\[
\Delta_x(T):=\max_{z\in T}z_1-\min_{z\in T}z_1,\qquad
\Delta_y(T):=\max_{z\in T}z_2-\min_{z\in T}z_2.
\tag{9}
\]
Suppose \(x_i\in C_{v_i}\) satisfy
\[
\delta_{v_i}(x_i)\geq t
\qquad(i=1,2,3)
\tag{10}
\]
for some \(t\geq0\), and let \(s:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^d\) be the unique
affine map with \(s(v_i)=x_i\).  Then
\[
\alpha_{\bar z}(\{v_1,v_2,v_3\},s)
\geq
t\,
\max\left\{
1,\
\left\lceil
\frac49\operatorname{area}(T)
-\Delta_x(T)-\Delta_y(T)
\right\rceil
\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The ceiling in (11) is applied to the displayed real number.  In
particular, the right side is valid even when that number is nonpositive.

## Proof

We first prove the pullback (4), because it is the link from every
certificate below to actual mixed depth at the prescribed lattice
coordinate.  Put
\[
p:=(\bar z,s(\bar z)).
\]
Let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\).  Moving
its boundary parallel toward \(p\) produces a closed
boundary-through-\(p\) halfspace \(H_0\subseteq H\).  Write
\[
H_0=
\left\{
(z,x):
a^\top(z-\bar z)+b^\top(x-s(\bar z))\geq0
\right\},
\qquad
(a,b)\neq(0,0).
\tag{12}
\]
On \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\), define the affine pullback
\[
\ell(z):=
a^\top(z-\bar z)+b^\top(s(z)-s(\bar z)).
\tag{13}
\]
One has \(\ell(\bar z)=0\).

Suppose first that \(\ell\) is nonconstant.  Then
\[
Q:=\{z\in\operatorname{aff}(E):\ell(z)\geq0\}
\tag{14}
\]
is a proper closed halfspace containing \(\bar z\).  If \(b\neq0\), then
for every \(z\in D_E\cap Q\), the restriction of \(H_0\) to \(C_z\) is a
proper closed fiber halfspace containing \(s(z)\), so that fiber
contributes at least \(\delta_z(s(z))\).  If \(b=0\), every such full
fiber is contained in \(H_0\), and it contributes
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\geq\delta_z(s(z))\).  Summing over
\(D_E\cap Q\) and using (2) gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\geq
\alpha_{\bar z}(E,s).
\tag{15}
\]

Suppose instead that \(\ell\) is identically zero.  If \(b\neq0\), the
restriction of \(H_0\) to every fiber over \(D_E\) is a proper closed
halfspace whose boundary contains \(s(z)\), hence every such fiber
contributes at least \(\delta_z(s(z))\).  If \(b=0\), the defining
functional in (12) vanishes on every base point of
\(\operatorname{aff}(E)\), so \(H_0\) contains every full fiber over
\(D_E\).  Thus in either case
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\geq
\sum_{z\in D_E}\delta_z(s(z)).
\tag{16}
\]
For \(q(E)>0\), the last sum is no smaller than the infimum in (2);
for \(q(E)=0\), it is exactly (3).  This proves (15) again.  The same
rank-zero argument also covers a vertical ambient halfspace whose base
boundary contains \(\bar z\): it contains the entire anchor fiber.  If the
whole space is admitted as a closed halfspace, it contains all fibers and
also satisfies the bound.  All boundaries remained closed, so no positive
mixed boundary mass was discarded.  Taking the infimum over ambient
halfspaces proves (4).

For (5), accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` states that the maximum of
\(x\mapsto\delta_{\bar z}(x)\) is attained on the compact fiber.  Equation
(3) then gives (5).

We next prove (7).  Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` states that
\[
(z,x)\longmapsto\delta_z(x)^{1/d}
\tag{17}
\]
is jointly concave on \(C\).  Affineness of \(s\), followed by (17), gives
\[
\delta_{v_k}(s(v_k))^{1/d}
\geq
\left(1-\frac{k}{m}\right)\delta_{v_0}(x_0)^{1/d}
+\frac{k}{m}\delta_{v_m}(x_m)^{1/d}
\]
for \(0\leq k\leq m\).  Hence
\[
\delta_{v_k}(s(v_k))\geq g_k.
\tag{18}
\]
Every proper closed halfspace of the affine line containing \(v_{k_\ast}\)
contains one of the two closed lattice rays
\[
\{v_k:0\leq k\leq k_\ast\},
\qquad
\{v_k:k_\ast\leq k\leq m\}.
\tag{19}
\]
The anchor point is correctly present in both closed rays.  Equations
(2), (18), and (19) prove (7), including the endpoint cases
\(k_\ast=0,m\).

It remains to prove (11).  Again by joint root concavity (17), for
\[
z=\lambda_1v_1+\lambda_2v_2+\lambda_3v_3,
\qquad
\lambda_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i\lambda_i=1,
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_z(s(z))^{1/d}
&\geq
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i\delta_{v_i}(x_i)^{1/d}\\
&\geq
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i t^{1/d}
=t^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\delta_z(s(z))\geq t
\qquad(z\in T).
\tag{20}
\]

We need an exact lower bound on the number of lattice points of \(T\) in
every closed halfspace containing its centroid.  We first record two
elementary planar facts.

First, every line through the centroid of a triangle cuts each of its two
closed sides in area at least \(4/9\) of the triangle area.  To see this,
let an affine functional defining such a line have values
\(r_1,r_2,r_3\) at the vertices.  Equation (8) gives
\[
r_1+r_2+r_3=0.
\tag{21}
\]
After reversing the functional and relabeling, suppose
\(r_1,r_2\geq0\) and
\[
r_3=-(r_1+r_2)\leq0.
\]
If \(r_1+r_2>0\), put
\[
u:=\frac{r_1}{r_1+r_2},\qquad 1-u:=\frac{r_2}{r_1+r_2}.
\]
The nonpositive cap is a triangle at \(v_3\).  Its two edge ratios are
\[
\frac1{1+u},
\qquad
\frac1{1+(1-u)},
\]
so its area fraction is
\[
\frac1{(1+u)(2-u)}
=\frac1{2+u-u^2}
\geq\frac49.
\tag{22}
\]
The same fraction is at most \(1/2\), so the complementary side has area
at least \(1/2\), and hence also at least \(4/9\).  Zero vertex values
follow by the same formula at \(u=0\) or \(1\).  The case with one
nonnegative and two nonpositive values follows by reversing the
functional.  This proves the first fact.

Second, every compact convex set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) satisfies
\[
\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\geq
\operatorname{area}(K)
-\Delta_x(K)-\Delta_y(K).
\tag{23}
\]
For a self-contained proof, let
\[
B_\infty:=[-1/2,1/2]^2,\qquad
K^\ominus:=\{x:x+B_\infty\subseteq K\}.
\]
Erode first by the horizontal segment
\([-1/2,1/2]\times\{0\}\).  Every horizontal slice of a convex set is an
interval, and its length decreases by at most \(1\).  Fubini therefore
shows that this first erosion removes area at most \(\Delta_y(K)\).
Erode the resulting convex set by
\(\{0\}\times[-1/2,1/2]\).  The same vertical-slice argument removes area
at most its horizontal width, which is no greater than
\(\Delta_x(K)\).  Successive erosions by these two segments equal erosion
by their Minkowski sum \(B_\infty\).  Hence
\[
\operatorname{area}(K^\ominus)
\geq
\operatorname{area}(K)-\Delta_x(K)-\Delta_y(K).
\tag{24}
\]
For every \(x\in K^\ominus\), round both coordinates to obtain
\(p\in\mathbb Z^2\) with \(x\in p+B_\infty\).  Since
\(p\in x+B_\infty\subseteq K\), this lattice point belongs to \(K\).
Thus the unit cells \(p+B_\infty\), for \(p\in K\cap\mathbb Z^2\), cover
\(K^\ominus\).  Their interiors are disjoint and each has area one, which
proves (23).

Now let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a proper closed halfspace containing
\(\bar z\).  Move its boundary parallel toward \(\bar z\) to obtain a
boundary-through-\(\bar z\) closed halfspace \(Q_0\subseteq Q\), and put
\[
K:=T\cap Q_0.
\]
The first planar fact gives
\[
\operatorname{area}(K)\geq\frac49\operatorname{area}(T).
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(K\subseteq T\),
\[
\Delta_x(K)\leq\Delta_x(T),\qquad
\Delta_y(K)\leq\Delta_y(T).
\]
Equations (23)-(25) give
\[
\#(T\cap Q\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\geq
\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\geq
\frac49\operatorname{area}(T)
-\Delta_x(T)-\Delta_y(T).
\tag{26}
\]
The count is an integer, so it is at least the ceiling in (11).
Furthermore, it is at least one because the closed halfspace and the
triangle both contain the lattice point \(\bar z\).  Combining (20) and
(26), and then taking the infimum over \(Q\), proves (11).  Equation (4)
makes every displayed rank bound a lower bound for actual mixed depth at
the corresponding point in the prescribed anchor fiber.
