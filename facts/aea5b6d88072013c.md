## Statement

Let $1/2<a<\alpha=(9+\sqrt{33})/24$ and $0\le b\le1$. Define
$$
\begin{gathered}
c=\frac1{3a},\quad \rho=\frac1{3(1-a)},\quad
A=\frac a{2a-1},\quad L=\frac{(1-a)^2}{(2a-1)(2-3a)},\quad B=\frac{2a-1}{2-3a},\\
k(b)=A(b-c)_+^2-L(b-\rho)_+^2,\quad
g=\frac{2-3a}{9(1-a)},\quad
\lambda=\frac{18a^3-24a^2+8a-1}{27a^2(a-1)},\quad v=\frac{(3a-1)(6a-1)}{27a^2},\\
M_0=\frac92-\frac1{2a(1-a)},\qquad M_1=\frac{g}{2/9-v},\qquad
M_2(b)=\frac32+b+\frac52 b^2,\\
E(b)=\begin{cases}
(1-b)^2/6,&b\ge\rho,\\
(1-\rho)^2/2-(1-\rho)^3/[3(1-b)],&b<\rho.
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
$$
Then
$$
g+(\lambda-2/9)\max\{M_0,M_1,M_2(b)\}+k(b)-\lambda[b^2+(1-b)^2/3]-2B E(b)\ge0.
$$

## Proof

Here $1/2<c<\rho<1$, and the two formulas for $E$ agree at $\rho$. The numerator of $v-2/9$ over $27a^2$ is $D=12a^2-9a+1$, whose larger root is $\alpha$ and smaller root is below $1/2$. Thus $2/9-v>0$. Also
$$
\lambda-2/9=\frac{(2a-1)(6a^2-6a+1)}{27a^2(a-1)}>0
$$
because $6a^2-6a+1<0$ on $[1/2,2/3]$. Let $F_M(a,b)$ be the left side of the claimed inequality with its maximum replaced by $M$. It is increasing in $M$.

Parametrize the three branches by $b_0=cy$, $b_1=c+(\rho-c)y$, and $b_2=\rho+(1-\rho)y$ for $0\le y\le1$. The respective formulas for $k(b_i)$ are $0$, $A(b_i-c)^2$, and $A(b_i-c)^2-L(b_i-\rho)^2$. The first two branches use the rational formula for $E$ with $b_i<\rho$, extended by equality to $\rho$, and the third uses $(1-b_i)^2/6$. Write $M_6=6$ and $J=3a^2+2ay-4a-y+1$. For the branch and choice of $M$ indicated by each subscript, define the clearing factors
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{0,M_1}&=-729a^4(-3a+y)(a-1)^3D,&
D_{1,M_0}=D_{1,M_2}&=1458a^4(a-1)^3J,\\
D_{2,M_0}&=-1458a^3(a-1)^3,&
D_{0,M_6}&=729a^4(-3a+y)(a-1)^3,\\
D_{1,M_6}&=729a^4(a-1)^3J,&
D_{2,M_6}&=-729a^2(a-1)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
They are positive on the parts of the actual domain where used. In fact $-3a+y<0$, $a-1<0$, $D<0$, and $J\le J(a,1)=a(3a-2)<0$ since $2a-1>0$. For each choice form $P_{i,M}=D_{i,M}F_M(a,b_i)$, canceling rational factors. The displayed formulas make these products polynomials. The endpoints $a=1/2,2/3$ below are used only for those polynomials.

For a polynomial $P(x,z)=\sum p_{k\ell}x^kz^\ell$ of bidegree at most $(m,n)$ set
$$
C_{ij}=\sum_{k\le i,\,\ell\le j}p_{k\ell}
\frac{\binom{i}{k}\binom{j}{\ell}}{\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{\ell}}.
$$
It equals the coefficient of the product of the degree-$m$ and degree-$n$ Bernstein basis polynomials with indices $(i,j)$. Indeed, multiply $x^k$ by $(x+(1-x))^{m-k}$ and use $\binom{m-k}{i-k}=\binom{m}{i}\binom{i}{k}/\binom{m}{k}$, and do the same for $z^\ell$. The products of the Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative on the square and sum to one. Therefore $P\ge\min_{i,j} C_{ij}$ there.

For each row, substitute $a=a_0+(a_1-a_0)x$ and $y=y_0+(y_1-y_0)z$ into the corresponding $P_{i,M}$. The exact minima of the coefficient formula above are as follows.

| Branch | $M$ | $[a_0,a_1]$ | $[y_0,y_1]$ | Degree | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $M_1$ | $[1/2,3/5]$ | $[0,1]$ | $(9,3)$ | $3/512$ |
| 1 | $M_0$ | $[1/2,11/20]$ | $[0,1/2]$ | $(9,3)$ | $3/256$ |
| 1 | $M_0$ | $[1/2,11/20]$ | $[1/2,1]$ | $(9,3)$ | $3/256$ |
| 1 | $M_2$ | $[11/20,3/5]$ | $[0,1/2]$ | $(9,3)$ | $13082/1953125$ |
| 1 | $M_0$ | $[11/20,3/5]$ | $[1/2,1]$ | $(9,3)$ | $15032430113/256000000000$ |
| 2 | $M_0$ | $[1/2,3/5]$ | $[0,1]$ | $(6,2)$ | $3/32$ |
| 0 | $M_6$ | $[3/5,2/3]$ | $[0,1]$ | $(8,3)$ | $148954/390625$ |
| 1 | $M_6$ | $[3/5,2/3]$ | $[0,1]$ | $(9,3)$ | $0$ |
| 2 | $M_6$ | $[3/5,2/3]$ | $[0,1]$ | $(5,2)$ | $12664/3125$ |

For reproducibility, each row is computed by expanding the canceled product into the power coefficients $p_{k\ell}$. At each fixed $\ell$ replace the list of coefficients in $k$ by the list $\sum_{k=0}^i p_{k\ell}\binom{i}{k}/\binom{m}{k}$ for $i=0,\ldots,m$. At each fixed $i$ apply the same replacement in the second index with degree $n$. The smallest resulting coefficient gives the last column. The first six rows cover all three branches for $a\le3/5$. In each such row the selected $M$ is at most the required maximum. The clearing factors and Bernstein bounds imply $F_M\ge0$ throughout those rectangles in the actual domain.

For the remaining range,
$$
g-6(2/9-v)=\frac{27a^3-44a^2+20a-2}{9a^2(a-1)}.
$$
After $a=3/5+x/15$ the negative of its numerator is $1/125+(91/375)x-(23/1125)x^2-(1/125)x^3$. Its cubic Bernstein coefficients are $1/125,4/45,22/135,2/9$ and hence are all positive on the unit interval. The denominator is negative. Since $2/9-v>0$ on the actual domain, this gives $M_1>6$ for $3/5\le a<\alpha$. The last three rows therefore prove the required inequality on that range as well. The branch changes cover both endpoints of $[0,1]$ and the shared knots.
