## Statement

Let $Q_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(-3,0),(-1,-1),(0,-1),(3,1),(2,1)\}$ and $P_0=Q_0\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Let $F\subset\mathbb R^d$ be any full-dimensional simplex of volume $v>0$, $d\ge1$, with centroid $g$, and set $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. At an integer anchor $p$ retain all actual fibers of $Q_0\times F$ with bases in $P_0[p]=\{z\in P_0:2p-z\in P_0\}$. Use their original unnormalized volumes, and write $D_p(x)$ for raw depth at $(p,x)$ over closed ambient halfspaces of this retained measure. For anchors $p_-=(-1,0),p_0=(0,0),p_+=(1,0)$, the respective maxima over $x\in F$ are $5\Gamma_d v,3\Gamma_d v,5\Gamma_d v$, attained at $g$. At level $4\Gamma_d v$ the projected and full mixed superlevel traces fail same-parity midpoint closure, with no qualifying point anywhere on the middle fiber.

## Proof

The polygon's five supporting inequalities are $-1\le y\le1$, $x+2y\ge-3$, $2x-3y\le3$, and $x-5y\ge-3$, as is checked from the five successive edges. Substituting $y=-1,0,1$ gives the exact lattice rows
$$
\{(-1,-1),(0,-1)\},\qquad
\{(j,0):-3\le j\le1,\ j\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad
\{(2,1),(3,1)\}.
$$
At $p_-$ the reflected set is the whole middle row. At $p_0$ it is $\{(-1,0),(0,0),(1,0)\}$. At $p_+$ it is $\{(-1,-1),(0,-1),(1,0),(2,1),(3,1)\}$. These are obtained by matching each point to its reflection $2p-z$ in the displayed complete list.

For any such retained base with $N$ points, the through-$(p,g)$ caps with a nonzero continuous normal have opposite scalar shifts on each reflected pair of identical simplex laws. Apply 6a7d56e450b3126f to uniform $X$ on $F$ with that normal and the absolute value of the shift. Each pair contributes at least $2\Gamma_d v$, and the same assertion at shift zero gives at least $\Gamma_d v$ for the own fiber. The through-point pure base caps include the own fiber and at least one member per pair, thus at least $(N+1)v/2\ge N\Gamma_d v$. Here $\Gamma_d\le1/2$ follows from $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$ by the binomial expansion. Translate any containing boundary to the point to extend the lower bound. For any $x\in F$, apply the ordinary simplex maximum in 2acc8bc803acf1c0 to the same $F$. Extending each ordinary closed halfspace containing $x$ across all base coordinates multiplies its cap mass by $N$, so $D_p(x)\le N\Gamma_d v$. These arguments apply even when the retained bases have lower affine dimension.

The two outer anchors are congruent modulo two and have midpoint $p_0$. The points $(p_-,g)$ and $(p_+,g)$ qualify, but even the best $x$ at $p_0$ fails. Thus the projected qualifying anchors cannot be the trace of a convex set on $P_0$, and the full mixed qualifying points cannot be a convex trace on $P_0\times F$. This obstructs an application of convex-set Helly theory directly to these varying-source superlevels.
