## Statement

Let $A,B>0$ and let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$, $m\ge1$, be a convex body centrally symmetric about $0$. Let $K$ have fibers $r(z)D$, where $r(z)=1+z/A$ on $[-A,0]$ and $r(z)=1-z/B$ on $[0,B]$. If $A=B$, every through-origin cap has half the volume. If $A\ne B$, the only through-origin cutting plane with centroid-zero section is $z=0$. In either case, every finite menu of such centroid-zero sections, with associated inward unit normals $u_j$, section areas $S_j$, and probability weights $\theta_j$ satisfying $\sum_j\theta_j S_j u_j=0$, has weighted mean cap fraction $1/2$.

## Proof

If $A=B$, central symmetry gives the cap fractions directly. Suppose $A\ne B$, and write $d=m+1$. For an oblique plane $z+c\cdot X=0$ with $c\ne0$, the axial coordinates $u=z/r(z)$ range over $\mathbb R$ on the interior of the axial interval. The reciprocals are $R(u)=1+u/B$ for $u\ge0$ and $R(u)=1-u/A$ for $u\le0$, so $R-uR'=1$ on each piece. To apply the coarea lemma with a positive radius on a closed interval, truncate the axial domain in the two interiors beyond the finite range of $s=-c\cdot q$ for $q\in D$. The section and its measure are unchanged. Its vertical first moment for raw coarea is $\int_D s\,w(s)\,dq$, since $z=s/R(s)$ and raw coarea is $w(s)R(s)dq$. Pair $q$ with $-q$ to write it as
$$
\int_{\{q\in D:s>0\}}s\bigl[(1+s/B)^{-d-1}-(1+s/A)^{-d-1}\bigr]\,dq.
$$
The sign is strict and fixed, since $A\ne B$ and $D$ has an interior neighborhood of $0$.

For a vertical cutting plane $c\cdot X=0$ with $c\ne0$, the section has horizontal slices $r(z)(D\cap c^\perp)$, so its vertical centroid is the centroid of $r(z)^{m-1}\,dz$ on $[-A,B]$. On the positive side the mass is $B/m$ and first moment $B^2/[m(m+1)]$ up to a common section factor. The negative side has mass $A/m$ and first moment $-A^2/[m(m+1)]$. The centroid is $(B-A)/(m+1)\ne0$. This includes $m=1$, for which the section is a segment. Thus only $z=0$ remains, with centroid zero. Its two orientations have the same area and complementary fractions. The balance equation gives equal total weight to the two orientations, and their weighted mean is $1/2$.
