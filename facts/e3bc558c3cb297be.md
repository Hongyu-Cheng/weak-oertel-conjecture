## Statement

Let \(n,d,m\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Retain the actual-body notation and normalization of accepted facts 03f071403cdfc69f and d887d95075dfcc06: \(C_z=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), \(T=2^{-n}W\), and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), over closed ambient halfspaces \(H\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero.

Let \(M_1,\ldots,M_n\) be arbitrary nonnegative integers, let \(B\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix of nonnegative real entries, and let \(f:[0,\infty)^m\to[0,\infty)\) be finite-valued and coordinatewise nonincreasing, with \(f(0)>0\). Assume joint log-concavity with the zero convention: for every \(a,b\in[0,\infty)^m\) and \(0<\theta<1\), \(f((1-\theta)a+\theta b)\ge f(a)^{1-\theta}f(b)^\theta\), where a positive power of zero is zero. Assume that the entire actual integer table is
\[
w_z=
\begin{cases}
f(Bz),&z\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad 0\le z_i\le M_i\text{ for all }i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
The function \(f\) is an arbitrary function of its \(m\) arguments satisfying these hypotheses; no common one-variable profile is assumed. No continuity of \(f\), rationality or positivity of individual entries of \(B\), or full-dimensionality of a coordinate restriction is required.

Put \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). For \(j\in D\) and finite \(t\ge0\), retain the original set-valued family \(F_j(t)=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ w_{j+u}\ge e^{-t}w_j\}\). It is exactly \(F_j(t)\) of accepted facts 102f49bbb2cedb49 and 34e95d2e044ea815 and the fixed-center \(F_t\) of d887d95075dfcc06. It is distinct from the unparameterized scalar all-pair score \(F_j\) discussed in 03f071403cdfc69f. Every coordinate inequality is componentwise.

At a fixed positive center retain \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). The dependence of \(\alpha\) on this center is suppressed as in the accepted facts. Retain
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
&& (L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The feature matrix \(B\) in (1) is separate from the scalar cutoff sum \(B_j^\Delta(L)\) in (2). All displacement sums are oriented and include zero. Every difference set counts each distinct integer difference once.

Write \(I=\{i:M_i\ge2\}\), \(r=|I|\), and let \(\iota:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R^n\) insert zeros outside \(I\), with the coordinates in \(I\) listed in increasing order. Define the scalar restriction \(\bar w_y=w_{\iota(y)}\) and \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^r}\bar w_y\). This \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}\) equals the scalar restricted total \(\bar W\) of 34e95d2e044ea815; the meanings of \(w,W,T\) remain the original ones. For \(r=0\), use \(\mathbb Z^0=\{0\}\), so \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}=w_0\).

Give the original corner maximum the name
\[
\operatorname{Corner}(w)
=\max_{\substack{j\in D\\t\ge0}}
\frac{w_je^{-t}|F_j(t)-F_j(t)|}{1+2t}.
\tag{3}
\]
This maximum is attained. For integers \(r,m\ge1\), define
\[
Q_{r,m}=\frac12\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r3^{-3m}
\exp\left[-m\log3\left(\sqrt{\frac{mr}{2}}+\frac m2\right)\right],
\qquad
\kappa_{r,m}=\frac{Q_{r,m}}{20e(r+1)^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Corner}(w)&\ge
\kappa_{r,m}\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}
\ge\kappa_{r,m}T
&& (r\ge1),\\
\operatorname{Corner}(w)&\ge \exp(-20(m+1)^3)T
&& (r\ge0),\\
\operatorname{Corner}(w)&\ge T
&& (r=0\text{ or }r\ge1000m^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover \(K(w)\ge\operatorname{Corner}(w)\), and there exists one \(p^\star\in S\), chosen before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(p^\star)&\ge\frac{K(w)}{12}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{Corner}(w)}{12}
\ge\frac{\exp(-20(m+1)^3)}{12}\,T,\\
h_S(p^\star)&\ge\frac{T}{12}
=\frac{W}{12\,2^n}
\qquad (r=0\text{ or }r\ge1000m^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the point works for every closed ambient halfspace containing it, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing whole fibers.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. First, (1) makes the entire table coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative integer orthant, including zeros. If \(0\le u\le v\) and \(v\) is in the capped box, so is \(u\), and \(Bu\le Bv\) gives \(w_u\ge w_v\). If \(v\) is outside the box its weight is zero. Also \(w_0=f(0)>0\), and all weights are at most \(w_0\).

For each fixed retained vector \(y\), every permitted assignment to coordinates outside \(I\) has weight at most \(w_{\iota(y)}\). There are \(\prod_{i\notin I}(M_i+1)\le2^{n-r}\) such assignments. Summing over \(y\), without removing any contribution from the original total, gives
\[
W\le2^{n-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}},
\qquad
2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\ge T.
\tag{7}
\]
This is a statement about a scalar restriction. No convex-body theorem is being applied to that restriction.

If \(r=0\), (7) gives \(w_0\ge T\). At the original center \(j=0\), one has \(F_0(0)=\{0\}\), and its corner value is \(w_0\). This proves every corner assertion in (5) for \(r=0\). Its depth consequence will follow from the original-body argument at the end.

Assume \(r\ge1\). Let \(\bar M_i\) be the retained caps, all at least two, and let \(B_I\) consist of the retained columns of \(B\), in the same order as \(\iota\). Thus \(B\iota(y)=B_Iy\). The scalar restriction has the exact formula \(\bar w_y=f(B_Iy)\) for \(0\le y_i\le\bar M_i\), and zero elsewhere. Write \(a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(m)}\) for the indexed rows of \(B_I\); repetitions and zero rows are allowed.

We first prove a count for every positive threshold \(0<\eta\le w_0\). In this count put
\[
E=\{z\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le z_i\le\bar M_i,\ f(B_Iz)\ge\eta\},
\qquad
A_p=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le u\le p,\ p+u\in E\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The set \(E\) is finite, contains zero, and is a downset. The meaning of \(A_p\) is the capped forward set of 34e95d2e044ea815, here for this original threshold prefix. We will find \(p\in E\) with \(p_i\le\lfloor\bar M_i/2\rfloor\) and
\[
|A_p-A_p|\ge Q_{r,m}\,2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(\ell:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be exactly the piecewise-linear interpolation used in 102f49bbb2cedb49 and 34e95d2e044ea815, with \(\ell(k)=\log(2k+1)\) at every nonnegative integer. Its slopes are \(\log((2k+3)/(2k+1))\), positive and decreasing, and are at most \(\log3\). The one-coordinate inequality \(k\log((2k+1)/(2k-1))\le\log3\) in equation (12) of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790 also gives \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\) wherever \(u>0\) and the derivative exists, by considering the interval \((k-1,k)\).

Set \(h_i=\lfloor\bar M_i/2\rfloor\), \(\bar z=|E|^{-1}\sum_{z\in E}z\), and \(x_i=\min\{3\bar z_i/4,h_i\}\). The capped finite-set estimate (10) in the proof of accepted fact 34e95d2e044ea815 gives
\[
\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^r\ell(x_i)\right)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{10}
\]
Its scope is every nonempty subset of the capped integer box; it does not require a two-constraint description. To make that application explicit, for an integer cap \(N\ge2\) let \(\mathcal M_N(s)=\max_{0\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\ k\in\mathbb Z}(2k+1)e^{-sk}\) and \(Z_N(v)=\sum_{q=0}^Ne^{-vq}\), exactly as in that accepted fact. Its equation (6) states \(2\mathcal M_N(s)/Z_N(3s/4)>11/10\) for every \(s\ge0\). If \(x_i<h_i\), use \(s_i=\log((2\lfloor x_i\rfloor+3)/(2\lfloor x_i\rfloor+1))\); if \(x_i=h_i\), use \(s_i=0\). Then
\(e^{\ell(x_i)-s_ix_i}=\mathcal M_{\bar M_i}(s_i)\) and \(s_ix_i=(3/4)s_i\bar z_i\). The probability
\(\pi(z)=\prod_i e^{-(3s_i/4)z_i}/Z_{\bar M_i}(3s_i/4)\) on the whole capped integer box has mass at most one on \(E\). Finite arithmetic-geometric mean therefore implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{|E|}
&\ge\left(\prod_{z\in E}\pi(z)\right)^{1/|E|}
=\prod_i\frac{e^{-s_ix_i}}{Z_{\bar M_i}(3s_i/4)},\\
\frac{\exp(\sum_i\ell(x_i))}{|E|}
&\ge\prod_i\frac{\mathcal M_{\bar M_i}(s_i)}
 {Z_{\bar M_i}(3s_i/4)}
>\left(\frac{11}{20}\right)^r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
This proves the claimed application, including clipped coordinates, zero means, and interpolation ties.

We will use the rounding observation (10) proved in 102f49bbb2cedb49 with its stated scope of any finite list of nonnegative linear forms. Explicitly, given \(v\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^r\) and \(q\ge1\) such forms, there is an integer \(p'\) in \(\prod_i[\lfloor v_i\rfloor,\lceil v_i\rceil]\) at which every form is at most its value at \(v\) and \(\sum_i\ell(p'_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(v_i)-q\log3\). Indeed, maximize the affine objective \(\sum_i\ell(y_i)\) on that rectangle subject to the form bounds. A maximum exists and its value is at least the value at \(v\). If a maximizing point has more than \(q\) noninteger coordinates, a nonzero direction on those coordinates annihilates all the forms. Small moves in either direction are feasible, so optimality forces zero objective slope. Move along that direction until a coordinate reaches an integer endpoint, preserving the objective. Repetition leaves at most \(q\) noninteger coordinates. Round them down; nonnegative coefficients preserve all form bounds, and each coordinate loses at most \(\log3\). Redundant or zero forms cause no exception. An integer upper cap containing \(v_i\) contains its entire rounding interval, so caps need not be included in the list of forms.

Apply this observation first to \(x\) and the \(m\) row forms \(a\cdot y\), where \(a\) runs through the indexed rows of \(B_I\). It gives an integer \(k\), with \(0\le k_i\le h_i\), such that
\[
\beta_a:=a\cdot k\le a\cdot x\le\frac34a\cdot\bar z
\quad\text{for every row }a,
\qquad
\sum_i\ell(k_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-m\log3.
\tag{12}
\]
For the shrink step define
\[
\gamma_m=\sqrt{\frac m2},
\qquad
s_{r,m}=\gamma_m(\sqrt r+\gamma_m)
=\sqrt{\frac{mr}{2}}+\frac m2,
\qquad
\theta_i=\max_{\substack{\text{indexed rows }a\\\beta_a>0}}
\frac{a_i k_i}{\beta_a},
\qquad
\widetilde k_i=\frac{k_i}{1+s_{r,m}\theta_i}.
\tag{13}
\]
The maximum over an empty collection in (13) is defined to be zero. The symbols \(\gamma_m,s_{r,m}\) are auxiliary reserve and shrink parameters; \(L\) continues to denote the original cutoff in (2). Nonnegativity gives \(0\le\theta_i\le1\), \(\sum_i\theta_i\le m\), and \(0\le\widetilde k_i\le k_i\le h_i\). In fact the maximum defining \(\theta_i\) is at most the sum of its nonnegative entries, and the sum over \(i\) for each active row is one.

For every indexed row put \(\delta_a=\beta_a-a\cdot\widetilde k\) and \(\zeta_a^2=\sum_i a_i^2k_i\widetilde k_i\), with \(\zeta_a\ge0\). These are the same row deficit and quadratic quantity as in 102f49bbb2cedb49, now for \(m\) rows and the shrink parameter in (13). For an active row \(\beta_a>0\), direct calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_a
&=\sum_i\frac{a_i k_i s_{r,m}\theta_i}{1+s_{r,m}\theta_i}
\ge\frac{s_{r,m}}{\beta_a}\zeta_a^2,\\
\beta_a-\delta_a
&=a\cdot\widetilde k
\le\sqrt r\left(\sum_i a_i^2\widetilde k_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt r\,\zeta_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The first inequality uses \(\theta_i\ge a_ik_i/\beta_a\); the last uses \(\widetilde k_i\le k_i\). These imply \(\delta_a\ge\gamma_m\zeta_a\). If \(\zeta_a=0\) this is immediate. Otherwise, supposing \(\delta_a<\gamma_m\zeta_a\), the second line gives \(\beta_a<(\sqrt r+\gamma_m)\zeta_a\), and the first line, with \(s_{r,m}=\gamma_m(\sqrt r+\gamma_m)\), gives \(\delta_a>\gamma_m\zeta_a\), a contradiction. If \(\beta_a=0\), then \(a_i k_i=0\) for every \(i\). Consequently \(\delta_a=\zeta_a=0\), and the same conclusion holds. This handles zero rows and all rows vanishing on the selected \(k\).

Apply the rounding observation a second time to \(\widetilde k\) and the \(2m\) nonnegative forms \(a\cdot y\) and \(\sum_i a_i^2k_i y_i\), for all indexed rows, with \(k\) fixed. The resulting integer \(p\) satisfies \(0\le p_i\le k_i\le h_i\), because \(\lceil\widetilde k_i\rceil\le k_i\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot p&\le a\cdot\widetilde k,\\
\sum_i a_i^2p_i^2&\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i p_i
\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i\widetilde k_i=\zeta_a^2,\\
\sum_i\ell(p_i)&\ge\sum_i\ell(\widetilde k_i)-2m\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Thus every row has the reserve
\[
a\cdot p+\gamma_m\left(\sum_i a_i^2p_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\beta_a\le\frac34a\cdot\bar z.
\tag{16}
\]
For a row with \(\beta_a=0\), both terms on the left are zero. If \(k=0\), (13) and the rounding intervals force \(\widetilde k=p=0\), and every displayed conclusion still holds.

The loss in the shrink step is controlled using \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\). For \(k_i>0\), its \(\widetilde k_i\) is positive. Integrating over the finitely many affine intervals between them, and noting that \(k_i=0\) implies \(\theta_i=0\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\bigl(\ell(k_i)-\ell(\widetilde k_i)\bigr)
&\le\log3\sum_{i:k_i>0}\log\frac{k_i}{\widetilde k_i}\\
&=\log3\sum_i\log(1+s_{r,m}\theta_i)
\le m s_{r,m}\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (10), (12), (15), and (17) proves
\[
\prod_i(2p_i+1)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r3^{-3m}
e^{-m s_{r,m}\log3}\,2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{18}
\]
This also covers \(k=0\) and the case in which there are no active rows.

The joint-profile step uses the uniform mean of exactly \(E\). Finite iteration of the assumed log-concavity, with all \(f(B_Iz)\ge\eta>0\), gives
\[
f(B_I\bar z)
\ge\left(\prod_{z\in E}f(B_Iz)\right)^{1/|E|}
\ge\eta.
\tag{19}
\]
For a singleton \(E\) the first inequality is equality. This is a finite convex-combination inequality, so no continuity or closure assumption on a superlevel set is involved. Equation (12) and \(p\le k\) imply \(B_Ip\le(3/4)B_I\bar z\le B_I\bar z\). Hence monotonicity, (19), and the caps show \(p\in E\).

Let \(V_i\) be independent, each uniform on the integers \(-p_i,\ldots,p_i\), and define \(V_i^+=\max\{V_i,0\}\), \(V_i^-=\max\{-V_i,0\}\). Equations (13)--(14) in accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790, for exactly this uniform integer law, give
\(\mathbb E V_i^+=p_i(p_i+1)/(2(2p_i+1))\le p_i/3\) and
\(\operatorname{Var}(V_i^+)\le2p_i^2/9\), including \(p_i=0\). Reflection gives the same estimates for \(V_i^-\). For an indexed row \(a\), the sums \(a\cdot V^+\) and \(a\cdot V^-\) therefore have a common mean \(\mu_a\le a\cdot p/3\) and a common variance \(\sigma_a^2\le(2/9)\sum_i a_i^2p_i^2\), using independence across coordinates. Dependence between different signed or row sums is permitted.

Equation (16) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot\bar z-a\cdot p
&\ge\frac13a\cdot p
+\frac43\gamma_m\left(\sum_i a_i^2p_i^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&\ge\mu_a+2\sqrt m\,\sigma_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The last coefficient follows from
\((4/3)\sqrt{m/2}=2\sqrt m\,(\sqrt2/3)\).
If \(\sigma_a>0\), the second-moment inequality bounds the probability that either specified signed sum exceeds its bound in (20) by \(1/(4m)\). Explicitly, for \(P=a\cdot V^+\) or \(P=a\cdot V^-\),
\(\mathbb P\{P>\mu_a+2\sqrt m\,\sigma_a\}\le
\mathbb E(P-\mu_a)^2/(4m\sigma_a^2)=1/(4m)\).
If \(\sigma_a=0\), the sum equals its mean almost surely and cannot violate (20). A union bound over the \(2m\) indexed row-and-sign conditions shows that, with probability at least one half,
\[
B_I(p+V^+)\le B_I\bar z,
\qquad
B_I(p+V^-)\le B_I\bar z.
\tag{21}
\]
Zero or repeated rows are already included in that bound.

On the event (21), both integer points \(p+V^\pm\) are inside the original retained capped box, since \(0\le V_i^\pm\le p_i\) and \(2p_i\le2h_i\le\bar M_i\). By monotonicity of \(f\) and (19), both have weight at least \(\eta\). Thus \(V^+,V^-\in A_p\), and \(V=V^+-V^-\in A_p-A_p\). The vector \(V\) is uniform on a box of \(\prod_i(2p_i+1)\) distinct integer vectors, so
\[
|A_p-A_p|\ge\frac12\prod_i(2p_i+1).
\tag{22}
\]
Every output here is one distinct difference; its number of other representations plays no role. Combining (18) and (22) proves (9) with exactly (4). The argument counts \(m\) features and their two signs, regardless of the number or shape of the joint profile's superlevel constraints.

For the required coefficient comparison, at \(m=2\) one has \(\gamma_2=1\) and \(s_{r,2}=\sqrt r+1\). The coefficient \(Q_r^{(2)}\) in 102f49bbb2cedb49 and 34e95d2e044ea815 is \((2\cdot3^6)^{-1}(11/10)^r e^{-(8/3)(\sqrt r+1)}\). Thus the exact map is
\[
Q_{r,2}
=\exp\left[\left(\frac83-2\log3\right)(\sqrt r+1)\right]Q_r^{(2)}
\ge Q_r^{(2)}.
\tag{23}
\]
The inequality uses \(\log3<4/3\), proved in 3a39f48aba7a2790. The new coefficient retains the exact \(\log3\) shrink loss instead of that predecessor's coarser numerical relaxation.

We now select a weighted prefix of the scalar restriction while preserving its exact original values. Put \(\bar D=\{y\in\mathbb Z^r:\bar w_y>0\}\). For every \(y\in\bar D\), coordinatewise floor-halving remains inside the retained capped box and gives \(B_I\lfloor y/2\rfloor\le B_Iy/2\). Monotonicity and log-concavity between zero and \(B_Iy\) yield
\[
\bar w_{\lfloor y/2\rfloor}
\ge f(B_Iy/2)
\ge\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}.
\tag{24}
\]
Each integer image of floor-halving has at most \(2^r\) preimages, of the form \(2u+\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r\). Summing over the finite positive support gives \(\sum_{y\in\bar D}\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\le2^rW_{\mathrm{zero}}\).

Set \(J=\lceil2(r+1)\log2\rceil\), so \(1\le J\le2(r+1)\). For positive weights with \(\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-J}\), one has \(\bar w_y\le e^{-J/2}\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\). Their total is consequently at most \(e^{-J/2}2^rW_{\mathrm{zero}}\le W_{\mathrm{zero}}/2\). The \(J\) bands
\(\{y\in\bar D:w_0e^{-t}<\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-(t-1)}\}\),
for integers \(1\le t\le J\), therefore include one of mass at least \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}/(2J)\). Their strict lower and weak upper endpoints partition all retained weights, including every boundary value. Choose such an index \(t_0\), let \(\eta=w_0e^{-t_0}\), and now use exactly the prefix \(E=\{y:\bar w_y\ge\eta\}\) in (8). Every weight of the selected band is at most \(e\eta\), so
\[
\eta|E|\ge\frac{W_{\mathrm{zero}}}{2eJ},
\qquad
1\le t_0\le J.
\tag{25}
\]
No real superlevel endpoint is being approximated.

Apply (9) to this prefix, obtaining \(p\in E\), and put \(\tau=\log(\bar w_p/\eta)\). The inequalities \(\eta\le\bar w_p\le w_0\) give \(0\le\tau\le t_0\le J\) and the exact identity \(\bar w_pe^{-\tau}=\eta\). Choose the original center \(j=\iota(p)\). Every original displacement \(0\le u\le j\) vanishes outside \(I\), so, with exactly the original forward weights,
\(F_j(\tau)=\iota(A_p)\) and \(w_j=\bar w_p\).
The linear injection \(\iota\) preserves differences and their cardinalities. Hence (9) and (25) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_je^{-\tau}|F_j(\tau)-F_j(\tau)|}{1+2\tau}
&\ge\frac{Q_{r,m}\,2^{-r}\eta|E|}{1+2J}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_{r,m}}{2eJ(1+2J)}\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_{r,m}}{20e(r+1)^2}\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The last inequality uses \(J\le2(r+1)\) and \(1+2J\le5(r+1)\). Together with (7), this proves the first line of (5).

We verify both explicit numerical bounds. The elementary estimates \(\log(11/10)>1/11\), \(\log3<4/3\), and \(\log40<4\) are proved in 3a39f48aba7a2790. The first also follows by integrating \(1/x>10/11\) over \(1\le x<11/10\). For real \(x\ge1\), the derivative of \(\sqrt x-\log(x+1)\) is \((\sqrt x-1)^2/(2\sqrt x(x+1))\ge0\), and its value at one is positive. Thus \(\log(r+1)\le\sqrt r\). Since \(2\sqrt2/3<1\), the exact formula (4) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\kappa_{r,m}
&=r\log\frac{11}{10}
-m\log3\left(\sqrt{\frac{mr}{2}}+\frac m2\right)
-3m\log3-2\log(r+1)-\log40-1\\
&>\frac r{11}-(m^{3/2}+2)\sqrt r
-\frac23m^2-4m-5\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\frac r{11}}-\frac{\sqrt{11}}2(m^{3/2}+2)\right)^2
-\frac{11}{4}(m^{3/2}+2)^2-\frac23m^2-4m-5\\
&\ge-\left(\frac{11}{4}m^3+\frac{35}{3}m^2+4m+16\right)\\
&\ge-20(m+1)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
For the penultimate line, drop the square and use \(m^{3/2}\le m^2\) for \(m\ge1\).
