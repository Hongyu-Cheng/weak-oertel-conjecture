## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(k\ge1\), put \(M=2^k\) and \(n=k+2\). For \(\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k\), define \(h(\boldsymbol\eta)=\sum_{i=1}^k2^{i-1}\eta_i\). Thus \(h\) ranges once over \(0,\ldots,M-1\). In the first two coordinates of the original lattice, set

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf u_h&=(1,h),&\mathbf v_h&=(-1,1-h),&\mathbf c&=(0,1),\\
\mathbf a_h&=(2,2h-1)=\mathbf u_h-\mathbf v_h,
&\mathbf b_h&=(-2,1-2h)=-\mathbf a_h,
&D_h&=\{\mathbf a_h,\mathbf b_h,\mathbf c,\mathbf0,\mathbf u_h,\mathbf v_h\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define the complete support and table in \(\mathbb Z^{k+2}\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\bigcup_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k}
\{(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\eta):\mathbf x\in D_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\},\\
p_{(\mathbf a_h,\boldsymbol\eta)}
&=p_{(\mathbf b_h,\boldsymbol\eta)}=\frac1{16M},
&p_{(\mathbf0,\boldsymbol\eta)}&=\frac1{8M},\\
p_{(\mathbf u_h,\boldsymbol\eta)}
&=p_{(\mathbf v_h,\boldsymbol\eta)}
=p_{(\mathbf c,\boldsymbol\eta)}=\frac1{4M},
&h&=h(\boldsymbol\eta),
\end{aligned}
\]

and set \(p_{\mathbf I}=0\) off \(D\). These sites and masses are fixed before choosing any matrix. The slice \(h=0\), after normalization to mass one, is exactly the six-site table of accepted fact `6aa711c2cd5e2a52`; all other slices have been specified in the same original lattice coordinates.

The table is a probability table with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with every lattice value, including every zero. Its normalization to accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=1/(4M)\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log((1/(4M))/p_{\mathbf I})\).

More strongly, the full-dimensional rational polytope
\(C^{(k)}=\operatorname{conv}\{(\mathbf I,0),(\mathbf I,p_{\mathbf I}):\mathbf I\in D\}\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}\)
has exact fibers \(C^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}=[0,p_{\mathbf I}]\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\) and empty fibers at every other integer site. Thus it realizes this unchanged table in continuous dimension \(d=1\).

Use the feasible arrays and conditional assignment entropy of accepted fact `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`, with both indices in this \(D\). For a symmetric positive-definite matrix \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) with \(\operatorname{tr}A=1\), replace only the objective by \(V_A\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I},\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V_A(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf J,\\
H(r)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\qquad 0\log0=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the column equation is imposed for every \(\mathbf I\in D\), and the vector row equation for every \(\mathbf J\in D\). Let \(r_A^\star\) be the unique maximizer of \(H\) on the entire face minimizing \(V_A\), and let \(q_A^\star\) be its target law. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(q_A^\star)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star>0}
q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star\log q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star,\\
G_A&=\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star>0}q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star
\left[\log p_{\mathbf J}
-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\frac{r_{A,\mathbf J,\mathbf I}^\star}{q_{A,\mathbf J}^\star}
\log p_{\mathbf I}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

For every such \(A\), the exact bounds are

\[
G_A\ge\frac18\log2,
\qquad
0\le n\log2-S(q_A^\star)+H(r_A^\star)
\le\frac{2\log2}{M}.
\]

Consequently, for every finite \(C\ge0\), choosing \(k\ge1\) with \(2^k>16C\) gives one table satisfying

\[
\forall A=A^{\mathsf T}>0\text{ with }\operatorname{tr}A=1,\qquad
G_A>C\bigl[n\log2-S(q_A^\star)+H(r_A^\star)\bigr].
\]

In particular, no absolute finite coefficient has the quantifier order required by the registered subgoal. A negative coefficient also fails, since the gap is positive and the allowance is nonnegative.

## Proof

We first establish the support and extension hypotheses in the displayed coordinates. For an integer \(h\), define

\[
P_h=\{(x,y):y-hx\le1,\ (1-h)x+y\le1,\ (1-2h)x+2y\ge0\}.
\]

This is the triangle with vertices \(\mathbf a_h,\mathbf b_h,\mathbf c\). To enumerate its lattice points, put \(z=y-hx\), an integer whenever \((x,y)\) is integral. The inequalities become \(z\le1\), \(x+z\le1\), and \(x+2z\ge0\). They imply \(-1\le z\le1\). At \(z=-1,0,1\), the possible integral \(x\)-coordinates are respectively \(2\), \(0,1\), and \(-2,-1,0\). Substituting \(y=z+hx\) gives exactly \(D_h\), so \(P_h\cap\mathbb Z^2=D_h\). This calculation only enumerates the specified sites; the quadratic objective is not changed.

Write \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). Every last coordinate of every point of \(P\) lies in \([0,1]\). If a point of \(P\) has a binary last-coordinate vector \(\boldsymbol\eta\), every point used with positive coefficient in any convex representation of it must have that same vector: a convex combination with coordinate zero uses only zeros, and one with coordinate one uses only ones. It follows that
\(P\cap(\mathbb R^2\times\{\boldsymbol\eta\})=P_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\times\{\boldsymbol\eta\}\).
Every integral last-coordinate vector in \(P\) is binary. The preceding two-dimensional enumeration therefore proves \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). The prescribed masses sum to one.

We verify the stronger exact realization stated above. The polytope \(C^{(k)}\) is the convex hull of finitely many rational points, and its projection onto the integer-coordinate space is \(P\). Fix \(\mathbf I=(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\eta)\in D\). A convex representation of any point of the fiber at \(\mathbf I\) uses only sites from that binary slice. Every lattice site in its triangle is either a vertex or an edge midpoint. At a vertex, all contributing base sites coincide with it. At an edge midpoint, all contributing base sites lie on that edge; the total coefficients on its two endpoints are equal. The height at the midpoint is at least the average endpoint height, because, before the common scaling by \(1/M\),

\[
\frac18\ge\frac{\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}}2,
\qquad
\frac14\ge\frac{\frac1{16}+\frac14}2.
\]

The first inequality applies at \(\mathbf0\), and the second at both \(\mathbf u_h\) and \(\mathbf v_h\). Thus every convex combination of the lifted generating points above \(\mathbf I\) has height at most \(p_{\mathbf I}\). The generating segment from \((\mathbf I,0)\) to \((\mathbf I,p_{\mathbf I})\) is already present. Hence the fiber is exactly \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). Every other integer fiber is empty since \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\).

The base \(P\) has dimension \(k+2\): differences within one triangular slice span the first two coordinates, and the projections of differences of its binary labels span the last \(k\) coordinates. The bottom of \(C^{(k)}\) contains \(P\times\{0\}\), and a positive-height generating point adds the final independent direction. Therefore this is a full-dimensional compact rational polytope in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\).

For a real \(x\in P\), let \(f(x)\) be the maximum height in its fiber. Compactness makes this a finite attained maximum. Convexity of \(C^{(k)}\) proves concavity of \(f\) on \(P\), by taking convex combinations of top fiber points. All heights are nonnegative. Therefore the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality shows that \(f\) is log-concave on \(P\). Extend it by zero off \(P\). When an endpoint lies off \(P\), the required log-concavity right-hand side is zero; otherwise use convexity of \(P\) and the preceding inequality. The exact fibers prove agreement with every lattice value of \(p\), including all zeros. This proves the globally extendable table hypothesis in precisely the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, without changing a source capacity.

We next verify the structural facts needed for the actual quadratic objective. For any finite lattice-convex support and positive source law, the diagonal array is feasible and each feasible coordinate lies in \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). Thus \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, compact, and convex. For every real symmetric positive-definite \(A\), its minimizing face is also nonempty, compact, and convex. The entropy \(H\) is continuous there and strictly concave in the array, because each summand \(-t\log(t/p_{\mathbf I})\) is strictly concave on \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\), including a segment with a zero endpoint. Hence its maximizer exists and is unique.

For completeness, let \(\mathcal T_A\) be the coordinates positive in some \(V_A\)-minimizer. Average one such minimizer for every coordinate in this finite set, obtaining \(r^\circ\) positive exactly on \(\mathcal T_A\). If an entropy maximizer vanished at one of these coordinates, mixing it with \(r^\circ\) would produce a positive entropy increment of the form \(-b t\log t+O(t)\), with \(b>0\), contradicting maximality for small positive \(t\). Thus its positive row set \(\mathcal J_A\) is exactly the union of the positive row sets throughout the minimizing face.

Suppose that an integral \(\mathbf K\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J_A)\) has a representation \(\mathbf K=\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J\) with positive coefficients of sum one and at least one used \(\mathbf J\ne\mathbf K\). Lattice convexity gives \(\mathbf K\in D\). Remove a subrow of mass \(\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathbf J}\), proportional to row \(\mathbf J\) of \(r^\circ\), from each used row, choosing \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small. Add their sum to row \(\mathbf K\). Column sums and all row moments are preserved. The decrease of the actual objective is

\[
\varepsilon\left[\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf J
-\mathbf K^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf K\right]
=\varepsilon\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}
(\mathbf J-\mathbf K)^{\mathsf T}A(\mathbf J-\mathbf K)>0.
\]

This contradiction proves that every member of \(\mathcal J_A\) is a vertex and that \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J_A)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J_A\). Distinct targets with the same parity would have an integral midpoint, again contradicting the displayed strict decrease. Therefore \(|\mathcal J_A|\le2^n\). These are direct positive-definite versions of the needed support conclusions of accepted fact `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`. They do not assume Euclidean invariance or a full-rank support. We do not require or assume any additional generalized row log-concavity theorem.

Return to the constructed table. A row target \((\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\eta)\) has binary last coordinates. For a last coordinate equal to zero, all its source-coordinate contributions to the row moment are nonnegative; for one equal to one, they are nonpositive. Their sum is zero in either case. Consequently every positive entry in this row has the same last-coordinate vector \(\boldsymbol\eta\). Every feasible array is therefore block diagonal across these slices.

For the slice indexed by \(h=h(\boldsymbol\eta)\), define the normalized source law and array by
\(p^{[h]}_{\mathbf z}=M p_{(\mathbf z,\boldsymbol\eta)}\) and
\(r^{[h]}_{\mathbf x,\mathbf z}=M r_{(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\eta),(\mathbf z,\boldsymbol\eta)}\).
Their indices lie in \(D_h\). These are precisely probability source laws and feasible barycentric arrays with masses \(1/16,1/16,1/8,1/4,1/4,1/4\) in the stated locations. Conversely, any independent choices of such feasible slice arrays give a full feasible array by dividing their entries by \(M\). Write \(q^{[h]}\) for their normalized row masses, and use \(H^{[h]}\) and \(S(q^{[h]})\) for the same entropy formulas in that slice.

Partition the actual matrix according to the first two and last \(k\) coordinates:

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}B&T\\T^{\mathsf T}&E\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta&\gamma\end{pmatrix}>0.
\]

The principal block \(B\) is positive definite. For a target \((\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\eta)\), its quadratic value is
\(\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf x+2\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}T\boldsymbol\eta+\boldsymbol\eta^{\mathsf T}E\boldsymbol\eta\).
In each normalized slice, summing all row moment equations gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf x}q^{[h]}_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x=\sum_{\mathbf z}p^{[h]}_{\mathbf z}\mathbf z\), and the total mass is one. The terms containing \(T\) and \(E\) thus have fixed sums on that slice. It follows that

\[
V_A(r)=\frac1M\sum_{h=0}^{M-1}V_B^{[h]}(r^{[h]})
+\text{a constant independent of }r,
\qquad
V_B^{[h]}(r^{[h]})=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_h}q^{[h]}_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf x.
\]

Since the feasible choices are independent across slices, a full array minimizes \(V_A\) if and only if each normalized slice minimizes this actual \(B\)-objective. Moreover,
\(H(r)=M^{-1}\sum_h H^{[h]}(r^{[h]})\), because both the source and array entries were divided by the same \(M\). Its unique maximum on the product minimizing face consequently uses the unique entropy maximizer in each slice. No off-diagonal part of the full matrix changes this conclusion.

We now describe the entire minimizing face in each slice for every positive-definite \(B\). The affine inequalities defining \(P_h\) first determine all possible row supports. At a target on a zero face of a nonnegative affine functional, the row mean makes the sum of that functional over its source row zero, so every positive source lies on that face. The three vertex rows receive only their own sources. The rows at \(\mathbf0,\mathbf u_h,\mathbf v_h\) receive only \(\{\mathbf a_h,\mathbf0,\mathbf b_h\}\), \(\{\mathbf a_h,\mathbf u_h,\mathbf c\}\), and \(\{\mathbf b_h,\mathbf v_h,\mathbf c\}\), respectively. Each of \(\mathbf0,\mathbf u_h,\mathbf v_h\) as a source can enter only its own row.

Using \(\mathbf0=(\mathbf a_h+\mathbf b_h)/2\), \(\mathbf u_h=(\mathbf a_h+\mathbf c)/2\), and \(\mathbf v_h=(\mathbf b_h+\mathbf c)/2\), the row means force equal endpoint entries in each nonvertex row. Thus, with \(s=1/16\), every feasible normalized slice array has exactly the following form, with all unlisted entries zero:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf0,\mathbf0}&=2s,
&r^{[h]}_{\mathbf0,\mathbf a_h}=r^{[h]}_{\mathbf0,\mathbf b_h}&=t_0,\\
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf u_h,\mathbf u_h}&=4s,
&r^{[h]}_{\mathbf u_h,\mathbf a_h}=r^{[h]}_{\mathbf u_h,\mathbf c}&=t_1,\\
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf v_h,\mathbf v_h}&=4s,
&r^{[h]}_{\mathbf v_h,\mathbf b_h}=r^{[h]}_{\mathbf v_h,\mathbf c}&=t_2,\\
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf a_h,\mathbf a_h}&=s-t_0-t_1,
&r^{[h]}_{\mathbf b_h,\mathbf b_h}&=s-t_0-t_2,\\
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf c,\mathbf c}&=4s-t_1-t_2,
&0\le t_0\le s,\quad 0\le t_1,t_2&\le s-t_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last bounds imply \(t_1+t_2\le2s\), so no additional constraint is needed for the \(\mathbf c\) entry. Conversely, each such triple gives the prescribed column sums, nonnegative entries, and row means. This is a direct derivation for all the displayed coordinates; the \(h=0\) specialization agrees with the full feasible-set parametrization in accepted fact `6aa711c2cd5e2a52`.

Relative to the diagonal array, the decrease of the \(B\)-objective is

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_B^{[h]}(r_{\mathrm{diag}}^{[h]})-V_B^{[h]}(r^{[h]})
&=b_0t_0+b_1t_1+b_2t_2,\\
b_0&=2(\mathbf u_h-\mathbf v_h)^{\mathsf T}B(\mathbf u_h-\mathbf v_h),\\
b_1&=2\mathbf v_h^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf v_h>0,
\qquad b_2=2\mathbf u_h^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf u_h>0,\\
b_0-b_1-b_2&=-4\mathbf u_h^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf v_h.
\end{aligned}
\]

For example, the first coefficient is the sum of the two endpoint quadratic values minus twice the midpoint value; all three coefficients follow by this same direct identity. For each fixed \(t_0\), strict positivity of \(b_1,b_2\) forces \(t_1=t_2=s-t_0\) at a minimizer. Put \(\sigma_h=\mathbf u_h^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf v_h\). The remaining objective is affine in \(t_0\), so the entire minimizing face is specified by

\[
t_1=t_2=s-t_0,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
t_0=s,&\sigma_h<0,\\
t_0=0,&\sigma_h>0,\\
0\le t_0\le s,&\sigma_h=0.
\end{cases}
\]

The entropy selection on the last segment exists and is unique by the strict-concavity argument already given. Our estimates below hold at every point of that segment, so no unstated tie rule is needed. At every minimizer, the positive targets are exactly \(\mathbf0,\mathbf u_h,\mathbf v_h,\mathbf c\), and their masses are

\[
q^{[h]}_{\mathbf0}=q^{[h]}_{\mathbf c}=2s+2t_0,
\qquad
q^{[h]}_{\mathbf u_h}=q^{[h]}_{\mathbf v_h}=6s-2t_0.
\]

In particular, when \(\sigma_h<0\), the full minimizing face is the singleton with \(t_0=s\), \(t_1=t_2=0\). Every source goes to exactly one target, every positive assignment share equals one, and the four target masses are \(1/4\). Therefore
\(H^{[h]}=0\), \(S(q^{[h]})=2\log2\), and the slice allowance \(2\log2-S(q^{[h]})+H^{[h]}\) is zero.

At an arbitrary slice minimizer, the original potential has source mean \((3/8)\log2\): the two endpoints have mass \(s\) and potential \(2\log2\), and the source at zero has mass \(2s\) and potential \(\log2\). Among the four positive targets, only zero has nonzero original potential. Thus its original averaged gap, using the original slice table \(p^{[h]}\), is exactly

\[
G^{[h]}=\left(\frac38-q^{[h]}_{\mathbf0}\right)\log2
=\left(\frac14-2t_0\right)\log2
\in\left[\frac18\log2,\frac14\log2\right].
\]

For any of these slice minimizers, \(H^{[h]}\ge0\) since every assignment share lies in \([0,1]\), and \(S(q^{[h]})\le\log4\) since there are four targets. Also

\[
S(q^{[h]})-H^{[h]}
=\sum_{\mathbf x:q^{[h]}_{\mathbf x}>0}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_h}
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf x,\mathbf z}
\log\frac{r^{[h]}_{\mathbf x,\mathbf z}}{q^{[h]}_{\mathbf x}p^{[h]}_{\mathbf z}}
\ge0.
\]

The last inequality is finite relative-entropy nonnegativity. Directly, sum \(a\log(a/b)-a+b\ge0\) over these entries, with \(a=r^{[h]}_{\mathbf x,\mathbf z}\), \(b=q^{[h]}_{\mathbf x}p^{[h]}_{\mathbf z}>0\), and use that both arrays have total mass one. The scalar inequality follows from the minimum of \(t\log t-t+1\). The same argument against the uniform law proves the entropy bound \(S(q^{[h]})\le\log4\). We have therefore proved, including the tied face,

\[
0\le2\log2-S(q^{[h]})+H^{[h]}\le2\log2.
\]

It remains to control all quadratic matrices simultaneously. Positive definiteness of \(B\) gives \(\gamma>0\) and \(\alpha\gamma-\beta^2>0\). Define \(x_h=h+\beta/\gamma\) and \(\delta=(\alpha\gamma-\beta^2)/\gamma^2>0\). Direct evaluation in the original coordinates gives

\[
\sigma_h=\mathbf u_h^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf v_h
=\gamma\bigl[x_h-x_h^2-\delta\bigr].
\]

If \(\sigma_h\ge0\), then \(x_h(1-x_h)\ge\delta>0\), so \(0<x_h<1\). For distinct integral indices \(h\), the numbers \(x_h\) differ by a nonzero integer. At most one of them can belong to the open interval \((0,1)\). Consequently, for every positive-definite \(B\), all but at most one of the \(M\) slices satisfy \(\sigma_h<0\) and have zero slice allowance. This argument includes every tied case \(\sigma_h=0\). The strict inequality \(\delta>0\) comes from the actual admissible matrix, not a limiting singular matrix.

Finally apply the preceding conclusions to the entropy-selected normalized slices of \(r_A^\star\). The last-coordinate label has mass \(1/M\) for every \(\boldsymbol\eta\), and assignments retain it exactly. The definitions therefore give

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(q_A^\star)&=\log M+\frac1M\sum_{h=0}^{M-1}S(q^{[h]}),\\
H(r_A^\star)&=\frac1M\sum_{h=0}^{M-1}H^{[h]},\\
n\log2-S(q_A^\star)+H(r_A^\star)
&=\frac1M\sum_{h=0}^{M-1}
\bigl[2\log2-S(q^{[h]})+H^{[h]}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

The last identity uses \(n=k+2\) and \(\log M=k\log2\). All summands are nonnegative, and at most one is nonzero; each is at most \(2\log2\). This proves the claimed allowance bounds. The common scaling by \(1/M\) changes neither the logarithmic gap in a slice nor any assignment share. Hence
\(G_A=M^{-1}\sum_hG^{[h]}\ge(\log2)/8\), proving the claimed gap bound.

For \(M>16C\), these two estimates give
\(C[n\log2-S(q_A^\star)+H(r_A^\star)]\le2C\log2/M<\log2/8\le G_A\)
for every positive-definite trace-one \(A\). The trace normalization was never needed beyond admissibility: the proof applies to every positive-definite \(A\), with its actual principal block and all its actual off-diagonal entries. This completes the required quantifier negation while keeping the source table, its capacities, its zeros, and its lattice fixed for every choice of selector.
