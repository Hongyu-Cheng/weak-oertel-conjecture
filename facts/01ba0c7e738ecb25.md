## Statement

## Proof

Accepted fact `6748814480d5deaf`, with its scale \(L=n\), gives the rational polytope, its complete lattice fiber table, and the zero depth of every point in a boundary singleton fiber. Its continuous dilation assertion gives (1)-(2), multiplying every fiber volume and ambient halfspace mass by \(d^d\). The halfspace correspondence under this invertible dilation preserves relative local depths and all ratios \(h_S/W\). This verifies the normalization maps in the statement without importing a separate geometric family. The origin fiber has volume \(\Omega_d>0\), and the bounded support is finite.

We first prove the local mass estimate needed for (6). Every lattice point of \([-n,n]^n\) lies in \(D_+\) and has \(A_{\mathbf z}\ge1-1/n\). Since \(\log(1-t)=-\int_0^t(1-u)^{-1}\,du\ge-2t\) for \(0\le t\le1/2\),
\[
W\ge\Omega_d(2n+1)^n(1-1/n)^{n^2}
\ge\Omega_d(2n+1)^n e^{-2n},
\qquad
T_n\ge\Omega_d(n+\tfrac12)^n e^{-2n}.
\tag{12}
\]
For any real center \(\mathbf J\) and radius \(b\ge0\), the closed unit cubes centered at lattice points in the ball of radius \(b\) have disjoint interiors and lie in the concentric ball of radius \(b+\sqrt n/2\). If \(v_n\) is the Euclidean unit-ball volume, their number is at most \(v_n(b+\sqrt n/2)^n\). For even \(n=2m\), \(v_n=\pi^m/m!\le(2\pi e/n)^{n/2}\). Here the factorial inequality follows from \(\log(m!)\ge\int_1^m\log t\,dt\ge m\log m-m\), including \(m=1\). Since every fiber mass is at most \(\Omega_d\), (12) yields, for every \(L\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sum_{\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2\le n\sqrt L}w_{\mathbf I}}{T_n}
&\le e^{2n}v_n
\left(\frac{n\sqrt L+\sqrt n/2}{n+1/2}\right)^n\\
&\le\left[
e^2\sqrt{2\pi e}\left(\sqrt{\frac Ln}+\frac1{2n}\right)
\right]^n
\le F_n(L).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The last constant is rigorous: \(e<3\) and \(\pi<4\) give \(e^2\sqrt{2\pi e}<9\sqrt{24}<45\). In particular this estimate includes \(L=0\).

Fix a component. Expanding its weighted squared distances and using its base barycenter gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2^2
=\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I\|_2^2-\|\mathbf J\|_2^2\).
Substituting the formula for \(A_{\mathbf I}\) proves the first equality of (5). Its right side is positive because its left side is a positive weighted average of positive source radii. Also \(w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}=\Omega_d^{1/d}A_{\mathbf I}\); the factor \(\Omega_d\) therefore cancels in the root-volume ratio (4), proving its exact logarithmic identity and nonnegativity. The same equality, read as
\(A_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}A_{\mathbf I}+R^{-2}\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2^2>0\), shows that any integer barycenter of positive-volume sources lies in \(D_+\). A central measure at any other target is zero because it is dominated by its zero-volume or empty fiber. If a zero-volume source is included in a purported positive-mass component, its marginal is dominated by the zero measure and hence the component has mass zero. These observations justify the statement for every integer target and the omission of all zero-volume normalizations.

For \(L_a\le L\), the variance identity gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_a}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2^2
=R^2A_{\mathbf J}(1-e^{-L_a/d})
\le R^2A_{\mathbf J}(1-e^{-L/d})
\le n^2L.
\tag{14}
\]
The last inequality uses \(A_{\mathbf J}\le1\), \(1-e^{-u}\le u\), and \(R^2/d=n^2\). Since the coefficients are positive and sum to one, at least one source of every such component lies in the closed ball of radius \(n\sqrt L\) about \(\mathbf J\).

Integrating (3) over the whole continuous space gives exactly the scalar capacities of `793210f8a4be3647`:
\[
\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}x_\circ
+\sum_{a:\mathbf I\in E_a}x_a\le w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{15}
\]
Every marginal of a finite joint measure has its entire mass \(x_a\), so no factor \(\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}\) occurs here. The ball contains the central target. Counting every low-cost component at all of its sources in that ball, at least one of which exists by (14), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_\circ+\sum_{a:L_a\le L}x_a
&\le
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D_+\\\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2\le n\sqrt L}}
\left(\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}x_\circ
+\sum_{\substack{a:\mathbf I\in E_a\\L_a\le L}}x_a\right)\\
&\le\sum_{\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2\le n\sqrt L}w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Together with (13), this proves (6). The sum in (15) includes every component sharing a source, including recurring supports with different coefficients; (16) never resets a source budget. If a source occurs repeatedly within a tuple, summing its coefficients preserves the variance and root-volume identities, while summing all its marginal occurrences charges a multiple of \(x_a\). This strengthens (15) when written with its distinct source set. Applying the same containment argument to the original tuple preserves (5), so the repeated-source version has the identical bounds. Deleting a zero coefficient removes its unused coordinate and weakly decreases source usage without changing the output or its cost.

For \(n\ge512\) and \(a_{\mathrm{cut}}=2^{-16}\),
\(45\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{cut}}}+45/(2n)\le45/256+45/1024=225/1024<1/4\).
This proves the explicit cutoff conclusion. If \(L_n/n\to0\), the base in the power defining \(F_n(L_n)\) tends to zero, so the uniform normalized supremum also tends to zero. For an unrestricted kernel, (6) at cutoff \(a_{\mathrm{cut}}n\) implies
\(\sum_{a:L_a>a_{\mathrm{cut}}n}x_a\ge M-4^{-n}T_n\).
Nonnegative costs then give
\(S_{\mathrm{cost}}\ge a_{\mathrm{cut}}n\sum_{a:L_a>a_{\mathrm{cut}}n}x_a\ge a_{\mathrm{cut}}n(M-4^{-n}T_n)\).
Dividing by \(M\ge cT_n>0\) proves the first inequality in (7). It remains valid when its right side is negative.

The homothetic containment is pointwise and requires no special coupling. If \(\mathbf y_{\mathbf I}\in C_{\mathbf I}\), then its weighted output is coordinatewise nonnegative and satisfies
\[
\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d
\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_a}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}y_{\mathbf I,j}\right)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_a}\lambda_{a,\mathbf I}A_{\mathbf I}
=A_{\mathbf J}e^{-L_a/d}.
\tag{17}
\]
By the exact simplex fiber in (2), this is precisely membership in \(e^{-L_a/d}C_{\mathbf J}\). Integrating proves the last clause of (5) for every joint measure. In particular all outputs lie in \(C_{\mathbf J}\).

Let \(\mathcal A=e^{-a_{\mathrm{cut}}n/d}C_{\mathbf J}\). It has uniform target probability \(q=U_{\mathbf J}(\mathcal A)=e^{-a_{\mathrm{cut}}n}\in(0,1)\), by the \(d\)-dimensional homothety. Every component of cost greater than \(a_{\mathrm{cut}}n\) is supported in \(\mathcal A\). Consequently, for \(P=\kappa_{\mathbf J}/M\) and \(M\ge cT_n\),
\(p=P(\mathcal A)\ge1-4^{-n}T_n/M\ge1-4^{-n}/c\).
If \(P\not\ll U_{\mathbf J}\), the asserted relative-entropy lower bound is immediate from its definition. If its relative entropy is infinite despite absolute continuity, the conclusion is again immediate. In the remaining case, apply conditional Jensen to the convex function \(t\log t\) for its density on \(\mathcal A\) and its complement. With \(0\log0=0\), this gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})
&\ge p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}\\
&=p\log\frac1q+(1-p)\log\frac1{1-q}
+p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p)\\
&\ge p\,a_{\mathrm{cut}}n-\log2
\ge\left(1-\frac{4^{-n}}c\right)a_{\mathrm{cut}}n-\log2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The binary entropy bound used here follows by convexity, since \(p\log p+(1-p)\log(1-p)\) has minimum \(-\log2\) at \(p=1/2\). The endpoint cases \(p=0,1\) are included by continuity. This proves the actual output statement, without a density regularity assumption or a choice of favorable couplings.

We next verify the explicit kernel. The equality \(A_{\mathbf z}=A_{-\mathbf z}\) gives \(C_{\mathbf z}=C_{-\mathbf z}\) and \(\mu_{\mathbf z}=\mu_{-\mathbf z}\) as measures on the continuous space. In (8) both marginals are therefore the exact actual source measures. Every nonzero positive source is used by its one selected pair, and source zero is used only by \(\sigma=\mu_{\mathbf0}\). Thus (3) is an equality for every positive source. The midpoint of the two identical continuous coordinates is that same coordinate, so the stated \(\kappa\) is the actual output. Pairing equal weights proves \(M=w_{\mathbf0}+(W-w_{\mathbf0})/2=(W+w_{\mathbf0})/2\).

The finite mixture contains positive rational radii with positive rational masses. Its specified median \(\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}\) therefore exists, is rational, and lies in \((0,1]\). Minimality at the first attained radius whose lower cumulative mass reaches \(M/2\) implies
\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathcal R\cup\{\mathbf0\}\\A_{\mathbf z}\le\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}}}w_{\mathbf z}\ge M/2,
\qquad
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathcal R\cup\{\mathbf0\}\\A_{\mathbf z}\ge\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}}}w_{\mathbf z}\ge M/2.
\tag{19}
\]
For the second inequality, the mass strictly below this median is less than \(M/2\); ties are retained on both indicated sides. The point \(\mathbf y^\star=d\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}\mathbf1_d/(d+1)\) is the centroid of \(d\rho_{\mathrm{mix}}\Delta_d\subseteq C_{\mathbf0}\), so it is rational and \(\mathbf p^\star=(\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\in S\).

Fix any proper continuous closed halfspace through \(\mathbf y^\star\), written as \(G=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y^\star)\ge0\}\), with \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\). A mixture component has centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}=dA_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_d/(d+1)\), and
\(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}-\mathbf y^\star)=d(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf1_d)(A_{\mathbf z}-\rho_{\mathrm{mix}})/(d+1)\).
If \(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf1_d>0\), the upper median side in (19) supplies contained centroids of total mixture mass at least \(M/2\); if it is negative, the lower side does so; if it is zero, every centroid belongs to \(G\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, at full metronoid mass \(m=w_{\mathbf z}\), gives \(\mu_{\mathbf z}(G)\ge\gamma_d w_{\mathbf z}\) for each selected centroid. Summing proves \(\kappa(G)\ge\gamma_dM/2\). Any proper continuous closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) contains its parallel sub-halfspace through that point, so the same estimate holds for every such halfspace. The whole continuous space has mass \(M\) and also satisfies the estimate.

The transfer to ambient halfspaces is exact. Fix any ambient closed halfspace \(H\) and write \(H_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in H\}\). For every \(\mathbf z\in\mathcal R\) and every \(\mathbf y\), the midpoint relation gives
\(\mathbf1_{H_{\mathbf0}}(\mathbf y)\le\mathbf1_{H_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf y)+\mathbf1_{H_{-\mathbf z}}(\mathbf y)\): if both source scores in a linear inequality defining \(H\) were strictly below its threshold, their average would also be strictly below. Integrating against \(\mu_{\mathbf z}\), summing over the disjoint pairs, and including the central measure gives
\[
\kappa(H_{\mathbf0})
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_+}\mu_{\mathbf I}(H_{\mathbf I})
=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H).
\tag{20}
\]
If \(H\) contains the already fixed \(\mathbf p^\star\) and its continuous normal is nonzero, its target section is a proper continuous halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), so (20) is at least \(\gamma_dM/2\). If its continuous normal is zero, it is vertical and its target section is the whole continuous space, so (20) is at least \(M\). The whole ambient space, if included in the halfspace convention, has mass \(W\ge M\). Thus all containing ambient halfspaces obey the same lower bound, without discarding boundary mass. Since \(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\) gives \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\), and \(M\ge W/2\), (9) follows.

For each noncentral component of (8), the exact cost is \(L_{\mathbf z}=d\log(1/A_{\mathbf z})\), since its two equal root volumes have mean \(w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d}\) and \(w_{\mathbf0}=\Omega_d\). Each positive radius is \((R^2-\|\mathbf z\|_2^2)/R^2\), with a positive integer numerator, hence \(A_{\mathbf z}\ge R^{-2}=n^{-4}\). Thus every component cost is at most \(4n^2\log n\). The exact cutoff-packing characterization in accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647` applies to these scalar masses, whose shared capacities have just been verified with equality. It gives
\(P_d(\mathbf0,4n^2\log n)\ge M\).
Dividing by \(1+4n^2\log n\), then using \(M\ge W/2\), proves (10). The ratio tends to infinity because \(2^n/(n^2\log n)\to\infty\). This step establishes no assertion about the score of any other body.

Finally, if \(\mathbf y\in\mathsf M_{\eta w_{\mathbf J}}(C_{\mathbf J})\), accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives relative own-fiber depth at least \(\gamma_d\eta\). Apply the all-locally-deep-point estimate of `6748814480d5deaf` to its simplex family at construction scale \(n\), then transport it by the continuous dilation already fixed in (1). The normalization \(T_n=2^{-n}W\) is preserved in this ratio. Since \(\log(1/(\gamma_d\eta))=\log(1/\eta)+d\log(1+1/d)<\log(1/\eta)+1\), its bound is exactly at most the right side of (11). If \(\log(1/\eta_n)=o(n)\), the bracket in (11) tends to zero, proving the stated uniform metronoid consequence. No additional geometric obstruction is needed.
