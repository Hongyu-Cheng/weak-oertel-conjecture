## Statement

For an integer \(n\ge1\), an admissible table is a nonzero, nonnegative, finitely supported table \(w=(w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) with a finite-valued extension \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying \(f(z)=w_z\) at every lattice point, including every zero, and \(f((1-t)x+ty)\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(y)^t\) for all \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\). A zero endpoint makes the right side zero. This is the global-extension hypothesis of accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2.

For such a table let \(A=\{z:w_z>0\}\), \(W=\sum_z w_z>0\), \(M=\max_z w_z\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_j&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},&
H_*&=\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^n}H_j,\\
G&=\log(2^nH_*/W),&
\rho&=2^nM/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

All displacement sums are oriented and include zero; all logarithms are natural. The symbol \(2^n\) will not be renamed \(K\), which is reserved for the curvature score of the predecessors. The normalized maximum atom \(\rho\) is distinct from the center-dependent off-diagonal mass \(\rho_j\) in accepted fact b85cce72a986b40e. Define \(p_j=w_j/W\), \(h_j=H_j/W\), \(h=H_*/W\), \(\pi_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}p_z\), and \(q=\sum_{b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}\pi_b^2\). This scalar \(q\) is the parity-collision probability, distinct from the thinning coefficients \(q_v\) of accepted fact 280b299bc1e87c14. Put \(t_{j,v}=\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}\) and \(R_j=\sum_v t_{j,v}^2\). On positive reflected pairs use exactly the curvature \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))=\log(p_j^2/(p_{j+v}p_{j-v}))\) of that accepted fact.

(i) Let \(X,Y\) be independent with probability table \(p\), and let \(\Gamma\) be their joint law conditioned on \(X\equiv Y\pmod2\). Under \(\Gamma\), define \(J=(X+Y)/2\), \(V=(X-Y)/2\), and \(\Delta=\Delta_J(V)=\log(p_J^2/(p_Xp_Y))\ge0\). Denote the law of \(J\) by \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\). It is a discrete base-midpoint law, distinct from the continuous fiber-midpoint laws in accepted fact 280b299bc1e87c14. One has \(2^{-n}\le q\le h\le1\), hence \(0\le G\le n\log2\), and the exact nonnegative decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^G-1
={}&2^n\sum_b(\pi_b-2^{-n})^2\\
&+2^n\sum_jp_j(h-h_j)\\
&+2^n\sum_{j,v}t_{j,v}(p_j-t_{j,v}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here every displayed sum is finite. In particular,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p^{\mathrm{mid}}_j&=\frac{R_j}{q}
 \le\frac{h_j}{q}p_j\le\frac hq p_j\le e^Gp_j,\\
\operatorname{TV}(p^{\mathrm{mid}},p)
&\le1-\frac qh\le1-e^{-G},\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\|p)
&\le\log(h/q)\le G,\\
\mathbb E_\Gamma e^{\Delta/2}
&=\frac1q\sum_jp_jh_j\le\frac hq\le e^G,\\
\mathbb E_\Gamma\Delta&\le2\log(h/q)\le2G,\\
\Gamma\{\Delta>L\}&\le\frac{e^G-1}{e^{L/2}-1}\quad(L>0),\\
\Gamma\{X=Y\}&=\frac{\sum_jp_j^2}{q}
 \le\frac{M}{qW}\le\rho.
\end{aligned}
\]

The conventions are \(\operatorname{TV}(a,b)=\frac12\sum_j|a_j-b_j|\) and \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)=\sum_{j:a_j>0}a_j\log(a_j/b_j)\); the displayed domination guarantees its finiteness here. The curvature estimates concern \(\Gamma\), not every Hellinger displacement law at an individual center.

(ii) Within the admissible class, \(G=0\) if and only if \(p\) is uniform on a support containing exactly one point of every parity class. In particular, \(G=0\) implies \(\rho=1\). No Cartesian-product classification of these supports is asserted.

(iii) For every integer \(k\ge1\), the table \(w^{\otimes k}_{(z_1,\ldots,z_k)}=\prod_{i=1}^k w_{z_i}\) is admissible in integer dimension \(kn\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
W(w^{\otimes k})&=W(w)^k,&
M(w^{\otimes k})&=M(w)^k,\\
H_{(j_1,\ldots,j_k)}(w^{\otimes k})
&=\prod_{i=1}^kH_{j_i}(w),&
H_*(w^{\otimes k})&=H_*(w)^k,\\
G(w^{\otimes k})&=kG(w),&
\rho(w^{\otimes k})&=\rho(w)^k.
\end{aligned}
\]

The following three assertions are equivalent, with every constant independent of dimension and of the table:

(a) There exists \(\varepsilon>0\) such that every admissible table in every integer dimension \(n\ge1\) with \(0\le G\le\varepsilon\) satisfies \(\rho\ge1/2\).

(b) There exist \(g_0>0\) and \(a\in(0,1)\) such that every admissible table in every integer dimension \(n\ge1\) with \(0\le G\le g_0\) satisfies \(\rho\ge a\).

(c) There exist \(g_1>0\) and \(C_1>0\) such that every admissible table in every integer dimension \(n\ge1\) with \(0\le G\le g_1\) satisfies \((-\log\rho)_+\le C_1G\).

More precisely, (b) gives (a) with \(\varepsilon=g_0/k\) for any integer \(k\ge\max\{1,\lceil\log(1/a)/\log2\rceil\}\). Assertion (a) gives (c) with \(g_1=\varepsilon/2\) and \(C_1=2\log2/\varepsilon\). Assertion (c) gives (a) with \(\varepsilon=\min\{g_1,\log2/C_1\}\). Assertion (c) also implies \((1-\rho)_+\le C_1G\) on the same domain.

For any sequence of admissible tables \(w_i\), possibly in varying dimensions, suppose \(G_i=G(w_i)\to0\), \(\delta_i=-\log\rho(w_i)>0\), and \(G_i/\delta_i\to0\). Then \(G_i>0\) by (ii), and the integers \(k_i=\lceil(\delta_iG_i)^{-1/2}\rceil\) yield admissible tables \(\widetilde w_i=w_i^{\otimes k_i}\) satisfying \(G(\widetilde w_i)\to0\) and \(\rho(\widetilde w_i)\to0\). Consequently, failure of (a) is equivalent to the existence of an admissible sequence with these latter two limits. The statement does not assert that such a sequence exists.

(iv) Write \(n=m+r\) with integers \(m\ge0\) and \(r\ge1\). For \(b\in\mathbb Z^m\), let \(w^{[b]}_u=w_{(b,u)}\), \(u\in\mathbb Z^r\), and \(W^{[b]}=\sum_u w^{[b]}_u\). Suppose at most \(2^m\) slices have positive mass. Choose a slice \(b\) of maximum mass and put \(\lambda=2^mW^{[b]}/W\ge1\). The restricted table is admissible. Writing \(G_b,H_{b,*},M_b,\rho_b\) for its quantities in integer dimension \(r\), one has

\[
0\le G_b\le G-\log\lambda\le G,\qquad
1\le\lambda\le e^G,\qquad
\rho\ge\lambda\rho_b\ge\rho_b.
\]

For \(m=0\), there is one slice and \(\lambda=1\). These assertions are invariant under affine unimodular changes of the integer coordinates. Let \(g_{\mathrm{1D}}\in(0,1/100]\) be the constant \(g_0\) of accepted fact b85cce72a986b40e. If \(r=1\) and \(0\le G\le g_{\mathrm{1D}}\), then

\((1-\rho)_+\le(1-\rho_b)_+\le4G_b^2\le4G^2.\)

Equivalently, this estimate holds for every admissible table whose positive support is contained in at most \(2^{n-1}\) parallel lines with a common primitive integer direction and which meets this small-gain hypothesis.

(v) For an actual convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(n,d\ge1\), use the canonical fibers \(C_z\), weights \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), mixed set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), total mass \(W>0\), and depth \(h_S\) of accepted fact 280b299bc1e87c14. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. Accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2 makes this table admissible, including all its zeros.

For the slicing in (iv), let \(S_b=C\cap(\{b\}\times\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let \(h_{S_b}^{\mathrm{slice}}\) be its depth using closed halfspaces in the affine space \(\{b\}\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\), with the same sum of \(d\)-dimensional fiber volumes. Every fixed point \(y\in S_b\) satisfies \(h_S(y)\ge h_{S_b}^{\mathrm{slice}}(y)\). Thus if such a point has \(h_{S_b}^{\mathrm{slice}}(y)\ge c\,2^{-r}W^{[b]}\), for some \(c>0\), it also has

\(h_S(y)\ge c\lambda\,2^{-n}W\ge c\,2^{-n}W.\)

This conditional transfer holds even when the coordinate slice is not full-dimensional. To obtain a slice point from a theorem for convex bodies, one may first restrict to the affine hull of its positive integer bases and use its complete intersection lattice. The resulting actual body has integer dimension equal to that affine rank, preserves every positive fiber and its \(d\)-dimensional volume, and introduces no lattice-index multiplier. Rank zero is treated as a single positive fiber.

Independently of a slicing assumption, if \(M\ge2^{-n}W/2\), a largest fiber admits a point \(p^\star\) with

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{\ell M}{16\log2}
\ge\frac{\ell}{32\log2}\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad \ell=-\log(1-e^{-1}).
\]

The centroid \(c_j\) of any fiber \(C_j\) with \(w_j=M\) gives the stronger bound \(h_S((j,c_j))\ge M/e\ge2^{-n}W/(2e)\). In both conclusions the point is chosen before every containing ambient halfspace. In particular, both conclusions hold for the small-gain line-support class in (iv), because \(\rho\ge1-4G^2\ge1/2\). If \(W=0\) and the mixed set is nonempty, every point gives the zero bound without normalizing a table.

(vi) The global-extension assumption cannot be dropped from a conclusion that uses only parity balance, light normalized atoms, and conditional-midpoint near-stationarity. For every integer \(s\ge3\), define an exact finite table on \(\mathbb Z\) by \(w^{(s)}_k=\mathbf1_{\{|k|\le s^2\}}/(s^2+k^2)\), and let \(p^{(s)}\) be its normalization. Every nonzero weight is rational. With parity probabilities \(\pi_b^{(s)}\) and conditional-midpoint table \(p^{(s),\mathrm{mid}}\) defined as in (i), these tables satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\max_k p_k^{(s)}&\longrightarrow0,\\
2\sum_{b=0}^1(\pi_b^{(s)}-1/2)^2&\longrightarrow0,\\
\operatorname{TV}(p^{(s),\mathrm{mid}},p^{(s)})&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]

They fail lattice log-concavity and have Hellinger gain exactly \(\log2\). Therefore they are not admissible counterexamples to any of (iii)(a)-(c).

## Proof

First consider an admissible table. Global log-concavity gives \(t_{j,v}\le p_j\), including when an endpoint product is zero. In particular, every positive same-parity pair has a positive integer midpoint. A positive \(H_j\) can occur only at \(j\in A\), so the maximum defining \(H_*\) is over finitely many positive centers and is attained. At a positive center the zero displacement contributes \(w_j\), hence \(H_*\ge M>0\). Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the two reflected square-root sequences gives \(H_j\le W\), hence \(h\le1\).

The change of variables \(x=j+v,\ y=j-v\) is a bijection between ordered same-parity endpoint pairs and integer pairs \((j,v)\). Consequently \(\sum_jR_j=\sum_b\pi_b^2=q\). Also \(R_j=\sum_vt_{j,v}^2\le p_j\sum_vt_{j,v}=p_jh_j\le hp_j\). Summing gives \(q\le h\). Cauchy-Schwarz on the \(2^n\) parity probabilities gives \(q\ge2^{-n}\), proving the stated range of \(G\).

Expansion of the parity squares gives \(2^n\sum_b(\pi_b-2^{-n})^2=2^nq-1\). Further,

\[
\sum_{j,v}t_{j,v}(p_j-t_{j,v})
=\sum_jp_jh_j-\sum_jR_j
=\sum_jp_jh_j-q.
\]

The last two terms in the asserted decomposition therefore sum to \(2^n(h-q)\), and all three sum to \(2^nh-1=e^G-1\). Their nonnegativity follows from the definition of \(h\) and from \(t_{j,v}\le p_j\).

The conditioning event has probability \(q>0\). Reindexing its endpoint probabilities gives \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}_j=R_j/q\) and the pointwise bounds in (i). The table \(R_j/h\) is dominated by \(p\), because \(R_j\le hp_j\), and by \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\), because \(q\le h\). It has mass \(q/h\). The identity \(\operatorname{TV}(a,b)=1-\sum_j\min(a_j,b_j)\) for probability tables therefore gives \(\operatorname{TV}(p^{\mathrm{mid}},p)\le1-q/h\). As \(q/h=2^nq e^{-G}\ge e^{-G}\), this proves the TV assertion. Where \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}_j>0\), its density ratio against \(p\) is at most \(h/q\). Multiplying the logarithm of this bound by \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}_j\) and summing proves the relative-entropy assertion, including zero terms under the stated convention.

Under \(\Gamma\), the pair \((J,V)=(j,v)\) has probability \(t_{j,v}^2/q\). Only positive endpoint products occur, so \(p_J>0\), the curvature is defined, and \(e^{\Delta/2}=p_J/t_{J,V}\). This gives exactly \(\mathbb E_\Gamma e^{\Delta/2}=q^{-1}\sum_{j,v}p_jt_{j,v}=q^{-1}\sum_jp_jh_j\). Jensen's inequality for the exponential gives \(\mathbb E_\Gamma\Delta\le2\log\mathbb E_\Gamma e^{\Delta/2}\). Applying Markov's inequality to the nonnegative random variable \(e^{\Delta/2}-1\) gives the asserted tail estimate. Diagonal endpoint pairs have total conditional probability \(q^{-1}\sum_jp_j^2\), and \(\sum_jp_j^2\le(M/W)\sum_jp_j=M/W\). These observations prove all of (i).

Suppose next that \(G=0\). Then \(h=2^{-n}\) and \(q=h\), and equality in the parity-square identity gives \(\pi_b=2^{-n}\) for every parity class. Since \(R_j\le hp_j\) and the two sides have equal total mass, \(R_j=hp_j\) at every site. Thus \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}=p\). Moreover the endpoint marginal under \(\Gamma\) is

\(\Gamma\{X=j\}=p_j\pi_{j\bmod2}/q=p_j,\)

and the same holds for \(Y\). The three variables \(X,Y,J\) therefore have the same marginal table \(p\) under \(\Gamma\). Finite support makes their second moments finite, and the exact identity

\[
\left\|\frac{X+Y}{2}\right\|^2
=\frac{\|X\|^2+\|Y\|^2}{2}
 -\frac{\|X-Y\|^2}{4}
\]

implies \(\mathbb E_\Gamma\|X-Y\|^2=0\). If two distinct positive sites had the same parity, their ordered endpoint pair would have positive conditional probability \(p_xp_y/q\), a contradiction. Each parity class has positive mass \(2^{-n}\), so each has exactly one atom of that mass. Conversely, for a uniform support with one site of every parity, the only positive reflected endpoint pairs are diagonal. Hence \(H_j=w_j=W2^{-n}\) on its support and zero elsewhere, proving \(G=0\) and (ii).

For (iii), multiply the given extensions on the \(k\) coordinate blocks. The result is finite-valued and log-concave: when both endpoint products are positive multiply the \(k\) log-concavity inequalities, and when either product is zero the required inequality is immediate. On every lattice tuple it agrees with the product table, including tuples having a zero factor. The product support is finite. Factoring the finite sums proves the formulas for \(W\) and for every \(H_{(j_1,\ldots,j_k)}\). The maximum of a product of nonnegative factors, each depending on a separate center, is the product of their attained maxima. The formulas for \(M,H_*,G,\rho\) follow.

Assertion (a) implies (b) with \(a=1/2\). Conversely, assume (b) and choose \(k\) as in the statement. If \(G(w)\le g_0/k\), apply (b) to \(w^{\otimes k}\) to obtain \(\rho(w)^k\ge a\). Thus \(\rho(w)\ge a^{1/k}\ge1/2\), proving (a) with the stated domain.

Assume (a) and let \(0<G\le\varepsilon/2\). Put \(k=\lfloor\varepsilon/G\rfloor\). Then \(kG\le\varepsilon\), and \(k\ge\varepsilon/(2G)\), because \(\varepsilon/G\ge2\). Applying (a) to the product gives \(\rho^k\ge1/2\), and hence

\((-\log\rho)_+\le(\log2)/k\le(2\log2/\varepsilon)G.\)

At \(G=0\), part (ii) gives \(\rho=1\), so this same estimate holds with both sides zero. This proves (c). If (c) holds and \(G\le\min\{g_1,\log2/C_1\}\), then \((-\log\rho)_+\le\log2\), which implies \(\rho\ge1/2\). Also \(1-\rho\le-\log\rho\) for \(0<\rho\le1\), proving its stated deficit consequence.

For amplification, the hypotheses \(\delta_i>0\) mean \(\rho(w_i)<1\). The already proved equality classification rules out \(G_i=0\) at every such index. This explicitly handles the zero-gain case before using the formula for \(k_i\). For the positive gains,

\[
\begin{aligned}
G(\widetilde w_i)=k_iG_i
&\le\sqrt{G_i/\delta_i}+G_i\longrightarrow0,\\
-\log\rho(\widetilde w_i)=k_i\delta_i
&\ge\sqrt{\delta_i/G_i}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the normalized atoms tend to zero. If (a) fails, for each \(i\ge1\) choose an admissible table with \(0\le G_i\le1/i\) and \(\rho_i<1/2\). Again \(G_i>0\), and \(\delta_i>\log2\), so \(G_i/\delta_i\to0\); the construction applies. Conversely, a sequence with \(G\to0\) and \(\rho\to0\) eventually violates any proposed positive threshold neighborhood. No zero-gain counterexample has been silently discarded.

For (iv), the maximum slice has \(W^{[b]}\ge2^{-m}W>0\). The function \(u\mapsto f(b,u)\) is finite-valued and log-concave on all of \(\mathbb R^r\), and its values at every \(u\in\mathbb Z^r\) are exactly \(w^{[b]}_u\). In particular its zero values are preserved; this is a restriction of the global extension, not a subsampling assertion.

For every \(r\)-dimensional center \(j\), the slice reflection sum is the part of the ambient sum at \((b,j)\) having outer displacement zero. All other summands are nonnegative, so \(H_{b,*}\le H_*\). The definitions give

\[
e^{G_b}=\frac{2^rH_{b,*}}{W^{[b]}}
\le\frac{2^{m+r}H_*}{\lambda W}
=\frac{e^G}{\lambda},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{2^{m+r}M}{W}
\ge\lambda\frac{2^rM_b}{W^{[b]}}
=\lambda\rho_b.
\]

Part (i) applies to the nonzero restricted table and gives \(G_b\ge0\). Taking logarithms proves all the slicing assertions, including \(\lambda\le e^G\).

For \(r=1\), the positive support of \(w^{[b]}\) is a nonempty consecutive integer interval. Indeed, between any two positive sites global log-concavity gives positivity at every intervening real and integer point. Applying the extension at consecutive integer midpoints gives the discrete log-concavity inequalities required by accepted fact b85cce72a986b40e. Its table is the present \(w^{[b]}\); its \(W,M,H_j,H_*,g\) are respectively \(W^{[b]},M_b,H_{b,j},H_{b,*},G_b\). For \(0<G_b\le g_{\mathrm{1D}}\) that fact gives \((1-\rho_b)_+\le4G_b^2\). For \(G_b=0\), its equality statement gives two equal adjacent atoms and \(\rho_b=1\), so the same bound holds. Since \(\rho\ge\rho_b\) and \(0\le G_b\le G\), the asserted quadratic transfer follows.

An affine change \(z=z_0+Uu\), where \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(U\) is an integer unimodular matrix, is a bijection of the lattice and takes integer displacements bijectively to integer displacements. Pulling back the extension and reindexing the sums preserves \(W,M,H_*,G,\rho\). A primitive integer direction can be sent to a coordinate vector by integer row operations implementing the Euclidean algorithm; these operations are unimodular, since coordinate swaps, sign changes, and additions of an integer multiple of one coordinate to another all have integer inverses. Thus a common primitive direction can be made the last coordinate direction. Every parallel line meeting \(\mathbb Z^n\) then has fixed outer integer coordinates, and its lattice points form one full copy of \(\mathbb Z\). This proves the invariant line-support formulation without an index factor.

For the actual-body statements, accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2 proves that \(x\mapsto\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)\), with zero assigned also to lower-dimensional real fibers, is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension. Its restriction to any affine base slice consequently retains all zeros as above.

Fix a point \(y\in S_b\) before choosing an ambient closed halfspace. Such a halfspace, when restricted to \(\{b\}\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\), is a relative closed halfspace or the whole affine space, since it contains \(y\). Its mass in the selected slice is at least \(h_{S_b}^{\mathrm{slice}}(y)\); the whole-space case also has this property, because that depth is at most \(W^{[b]}\). Its full mixed mass is at least its slice mass. Taking the infimum over the ambient halfspaces proves \(h_S(y)\ge h_{S_b}^{\mathrm{slice}}(y)\). Finally \(2^{-r}W^{[b]}=\lambda2^{-n}W\), proving the conditional transfer at the same point.

We give the affine-rank justification needed if a theorem for convex bodies is to supply this slice point. Let \(A_b=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:w_{(b,u)}>0\}\), choose \(u_0\in A_b\), and let \(E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(A_b-u_0)\) have dimension \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}\). The group \(E\cap\mathbb Z^r\) is saturated and has a lattice basis of \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}\) real-linearly independent vectors, as established in the primitive-lattice construction of accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2. With these basis vectors as columns of \(U\), the map \(\phi(t)=u_0+Ut\) is an affine bijection \(\mathbb R^{r_{\mathrm{aff}}}\to\operatorname{aff}A_b\) and takes \(\mathbb Z^{r_{\mathrm{aff}}}\) onto the entire intersection \(\operatorname{aff}A_b\cap\mathbb Z^r\).

When \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}\ge1\), consider

\(D=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb R^{r_{\mathrm{aff}}}\times\mathbb R^d:(b,\phi(t),x)\in C\}.\)

It is compact and convex. It has nonempty interior in this ambient space: choose \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}+1\) affinely independent positive integer bases in \(A_b\), choose \(d+1\) affinely independent points of the positive \(d\)-dimensional fiber at the first base, and choose one point in each of the other positive fibers. Their images in \(D\) are \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}+d+1\) affinely independent points. Their convex hull is a full-dimensional simplex contained in \(D\). Thus \(D\) is an actual convex body in integer dimension \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}\) and continuous dimension \(d\).

Every positive integer fiber in the original slice is present in \(D\), with exactly the same vertical coordinates. Conversely every integer base of \(D\) corresponds to an integer base of that slice. Those outside \(A_b\) have zero volume. Integer fibers of the slice outside \(\operatorname{aff}A_b\) also have zero volume by the definition of \(A_b\). Therefore all mixed masses of corresponding halfspace cuts agree after pullback. No Jacobian is applied to the fiber volume: the continuous coordinate \(x\) is unchanged. A bound \(c\,2^{-r_{\mathrm{aff}}}W^{[b]}\) for a point of \(D\) transfers to at least that bound in the coordinate slice, and it is at least \(c\,2^{-r}W^{[b]}\). If \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}=0\), the only positive integer base is \(u_0\), and the positive compact convex fiber there is the required \(d\)-dimensional body; its centroid gives depth at least \(W^{[b]}/e\). Zero-volume fibers elsewhere do not affect these comparisons. This handles the possible degeneracy of the coordinate slice without applying a full-dimensional body theorem outside its domain.

For the first heavy-atom consumer, choose a largest actual fiber \(j\). Apply the even-thinning assertion of accepted fact 280b299bc1e87c14 with \(q_0=M\) and \(q_v=0\) for \(v\ne0\). These coefficients are even, satisfy \(0\le q_v\le\alpha(v)=\min(w_{j+v},w_{j-v})\), and have \(M_q=M>0\). Their average curvature is \(\overline\Delta_{j,q}=0\), because \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). With the identical actual fibers, weights, and oriented displacement convention, that accepted fact supplies a single point with \(h_S(p^\star)\ge\ell M/(16\log2)\). Substituting the assumed atom bound proves the displayed conclusion.

For the centroid consumer, \(C_j\) is compact, convex, and has positive \(d\)-dimensional volume \(M\). Grünbaum's centroid inequality gives, for every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\) through its centroid, a volume fraction at least \((d/(d+1))^d\ge e^{-1}\). The last inequality follows from \(\log(1+1/d)\le1/d\). A containing fiber halfspace contains a parallel one through the centroid. An ambient halfspace containing \((j,c_j)\) cuts the central fiber in such a containing halfspace or in the entire fiber, so its mixed volume is at least \(M/e\). This includes vertical ambient halfspaces and places the point before the halfspace. The small-gain line-support conclusion follows because \(G\le g_{\mathrm{1D}}\le1/100\) implies \(1-4G^2\ge1/2\).

It remains to verify the Cauchy obstruction (vi). All statements in this paragraph and the remaining Cauchy argument concern this separate one-dimensional family; global log-concavity is not assumed for it. For a real \(s>0\), the partial-fraction expansions of the hyperbolic cotangent and cosecant, evaluated at the positive real number \(\pi s\), give

\[
Z_s:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\frac1{s^2+k^2}
=\frac{\pi}{s}\coth(\pi s),\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\frac{(-1)^k}{s^2+k^2}
=\frac{\pi}{s}\operatorname{csch}(\pi s).
\]

Both series converge absolutely. Define a probability table on all of \(\mathbb Z\) by \(a_k^{(s)}=1/[Z_s(s^2+k^2)]\). The difference of its even and odd masses is \(\operatorname{sech}(\pi s)\). Write \(\epsilon_s=\operatorname{sech}^2(\pi s)\).

We derive the midpoint convolution identity, including its exceptional central term. For an integer \(j\ne0\), elementary algebra gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{(s^2+(j+v)^2)(s^2+(j-v)^2)}\\
&\quad=\frac1{2(s^2+j^2)}
\left[
\frac{1+v/(2j)}{s^2+(j+v)^2}
+\frac{1-v/(2j)}{s^2+(j-v)^2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

For completeness, after multiplication by the product of the two denominators, the bracket becomes \(2(s^2+j^2)\); this checks the identity exactly. The symmetric sums of \(v/(s^2+(v+j)^2)\) and \(v/(s^2+(v-j)^2)\) tend respectively to \(-jZ_s\) and \(jZ_s\). Indeed, shift the variable and write \(v=(v+j)-j\) or \(v=(v-j)+j\). The odd function \(k/(s^2+k^2)\) has zero symmetric sum; shifting its summation interval by the fixed integer \(j\) changes that sum by only finitely many endpoint terms tending to zero. The remaining absolutely convergent denominator sum tends to \(Z_s\). Summing the algebraic identity symmetrically, whose left side is absolutely summable, therefore gives

\(\sum_v[(s^2+(j+v)^2)(s^2+(j-v)^2)]^{-1}=Z_s/[2(s^2+j^2)]\quad(j\ne0).\)

At \(j=0\), differentiation of the normalization formula gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_v\frac1{(s^2+v^2)^2}
&=-\frac{Z_s'}{2s}\\
&=\frac{Z_s}{2s^2}
 +\frac{\pi^2}{2s^2}\operatorname{csch}^2(\pi s).
\end{aligned}
\]

Termwise differentiation is justified on every compact interval in \(s>0\): the derivatives of the tails are uniformly bounded by a constant times \(k^{-4}\). Dividing these formulas by \(Z_s^2\) proves the exact identity

\[
\sum_v a_{j+v}^{(s)}a_{j-v}^{(s)}
=\frac12a_j^{(s)}+\frac12\epsilon_s\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}.
\]

Thus the parity-collision probability of \(a^{(s)}\) is \((1+\epsilon_s)/2\), either by summing this identity or by its two parity masses, and

\[
a^{(s),\mathrm{mid}}
=\frac{a^{(s)}+\epsilon_s\delta_0}{1+\epsilon_s},
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(a^{(s),\mathrm{mid}},a^{(s)})
=\frac{\epsilon_s}{1+\epsilon_s}(1-a_0^{(s)})
\le\epsilon_s.
\]

Here \(\delta_0\) is unit mass at zero. Also \(2a_0^{(s)}=2\tanh(\pi s)/(\pi s)\to0\).

Now restrict to integer \(s\ge3\). Put \(\eta_s=\sum_{|k|>s^2}a_k^{(s)}\). Since \(Z_s\ge\pi/s\), monotonicity of \(x^{-2}\) gives

\(\eta_s\le(2/Z_s)\sum_{k>s^2}k^{-2}\le2/(Z_ss^2)\le2/(\pi s)<1.\)

The normalized finite table \(p^{(s)}\) is exactly \(a^{(s)}\) conditioned on \(|k|\le s^2\). Hence \(\operatorname{TV}(p^{(s)},a^{(s)})=\eta_s\), and their independent-pair laws have TV distance at most \(2\eta_s\). The latter bound follows by inserting the product of one factor from each law and using the triangle inequality; taking a product with an unchanged probability law preserves TV.

We also justify the conditioning estimate used here. For two probability laws \(P,Q\) and an event \(E\) of positive probability for both, set \(\tau=\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\). For any event \(B\), subtract \(P(B\cap E)/P(E)\) and \(Q(B\cap E)/Q(E)\), first changing the numerator and then the denominator. Since \(|P(B\cap E)-Q(B\cap E)|\le\tau\), \(|P(E)-Q(E)|\le\tau\), and \(Q(B\cap E)\le Q(E)\), the difference is at most \(2\tau/P(E)\). Taking the supremum over \(B\) gives the valid bound \(2\tau/\min(P(E),Q(E))\).

For either independent-pair law, the equal-parity event has probability at least \(1/2\), by the sum-of-squares bound on its two parity probabilities. Consequently their laws after equal-parity conditioning have TV distance at most \(8\eta_s\). The midpoint map cannot increase TV, since probabilities of output events are probabilities of their inverse images. The triangle inequality therefore gives

\(\operatorname{TV}(p^{(s),\mathrm{mid}},p^{(s)})\le9\eta_s+\epsilon_s\to0.\)

The largest finite atom is at zero and satisfies \(2\max_kp_k^{(s)}=2a_0^{(s)}/(1-\eta_s)\to0\). The difference of the even and odd masses changes by at most \(2\eta_s\) under truncation and normalization. As \(2\sum_{b=0}^1(\pi_b-1/2)^2=(\pi_0-\pi_1)^2\), the finite parity defect is at most \((\operatorname{sech}(\pi s)+2\eta_s)^2\to0\). These are all three limits asserted in (vi).

Finally, at every interior integer index \(j\) of the finite support, exact simplification gives

\[
\frac{(w_j^{(s)})^2}{w_{j-1}^{(s)}w_{j+1}^{(s)}}
=1+\frac{2s^2+1-2j^2}{(s^2+j^2)^2}.
\]

For \(j=2s\), both adjacent indices lie in \([-s^2,s^2]\) when \(s\ge3\), and the numerator of the added term is \(1-6s^2<0\). Thus the required midpoint log-concavity inequality fails. A global log-concave extension would imply that inequality, so no such extension exists. Symmetry gives \(H_0=W\) for this finite table, while Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(H_j\le W\) for every center. Hence \(H_*=W\) and its one-dimensional gain is exactly \(\log2\), as claimed.
