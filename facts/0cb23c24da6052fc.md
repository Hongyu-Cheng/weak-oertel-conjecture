## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $\mu$ be uniform probability on the barycentric simplex $\Delta_d$, and let the raw vertex-height vector $c$ have both signs. Put $H=\{\xi\in\Delta_d:c\cdot\xi\ge0\}$, $P=\mu(H)$, and let $b$ be the centroid of the section $\Delta_d\cap\{c\cdot\xi=0\}$ for its uniform $(d-1)$-dimensional volume. Let $L$ be the derivative at zero of $s\mapsto\mu\{c\cdot\xi\ge-s\}$. For independent unit-rate exponentials $E_j$, let $f$ be the continuous density value at zero of $V=\sum_j c_jE_j$. Then $L=df>0$ and, for every $j$,
$$
\int_H\xi_j\,d\mu=\frac{P+Lc_jb_j}{d+1}.
$$
For $d=1$ the section centroid is its single point. The identities allow repeated or zero raw coefficients.

## Proof

Put $N=\operatorname{proj}_{\{\sum_j v_j=0\}}c$, which is nonzero because $c$ has both signs. Coarea gives the density of $U=c\cdot \xi$ as the parallel-section volume divided by $|\Delta_d|\lVert N\rVert$. The density is bounded by projecting the bounded simplex onto a fixed transverse hyperplane and is continuous at zero: translated slice indicators converge off the intersection of the central section with the simplex boundary. No original facet lies in the central section plane, since the latter meets the relative interior. That intersection therefore has $(d-1)$-volume zero. For $d=1$ the projection density is constant near the interior cutting value. Thus this density at zero is $L>0$.

The affine tangent field $W_j(\xi)=\xi_j(e_j-\xi)$ has divergence $1-(d+1)\xi_j$. For example choose a barycentric chart that retains coordinate $j$ and drops a different coordinate. The $j$th diagonal derivative is $1-2\xi_j$ and the other $d-1$ derivatives are $-\xi_j$. The chart has constant volume Jacobian. On each original facet the field is tangent or zero. On the proper cut its outward flux density is $-c\cdot W_j/\lVert N\rVert=-c_j\xi_j/\lVert N\rVert$. The divergence theorem on the cut polytope, divided by the simplex volume, gives
$$
P-(d+1)\int_H\xi_j\,d\mu=-Lc_jb_j.
$$
The only new facet of the cut is the actual section, with intersections of distinct facets having zero surface measure. In dimension one the divergence theorem here is the fundamental theorem of calculus.

For $G=\sum_j E_j$ the substitution $E=G\xi$ has Jacobian proportional to $G^d$, so $\xi$ is uniform and independent of the shape-$(d+1)$, rate-one gamma variable $G$. Since $V=GU$, its conditional density given $G$ is $f_U(v/G)/G$. Boundedness of $f_U$ and $\mathbb E(1/G)=1/d$ give continuity of this integrated density at zero and its value $L/d$ by dominated convergence.
