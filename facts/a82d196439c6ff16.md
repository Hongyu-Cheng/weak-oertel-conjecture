## Statement

Let $\mu$ be actual mixed volume on a compact convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $n,d\ge1$, and let $P$ be its finite set of positive-volume bases. Fix $j\in P$ with raw marginal closed-halfspace depth $D_j$. For $u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}$ define
$$
V_u(s)=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R^n}\mu\{u\cdot x+a\cdot(z-j)\ge s\}.
$$
Then $V_u$ is the Lipschitz continuous closed survival function of an atomless compactly supported measure on $\mathbb R$ of total mass $D_j$. For $0<t\le D_j$ let $J_t(u)$ be the mean of the upper mass-$t$ part of this law. Let $M_t(j)$ be the continuous means of dominated sources of mass $t$ with base mean $j$. It is nonempty and
$$
h_{M_t(j)}(u)\ge J_t(u)
=\frac1t\inf_{b\in\mathbb R}\left[t b+\int_b^\infty V_u(s)\,ds\right].
$$
At $t=D_j$, $J_t(u)$ is the mean of the full law.

## Proof

Write $r_z=z-j$, $w_z=|C_z|$, and let $U=\max |u\cdot x|$ over the positive fibers. Each fiber's projection density in the $u$-coordinate is bounded by the supremum of its parallel $(d-1)$-section volumes divided by $\|u\|$ (for $d=1$, the section volumes are at most one). Thus the cap masses are nonincreasing and share a Lipschitz constant independent of the tilt $a$. Their infimum has these same properties and is zero for $s>U$ by taking $a=0$.

For $s<-U$, all fibers with $a\cdot r_z\ge s+U$ are fully included, and these bases form a closed halfspace trace containing $j$. This gives $V_u(s)\ge D_j$. Choose a minimizing trace for $D_j$, which exists by finiteness. Move its boundary strictly away from all bases without changing the trace and normalize the threshold to $-1$. Thus there are $a_0$ and $\delta>0$ such that $1+a_0\cdot r_z\ge\delta$ for the selected bases and $1+a_0\cdot r_z\le-\delta$ otherwise. For $s<0$ take $a=(-s)a_0$. The cap margin is $u\cdot x+(-s)(1+a_0\cdot r_z)$, so for $-s>U/\delta$ precisely the selected fibers are included. Hence $V_u(s)=D_j$ sufficiently far to the left. A continuous nonincreasing function with these plateaus is the survival function of the stated law; it has no atoms.

For a finite atomless law with this survival function, a threshold $b_*$ with $V_u(b_*)=t$ exists by continuity and the plateaus. Filling the upper tail at $b_*$ maximizes the first moment among dominated mass-$t$ submeasures, by the pointwise inequality $(s-b_*)f(s)\le(s-b_*)_+$. Integrating the positive part by layer cake shows equality at $b_*$ and the stated infimum formula.

Fact 08b344179e054c1c gives maximal balanced mass $T\ge D_j$ for these original fiber weights; a maximizing row realized by constant fiber densities and scaled by $t/T$ gives feasibility. For the balanced support dual, fact 8c6a53dd27e5c125 gives the compact convex joint graph in coordinates $(\int r_z f,\int f,\int u\cdot x\, f)$. Set $v=t h_{M_t(j)}(u)$. Strictly separate $(0,t,v+\varepsilon)$ from the graph. Comparison with $(0,t,v)$ makes the last coefficient positive; normalize the separator to $(a,-b,1)$. Its supremum over the graph is the integral of the hinge, since pointwise maximization selects the nonnegative part. Therefore $tb+\int(u\cdot x+a\cdot r_z-b)_+\,d\mu<v+\varepsilon$. Conversely every such dual value is at least $v$ by hinge domination and the balance constraints. Letting $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero gives equality with the infimum. For each fixed pair $(a,b)$ the integral in that formula equals $\int_b^\infty\mu\{u\cdot x+a\cdot r_z\ge s\}\,ds$ and is at least $\int_b^\infty V_u(s)\,ds$. Take the infima after this comparison.
