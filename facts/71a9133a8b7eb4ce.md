## Statement

Use the exponential-floor separator setting and put $r_j=\lambda_j(\ell_j)_+$. Suppose the normal and physical support ranges are compact, $\lambda_j\to0$, $\beta_j\to\beta\ge0$, $L_j\to L$ uniformly on the physical range with $L\ge0$, $\eta_j\rightharpoonup\eta$, $W_j\eta_j\rightharpoonup W\eta$, $\eta(Z)=0$ for $Z=\{(n,w):L(w)=0\}$, and $\sup_j\int(\ell_j)_+\,d\eta_j<\infty$. Record the rate in the measures
$$
\widehat B_j=(\ell_j)_+\eta_j\delta_{r_j},\qquad
\widehat A_j=W_j(\ell_j)_+\eta_j\delta_{r_j}.
$$
Along any joint weakly converging subsequence their limits are $\ell_+\eta\delta_0+\widehat b$ and $W\ell_+\eta\delta_0+\widehat a$, respectively, with $\ell=-\log L$ off $Z$ and $0\le\widehat a\le\widehat b$ supported on $Z\times[0,\beta]$. Let $\pi$ forget the rate, and let $b,a$ be the residuals after removing the ordinary parts of the unlifted body and cap-load log limits. Then $b=\pi_*\widehat b$, $a=\pi_*\widehat a$, and $\lambda_j(\ell_j)_+^2\eta_j\rightharpoonup q=\pi_*(r\widehat b)$ on this subsequence.

## Proof

The exponential floor bounds $r_j$ by $\max(0,\beta_j+C\lambda_j)$ on each sampled boundary. All the rates lie in a common compact interval, and the uniform first log mass yields subsequential weak compactness for both measures. Bounded logarithmic truncations show that $\ell_+$ is $\eta$-integrable. On compact sets where $L>0$, the logarithms converge uniformly and $r_j$ tends uniformly to zero. Use $\eta_j\rightharpoonup\eta$ and $W_j\eta_j\rightharpoonup W\eta$ to identify both ordinary parts there, including for continuous tests of the rate coordinate. Since $\eta(Z)=0$, the residual measures are exactly the restrictions to $Z$ and preserve the order $0\le\widehat a\le\widehat b$ coming from $0\le W_j\le1$. The rate upper bound tends to $\beta$, which restricts their support to $[0,\beta]$.

Projection gives the first two identities. The exact prelimit identity is $\lambda_j(\ell_j)_+^2\eta_j=\pi_*(r\widehat B_j)$. The rate multiplier is continuous and bounded on the common compact interval, so it passes to the weak limit. The ordinary part has rate zero, giving the asserted $q$.
