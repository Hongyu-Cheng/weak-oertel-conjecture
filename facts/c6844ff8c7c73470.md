## Statement

Retain one canonical hard instance of accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 7ec63df101c30343. Thus \(r=(r_e)\) is the half-strength canonical product reservoir,
\[
 Q:=\sum_e r_e>0,
 \qquad
 d_y(r):=\sum_{e\ni y}r_e,
\]
its load-minimal optimizer has loads \(0\leq\omega_e\leq r_e\),
\[
 \Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
 \qquad
 \beta_y:=\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_e,
\]
and
\[
 Q-\Omega\leq R.
\tag{1}
\]
With the exact normalizations of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, put
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+2^nh_{\max}(C).
\]
Assume the full canonical hard inequality
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(R<Q/100\).

For every retained parity class \(\xi\), put
\[
 L_\xi:=\sum_{y\equiv\xi}\beta_y,
 \qquad
 \max_{y\equiv\xi}\beta_y\leq\frac{L_\xi}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]
Its positive canonical \(I\)-triangles are labels
\(\tau=(z;\{a,b\})\), with \(z,a,b\) distinct and noncollinear, and mass
\[
 \theta_\tau
 :=\frac{\beta_z\beta_a\beta_b}{2L_\xi^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
For an arbitrary subfamily \(\mathcal U\) of these triangles and a loaded source \(y\), define the pivot and opposite-endpoint occurrence masses
\[
 P_y(\mathcal U)
 :=\sum_{\substack{\tau=(y;\{a,b\})\in\mathcal U}}
 \theta_\tau,
 \qquad
 E_y(\mathcal U)
 :=\sum_{\substack{\tau=(z;\{a,b\})\in\mathcal U\\
                    y\in\{a,b\}}}
 \theta_\tau.
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{P_y(\mathcal U)\leq\frac14\beta_y,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{E_y(\mathcal U)\leq\frac12\beta_y,}
\tag{6}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{P_y(\mathcal U)+E_y(\mathcal U)
 \leq\frac34\beta_y.}
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, for every set \(E\subseteq\{y:\beta_y>0\}\), if
\[
 \mathcal U_{\mathrm{touch}}(E)
 :=\{\tau\in\mathcal U:\{z,a,b\}\cap E\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal U_{\mathrm{rem}}(E)
 :=\mathcal U\setminus\mathcal U_{\mathrm{touch}}(E),
\tag{8}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{touch}}(E)}\theta_\tau
 \leq\frac34\sum_{y\in E}\beta_y,}
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{rem}}(E)}\theta_\tau
 \geq
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U}\theta_\tau
 -\frac34\sum_{y\in E}\beta_y.}
\tag{10}
\]

Write
\[
 K^\circ:=\{\varphi:h_K(\varphi)\leq1\}
\]
for the polar of an origin-symmetric convex body. Let \(F\) be an exposed face of \(D_*^\circ\), where
\[
 D_*:=\bigcap_{\beta_y>0}D_y,
\]
and put
\[
 E(F):=\{y:\beta_y>0,\ D_y^\circ\cap F\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 B_F:=\sum_{y\in E(F)}\beta_y.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(\mathcal U_F\) be any family of positive \(I\)-triangles for which a norming covector \(\varphi_\tau\) has been chosen in \(\operatorname{relint}F\). Every polar decomposition
\[
 \varphi_\tau
 =\sum_{y:\beta_y>0}\lambda_{\tau,y}\psi_{\tau,y},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\tau,y}\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_y\lambda_{\tau,y}=1,
 \qquad
 \psi_{\tau,y}\in D_y^\circ,
\tag{12}
\]
may have \(\lambda_{\tau,y}>0\) only when \(y\in E(F)\). Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\tau\in(\mathcal U_F)_{\mathrm{touch}}(E(F))}
 \theta_\tau\leq\frac34B_F,}
\tag{13}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\tau\in(\mathcal U_F)_{\mathrm{rem}}(E(F))}
 \theta_\tau
 \geq
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U_F}\theta_\tau-\frac34B_F.}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus, if a proposed face cut
\(\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U_F}\theta_\tau\leq CB_F\)
fails, then its all-three-sources-remote subfamily has mass strictly larger than
\[
 \left(C-\frac34\right)B_F.
\tag{15}
\]

Finally, every \(E\subseteq\{y:\beta_y>0\}\), and in particular \(E(F)\), obeys the exact reservoir-deficit estimates
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{y\in E}d_y(r)
 \leq\sum_{y\in E}\beta_y+2R,}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{y\in E}a_y
 \leq\frac{16}{3}
 \left(\sum_{y\in E}\beta_y+2R\right).}
\tag{17}
\]
Under (2), (17) gives the strict hard-branch specialization
\[
 \sum_{y\in E(F)}a_y
 <\frac{16}{3}\left(B_F+\frac Q{50}\right).
\tag{18}
\]

## Proof

Fix a loaded source \(y\), and let \(\xi\) be its parity class. Enlarging \(\mathcal U\) to all formally permitted noncollinear triangles can only increase the two nonnegative sums in (5). When \(y\) is the pivot, (4) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_y(\mathcal U)
 &\leq
 \frac{\beta_y}{2L_\xi^2}
 \sum_{\substack{a<b\\a,b\ne y}}\beta_a\beta_b\\
 &\leq
 \frac{\beta_y}{2L_\xi^2}
 \cdot\frac12
 \left(\sum_{a\ne y}\beta_a\right)^2\\
 &=\frac{\beta_y(L_\xi-\beta_y)^2}{4L_\xi^2}
 \leq\frac14\beta_y.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
This proves the first inequality in (6).

When \(y\) is an opposite endpoint, a triangle is uniquely specified by its pivot \(z\ne y\) and its other opposite endpoint \(b\ne y,z\). Dropping noncollinearity again only increases the sum, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_y(\mathcal U)
 &\leq
 \frac{\beta_y}{2L_\xi^2}
 \sum_{z\ne y}\beta_z
 \sum_{b\ne y,z}\beta_b\\
 &=\frac{\beta_y}{2L_\xi^2}
 \left(
 (L_\xi-\beta_y)^2
 -\sum_{z\ne y}\beta_z^2
 \right)\\
 &\leq\frac{\beta_y(L_\xi-\beta_y)^2}{2L_\xi^2}
 \leq\frac12\beta_y.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
This proves the second inequality in (6), and (7) follows by addition.

Every triangle in \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{touch}}(E)\) contributes its mass to at least one occurrence sum \(P_y+E_y\) with \(y\in E\). Therefore the union bound and (7) give
\[
 \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{touch}}(E)}\theta_\tau
 \leq
 \sum_{y\in E}
 \bigl(P_y(\mathcal U)+E_y(\mathcal U)\bigr)
 \leq\frac34\sum_{y\in E}\beta_y.
\tag{21}
\]
This proves (9). The two families in (8) partition \(\mathcal U\), so subtraction proves (10).

We next audit the face ownership in (12). Since the loaded source set is finite and every \(D_y^\circ\) is compact and convex, the set
\[
 K:=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{\beta_y>0}D_y^\circ
\]
is compact and convex. Polarity gives
\[
 K^\circ
 =\bigcap_{\beta_y>0}(D_y^\circ)^\circ
 =\bigcap_{\beta_y>0}D_y
 =D_*.
\tag{22}
\]
The bipolar theorem in finite dimension therefore gives
\[
 D_*^\circ=K.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus decompositions (12) exist. If \(\varphi_\tau\in F\) is written as a finite convex combination of points of \(D_*^\circ\), the defining face property forces every point having positive coefficient to belong to \(F\). Hence
\[
 \lambda_{\tau,y}>0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \psi_{\tau,y}\in D_y^\circ\cap F,
\]
which implies \(y\in E(F)\). Applying (9)--(10) with \(E=E(F)\) proves (13)--(14), and (15) follows immediately.

It remains to prove the hard reservoir-deficit ledger. Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives
\[
 \sum_y d_y(r)=2Q,
 \qquad
 \sum_y\beta_y=2\Omega,
 \qquad
 0\leq\beta_y\leq d_y(r).
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_y\bigl(d_y(r)-\beta_y\bigr)
 =2(Q-\Omega)\leq2R
\tag{25}
\]
by (1). Restricting the nonnegative sum in (25) to \(E\) proves (16).

Every loaded source belongs to a good parity class, because positive load requires an incident positive reservoir edge. In such a class, accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives
\[
 d_y(r)=\frac{a_y}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_y}{A_\xi}\right),
 \qquad
 \frac{a_y}{A_\xi}\leq\frac14.
\tag{26}
\]
Hence \(d_y(r)\geq3a_y/16\). Sum this inequality over \(E\), then apply (16), to obtain (17). Finally (2) implies \(2R<Q/50\), and substituting this strict bound into (17) with \(E=E(F)\) proves (18).

Equations (13)--(18) show that the already available source-incidence and unloaded-reservoir ledgers leave exactly one unsupported term: the mass of triangles whose three sources all lie outside \(E(F)\). No affine-realizable submatching or other terminal capacity is asserted for that remote mass.
