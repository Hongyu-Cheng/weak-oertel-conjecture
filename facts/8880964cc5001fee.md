## Statement

In the six-fiber full-parity certificate, replace the three small square radii by a common $0<\rho\le1/100$, keeping all other data fixed. The two nonroot midpoint sets with their designated same-parity partners are identical, centered at $(3,-1)$ with infinity-norm radius $(1/10+\rho)/2$, equal to $11/200$ at $\rho=1/100$. Using the partners' centroids as deterministic anchors gives identical transported squares of radius $1/20$. In each cap group the two entire transported images and their anchors lie strictly inside cap 1, while their sources lie strictly inside caps 2 and 3 respectively and strictly outside cap 1. Each incoming source has area $1/25$ and its anchor fiber has area $4\rho^2$. Their ratio is unbounded as $\rho\downarrow0$, even with the same finite forest and its bounded indegree.

## Proof

Use the six-fiber certificate's fixed projection, centers, large square radii, roots and caps. With the smaller radii, the same convex-hull argument works: the projected vertices retain their prescribed squares, and the maximum radius bound at the only other sampled base $(1,1)$ remains $1/10$. The new small squares are subsets of the old ones, so strict cap signs persist. The auxiliary body, contacts, expanded witnesses and forest depend only on the unchanged base and cap data. The common strict budget remains valid as cap masses can only fall.

The partner of $(2,1)$ is $(0,1)$ and the partner of $(1,2)$ is $(1,0)$, using coordinatewise parity. Both pairs have midpoint base $(1,1)$. Since $b$ is affine, their averaged centers both equal $b(1,1)=(3,-1)$. Minkowski averaging two axis-aligned squares adds their radii and divides by two, so both means coincide as stated. Averaging with a single centroid instead leaves the same center and halves only the large square radius. The entire resulting square lies in the large root square, which has strict cap color 1. The small partner squares and their centroids have the same cap color, whereas the two large source squares have colors 2 and 3. Thus incidence of the image in cap 1 can be attributed only to the anchor in these two transports. Dividing the unchanged source area by $4\rho^2$ proves divergence.
