## Statement

Let $K\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be a compact convex body with positive mixed length $\mu(K)$ on $N$ positive integer interval fibers. Let $g$ be its true mixed-length centroid, allowing a noninteger base. Suppose a nonvertical closed halfspace $H$ has boundary through $g$ that meets every positive-length integer fiber, with tangency allowed. Both $H$ and its opposite closed halfspace have normalized mixed length at least $1/(1+\kappa_N)$, with $\kappa_1=1$, $\kappa_2=\sqrt2$, and $\kappa_N^2=2N(2N-3)/(3(N-1)(N-2))$ for $N\ge3$. In particular the universal bound is $(\sqrt3-1)/2$. It is attained by the actual lifted hull with fibers $[-1,0],[-1,\sqrt3],[-1,0]$ at $-1,0,1$ and is approached even if all crossings must be strict.

## Proof

Use the affine margin of $H$ as the continuous coordinate $X$ with $H=\{X\ge0\}$. The base is unchanged and every fiber length is multiplied by the same positive constant, so this maps the original mixed centroid to one with $X$ coordinate zero. The positive interval fibers occur at consecutive indices, since a convex combination of two positive intervals has positive length. The hypothesis writes each of these intervals as $[-a_i,b_i]$ with $a_i,b_i\ge0$. Both sequences are concave by convexity of the lower boundary and concavity of the upper boundary. Null fibers have zero first moment and zero mass, and the restrictions to the positive index range remain concave.

The true first moment yields $\sum_i a_i^2=\sum_i b_i^2$ by integration of $X$. Both sums are positive because the total length is positive. With $A=\sum_i a_i$, $B=\sum_i b_i$, apply Cauchy–Schwarz and sharp-finite-concave-sequence-discrete-square-ratio to obtain
$$
A\le\sqrt{N\sum_i a_i^2}=\sqrt{N\sum_i b_i^2}\le\kappa_N B .
$$
It follows that the cap fraction $B/(A+B)$ is at least $1/(1+\kappa_N)$, and the same argument with the roles reversed proves the bound for the opposite cap. The boundary is length-null in every fiber.

For the witness, the given endpoint intervals have midpoint interval $[-1,0]$ inside the given middle interval, so the three intervals are exactly the integer fibers of their full-dimensional hull. Base symmetry gives base centroid zero. The sum of squared upper heights and of squared lower heights are both three, so the continuous mean is zero. The cap $X\ge0$ has fraction $\sqrt3/(3+\sqrt3)=(\sqrt3-1)/2$. For $0<\varepsilon<1$, replace the endpoint upper heights by $\varepsilon$ and the middle upper height by $\sqrt{3-2\varepsilon^2}>\varepsilon$. The same midpoint inclusion and centroid moment identities apply. Every crossing is proper and its cap fraction tends to the same value as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
