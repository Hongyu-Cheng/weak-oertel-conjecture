## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite nonempty complete base in an affine lattice, with positive compact convex $d$-fibers $K_z$ ($d\ge1$) satisfying the anchored Minkowski inclusions $\sum_z\lambda_z K_z\subseteq K_p$ whenever $\lambda_z\ge0$, $\sum_z\lambda_z=1$, and $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$. Put $S=\bigcup_z\{z\}\times K_z$, $C=\operatorname{conv}S$, $dm=\sum_z\delta_z\otimes dX|_{K_z}$ and $M=m(S)$. All affine functions are relative to $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$. Let $\varepsilon>0$, $N=d+1$, and $G(u)=(M^{-1}\sum_z\int_{K_z}[u\cdot(X-g_z)]^2\,dX)^{1/2}$, where $g_z$ are the fiber centroids. Use marked energy $E_K(y)=\inf_a\int_K[1+a\cdot(X-y)]^{-d-1}\,dX$ over denominators strictly positive throughout $K$, extended by zero on $\partial K$. For a parameter $L$ put $f(z,y)=[L(z,y)E_{K_z}(y)/M]^{1/N}$ and use the infimum $b_L(p,y)$ of mixed masses of the caps
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\varepsilon G(u)f(p,y),\qquad u\ne0,
$$
and of closed pure-base caps containing $p$ (including the whole-base trace). Write $b_{\rm base}(p)$ for the latter minimum alone. Let $Q=\max_S b_L$, $I$ be the bases of maximizing marks, $J=\{p\in I:b_{\rm base}(p)>Q\}$, $A=I\setminus J$, and $k(T)$ count inherited lattice parities occupied by $T$, with $k(\varnothing)=0$.

Use a positive continuous concave log-normalized $L$, and let the fixed old marks $Y_i$ be proper deepest marks carrying a finite contact certificate: positive weights $\sigma_i$ sum to one, and probabilities $\rho_i$ on $S$ have respective means $Y_i$ and $L_i=L(Y_i)$ for the coordinate vector and $L$, with $\sum_i(\sigma_i/L_i)\rho_i=dm/(ML)$. On the same $P$, let compatible positive fibers $K_z(t)$ approach $K_z$ by bounded signed inward radial first speeds $v_z$, meaning the old radial endpoint $X$ about an old interior center $o_z$ becomes $o_z+(1-s_{z,t}(X))(X-o_z)$, with $|s_{z,t}|=O(t)$ uniformly and $s_{z,t}/t\to v_z/[n_z\cdot(X-o_z)]$ surface-almost everywhere. Let $U_t$ be positive continuous concave log-normalized parameters whose actual current raw maximum $q_t$ is within $o(1)$ of the optimal raw maximum at the current family. At a fixed old mark $Y_i$ let $B_i(t,s)$ be the current raw buffered depth with positive parameter value $s$, using the current energy, total mass, covariance, and pure-base terms. For small $t$ there is a common depth interval about $Q$ with a continuous increasing inverse $s_i(t,q)$ of $B_i(t,\cdot)$ near $L_i$. Define $H_t(q)=\sum_i\sigma_i\log[s_i(t,q)/L_i]$ and $s(v)=M^{-1}\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}\log L\,v_z\,dS$. Then
$$
H_t(q_t)\ge t s(v)+o(t).
$$
If independently $H_t(Q+tr)=t(Ar+B)+o(t)$ for bounded $r$, with $A>0$, then $\liminf_{t\downarrow0}(q_t-Q)/t\ge(s(v)-B)/A$. If $M_t=M-tD+o(t)$, the corresponding lower coefficient for $q_t/M_t$ relative to $Q/M$ is $[(s(v)-B)/A+(Q/M)D]/M$.

## Proof

The accepted moving-body parameter continuity theorem 569a05581be52e0e applies at fixed $\varepsilon$ with these same actual positive fibers, contact rows, old global deepest marks and strict pure-base gaps. It yields old-coordinate interior convergence and $q_t\to Q$ for the near-optimal parameters.

For each old mark, $B_i(t,s)$ converges locally uniformly in $s$ to $B_i(0,s)$ near $L_i$. Indeed, $Y_i$ remains interior to its own fiber, its energy converges by the support-function homothetic sandwich about the fixed interior mark and energy monotonicity/scaling, and the current pooled covariance and mass converge by moment convergence. In Euclidean unit normals, the resulting threshold differences are uniform in the base tilts and bounded $s$ intervals. The all-fiber slab bound and the sum of sampled symmetric-difference volumes control cap-mass differences, and pure-base differences are bounded by this same volume discrepancy. The resulting uniform comparison passes to the infimum. Here energy covariance and monotonicity are as in d90ba05577427854. The same slab estimate gives continuity in $s$ at each $t$. Choose a small closed positive interval about $L_i$ so $B_i(t,s)$ stays strictly below the current pure-base depth throughout. The nonvertical minimizer exists and has a proper own section by 20210aa9d661295a. For $s'<s''$ in the interval, use a minimizing cap at $s''$ and raise its threshold to the value for $s'$. It loses strictly positive own mass, so $B_i(t,s')<B_i(t,s'')$. By strictness at $t=0$, locally uniform convergence leaves a fixed interval about $Q$ inside each inverse range, intersected over the finite set of marks.

For small $t$, $q_t$ is in this interval and $U_t(Y_i)$ in the scalar parameter interval. Thus $B_i(t,U_t(Y_i))\le q_t$ implies $U_t(Y_i)\le s_i(t,q_t)$. Apply the touching envelope from pk-w35-tilt-touching-envelope. The old logarithmic certificate in b817b4ab3e2256b0 and current normalization bound $\sum_i\sigma_i\log[U_t(Y_i)/L_i]$ below by the signed difference of the two sampled integrals of $\log V_t$ divided by $M$. The common intersection cancels and the $O(t)$ signed shell may use the old continuous reference integrand by uniform log convergence. Radial integration gives the coefficient $\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}\log L\,v_z\,dS/M$: the volume factor $n_z\cdot(X-o_z)$ cancels the denominator in the radial first speed, and bounded convergence treats both signs (endpoint counting for $d=1$). This gives the asserted bound for $H_t$.

For every fixed $r<(s(v)-B)/A$, the additional scalar expansion gives $H_t(Q+tr)<H_t(q_t)$ for all small $t$. Monotonicity yields $q_t>Q+tr$; then take the supremum over these $r$. This bracketing does not require a prior bound on $(q_t-Q)/t$. Expansion of the mass denominator using $q_t\to Q$ gives the normalized coefficient.
