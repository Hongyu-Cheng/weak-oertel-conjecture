## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and give each \(x\in A\) a positive raw weight \(w_x\). Suppose that every pair of distinct coordinatewise same-parity atoms \(u,v\in A\) has midpoint \(j=(u+v)/2\in A\) satisfying \(w_j^2=w_uw_v\). Let \(K\) be the number of occupied coordinatewise parity classes. Define the normalization and full rows by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=\sum_{x\in A}w_x,&
p_x&=w_x/Z,&
t_x&=Kp_x,\\
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,&
h_x&=|R_x|,&
\Psi_x&=t_x(h_xt_x-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

An extreme weight level means either \(W=\lbrace x\in A:w_x=\min_{y\in A}w_y\rbrace\) or \(W=\lbrace x\in A:w_x=\max_{y\in A}w_y\rbrace\). If \(a\ge1\) is an odd integer, \(t_x=1/a\) for every \(x\in W\), and \(N:=|W|\ge a-1\), then the order-tail estimate is

$$
\sum_{x\in W}\Psi_x\ge\frac{a^2-1}{3a^2}.
\tag{1}
$$

For \(a=1\), this asserts the nonnegative bound zero and uses empty tails.

For the census calibration, impose the following further hypotheses on the same actual midpoint structure. Let \(m\ge2\), \(q=2^m-1\), \(n=m+3\), and \(K=2^n=8(q+1)\). Require \(w_x\in\lbrace9,3,1\rbrace\), and call the corresponding levels \(\mathrm H,\mathrm B,\mathrm L\). Require precisely the following occupied parity classes:

* \(q\) pure \(\mathrm H\) classes, each containing \(3q\) atoms;
* \(4q+4\) pure \(\mathrm B\) classes, each containing \(9q\) atoms;
* \(3q+3\) pure \(\mathrm L\) classes, each containing \(27q\) atoms;
* one mixed class containing \(2q\) atoms of level \(\mathrm H\) and \(9q\) of level \(\mathrm L\).

Retain the canonical full-row energy names by defining

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x)^2,\\
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),&
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\frac1K\sum_{x\in A}\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
$$

For \(y\in R_x\), put \(\kappa_x(y)=\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}/H_p(x)\) and \(Y_x(y)=\frac12\log(p_y/p_{2x-y})\), and define

$$
\nu=
\frac{1}{b(p)}
\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x)^2
\sum_{y\in R_x}\kappa_x(y)Y_x(y)^2.
$$

Each central atom occurs once in its row and each noncentral reflected pair occurs in both orientations. The normalizations are \(Z=27qK\), \(t_{\mathrm H}=1/(3q)\), \(t_{\mathrm B}=1/(9q)\), and \(t_{\mathrm L}=1/(27q)\). Moreover,

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=1+\mathcal V_{\mathcal J},\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}
&\ge\frac1K\left(\frac23-\frac{82}{2187q^2}\right)
\ge\frac1{2K},\\
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
&\le\frac{52}{9}(\log3)^2\mathcal V_{\mathcal J},\\
\nu&\le\frac{52}{9}(\log3)^2\log A_{\mathcal J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Finally, consider the supplied formal multiplier profile on an atom set with the same census: its \(\mathrm H\) multipliers belong to \(\lbrace3q-2,3q\rbrace\), and its \(\mathrm L\) multipliers belong to \(\lbrace27q-2,27q\rbrace\), with the precise multiplicities recorded in the proof. Under any identification with an actual realization satisfying these hypotheses, at least \(3q-3\) of the \(\mathrm H\) multipliers and at least \(27q-3\) of the \(\mathrm L\) multipliers must differ from their formal values. Thus at least \(30q-6\) entries change. In particular, changing only \(O(\log q)\) entries cannot produce actual realizations along an unbounded sequence of these \(q\).

## Proof

For any noncentral row pair \(\lbrace y,2x-y\rbrace\), its endpoints are distinct same-parity atoms with midpoint \(x\). The midpoint-product hypothesis therefore gives \(\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}=p_x\). The same equality holds for the central atom \(y=x\). It follows that \(H_p(x)=h_xp_x\), that \(\kappa_x\) is uniform on \(R_x\), and that \(h_x\) is an odd positive integer. Distinct noncentral reflection orbits at a center use disjoint endpoints.

Choose a real linear functional taking distinct values on all atoms of \(A\). Such a functional exists because the forbidden functionals lie in finitely many proper hyperplanes. At a center in an extreme weight level \(W\), every row endpoint also belongs to \(W\): if the central weight is minimal, both endpoint weights are at least that weight and their product equals its square, forcing equality of both; the maximal case is identical with the inequalities reversed.

Order the \(N\) atoms of \(W\) by the chosen functional. If a center \(x\) has rank \(r\), each noncentral reflected pair has exactly one endpoint of smaller rank and one of larger rank. Indeed, the functional value at \(x\) is the average of its distinct endpoint values. The disjointness of those pairs gives

$$
h_x\le2\min\lbrace r-1,N-r\rbrace+1.
\tag{3}
$$

We prove (1). When \(a=1\), both sides are nonnegative and the right side is zero. Otherwise write \(s=(a-1)/2\). The first \(s\) ranks and last \(s\) ranks are disjoint because \(N\ge2s\). For the atom of rank \(r\) in the first tail, where \(1\le r\le s\), (3) gives \(h_x\le2r-1<a\). Since \(t_x=1/a\),

$$
\Psi_x=\frac1a\left(\frac{h_x}{a}-1\right)^2
\ge\frac1a\left(1-\frac{2r-1}{a}\right)^2.
$$

The last tail gives the same bounds in reverse order. The other atom energies are nonnegative, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in W}\Psi_x
&\ge\frac2{a^3}\sum_{r=1}^{s}(a-2r+1)^2\\
&=\frac8{a^3}\sum_{k=1}^{s}k^2
=\frac8{a^3}\frac{s(s+1)(2s+1)}6
=\frac{a^2-1}{3a^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves the general order-tail estimate using actual midpoint equations and endpoint disjointness.

Now impose the stated three-weight census. Every occupied parity class has raw mass \(27q\), and there are \(K\) such classes. Hence \(Z=27qK\), every parity class has probability \(1/K\), and the three scaled weights are those in the statement. The level cardinalities are

$$
N_{\mathrm H}=3q^2+2q,\qquad
N_{\mathrm B}=36q^2+36q,\qquad
N_{\mathrm L}=81q^2+90q.
$$

Sending each ordered same-parity pair \((u,v)\), including pairs with \(u=v\), to its midpoint counts exactly the \(h_x\) row summands at each \(x\). Midpoint products and the equal parity-class probabilities give

$$
\sum_{x\in A}h_xp_x^2
=\sum_{\eta\text{ occupied}}
\left(\sum_{x\in A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)}p_x\right)^2
=\frac1K.
$$

Since \(\sum_xp_x=1\), expansion now yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}
&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x(Kh_xp_x-1)^2\\
&=K^2\sum_{x\in A}h_x^2p_x^3
-2K\sum_{x\in A}h_xp_x^2+1\\
&=A_{\mathcal J}-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

Apply (1) to level \(\mathrm H\) with \(a=3q\) and to level \(\mathrm L\) with \(a=27q\). Both values of \(a\) are odd. Their required cardinality bounds hold because \(N_{\mathrm H}\ge3q-1\) and \(N_{\mathrm L}\ge27q-1\) for \(q\ge3\). Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}
&\ge\frac{(3q)^2-1}{3(3q)^2}
+\frac{(27q)^2-1}{3(27q)^2}\\
&=\frac23-\frac{82}{2187q^2}
\ge\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

The last inequality follows from \(q\ge3\) and \(82/19683<1/6\).

To control the score numerator without restrictions on multiplier repairs, let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of unordered distinct same-parity pairs. For \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\in\mathscr Q\), choose either endpoint order, let \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\), and put \(z_e=\frac12\log(p_{v_e}/p_{u_e})\). Uniformity of the full row law implies

$$
\sum_{y\in R_x}\kappa_x(y)Y_x(y)^2
=\frac2{h_x}\sum_{e:j_e=x}z_e^2.
$$

Using \(H_p(x)=h_xp_x\) and \(t_x=Kp_x\) in the definition of \(\nu\) consequently gives

$$
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
=\frac1K\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}
2h_{j_e}t_{j_e}^3z_e^2.
\tag{6}
$$

The only pairs with nonzero scores are the \(\mathrm H\)-\(\mathrm L\) pairs in the unique mixed parity class. There are precisely \((2q)(9q)=18q^2\) such unordered pairs. Their midpoint belongs to level \(\mathrm B\), and each has squared score \((\log3)^2\). Every other pair in \(\mathscr Q\) has equal endpoint weights and score zero. Put \(T=\log3\) for the following estimates.

For a nonzero-score pair whose midpoint \(x\) satisfies \(h_xt_{\mathrm B}\le2\), its coefficient obeys \(2h_xt_{\mathrm B}^3\le4t_{\mathrm B}^2=4/(81q^2)\). Therefore all such pairs contribute at most

$$
18q^2\frac4{81q^2}T^2=\frac89T^2
\tag{7}
$$

to the sum in (6), before division by \(K\).

For each remaining \(\mathrm B\) center \(x\), let \(r_x\) count the \(\mathrm H\)-\(\mathrm L\) pairs with midpoint \(x\). These are disjoint noncentral reflection orbits, so \(2r_x\le h_x-1\). With \(a_x=h_xt_{\mathrm B}>2\), the entire contribution at that center, before division by \(K\), is at most

$$
\begin{aligned}
2h_xt_{\mathrm B}^3r_xT^2
&\le T^2h_x(h_x-1)t_{\mathrm B}^3\\
&\le T^2t_{\mathrm B}a_x^2
\le4T^2t_{\mathrm B}(a_x-1)^2
=4T^2\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

Here \(a_x^2\le4(a_x-1)^2\) holds for \(a_x>2\). Summing (8) charges each such center once. From (5)-(8),

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
&\le4T^2\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+\frac{8T^2}{9K}\\
&\le\left(4+\frac{16}{9}\right)T^2\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}
=\frac{52}{9}(\log3)^2\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Equation (4) gives \(A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\ge1\). Dividing by \(A_{\mathcal J}\) and using \((A_{\mathcal J}-1)/A_{\mathcal J}\le\log A_{\mathcal J}\) proves the asserted price in (2).

For calibration, the unmodified formal profile assigns multiplier \(3q-2\) to \(q^2\) atoms of level \(\mathrm H\), and multiplier \(3q\) to its other \(2q^2+2q\) atoms. It assigns \(9q+2\) to \(18q^2\) atoms of level \(\mathrm B\), and \(9q\) to its other \(18q^2+36q\) atoms. It assigns \(27q-2\) to \(81q^2\) atoms of level \(\mathrm L\), and \(27q\) to its other \(90q\) atoms. Its formal energy \(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{formal}}\), defined by the same sum of \(\Psi_x/K\) using these prescribed multipliers, satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
K\mathcal V_{\mathrm{formal}}
&=q^2\frac4{(3q)^3}
+18q^2\frac4{(9q)^3}
+81q^2\frac4{(27q)^3}\\
&=\frac{64}{243q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

If one formally assigns one nonzero-score pair to each of its \(18q^2\) elevated \(\mathrm B\) centers, define \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{formal}}\) by the right side of (6) with that assignment. Direct arithmetic gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
K\mathcal N_{\mathrm{formal}}
&=18q^2\frac{2(9q+2)}{(9q)^3}(\log3)^2\\
&=\frac{4(9q+2)}{81q}(\log3)^2,\\
\frac{\mathcal N_{\mathrm{formal}}}{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{formal}}}
&=\frac{3(9q+2)}{16}(\log3)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the supplied formal energy and numerator are arithmetically correct. Actual order tails instead force the positive constant in (5) for \(K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\), and (7)-(8) ensure that arbitrary multiplier compensations still obey (2).

Finally, apply (3) to the first \((3q-3)/2\) and last \((3q-3)/2\) atoms of level \(\mathrm H\). Each of these \(3q-3\) atoms has multiplier at most \(3q-4\), smaller than every formal \(\mathrm H\) multiplier. The first \((27q-3)/2\) and last \((27q-3)/2\) atoms of level \(\mathrm L\) similarly give \(27q-3\) atoms with multiplier at most \(27q-4\), smaller than every formal \(\mathrm L\) multiplier. The two tails within each level are disjoint by the level cardinalities, and the two levels are disjoint. Hence at least \((3q-3)+(27q-3)=30q-6\) entries change under every identification with the formal profile. Since \((\log q)/q\to0\), only \(O(\log q)\) changes cannot suffice along an unbounded sequence.
