## Statement

Use the two-core scalar tables associated with accepted facts 9e1805a461c7be2c and 4bb26a33ac78ee45. Here m_a>=1 denotes the total number of noncentral pairs of core a, M_a=m_a+1 is its pair capacity, q_ov in {1,2} is the overlap cardinality, and
$$
K=m_0+m_1+2-q_{\mathrm{ov}}
$$
is the occupied parity count. The generic row of 9e1805a461c7be2c at c_a is denoted R_a. The overlap cardinality is unrelated to any auxiliary center law.

Normalize the original positive weights only by their original normalizer Z: write a_a=w_{c_a}/Z, let S_a be the sum of the original probabilities at the endpoints of the m_a pairs in R_a, and retain p_x=w_x/Z at every supported atom. Every pair in core a has endpoint product a_a^2. With q_ov=1, let h in {0,1} denote the core that contains the other center. Its forced pair has endpoint probabilities a_{1-h},a_h^2/a_{1-h}, and the support is the disjoint union of the singleton c_h and the two endpoint sets. Thus a_h+S_0+S_1=1. With q_ov=2, both forced pairs occur and the two endpoint sets partition the support, so S_0+S_1=1. Nonforced pairs may have arbitrary positive endpoint probabilities with the stated products.

Define, from this unchanged original law,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=K^2\sum_xp_x^3, &\Delta_a&=4m_a(m_a+1)K^2a_a^3,\\
\mu_a&=\frac{S_a/a_a+2}{2(m_a+1)},
&A_{\mathrm{full}}&=A+\Delta_0+\Delta_1,\\
d&=-\log A,
&r_a&=\frac12\left[\log\frac{A+\Delta_a}{A}
 +\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{full}}}{A+\Delta_{1-a}}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
In particular mu_a>=1. Assume A<1 and mu_0>=2mu_1. Set
$$
u=\frac{S_1}{a_1}\ge2m_1,
\qquad
\xi=\begin{cases}
1,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\text{ and }h=1,\\
1+(a_0/a_1)^2,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\text{ or }(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\text{ and }h=0),
\end{cases}
$$
where u and xi are dimensionless algebraic variables, not score variances. For m>=1, u>=2m, and b>0 define
$$
F_m(u;b)=\frac{(u+b)^3}{(m+1)^2}-b^3-\frac{u^3}{m^2}+3u.
\tag{2}
$$
Finally put
$$
\mathcal R=3K^2a_0^2S_0
 +K^2a_1^3\bigl[F_{m_1}(u;\xi)-F_{m_1}(u;1)\bigr].
\tag{3}
$$
The following signed compensation estimate holds on the entire stated scalar domain, without any restriction on either count or field:
$$
1-A\le\mathcal R+\frac{15}{32\mu_1}\Delta_1.
\tag{4}
$$
The correction in (3) is explicitly
$$
F_m(u;b)-F_m(u;1)
=\frac{b-1}{(m+1)^2}
 \left[3u^2+3u(b+1)-m(m+2)(b^2+b+1)\right].
\tag{5}
$$
It may be negative and is not replaced by an absolute value in (4).

Consequently the two explicit additional conditions
$$
A_{\mathrm{full}}\le\frac98,
\qquad
\mathcal R\le\frac{\Delta_0}{4\mu_0}
\tag{6}
$$
imply the exact requested logarithmic inequality with one constant B=256:
$$
d\le\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}\,r_a.
\tag{7}
$$
A more elementary sufficient condition for the second inequality in (6) is
$$
m_0\ge6\mu_0^2,
\qquad
\bigl[\xi=1\ \text{or}\ \xi\ge2u/m_1\bigr].
\tag{8}
$$
In particular, when q_ov=1 and the low-mean core contains the high-mean center, (8) reduces to m_0>=6mu_0^2. All counts and means in this subregion remain unbounded. Neither (4), (6), nor their proof assumes r_1>=d.

The assertions also apply to the exact forced-spine Jensen minima used to test the Shapley condition: for fixed hub probabilities and endpoint sums, set all nonforced pair totals within each core equal. The additional-axis counts of 4bb26a33ac78ee45 are (m_0-1,m_1) when q_ov=1,h=0, are (m_0-1,m_1-1) when q_ov=2, and after interchanging that construction's two core labels are (m_1-1,m_0) when q_ov=1,h=1. There is no replacement of K by 2^n. The realization preserves the minimizing table's probabilities and every normalized quantity in (1)-(8), with the following explicit raw-weight map. Let W be the original weight w_{c_0} when q_ov=2 or q_ov=1,h=0, and let W=w_{c_1} when q_ov=1,h=1. The predecessor's anchored weights are the minimizing raw weights divided by W, and its raw normalizer is Z/W. Multiplying all anchored weights and their normalizer by W recovers the minimizing table's raw scale Z. These are common scalings, not substitutions of a different normalizer into the original law.

## Proof

First establish the scalar repair inequality
$$
F_m(u;1)\le\frac{15}{32\mu}\,4m(m+1),
\qquad \mu=\frac{u+2}{2(m+1)},\quad m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},\quad u\ge2m.
\tag{9}
$$
It is equivalent to
$$
(u+2)F_m(u;1)\le\frac{15}{4}m(m+1)^2.
\tag{10}
$$
Here is an exact proof for all unbounded m and u. For 1<=m<=7 put v=u-2m>=0 and multiply the difference in (10) by m^2(m+1)^2. The result is the polynomial
$$
P_m(v)=(2m+1)v^4+(13m^2+12m+2)v^3+Q_mv^2+L_mv+C_m,
\tag{11}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=-3m(m^3-8m^2-12m-4),\\
L_m&=-m^2(4m+3)(3m^2-2m-4),\\
C_m&=\frac14m^3(m-1)(m+1)(15m^2+12m+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
At m=1 this is 3v^4+27v^3+69v^2+21v>=0. At each integer 2<=m<=7, Q_m>0, and direct integer substitution gives the following positive values of 4Q_m C_m-L_m^2, in increasing order of m:
$$
605504,\quad23154255,\quad266425344,\quad1496924375,
\quad4349070144,\quad444086559.
$$
Thus Q_mv^2+L_mv+C_m is strictly positive by completing its square; the other terms in (11) are nonnegative. This proves (10) for all these m without a numerical approximation.

For m>=8 write
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1&=\frac{2m+1}{m^2(m+1)^2},&c_2&=\frac3{(m+1)^2},\\
c_3&=3+\frac3{(m+1)^2},&c_4&=1-\frac1{(m+1)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then F_m(u;1)=-c_1u^3+c_2u^2+c_3u-c_4. The inequality m^2-7m-5>=0 implies c_1>=5/(3m^3). Also c_2-2c_1<=3/m^2. Since u>=2m>=16,
$$
(c_3+2c_2)u^2+(2c_3-c_4)u
\le\left(\frac{28}{9}+\frac{103}{324}\right)u^2
<\frac72u^2.
$$
Indeed c_3+2c_2=3+9/(m+1)^2<=28/9 and 2c_3-c_4=5+7/(m+1)^2<=412/81. As c_4>0, expansion therefore gives
$$
(u+2)F_m(u;1)
\le-\frac{5u^4}{3m^3}+\frac{3u^3}{m^2}+\frac72u^2.
$$
Completing a square gives
$$
\frac{3u^3}{m^2}\le\frac{u^4}{3m^3}+\frac{27u^2}{4m}
\le\frac{u^4}{3m^3}+\frac{27u^2}{32}.
$$
A second completion of a square, now as a quadratic in u^2, yields
$$
(u+2)F_m(u;1)
\le-\frac{4u^4}{3m^3}+\frac{139u^2}{32}
\le\frac{57963}{16384}m^3
<\frac{15}{4}m(m+1)^2.
$$
This proves (10) and hence (9) for every m. Equality in (9) occurs at m=1,u=2.

We next derive the signed estimate before using (9). The core/parity description in 9e1805a461c7be2c says that the endpoint sets are disjoint. Collapse each of the m_0 pairs in core zero to its total probability. For a pair with total s and product a_0^2, its endpoint cubic sum is exactly s^3-3a_0^2s. Thus this collapse changes the scaled cubic sum A by exactly
$$
E_0=3K^2a_0^2S_0.
\tag{12}
$$
It is only an algebraic grouping of probabilities in a cubic estimate; it does not define a new original law.

When q_ov=2 or q_ov=1,h=0, one of those collapsed totals is the forced value
$$
L_0=a_1+a_0^2/a_1=\xi a_1.
$$
Group that total with all m_1 core-one pairs. Its mass is a_1(u+xi), and assign this block capacity m_1+1. The other collapsed core-zero totals have capacity one each. In the q_ov=1,h=0 case the outside singleton a_0 has capacity one as well. The total capacity is respectively
$$
(m_1+1)+(m_0-1)=K,
\qquad
(m_1+1)+(m_0-1)+1=K.
$$
When q_ov=1,h=1, instead group the actual singleton a_1 with the core-one endpoints; then xi=1 and the remaining m_0 collapsed totals have total capacity m_0. Again the total capacity is K.

If t_j are the core-one pair totals, then sum_j t_j=S_1 and every t_j>=2a_1. Jensen for their cubes gives
$$
\sum_{\text{core-one endpoints }x}p_x^3
=\sum_{j=1}^{m_1}(t_j^3-3a_1^2t_j)
\ge a_1^3\left(\frac{u^3}{m_1^2}-3u\right).
\tag{13}
$$
Definition (2) says that adding a_1^3F_{m_1}(u;xi) to this block's cubic sum, including its designated central total (xi a_1)^3, makes that sum at least
$$
\frac{[a_1(u+\xi)]^3}{(m_1+1)^2}.
$$
All other blocks are singleton totals. Their masses and this block's mass sum to one, and their capacities sum to K. Weighted Holder therefore proves
$$
A+E_0+K^2a_1^3F_{m_1}(u;\xi)\ge1.
\tag{14}
$$
No sign condition on F is used. Subtract F_{m_1}(u;1), apply (9) with mu=mu_1, and use Delta_1=4m_1(m_1+1)K^2a_1^3. This proves (4). Expanding two cubes proves (5).

For the logarithmic conclusion, the atom count is 2K-1 when q_ov=1 and 2K when q_ov=2. Holder applied directly to the original probabilities consequently gives A>=1/4. Set delta=1-A. The function (-log A)/(1-A) is decreasing for 0<A<1, as follows for example from its integral representation
$$
\frac{-\log A}{1-A}=\int_0^1\frac{dt}{A+t(1-A)}.
$$
Hence
$$
d\le\frac43\log4\,\delta<\frac{28}{15}\delta.
\tag{15}
$$
For the strict elementary numerical bound, log2<7/10 because the first five terms of the exponential series at 7/10 already sum to more than 2.

Under (6), estimate (4) implies
$$
\delta\le\frac14\frac{\Delta_0}{\mu_0}
 +\frac{15}{32}\frac{\Delta_1}{\mu_1}
\le\frac{15}{32}\sum_a\frac{\Delta_a}{\mu_a}.
$$
Thus (15) gives d<(7/8)sum_a Delta_a/mu_a. Both logarithms defining r_a in (1) are at least Delta_a/A_full, using log(1+s)>=s/(1+s). Therefore
$$
r_a\ge\frac{\Delta_a}{A_{\mathrm{full}}}.
$$
Since mu_a>=1,
$$
\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}
=\frac{128}{128+\mu_a}\ge\frac{128}{129\mu_a}.
$$
Combining these bounds with A_full<=9/8 yields
$$
\sum_a\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}r_a
\ge\frac{1024}{1161}\sum_a\frac{\Delta_a}{\mu_a}
>\frac78\sum_a\frac{\Delta_a}{\mu_a}>d,
$$
where 8192>8127 verifies the middle strict comparison. This proves (7) with its exact Shapley increments.

Finally,
$$
\frac{E_0}{\Delta_0/\mu_0}
=\frac{3\mu_0[(m_0+1)\mu_0-1]}{2m_0(m_0+1)}
\le\frac{3\mu_0^2}{2m_0}.
$$
Thus m_0>=6mu_0^2 implies E_0<=Delta_0/(4mu_0). For b=xi>=2u/m_1, we have b>=4 and u<=m_1b/2. These inequalities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
3u^2+3u(b+1)
&\le\frac34m_1^2b^2+\frac{15}{8}m_1b^2\\
&<m_1(m_1+2)b^2
\le m_1(m_1+2)(b^2+b+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Formula (5) consequently makes the correction in (3) nonpositive. It is zero when xi=1. This proves (8).

All preceding estimates hold for arbitrary admissible pair totals, so they hold in particular at the exact forced-spine minima. For completeness, equalizing the nonforced pair totals within a core minimizes their cubic contribution because a pair of product a^2 and total t has cubic sum t^3-3a^2t. Perform that equalization at the raw-weight scale, keeping each hub weight w_{c_a}, each forced pair, and the sum of the raw endpoint weights in each core fixed. Denote the resulting minimizing raw weights by \(\bar w_x\). Their sum is still the original \(Z\), and their hub weights are still \(w_{c_a}\). Their normalized endpoint products are \(a_a^2\), and all the means, increments, and signed correction appearing in the statement use exactly \(\bar p_x=\bar w_x/Z\). The atomic energy for this application is the minimizing table's \(K^2\sum_x\bar p_x^3\).

Here is the complete scale map to 4bb26a33ac78ee45. When q_ov=2 or q_ov=1,h=0, identify its core labels with ours, use the stated additional-axis counts, set \(W=w_{c_0}\), and choose
$$
\tau=\frac{w_{c_1}}{W}=\frac{a_1}{a_0}.
$$
When q_ov=1,h=1, identify its core zero with our core one and its core one with our core zero, use additional-axis counts \((m_1-1,m_0)\), set \(W=w_{c_1}\), and choose \(\tau=w_{c_0}/W=a_0/a_1\). Thus its coordinate-zero hub always corresponds to the hub of original raw weight W, and has anchored predecessor weight one.

For every nonforced pair at that anchoring hub, choose one of its minimizing raw endpoint weights \(\bar w_x\) and take the predecessor parameter \(u_i=\bar w_x/W\). Its inverse is the other endpoint weight divided by W because the raw pair product is \(W^2\). For every nonforced pair at the other hub, whose original raw weight is \(w_{\mathrm{other}}=\tau W\), take \(v_j=\bar w_x/w_{\mathrm{other}}\). Its two predecessor weights \(\tau v_j,\tau v_j^{-1}\) are the two minimizing raw endpoint weights divided by W. The forced weights \(\tau^{-1},1,\tau\), and, when q_ov=2, \(\tau^2\), are likewise exactly the corresponding minimizing raw weights divided by W, by the forced pair product identities. These choices exist for empty and nonempty nonforced collections under the domain conditions already stated.

Consequently there is a bijection \(\iota\) of the minimizing table's atoms with the predecessor's atoms, respecting the mapped hub labels, forced atoms, and indexed nonforced pairs, such that its raw weights and raw normalizer satisfy
$$
\widetilde w_{\iota(x)}=\frac{\bar w_x}{W},
\qquad
\widetilde Z=\sum_x\widetilde w_{\iota(x)}=\frac ZW,
\qquad
\frac{\widetilde w_{\iota(x)}}{\widetilde Z}
=\frac{\bar w_x}{Z}=\bar p_x.
$$
The predecessor's complete globally affine support therefore has exactly the same minimizing probabilities, occupied count K, and all normalized scalar quantities used in this lemma. Its anchored raw normalizer is \(Z/W\), not an unqualified equality with Z. If one restores the original raw scale, multiplying every predecessor weight and its normalizer by W gives \(W\widetilde w_{\iota(x)}=\bar w_x\) and \(W\widetilde Z=Z\). In terms of the predecessor's linear exponent \(\ell\cdot\iota(x)\), this rescaling is precisely the common affine offset \(\log W\); it does not change any probability. This specifies both raw scales explicitly and uses no geometric relaxation gap or changed original-law normalization.
