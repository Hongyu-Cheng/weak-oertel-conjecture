## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let \(\ell_i:T\to[0,\infty)\), \(1\le i\le n\), be bounded upper-semicontinuous concave functions. For \(\mathbf y\in T\), put
\[
 M_i(\mathbf y)=\lfloor\ell_i(\mathbf y)\rfloor,
 \qquad
 A(\mathbf y)=\prod_{i=1}^n(M_i(\mathbf y)+1),
 \qquad
 R(\mathbf y)=\prod_{i=1}^n(2M_i(\mathbf y)+1),
\]
and let \(\mathbb E_T\) denote expectation for normalized Lebesgue measure on \(T\). For \(0<\alpha<1\), define
\[
 \Psi_\alpha(T,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n)
 =\frac{(\mathbb E_T R^\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}{\mathbb E_T A},
 \qquad
 b_0=1,
 \qquad
 b_m=(2m+1)^\alpha-(2m-1)^\alpha\quad(m\ge1).
\]

The following assertions hold.

(a) If two such systems are combined by taking the Cartesian product of their base bodies and concatenating their roof lists, with every roof depending only on its original base variable, then \(\Psi_\alpha\) of the product is the product of the two original values. Consequently, for each fixed \(\alpha\), the existence of a positive constant \(c_\alpha\), independent of every dimension, base body, and roof list, such that \(\Psi_\alpha\ge c_\alpha\) for all admissible systems is equivalent to the exact inequality \(\Psi_\alpha\ge1\) for all admissible systems.

(b) For \(\mathbf t\in[0,\infty)^n\), define
\[
 T_{\mathbf t}=\{\mathbf y\in T:\ell_i(\mathbf y)\ge t_i\text{ for every }i\},
 \qquad
 f(\mathbf t)=\frac{|T_{\mathbf t}|}{|T|}.
\]
Then \(f(\mathbf0)=1\), \(f\) is coordinatewise nonincreasing, and \(f^{1/d}\) is concave on \(\{\mathbf t:f(\mathbf t)>0\}\). In particular, \(f\) is log-concave there. The exact finite identities are
\[
 \mathbb E_T A
 =\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}f(\mathbf m),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_T R^\alpha
 =\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}
   \left(\prod_{i=1}^n b_{m_i}\right)f(\mathbf m).
\]
Thus \(\Psi_\alpha\ge1\) is exactly the weighted survival inequality
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}
   \left(\prod_{i=1}^n b_{m_i}\right)f(\mathbf m)
 \ge
 \left(\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}f(\mathbf m)\right)^\alpha.
\]

(c) Let \((u_m)_{m\ge0}\) be any finitely supported nonnegative sequence with \(u_0=1\), \(u_{m+1}\le u_m\), and \(u_m^2\ge u_{m-1}u_{m+1}\) for every \(m\ge1\). Then, for every \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
 \sum_{m\ge0}b_m u_m\ge\left(\sum_{m\ge0}u_m\right)^\alpha.
\]
Consequently, if
\(f(\mathbf m)=\prod_{i=1}^n u^{(i)}_{m_i}\), where every \(u^{(i)}\) satisfies these hypotheses, then the weighted survival inequality in (b) holds. In particular, it holds for Cartesian products of one-roof geometric systems.

The factorization in (c) is essential to the conclusion proved here. There is no assertion that the weighted survival inequality holds for general coupled geometric roofs or for arbitrary multivariate log-concave functions. At \(\alpha=2/3\), accepted fact \(73cea4a618e64e9a\) gives admissible affine coupled roofs for which \(\Psi_{2/3}\to0\); its scope is the failure of this fractional-moment comparison, while the same accepted fact separately gives positive actual mixed depth on those examples. Thus neither that failure nor the present lemma is a conclusion about the general actual-depth conjecture.

## Proof

For (a), consider systems \((T^{(j)},\ell^{(j)})\), \(j=1,2\), and their product. The floor counts factor pointwise:
\[
 A(\mathbf y_1,\mathbf y_2)=A_1(\mathbf y_1)A_2(\mathbf y_2),
 \qquad
 R(\mathbf y_1,\mathbf y_2)=R_1(\mathbf y_1)R_2(\mathbf y_2).
\]
Normalized Lebesgue measure on \(T^{(1)}\times T^{(2)}\) is the product of the two normalized measures, so Fubini's theorem gives
\[
 \mathbb E A=(\mathbb E A_1)(\mathbb E A_2),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E R^\alpha=(\mathbb E R_1^\alpha)(\mathbb E R_2^\alpha).
\]
This proves multiplicativity of \(\Psi_\alpha\). If some admissible system has \(q:=\Psi_\alpha<1\), its \(k\)-fold Cartesian power has value \(q^k\), which tends to zero. Hence no positive universal lower bound can coexist with such a system. Therefore a positive universal lower bound implies \(\Psi_\alpha\ge1\) for every system. The converse follows immediately by taking \(c_\alpha=1\).

For (b), every \(T_{\mathbf t}\) is compact and convex: upper semicontinuity makes each roof superlevel set closed, and concavity makes it convex. If \(\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in[0,\infty)^n\) and \(0\le\lambda\le1\), concavity of every roof gives the geometric inclusion
\[
 (1-\lambda)T_{\mathbf s}+\lambda T_{\mathbf t}
 \subseteq T_{(1-\lambda)\mathbf s+\lambda\mathbf t}.
\]
When \(f(\mathbf s),f(\mathbf t)>0\), the \(d\)-dimensional Brunn--Minkowski inequality and monotonicity of volume therefore give
\[
 f((1-\lambda)\mathbf s+\lambda\mathbf t)^{1/d}
 \ge
 (1-\lambda)f(\mathbf s)^{1/d}+\lambda f(\mathbf t)^{1/d}.
\]
The right side is positive, so the positive support is convex and \(f^{1/d}\) is concave there. Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean then yields
\(f((1-\lambda)\mathbf s+\lambda\mathbf t)\ge f(\mathbf s)^{1-\lambda}f(\mathbf t)^\lambda\), proving log-concavity. The claims \(f(\mathbf0)=1\) and coordinatewise monotonicity follow directly from \(\ell_i\ge0\) and nesting of the threshold sets.

For a nonnegative integer \(M\), one has the two finite threshold expansions
\[
 M+1=\sum_{m\ge0}\mathbf1_{\{M\ge m\}},
 \qquad
 (2M+1)^\alpha=\sum_{m\ge0}b_m\mathbf1_{\{M\ge m\}}.
\]
The second identity telescopes because \(b_0=1\) and \(b_m=(2m+1)^\alpha-(2m-1)^\alpha\) for \(m\ge1\). Multiplying the first expansion over the coordinates gives
\[
 A(\mathbf y)=
 \sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}
 \mathbf1_{\{\ell_i(\mathbf y)\ge m_i\ \forall i\}},
\]
and multiplying the second gives the analogous formula for \(R(\mathbf y)^\alpha\), with coefficient \(\prod_i b_{m_i}\). All sums are finite because the roofs are bounded. Integration against normalized Lebesgue measure proves both identities and their stated equivalence to \(\Psi_\alpha\ge1\).

We prove (c). Put \(Z=\sum_{m\ge0}u_m\). If \(Z=1\), then \(u_m=0\) for \(m\ge1\), and equality holds. Suppose \(Z>1\), and set
\[
 r=1-\frac1Z\in(0,1),
 \qquad
 g_m=r^m.
\]
Then \(g_0=u_0=1\) and \(\sum_{m\ge0}g_m=(1-r)^{-1}=Z=\sum_{m\ge0}u_m\).

Because \((u_m)\) is nonincreasing, its positive support is an initial interval \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\). On this interval, log-concavity says that
\[
 m\longmapsto \log u_m-m\log r
\]
is a concave sequence. Its superlevel set at zero is therefore an interval containing \(0\). It cannot equal only \(\{0\}\), since that would give \(u_m<g_m\) for every \(m\ge1\), contradicting equality of the two total sums. Hence there is an integer \(k\ge1\) such that
\[
 u_m-g_m\ge0\quad(1\le m\le k),
 \qquad
 u_m-g_m\le0\quad(m>k),
\]
where the second range includes \(m>N\). Thus the difference has a single crossing from nonnegative to nonpositive.

The function \(x\mapsto x^\alpha\) is increasing and concave on \((0,\infty)\). Hence \(b_m>0\), and its increments over the equal-length intervals \([2m-1,2m+1]\) decrease with \(m\ge1\). Thus \((b_m)_{m\ge1}\) is nonincreasing. Since \(\sum_{m\ge1}(u_m-g_m)=0\), the single-crossing property gives
\[
 \sum_{m\ge1}b_m(u_m-g_m)
 =\sum_{m\ge1}(b_m-b_k)(u_m-g_m)\ge0.
\]
The terms at \(m=0\) agree, so
\[
 \sum_{m\ge0}b_m u_m\ge\sum_{m\ge0}b_m r^m.
\]

It remains to bound the geometric comparator. Telescoping gives
\[
 \sum_{m\ge0}b_mr^m
 =(1-r)\sum_{m\ge0}(2m+1)^\alpha r^m.
\]
We claim that, for every integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
 (2m+1)^\alpha\ge\frac{(\alpha+1)_m}{m!},
\]
where \((\alpha+1)_0=1\) and \((\alpha+1)_m=(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+m)\) for \(m\ge1\). The case \(m=0\) is equality. For the induction step, put \(x=1/(2m+2)\). The elementary inequality
\[
 \log\frac{1+x}{1-x}\ge2x\qquad(0\le x<1)
\]
follows because the difference vanishes at zero and has derivative \(2/(1-x^2)-2\ge0\). Therefore
\[
 \alpha\log\frac{2m+3}{2m+1}
 \ge\frac{\alpha}{m+1}
 \ge\log\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{m+1}\right).
\]
After exponentiation, the ratio of the left side of the claimed coefficient inequality at \(m+1\) to its value at \(m\) is at least
\(1+\alpha/(m+1)\), which is exactly the corresponding ratio for \((\alpha+1)_m/m!\). This proves the claim by induction.

For completeness, if \(c_m=(\alpha+1)_m/m!\) and \(B(r)=\sum_{m\ge0}c_mr^m\), then the recurrence \((m+1)c_{m+1}=(m+\alpha+1)c_m\) gives
\((1-r)B'(r)=(\alpha+1)B(r)\) and \(B(0)=1\). Hence \(B(r)=(1-r)^{-\alpha-1}\) for \(0\le r<1\). The coefficient inequality now yields
\[
 \sum_{m\ge0}b_mr^m
 \ge(1-r)B(r)
 =(1-r)^{-\alpha}
 =Z^\alpha.
\]
Together with the geometric comparison, this proves the scalar inequality.

Finally, if \(f(\mathbf m)=\prod_i u^{(i)}_{m_i}\), finite product factorization and the scalar inequality give
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf m}
 \left(\prod_i b_{m_i}\right)f(\mathbf m)
 =\prod_i\left(\sum_{m\ge0}b_m u^{(i)}_m\right)
 \ge\prod_i\left(\sum_{m\ge0}u^{(i)}_m\right)^\alpha
 =\left(\sum_{\mathbf m}f(\mathbf m)\right)^\alpha.
\]
For a one-roof geometric system, its scalar survival sequence is finitely supported, nonincreasing, has zeroth term one, and is log-concave by (b). Independent Cartesian products of such systems therefore satisfy the hypotheses just used. This proves every assertion.

## External sources

The only external input is the \(d\)-dimensional Brunn--Minkowski inequality: for nonempty measurable sets \(K,L\subset\mathbb R^d\) of finite positive volume and \(0\le\lambda\le1\),
\[
 |(1-\lambda)K+\lambda L|^{1/d}
 \ge(1-\lambda)|K|^{1/d}+\lambda|L|^{1/d}.
\]
See Richard J. Gardner, “The Brunn--Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355--405, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. In the proof it is applied only to the compact convex sets \(T_{\mathbf s},T_{\mathbf t}\subset\mathbb R^d\) when both have positive finite volume; the displayed Minkowski inclusion then transfers its conclusion to \(T_{(1-\lambda)\mathbf s+\lambda\mathbf t}\).
