## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For an integer \(m\ge1\), let
\[
D_m:=\{\mathbf x=(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}:x_1+x_2\le m\},
\qquad
p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x)
:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}}
        {Z(\boldsymbol\theta)},
\qquad
Z(\boldsymbol\theta):=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_m}
                         e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},
\]
where \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2\). For each \(\mathbf j\in D_m\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}:=r_{D_m,\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy law on \(D_m\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), using the notation \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Its support is the intersection of \(D_m\) with the minimal face containing \(\mathbf j\). The remaining exact aliases for that fact are \(A=D_m\), \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\theta\), and \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\nu_{\mathbf j})&:=-\sum_{\mathbf x:\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)>0}
                     \nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x),\\
C_{\mathbf j}&:=\mathsf C_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf j)
              =p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)e^{H(\nu_{\mathbf j})},\\
B_2&:=\mathcal B_2(p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
       =\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^2,\\
\omega_{\mathbf j}&:=\frac{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^2}{B_2},\\
V_{\mathbf j}&:=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
       \left(\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)}
                          {p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf X)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The capacity identity is supplied by bf1ff94487296374. Then, for every \(m\ge1\) and every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}
\le 16+3\cdot2^{18}e^4.
\]
Moreover, every row satisfies \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)\le8\). At vertices \(V_{\mathbf j}=0\).

## Proof

Accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 gives the unique mean-constrained entropy minimizer and its exponential representation on the minimal face. For a mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\), log-affinity of the source gives
\[
D(\nu\|p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
=-H(\nu)-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j+\log Z(\boldsymbol\theta)
=-H(\nu)-\log p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j).
\]
Consequently that minimizer is precisely \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), and the accepted capacity formula gives \(C_{\mathbf j}=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)e^{H(\nu_{\mathbf j})}\), including boundary rows. In an interior row, define its full exponential parameter \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\) by \(\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)=e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\cdot\mathbf x}/Z(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})\), and let \(\Sigma_{\mathbf j}:=\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\mathbf X)\). The defining variance is then \((\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}-\boldsymbol\theta)^T\Sigma_{\mathbf j}(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}-\boldsymbol\theta)\). On a proper face the same calculation uses its affine coordinate chart. Any normal component is constant on the face and cancels from the variance. The proof below works directly with the log densities and thus includes these face restrictions.

Write \(x_3=m-x_1-x_2\), so each point is a nonnegative integer composition \((x_1,x_2,x_3)\) of \(m\). Permuting these three coordinates acts by an invertible integer affine map on \(D_m\). For example, interchanging coordinates \(1\) and \(3\) acts as \((x_1,x_2)\mapsto(m-x_1-x_2,x_2)\), whose linear determinant is \(-1\); the other transpositions have the same property. These maps preserve the full affine integer lattice and map mean-constrained laws bijectively to the corresponding mean-constrained laws. Entropy, capacity weights, and likelihood variances are therefore unchanged by these relabelings.

Give the source its three coefficients \((\theta_1,\theta_2,0)\). Relabel so that a largest coefficient is the third coefficient, and subtract that coefficient from all three. The subtraction changes every score by the same constant, since \(x_1+x_2+x_3=m\), and so does not change the probability law. Thus we may suppose, for the rest of the proof, that
\[
\boldsymbol\theta=(-a,-b),\qquad a,b\ge0,
\qquad
p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x)
=Z(\boldsymbol\theta)^{-1}e^{-a x_1-b x_2}.
\]
This relabeling is fixed by the source. Any later relabeling used to bound a row's own log density does not alter this source coordinate choice.

First we bound the second moments of the coordinate marginals of each row. A finite probability mass function \((f_k)\) supported on a consecutive interval of nonnegative integers is called log-concave here when \(f_k^2\ge f_{k-1}f_{k+1}\). If \(Y\) has such a law and \(s_k=\mathbb P(Y\ge k)\), then the ratios \(s_{k+1}/s_k\), wherever defined, are nonincreasing. To prove this, on the positive support the ratios \(f_{k+1}/f_k\) are nonincreasing. Hence each term \(f_{k+t}/f_k\), and therefore their sum \(s_k/f_k\), is nonincreasing in \(k\). It follows that \(f_k/s_k\) is nondecreasing and \(s_{k+1}/s_k=1-f_k/s_k\) is nonincreasing. Before the positive support begins these survival ratios are \(1\), so the assertion holds there as well. After the survival function vanishes, all later values vanish.

Since \(s_0=1\), the decreasing-ratio property gives \(s_{r+t}\le s_rs_t\) for all nonnegative integers \(r,t\), by comparing the products of \(t\) consecutive ratios starting at \(r\) and at \(0\). Cases involving a zero survival value give the same inequality directly. The finite tail-sum identities now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathbb E[Y(Y-1)]}{2}
&=\sum_{k\ge2}(k-1)s_k
 =\sum_{r,t\ge1}s_{r+t}\\
&\le\left(\sum_{r\ge1}s_r\right)^2
 =(\mathbb EY)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, \(\mathbb E Y^2\le2(\mathbb EY)^2+\mathbb EY\).

Every coordinate marginal of \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) has the preceding log-concavity property. For an interior row, its exponential representation shows that its first marginal at \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) is proportional to \(e^{\lambda_{\mathbf j,1}k}\sum_{h=0}^{m-k}e^{\lambda_{\mathbf j,2}h}\). For \(q>0\), put \(b_s(q)=\sum_{h=0}^s q^h\). Direct expansion gives \(b_s(q)^2-b_{s-1}(q)b_{s+1}(q)=q^s>0\) for \(s\ge1\). Therefore this marginal is log-concave; exchanging coordinates proves the same assertion for the second marginal. On each edge, the nonconstant coordinate marginals are truncated geometric laws, whose successive ratios are constant. The other marginals are deterministic. Vertex marginals are also deterministic. Thus in all cases, since \(\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}X_i=j_i\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}X_i^2\le2j_i^2+j_i\le3j_i^2
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]
The last inequality uses that \(j_i\) is a nonnegative integer; if \(j_i=0\), that coordinate is zero almost surely. Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf X)
\le\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(aX_1+bX_2)^2
\le6\bigl((a j_1)^2+(b j_2)^2\bigr).
\]

Next we bound the row's own log-density variance. A geometric law \(G\) on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with mass \((1-e^{-u})e^{-uk}\) for \(u>0\), satisfies
\[
u^2\mathbb E G^2
=u^2\frac{e^{-u}(1+e^{-u})}{(1-e^{-u})^2}
\le2.
\]
Indeed, summing the differentiated geometric series gives the displayed moment formula, and \(e^{-u}/(1-e^{-u})^2=(4\sinh^2(u/2))^{-1}\le u^{-2}\).

If a probability law is obtained from this geometric law by weighting with a bounded nonnegative nonincreasing function \(h\) of positive expectation, then the expectation of every nondecreasing integrable function is no larger than under \(G\). For completeness, if \(G'\) is an independent copy, then
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(f(G),h(G))
=\mathbb E[(f(G)-f(G'))(h(G)-h(G'))]\le0
\]
for nondecreasing \(f\). Dividing \(\mathbb E[f(G)h(G)]\le\mathbb E f(G)\,\mathbb E h(G)\) by \(\mathbb E h(G)\) proves the assertion. We use it only with bounded \(h\) and \(f(k)=k^2\).

For an interior row, permute the three coefficients of its exponential parameter so that a largest coefficient is at the third vertex, then subtract that coefficient. In the resulting composition coordinates \((Y_1,Y_2,m-Y_1-Y_2)\), the row has density proportional to \(e^{-uY_1-vY_2}\) on \(D_m\), with \(u,v\ge0\). If \(u>0\), the first marginal is obtained from the preceding infinite geometric law with parameter \(u\) by the weight
\[
h(k)=\begin{cases}
\sum_{t=0}^{m-k}e^{-vt},&0\le k\le m,\\
0,&k>m.
\end{cases}
\]
This weight is nonnegative and nonincreasing. Thus \(u^2\mathbb E Y_1^2\le2\). The analogous argument gives \(v^2\mathbb E Y_2^2\le2\) when \(v>0\); a zero coefficient contributes zero without requiring an infinite geometric law at that parameter. It follows that
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)
=\operatorname{Var}(uY_1+vY_2)
\le2u^2\mathbb E Y_1^2+2v^2\mathbb E Y_2^2
\le8.
\]
For an edge row, choose its favored endpoint as coordinate zero. Its density is proportional to \(e^{-uk}\) on \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\), for some \(u\ge0\). The same domination argument with weight \(\mathbf1_{\{k\le m\}}\) bounds its log-density variance by \(2\) when \(u>0\); when \(u=0\) it is zero. A vertex row is deterministic. This proves the asserted self-row bound in all cases.

The elementary inequality \(\operatorname{Var}(U-W)\le2\operatorname{Var}U+2\operatorname{Var}W\), applied to the log densities, now yields
\[
V_{\mathbf j}
\le16+12\bigl((a j_1)^2+(b j_2)^2\bigr).
\]
It remains to average the source terms with the prescribed weights.

For any finitely supported nonnegative integer-valued law of mean \(r>0\), comparison with the infinite geometric law of that mean gives entropy at most \((r+1)\log(r+1)-r\log r\). This follows by writing its nonnegative relative entropy with respect to that geometric law. Nonnegativity itself follows from \(\log t\le t-1\). A zero-mean law is deterministic. Entropy subadditivity follows in the same way from relative entropy with respect to the product of the two marginal laws. Therefore, without assuming that the coordinates of \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) are independent,
\[
H(\nu_{\mathbf j})
\le\log(j_1+1)+\log(j_2+1)+2,
\]
since \(r\log(1+1/r)\le1\) for \(r>0\). Conversely, if \(2(j_1+j_2)\le m\), the uniform law on the contained rectangle \(\{0,\ldots,2j_1\}\times\{0,\ldots,2j_2\}\) has mean \(\mathbf j\). Maximality of the entropy row gives
\[
H(\nu_{\mathbf j})
\ge\log(2j_1+1)+\log(2j_2+1).
\]
Both assertions include zero coordinates.

The common factor \(Z(\boldsymbol\theta)^{-3}\) cancels from the capacity weights. Define their unnormalized denominator by
\[
\mathcal W:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}
 e^{-3(a j_1+b j_2)+2H(\nu_{\mathbf j})},
\qquad
\omega_{\mathbf j}
=\mathcal W^{-1}e^{-3(a j_1+b j_2)+2H(\nu_{\mathbf j})}.
\]
For \(N\ge0\) and \(t\ge0\), introduce the two finite sums
\[
\mathcal P_N(t):=\sum_{k=0}^N(k+1)^2e^{-3tk},
\qquad
\mathcal Q_N(t):=\sum_{k=0}^N(tk)^2(k+1)^2e^{-3tk}.
\]
Set \(L=\lfloor m/4\rfloor\). Every row with \(0\le j_1,j_2\le L\) satisfies \(2(j_1+j_2)\le m\). The entropy lower bound gives
\[
\mathcal W
\ge\sum_{j_1,j_2=0}^L
 e^{-3(a j_1+b j_2)}(2j_1+1)^2(2j_2+1)^2
\ge\mathcal P_L(a)\mathcal P_L(b).
\]
This denominator is positive even when \(L=0\).

There are two elementary comparisons of the finite sums. In \(\mathcal P_m(t)\), write \(k=4h+r\), where \(0\le r\le3\). Then \(h\le L\), \(k+1\le4(h+1)\), and \(e^{-3tk}\le e^{-3th}\). Summing at most four terms for each \(h\) gives \(\mathcal P_m(t)\le64\mathcal P_L(t)\). Likewise, writing \(k=2h+r\), where \(0\le r\le1\), gives \(\mathcal P_m(t/2)\le8\mathcal P_m(t)\).

Finally, \(s^2e^{-3s/2}\le1\) for \(s\ge0\): its maximum is \(16/(9e^2)<1\). Thus \(\mathcal Q_m(t)\le\mathcal P_m(t/2)\), and the two comparisons imply
\[
\mathcal P_m(t)\le64\mathcal P_L(t),
\qquad
\mathcal Q_m(t)\le512\mathcal P_L(t).
\]
These inequalities include \(t=0\) and every \(m\ge1\).

Using the entropy upper bound, enlarging the triangle to the box \(\{0,\ldots,m\}^2\) only in the numerator, and using the preceding denominator lower bound, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}\omega_{\mathbf j}
       \bigl((a j_1)^2+(b j_2)^2\bigr)
&\le e^4
 \frac{\mathcal Q_m(a)\mathcal P_m(b)
       +\mathcal P_m(a)\mathcal Q_m(b)}
      {\mathcal P_L(a)\mathcal P_L(b)}\\
&\le e^4(512\cdot64+64\cdot512)
 =2^{16}e^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Averaging the row variance bound therefore gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}
\le16+12\cdot2^{16}e^4
=16+3\cdot2^{18}e^4,
\]
as claimed. At a vertex the entropy row is a point mass, so the defining likelihood random variable is constant and its variance is zero.
