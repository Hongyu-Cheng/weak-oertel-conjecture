## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume \(D_+\neq\varnothing\). For \(z\) with \(C_z\neq\varnothing\) and
\(x\in C_z\), define
\[
\delta_z(x):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
M_z:=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_z(x),
\qquad
K_z(t):=\{x\in C_z:\delta_z(x)\geq t\}
\quad(t\geq0).
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` implies that \(M_z\) is attained and that
each \(K_z(t)\) is compact and convex, possibly empty. In particular,
\[
K_z(t)\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\leq t\leq M_z.
\tag{4}
\]

For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq D_+\), put
\[
q(E):=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E).
\]
If \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\) is affine and satisfies
\(s(z)\in C_z\) for every \(z\in E\), affineness and convexity make
\(s(z)\in C_z\) for every
\(z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Define
\[
\mathfrak A(C):=
\sup_{E,s}
2^{-q(E)}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\delta_z(s(z)),
\tag{5}
\]
where the supremum ranges over all such pairs \((E,s)\).

For \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), let \(\mathcal P_q\) be the finite family of
\(q\)-dimensional lattice polytopes \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) whose vertices
belong to \(D_+\), and put
\[
Z(P):=P\cap\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{6}
\]
A zero-dimensional lattice polytope is one lattice point, and a
one-dimensional lattice polytope is a lattice segment. For
\(P\in\mathcal P_q\), choose any affine isomorphism
\[
\xi:\operatorname{aff}(P)\longrightarrow\mathbb R^q
\tag{7}
\]
and write \(\xi_z:=\xi(z)\). Every affine map
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(P)\to\mathbb R^d\) then has the form
\[
s(z)=B\xi_z+b,
\qquad
B\in\mathbb R^{d\times q},\quad b\in\mathbb R^d.
\tag{8}
\]

Call \(P\in\mathcal P_q\) admissible if there is a pair \((B,b)\) satisfying
\[
B\xi_v+b\in C_v
\qquad(v\in\operatorname{vert}P),
\tag{9}
\]
and denote the admissible family by
\(\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\). For
\(P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\), define
\[
\mathfrak A(P):=
2^{-q}
\max_{\substack{B,b\\
B\xi_v+b\in C_v\ (v\in\operatorname{vert}P)}}
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}
\delta_z(B\xi_z+b).
\tag{10}
\]
The maximum in (10) is attained. Define
\[
\mathfrak A_q(C):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\max_{P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}}\mathfrak A(P),
&\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing,\\[2mm]
0,
&\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
If \(\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing\), the maximum in the first
line of (11) is attained by an admissible polytope and an affine map. No
attainment is asserted for the conventional zero in the second line. The
registered affine-certificate capacity satisfies
\[
\mathfrak A(C)
=
\max_{q=0,1,2}\mathfrak A_q(C),
\tag{12}
\]
and the supremum in (5) is attained. The latter assertion is unconditional
because \(\mathcal P_0^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing\).

For \(P\in\mathcal P_q\) and a threshold allocation
\(t=(t_z)_{z\in Z(P)}\in[0,\infty)^{Z(P)}\), put
\[
\mathcal S_z(t_z):=
\left\{
(B,b)\in\mathbb R^{d\times q}\times\mathbb R^d:
B\xi_z+b\in K_z(t_z)
\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal T_q:=
\left\{
(P,t):
P\in\mathcal P_q,\ t_z\geq0,\
\bigcap_{z\in Z(P)}\mathcal S_z(t_z)\neq\varnothing
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal T_q\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing.
\tag{15}
\]
If these equivalent families are nonempty, the exact attained
threshold-allocation identity is
\[
2^q\mathfrak A_q(C)
=
\max_{(P,t)\in\mathcal T_q}
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}t_z.
\tag{16}
\]
If they are empty, \(\mathfrak A_q(C)=0\) only by the convention in (11),
and there is no rank-\(q\) feasible threshold allocation.

Fix \(P,t\), assume all \(K_z(t_z)\) are nonempty, and let
\[
\mathcal U(P):=
\left\{
(u_z)_{z\in Z(P)}\in(\mathbb R^d)^{Z(P)}:
\sum_z u_z=0,\quad
\sum_z \xi_{z,j}u_z=0\ (j=1,\ldots,q)
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
All equalities in (17) are vector equalities in \(\mathbb R^d\). Then
\[
\bigcap_{z\in Z(P)}\mathcal S_z(t_z)\neq\varnothing
\tag{18}
\]
if and only if
\[
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}
\min_{x\in K_z(t_z)}\langle u_z,x\rangle
\leq0
\qquad(u\in\mathcal U(P)).
\tag{19}
\]
Equivalently, (18) fails if and only if there exists
\(u\in\mathcal U(P)\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}
\min_{x\in K_z(t_z)}\langle u_z,x\rangle
>0.
\tag{20}
\]
If some \(K_z(t_z)\) is empty, that one constraint already witnesses the
failure of (18).

Whenever (18) fails, some subfamily indexed by
\[
J\subseteq Z(P),
\qquad
|J|\leq d(q+1)+1,
\tag{21}
\]
already has empty intersection. If every \(K_z(t_z)\), \(z\in J\), is
nonempty, there is a strict witness in (20) supported on \(J\). Explicitly,
after extending \(u_z=0\) outside \(J\), it satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in J}u_z=0,\qquad
\sum_{z\in J}\xi_{z,j}u_z=0
\quad(j=1,\ldots,q),
\tag{22}
\]
and the strict sum in (20) may be restricted to \(J\).

Consequently, for every \(\alpha>0\),
\[
\mathfrak A(C)<\alpha
\tag{23}
\]
if and only if all three of the following hold.

First,
\[
M_z<\alpha\qquad(z\in D_+).
\tag{24}
\]
Second, for every lattice segment \(P\in\mathcal P_1\) and every allocation
with
\[
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}t_z\geq2\alpha,
\tag{25}
\]
there is an infeasible subfamily of at most \(2d+1\) constraints, with an
empty depth region or a strict dual witness satisfying (22). If
\(z=a+kv\) parametrizes the line with \(v\in\mathbb Z^2\) primitive, the
moment equations are
\[
\sum_k u_k=0,\qquad
\sum_k k\,u_k=0.
\tag{26}
\]
Third, for every lattice polygon \(P\in\mathcal P_2\) and every allocation
with
\[
\sum_{z\in Z(P)}t_z\geq4\alpha,
\tag{27}
\]
there is an infeasible subfamily of at most \(3d+1\) constraints. Using the
ordinary coordinates \(\xi_z=z\), its strict dual witness satisfies
\[
\sum_z u_z=0,\qquad
\sum_z z_1u_z=0,\qquad
\sum_z z_2u_z=0.
\tag{28}
\]
These equivalences include the empty-family cases: when
\(\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\), one has
\(\mathfrak A_q(C)=0<\alpha\) and every rank-\(q\) allocation is infeasible.

Finally, let \(a,b\in D_+\) be distinct and satisfy
\[
a\equiv b\pmod2,
\qquad
m:=\frac{a+b}{2}\in\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{29}
\]
Then
\[
\mathfrak A(C)
\geq
\frac12
\left[
M_a+M_b+
\left(
\frac{M_a^{1/d}+M_b^{1/d}}2
\right)^d
\right].
\tag{30}
\]
With
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{31}
\]
Grünbaum's centroid inequality gives the profile-only consequence
\[
\mathfrak A(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
\left[
w_a+w_b+
\left(
\frac{w_a^{1/d}+w_b^{1/d}}2
\right)^d
\right].
\tag{32}
\]
The rank-zero specialization also gives
\[
\mathfrak A(C)\geq M_z\geq\gamma_dw_z
\qquad(z\in D_+).
\tag{33}
\]

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that \(D_+\) is finite,
so every \(\mathcal P_q\) is finite. If \(P\in\mathcal P_q\), every
\(z\in P\) has a positive-volume fiber. Indeed, write
\[
z=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i v_i,
\qquad
v_i\in\operatorname{vert}P,\quad
\lambda_i\geq0,\quad
\sum_i\lambda_i=1.
\tag{34}
\]
Choose an index \(i_0\) with \(\lambda_{i_0}>0\), and choose
\(x_i\in C_{v_i}\) for \(i\neq i_0\). Convexity gives
\[
\lambda_{i_0}C_{v_{i_0}}
+
\sum_{i\neq i_0}\lambda_i x_i
\subseteq C_z.
\tag{35}
\]
The set on the left is a translate of a positive homothetic copy of the
positive-volume fiber \(C_{v_{i_0}}\), so \(w_z>0\). In particular, every
quantity above is defined on a positive-volume fiber.

We first reduce (5) to (10)-(12). Let \((E,s)\) be admissible in (5) and put
\(P=\operatorname{conv}(E)\). Every vertex of \(P\) belongs to \(E\), hence
to \(D_+\), and
\[
q(E)=\dim P.
\tag{36}
\]
The polytope \(P\) belongs to
\(\mathcal P_{q(E)}^{\mathrm{adm}}\), and the objective depends only on
\(P\) and the restriction of \(s\) to \(\operatorname{aff}(P)\).
Conversely, let \(P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\) and suppose \(s\) is
feasible at every vertex. Taking
\[
E=\operatorname{vert}P
\tag{37}
\]
gives an admissible pair in (5). For every \(z\in P\), write \(z\) as in
(34). Affineness and convexity give
\[
s(z)=\sum_i\lambda_i s(v_i)\in C_z.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus the objective in (10) is exactly the objective in (5) for (37).

We now prove the precise attainment assertions. Fix
\(P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\) and choose \(q+1\) affinely independent
vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_q\). Evaluation at these vertices is a linear
isomorphism from the \(d(q+1)\)-dimensional parameter space in (8) onto
\((\mathbb R^d)^{q+1}\). The feasible parameter set in (10) is nonempty
and closed. Under the evaluation isomorphism, it is contained in
\[
C_{v_0}\times\cdots\times C_{v_q},
\]
so it is compact. Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` states that
\(\delta_z^{1/d}\), and hence \(\delta_z\), is upper semicontinuous.
Therefore the finite sum in (10) is upper semicontinuous and attains its
maximum.

If \(\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing\), its finiteness now proves
that the first line of (11) is attained. If it is empty, (11) is only a
definition of the value zero and there is no associated polytope. Finally,
\(\mathcal P_0^{\mathrm{adm}}\neq\varnothing\), because every
\(z\in D_+\) gives the admissible singleton \(P=\{z\}\). Hence the finite
union
\[
\bigcup_{q=0}^2\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}
\]
is nonempty. Maximizing the attained values (10) over this finite union
proves both (12) and unconditional attainment of the supremum in (5). The
zero conventions for empty rank families do not alter this maximum because
every actual value (10) is nonnegative.

We next prove (15) and (16), including their empty-family qualifications.
If \((P,t)\in\mathcal T_q\), then the common parameter in (14) is feasible
at every vertex, so \(P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\). Conversely, if
\(P\in\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}\), convexity gives a selector feasible at
every point of \(P\). Since
\[
K_z(0)=C_z,
\tag{39}
\]
the zero allocation belongs to \(\mathcal T_q\). This proves (15).

Assume these families are nonempty. If \((P,t)\in\mathcal T_q\), then
\[
\delta_z(B\xi_z+b)\geq t_z
\qquad(z\in Z(P))
\tag{40}
\]
for its common parameter. Thus
\[
\sum_z t_z
\leq 2^q\mathfrak A(P)
\leq2^q\mathfrak A_q(C).
\tag{41}
\]
Conversely, choose an admissible \(P\) and affine map attaining
\(\mathfrak A_q(C)\), whose existence was proved above, and put
\[
t_z:=\delta_z(B\xi_z+b).
\tag{42}
\]
Then \((P,t)\in\mathcal T_q\), and its allocation sum is exactly
\(2^q\mathfrak A_q(C)\). This proves the attained identity (16). If the
families are empty, (15) and the second line of (11) give precisely the
stated alternative, with no maximum or attainment claim.

For the dual, work in the finite-dimensional Euclidean product
\[
\mathcal X:=(\mathbb R^d)^{Z(P)}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal K(t):=\prod_{z\in Z(P)}K_z(t_z)
\tag{43}
\]
and the evaluation subspace
\[
\mathcal L(P):=
\left\{
(B\xi_z+b)_{z\in Z(P)}:
B\in\mathbb R^{d\times q},\ b\in\mathbb R^d
\right\}.
\tag{44}
\]
The set \(\mathcal K(t)\) is nonempty, compact, and convex, while
\(\mathcal L(P)\) is a linear subspace. Expanding the Euclidean inner
product shows that
\[
\mathcal L(P)^\perp=\mathcal U(P).
\tag{45}
\]
Moreover, (18) is equivalent to
\[
\mathcal K(t)\cap\mathcal L(P)\neq\varnothing.
\tag{46}
\]

If \(x\in\mathcal K(t)\cap\mathcal L(P)\) and
\(u\in\mathcal L(P)^\perp\), then
\[
\min_{y\in\mathcal K(t)}\langle u,y\rangle
\leq\langle u,x\rangle=0.
\]
The product structure in (43) makes the minimum on the left equal to the
sum in (19), proving necessity.

Conversely, suppose the intersection in (46) is empty. Choose
\(x^\star\in\mathcal K(t)\) minimizing its Euclidean distance to
\(\mathcal L(P)\), let \(\ell^\star\) be the orthogonal projection of
\(x^\star\) onto \(\mathcal L(P)\), and set
\[
u:=x^\star-\ell^\star.
\tag{47}
\]
Then \(u\neq0\) and \(u\in\mathcal L(P)^\perp\). The first-order condition
for minimizing squared distance over the convex set \(\mathcal K(t)\) gives
\[
\langle u,x-x^\star\rangle\geq0
\qquad(x\in\mathcal K(t)).
\tag{48}
\]
Since \(u\perp\ell^\star\),
\[
\min_{x\in\mathcal K(t)}\langle u,x\rangle
=
\langle u,x^\star\rangle
=
\|u\|_2^2>0.
\tag{49}
\]
Using the product minimum again proves (20), hence (18)-(20).

Each set \(\mathcal S_z(t_z)\) is convex in the
\(d(q+1)\)-dimensional parameter space in (8). Helly's theorem therefore
implies that an empty intersection is already witnessed by at most
\[
d(q+1)+1
\]
of these sets. If the selected depth regions are nonempty, apply the dual
argument above to the restricted product and restricted evaluation map.
This gives a witness supported on the selected set \(J\), with the moment
equations (22).

We now audit the strict-threshold equivalence. Fix \(\alpha>0\) and a rank
\(q\). If \(\mathcal T_q=\varnothing\), then
\(\mathcal P_q^{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\) by (15), so
\[
\mathfrak A_q(C)=0<\alpha,
\]
and every rank-\(q\) threshold allocation is infeasible. If
\(\mathcal T_q\neq\varnothing\), the maximum in (16) is attained. Therefore
\[
\mathfrak A_q(C)<\alpha
\tag{50}
\]
if and only if there is no feasible allocation satisfying
\[
\sum_z t_z\geq2^q\alpha.
\tag{51}
\]
Indeed, a feasible allocation satisfying (51) contradicts (50) by (16),
while failure of (50) lets an attaining allocation in (16) satisfy (51).
Thus no unattained supremum can occur at the strict threshold.

For \(q=0\), every singleton is admissible and
\[
\mathfrak A_0(C)=\max_{z\in D_+}M_z.
\tag{52}
\]
For \(q=1,2\), Helly's theorem and the dual give the respective
\(2d+1\) and \(3d+1\) witnesses stated in (25)-(28). Finally, (12) gives
\[
\mathfrak A(C)<\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak A_q(C)<\alpha\quad(q=0,1,2).
\]
Together with (50)-(52), this proves the full equivalence (23)-(28),
including every empty-rank case.

It remains to prove the segment consequence. Choose
\(x_a\in C_a\) and \(x_b\in C_b\) attaining \(M_a\) and \(M_b\), and let
\(s\) be their affine interpolant on \([a,b]\). Since \(a\equiv b\pmod2\),
the midpoint \(m\) in (29) is a lattice point. Joint root concavity in
accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` gives
\[
\delta_m(s(m))^{1/d}
\geq
\frac{\delta_a(x_a)^{1/d}+\delta_b(x_b)^{1/d}}2
=
\frac{M_a^{1/d}+M_b^{1/d}}2.
\tag{53}
\]
The segment \([a,b]\) is admissible: the two chosen endpoint values define
its affine selector, and convexity makes it feasible over the whole segment.
Its rank-one capacity includes the two endpoints, the midpoint, and only
additional nonnegative terms. Thus (53) gives (30).

For each \(z\in D_+\), the fiber \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.
Its centroid belongs to \(K_z(\gamma_dw_z)\) by Grünbaum's centroid
inequality, so
\[
M_z\geq\gamma_dw_z.
\tag{54}
\]
Substituting (54) into (30) proves (32), and the rank-zero polytope
\(\{z\}\) proves (33).

## External sources

Eduard Helly, "Über Mengen konvexer Körper mit gemeinschaftlichen Punkten,"
Jahresbericht der Deutschen Mathematiker-Vereinigung 32 (1923), 175-176.
Exact form used: for a finite family of convex subsets of
\(\mathbb R^m\), if the whole intersection is empty, then some subfamily of
at most \(m+1\) sets already has empty intersection. Applicability: the
sets \(\mathcal S_z(t_z)\) in (13) are convex subsets of the parameter space
\(\mathbb R^{d(q+1)}\).

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by
Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261,
Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pp. 1259-1260,
https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. Exact form used: if
\(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(c(K)\), then
every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c(K)\)
satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap J)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K).
\]
Applicability: every \(z\in D_+\) has \(w_z>0\), so \(C_z\) is a
full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\), and (54) follows from its
centroid.
