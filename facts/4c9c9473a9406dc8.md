## Statement

Let $Z=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,1)\}$. Two copies of the uniform probability on $Z$ have a midpoint coupling whose image has equal atoms at $(1/2,1/2),(3/2,1),(1,3/2)$. The same probability is realized by a median-pair coupling at base $0$ for normalized mixed volume on $[-1,1]\times Z$. Its maximum closed-halfspace depth is exactly $1/3<4/9$.

## Proof

Let $a=(1,0),b=(0,1),c=(1,1)$. The eight sums of subsets of $\{a,b,c\}$ have convex hull $Z$, hence $Z=[0,a]+[0,b]+[0,c]$. Its three tiles
$$
Q_1=[0,a]+[0,b],\qquad Q_2=a+[0,b]+[0,c],\qquad Q_3=b+[0,a]+[0,c]
$$
are all contained in $Z$ and each has area one. The interiors of $Q_2,Q_3$ lie in $x>1,y>1$ respectively, so each is disjoint from the interior of $Q_1$. Also the interior of $Q_2$ satisfies $x>y$ and that of $Q_3$ satisfies $y>x$. The six listed vertices give area three by the polygon area formula, so the tiles cover up to a null set. Reflecting a uniform point about its own tile center preserves the uniform probability on $Z$ and sends its pair midpoint to that center. Each tile carries probability $1/3$ and the three centers are exactly the listed ones.

In the actual product $[-1,1]\times Z$, each integer fiber carries mass $1/3$. The normalized median probability marginals of 2f355ace664c0068 have weights $2/3$ at their respective outer bases and $1/3$ at the central base. Couple the two outer parts together and the two central parts together, using the tile reflection for both continuous-coordinate couplings. Both maps take the ordinary midpoint and the resulting target has exactly the three atoms above.

The atom centers are noncollinear. A point outside their triangle has a separating halfspace of mass zero. At a point inside the triangle, some barycentric coordinate is positive; the closed halfspace where that coordinate is at least its value at the point contains just the corresponding atom. Conversely any halfspace containing a point of the triangle contains at least one atom by convexity of the open complement. Thus depth is $1/3$ throughout the triangle, including its boundary, and the maximum is $1/3<4/9$.
