## Statement

For an integer $m\ge1$ and $0\le x_j\le h<1$ with some $x_j>0$, put $\rho=\prod_j(1-x_j)$, $E=\rho^{-1}-1$, and
$$
L=\left(1-\frac{\sum_j x_j}{E}\right)\prod_j(1+x_j/E),\qquad
R=\rho(E+h)\left[1-\frac{(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)}{h^{m+1}}\right].
$$

Fix $m$, $h\in(0,1)$ and $E>0$ such that $\Omega=\{x\in[0,h]^m:\prod_j(1-x_j)=1/(1+E)\}$ is nonempty. The gap $L-R$ attains its minimum on $\Omega$. Every minimizer has $r$ coordinates equal to $h$, $\ell$ coordinates all equal to one $t\in(0,h)$, and $m-r-\ell$ coordinates equal to zero. If $\ell>0$ then $t=1-[(1+E)^{-1}(1-h)^{-r}]^{1/\ell}$, and if $\ell=0$ then $(1-h)^r=(1+E)^{-1}$.

Consequently, if the gap is nonnegative at every nonzero upper face with some $x_j=h$ in all dimensions, its nonnegativity for all interior vectors is equivalent to its nonnegativity on all equal interior vectors. A counterexample would then have an equal-vector witness of at least two coordinates.

## Proof

The constraint is closed in the compact cube and excludes the all-zero vector. Hence the continuous gap attains a minimum on $\Omega$. If two unequal coordinates $x_i,x_j$ are interior, fix the other coordinates and put $u=1-x_i$, $v=1-x_j$, $k=uv$. With $k$ fixed the pair sum $T_0=u+k/u$ varies over an open interval around its original value since its derivative in $u$ is $1-k/u^2\ne0$ there. These variations remain feasible and keep $E$ fixed. With $S_o=\sum_{l\ne i,j}x_l$ and $C_*=\prod_{l\ne i,j}(E+x_l)/E^{m+1}>0$, the varying expression is
$$
L(T_0)=C_*(E-S_o-2+T_0)\big[(E+1)^2-(E+1)T_0+k\big].
$$
The moving factor in the product defining $R$ is $(h-1)^2+(h-1)T_0+k$. Thus the gap has second derivative $-2C_*(E+1)<0$ along the feasible open interval. Its midpoint value strictly exceeds the average at two distinct sufficiently close symmetric points, ruling out a local minimum. All interior coordinates at any minimizer must therefore be equal. The displayed formula for $t$ and the zero-interior feasibility condition follow by taking the product of $1-x_j$ at this form.

Deleting a zero coordinate preserves $E$ and $L$, and preserves $R$ because its numerator factor $h$ cancels a denominator factor $h$. If an interior vector has a negative gap, minimize at its fixed $E,h$. The attained negative minimum cannot use an upper coordinate under the hypothesis. Deleting its zeros leaves a nonempty equal interior vector with the same negative gap. Conversely such a vector is already an interior counterexample. In dimension one, substituting $E=x/(1-x)$ gives
$$
L-R=\frac{x(h-x)[2h(1-x)+x]}{h^2(1-x)}\ge0
\qquad(0<x\le h<1).
$$
Thus a reduced counterexample has at least two coordinates.
