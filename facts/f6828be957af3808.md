## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) a convex body. Retain the canonical objects \(C_{\mathbf I}:=\{y:(\mathbf I,y)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf I}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf I})\), \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(W:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf I}>0\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. Put \(\mathcal I:=\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\). For \(\mathbf I\in\mathcal I\), let \(\mu_{\mathbf I}\) be Lebesgue measure restricted to \(C_{\mathbf I}\), and let \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}:=\mu_{\mathbf I}/w_{\mathbf I}\) be its uniform probability law, with exactly the normalization of 03f650d1c4f1b86d. Write \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), over closed ambient halfspaces, and \(h_{\max}(C):=\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)\). The existence of this last maximum is part of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f. All logarithms below are natural.

First suppose every positive-volume integer fiber is symmetric about zero, and fix \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal I\). An admissible scheme consists of a finite nonnegative central measure \(\sigma\) on \(C_{\mathbf J}\) and finitely many finite nonnegative joint measures \(\pi_a\), each on a product of \(s_a\in\{2,3\}\) pairwise distinct positive fibers indexed by \(\mathbf I_{a,1},\ldots,\mathbf I_{a,s_a}\). Its barycentric coefficients \(\theta_{a,i}>0\) satisfy \(\sum_i\theta_{a,i}=1\) and \(\sum_i\theta_{a,i}\mathbf I_{a,i}=\mathbf J\). Require \(\sigma\) to be symmetric about zero, each \(\pi_a\) to be invariant under simultaneous reflection of its continuous coordinates, and the global measure inequalities
\[
\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}\sigma+
\sum_{a,i:\,\mathbf I_{a,i}=\mathbf I}(\operatorname{pr}_i)_\#\pi_a
\le\mu_{\mathbf I}
\qquad(\mathbf I\in\mathcal I).
\tag{1}
\]
Define \(B_a(y_1,\ldots,y_{s_a}):=\sum_i\theta_{a,i}y_i\) and the new generalized kernel \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}:=\sigma+\sum_a(B_a)_\#\pi_a\). This kernel extends the type of generalized kernel in 604889294a17d7df; it is not identified with the canonical two-fiber midpoint kernel. Let \(\mathcal E_{2,3}(\mathbf J)\) consist of all subsets \(E\subseteq\mathcal I\) of size two or three that admit strictly positive barycentric coefficients with average \(\mathbf J\). Then the maximum admissible kernel mass is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max\quad&x_\circ+\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{2,3}(\mathbf J)}x_E,\\
\text{subject to}\quad&
\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}x_\circ+
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{2,3}(\mathbf J)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in\mathcal I),\\
&x_\circ\ge0,\qquad x_E\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Repeated source sets with different coefficient vectors do not change this equality. Every admissible scheme satisfies \(h_S((\mathbf J,0))\ge|\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}|/2\). The symmetry restriction also leaves the optimum unchanged if schemes without reflection invariance are initially allowed.

Now remove the symmetry assumption. For any unordered triple \(E=\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2,\mathbf I_3\}\subseteq\mathcal I\) of distinct indices with \(\mathbf I_1+\mathbf I_2+\mathbf I_3=3\mathbf J\), let \(\nu_E^{(3)}\) be the law of the average of independent samples with laws \(\lambda_{\mathbf I_1},\lambda_{\mathbf I_2},\lambda_{\mathbf I_3}\). Let \(\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\) be the average law of three independent samples with law \(\lambda_{\mathbf J}\). The subscript zero indicates this central reference, not a distinct-source triple. The target has positive volume, and the triple deficit \(\Delta_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(E):=\log(w_{\mathbf J}^3/\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}w_{\mathbf I})\) is nonnegative. As Borel measures,
\[
\nu_E^{(3)}\le e^{\Delta_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(E)}\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}.
\tag{3}
\]
The central reference has a compactly supported log-concave Lebesgue density, and both laws are supported in \(C_{\mathbf J}\).

For any finite collection of these triples and masses \(x_E\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E\le w_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in\mathcal I\), put \(M_3:=\sum_E x_E\) and \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}:=\sum_E x_E\nu_E^{(3)}\). The symbol \(M_3\) is a kernel mass, distinct from the maximum atom \(M(w)\) in 604889294a17d7df. If \(M_3>0\) and every triple with \(x_E>0\) satisfies \(\Delta_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(E)\le L\), where \(L\ge0\), there exists \(y^\star\in C_{\mathbf J}\), selected before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,y^\star))\ge\frac{M_3}{6(1+L)}.
\tag{4}
\]
If every used deficit is zero, then \(\nu_E^{(3)}=\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\) for every used triple, \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}=M_3\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\), and
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,c_{\mathbf J}))\ge M_3/e,
\qquad
c_{\mathbf J}:=\int y\,d\lambda_{\mathbf J}(y).
\tag{5}
\]

For the scalar consumer, let \(\mathcal E_3(\mathbf J,L)\) be the set of unordered triples of distinct positive indices with sum \(3\mathbf J\) and deficit at most \(L\). Define

An empty triple family gives \(P_3=0\). All maxima and minima in (6) are attained, and only zero and the finitely many attained triple deficits need be considered as cutoffs. Retain the exact canonical two-fiber score of 03f071403cdfc69f and give it the explicit alias

## Proof

The projection of a compact body onto its integer-coordinate space is compact and contains finitely many lattice points. Thus \(\mathcal I\), all families of source sets, and all lattice sums used below are finite. Positive-volume fibers are compact full-dimensional convex bodies, so the stated uniform laws are defined. Convexity gives \(\sum_i\theta_i C_{\mathbf I_i}\subseteq C_{\sum_i\theta_i\mathbf I_i}\) whenever \(\theta_i>0\) and \(\sum_i\theta_i=1\). In particular, every barycentric output used here lies in its target fiber. Zero-volume fibers contribute the zero measure to mixed volume, even if they are nonempty.

We first establish the global halfspace transfer in a form that does not require symmetry or independence. Consider any of the schemes in (1), temporarily dropping its reflection requirements, and any closed ambient halfspace \(H\). Write \(H_{\mathbf I}:=\{y:(\mathbf I,y)\in H\}\). If all source points of a tuple lie outside a proper closed halfspace, their strictly weighted convex combination also lies outside it: for an affine inequality defining the halfspace, all the corresponding scores are strictly negative and their weighted sum is strictly negative. The barycentric base identity therefore gives
\[
\mathbf1_{H_{\mathbf J}}(B_a(y_1,\ldots,y_{s_a}))
\le\sum_{i=1}^{s_a}\mathbf1_{H_{\mathbf I_{a,i}}}(y_i).
\tag{13}
\]
For the whole space this inequality is immediate. Integrating (13), adding the central measure, and using the global measure inequalities on the Borel sections \(H_{\mathbf I}\) proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}(H_{\mathbf J})
&\le \sigma(H_{\mathbf J})+
\sum_{a,i}((\operatorname{pr}_i)_\#\pi_a)(H_{\mathbf I_{a,i}})\\
&\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathcal I}\mu_{\mathbf I}(H_{\mathbf I})
=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The same argument applies when only triple components are present. It accounts for every use of a shared source and for any use of the target as a source. In particular it does not charge one source separately for different triples after its capacity has been exhausted.

If \(H\) contains \((\mathbf J,y)\), then \(H_{\mathbf J}\) is either a proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^d\) containing \(y\), or the entire space. The latter includes every vertical ambient halfspace containing the chosen point. Consequently a probability-depth lower bound for the kernel at \(y\) transfers through (14) to every containing ambient halfspace. This argument includes halfspaces with entire fibers on their boundary, since (13) used the complement of a closed halfspace and (14) evaluated the closed sections directly.

For a symmetric scheme, each barycentric pushforward and the central measure are invariant under \(y\mapsto-y\). If a continuous closed halfspace \(G\) contains zero, then \(G\cup(-G)=\mathbb R^d\); hence symmetry gives \(2\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}(G)\ge|\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}|\). The whole-space section satisfies the same inequality. With (14) this proves the asserted depth bound at \((\mathbf J,0)\). This point belongs to \(S\), because a nonempty convex symmetric target fiber contains zero. For any scheme without reflection invariance on these centered fibers, average each joint measure with its simultaneous reflection, and average the central measure with its reflection. Reflection preserves each \(\mu_{\mathbf I}\), so applying it to (1) and averaging preserves all global capacities. This operation preserves every component mass and makes the resulting scheme symmetric. Thus requiring symmetry cannot lower the optimal kernel mass.

To prove (2), integrate (1) over each whole fiber. For a given scheme, set \(x_\circ=|\sigma|\) and \(x_E=\sum_{a:\{\mathbf I_{a,1},\ldots,\mathbf I_{a,s_a}\}=E}|\pi_a|\). A marginal has the same mass as its joint measure. The resulting inequalities are exactly the constraints in (2), while pushforward and finite nonnegative summation preserve total mass. Thus the objective equals \(|\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(\le3)}|\). Aggregation remains valid when the components use different coefficient vectors for the same source set.

Conversely, for each \(E\in\mathcal E_{2,3}(\mathbf J)\), choose any one of its admissible positive coefficient vectors and an ordering of its sources. Given a feasible point of (2), use \(\sigma=x_\circ\lambda_{\mathbf J}\) and \(\pi_E=x_E\bigotimes_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\), omitting zero-mass components. At a fixed source \(\mathbf I\), the aggregate measure used is exactly
\[
\left(\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}x_\circ+
\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E\right)\lambda_{\mathbf I}
=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}x_\circ+
\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E}{w_{\mathbf I}}\mu_{\mathbf I}
\le\mu_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{15}
\]
This is the required measure domination, including any simultaneous central and component use of \(\mathbf J\). The uniform laws are symmetric, so these products and the central measure satisfy the reflection conditions. Their output mass is the objective in (2). Finally the feasible set is closed, nonempty, and bounded: \(x_\circ\le w_{\mathbf J}\), and each \(x_E\) is bounded by the weight of each of its sources. It is finite dimensional. Thus (2) has an attained maximum, which its product realization attains as a scheme.

For the shape-independent comparison, fix a distinct-source triple and its integer average \(\mathbf J\). The Brunn-Minkowski inequality in the exact form recorded in accepted fact 03f650d1c4f1b86d, applied twice to compact convex fibers, gives
\[
w_{\mathbf J}^{1/d}
\ge \operatorname{vol}_d\left((C_{\mathbf I_1}+C_{\mathbf I_2}+C_{\mathbf I_3})/3\right)^{1/d}
\ge\frac{w_{\mathbf I_1}^{1/d}+w_{\mathbf I_2}^{1/d}+w_{\mathbf I_3}^{1/d}}3
\ge\left(\prod_{i=1}^3w_{\mathbf I_i}\right)^{1/(3d)}.
\tag{16}
\]
The contained Minkowski average has positive volume, so \(w_{\mathbf J}>0\). Raising (16) to the power \(3d\) proves \(\Delta_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(E)\ge0\).

For \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), define the compact convex sets in \(\mathbb R^{2d}\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_E(y)&:=\{(y_1,y_2):y_1\in C_{\mathbf I_1},\ y_2\in C_{\mathbf I_2},\
3y-y_1-y_2\in C_{\mathbf I_3}\},\\
D_0(y)&:=\{(y_1,y_2):y_1,y_2,3y-y_1-y_2\in C_{\mathbf J}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Tonelli's theorem and the change of variables from a sum to its average give density versions
\[
f_E(y)=\frac{3^d\operatorname{vol}_{2d}D_E(y)}{\prod_{i=1}^3w_{\mathbf I_i}},
\qquad
f_0(y)=\frac{3^d\operatorname{vol}_{2d}D_0(y)}{w_{\mathbf J}^3}.
\tag{18}
\]
For example, the first numerator is \(3^d\int\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf I_1}}(y_1)\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf I_2}}(y_2)\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf I_3}}(3y-y_1-y_2)\,dy_1\,dy_2\); integrating it in \(y\) cancels the factor \(3^d\) and gives the product of the three volumes. Thus both formulas are normalized probability densities.

Consider \(T_y(y_1,y_2):=(3y-y_1-y_2,y_1)\). It satisfies \(T_y^3=\operatorname{id}\). Grouping each pair of scalar coordinates, its linear part is a direct sum of the matrices \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), each of determinant one. It therefore preserves \(2d\)-dimensional volume. A pair in (17) represents the ordered triple \((y_1,y_2,3y-y_1-y_2)\), whose entries sum to \(3y\). The map \(T_y\) cyclically permutes these three entries. Thus the three sets \(D_E(y),T_yD_E(y),T_y^2D_E(y)\) have equal volume, and their represented triples have fiber orders respectively \((\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2,\mathbf I_3)\), \((\mathbf I_3,\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2)\), and \((\mathbf I_2,\mathbf I_3,\mathbf I_1)\).

Choose any one element from each of these three sets. In each of the three represented coordinate positions their average takes one point from each source fiber, so it belongs to \(C_{\mathbf J}\) by convexity. The sum of the three averaged entries is still \(3y\). This proves the set containment
\[
\bigl(D_E(y)+T_yD_E(y)+T_y^2D_E(y)\bigr)/3\subseteq D_0(y).
\tag{19}
\]
When \(\operatorname{vol}_{2d}D_E(y)>0\), apply the same accepted Brunn-Minkowski statement in dimension \(2d\), twice, to the three equal-volume compact convex sets in (19). It gives \(\operatorname{vol}_{2d}D_0(y)\ge\operatorname{vol}_{2d}D_E(y)\). When the latter volume is zero, including the empty-set case, this inequality follows from nonnegativity without invoking Brunn-Minkowski. Substituting in (18) gives \(f_E(y)\le e^{\Delta_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(E)}f_0(y)\) at every \(y\), including points where \(f_0(y)=0\). Integration proves (3).

The probability density \(w_{\mathbf J}^{-1}\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf J}}\) is log-concave. Applying the convolution-closure statement recorded in 03f650d1c4f1b86d twice, then rescaling the sum by \(1/3\), proves log-concavity of \(f_0\). The samples and their averages lie in the compact convex set \(C_{\mathbf J}\); hence the reference has compact support and a finite first absolute moment. Convexity likewise puts the support of every \(\nu_E^{(3)}\) in \(C_{\mathbf J}\).

For a feasible triple packing, use the independent joint measures \(\pi_E=x_E\bigotimes_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\). At every source, total usage is \((\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E)\lambda_{\mathbf I}\le\mu_{\mathbf I}\). Thus the global transfer (14) applies to \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}\), even though these fibers or couplings need not be symmetric. If \(M_3>0\) and the used deficits are at most \(L\), (3) gives \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}/M_3\le e^L\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\) on every Borel set.

Apply the independent probability theorem in 03f071403cdfc69f with its probability \(\nu\) equal to \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}/M_3\), its reference probability \(Q\) equal to \(\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\), and its cutoff equal to this \(L\). This is an explicit substitution of that theorem's probability symbol \(Q\); it has no relation to the tree depth used later. All its hypotheses have been checked: the reference has a measurable log-concave Lebesgue density and compact support, the mixture is a probability, and the stated Borel domination holds. It supplies \(y^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)})\subseteq C_{\mathbf J}\) with continuous-space halfspace depth at least \(1/[6(1+L)]\) for the normalized kernel. Multiplying by \(M_3\) and applying (14) proves (4), including the whole-space target section of a vertical halfspace.

If a used deficit is zero, (3) says that one probability measure is dominated by the other with coefficient one. Their difference is a nonnegative measure of total mass zero, so \(\nu_E^{(3)}=\nu_{\mathbf J,0}^{(3)}\). Summing proves the claimed synchronization of the entire output law. Independence is not needed to compute its mean: the central average law has mean \((c_{\mathbf J}+c_{\mathbf J}+c_{\mathbf J})/3=c_{\mathbf J}\). This centroid belongs to \(C_{\mathbf J}\). The exact log-concave centroid inequality recorded in accepted fact 5bd39b316ef3c194 states that every halfspace containing the centroid of a log-concave probability density has probability at least \(1/e\). It applies here because \(f_0\) is normalized, log-concave, and compactly supported. Consequently every continuous closed halfspace containing \(c_{\mathbf J}\) has kernel mass at least \(M_3/e\). The global transfer (14) proves (5) for the same point and every containing ambient halfspace.

We verify the finite optimization assertions in (6). At a fixed \(\mathbf J,L\), every variable in its primal is bounded by each of its positive source weights. Its feasible set is nonempty and compact, so the maximum exists. Summing its source constraints gives \(3\sum_E x_E\le W\). The dual is feasible, for instance with all \(u_{\mathbf I}=1/3\). Replacing any coordinate \(u_{\mathbf I}>1\) by one preserves every covering constraint and cannot increase the objective. Thus the dual minimum is attained on its nonempty compact feasible subset of \([0,1]^{\mathcal I}\).

Here is also a direct finite-dimensional justification of equality of these two optima. The empty-family case has primal and dual values zero. Otherwise let \(A\) be the incidence matrix, with entry one exactly when source \(\mathbf I\) belongs to triple \(E\), and let \(p\) be the primal optimum. Weak duality follows by multiplying the source inequalities by a nonnegative cover: \(\sum_E x_E\le\sum_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}(Ax)_{\mathbf I}\le\sum_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}\). In the Euclidean space with coordinates indexed by \(\mathcal I\) and one extra coordinate, consider the cone generated by \((A_{\cdot E},1)\), all \((e_{\mathbf I},0)\), and \((0,-1)\). It is the set of pairs \((s,t)\) for which some \(x\ge0\) has \(s\ge Ax\) and \(t\le\sum_E x_E\). This finitely generated cone is closed. One elementary verification is to remove linearly dependent generators from each nonnegative representation by subtracting a suitable multiple of a linear relation until some coefficient vanishes. Every point then has a representation on a linearly independent subfamily. Along a convergent sequence, one such subfamily occurs on a subsequence because there are finitely many. Its coefficients converge, by continuity of the inverse on its span, and remain nonnegative, proving closedness.

For any \(\varepsilon>0\), the point \(((w_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathcal I},p+\varepsilon)\) is outside this closed convex cone. There is a linear functional \((u,\beta)\) nonnegative on the cone and negative on this point. To see the separation directly, subtract the outside point from its nearest point in the closed cone. Convexity gives nonnegative inner product of this vector with every displacement from the nearest point into the cone. Applying this to zero and twice the nearest point shows that its inner product with the nearest point is zero. Its inner product with the outside point is then the negative squared distance, while its inner product with the whole cone is nonnegative. A nearest point exists by restricting the distance minimization to a sufficiently large closed ball. Values of the resulting functional on the listed generators imply \(u\ge0\), \(\beta\le0\), and \(u^\top A_{\cdot E}+\beta\ge0\) for every triple. Since every \(w_{\mathbf I}>0\), negativity on the separated point rules out \(\beta=0\). Hence \(u/(-\beta)\) is a nonnegative cover with objective less than \(p+\varepsilon\). Letting \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero and using the attained dual minimum proves that this minimum is at most \(p\). Combined with weak duality, this proves the equality in (6).
