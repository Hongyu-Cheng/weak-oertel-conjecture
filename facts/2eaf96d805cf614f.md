## Statement

Let $m\ge1$ be an integer and $p\in[0,1]$. Let $E_0,E_1,E_2$ be mean-one exponentials and $Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1)$ have shape $m$ and scale one, all independent. Put $P_m(p)=\Pr(E_1+pE_2\ge E_0+(p/m)Y)$. Then
$$
P_m(p)\ge P_m(1)=\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m\frac{5m+3}{4(m+1)}.
$$
Also $P_m(0)=1/2$. Equality in the endpoint bound holds exactly when $p=1$ or $m=1$. For $m=1$ the probability is $1/2$ on the entire interval.

## Proof

For $m=1$ the two sides of the comparison are independent copies of the same continuous law, so $P_1(p)=1/2$ for every $p\in[0,1]$, in agreement with the formula. For arbitrary $m$, the probability at $p=0$ is $1/2$ by the symmetry of $E_1$ and $E_0$. The probabilities are continuous in $p$ on $[0,1]$. Indeed, couple the variables for every $p$ using the same exponentials and $Y$. The signed difference converges almost surely when $p$ converges, and its limiting law has no atom at zero by conditioning on all terms except $E_1$. Bounded convergence applies to the sign indicator.

For $0<p<1$ and $x\ge0$, conditioning on $pE_2$ gives
$$
\Pr(E_1+pE_2\ge x)
=\int_0^x e^{-(x-u)}p^{-1}e^{-u/p}\,du+e^{-x/p}
=\frac{e^{-x}-p e^{-x/p}}{1-p}.
$$
The Laplace transform of $E_0+(p/m)Y$ at $s\ge0$ is $(1+s)^{-1}(1+sp/m)^{-m}$, since $Y$ is a sum of $m$ independent mean-one exponentials. Evaluating this transform at $1$ and $1/p$ gives
$$
P_m(p)=\frac{N(p)}{1-p},
\qquad N(p)=\frac12(1+p/m)^{-m}-A\frac{p^2}{1+p},
\qquad A=(1+1/m)^{-m}.
$$
Since $N(1)=0$, continuity gives
$$
P_m(1)=-N'(1)
=A\left(\frac{m}{2(m+1)}+\frac34\right)
=A\frac{5m+3}{4(m+1)}.
$$
For $m>1$, the binomial expansion gives
$$
A^{-1}\ge 2+\frac{m-1}{2m}
=\frac52-\frac{1}{2m}
>\frac52-\frac1{m+1}
=\frac{5m+3}{2(m+1)}.
$$
In particular $P_m(1)<P_m(0)$ for $m>1$.

Fix $m>1$. The second derivative is
$$
N''(p)=\frac{m+1}{2m}(1+p/m)^{-m-2}-\frac{2A}{(1+p)^3}.
$$
Let $R(p)$ be the ratio of the first term to the positive magnitude of the second. Then
$$
(\log R)'(p)=\frac{3}{1+p}-\frac{m+2}{m+p}
=\frac{(m-1)(2-p)}{(1+p)(m+p)}>0
\quad (0\le p\le1).
$$
Also $N''(1)=A(m-1)/(4(m+1))>0$. Consequently there is a $c\in[0,1)$ with $N''>0$ on $(c,1]$ and, if $c>0$, $N''<0$ on $[0,c)$.

Set $Q(p)=N(p)-P_m(1)(1-p)$. Then $Q(1)=Q'(1)=0$, $Q(0)=1/2-P_m(1)>0$, and $Q''=N''$. For $c\le p<1$, the integral $Q(p)=\int_p^1(t-p)Q''(t)\,dt$ is strictly positive. If $c>0$, concavity on $[0,c]$ bounds $Q$ below by the chord between $Q(0)>0$ and $Q(c)>0$. Thus $Q(p)>0$ throughout $[0,1)$, and dividing by $1-p$ gives the strict comparison for $0<p<1$. The endpoint values and the case $m=1$ give the stated equality criterion.
