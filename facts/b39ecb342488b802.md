## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)\subset\mathbb R^2$ and $V=|T|$, where $|\cdot|$ denotes planar area. For $r>0$ and every $t\in\mathbb R$, the minimum of $|rT\cap\lbrace x:w\cdot x\ge t\rbrace|/V$ over $w_1,w_2\ge0$ with $w_1+w_2=1$ is
$$
P_r(t)=
\begin{cases}
r^2,& t\le0,\\
(r-t)^2,& 0\le t\le r/3,\\
4t(r-2t),& r/3\le t\le r/2,\\
0,& t\ge r/2 .
\end{cases}
$$
In particular, $P_r(t)\ge(4/9)r^2$ for $t\le r/3$, with equality at $t=r/3$.

## Proof

If $t\le0$, every eligible normal captures the whole triangle. If $t\ge r/2$, the normal $(1/2,1/2)$ captures a set of zero area, including the supporting edge at $t=r/2$.

Suppose $0<\theta=t/r<1/2$. After division of the coordinates by $r$ and interchange of the two coordinates if needed, write $w=(\alpha,1-\alpha)$ with $1/2\le\alpha\le1$. The heights of the three vertices of $T$ are $0$, $1-\alpha$, and $\alpha$. If $\theta\le1-\alpha$, the complement triangle at the origin has edge fractions $\theta/\alpha$ and $\theta/(1-\alpha)$. If $\theta\ge1-\alpha$, the cap triangle at $e_1$ has edge fractions $(\alpha-\theta)/\alpha$ and $(\alpha-\theta)/(2\alpha-1)$. Thus the fraction of $rT$ captured is
$$
\begin{cases}
1-\theta^2/[\alpha(1-\alpha)],&1/2\le\alpha\le1-\theta,\\
(\alpha-\theta)^2/[\alpha(2\alpha-1)],&1-\theta\le\alpha\le1 .
\end{cases}
$$
The branches agree at $\alpha=1-\theta$. The first is nonincreasing in $\alpha$ on its interval. The derivative of the second has the sign of $\alpha(4\theta-1)-\theta$, since its other factors are positive there. If $\theta\le1/3$, this quantity is nonpositive throughout that interval, so $\alpha=1$ gives the global minimum $(1-\theta)^2$. If $1/3<\theta<1/2$, the sign changes from nonpositive to nonnegative at $\alpha=\theta/(4\theta-1)$. This value is at most one and exceeds $1-\theta$ by $(1-2\theta)^2/(4\theta-1)$. Substitution yields $4\theta(1-2\theta)$. Multiplying these fractions by $r^2$ gives the displayed area divided by $V$. The formulas coincide at every threshold interface. Each cap area is nonincreasing in its threshold, so its minimum over normals is also nonincreasing and has the asserted lower bound at thresholds at most $r/3$.
