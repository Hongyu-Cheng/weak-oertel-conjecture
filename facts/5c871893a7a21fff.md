## Statement

Among the area-one bodies with the fixed normals and cap bounds of the quantitative planar source-ball lemma, impose any further closed condition on the finite vector $(q_i)_i$. The resulting class is Hausdorff compact. If it is nonempty, all functions $\lambda_i=(1/\sigma_i)/\sum_j(1/\sigma_j)$ with $\sigma_i=\mathcal H_1(K\cap u_i^\perp)/\|u_i\|$, and the function $\min_i\lambda_i$, attain their minima. For $u_1=(1,0),u_2=(0,1),u_3=(-1,-1)$ and $c=128/279$, the class with $q_{(1)}+q_{(2)}+q_{(3)}/4\le1$ (increasing rearrangement) is nonempty.

## Proof

With $k=1$, the bounds give uniform origin-centered inner and outer balls. Blaschke selection in the outer ball gives subsequential Hausdorff limits. The inner ball remains contained in every limit, and indicators converge off the null boundary of the limit: exterior points eventually stay out, while any fixed interior point has a strictly positive support margin and eventually stays in. Thus area and each fixed cap area converge, and every closed condition survives. If $K_m\to K$ with Hausdorff distance $e_m<\rho$ and $\rho B_2\subset K$, comparison of support functions gives
$$
(1-e_m/\rho)K\subset K_m\subset (1+e_m/\rho)K .
$$
Intersecting with the fixed lines through zero proves convergence of the positive chord lengths. The inverse-section functions and their finite minimum are continuous, so they attain minima on the nonempty compact class. For the example use the area-one homothet about zero of $\operatorname{conv}((2,-1),(-1,2),(-1,-1))$. Each normal takes values $2,-1,-1$ at its three vertices, up to permutation. Its cap has area fraction $(2/3)^2=4/9=124/279$, and the displayed profile is $(2+1/4)(4/9)=1$.
