## Statement

On $[-1,1]$, let $r_L(x)=-L|x|$ for $L\ge1$. The two selectors obtained by clamping $g_\pm(x)=1/2\pm Lx-L|x|$ to $[0,1]$ tend almost everywhere to one half on their respective half intervals and zero on the other halves. No affine gauge makes the common roof uniformly bounded as $L\to\infty$. They are actual outside-support roof selectors from a compatible body with a uniform outward ramp. The reflected cap budgets agree. In the covariance-normalized convention $\Gamma=1$, the common limiting strip records are $S=1/2$, $A/h=-3/8$ and $A/h+S/2=-1/8$. The limiting transition integral $\int\tau(1-\tau)$ is $1/4$ for each cap, and its signed transition quadratic form is nonnegative.

## Proof

The midpoint defect $r_L(0)-(r_L(-1)+r_L(1))/2=L$ is invariant under subtraction of an affine function. A uniform bound on the shifted roof would bound its midpoint defect by twice that bound. For $g_+$ the argument is $1/2$ on $x\ge0$ and tends to $-\infty$ for each $x<0$. Reflection gives the other selector. The surviving half intervals have full dimension within the original facet.

Take the actual two-dimensional convex fiber with $-1\le x\le1$ and $-\sqrt{12}\le y\le-|x|/L$. Its limit is the rectangle of height $\sqrt{12}$ and width two. It can be used at every base of any fixed finite lattice-complete base by taking a product. The raw normals are $(\pm1/L,1)$, the threshold is $\delta_L/2$ and the width is $\delta_L=L^{-2}$. The strip argument at the roof is $q_\pm^F=g_\pm-1$. Reflection in $x$ preserves the actual body and interchanges the caps, including their ramp budgets. Let $\Gamma_L$ be the common covariance norm of the two raw continuous normals. Bounded convergence of the actual uniform fibers gives convergence of their covariances to the rectangle covariance. The vertical variance of that rectangle is $12/12=1$ and the horizontal normal component tends to zero, so $\Gamma_L\to1$. Dividing the normals and threshold by $\Gamma_L$ and using $h=\delta_L/\Gamma_L$ keeps the strip argument $q$ exactly unchanged.

Define $S=h^{-1}\int\mathbf1_{-1<q<0}\,dx\,dy$ and $A=\int q\mathbf1_{-1<q<0}\,dx\,dy$ per sampled fiber. For each $x$, the bottom is below the strip, the upper roof is below $q=0$, and $dy=\delta_L\,dq$. Thus $S/\Gamma_L$ and $(A/h)/\Gamma_L$ are the $x$ integrals of $\int_{-1}^{q^F}1\,dq$ and $\int_{-1}^{q^F}q\,dq$, with zero contribution for $q^F\le-1$. On the surviving half interval $q^F=-1/2$, giving $1/2$ and $-3/8$. The remaining contributing interval has length $1/(4L)$, so both its contributions tend to zero. The signed combination is $-1/8$. The lower strip endpoint meets the fiber on the surviving half interval and a shrinking extension, while the upper strip endpoint is absent. Hence the transition density there tends to one and the signed transition quadratic is nonnegative, since only outside support contributes. The limiting selector $\tau=1/2$ on that half interval gives $\int\tau(1-\tau)=1/4$.
