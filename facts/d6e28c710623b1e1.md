## Statement

Let $F\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a compact convex body, $x\in\operatorname{int}F$, $Q\subset\mathbb R^k$ a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone, and $v\in\operatorname{int}Q$, $k\ge1$. Use the unit counting measure if $m=0$. Let $n_v,J_*(v)$ be the normalized cone Laplace minimizer and value. Let $a$ minimize $B=\int_F[1+a\cdot(z-x)]_+^{k+1}\,dz$ and write $h(z)=[1+a\cdot(z-x)]_+$. Then the maximum mass of a density in $[0,1]$ on $F\times Q$ of finite first moments and centroid $(x,v)$ is
$$
T=\frac{(k+1)^k}{k!\,k^k}J_*(v)B.
$$
The unique optimizer almost everywhere is the indicator of the bounded cap $k n_v\cdot q\le (k+1)[1+a\cdot(z-x)]$ in $F\times Q$. Its normalized marginal on $F$ is $h^k/B$, the unique minimum-power density of mean $x$ with exponent $(k+1)/k$, and the conditional normal mean is $h(z)v$ wherever the marginal is positive. The unique minimizer of the hinge integral $\int_{F\times Q}[1+u\cdot(z-x)-b\cdot(q-v)]_+\,dz\,dq$ is $(u,b)=((k+1)a,k n_v)$, and its value is $T$.

## Proof

A finite hinge integral requires $b\in\operatorname{int}Q^*$. If $b\cdot w\le0$ for a nonzero cone vector, take a small product neighborhood of $(x,v)$ on which the hinge is positive. Disjoint translates of its normal ball by positive multiples of $w$ remain in $Q$ without decreasing the integrand. For strictly dual $b$, sublevel scaling and integration against $e^{-t}$ give
$$
|\{q\in Q:b\cdot q\le t\}|=\frac{J_Q(b)}{k!}t^k\quad(t\ge0),
\qquad
\int_Q[A-b\cdot q]_+\,dq=\frac{J_Q(b)}{(k+1)!}[A]_+^{k+1}.
$$
Set $\beta=b\cdot v>0$, $n=b/\beta$. The hinge objective separates into $J_Q(n)$, the factor $(1+\beta)^{k+1}/((k+1)!\beta^k)$, and the tangential power integral with parameter $u/(1+\beta)$. The three minima are attained uniquely at $n_v,\ \beta=k,\ u/(1+\beta)=a$. This gives $T$ and the stated cap.

At each $z$ the cap section has volume $\frac{(k+1)^k}{k!\,k^k}J_*(v)h(z)^k$. The power hinge identities give its total volume $T$ and tangential mean $x$. Under cone polar coordinates $q=t s$ on $n_v\cdot s=1$, the section law in $s$ is independent of $t$, by homogeneity of volume; the radial volume is proportional to $t^{k-1}\,dt$. The exponential cone law has mean $kv$ and radial mean $k$, so the mean of $s$ is $v$. Uniform volume up to $t=(k+1)h(z)/k$ has radial mean $h(z)$, yielding the asserted conditional mean. Its average is $v$ by $\int_Fh^{k+1}=\int_Fh^k$.

For every admissible density, integrate the optimal affine hinge argument against it: by the centroid constraint this is exactly its mass, and it is bounded by the integrated positive part because the density lies in $[0,1]$. The cap attains the bound, and equality off its null cutting plane forces the cap indicator. The power hinge theorem identifies the marginal's density minimization.
