## Statement

Let $P_i\subset\mathbb R^N$ be finite, $1\le i\le k$, and let $\gamma_i$ be finite nonnegative measures on $P_i$ of common mass. Let $\lambda_i>0$ sum to one, let $b(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i x_i$, and suppose $\gamma_i(\{x\})\le a_i(x)$ for finite nonnegative functions $a_i$ on $P_i$. Define
$$
c(z)=\max_{b(x)=z,\ x_i\in\operatorname{supp}\gamma_i}\ \min_i a_i(x_i),
$$
with the empty maximum zero. For any old coupling $\pi_0$ of the $\gamma_i$, one coupling $\pi$ of the same marginals satisfies $b_\#\pi_0\preceq_{\rm cx}b_\#\pi$ and $(b_\#\pi)(\{z\})\le c(z)$ for every $z$. Any fixed output atom ceiling dominating the displayed product envelope may also be used. Convex order tests all finite convex functions on the physical space.

## Proof

The zero-mass case uses the zero coupling. Otherwise fix a smooth uniformly strongly convex surplus $\phi$ on the physical Euclidean space and maximize $\int\phi(b(x))\,d\pi(x)$ over the compact finite-dimensional coupling polytope. By the inverse endpoint lemma, at each output $z$ of positive mass the optimizer assigns a single endpoint $F_i(z)$ in slot $i$, with $F_i(z)\in\operatorname{supp}\gamma_i$ and $\sum_i\lambda_iF_i(z)=z$. The full output atom is included in the marginal atom at $F_i(z)$ for each $i$, so its mass is at most $\min_i\gamma_i(\{F_i(z)\})\le\min_i a_i(F_i(z))\le c(z)$. In the zero-dimensional ambient space all supports of positive mass are the single point and the conclusion is immediate.

Let $\mathcal D$ be the set of couplings with output atoms bounded by $c$. It is compact and convex by the finitely many linear inequalities and nonempty by the surplus result for $\phi(z)=|z|^2$. Every finite convex function on $\mathbb R^N$ is a uniform limit on the finite output set of smooth uniformly strongly convex functions, by mollification and addition of a positive quadratic with coefficient tending to zero. The maxima over $\mathcal D$ for these approximants exceed or equal the values at $\pi_0$. Taking limits gives the same inequality for each convex test. For any finite list of such tests, their integral differences from $\pi_0$ form a compact convex set of vectors as $\pi$ ranges over $\mathcal D$. Disjointness from the closed nonnegative orthant would strictly separate that set by a nonnegative linear combination of the tests, contradicting the single-test inequality for that combination. The compact finite intersection property now yields one $\pi\in\mathcal D$ for every test.

The minimum at a reconstructed tuple is sharp from the atom ceilings alone. If each $P_i$ is a singleton, put mass $\min_i a_i$ on its sole point. The unique coupling then reaches this bound at its barycenter.
