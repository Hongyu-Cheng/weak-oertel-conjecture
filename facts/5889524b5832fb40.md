## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0.
\]
For an ambient closed halfspace \(H\), write
\[
H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\},\qquad
\mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z),
\]
put \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and define
\[
h_{\max}(C):=
\sup_{p\in S}\inf\{\mu_C(H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
Let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\), let
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{\frac1t\int_Kxf(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=t\right\},
\]
and put
\[
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z,\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
Put
\[
H:=\frac{2^nh_{\max}(C)}W.
\]

Let \(q=(q_e)\) be a nonzero off-diagonal same-parity fractional matching with \(q_e\geq0\) on unordered pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z\), with
\[
d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\leq\gamma_da_z.
\]
For every edge \(e\) with \(q_e>0\), set
\[
m_e:=\frac{z+z'}2,\qquad
\bar c_e:=\frac{c_z+c_{z'}}2,\qquad
D_e:=\frac12(D_z+D_{z'}),
\]
and define
\[
\Delta_C(q):=
\min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\sum_e q_e\bigl(\|s(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\bigr)_+.
\]
Choose the load-minimal primal-dual optimizer of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`, write its dual stress as \((v_e)\), and set
\[
\omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),\qquad
\Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
\]
\[
U_z:=\frac12\sum_{e\ni z}v_e,\qquad
\tau_z:=h_{D_z}(U_z),\qquad
T:=\sum_{z\in Z}\tau_z.
\]
Assume \(\Delta_C(q)>0\). Then \(T>0\). With
\[
E_T:=\{z\in Z:\tau_z>0\},\qquad
\rho_T:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E_T),
\]
\[
r_T:=
\min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_T)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}\{c_z-s(z):z\in E_T\},
\]
and
\[
A_T:=
\inf_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_T)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\frac1T\sum_{z\in E_T}\tau_z\|c_z-s(z)\|_{D_z},
\]
one has \(H>0\) and
\[
A_T-1\geq\frac{\Delta_C(q)}T,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
r_T+1\geq
2^{\,n-\rho_T}\frac{T}{HW},
\tag{2}
\]
and therefore
\[
(r_T+1)(A_T-1)
\geq
2^{\,n-\rho_T}\frac{\Delta_C(q)}{HW}.
\tag{3}
\]

Now let \((C_j)\) be any sequence of such convex bodies, with dimensions \(n_j,d_j\) allowed to vary, \(W_j>0\), and
\[
H_j:=\frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For every \(j\), choose an exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching \(q_j\). When \(q_j\neq0\), choose its load-minimal optimizer as in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`; when \(q_j=0\), take the trivial load-minimal zero stress. Put
\[
Q_j:=\sum_eq_{j,e},\quad
\Delta_j:=\Delta_{C_j}(q_j),\quad
\Omega_j:=\sum_e\omega_{j,e},\quad
T_j:=\sum_z\tau_{j,z}.
\]
On the eventually positive range, define \(E_{T,j},\rho_{T,j},r_{T,j},A_{T,j}\) by the preceding static definitions.
Put
\[
\delta_*:=\frac1{\log2}-1>0.
\]
Then, for all sufficiently large \(j\), \(Q_j,\Delta_j,\Omega_j,T_j\) are positive, and
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_j}{W_j}
\geq\frac{\delta_*}{2e},
\qquad
\frac{\Omega_j}{Q_j}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_j}{\Omega_j}
\geq\delta_*.
\tag{4}
\]
Writing \(\theta_j:=T_j/\Omega_j\in(0,1]\), there exist nonnegative scalar sequences \(\alpha_j,\beta_j,\varepsilon_j\to0\), defined on this eventually positive range and independent of any fixed-dimension assumption, such that
\[
A_{T,j}-1
\geq\frac{\delta_*-\alpha_j}{\theta_j},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
r_{T,j}+1
\geq
2^{\,n_j-\rho_{T,j}}
\frac{\theta_j(1/(2e)-\beta_j)}{H_j},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
(r_{T,j}+1)(A_{T,j}-1)
\geq
2^{\,n_j-\rho_{T,j}}
\frac{\delta_*/(2e)-\varepsilon_j}{H_j}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, after increasing the starting index so that the numerator is positive,
\[
\max\{r_{T,j}+1,A_{T,j}-1\}
\geq
2^{(n_j-\rho_{T,j})/2}
\sqrt{\frac{\delta_*/(2e)-\varepsilon_j}{H_j}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{8}
\]

The precise subsequence dichotomy is the following. For every subsequence \((j_k)\):

* if \(\liminf_{k\to\infty}T_{j_k}/W_{j_k}>0\), equivalently the ratio is eventually bounded below by a positive constant, then \(r_{T,j_k}\to\infty\);
* if \(T_{j_k}/W_{j_k}\to0\), then \(A_{T,j_k}\to\infty\).

Finally, for every sufficiently large \(j\) and \(z\in Z_j\), put
\[
\Sigma_{j,z}:=\frac1{a_{j,z}}\int_{C_{j,z}}
(x-c_{j,z})(x-c_{j,z})^{\mathsf T}\,dx
\]
and let the covariance-polar body of accepted fact `107b9182522633f9` be
\[
\mathcal E_{j,z}:=
\left\{u\in\mathbb R^{d_j}:
\max_{x\in C_{j,z}}
\left|\left\langle\Sigma_{j,z}^{-1}u,x-c_{j,z}\right\rangle\right|
\leq1\right\}.
\]
Define the two best affine Chebyshev radii
\[
\mathscr R_{D,j}:=
\inf_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_{T,j})\to\mathbb R^{d_j}\ \mathrm{affine}}
\max_{z\in E_{T,j}}\|c_{j,z}-s(z)\|_{D_{j,z}},
\]
\[
\mathscr R_{\mathcal E,j}:=
\inf_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_{T,j})\to\mathbb R^{d_j}\ \mathrm{affine}}
\max_{z\in E_{T,j}}\|c_{j,z}-s(z)\|_{\mathcal E_{j,z}}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathscr R_{\mathcal E,j}\geq\mathscr R_{D,j}\geq A_{T,j}.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently the second branch of the dichotomy forces the best covariance-normalized affine centroid spread to diverge.

## Proof

Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives, for the endpoint collapse of the chosen load-minimal stress,
\[
0\leq T\leq\Omega
\tag{10}
\]
and, for every affine map \(\widetilde s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\sum_{z\in Z}\tau_z
\bigl(\|c_z-\widetilde s(z)\|_{D_z}-1\bigr)
\geq\Delta_C(q).
\tag{11}
\]
If \(T=0\), nonnegativity of every \(\tau_z\) makes the left side of (11) zero, contradicting \(\Delta_C(q)>0\). Hence \(T>0\), so \(E_T\) is nonempty.

We now justify the affine domain in the definition of \(A_T\). Fix \(z_0\in E_T\) and let
\[
L:=\operatorname{span}(E_T-z_0).
\]
Every affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E_T)\to\mathbb R^d\) has the form
\[
s(z_0+u)=b+A_Lu\qquad(u\in L)
\]
for a linear map \(A_L:L\to\mathbb R^d\). Extend a basis of \(L\) to a basis of \(\mathbb R^n\), extend \(A_L\) linearly to a map \(A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and set
\[
\widetilde s(x):=b+A(x-z_0).
\]
Thus every affine map on \(\operatorname{aff}(E_T)\) is the restriction of an affine map on \(\mathbb R^n\). Applying (11) to this extension and using \(\tau_z=0\) outside \(E_T\) gives
\[
\frac1T\sum_{z\in E_T}\tau_z\|c_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}
\geq1+\frac{\Delta_C(q)}T.
\]
Taking the infimum over all such \(s\) proves (1).

The endpoint residual-capacity estimate in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` and the general depth-capacity estimate in accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b` give, respectively,
\[
R_{\rm sel}(C)
\geq2^{\,n-\rho_T}\frac{T}{r_T+1},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}R_{\rm sel}(C).
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(T>0\), (12) first shows \(h_{\max}(C)>0\), hence \(H>0\), and then gives
\[
HW=2^nh_{\max}(C)
\geq R_{\rm sel}(C)
\geq2^{\,n-\rho_T}\frac{T}{r_T+1}.
\]
Rearrangement proves (2). Both factors in (1) and (2) are positive, so multiplying them proves (3), with the factors \(2^{n-\rho_T}\), \(H\), and \(W\) unchanged.

We turn to the sequence. Accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b`, uniformly over every exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching and with no restriction that \(n_j\) or \(d_j\) be fixed, gives
\[
Q_j>0\ \text{eventually},
\qquad
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_j}{Q_j}\geq\delta_*.
\tag{13}
\]
Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives scalar asymptotics
\[
0\leq\Omega_j\leq Q_j,\qquad
\frac{Q_j}{W_j}\geq\frac1{2e}-o(1),
\qquad
\frac{Q_j-\Omega_j}{W_j}=o(1),
\qquad
\frac{\Omega_j}{W_j}\geq\frac1{2e}-o(1).
\tag{14}
\]
Here and below, every \(o(1)\) denotes a scalar sequence indexed by \(j\) and tending to zero; it does not hide a constant depending on fixed \(n\) or fixed \(d\).

All ratios in the following calculation are therefore defined for sufficiently large \(j\). Since the relevant quantities are nonnegative, (13) and the first mass lower bound in (14) imply
\[
\liminf_j\frac{\Delta_j}{W_j}
=\liminf_j
\left(\frac{\Delta_j}{Q_j}\frac{Q_j}{W_j}\right)
\geq\frac{\delta_*}{2e}.
\tag{15}
\]
For complete quantifier precision, given \(\eta>0\), both factors are eventually at least \(\delta_*-\eta\) and \(1/(2e)-\eta\), respectively; letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves (15). In particular, \(\Delta_j>0\) eventually. The static argument above then proves \(T_j>0\), and (10) gives \(\Omega_j\geq T_j>0\), for all sufficiently large \(j\).

Write
\[
q'_j:=\frac{Q_j}{W_j},
\qquad
\eta_j:=\frac{Q_j-\Omega_j}{W_j}\geq0.
\]
By (14), \(\liminf q'_j\geq1/(2e)\) and \(\eta_j\to0\). Consequently
\[
0\leq1-\frac{\Omega_j}{Q_j}
=\frac{\eta_j}{q'_j}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which proves \(\Omega_j/Q_j\to1\). Together with (13), this yields
\[
\liminf_j\frac{\Delta_j}{\Omega_j}
=\liminf_j
\left(\frac{\Delta_j}{Q_j}\frac{Q_j}{\Omega_j}\right)
\geq\delta_*.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (15), (16), and (14) prove (4).

By (16), the last inequality in (14), and (15), there are nonnegative scalar sequences \(\alpha_j,\beta_j,\varepsilon_j\to0\) such that, for every sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\frac{\Delta_j}{\Omega_j}\geq\delta_*-\alpha_j,
\qquad
\frac{\Omega_j}{W_j}\geq\frac1{2e}-\beta_j,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_j}{W_j}\geq\frac{\delta_*}{2e}-\varepsilon_j.
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(0<T_j\leq\Omega_j\), \(\theta_j=T_j/\Omega_j\) lies in \((0,1]\). Applying (1) and (2) to \(C_j\) gives
\[
A_{T,j}-1
\geq\frac{\Delta_j}{T_j}
=\frac{\Delta_j/\Omega_j}{\theta_j},
\]
\[
r_{T,j}+1
\geq2^{\,n_j-\rho_{T,j}}
\frac{T_j/W_j}{H_j}
=2^{\,n_j-\rho_{T,j}}
\frac{\theta_j(\Omega_j/W_j)}{H_j}.
\]
Using (17) proves (5) and (6). The exact product bound (3) and the third inequality in (17) give (7). Because \(E_{T,j}\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_j}\), one has \(\rho_{T,j}\leq n_j\), so \(2^{n_j-\rho_{T,j}}\geq1\). The numerator in (7) tends to the positive constant \(\delta_*/(2e)\), while \(H_j\to0\); hence the right side tends to infinity even when the dimensions vary.

For nonnegative \(x,y\), \(\max\{x,y\}\geq\sqrt{xy}\). Apply this with \(x=r_{T,j}+1\) and \(y=A_{T,j}-1>0\), then use (7). This proves (8), including its divergence.

Let \((j_k)\) be a subsequence. If
\[
\liminf_k\frac{T_{j_k}}{W_{j_k}}=:\kappa>0,
\]
then (2), \(2^{n_{j_k}-\rho_{T,j_k}}\geq1\), and \(H_{j_k}\to0\) give, eventually,
\[
r_{T,j_k}+1
\geq\frac{\kappa/2}{H_{j_k}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus \(r_{T,j_k}\to\infty\). If instead \(T_{j_k}/W_{j_k}\to0\), then (1) and (17) give
\[
A_{T,j_k}-1
\geq
\frac{\Delta_{j_k}/W_{j_k}}{T_{j_k}/W_{j_k}}
\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because the numerator is eventually bounded below by a positive constant. This proves the stated subsequence dichotomy with its exact quantifiers.

It remains to compare the Chebyshev radii. For every affine \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E_{T,j})\to\mathbb R^{d_j}\), the numbers \(\tau_{j,z}/T_j\) are positive and sum to one on \(E_{T,j}\). Therefore
\[
\max_{z\in E_{T,j}}\|c_{j,z}-s(z)\|_{D_{j,z}}
\geq
\frac1{T_j}\sum_{z\in E_{T,j}}
\tau_{j,z}\|c_{j,z}-s(z)\|_{D_{j,z}}.
\]
Taking the infimum over the same affine-map domain proves \(\mathscr R_{D,j}\geq A_{T,j}\).

Accepted fact `107b9182522633f9` gives the set inclusion
\[
\mathcal E_{j,z}\subseteq D_{j,z}
\qquad(z\in Z_j).
\tag{18}
\]
For origin-symmetric convex bodies, inclusion of unit balls reverses the corresponding gauges, so (18) implies
\[
\|u\|_{\mathcal E_{j,z}}\geq\|u\|_{D_{j,z}}
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R^{d_j}).
\]
Taking first the maximum over \(z\in E_{T,j}\) and then the infimum over the identical affine-map domain gives
\[
\mathscr R_{\mathcal E,j}\geq\mathscr R_{D,j}\geq A_{T,j},
\]
which is (9). Along the second subsequence branch, \(A_{T,j_k}\to\infty\), so both Chebyshev radii, in particular the covariance-polar radius, diverge. This completes the proof.
