## Statement

Use the original-law notation of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705: for a positive law \(p\) on a finite lattice support \(A\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(v)&=\inf\lbrace p(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }v\rbrace,\\
H_p(v)&=\sum_{x\in A:\,2v-x\in A}\sqrt{p_xp_{2v-x}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{v\in A}p_vH_p(v)^2,\qquad
M_s(p)=\sum_{v\in A}p_v\tau_p(v)^s\quad(s>0).
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular the reflected sum is oriented and retains its central atom once. All halfspaces below are ambient halfspaces.

Construct a finite rooted tree of atoms starting at \(0\). At each pair operation choose an existing tree atom \(v\), use a coordinate that is zero on every existing atom, and create two new atoms \(v+e\) and \(v-e\). Each pair uses its own coordinate; each new atom has the chosen \(v\) as its unique parent. A node may have arbitrarily many child pairs. After the pair operations, optionally add finitely many terminal singleton apices \(a_f+e_f\), where each \(a_f\) is a tree atom and each \(e_f\) is a further coordinate used only for that apex. Let \(m\) and \(f\) be the numbers of pairs and apices. Work in \(\mathbb R^n\) with \(n\ge\max\lbrace1,m+f\rbrace\), using standard lattice coordinates and setting every unused coordinate to zero. Let \(A\) be the resulting support. This pair attachment is the fresh-coordinate operation used in accepted fact db278433c752d02f; no singleton-row condition is imposed on its centers here.

Give the atoms positive raw weights \(w_x\), with \(w_0>0\), satisfying
$$
w_{v+e}w_{v-e}=w_v^2
\tag{1}
$$
for each child pair. Apex weights are arbitrary positive numbers. Always use the original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\) and \(p_x=w_x/Z\). For a tree node \(v\), let \(T_v\) be the set consisting of \(v\) and its tree descendants, excluding all singleton apices. Define recursively
$$
F(v)=w_v+\sum_{\text{child pairs }(a,b)\text{ at }v}\min\lbrace F(a),F(b)\rbrace,
\qquad
C(v)=1+2\sum_{\text{child pairs }(a,b)\text{ at }v}\sqrt{C(a)C(b)}.
\tag{2}
$$
Thus a tree leaf has \(F(v)=w_v\) and \(C(v)=1\). For a singleton apex set \(F(v)=w_v\), \(C(v)=1\), and \(T_v=\lbrace v\rbrace\).

The following conclusions hold.

First, \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), it has exactly
$$
K_{\rm occ}=m+f+1
\tag{3}
$$
occupied parity classes, and every nontrivial supported reflected pair is one of the prescribed child pairs. There is one affine exponential weight law on all of \(A\): some \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfies \(w_x=w_0\exp(\ell\cdot x)\).

Second, the exact actual depth at every atom is
$$
\tau_p(v)=\frac{F(v)}{Z}.
\tag{4}
$$
The recursion (4) in fact holds for arbitrary positive weights, without (1).

Third, let \(\mathcal Q_v\) be the probability laws \(q\) on the entire \(A\) with \(\mathbb E_q X=v\), and let \(\mathcal H(q)=-\sum_xq_x\log q_x\), with \(0\log0=0\). Every law in \(\mathcal Q_v\) is supported on \(T_v\). There is a unique entropy maximizer \(Q_v\), positive at every point of \(T_v\), and
$$
\max_{q\in\mathcal Q_v}\mathcal H(q)=\log C(v),
\qquad
C(v)=\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb R^n}\sum_{x\in A}\exp\bigl(\theta\cdot(x-v)\bigr).
\tag{5}
$$
The infimum on the right is attained if and only if \(T_v=A\); boundary infima are included. In terms of the original law,
$$
I_p(v):=\min_{q\in\mathcal Q_v}D(q\Vert p)
=\log Z-\log w_v-\log C(v),
\qquad
s_p(v):=e^{-I_p(v)}=\frac{w_vC(v)}Z\le1.
\tag{6}
$$
Write
$$
\mathcal C_2(p):=\sum_{v\in A}p_v s_p(v)^2
=\frac1{Z^3}\sum_{v\in A}w_v^3C(v)^2.
\tag{7}
$$
If \(d(v)\) is the number of child pairs at \(v\), then
$$
C(v)\ge 1+2d(v),\qquad
H_p(v)=(1+2d(v))p_v,\qquad
\mathcal C_2(p)\ge b(p).
\tag{8}
$$
For apices, \(d(v)=0\).

For independent products of these laws, with \(P=\bigotimes_i p_i\) and target \(J=(v_i)_i\), the maximum mean-\(J\) entropy is \(\sum_i\log C_i(v_i)\), its unique maximizing law is \(\bigotimes_i Q_{i,v_i}\), and
$$
s_P(J)=\prod_i s_{p_i}(v_i),\qquad
\mathcal C_2(P)=\prod_i\mathcal C_2(p_i).
\tag{9}
$$
Here \(s_P\) is defined by the same relative-entropy minimum as in (6). Formula (9) makes no assertion that \(\tau_P(J)\) or \(M_s(P)\) factorizes. There is no absolute constant \(c>0\) such that \(M_2(p)\ge c\mathcal C_2(p)\) for all individual trees in this statement.

## Proof

For the geometry, consider one operation applied to an old convex hull \(Q\), embedded at new-coordinate height zero. For a pair attachment, the new hull is \(\operatorname{conv}(Q\times\lbrace0\rbrace,(v,1),(v,-1))\). Its integer heights can only be \(-1,0,1\). Each extreme slice is the stated single apex. Its zero slice equals \(Q\): equal coefficients on the two apices average to the old point \(v\in Q\), and conversely the old \(Q\) is present. Thus the lattice census adds precisely the two prescribed points. For a singleton attachment, the integer heights are zero and one, with old zero slice and singleton top slice. Induction proves completeness, including unused ambient coordinates. This repeats the census mechanism of db278433c752d02f and shows why no singleton-row assumption is needed for completeness.

At each operation the new atom or atoms have odd new coordinate, whereas all old atoms have zero there. The two atoms of a pair have the same old coordinates and the same parity in the new coordinate. Every later operation uses a further new coordinate, so its atoms cannot join any existing parity class. Hence the classes consist of the root singleton, one two-point class for each pair, and one singleton class for each apex. This proves (3). Distinct reflected endpoints have an integer midpoint only when they share a parity class. Therefore the prescribed pairs enumerate all nontrivial rows, and a row at a tree node contains exactly that node and the endpoints of its child pairs. Apex rows are singletons.

We use the following affine freedom. Prescribe a real value \(h(x)\) at every atom. These values are restrictions of an affine functional if and only if
$$
h(a)+h(b)=2h(v)
\tag{17}
$$
for every child pair \((a,b)\) at \(v\). Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, start with the prescribed value at zero. At the creation of \(v\pm e\), the previously assigned affine functional already takes value \(h(v)\) at the center. Set its coefficient in the new coordinate to \(h(v+e)-h(v)\); (17) gives the desired value at the negative child as well. At a singleton apex set the new coefficient to \(h(a_f+e_f)-h(a_f)\). Later coefficients do not change old values. This constructs the functional on all coordinates. Apply this argument to \(h(x)=\log w_x\), using (1), to obtain the asserted global affine weight law.

To prove the depth lower bound, fix a containing halfspace represented as \(\lbrace h\ge0\rbrace\), with \(h(v)\ge0\). If a tree node has nonnegative value, at least one child in each of its child pairs has nonnegative value by (17). Inducting up from the leaves, the weight in that halfspace within its descendant subtree is at least \(F(v)\). The subtrees from different child pairs are disjoint, so the sums in (2) are legitimate. At a singleton apex its own positive weight is unavoidable. The full halfspace weight is therefore at least \(F(v)\).

Here is one simultaneous realizing functional for the reverse bound at a tree node \(v\). Give \(v\) value zero. At every node selected in its descendant recursion, choose in each child pair a child whose \(F\) is smaller, breaking ties arbitrarily. If the selected parent has value \(t\ge0\), give that chosen child value \(2t+1\) and its other child value \(-1\). Give all descendants of an unchosen negative child that child's same negative value. This satisfies (17) throughout \(T_v\), and the nonnegative atoms there are precisely the recursive choices whose total weight is \(F(v)\).

If \(v\ne0\), give every strict ancestor of \(v\) value \(-1\). For the pair whose child is \(v\), give its other child value \(-2\). At every earlier pair along the root-to-\(v\) path, the path child and its sibling both have value \(-1\). All other child pairs at these ancestors have both values \(-1\). Extend each resulting off-path subtree constantly at its negative root value. Give every singleton apex value \(-1\). These assignments satisfy (17) and exclude every atom outside \(T_v\). If \(v=0\), only the apex assignments outside \(T_v\) are needed. By affine freedom a single affine functional realizes all these values. Except in the singleton-support case it is nonconstant, so \(\lbrace h\ge0\rbrace\) is a genuine closed halfspace; for singleton support a nonzero normal in an ambient coordinate gives the same supported set. At an apex, assign that apex value zero and every other atom value \(-1\); (17) again holds. This proves (4), including every boundary atom and all ties, without using (1).

We next identify the admissible barycentric support. A tree node has zero in every apex coordinate. Since only the corresponding apex has value one in such a coordinate, a mean-node law must give every apex mass zero. Along the path to a tree node \(v\), its coordinate in each traversed pair direction is either one or minus one. Every tree atom has coordinate between minus one and one in that direction. Having mean equal to the corresponding extreme value forces all the law's mass into the chosen child subtree. Repeating down the path forces its support into exactly \(T_v\). At an apex, its own unique coordinate has mean one, forcing the entire law to be its point mass.

We prove the entropy formula constructively. For a tree leaf take \(Q_v=\delta_v\) and an affine function \(h_v\) zero on its singleton subtree. Inductively, suppose \(Q_a,Q_b\) on child subtrees are mean-child laws of the form
$$
Q_a(x)=\frac{e^{h_a(x)}}{C(a)},\qquad h_a(a)=0,
\qquad
Q_b(x)=\frac{e^{h_b(x)}}{C(b)},\qquad h_b(b)=0,
\tag{18}
$$
where the displayed functions obey all their subtree midpoint equations. Set \(h_v(v)=0\). For each child pair let
$$
t_{a,b}=\tfrac12\log\frac{C(b)}{C(a)},
\qquad
h_v(x)=t_{a,b}+h_a(x)\ (x\in T_a),
\qquad
h_v(x)=-t_{a,b}+h_b(x)\ (x\in T_b).
\tag{19}
$$
All midpoint equations hold. The sum of \(e^{h_v}\) on \(T_v\) is exactly
$$
1+\sum_{(a,b)}\left(e^{t_{a,b}}C(a)+e^{-t_{a,b}}C(b)\right)
=1+2\sum_{(a,b)}\sqrt{C(a)C(b)}=C(v).
\tag{20}
$$
Define \(Q_v(x)=e^{h_v(x)}/C(v)\) on \(T_v\). It assigns mass \(1/C(v)\) to \(v\), and mass \(\sqrt{C(a)C(b)}/C(v)\) to each of \(T_a\) and \(T_b\), conditionally distributed as \(Q_a,Q_b\). Equal masses at the two child means average to \(v\), so \(\mathbb E_{Q_v}X=v\). Every atom in \(T_v\) has positive mass. The values of \(h_v\) extend to an ambient affine function with value zero at \(v\): assign value zero to all atoms outside \(T_v\), and then apply (17). Indeed all ancestor-path values, including \(v\), are zero, so all the newly imposed midpoint equations hold.

For any probability law \(q\) of mean \(v\), already forced onto \(T_v\), the elementary nonnegativity of relative entropy gives
$$
0\le D(q\Vert Q_v)
=-\mathcal H(q)-\mathbb E_q h_v(X)+\log C(v)
=\log C(v)-\mathcal H(q).
\tag{21}
$$
The last equality uses affinity and \(h_v(v)=0\). Equality holds precisely when \(q=Q_v\). Thus \(\mathcal H(Q_v)=\log C(v)\) and the maximizer is unique. Nonnegativity and its equality case follow, for example, from \(-\log u\ge1-u\), summed against \(q\), including zero coordinates by continuity. The apex case is immediate.

For the variational identity, fix \(\theta\) and normalize \(e^{\theta\cdot(x-v)}\) on \(T_v\). Applying nonnegative relative entropy with first law \(Q_v\), and using its mean \(v\), yields
$$
\sum_{x\in T_v}e^{\theta\cdot(x-v)}\ge C(v).
\tag{22}
$$
To obtain the full-support infimum, construct an affine \(g\) zero on \(T_v\) and strictly negative on its complement. For a tree node use the ancestor and off-path assignments from the depth proof, with every descendant value now zero; all equations (17) still hold. For an apex use \(g(x)=x_f-1\). The previously constructed affine extension \(h_v\) has value zero at \(v\), as does \(g\). Therefore \(h_v+tg\) has the form \(\theta_t\cdot(x-v)\). Its exponential sum on \(T_v\) is exactly \(C(v)\), and its sum outside tends to zero as \(t\to+\infty\). This proves (5). If the complement is nonempty, every finite \(\theta\) contributes strictly positive mass there in addition to the lower bound (22), so the infimum is not attained. If the complement is empty, \(h_v\) attains it.

For a mean-\(v\) law, global affinity of the raw weights gives \(\mathbb E_q\log w_X=\log w_v\). Consequently
$$
D(q\Vert p)=\log Z-\log w_v-\mathcal H(q).
\tag{23}
$$
Equations (21) and (23) prove (6), with the same unique optimizer. Nonnegativity gives \(s_p(v)\le1\). The row census and (1) give \(H_p(v)=(1+2d(v))p_v\). Since every \(C\ge1\), (2) gives \(C(v)\ge1+2d(v)\), proving (7)-(8).

For completeness, a joint law of mean \(J=(v_i)_i\) on a product support has mean-\(v_i\) marginals. Its entropy is at most the sum of their entropies, with equality exactly for the product of its marginals; this follows from the nonnegativity of its relative entropy to that product. Each marginal entropy is at most \(\log C_i(v_i)\) by (21). The product of the \(Q_{i,v_i}\) attains both equalities, uniquely. Applying (23) to the product affine law proves the first identity in (9), and direct finite-sum factorization proves the second.

There is a concrete obstruction to converting this entropy energy uniformly into actual moments. Take the complete binary midpoint tree of height \(h\), with exactly one child pair at every nonterminal node, and assign every raw weight one. At depth \(d\) it has \(2^d\) atoms, and its total number of atoms is \(a_h=2^{h+1}-1\). Recursions (2) give
$$
F(v)=h-d+1,\qquad C(v)=2^{h-d+1}-1.
\tag{24}
$$
At the root, \(p=Q_0\), \(s_p(0)=1\), and hence \(\mathcal C_2(p)\ge1/a_h\). But
$$
M_2(p)=\frac{2^h}{a_h^3}\sum_{k=0}^h\frac{(k+1)^2}{2^k}
\le\frac{12\,2^h}{a_h^3}.
\tag{25}
$$
The infinite series equals 12, by differentiating the geometric series twice. Thus
$$
\frac{M_2(p)}{\mathcal C_2(p)}\le\frac{12\,2^h}{a_h^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
$$
Every member is a finite complete globally affine tree of the stated kind. This proves the claimed failure of an absolute energy conversion, and even at the root the ratio \(\tau_p(0)/s_p(0)=(h+1)/a_h\) tends to zero.

It remains to verify the level-tree calibration rather than infer it from an actual-depth property of the old embedding. The parameters (10), and the inequalities used next, are the parameters of accepted fact 03229943d27787ae. In particular, for \(0\le\ell\le L\),
$$
w_\ell^2\le w_{\ell+1}\le\tfrac43w_\ell^2<w_\ell\le c,
\tag{27}
$$
and, with \(M_\ell=N_\ell w_\ell\) for \(0\le\ell\le L\),
$$
M_{\ell+1}\le\tfrac23M_\ell\ (\ell<L),\qquad
\sum_{\ell=0}^L M_\ell\le3c=\tfrac13,
\qquad d_\ell\ge4\ (\ell<L).
\tag{28}
$$
The identity \(w_{\ell+1}(1-w_{\ell+1})=w_\ell^2\) proves (1) on every pair. Each pair has raw total one, each filler has weight one, and the root has weight \(c\), proving (11). The general recursions immediately give (12), including \(C_L=h_L\) and \(C_\ell>h_\ell\) above the last generation.

Put \(M_{L+1}=N_Ld_Lw_{L+1}\) and \(\rho=N_Ld_Lw_L^2\). The ceiling defining \(d_L\) implies
$$
1\le\rho<1+N_Lw_L^2\le1+c^2.

$$
1<M_{L+1}=\frac{\rho}{1-w_{L+1}}
<\frac{1+c^2}{1-c}=\frac{41}{36}.
\tag{29}
$$
Since \(N_Lw_L^2\le c^2=1/81\), the ceiling also gives \(d_L\ge81\). Therefore (28) gives \(N_\ell\ge4^\ell\) for every \(0\le\ell\le L+1\). The sum \(S_\ell:=\sum_{r=\ell}^{L+1}M_r\) is smaller than \(1/3+41/36=53/36<3/2\). For every \(1\le\ell\le L+1\), consequently,
$$
\frac{S_\ell}{N_\ell}<\frac38<1-c<1-w_\ell.
\tag{30}
$$
Start at \(\ell=L+1\) and induct backwards in (12). Equation (30) says that the proposed value at each next tiny child is smaller than the competing large-leaf weight. The recursion therefore always selects that tiny child, and gives exactly (13). Equations (29) and (13) give \(F_0=S_0>1+c\). Also \(d_0=4\), so \(h_0w_0=9c=1\), proving (14).

To compute the original actual moments, let
$$
S_2^{\rm pair}=\sum_{\ell=0}^LN_\ell d_\ell w_\ell^2,
\qquad
S_4^{\rm pair}=\sum_{\ell=0}^LN_\ell d_\ell w_\ell^4.
$$
Then \(S_2^{\rm pair}\ge\rho\ge1\), and \(S_4^{\rm pair}\le c^2S_2^{\rm pair}\). Each pair has endpoint cube sum \(1-3w_\ell^2\) and endpoint fourth-power sum \(1-4w_\ell^2+2w_\ell^4\). Since the pairs' newly created endpoints partition all nonroot nonfiller atoms, the raw atomic sums are exactly
$$
J_3:=\sum_xw_x^3=K-1+c^3-3S_2^{\rm pair}\le K-4+c^3,
\tag{31}
$$
$$
J_4:=\sum_xw_x^4=K-1+c^4-4S_2^{\rm pair}+2S_4^{\rm pair}
\le K-5+2c^2+c^4.
\tag{32}
$$
All large atoms, final tiny atoms, and filler apices have singleton descendant trees. At the remaining tiny centers, (13), \(S_\ell<3/2\), \(w_\ell\le c\), and \(N_\ell\ge4^\ell\) imply

\sum_{\ell=0}^LN_\ell w_\ell F_\ell^2
=\sum_{\ell=0}^L\frac{w_\ell S_\ell^2}{N_\ell}
\le\frac94c\sum_{\ell\ge0}4^{-\ell}=\frac13,
\tag{33}
$$
$$
\sum_{\ell=0}^LN_\ell w_\ell F_\ell^3
=\sum_{\ell=0}^L\frac{w_\ell S_\ell^3}{N_\ell^2}
\le\frac{27}8c\sum_{\ell\ge0}16^{-\ell}=\frac25.

Using the exact depth (4), and adding only the center increments to the atomic sums, gives

Z^3M_2(p)

\tag{35}

Z^4M_3(p)
=J_4+\sum_{\ell=0}^LN_\ell w_\ell(F_\ell^3-w_\ell^3)

\tag{36}
$$
Write \(a=1-c=8/9\), so \(Z=K-a<K\). Bernoulli's inequality, or direct expansion, yields

\frac{Z^3}{K^2}\ge K-3a=K-\frac83,
\qquad
\frac{Z^4}{K^3}\ge K-4a=K-\frac{32}9.
\tag{37}
$$
Subtract (35) and (36), respectively, from (37). Multiplication by \(K^2/Z^3>1/K\) or \(K^3/Z^4>1/K\) proves (15)-(16). The stated \(\gamma>1\) follows directly from its rational expression.
