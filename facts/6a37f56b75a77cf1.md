## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex with actual integer fibers $K_z$, $n\ge1,d\ge2$. Let $h_0,\ldots,h_d$ be affine cap margins with nonzero continuous normals of rank $d$ and $\sum_\ell h_\ell=0$. For two distinct labels $i,j$, select convex measurable pieces $A_z,B_z$ inside $K_z$ on which respectively all margins other than $i$, or other than $j$, are nonpositive. Fix $H>0$, $0<\eta<1$, and $1\le q\le1/(1-\eta)$ and impose
$$
H\le h_i\le qH,\quad -h_j\le(1-\eta)h_i\ \text{on }A_z,\qquad
H\le h_j\le qH,\quad -h_i\le(1-\eta)h_j\ \text{on }B_z.
$$
For one parity $r$ with positive source masses $A_r=\sum_{z\equiv r}|A_z|$ and $B_r=\sum_{z\equiv r}|B_z|$, the summed $d$-volume in the actual fibers where $h_i,h_j\ge0$ and $h_\ell\le0$ for $\ell\notin\lbrace i,j\rbrace $ is at least $\Phi_d(A_r,B_r)=2^{-d}(A_r^{1/d}+B_r^{1/d})^d$. For multiple unordered pairs these bounds add, using one best parity bound per pair.

If all margins vanish at a common section point in each relevant fiber, the source windows may instead be $[a_z,qa_z]$ and $[b_z,qb_z]$ with positive lower heights and the same angular slack. For $H$ at most all selected lower heights, the same overlap conclusion holds with masses $H^d\sum_{z\equiv r}|A_z|/a_z^d$ and $H^d\sum_{z\equiv r}|B_z|/b_z^d$, with at most one piece per label and base.

## Proof

If $(z,x)\in A_z$ and $(w,y)\in B_w$, then at their midpoint both selected margins are at least $[1-(1-\eta)q]H/2\ge0$, and every other margin is nonpositive, by affinity. If $z,w$ have the same parity, write them as $r+2u,r+2v$ for integer $u,v$. The target base is $r+u+v$, and the actual fiber there contains $(A_z+B_w)/2$ by convexity of $C$. The midpoint sets of the convex positive-volume pieces are measurable and satisfy Brunn–Minkowski. Applying bff19d5abf55e3e2 to the two positive source arrays indexed by $u,v$, with target set at index $u+v$ equal to the designated pair region in its actual target fiber, proves the estimate on the sum of target volumes. A point in two distinct pair regions lies on some margin-zero hyperplane, which is null on each fiber. This justifies summing one bound per unordered pair.

In the alternate windows, contract each $A_z$ about the common zero-margin section point at that base by the factor $H/a_z\le1$, and each $B_z$ by $H/b_z$. Convexity of each fiber retains the pieces in their original fibers, and every margin is multiplied by the corresponding factor because it vanishes at the center. The contracted pieces satisfy the common-window bounds and their volumes are multiplied by the exact $d$th powers of the factors. Applying the first part gives the claimed masses.
