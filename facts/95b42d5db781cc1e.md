## Statement

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite loopless oriented multigraph after discarding isolated vertices, with \(N:=|E|\ge1\). Write \(d_v:=\deg_G(v)>0\), and let \(\widehat A\) be the full incidence matrix, with \(-1\) at an edge's tail and \(+1\) at its head. Obtain \(A\) by omitting one vertex row from each connected component. Put \(r:=|V|-\operatorname{cc}(G)\), assume \(r<N\), and set \(n:=N-r\). Here \(V\) is the graph vertex set, as in accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26, not a total-volume variable. Any total-volume notation \(V\) used elsewhere is unused here; the total original capacity is denoted \(T\), as in accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2. The graph degrees \(d_v\) are unrelated to the continuous dimension \(d\) of the locked problem.

For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^E\), use exactly the section, partition, probability, actual closed-halfspace depth, and cubic moment of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2:
\[
\begin{aligned}
D&:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^E:A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},&
Q&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},&
p_{\mathbf z}&:=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}/Q,\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&:=\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^E\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},&
M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every halfspace has a nonzero normal. The tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is the exact alias of \(\boldsymbol\ell\) in accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26. The integer kernel has the saturated, full-dimensional, complete lattice chart of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, and \(\tau_p\) equals actual depth in that chart, including boundary atoms.

Define the degree product and factorial factor
\[
R_G:=\prod_{v\in V}(2d_v+1),\qquad
F_G:=\frac{(2N)!}{(2N)^{2N}}
       \prod_{v\in V}\frac{d_v^{d_v}}{d_v!}.
\]
Then, for every tilt,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{(25/162)^N F_G}{64R_G^6}.
\tag{1}
\]
One may replace \(F_G\) in (1) by the componentwise factor
\[
F_G^{\mathrm{comp}}:=
\prod_{C\in\operatorname{Comp}(G)}
\left[\frac{(2|E(C)|)!}{(2|E(C)|)^{2|E(C)|}}
\prod_{v\in V(C)}\frac{d_v^{d_v}}{d_v!}\right].
\tag{2}
\]
All components have at least one edge. No restriction on the number of column types of \(A\) is imposed.

For arbitrary positive original capacities \(c_{\mathbf z}=\alpha e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\), where \(\alpha>0\), set \(T:=\sum_Dc_{\mathbf z}\) and \(h_c(\mathbf z):=\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{D\cap H}c_{\mathbf x}\). Then \(p=c/T\), \(h_c=T\tau_p\), and
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
=T^4M_3(p)\ge T^4\frac{(25/162)^NF_G}{64R_G^6},
\tag{3}
\]
again with the optional replacement (2).

The absolute dense-graph consequence is the following. If \(m:=\min_{v\in V}d_v\ge1024\) and \(\max_vd_v\le2m\), then for every tilt
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}\,8^{-n},\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
\ge\frac{T^4}{64}\,8^{-n}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently some \(\mathbf z^\star\in D\) satisfies \(\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)\ge\tfrac14\,2^{-n}\), and \(h_c(\mathbf z^\star)\ge\tfrac T4\,2^{-n}\). In particular (4), with the single absolute coefficient \(\kappa=1/64\), holds for every nonempty \(\Delta\)-regular loopless multigraph with \(\Delta\ge1024\), for arbitrary vertex count and arbitrary orientations.

## Proof

We use only the structural, auxiliary-law, and gauge constructions of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, and the graphical reflected counts of accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26. We do not use the former fact's final fixed-catalog moment estimate or its fixed-catalog auxiliary probability bound. The permanent normalization below is proved independently.

First check the incidence and chart hypotheses. Rows retained in \(A\) are independent: extend a zero linear combination's coefficients by zero at omitted vertices. An edge forces its two endpoint coefficients to agree, so each component has constant coefficient, and its omitted vertex makes that constant zero. Conversely, the full rows sum to zero on each component. Thus \(A\) has row rank \(r\), and for every real edge vector \(\mathbf u\), \(A\mathbf u=0\) if and only if \(\widehat A\mathbf u=0\). The same equivalence holds for every restricted edge set \(I\subseteq E\), even if deleting edges splits components: the omitted full row in each original component is minus the sum of its other full rows, before and after column restriction.

The incidence matrix is totally unimodular. In a square submatrix, a column with at most one nonzero entry allows expansion, reducing its determinant to zero or a signed smaller determinant. If every column has two nonzero entries, they are \(-1,+1\), so the sum of the rows is zero and its determinant vanishes. Induction proves that all square minors are \(0,\pm1\). Row deletion preserves this property. Thus accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2 applies, if desired with the harmless catalog upper bound \(t=N\); none of its catalog-dependent estimates will be invoked.

For precision, its chart construction uses a set of \(r\) independent columns with matrix \(A_B\), whose determinant is \(\pm1\), and the complementary edge-index set \(K\). For each \(j\in K\), set the \(j\)-coordinate of \(\mathbf h_j\) to 1, its other \(K\)-coordinates to zero, and its basic coordinates to \(-A_B^{-1}\mathbf a_j\). Replacement-column minors show that \(\mathbf h_j\) is ternary. The map \(\Phi:\mathbb R^n\to\ker A\) with columns \(\mathbf h_j\) has inverse the projection to \(K\), and restricts to an isomorphism \(\mathbb Z^n\to\ker A\cap\mathbb Z^E\). These are exactly \(\Phi,\mathbf h_j,K\) of that accepted fact. Its TU box-integrality clause yields \(\ker A\cap[-1,1]^E=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). The chart image \(\widehat D:=\Phi^{-1}(D)\) contains \(\mathbf0\) and all \(\pm\mathbf e_j\), so is full-dimensional; the integer-lattice isomorphism gives \(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\).

An ambient containing halfspace pulls back under \(\Phi\) to either a genuine containing chart halfspace or the whole chart. The whole-chart case has mass 1 and cannot lower the minimum. Every chart halfspace lifts because \(\Phi^T\) is onto \(\mathbb R^n\), and a lift of a nonzero chart normal is nonzero. These pullbacks preserve the weak inequalities defining closed halfspaces, hence preserve every boundary atom. This proves the stated equality of actual depths, using the chart clauses of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2. All depth minima exist: on the finite set \(D\), only finitely many cap subsets occur.

We next state and verify the auxiliary-law clauses used from accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, in its exact normalization. For \(\eta\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
Z(\eta):=1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta},\qquad
v_+(\eta):=\frac{e^{4\eta}}{Z(\eta)^4},\quad
v_-(\eta):=\frac{e^{-4\eta}}{Z(\eta)^4},\quad
v_0(\eta):=\frac{729}{Z(\eta)^7}.
\tag{5}
\]
Put \(w_0=v_0\). For \(\eta\ge0\), put \(w_+=v_+\) and \(w_-:=\min\{v_-,v_0^2/(4v_+)\}\); for \(\eta\le0\), put \(w_-=v_-\) and \(w_+:=\min\{v_+,v_0^2/(4v_-)\}\). Use \(v_1=v_+,v_{-1}=v_-\) and likewise for \(w\). Their sum \(F(\eta):=w_-+w_0+w_+\) satisfies
\[
0<w_\sigma\le v_\sigma,\qquad
w_0^2\ge4w_-w_+,\qquad F(\eta)\ge25/162.
\tag{6}
\]
Here is the scalar lower-bound proof from that accepted construction. Put \(\rho=e^\eta+e^{-\eta}\ge2\) and \(s=\rho-2\ge0\). The numerator of \(6Z^7(v_-+v_0+v_+-1/6)\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&6(\rho^4-4\rho^2+2)(\rho+1)^3+4374-(\rho+1)^7\\
&\quad=5s^7+81s^6+525s^5+1689s^4+2577s^3
 +108s^2+729(s^2-3s+3)+324>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s^2-3s+3=(s-3/2)^2+3/4\). Only the smaller endpoint is thinned, and its original value is at most \(1/81\): for \(\eta\ge0\), \(e^{-\eta}/Z(\eta)\le1/3\), and the other sign follows by symmetry. Thus the sum after thinning is at least \(1/6-1/81=25/162\). Positivity and the discriminant inequality in (6) follow directly from the definitions. At zero the prescriptions agree, with \(v_0=1/3\), \(v_-=v_+=1/81\), and strict unthinned discriminant inequality. Hence \(w\) and \(F\) are continuous, and \(w_+(-\eta)=w_-(\eta)\).

Let \(q_\eta(\sigma):=w_\sigma(\eta)/F(\eta)\), and let \(Y(\eta)\) have this ternary law. The polynomial \(w_-+w_0x+w_+x^2\) has two negative real roots, with multiplicity, by (6). Calling them \(-\rho_1,-\rho_2\), where \(\rho_i>0\), its normalized factorization is
\[
q_\eta(-1)+q_\eta(0)x+q_\eta(1)x^2
=\frac{\rho_1+x}{\rho_1+1}\frac{\rho_2+x}{\rho_2+1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(Y(\eta)=\xi_1+\xi_2-1\) in law, where the two Bernoulli variables are independent and have success probabilities \(\beta_a=1/(1+\rho_a)\in(0,1)\). This is the exact real-rooted auxiliary normalization of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, not the original law \(p\).

The auxiliary mean \(\mu(\eta):=(w_+-w_-)/F\) is continuous and has limits \(1,-1\) at \(+\infty,-\infty\). Indeed the retained positive endpoint tends to 1 at \(+\infty\), whereas the other two weights tend to zero; symmetry gives the negative limit. These properties give a gauge \(\boldsymbol\eta=\boldsymbol\theta+A^T\boldsymbol\lambda\) such that
\[
A\mathbb E\mathbf Y=\sum_{e\in E}\mathbf a_e\mu(\eta_e)=0,
\qquad \widehat A\mathbb E\mathbf Y=0,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(Y_e\) are independent with laws \(q_{\eta_e}\). To verify existence as in accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, set \(f(u):=\int_0^u\mu(v)\,dv\). The limits give \(f(u)\ge|u|/2-C\) for some finite \(C\). Full row rank gives \(\|A^T\lambda\|_1\ge c\|\lambda\|_2\) for some \(c>0\), by the positive minimum on the unit sphere. The differentiable function \(\sum_e f(\theta_e+\mathbf a_e\cdot\lambda)\) is consequently coercive and attains a minimum. Its vanishing gradient is the first equation in (8); the omitted-row equivalence proves the second. If \(r=0\), take the empty gauge. Convexity or monotonicity of \(\mu\) is not required.

We now prove the graphical probability estimate, including cloning and its normalization. On one product probability space take all \(2N\) Bernoulli variables \(\xi_{e,a}\), \(e\in E\), \(a\in\{1,2\}\), mutually independent, with the probabilities supplied by (7), and set \(Y_e:=\xi_{e,1}+\xi_{e,2}-1\). Each copy chooses the head of its edge when \(\xi_{e,a}=1\) and the tail otherwise. Write \(I_v\) for the number of copies choosing vertex \(v\), that is, its random indegree in the doubled-edge orientation. For an edge, its two copies contribute \(1+Y_e\) to its head and \(1-Y_e\) to its tail. Therefore
\[
\mathbf I=(d_v)_{v\in V}+\widehat A\mathbf Y,\qquad
\mathbb E I_v=d_v,\qquad \sum_vd_v=2N.
\tag{9}
\]
The symbol \(I_v\) denotes a random indegree, whereas an un-subscripted \(I\subseteq E\) below denotes the zero-edge set of a root; they are different objects. Equations (9) and the omitted-row equivalence give \(\mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)=\mathbb P(I_v=d_v\text{ for every }v)\).

Index the copies by \(b=(e,a)\), and define \(P_{b,v}\) as the probability that copy \(b\) chooses \(v\). It is \(\beta_{e,a}\) at the head, \(1-\beta_{e,a}\) at the tail, and zero otherwise. Looplessness makes those endpoints distinct. Each row of this \(2N\)-by-\(|V|\) array sums to 1, and (9) gives \(\sum_bP_{b,v}=d_v\). Independence implies the exact coefficient identity
\[
\mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)
=\left[\prod_{v\in V}x_v^{d_v}\right]
 \prod_b\left(\sum_{v\in V}P_{b,v}x_v\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The bracket means the coefficient of the indicated monomial, not evaluation at that monomial.

Make \(d_v\) separate clones \((v,j)\), \(1\le j\le d_v\), of each vertex. The square \(2N\)-by-\(2N\) clone matrix is
\[
\mathcal C_{b,(v,j)}:=P_{b,v}/d_v.
\tag{11}
\]
Every row sums to \(\sum_v d_vP_{b,v}/d_v=1\); every clone column sums to \(\sum_bP_{b,v}/d_v=1\). All entries are nonnegative. Thus \(\mathcal C\) is doubly stochastic, with no rounding and no balancing beyond the gauge (8).

For completeness, a permutation contributing to \(\operatorname{per}(\mathcal C)\) assigns each copy to a distinct clone. Forgetting clone labels gives an endpoint assignment with exactly \(d_v\) copies at each vertex. Conversely, each such endpoint assignment has exactly \(\prod_vd_v!\) clone bijections: at vertex \(v\), biject its \(d_v\) selected copies with its \(d_v\) labeled clones. Each clone bijection's weight is its endpoint-assignment probability times \(\prod_vd_v^{-d_v}\). Summing proves the exact normalization
\[
\operatorname{per}(\mathcal C)
=\left(\prod_v\frac{d_v!}{d_v^{d_v}}\right)
 \mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0).
\tag{12}
\]
This proves the coefficient/permanent factor without assuming a packet about cloned permanents.

The van der Waerden theorem, in the primary source specified under EXTERNAL SOURCES, says that every \(L\)-by-\(L\) doubly stochastic matrix has permanent at least \(L!/L^L\). It applies to (11) with \(L=2N\), so (12) gives
\[
\mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)
\ge\frac{(2N)!}{(2N)^{2N}}
 \prod_v\frac{d_v^{d_v}}{d_v!}=F_G.
\tag{13}
\]
An explicit check of the source's polynomial version is also possible: \(\prod_b\sum_j\mathcal C_{b,j}x_j\) is homogeneous of degree \(2N\), has nonnegative coefficients, and has no zero when every variable has positive real part. Weighted AM-GM, first row by row and then using column sums, bounds it below by \(\prod_jx_j\) for positive real variables. Equality at all ones gives capacity 1. Its square-free coefficient is exactly \(\operatorname{per}(\mathcal C)\). Thus all hypotheses of the source's Corollary 2.5 are satisfied, including when the graph is disconnected and the clone matrix has zero entries. Alternatively apply the same argument separately to each component. The independent copy families make the component zero-flow events independent, and their probability bounds multiply, giving (13) with \(F_G^{\mathrm{comp}}\) in place of \(F_G\).

We next connect (13) to the original halfspace caps. This requires separating two predecessor objects that have the same legacy name. For a root \(\mathbf z\in D\), let \(I:=\{e:z_e=0\}\), \(J:=E\setminus I\), and \(G_I=(V,I)\). Let \(J_I\subseteq V\) omit one vertex per component of \(G_I\), including its isolated vertices, and set
\[
Q_I^{\mathrm{cnt}}:=\prod_{v\in J_I}(2\deg_{G_I}(v)+1).
\]
Here \(Q_I^{\mathrm{cnt}}\) is exactly the object named \(Q_I\) in accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26. We reserve \(Q_I\) without a superscript for the weighted section partition of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2. The symbol \(J\) is the nonzero-edge set from that latter fact, not the vertex-row set called \(J\) in the former fact.

Use exactly the reflected set, count, and original probability
\[
R_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\},\qquad
k_{\mathbf z}:=|R_{\mathbf z}|,\qquad B_{\mathbf z}:=p(R_{\mathbf z}).
\]
The count \(k_{\mathbf z}\) is the \(k_{\mathbf z}\) of accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26 and the \(s_{\mathbf z}\) of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2; it is not the number of zero coordinates. Accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26 gives
\[
R_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf x_J=\mathbf z_J,
 \ \mathbf x_I\in\{-1,0,1\}^I,\ A_I\mathbf x_I=0\},\qquad
k_{\mathbf z}\ge\frac{3^{|I|}}{Q_I^{\mathrm{cnt}}}
\ge\frac{3^{|I|}}{R_G}.
\tag{14}
\]
The last step follows because \(\deg_{G_I}(v)\le d_v\) and \(J_I\subseteq V\), with every omitted factor at least 1, so \(Q_I^{\mathrm{cnt}}\le R_G\). This includes \(I=\varnothing\), where its product and its count are 1.

The all-cap assertion in accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26 applies to the same \(D,p\) and every containing closed halfspace. To make the boundary and weight issue explicit, reflection pairs all points of \(R_{\mathbf z}\) except its fixed point \(\mathbf z\). A containing closed halfspace contains at least one member of every pair, and also \(\mathbf z\); a boundary pair may contribute both. Hence \(|R_{\mathbf z}\cap H|\ge(k_{\mathbf z}+1)/2\). The original exponential law gives \(p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}^2\). Cauchy-Schwarz over \(R_{\mathbf z}\cap H\) therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|R_{\mathbf z}\cap H|p_{\mathbf z}
&\le\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\cap H}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}\right)^{1/2}B_{\mathbf z}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge k_{\mathbf z}^2p_{\mathbf z}^2/(4B_{\mathbf z})\). This is valid for actual minimizing caps, arbitrary equality atoms, and normals orthogonal to some reflected differences; no generic-position argument is used.

The gauge changes no original weight on \(D\), since \(A\mathbf x=0\) gives \(\boldsymbol\eta\cdot\mathbf x=\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x\). Thus \(Q=\sum_De^{\boldsymbol\eta\cdot\mathbf x}\) is still the exact original section partition. Put \(Z_e:=Z(\eta_e)\), and use the weighted restricted section partition
\[
Q_I:=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf y\in\{-1,0,1\}^I\\A_I\mathbf y=0}}
 e^{\sum_{e\in I}\eta_ey_e}.
\]
This \(Q_I\) is exactly the \(Q_I\) in accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, not \(Q_I^{\mathrm{cnt}}\). Reflection fixes the coordinates in \(J\), so the exact original normalizations and the two upper bounds are
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf z}&=\frac{e^{\sum_{e\in J}\eta_ez_e}}{Q},&
B_{\mathbf z}&=\frac{e^{\sum_{e\in J}\eta_ez_e}Q_I}{Q},\\
Q&\le\prod_{e\in E}Z_e,& Q_I&\le\prod_{e\in I}Z_e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The inequalities hold because each section sum is a sum over a subset of the full ternary product, with positive summands. Empty-coordinate sums and products equal 1. In particular \(B_{\mathbf z}\) has not been replaced by a conditional reflected law or by 1.

Cubing the actual-cap lower bound and multiplying by the original \(p_{\mathbf z}\), then using (14), gives
\[
p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
\ge\frac{k_{\mathbf z}^6e^{4\sum_{e\in J}\eta_ez_e}}
 {64Q^4Q_I^3}
\ge\frac{729^{|I|}e^{4\sum_{e\in J}\eta_ez_e}}
 {64R_G^6Q^4Q_I^3}.
\tag{16}
\]
All powers of the partitions in these denominators are positive. Replacing them by their upper bounds in (15) makes the displayed lower bound smaller, which is the required direction. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
&\ge\frac1{64R_G^6}
 \prod_{e\in J}\frac{e^{4\eta_ez_e}}{Z_e^4}
 \prod_{e\in I}\frac{729}{Z_e^7}\\
&=\frac1{64R_G^6}\prod_{e\in E}v_{z_e}(\eta_e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The three additional partition powers at zero coordinates come from the exact original reflected probability \(B_{\mathbf z}\).

Summing (17) over the original section \(D\), thinning only the nonnegative auxiliary weights, and using the independent auxiliary law gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac1{64R_G^6}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\prod_ev_{z_e}(\eta_e)\\
&\ge\frac1{64R_G^6}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\prod_ew_{z_e}(\eta_e)\\
&=\frac{\prod_eF(\eta_e)}{64R_G^6}\mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)\\
&\ge\frac{(25/162)^NF_G}{64R_G^6}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (1), and the componentwise version follows from its already proved alternative probability bound. The auxiliary \(q_{\eta_e}\) are used only in this sum identity and (13); they never replace \(p\) in a cap. Since \(p=c/T\) and all containing halfspace masses scale by \(T\), \(h_c=T\tau_p\) and \(\sum_Dc_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3=T^4M_3(p)\). This proves (3).

It remains to prove the uniform dense-graph coefficient with the fixed threshold 1024. We first prove an elementary factorial envelope, without an asymptotic approximation. For an integer \(k\ge1\), let
\(s_k:=\log(k!)-(k+1/2)\log k+k\). Then \(s_1=1\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_k-s_{k+1}
&=(k+1/2)\log(1+1/k)-1\\
&=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{2t^2}{(k+1/2)^2-t^2}\,dt,
\end{aligned}
\]
so \(0\le s_k-s_{k+1}\le1/[12k(k+1)]\). The integral identity follows by writing the logarithm as an integral on \([k,k+1]\) and pairing points equidistant from \(k+1/2\). Telescoping gives \(11/12\le s_k\le1\). As \(\log2<1\), in particular \(s_k\ge\tfrac12\log2\). Therefore, for every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
\sqrt{2k}(k/e)^k\le k!\le e\sqrt{k}(k/e)^k.
\tag{18}
\]
Apply the lower bound to \((2N)!\) and the upper bounds to every \(d_v!\). Using \(\sum_vd_v=2N\) cancels all exponential-in-degree factors and gives
\[
F_G\ge\frac{\sqrt{4N}\,e^{-|V|}}{\prod_{v\in V}\sqrt{d_v}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus this envelope is valid uniformly, not just for large degrees.

Put \(b:=\log(100/81)>0\), so \(b=\log(8\cdot25/162)\). From (1) and (19),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\bigl(M_3(p)8^n\bigr)
\ge{}&Nb-r\log8-6\sum_v\log(2d_v+1)\\
&+\tfrac12\log(4N)-|V|-\tfrac12\sum_v\log d_v-\log64.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Suppose \(m=\min_vd_v\ge1024\) and \(\max_vd_v\le2m\). Then \(N\ge m|V|/2\), \(r\le|V|\), \(2d_v+1\le4m+1\), and \(d_v\le2m\). Also
\(b\ge19/100\), since \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\) for \(x\ge0\), applied to \(x=19/81\). This elementary inequality follows by integrating \(1/(1+t)\ge1/(1+x)\) on \([0,x]\).

Define, for real \(x\ge1024\),
\(\psi(x):=19x/200-\log8-1-6\log(4x+1)-\tfrac12\log(2x)\).
The preceding bounds make the right-hand side of (20) at least
\( |V|\psi(m)+\tfrac12\log(4N)-\log64\).
Moreover
\[
\psi'(x)=\frac{19}{200}-\frac{24}{4x+1}-\frac1{2x}
\ge\frac{19}{200}-\frac{13}{2x}>0
\quad(x\ge1024).
\]
At the prescribed endpoint, \(4\cdot1024+1=4097<8192=2^{13}\) and \(2\cdot1024=2048=2^{11}\). Using \(\log2<1\) gives the exact, non-optimized certificate
\[
\psi(1024)>
\frac{19\cdot1024}{200}-3-1-6\cdot13-\frac{11}{2}
=\frac{489}{50}>0.
\]
Hence \(\psi(m)>0\). Since \(N\ge1\), (20) implies \(\log(M_3(p)8^n)\ge-\log64\), proving (4). This proof keeps 1024 fixed and makes no numerical parameter search. A regular graph has \(m=\Delta\) and \(\max_vd_v=m\), so is included for every \(\Delta\ge1024\).

Finally \(M_3(p)\le\max_{\mathbf z\in D}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\), because \(p\) is a probability distribution. Taking cube roots of (4) gives a root of depth at least \((1/64)^{1/3}8^{-n/3}=\tfrac14\,2^{-n}\). Scaling gives its stated original-capacity depth. The coefficient \(1/64\) and its cube root \(1/4\) depend on neither rank, catalog size, degree, vertex count, nor tilt.

Leonid Gurvits, “Van der Waerden/Schrijver-Valiant like Conjectures and Stable (aka Hyperbolic) Homogeneous Polynomials: One Theorem for all,” arXiv:0711.3496, primary PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.3496 (the retrieved PDF identifies itself as arXiv:0711.3496v2). Page 2 defines a doubly stochastic square matrix to have nonnegative entries and every row and column sum 1, and defines its permanent as the sum over permutations of products of corresponding entries. Page 8, Corollary 2.5, gives the following exact inequality, with \(L\) here an explicit alias of the source's \(n\), not the intrinsic dimension \(n=N-r\) above: if \(\mathfrak p\) is a homogeneous degree-\(L\) polynomial in \(L\) variables with nonnegative coefficients and is H-stable, then
\[
\frac{\partial^L}{\partial x_1\cdots\partial x_L}\mathfrak p(0,\ldots,0)
\ge\frac{L!}{L^L}\operatorname{Cap}(\mathfrak p),\qquad
\operatorname{Cap}(\mathfrak p):=\inf_{x_1,\ldots,x_L>0}
\frac{\mathfrak p(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}{x_1\cdots x_L}.
\]
The capacity definition appears on page 6. H-stability means nonvanishing whenever all variables have positive real parts, as defined on page 7. Example 2.6 starts on page 8, and part (1), on page 9 in this PDF, specializes the corollary to the product of row linear forms of a doubly stochastic matrix and states \(\min_{B\in\Omega_L}\operatorname{per}(B)=L!/L^L\). We use only its lower-bound direction. The applicability check is in (11)–(13): the clone matrix is square, nonnegative, and exactly doubly stochastic; its product polynomial is H-stable and has capacity 1, and its indicated derivative equals the permanent. Zero entries and disconnected blocks are allowed by the theorem. No other external theorem is used; in particular the cloning identity and the factorial envelope (18)–(19) are proved in the packet.
