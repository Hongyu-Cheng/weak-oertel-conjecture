## Statement

In the median-pair depth problem in continuous dimension $d\ge1$, fix a median-fiber mark $y$. Let $v_y=\max_\sigma f_y(\sigma)$ over the prescribed probability couplings and let $v_F=\max_\sigma\min_i\int c_i\,d\sigma$ for a nonempty finite list of closed through-$y$ caps $H_i$, with $c_i=\mathbf1_{H_i}\circ T$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ some list satisfies $v_y\le v_F<v_y+\varepsilon$. For every finite list,
$$
v_F=\min_{\lambda_i\ge0,\,\sum_i\lambda_i=1}\ \max_\sigma\int c_\lambda\,d\sigma,
\qquad c_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i c_i.
$$
Both optima and a finite primal optimum are attained. For any minimizing $\lambda$, every finite primal optimizer maximizes the weighted cost and satisfies $\int c_i\,d\sigma_F=v_F$ whenever $\lambda_i>0$. At a joint optimizer $(\sigma_*,y_*)$ of value $V$, choose an approximating list at $y_*$. Its minimizing weights satisfy
$$
0\le\max_\sigma\int c_\lambda\,d\sigma-\int c_\lambda\,d\sigma_*<\varepsilon,
\qquad
0\le\sum_i\lambda_i\bigl(\nu_{\sigma_*}(H_i)-V\bigr)<\varepsilon.
$$

## Proof

The feasible coupling space is weakly compact and convex, and each closed-cap mass is affine and upper semicontinuous. Hence any finite minimum attains its maximum. If the closed constraints $\int\mathbf1_H\circ T\,d\sigma\ge v_y+\varepsilon$ for caps through $y$ were feasible for every finite subfamily, their finite intersection property on the compact coupling set would give one feasible coupling for the whole family. This contradicts the definition of $v_y$. An infeasible finite family has optimum strictly below the threshold by attainment, and using only its constraints cannot decrease the optimum.

For the finite list consider the set $B\subset\mathbb R^m$ of vectors whose $i$th coordinate is at most $\int c_i\,d\sigma$ for some coupling $\sigma$, simultaneously for all $i$. It is convex by affinity and closed by weak compactness and upper semicontinuity in every coordinate. It is downward closed, and the largest point of the form $(t,\ldots,t)$ it contains is $(v_F,\ldots,v_F)$. A supporting hyperplane there has a nonzero outward vector with nonnegative coordinates by downward closure. Normalize this vector to have sum one, obtaining $\lambda$. The support bound is $\sum_i\lambda_i\int c_i\,d\sigma\le v_F$ for all $\sigma$. Conversely any nonnegative weights of sum one give a weighted average at least the least of the cap masses, so their optimized weighted cost is at least $v_F$. This proves the attained dual identity, with the weighted maximum attained by upper semicontinuity. At any finite primal optimizer, each cap mass is at least $v_F$, and the weighted average equals $v_F$ for any minimizing weights. This gives exact activity at positive weights and weighted transport optimality.

At $y_*$, the fixed-center value is $V$. Each of its caps has mass at least $V$ under $\sigma_*$. The selected weighted cost at $\sigma_*$ is therefore between $V$ and the optimal weighted value $v_F<V+\varepsilon$. The two stated inequalities follow.
