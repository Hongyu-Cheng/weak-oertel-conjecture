## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(E\) be any set of ordered pairs of distinct labels in \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let \(G\) be its underlying simple undirected graph: \(\{u,v\}\) is an edge of \(G\) if \((u,v)\in E\) or \((v,u)\in E\). Both orientations are allowed in \(E\). Let \(\Delta\ge0\) be any real upper bound for the maximum degree of \(G\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_E&=\{z\in[0,2]^n:z_u-z_v\le1\text{ for every }(u,v)\in E\},\\
A_E&=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n:x_u-x_v\le1\text{ for every }(u,v)\in E\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(Q_E\) is nonempty, compact and convex, and \(A_E=Q_E\cap\mathbb Z^n\).

For an arbitrary field \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), retain the original affine law
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in A_E}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z}\quad(x\in A_E),
$$
extended by zero off \(A_E\). Use exactly the full reflected quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, with the following identity map of notation and normalization:
$$
H_p(j)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\qquad
b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2.
$$
The displacement sum is oriented and includes zero once. Put
$$
\kappa_\Delta=-\log\left(1-\frac{5}{48\,2^{\Delta+1}}\right)>0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_\Delta=\frac{2\kappa_\Delta}{1+\Delta^2}>0.
$$
Then
$$
4^n b(p)\ge\exp(\varepsilon_\Delta n).
\tag{1}
$$
This includes isolated vertices, \(\Delta=0\), disconnected graphs, and arbitrary real fields.

In particular, for every \(n\ge1\), the cyclic census
$$
A_n=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n:x_i-x_{i+1}\le1\text{ for all }i\},
\qquad x_{n+1}=x_1,
$$
with its original affine law and full reflected \(b(p)\), satisfies
$$
4^n b(p)\ge
\exp\left(\frac25\log\frac{384}{379}\,n\right)
\ge e^{n/192}.
\tag{2}
$$
Thus the requested bounded-field cyclic surplus holds with \(a=1\) and \(\varepsilon=1/192\).

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. The set \(Q_E\) is the intersection of the compact box \([0,2]^n\) with finitely many closed halfspaces, so it is compact and convex. It contains \(\{0,1\}^n\), because the difference of any two coordinates of such a point is at most one. In particular it is nonempty. Its integer points are exactly \(A_E\), proving the complete census assertion.

Let \(X\) have the original law \(p\), and let \(B_i=X_i\pmod2\in\{0,1\}\). Write \(B=(B_1,\ldots,B_n)\) and \(\mu(\beta)=\mathbb P(B=\beta)\). Every \(\beta\in\{0,1\}^n\) has positive mass, since the point \(x=\beta\) belongs to \(A_E\). Its Shannon entropy is
$$
\mathsf H(B)=-\sum_{\beta\in\{0,1\}^n}\mu(\beta)\log\mu(\beta).
$$

We first establish the exact parity collision identity with the original normalizer. If \(x,y\in A_E\) have the same coordinatewise parity, their midpoint \(j=(x+y)/2\) is integral and belongs to \(Q_E\) by convexity. Hence \(j\in A_E\). The correspondence
$$
(x,y)\longleftrightarrow(j,v)
=\left(\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{x-y}{2}\right)
$$
is a bijection between ordered same-parity pairs in \(A_E^2\) and supported full reflected pairs \((j,v)\). Log-affinity, using the same original \(Z\), gives \(p_xp_y=p_j^2\) and \(\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}=p_j\) for each such pair. Consequently the canonical \(\chi(p)\) of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\beta}\mu(\beta)^2
&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A_E\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}p_xp_y\\
&=\sum_{j\in A_E}p_j
\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}
=\chi(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The Jensen comparison used in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 therefore gives, with no change to its full reflected quantities,
$$
b(p)=\mathbb E_p H_p(X)^2
\ge\bigl(\mathbb E_p H_p(X)\bigr)^2
=\chi(p)^2.
\tag{4}
$$
Concavity of the logarithm also yields
$$
\log\chi(p)
=\log\left(\sum_{\beta}\mu(\beta)\mu(\beta)\right)
\ge\sum_{\beta}\mu(\beta)\log\mu(\beta)
=-\mathsf H(B).
\tag{5}
$$

We now prove the local entropy deficit. For a vertex \(i\), let \(d_i\) be its degree in \(G\), let \(C_i\) be its closed neighborhood, and let \(\mu_i\) be the marginal law of \(B_{C_i}\). Thus \(|C_i|=d_i+1\). Define two parity strings on \(C_i\): let \(s_i^{(0)}\) have center coordinate zero and every neighbor coordinate one, and let \(s_i^{(1)}\) have every coordinate one. Then the exact local event ratio is
$$
\mu_i(s_i^{(0)})
=(e^{-\ell_i}+e^{\ell_i})\mu_i(s_i^{(1)})
\ge2\mu_i(s_i^{(1)}).
\tag{6}
$$
Indeed, a coordinate of parity one must equal one. In a configuration in the event \(B_{C_i}=s_i^{(1)}\), the actual center and all its neighbors therefore equal one. Replacing the center value by zero or by two preserves every constraint. All constraints incident to the center have the other endpoint equal to one, so their coordinate differences, in either orientation, belong to \(\{-1,1\}\). All remaining constraints are unchanged. Conversely every configuration in the event \(B_{C_i}=s_i^{(0)}\) has center value zero or two and arises uniquely from the corresponding replacement. The two unnormalized weight ratios are \(e^{-\ell_i}\) and \(e^{\ell_i}\), respectively. Summing over the exterior configurations and dividing by the unchanged original \(Z\) proves equality in (6). The inequality follows from \(e^t+e^{-t}\ge2\). This reasoning also covers \(d_i=0\), when the condition on neighbors is vacuous.

On the \(2^{d_i+1}\) parity strings on \(C_i\), put
$$
f_i(s)=\mathbf1_{\{s=s_i^{(0)}\}}-2\mathbf1_{\{s=s_i^{(1)}\}},
\qquad \lambda=\log\frac43.
$$
Equation (6) says \(\mathbb E_{\mu_i}f_i\ge0\). Introduce the positive reference probability law
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_i(s)&=\frac{e^{\lambda f_i(s)}}{\mathcal S_i},\\
\mathcal S_i
&=2^{d_i+1}-2+\frac43+\frac9{16}
=2^{d_i+1}-\frac5{48}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For any two positive probability laws \(u,v\) on a common finite set, the elementary inequality \(-\log t\ge1-t\) implies
$$
\sum_su(s)\log\frac{u(s)}{v(s)}
\ge\sum_su(s)\left(1-\frac{v(s)}{u(s)}\right)=0.
\tag{7}
$$
Applying (7) to \(\mu_i,\rho_i\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le\sum_s\mu_i(s)\log\frac{\mu_i(s)}{\rho_i(s)}\\
&=-\mathsf H(B_{C_i})-\lambda\mathbb E_{\mu_i}f_i
+\log\mathcal S_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since \(\lambda>0\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf H(B_{C_i})
&\le\log\mathcal S_i\\
&=(d_i+1)\log2
+\log\left(1-\frac{5}{48\,2^{d_i+1}}\right)\\
&\le(d_i+1)\log2-\kappa_\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The last step holds because \(d_i\le\Delta\) and the positive deficit \(-\log(1-5/[48\,2^{d+1}])\) decreases as \(d\) increases.

Choose centers greedily as follows. Retain any remaining vertex as a center and remove from consideration every remaining vertex at graph distance at most two from that center, with distances measured in \(G\). Continue until no vertices remain. Any two retained centers are at distance at least three, so their closed neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint. A radius-two ball about \(i\) contains at most
$$
1+d_i+\sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(d_j-1)
\le1+d_i+d_i(\Delta-1)
=1+d_i\Delta
\le1+\Delta^2
$$
vertices: a vertex at distance two is reached through a neighbor and may be counted more than once in the sum. If \(d_i=0\), the sum is empty and the same bound reads \(1\le1+\Delta^2\). Thus, if \(m\) centers are retained, the removals cover all \(n\) vertices and give
$$
m\ge\frac{n}{1+\Delta^2}.
\tag{9}
$$
This includes \(\Delta=0\), when every vertex is selected and its closed neighborhood is a singleton.

Use the selected closed neighborhoods as blocks, and use every vertex outside their union as a singleton block. Entropy is subadditive across these blocks even though their laws need not be independent. To check this using only (7), compare the joint law of all the blocks with the product of its block marginals. Their relative entropy is exactly the sum of the block entropies minus the joint entropy and is nonnegative. A singleton bit has entropy at most \(\log2\), by (7) with its uniform reference law. Applying (8) to the \(m\) selected closed neighborhoods therefore proves
$$
\mathsf H(B)
\le n\log2-m\kappa_\Delta
\le n\log2-\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{1+\Delta^2}\,n.
\tag{10}
$$
Combining (3)--(5) with (10) gives
$$
4^n b(p)
\ge4^n\chi(p)^2
\ge4^n e^{-2\mathsf H(B)}
\ge\exp\left(\frac{2\kappa_\Delta}{1+\Delta^2}\,n\right).
$$
This is (1), with the full reflected rows and the original affine law throughout.

For the cyclic census, discard the vacuous self-loop when \(n=1\). When \(n=2\), take both directed orientations of its single undirected edge. For \(n\ge3\), take the directed edges \((i,i+1)\), cyclically. In every case the underlying simple undirected graph has degree at most two, and its census is exactly \(A_n\). We may therefore take \(\Delta=2\) in (1), obtaining \(\kappa_2=\log(384/379)\) and the first inequality of (2). Finally, \(-\log(1-u)\ge u\) for \(0\le u<1\), so
$$
\frac25\log\frac{384}{379}
=\frac25\left[-\log\left(1-\frac5{384}\right)\right]
\ge\frac25\frac5{384}=\frac1{192}.
$$
This proves (2) and the stated cyclic constants.
