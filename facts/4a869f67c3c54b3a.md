## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be in the canonical hard-branch setting of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66`, and write
\[
 C_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_y:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y),\qquad
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx
\]
for every positive-volume integer fiber.  Put
\[
 \mathcal K_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y),\qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C).
\]
Let \(Q>0\) be the half-strength canonical product-reservoir mass.  The hard branch and the complete transverse atlas give
\[
 D+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\frac Q{100},\qquad
 P:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\theta_\sigma>\frac{99}{200}Q.
\tag{1}
\]

Here is the exact notation map from `036a276c54ec5e66`.  A label there is \(\sigma=(z;e,f)\), where
\[
 e=\{z,u_\sigma\},\qquad f=\{z,v_\sigma\}.
\]
The two bases \(u_\sigma\ne v_\sigma\) are the label's opposite sources and satisfy \(u_\sigma\equiv v_\sigma\pmod2\).  Define the occurrence contacts by
\[
 x_{\sigma,u_\sigma}:=x_{e,u_\sigma},\qquad
 x_{\sigma,v_\sigma}:=x_{f,v_\sigma}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
 x_{\sigma,y}\in\mathcal K_y
 \quad(y\in\{u_\sigma,v_\sigma\}),\qquad
 m_\sigma:=\frac{u_\sigma+v_\sigma}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]
\[
 X_\sigma:=\frac{x_{\sigma,u_\sigma}+x_{\sigma,v_\sigma}}2,
 \qquad (m_\sigma,X_\sigma)\in C.
\tag{3}
\]

For every nonempty whole-label subfamily \(\mathcal F\subseteq\Sigma\), put
\[
 P_{\mathcal F}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}\theta_\sigma,
\]
and count endpoint occurrences by
\[
 \ell_y(\mathcal F):=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}\theta_\sigma
 \bigl(\mathbf1_{\{u_\sigma=y\}}+
       \mathbf1_{\{v_\sigma=y\}}\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]
Then the exact physical occurrence budget is
\[
 \boxed{\ell_y(\mathcal F)\leq\ell_y(\Sigma)\leq\frac{a_y}{4}}
 \qquad(y\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{5}
\]

Reflect every occurrence contact through its physical fiber centroid:
\[
 x_{\sigma,y}^{\dagger}:=2c_y-x_{\sigma,y}
 \quad(y\in\{u_\sigma,v_\sigma\}),
\]
\[
 X_\sigma^{\dagger}:=
 \frac{x_{\sigma,u_\sigma}^{\dagger}+
       x_{\sigma,v_\sigma}^{\dagger}}2,
 \qquad
 q_\sigma^c:=\frac{X_\sigma+X_\sigma^{\dagger}}2.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{x_{\sigma,y}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal K_y},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 q_\sigma^c
 =\frac{c_{u_\sigma}+c_{v_\sigma}}2,\qquad
 (m_\sigma,X_\sigma^{\dagger}),
 (m_\sigma,q_\sigma^c)
 \in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).}
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(\mu_C\) be the canonical mixed-volume measure.  The two measures
\[
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{+}:=
 2\gamma_d\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}
 \theta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)},
 \qquad
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{-}:=
 2\gamma_d\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}
 \theta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma^{\dagger})}
\tag{9}
\]
are each halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\).  Consequently the collision-pooled paired measure
\[
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{\pm}:=
 \frac12\bigl(\nu_{\mathcal F}^{+}+\nu_{\mathcal F}^{-}\bigr)
 =\gamma_d\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}\theta_\sigma
 \left(
  \delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)}+
  \delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma^{\dagger})}
 \right)
\tag{10}
\]
and the centroid-midpoint measure
\[
 \sigma_{\mathcal F}^c:=
 2\gamma_d\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}
 \theta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,q_\sigma^c)}
\tag{11}
\]
satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{\pm}(H)\leq\mu_C(H),\qquad
 \sigma_{\mathcal F}^c(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{12}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\), and
\[
 |\nu_{\mathcal F}^{\pm}|=2\gamma_dP_{\mathcal F},
 \qquad
 |\sigma_{\mathcal F}^c|=2\gamma_dP_{\mathcal F}.
\tag{13}
\]
All coincident original atoms, reflected atoms, and centroid-midpoint atoms are aggregated as measures in (9)--(13); no target fiber is charged separately for colliding labels.

Define the intrinsic ranks after this aggregation by
\[
 A_{\mathcal F}^m:=
 \operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}
 (\operatorname{supp}\sigma_{\mathcal F}^c),
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathcal F}^m:=\dim A_{\mathcal F}^m,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
 r_{\mathcal F}^m:=
 \min_{s:A_{\mathcal F}^m\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q_\sigma^c-s(m_\sigma):\sigma\in\mathcal F\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Repeated copies of one support point in (15) are harmless and may equivalently be removed.  There exists
\[
 p_{\mathcal F}\in
 C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)
\tag{16}
\]
such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni p_{\mathcal F}\) satisfies
\[
 \mu_C(H)\geq
 \frac{2\gamma_dP_{\mathcal F}}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathcal F}^m}(r_{\mathcal F}^m+1)}.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^m}
 \frac{P_{\mathcal F}}{r_{\mathcal F}^m+1}.}
\tag{18}
\]

Let \(A_{\mathcal F}^e:=\{u_\sigma,v_\sigma:\sigma\in\mathcal F\}\), let
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal F}^e:=\dim\operatorname{aff}A_{\mathcal F}^e,
\]
and define the endpoint-centroid residual rank
\[
 r_{\mathcal F}^e:=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}A_{\mathcal F}^e\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-a(y):y\in A_{\mathcal F}^e\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Form the endpoint multigraph \(G_{\mathcal F}\) with vertex set \(A_{\mathcal F}^e\) and one edge \(\{u_\sigma,v_\sigma\}\) for each label, retaining parallel edges.  Let \(b(\mathcal F)\) be the number of connected components of \(G_{\mathcal F}\) that are bipartite.  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_{\mathcal F}^m\leq\rho_{\mathcal F}^e,
 \qquad
 r_{\mathcal F}^e\leq r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F).}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular, if \(G_{\mathcal F}\) is connected, then
\[
 r_{\mathcal F}^e\leq r_{\mathcal F}^m+1,
\tag{21}
\]
and the sharper \(r_{\mathcal F}^e\leq r_{\mathcal F}^m\) holds when that connected component is nonbipartite.

The same occurrence weights give one endpoint-centroid selector.  Namely,
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 8\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e}
 \frac{P_{\mathcal F}}{r_{\mathcal F}^e+1}
 \geq
 8\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e}
 \frac{P_{\mathcal F}}
 {r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F)+1}.}
\tag{22}
\]
Together with (18), this gives the exact non-extraction interface
\[
 2\gamma_dP_{\mathcal F}
 \left[
  \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^m}}
       {r_{\mathcal F}^m+1}
  +
  \frac{4\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e}}
       {r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F)+1}
 \right]
 \leq D+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 <\frac Q{100}.
\tag{23}
\]
In particular, every nonempty \(\mathcal F\) obeys the strict rank screens
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathcal F}^m+1>
 200\gamma_d\frac{P_{\mathcal F}}Q
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^m},
 \qquad
 r_{\mathcal F}^e+1>
 1600\gamma_d\frac{P_{\mathcal F}}Q
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e},
 \qquad
 r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F)+1>
 1600\gamma_d\frac{P_{\mathcal F}}Q
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e}.}
\tag{24}
\]

For the complete atlas \(\mathcal F=\Sigma\), equations (1) and (24) force
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\Sigma}^m+1>
 99\gamma_d\,
 2^{n-\rho_{\Sigma}^m}.}
\tag{25}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\Sigma}^e+1>
 792\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\Sigma}^e},
 \qquad
 r_{\Sigma}^m+b(\Sigma)+1>
 792\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\Sigma}^e}.}
\tag{26}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 (\operatorname{supp}\sigma_{\Sigma}^c)
 =\rho_{\Sigma}^m+r_{\Sigma}^m
 \geq n+6,}
\tag{27}
\]
while
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{(y,c_y):y\in A_\Sigma^e\}
 =\rho_\Sigma^e+r_\Sigma^e
 \geq n+9.}
\tag{28}
\]
Thus the centroid-midpoint support contains at least \(n+7\) distinct actual mixed-integer points, and the endpoint-centroid support contains at least \(n+10\) distinct physical fibers.  If \(\rho_{\Sigma}^m=n\), then
\[
 \boxed{r_{\Sigma}^m\geq36,}
\tag{29}
\]
and if \(\rho_\Sigma^e=n\), then
\[
 \boxed{r_\Sigma^e\geq288.}
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (18), (20), and (22)--(30) are screens and interfaces only.  They do not assert the existence of a proper subfamily whose two legal scores reach the hard-ledger threshold, and they do not close the locked conjecture.

## Proof

1. Exact occurrence capacity.  Fix a physical opposite-source base \(y\).  In the notation of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66`, an occurrence of \(y\) in a label uses a unique reservoir edge \(e\ni y\), with the transverse pairing performed at the other endpoint of \(e\).  For fixed \(e\), the total weight of all such labels is a sub-sum of the transverse degree
\[
 \kappa_e=\sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal T}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
 \leq\omega_e.
\]
Therefore, for every subfamily,
\[
 \ell_y(\mathcal F)
 \leq\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_e
 \leq\sum_{e\ni y}r_e
 =d_y(r)
 \leq\frac{a_y}{4}.
\tag{31}
\]
This sums over the distinct physical source fiber only once, even when many labels, contacts, or reservoir edges use it.  Transversality makes the two opposite sources of one label distinct, so (4) counts exactly its two occurrences.  This proves (5).

2. Reflection of the half-metronoid.  Fix a positive-volume fiber \(C_y\) and \(x\in\mathcal K_y\).  Choose a representing density \(f:C_y\to[0,1]\) with
\[
 \int_{C_y}f=\frac{a_y}{2},\qquad
 \int_{C_y}zf(z)\,dz=\frac{a_y}{2}x.
\]
The complementary density \(1-f\) has the same mass, and
\[
 \frac2{a_y}\int_{C_y}z(1-f(z))\,dz
 =2c_y-x.
\tag{32}
\]
Hence \(2c_y-x\in\mathcal K_y\), proving (7).  Applying this to the two contacts of every label gives
\[
 X_\sigma^{\dagger}
 =c_{u_\sigma}+c_{v_\sigma}-X_\sigma,
 \qquad
 q_\sigma^c
 =\frac{c_{u_\sigma}+c_{v_\sigma}}2.
\tag{33}
\]
Every point of a half-metronoid belongs to its fiber body.  Convexity of \(C\), together with the same-parity integrality of \(m_\sigma\), first puts \((m_\sigma,X_\sigma^{\dagger})\) in \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and then puts the midpoint of \(X_\sigma\) and \(X_\sigma^{\dagger}\) there as well.  This proves (8), including the possibility that the target fiber has zero \(d\)-volume.

3. Collision-safe original, reflected, paired, and center measures.  For a fixed \(\mathcal F\), equation (5) is precisely the quarter-occurrence hypothesis of accepted fact `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd`.  For its first simultaneous choice take the two original half-metronoid contacts, whose average is \(X_\sigma\); for its second simultaneous choice take the reflected contacts from (7), whose average is \(X_\sigma^{\dagger}\).  The accepted theorem applies independently to the two choices and gives
\[
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{+}(H)\leq\mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 \nu_{\mathcal F}^{-}(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{34}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace.  Averaging (34) proves the first inequality in (12) for (10).  The theorem explicitly counts repeated sources by occurrences and aggregates coincident target atoms, so (34) uses neither a duplicated source capacity nor separate target-fiber capacity.

For the stronger center measure, observe that the constant density \(1/2\) on \(C_y\) has mass \(a_y/2\) and barycenter \(c_y\).  Hence \(c_y\in\mathcal K_y\).  Apply `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd` a third time, now choosing the two endpoint centroids for every label.  Their average is exactly \(q_\sigma^c\), so the theorem gives directly
\[
 2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F}\theta_\sigma
 \delta_{(m_\sigma,q_\sigma^c)}(H)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{35}
\]
This is (11)--(13) with mass \(2\gamma_dP_{\mathcal F}\).  It is strictly stronger than passing from the paired measure by the elementary center indicator inequality, which would retain only half this coefficient.  Collision aggregation is already part of the accepted theorem, so (35) remains one physical measure.

4. The mixed-integer centerpoint and depth screen.  Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to the nonzero measure \(\sigma_{\mathcal F}^c\).  Equation (8) supplies its support in \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), (35) supplies halfspace domination, and (14)--(15) are exactly its intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks.  That theorem both attains the minimum in (15) and gives a point \(p_{\mathcal F}\) in the same mixed-integer convex body satisfying (17).  This proves (16)--(17); multiplying the corresponding bound for \(h_{\max}(C)\) by \(2^n\) proves (18).  Thus the centerpoint is not merely a center of the atomic measure: it is an actual point of \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

5. Endpoint-centroid rank.  Since every \(m_\sigma\) is the midpoint of two endpoint bases,
\[
 A_{\mathcal F}^m\subseteq\operatorname{aff}A_{\mathcal F}^e,
\]
which proves the base-rank inequality in (20).  Choose an affine map \(s\) attaining (15), and put
\[
 V:=\operatorname{span}
 \{q_\sigma^c-s(m_\sigma):\sigma\in\mathcal F\},
 \qquad \dim V=r_{\mathcal F}^m.
\tag{36}
\]
Extend \(s\) affinely from \(A_{\mathcal F}^m\) to \(\operatorname{aff}A_{\mathcal F}^e\), calling the extension \(a\), and set
\[
 w_y:=c_y-a(y).
\]
For every label edge,
\[
 \frac{w_{u_\sigma}+w_{v_\sigma}}2
 =q_\sigma^c-s(m_\sigma)\in V.
\tag{37}
\]
In the quotient \(\mathbb R^d/V\), equation (37) says
\[
 \overline w_{u_\sigma}=-\overline w_{v_\sigma}.
\tag{38}
\]
On a connected bipartite component, all quotient vectors are therefore signed copies of one vector, so that component contributes at most one quotient dimension.  On a connected nonbipartite component, transport around an odd cycle yields \(\overline w_y=-\overline w_y\), hence every quotient vector on that component is zero.  Consequently, if \(W_0:=\operatorname{span}\{w_y:y\in A_{\mathcal F}^e\}\), then
\[
 \dim\bigl(W_0/(W_0\cap V)\bigr)\leq b(\mathcal F).
\]
It follows that
\[
 \dim W_0
 \leq\dim V+b(\mathcal F)
 =r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F).
\tag{39}
\]
The map \(a\) is admissible in (19), so minimizing there proves the residual-rank inequality in (20), and (21) follows with the stated nonbipartite sharpening.

6. Endpoint-centroid selector.  For every \(y\in A_{\mathcal F}^e\), the occurrence weight \(\ell_y(\mathcal F)\) is positive, and (5) gives
\[
 0<\ell_y(\mathcal F)\leq\frac{a_y}{4}.
\]
Because every label has two distinct endpoint occurrences,
\[
 \sum_{y\in A_{\mathcal F}^e}\ell_y(\mathcal F)
 =2P_{\mathcal F}.
\tag{40}
\]
Apply the centroid-amplified selector theorem of accepted fact `844dca4a76115be3` with the single support \(A_{\mathcal F}^e\) and calibration weights \(\lambda_y=\ell_y(\mathcal F)\).  Equations (19) and (40) give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 4\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal F}^e}
 \frac{2P_{\mathcal F}}{r_{\mathcal F}^e+1}.
\tag{41}
\]
The residual-selector inequality recorded in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives \(D\geq R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), so (41) proves the full first chain in (22).  The same depth inequalities also make
\[
 \zeta_{\mathcal F}^e:=
 4\gamma_d\sum_{y\in A_{\mathcal F}^e}
 \ell_y(\mathcal F)\delta_{(y,c_y)}
\]
one halfspace-dominated measure: each atom is bounded by the absolute depth of \(c_y\) in its own distinct physical fiber.  Its intrinsic ranks are exactly \((\rho_{\mathcal F}^e,r_{\mathcal F}^e)\), so the coordinate-free rank identity of `be359f8a6c628483` applies to the endpoint-centroid set.  Equation (20) permits replacing \(r_{\mathcal F}^e\) by the possibly larger \(r_{\mathcal F}^m+b(\mathcal F)\), proving the second inequality in (22).  Adding the independently legal lower bounds (18) and the second hard-ledger term \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) proves the first inequality in (23); the hard ledger (1) proves its strict upper bound.  The inequalities \(D<Q/100\) and (18) give the first screen in (24).  For either denominator in (22), both \(D\) and \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) are at least the displayed endpoint score.  Hence \(D+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) is at least twice that score; combining this with (1) and rearranging proves the other two screens in (24).  Only one selector support and one affine endpoint map are used in (41); the factor two in the endpoint screens comes from the two distinct numerical ledger terms, not from duplicating a physical selector.

7. Complete-atlas consequences.  Take \(\mathcal F=\Sigma\).  From (1) and (18),
\[
 \frac Q{100}>D
 \geq2\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_\Sigma^m}
 \frac{P}{r_\Sigma^m+1}
 >\frac{99\gamma_d}{100}
 2^{n-\rho_\Sigma^m}
 \frac Q{r_\Sigma^m+1},
\]
which independently confirms (25).  Substituting \(P>99Q/200\) into the other two screens in (24) gives both strict inequalities in (26).

For completeness, the elementary estimates
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 <\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <\frac{11}{4}
\]
give \(\gamma_d>4/11\).  The second strict inequality follows, for example, by summing through \(k=3\) and bounding the remaining series by
\[
 \sum_{k=4}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 \leq\frac1{4!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{5^j}
 =\frac5{96}.
\]
Hence (25) and (26) give
\[
 r_\Sigma^m+1>36\,2^{n-\rho_\Sigma^m},
 \qquad
 r_\Sigma^e+1>288\,2^{n-\rho_\Sigma^e}.
\tag{42}
\]
For every integer \(r\geq0\), \(r+1\leq2^r\).  Therefore
\[
 2^{\rho_\Sigma^m+r_\Sigma^m}
 \geq2^{\rho_\Sigma^m}(r_\Sigma^m+1)
 >36\,2^n
 >2^{n+5},
\tag{43}
\]
and similarly
\[
 2^{\rho_\Sigma^e+r_\Sigma^e}
 \geq2^{\rho_\Sigma^e}(r_\Sigma^e+1)
 >288\,2^n
 >2^{n+8}.
\tag{44}
\]
The intrinsic-rank identity in `be359f8a6c628483` identifies the exponent in (43) with the affine dimension of the aggregated support of \(\sigma_\Sigma^c\); the same elementary identity applied to the endpoint-centroid set identifies the exponent in (44).  Since the dimensions are integers, (43)--(44) prove (27)--(28) and the support-size conclusions.  If \(\rho_\Sigma^m=n\), the first inequality in (42) proves (29); if \(\rho_\Sigma^e=n\), its second inequality proves (30).  Nothing in this argument selects a proper hereditary subfamily whose score contradicts (23), so no extraction conclusion follows.
