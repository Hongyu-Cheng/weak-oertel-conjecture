## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $P$ be a finite nonempty lattice-complete set, $B=\operatorname{conv}P$, and $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be positive compact convex fibers satisfying all anchored Minkowski inclusions on $P$. For each action let a finite family $\ell_i(u,z)$ be continuous in the unit normal $u$ and affine in $z$, with $\ell_i(u,z)\ge h_{K_z}(u)$ and $\min_i\ell_i(u,z)=h_{K_z}(u)$ for every $(z,u)$ on the sampled bases. Let $V_i(u,z,X)$ be jointly continuous, finite and, for fixed $u$, convex in the full physical variables $(z,X)$. Define
$$
C_t=\{(z,X):z\in B,\ u\cdot X+tV_i(u,z,X)\le\ell_i(u,z)\text{ for all }i,u\}.
$$
For all sufficiently small $t\ge0$ this is compact and convex with projection $B$ and positive integer fibers exactly over $P$. Its sampled fibers start at $K_z$ and are uniformly $O(t)$ Hausdorff and radially close to them. At each regular $X\in\partial K_z$ with outward unit normal $u$, their signed inward normal speed is
$$
v_z(X)=\max_{i:\ell_i(u,z)=h_{K_z}(u)} V_i(u,z,X).
$$
For finitely many such actions on the same family, every finite combination of their speeds with nonnegative coefficients is the speed of one such action. The first changes of integrals against fixed continuous weights, and the first losses of a fixed cap whose fiber traces are proper or strictly full or empty, add with the same coefficients. The same cap conclusion applies to a supporting trace whose cutting section has zero surface measure. In general fixed cap losses at positive-area supporting traces fail to add.

## Proof

On $B\times\{\|X\|\le1\}$ and the normal sphere all fields are uniformly bounded in absolute value. Convexity on each ray $s\mapsto (z,s\omega)$, applied to the convex combination of $s=0$ and $s=\|X\|$ yielding $s=1$, gives a common lower bound $V_i(u,z,X)\ge-C(1+\|X\|)$. Every baseline is bounded on the compact base hull and sphere. For $X\ne0$, use the constraint at $u=X/\|X\|$ to get $(1-tC)\|X\|\le\sup\ell_i+tC$. This bounds the whole feasible set by a common ball for small $t$. The fields are therefore uniformly bounded there in absolute value by a constant $A$. Enlarge the ball and $A$ to include all old fibers.

The constraints are closed and convex for $t\ge0$. Completeness gives $(C_t)_z\subset K_z+tA B_d$ by the support inequalities, where $B_d$ is the unit ball. For $o_z+r_zB_d\subset K_z$, the homothet about $o_z$ of factor $1-tA/r_z$ has slack at least $tA$ in every old support direction and satisfies all cuts. This supplies positive fibers and the inner radial bound. The outer comparison also gives the outer homothet of factor $1+tA/r_z$ about $o_z$, since $r_z B_d\subset K_z-o_z$. The finite number of sampled bases gives uniform bounds. Their occupancy forces projection $B$ by convexity, and lattice completeness excludes other sampled bases. Anchored inclusions hold for the deformed fibers because $C_t$ is convex. The canonical hull of the new sampled fibers has the same sampled fibers by its containment in $C_t$.

Write the new radial endpoint along $X-o_z$ as $X_t=o_z+(1-s_t)(X-o_z)$, where $|s_t|=O(t)$. An old active cut at $u$ implies $s_t u\cdot(X-o_z)\ge tV_i(u,z,X_t)$. The denominator is at least $r_z$. For the reverse bound every new radial endpoint has a tight cut, since otherwise compactness of the constraint indices and continuity give strictly positive minimum slack and permit radial extension. Along a subsequence realizing the upper limit of $s_t/t$, take its tight index fixed and its normal $u_t$ tending to $u_*$. The old slack $\ell_i(u_t,z)-u_t\cdot X$ is nonnegative and $O(t)$ from the tight equality and the uniform bounds. Its limit is zero. Its two nonnegative summands $\ell_i-h_{K_z}$ and $h_{K_z}-u_t\cdot X$ both vanish there. Regularity implies $u_*=u$, and $i$ is active there. Dropping the nonnegative old slack in the tight equality bounds the upper limit of $s_t/t$ by $V_i(u,z,X)/[u\cdot(X-o_z)]$. Together these bounds prove the stated signed speed.

Surface-almost every convex boundary point is regular. The signed integral from $0$ to $s_t$ in radial-layer coordinates has Jacobian $(1-s)^{d-1}u\cdot(X-o_z)$ relative to $dS(X)\,ds$, where $dS$ denotes $\mathcal H^{d-1}$. This follows by multiplying the $d-1$ tangential derivatives by $1-s$ and taking the normal component of the radial derivative. The uniform bound on $|s_t|/t$ gives domination and yields first loss $\int_{\partial K_z}G(X)v_z(X)\,dS(X)$ for every fixed continuous $G$. For $d=1$ the two endpoint intervals give the same formula with counting measure. A fixed cap indicator is locally constant at an old boundary point off its cutting plane, so the same proof applies when the intersection of its cutting plane with the old boundary is surface-null. This holds at a proper trace since those boundary points are in the relative boundary of the section, at a supporting trace with zero surface measure, and at strict full or empty traces. Pure-base whole or empty fibers contribute the corresponding constant selector.

For the addition, let $\alpha_a\ge0$ be the desired field coefficients and take $\rho_a>0$ with sum one. Use all tuples of action indices, with baseline $\sum_a\rho_a\ell_{ai_a}$ and field $\sum_a\alpha_a V_{ai_a}$. A tuple is active precisely when every component is active, since its old slack is a strictly positively weighted sum of nonnegative old slacks. Each individual active set is nonempty. The maximum over their Cartesian product of the field sum is the sum of the individual maxima, proving exact speed addition. The integral formulas give addition of first losses in the stated scope.

For the supporting example take $P=\{0\}$, $K_0=[-1,1]$, and its two complete endpoint inequalities. With zero field at the left endpoint and constant $1$ or $-1$ at the right endpoint the bodies are $[-1,1-t]$ and $[-1,1+t]$. The fixed cap $X\ge1$ has losses $0$ and $-t$, respectively. The path with their summed field has zero loss.
