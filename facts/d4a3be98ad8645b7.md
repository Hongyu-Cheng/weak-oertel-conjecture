## Statement

Let \(d\ge1\), let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let \(\rho:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]\) be a measurable integrable function that vanishes almost everywhere outside \(T\). Write \(|T|=\operatorname{vol}_d(T)\). For \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^d\ \text{a proper closed halfspace}\\
                  \mathbf y\in H}}
  \int_H\rho(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z .
\]
The maximum of \(\tau_\rho\) over \(T\) is attained. It satisfies both
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\mathbf y\in T}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
&\ge
\frac{\left(\int_T\sqrt{\rho(\mathbf z)}\,d\mathbf z\right)^2}
     {(5+2\sqrt6)|T|},\\
\max_{\mathbf y\in T}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
&\ge
\left(\sqrt[3]3-\sqrt[3]2\right)^{3/2}
\frac{\left(\int_T\rho(\mathbf z)^{2/3}\,d\mathbf z\right)^{3/2}}
     {|T|^{1/2}} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
These assertions include the case \(\rho=0\) almost everywhere.

Now let \(n,d\ge1\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and suppose that \(C\) is invariant under each map \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\), leaving every other coordinate unchanged. Suppose in addition that
\[
C\subseteq[-1,2]^n\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{2}
\]
Use the canonical objects
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,\qquad
C_I=\{\mathbf y:(I,\mathbf y)\in C\},\\
W=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_I|>0,\qquad
N_C(\mathbf y)=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathbf1_{C_I}(\mathbf y),\qquad
R_C(\mathbf y)=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}
                \mathbf1_{C_I\cap C_{-I}}(\mathbf y).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
Here and below volume of a continuous fiber is its \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume. The original total mass in (3) is \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Then \(T=C_{\mathbf0}\) is a convex body and there is a point \(\mathbf y^\star\in T\) such that
\[
\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{3W}{40\,2^n},
\qquad
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))
\ge\frac{3W}{80\,2^n}.
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where the infimum uses every containing closed ambient halfspace, including halfspaces with zero continuous normal. The point \(\mathbf y^\star\) is selected before those halfspaces.

The following exact census identifies the registered single-expansion family as a special case. Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body and let \(K_1,\ldots,K_n\subseteq T\) be compact convex sets, with empty or lower-dimensional sets permitted. Put \(B_{\mathrm{in}}=[0,1]^n\). For each \(i\), let \(B_i\) be the box obtained from \(B_{\mathrm{in}}\) by replacing its \(i\)-th coordinate interval by \([-1,2]\), and set
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(B_{\mathrm{in}}\times T)\ \cup\
\bigcup_{i=1}^n(B_i\times K_i)\right).
\tag{5}
\]
For \(I\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^n\), call a coordinate outer if its value is \(-1\) or \(2\). The complete fibers are
\[
C_I=
\begin{cases}
T,&I\in\{0,1\}^n,\\
K_i,&I\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^n
       \text{ has exactly one outer coordinate, at }i,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
For \(m(\mathbf y)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{K_i}(\mathbf y)\), the exact densities and original mass are
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_C(\mathbf y)&=2^n\bigl(\mathbf1_T(\mathbf y)+m(\mathbf y)\bigr),\\
R_C(\mathbf y)&=\mathbf1_T(\mathbf y)+m(\mathbf y)+m(\mathbf y)^2,\\
W&=2^n\left(|T|+\sum_{i=1}^n|K_i|\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus (4) applies to every body (5). Its conclusion also follows directly from the first inequality of (1) and the exact second identity of (7).

## Proof

We first prove (1). Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y\) contains a parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf y\). Consequently
\[
\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)=
\inf_{\mathbf b\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}
\int_{\{\mathbf z:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y)\le0\}}
          \rho(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z .
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(\mathbb S^{d-1}\) is the Euclidean unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^d\). For each fixed \(\mathbf b\), the function of \(\mathbf y\) inside this infimum is continuous. Indeed, along a convergent sequence of points the halfspace indicators converge at every \(\mathbf z\) outside the limiting boundary hyperplane. That hyperplane has Lebesgue measure zero, and the integrable function \(\rho\) is a dominating function. Dominated convergence therefore applies. The infimum in (8) of continuous functions is upper semicontinuous. It is finite and nonnegative, and hence attains a maximum \(\delta\) on the compact set \(T\). If \(\rho=0\) almost everywhere, \(\delta=0\), every point attains it, and both inequalities in (1) are immediate. We may continue with a nonzero density.

We use the following classical centroid theorem in its continuous volume form: if \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(\mathbf g_K\), every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf g_K\) contains at least \((d/(d+1))^d|K|\) of its volume. The primary source and exact applicability are stated below. Since \((1+1/d)^d<e<3\), this gives the weaker, dimension-independent bound \(|K|/3\).

Fix \(\eta>0\). Start with \(T_0=T\). Whenever \(T_j\) has positive volume, it is a convex body. Let \(\mathbf g_j\) be its volume centroid. Since \(T_j\subseteq T\), (8) and the definition of \(\delta\) give a proper closed halfspace \(H_j\) through \(\mathbf g_j\) with
\[
\int_{H_j}\rho\le\delta+\eta .
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(H_j'\) be the other closed halfspace with the same boundary and put \(T_{j+1}=T_j\cap H_j'\). Write \(V_j=|T_j|\) and \(v_j=V_j-V_{j+1}\). The centroid theorem gives
\[
v_j\ge V_j/3,\qquad V_{j+1}\le2V_j/3 .
\tag{10}
\]
If a zero-volume remainder occurs, terminate the construction. Otherwise continue indefinitely. All remainders are compact and convex; positive-volume remainders have nonempty interior, so every use of the centroid theorem has the stated hypotheses.

Let \(D_j=T_j\setminus T_{j+1}\). These sets are pairwise disjoint, satisfy \(D_j\subseteq H_j\), and have volume \(v_j\). All shared boundaries lie in countably many hyperplanes and have both Lebesgue measure and \(\rho\)-measure zero. By (10), an infinite residual intersection has volume zero. Thus the sets \(D_j\) cover \(T\) up to a null set, in both the finite and infinite cases. From (9),
\[
\int_{D_j}\rho\le\delta+\eta.
\tag{11}
\]

For either \(\beta=1/2\) or \(\beta=1/3\), (10) implies
\[
v_j^\beta
\le
\frac{V_j^\beta-V_{j+1}^\beta}{3^\beta-2^\beta}.
\tag{12}
\]
To check this scalar inequality, set \(t=V_{j+1}/V_j\), so \(0\le t\le2/3\). The function
\((1-t)^\beta/(1-t^\beta)\) is increasing on \([0,1)\): for \(0<t<1\), its logarithmic derivative has the sign of \(t^{\beta-1}-1\), which is positive. Its value at \(2/3\) is \(1/(3^\beta-2^\beta)\). This proves (12), including \(t=0\) by continuity. Summing the telescoping right side gives
\[
\sum_jv_j^\beta
\le\frac{|T|^\beta}{3^\beta-2^\beta}.
\tag{13}
\]
In an infinite construction this follows first for finite partial sums and then by monotone convergence.

Hölder's inequality on each \(D_j\), followed by (11) and (13), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_T\rho^{1-\beta}
&=\sum_j\int_{D_j}\rho^{1-\beta}\\
&\le\sum_j v_j^\beta
                \left(\int_{D_j}\rho\right)^{1-\beta}\\
&\le
\frac{|T|^\beta(\delta+\eta)^{1-\beta}}
     {3^\beta-2^\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The fractional powers of \(\rho\) are integrable on \(T\), again by Hölder and the finiteness of \(|T|\) and \(\int_T\rho\). Let \(\eta\) decrease to zero in (14). With \(\beta=1/2\), rearranging gives
\[
\delta\ge(\sqrt3-\sqrt2)^2
             \frac{\left(\int_T\sqrt\rho\right)^2}{|T|}
=\frac{\left(\int_T\sqrt\rho\right)^2}
       {(5+2\sqrt6)|T|}.
\]
With \(\beta=1/3\), rearranging gives the second inequality in (1). This proves both density estimates and all attainment and zero-density assertions.

We next establish the exact integer counting needed under (2). Put \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and for \(J\subseteq[n]\) let \(\mathbf u_J\in\{0,1\}^n\) have value one exactly on \(J\). For a fixed continuous point \(\mathbf y\), define
\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf y}
=\{J\subseteq[n]:\mathbf y\in C_{-\mathbf u_J}\},
\qquad
a(\mathbf y)=|\mathcal E_{\mathbf y}|.
\tag{15}
\]
These are a finite family of subsets and its cardinality, rather than the original marginal \(N_C\) or the new score \(R_C\).

All possible integer sites under (2) lie in \(\{-1,0,1,2\}^n\). For such a site \(I\), let \(J(I)=\{i:I_i\in\{-1,2\}\}\). Coordinate reflections send \(I\) to \(-\mathbf u_{J(I)}\), preserving the continuous coordinates. Hence \(C_I=C_{-\mathbf u_{J(I)}}\). For each fixed subset \(J\), there are exactly \(2^n\) sites with \(J(I)=J\): each outer coordinate has two choices, and each other coordinate also has two choices. Consequently
\[
N_C(\mathbf y)=2^n a(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R^d}a(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y=W/2^n .
\tag{16}
\]

The family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf y}\) is downward closed. If \(J\) is in it and \(i\in J\), reflect the \(i\)-th coordinate of \((-\mathbf u_J,\mathbf y)\), obtaining value \(2\) in that coordinate and the same other coordinates. The segment between the original and reflected points is in \(C\) and contains the point with value \(0\) in the \(i\)-th coordinate. Thus \(J\setminus\{i\}\) is also in \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf y}\). Repeating removes any desired subset. In particular, this family is nonempty exactly when \(\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf0}\).

All continuous fibers therefore lie in \(T=C_{\mathbf0}\). Since \(W>0\) and only finitely many fibers are possible, at least one has positive \(d\)-volume. Its containment in \(T\) shows that \(T\) has positive volume. It is compact and convex, so it is a convex body. Moreover,
\[
a(\mathbf y)\ge1\quad(\mathbf y\in T),\qquad
a(\mathbf y)=0\quad(\mathbf y\notin T),\qquad
\int_Ta=W/2^n\ge|T|.
\tag{17}
\]

For both \(C_I\) and \(C_{-I}\) to be nonempty under (2), every coordinate of \(I\) must belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Write \(P=\{i:I_i=1\}\) and \(M=\{i:I_i=-1\}\). These are disjoint subsets, and reflection of the inner coordinates gives
\[
C_I=C_{-\mathbf u_M},\qquad
C_{-I}=C_{-\mathbf u_P}.
\]
Conversely each ordered disjoint pair \((P,M)\) specifies the unique such site \(I=\mathbf u_P-\mathbf u_M\). Thus the complete reflected-intersection census is
\[
R_C(\mathbf y)
=\bigl|\{(P,M)\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf y}^2:
                  P\cap M=\varnothing\}\bigr|.
\tag{18}
\]
This identity includes the empty subset, the zero site, every opposite-sign site, and all closed fiber boundaries.

We prove an elementary bound for any downward-closed family \(\mathcal E\) of subsets of \([n]\):
\[
\sum_{J\in\mathcal E}2^{-|J|}\ge\sqrt{|\mathcal E|}.
\tag{19}
\]
The empty family is allowed, with both sides zero. Induct on \(n\), with the assertion immediate for \(n=0\). Split the family into
\[
\mathcal E_0=\{J\subseteq[n-1]:J\in\mathcal E\},\qquad
\mathcal E_1=\{J\subseteq[n-1]:J\cup\{n\}\in\mathcal E\}.
\]
Both are downward closed and \(\mathcal E_1\subseteq\mathcal E_0\). If their cardinalities are \(a_0\ge a_1\), induction bounds the left side of (19) below by \(\sqrt{a_0}+\frac12\sqrt{a_1}\). This is at least \(\sqrt{a_0+a_1}\), because
\[
\left(\sqrt{a_0}+\tfrac12\sqrt{a_1}\right)^2
 -(a_0+a_1)
=\sqrt{a_0a_1}-\tfrac34a_1
\ge\tfrac14a_1\ge0.
\]
This proves (19).

For a fixed \(J\in\mathcal E\), the map \(K\mapsto K\setminus J\) sends \(\mathcal E\) to its members disjoint from \(J\), by downward closure, and each image has at most \(2^{|J|}\) preimages. There are therefore at least \(|\mathcal E|\,2^{-|J|}\) members disjoint from \(J\). Sum this bound over \(J\) and use (19). The number of ordered disjoint pairs in \(\mathcal E\) is at least \(|\mathcal E|^{3/2}\). Applying this to (18) yields the pointwise inequality
\[
R_C(\mathbf y)\ge a(\mathbf y)^{3/2}.
\tag{20}
\]
No log-concavity of either density has been asserted.

The function \(R_C\) is a finite sum of indicators of compact convex intersections, so it is a nonnegative integrable density supported on \(T\). Apply the second estimate in (1), then (20), (16), and (17). At a maximizing point \(\mathbf y^\star\in T\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star)
&\ge
\left(\sqrt[3]3-\sqrt[3]2\right)^{3/2}
\frac{\left(\int_T R_C^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}}{|T|^{1/2}}\\
&\ge
\left(\sqrt[3]3-\sqrt[3]2\right)^{3/2}
\frac{(W/2^n)^{3/2}}{|T|^{1/2}}\\
&\ge
\left(\sqrt[3]3-\sqrt[3]2\right)^{3/2}\frac{W}{2^n}
>\frac{3W}{40\,2^n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Here the last comparison is exact: \((36/25)^3<3\) and \((63/50)^3>2\), so \(\sqrt[3]3-\sqrt[3]2>9/50\). Furthermore \((9/50)^3>9/1600=(3/40)^2\). Taking positive square roots proves the coefficient comparison in (21).

We verify the actual-depth consumer directly. This step applies to any compact convex \(C\), whenever the displayed reflected score and the point \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\in C\) are defined; it does not require (2). At each continuous coordinate \(\mathbf z\), the finite set \(\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}=\{I\in\mathbb Z^n:\mathbf z\in C_I\cap C_{-I}\}\) is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf0\), has cardinality \(R_C(\mathbf z)\), and consists of actual integer sites of \(C\) at \(\mathbf z\).

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\) contains a halfspace of the form
\[
H_0=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf z):
       \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+
       \mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0\},
\qquad(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0).
\tag{22}
\]
If \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0\), at least half the points of \(\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}\) satisfy \(\mathbf a\cdot I\le0\), by pairing \(I\) with \(-I\); the fixed point \(I=\mathbf0\), when present, also satisfies the inequality. All these points are in \(H_0\). If \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), integration over the indicated continuous halfspace gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12
\int_{\{\mathbf z:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0\}}
R_C(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z
\ge\frac12\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star).
\tag{23}
\]
If \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), the same pointwise count holds at every \(\mathbf z\), and gives \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)\ge\frac12\int R_C\ge\frac12\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star)\). Boundary points are included throughout. A whole-space halfspace, if admitted, also satisfies the bound because \(R_C\le N_C\) pointwise. Taking the infimum over every containing halfspace proves
\[
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))
\ge\frac12\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star).
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (21) and (24) proves (4).

It remains to check the registered special family and its exact identities. In (5) all defining products are compact and convex; the nonempty products form a finite collection. Their convex hull is compact and convex and contains \(B_{\mathrm{in}}\times T\), which has nonempty interior. Thus \(C\) is a convex body. Each defining box is preserved by every coordinate reflection, so \(C\) has all required reflections. It satisfies (2), and all its continuous coordinates belong to \(T\).

Every inner integer site has the entire fiber \(T\), supplied by \(B_{\mathrm{in}}\times T\). Suppose an integer site has an outer coordinate at \(i\). Group a convex representation of a point in \(C\) according to the defining products and let \(\theta_i\) be the total weight assigned to \(B_i\times K_i\). Its \(i\)-th coordinate is between \(-\theta_i\) and \(1+\theta_i\), because all other defining products have that coordinate in \([0,1]\). Attaining \(-1\) or \(2\) forces \(\theta_i=1\). The entire point must then belong to \(B_i\times K_i\), since this product is convex. No other coordinate can be outer. Conversely this product supplies \(K_i\) at every integer site whose sole outer coordinate is \(i\). No integer site outside \(\{-1,0,1,2\}^n\) can occur. This proves every case of (6), also when \(K_i\) is empty or lower-dimensional.

There are \(2^n\) inner sites and, for each \(i\), exactly \(2\cdot2^{n-1}=2^n\) sites with sole outer coordinate \(i\). These sets of sites are disjoint, so (6) gives the first and third identities in (7).

For the reflected score, both \(I\) and \(-I\) must have coordinates in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). By (6), \(I\) has at most one negative coordinate and at most one positive coordinate. The zero site contributes \(\mathbf1_T\). The two sites \(I=\mathbf e_i\) and \(I=-\mathbf e_i\) each contribute \(\mathbf1_{K_i}\), where \(\mathbf e_i\) is the \(i\)-th integer-coordinate unit vector. For each ordered pair \(i\ne j\), the site \(I=\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_j\) contributes \(\mathbf1_{K_i\cap K_j}\). There are no other contributing sites. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_C
&=\mathbf1_T+2\sum_i\mathbf1_{K_i}
          +\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbf1_{K_i\cap K_j}\\
&=\mathbf1_T+m+m^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
This is an exact indicator identity, including all continuous boundaries and all overlaps among the \(K_i\).

For a direct use of the registered square-root estimate, on \(T\) the identity (25) gives \(4R_C-3(1+m)^2=(m-1)^2\ge0\). Consequently \(\int_T\sqrt{R_C}\ge(\sqrt3/2)\int_T(1+m)=(\sqrt3/2)W/2^n\). The first inequality of (1), together with \(W/2^n\ge|T|\), supplies a maximizing point with
\[
\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{3}{4(5+2\sqrt6)}
           \frac{(W/2^n)^2}{|T|}
\ge\frac{3}{4(5+2\sqrt6)}\frac{W}{2^n}
>\frac{3W}{40\,2^n},
\]
where \(5+2\sqrt6<10\). Applying (24) gives the required bound \(3W/(80\,2^n)\) for (5) directly as well. This completes every assertion. The bounded integer-coordinate support in (2) was essential for the Boolean description (15) and the exact disjoint-pair formula (18); no extension to longer integer supports is inferred.

The exact statement used is: for every \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) with volume centroid \(\mathbf g_K\), every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf g_K\) satisfies \(|K\cap H|\ge(d/(d+1))^d|K|\). The proof explicitly establishes the assertion at the centroid. In the argument above, every positive-volume remainder \(T_j\) is compact and convex with nonempty interior, and \(H_j\) passes through its volume centroid. Thus all hypotheses hold at every use. Only the weaker consequence \(|T_j\cap H_j|\ge|T_j|/3\) is needed. No other external result beyond the explicitly applied elementary integration and inequalities is used.
