## Statement

For $x,y>0$ with $U=x+y\le4/7$, set
$$
1+g_1=\frac{x}{(1-x)(x+y^2)},\qquad
1+g_2=\frac{U^2}{2x(x+y^2)},\qquad g_3=g_2.
$$
If $g_2\ge1/3$ then $g_1<23/96$, and if $g_1\ge1/3$ then $g_2<11/43$. In particular, when $\max_i g_i\ge1/3$ one has $\sum_i\min(g_i,1/3)<29/32$.

Consequently, let $K$ be a planar convex body with interior mark $0$ and actual cutting-ray endpoints $(A,0),(-a,0),(0,B),(0,-b),(r,-r),(-t,t)$ with $B=b=1/2$, $A=r=x>0$ and $a=t=y>0$. Let $q_i$ be its cap areas for $x_1\ge0$, $x_2\ge0$ and $x_1+x_2\le0$. If each $q_i\le(128/279)|K|$ and $q_{\min}+q_{\mathrm{mid}}+q_{\max}/4\le|K|$, then its normalized inverse vertical chord weight $(x+y)/(x+y+2)$ is strictly greater than $2/9$.

## Proof

For fixed $U<1$ and $0<x<U$, the reciprocal of $1+g_1$ has derivative $2y-U^2/x^2<0$. In fact $U^2/x^2=(1-y/U)^{-2}\ge1+2y/U>2y$. The denominator factor $x[x+(U-x)^2]$ of $1+g_2$ has derivative $2x(1-y)+y^2>0$. Thus $g_1$ increases in $x$ and $g_2$ decreases at fixed $U$. At fixed $x$ the derivative of $U^2/[x+(U-x)^2]$ has the sign of $x(1-y)$, so $g_2$ increases with $U$ while $0<y<1$.

For each $U\in(0,4/7]$, there is a unique $x_*(U)\in(0,U)$ at which $g_2=1/3$, by the decreasing function's limits $+\infty$ and $-1/2$. Hence $g_2\ge1/3$ implies $g_1\le g_1(x_*(U))$. At $x=U/2$, $g_2=(2-U)/(2+U)>1/3$, so $r=x_*(U)/U>1/2$. The boundary equation gives
$$
U=\frac{3-8r^2}{8r(1-r)^2},\qquad \frac12<r<\sqrt{3/8}.
$$
Its derivative is $(8r^3+8r^2-9r+3)/(8r^2(r-1)^3)<0$. The numerator equals $3/2$ at $r=1/2$ and has positive derivative there and afterward. Thus $r$ decreases with $U$, and $z=r(1-r)$ increases. At the boundary $1+g_1=64z^2/[3(5-16z)]$, increasing for $0<z\le1/4$ by differentiation. The largest boundary value of $g_1$ therefore occurs at $U=4/7$. At this $U$, substitution of $x=321/1000$ gives
$$
g_2=\frac{1964555111}{6035444889}<\frac13,\qquad
g_1=\frac{423205127}{1823794873}<\frac{23}{96}.
$$
The first inequality and monotonicity put $x_*(4/7)$ strictly below $321/1000$, so the second gives the desired strict bound.

If $g_1\ge1/3$ then $1+g_1\le1/(1-x)$ gives $x\ge1/4$. For $x\ge1/3$, the two monotonicities give $g_2(U,x)\le g_2(4/7,1/3)=11/43$. Equality forces $x=1/3$, $U=4/7$, where $g_1=97/344<1/3$. For $1/4\le x\le1/3$, the same premise gives $y^2\le x(4x-1)/[4(1-x)]$. Monotonicity in $y$ bounds $1+g_2$ by its value at equality, namely
$$
\frac23\left[2-x-\frac1{4x}+\sqrt{5-4x-\frac1x}\right].
$$
Both variable expressions increase on this interval, since the derivatives of $-x-1/(4x)$ and of the radicand are positive. Its value is thus at most its value at $x=1/3$, where the corresponding $U=1/3+\sqrt{1/24}$ is less than $4/7$ by $1/24<25/441$. The strict increase of $g_2$ with $U$ gives $g_2<11/43$ also here. In the first branch the clipped sum is strictly below $23/96+2/3=29/32$. In the second branch it is strictly below $1/3+22/43=109/129<29/32$.

For the geometric conclusion suppose the normalized weight is at most $2/9$. This is equivalent to $U\le4/7$, since the three coarea chord lengths for the stated canonical normals are $1,U,U$. In the actual two-bridge envelope the determinants for the three unique sectors specialize to $x(1-x),xy,xy$, all positive. Substitution of $B=b=1/2$, $A=r=x$, $a=t=y$ gives unique-sector upper bounds
$$
E_1=\frac{x^2}{2(1-x)},\qquad E_2=E_3=\frac{y^2}{4x}+\frac y2-\frac{y^2}2.
$$
The corresponding opposite double cores have areas $y^2/2,x/4,x/4$, of sum $C=(x+y^2)/2$. Subtract the respective core area from each upper bound and divide by $C$ to get precisely $g_1,g_2,g_3$ in the statement. The actual cap ceiling and sorted profile are the hypotheses of 1a5dac21f3a264d0 at $c=128/279$. They require $\max_i g_i\ge1/3$ and clipped sum at least $29/32$, contrary to the first part. This proves the strict chord-weight bound.
