## Statement

Let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be positive convex bodies at finitely many bases. For an actual cap $H=\{a\cdot z+u\cdot X\ge c\}$ with $u\ne0$, write $\tau_z=c-a\cdot z$, $q_{Hz}=|K_z\cap\{u\cdot X\ge\tau_z\}|$, and let $T_{Hz}$ be the mass of the allocated coarea of its section. This measure is $d\mathcal H^{d-1}/|u|$ for a proper cut, $(1-s_H)d\mathcal H^{d-1}/|u|$ at inside support and $s_Hd\mathcal H^{d-1}/|u|$ at outside support, where $s_H$ is the selected fraction of old boundary on the supporting face. It is zero for strict full and empty traces. Define
$$
G_{Hz}=[h_{K_z}(u)-\tau_z]T_{Hz},\qquad D_{Hz}=d q_{Hz}-G_{Hz}.
$$
Both terms are nonnegative in every status and $d\sum_zq_{Hz}=\sum_z(G_{Hz}+D_{Hz})$. For a pure-base cap the same identity uses $G_{Hz}=T_{Hz}=0$ and $D_{Hz}=dq_{Hz}$.

For an equal-mixed-mass probability law $\theta$ of such caps, write $Q=\sum_zq_{Hz}$, fix $Y=(p,y)$, and set $T_H=\sum_zT_{Hz}$, $b_H=\sum_zT_{Hz}(z-p)$, and $\delta_H=c-a\cdot p-u\cdot y$. For pure-base caps use $u=0$ in the weighted terms. Whenever the displayed terms are integrable,
$$
dQ=\int\left[\sum_z h_{K_z}(u)T_{Hz}+a\cdot b_H-\delta_HT_H-y\cdot T_Hu+\sum_zD_{Hz}\right]d\theta(H).
$$
In particular, if $b_H=0$ and $\delta_HT_H=0$ almost everywhere and $\int T_Hu\,d\theta=0$, then $dQ=\int\sum_z[h_{K_z}(u)T_{Hz}+D_{Hz}]\,d\theta$. A sufficient replacement for the first two conditions is that the full allocated section has mean $Y$ for almost every cap with $T_H>0$.

## Proof

For a proper cut, a farthest captured point and the actual section form a pyramid contained in the cap. Its Euclidean height is $(h_{K_z}(u)-\tau_z)/|u|$ and its base area is $|u|T_{Hz}$, so its volume is $G_{Hz}/d$. This also applies to a full supporting facet on the inside of the cap, where the allocated mass is at most the full facet area divided by $|u|$. At an outside support the height and captured volume are zero. Contacts of dimension less than $d-1$ have zero allocated coarea, and strict full and empty traces are immediate. These arguments use endpoint counting for $d=1$. For a pure-base cap the captured volumes are whole-fiber volumes or zero by definition.

For each cap, $h_{K_z}(u)-\tau_z=h_{K_z}(u)+a\cdot(z-p)-\delta_H-u\cdot y$. Multiply by $T_{Hz}$, sum in $z$, and integrate the volume identity. The first two stated balance conditions kill the $a\cdot b_H$ and $\delta_HT_H$ terms capwise. The last kills the term $y\cdot T_Hu$ after integration. In the alternative condition, the base component of the full mean gives $b_H=0$. Averaging the actual plane equation over that same section gives $a\cdot p+u\cdot y=c$. A cap with $T_H=0$ makes no contribution to these terms. A common translation of all continuous bodies changes the remaining support integral by the translation dotted with $\int T_Hu\,d\theta=0$.
