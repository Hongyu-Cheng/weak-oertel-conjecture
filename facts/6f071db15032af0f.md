## Statement

Let $C=\{(z,x):F(z,x)\le0\}$ be a convex body in $\mathbb R^{n+d}$ for a smooth coercive $F$ with $\nabla^2F\ge cI$ for some $c>0$ and $\nabla F\ne0$ on $\partial C$. On any finite collection of its positive-volume integer fibers $K_z$, prescribe arbitrary smooth outward normal speeds $\psi_z$ on $\partial K_z$. There is a smooth family of actual convex bodies $C_{\mathbf t}$ for a neighborhood of $\mathbf 0$ in the full signed parameter space, where $t_z$ produces speed $\psi_z$ on its own fiber and every other integer fiber is unchanged along that coordinate.

## Proof

On a positive-volume fiber, the strongly convex function $x\mapsto F(z,x)$ has a negative minimum. Its boundary level is regular, since a stationary point at level zero would be its unique minimum. A tubular-neighborhood extension and smooth cutoff give a smooth compactly supported $\chi_z(z',x)$ equal to $\lVert\nabla_x F(z,x)\rVert\psi_z(x)$ on $\{z\}\times\partial K_z$, supported in the base within distance $1/3$ of $z$. When $n=0$, use only the tubular extension and cutoff.

Take $C_{\mathbf t}=\{F-\sum_z t_z\chi_z\le0\}$. All Hessians of the finitely many perturbations are bounded, so the defining function still has Hessian at least $(c/2)I$ for small signed $\mathbf t$. Compact support preserves coercivity, and a fixed negative point persists, so the sublevel set is a convex body with regular boundary. Each bump vanishes at every other integer base. In the selected fiber, the implicit function theorem on old normal lines gives speed $\chi_z(z,x)/\lVert\nabla_x F(z,x)\rVert=\psi_z(x)$. The other coordinate bumps vanish there. The original positive fibers stay positive by their negative interior values, and the other fibers stay fixed even if they were empty or null.
