## Statement

Let \(n\to\infty\), let integers \(3\le k=k(n)\le n\) satisfy \(k(n)\to\infty\), and let \(\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>0\) be arbitrary. Index the continuous coordinates by pairs \((I,\sigma)\), where \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(|I|=k\), and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^I\). Thus \(d=2^k\binom nk\). Define the full signed activation body by
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{I,\sigma}(\mathbf x)
&=\epsilon\left(\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_i x_i-k+1\right)_+,\\
C
&=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf x\in[-1,1]^n,\quad
y_{I,\sigma}\ge f_{I,\sigma}(\mathbf x)\text{ for every }(I,\sigma),\quad
\sum_{I,\sigma}y_{I,\sigma}\le d\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(u_+=\max(u,0)\) and \(\Delta_d=\{\mathbf y\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i y_i\le1\}\).

Use the canonical fiber notation of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`: \(C_z=\{\mathbf y:(z,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(a_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_z a_z\), \(Z=\{z:a_z>0\}\), and
\[
\mathcal K_z
=\left\{\frac2{a_z}\int_{C_z}\mathbf y g(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y:
0\le g\le1,\quad \int_{C_z}g=\frac{a_z}2\right\},
\qquad D_z=\mathcal K_z-\mathbf c_z\quad(z\in Z),
\]
where \(\mathbf c_z\) is the actual centroid of \(C_z\). Thus \(\mathbf c_z\) is the vector called \(c_z\) in that accepted fact and the assignment, and the assignment's \(K_z\) means \(\mathcal K_z\). The alternative notations \(w_z\) and \(D_+\) mean \(a_z\) and \(Z\), respectively. Write \(a_0=a_{\mathbf0}\) and \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf0}\) for the zero-base fiber mass and centroid; the latter is not the conjecture's scalar \(c_0\). The gauge is \(\|v\|_{D_z}=\inf\{t\ge0:v\in tD_z\}\).

For \(0\le m\le n\), put \(B_m=\binom mk\), with \(B_m=0\) when \(m<k\), \(N_m=2^m\binom nm\), and \(A_m=1-\epsilon B_m/d\). The exact original mass ledger is
\[
a_0=\frac{d^d}{d!},\qquad
W=a_0\sum_{m=0}^nN_m(A_m)_+^d,\qquad
W_{\mathrm{inactive}}=a_0\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}N_m.
\]
For a root \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\), let \(m(z)=|\{i:z_i\ne0\}|\). Writing \(m=m(z)\), its fiber is \(f(z)+dA_m\Delta_d\) if \(A_m>0\), the singleton \(\{f(z)\}\) if \(A_m=0\), and empty if \(A_m<0\). Fibers at every other integer base are empty. Every singleton fiber has actual ambient depth zero.

Fix \(\eta>0\) and \(0<\delta\le1\), and define
\(\mathcal G_\eta=\{z\in Z:\|\mathbf c_z+\mathbf c_{-z}-2\mathbf c_{\mathbf0}\|_{D_z}\ge\eta\}\).
Assume \(2^na_0/W\to0\) and \(\sum_{z\in\mathcal G_\eta}a_z\ge\delta W\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). For \(\delta>1\) the mass hypothesis is impossible, so the implication for arbitrary fixed \(\eta,\delta>0\) reduces to the stated range. Then \(k>n/16\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), and
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{W_{\mathrm{inactive}}}{W}\ge\frac\delta{32}.
\]
More explicitly, for sufficiently large \(n\) the following bound holds, independently of \(\epsilon\):
\[
\delta W\le\bigl(32+E_k(\eta)\bigr)W_{\mathrm{inactive}}
+a_0\,3^n2^{-d},
\qquad
E_k(\eta)=
\frac{1024e^{-\eta\sqrt{k+1}/48}}
{1-32e^{-\eta\sqrt{k+1}(\sqrt{17/16}-1)/48}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The denominator in this definition is positive for sufficiently large \(k\).

For every \(n,k,\epsilon\) above, not just those passing the two screens, the point \(\mathbf p_0=(\mathbf0,\mathbf c_{\mathbf0})\) belongs to \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies the actual ambient bound \(h_S(\mathbf p_0)\ge W_{\mathrm{inactive}}/72\). In particular, under the two screens,
\(\liminf h_S(\mathbf p_0)/W\ge\delta/2304\).

## Proof

First establish the entire fiber table. Every component of \(f\) is nonnegative, continuous, and convex. Thus the defining inequalities of \(C\) are convex inequalities. Also \(0\le y_{I,\sigma}\le d\), so \(C\) is compact. At \((\mathbf0,\mathbf1_d/2)\), all inequalities are strict: every activation expression before taking its positive part equals \(1-k<0\), and the continuous-coordinate sum is \(d/2<d\). Continuity gives a neighborhood in \(C\), so \(C\) is a convex body.

The only integer bases in the box are roots \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Let \(m\) be the number of nonzero coordinates of such a root. Each integer \(\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_i z_i\) is at most \(k\). It equals \(k\) exactly when \(I\) is contained in the nonzero support of \(z\) and \(\sigma_i=z_i\) on \(I\); all other values are at most \(k-1\). Consequently \(f(z)\) has exactly \(B_m\) coordinates equal to \(\epsilon\), all others zero, and \(\sum_i f_i(z)=\epsilon B_m\). Subtracting \(f(z)\) from the fiber inequalities gives precisely the three cases in the statement. There are exactly \(N_m\) roots of occupancy \(m\).

The elementary simplex volume formula is \(\operatorname{vol}_d(r\Delta_d)=r^d/d!\) for \(r>0\). It gives the stated ledger and \(a_0=d^d/d!\), since \(B_0=0\). In particular \(W\ge a_0>0\), and every root of occupancy less than \(k\) has the identical original fiber \(d\Delta_d\). For a singleton fiber, \(\sum_i f_i(z)=d\). The ambient closed halfspace \(\sum_i y_i\ge d\) contains that singleton and meets every positive fiber only on the hyperplane \(\sum_i y_i=d\), a set of zero \(d\)-volume. Its mixed-integer volume is therefore zero. This proves the asserted actual depth for all zero-volume fibers; empty fibers contribute nothing.

We next prove dimension-uniform moment and metronoid estimates, rather than assume the proposed curvature approximation. Let \(Y\) be uniform on \(r\Delta_d\), with \(r>0\), and let \(\lambda_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^dY_i/r\), \(\lambda_i=Y_i/r\) for \(1\le i\le d\). Put \(N=d+1\). For nonnegative integer exponents \(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_d\), of total \(q\), direct integration gives
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i=0}^d\lambda_i^{\alpha_i}
=\frac{d!\prod_{i=0}^d\alpha_i!}{(d+q)!}.
\tag{1}
\]
For completeness, the unnormalized integral on \(\Delta_d\) equals \(\prod_i\alpha_i!/(d+q)!\). This follows inductively by putting the last coordinate equal to \((1-\sum_{i<d}x_i)t\): the \(t\)-integral is \(\alpha_d!\alpha_0!/(\alpha_d+\alpha_0+1)!\), and the remaining integral has slack exponent \(\alpha_d+\alpha_0+1\). The one-dimensional beta integral used here follows by repeated integration by parts. Multiplication by the uniform density \(d!\) proves (1).

It follows that \(\mathbb E Y=r\mathbf1_d/N\). For a centered linear functional \(T=u\cdot(Y-\mathbb E Y)\), write \(T=\sum_{i=0}^d b_i\lambda_i\) with \(\sum_i b_i=0\): start with coefficients \(0,ru_1,\ldots,ru_d\) and subtract their common mean. Expanding the second and fourth powers using (1) and \(\sum_i b_i=0\) gives
\[
\mathbb E T^2=\frac{\sum_i b_i^2}{N(N+1)},\qquad
\mathbb E T^4=
\frac{3(\sum_i b_i^2)^2+6\sum_i b_i^4}
{N(N+1)(N+2)(N+3)}
\le9(\mathbb E T^2)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
One way to check the fourth-power simplification explicitly is to expand \(\prod_i(1-b_it)^{-1}\). Its coefficient of \(t^4\), when \(\sum_i b_i=0\), is \((\sum_i b_i^2)^2/8+\sum_i b_i^4/4\). The multinomial formula and (1) multiply this coefficient by \(4!/[N(N+1)(N+2)(N+3)]\), yielding (2). The last inequality uses \(\sum_i b_i^4\le(\sum_i b_i^2)^2\) and \(N(N+1)\le(N+2)(N+3)\).

If \(\sigma_T=(\mathbb E T^2)^{1/2}\), Hölder's inequality applied to \(|T|^{2/3}|T|^{4/3}\) yields
\(\mathbb E T^2\le(\mathbb E|T|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E T^4)^{1/3}\).
Therefore (2) gives \(\mathbb E|T|\ge\sigma_T/3\), with the same assertion trivial when \(\sigma_T=0\).

Consider the centered half-mass metronoid of this simplex, in the normalization of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`. Its support in direction \(u\) is
\(\sup\{2\mathbb E[Tg]:0\le g\le1,\ \mathbb E g=1/2\}\).
For every admissible \(g\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(2\mathbb E[Tg]=2\mathbb E[T(g-1/2)]\le\sigma_T\), because \(\mathbb E(g-1/2)^2\le1/4\). Conversely,
\(g=1/2+(\operatorname{sgn}T-\mathbb E\operatorname{sgn}T)/4\)
is admissible and gives \(2\mathbb E[Tg]=\mathbb E|T|/2\ge\sigma_T/6\). Thus, writing \(D\) for this centered metronoid,
\[
\frac{\sigma_T}{6}\le h_D(u)\le\sigma_T.
\tag{3}
\]
Translation of the original simplex does not change this estimate for its centered metronoid.

The covariance matrix and its inverse, obtained from (1), are
\[
\Sigma=\frac{r^2}{N^2(N+1)}(NI_d-\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_d^{\mathsf T}),
\qquad
\Sigma^{-1}=\frac{N(N+1)}{r^2}(I_d+\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_d^{\mathsf T}).
\tag{4}
\]
The centered covariance ellipsoid \(\{v:v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}v\le1\}\) has support \(\sigma_T\). The compact convex centered metronoid framework of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, together with (3), therefore gives that this ellipsoid divided by six is contained in \(D\), and \(D\) is contained in the ellipsoid. These containments can also be read directly from supporting halfspaces: a violation would give a separating linear functional contradicting (3). In particular \(D\) has interior and its gauge satisfies
\[
\sqrt{v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}v}
\le\|v\|_D
\le6\sqrt{v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}v}.
\tag{5}
\]

Apply this calculation to a positive root of occupancy \(m\). In this paragraph let \(B=B_m\) and \(A=A_m>0\). Let \(p_z\) be the zero-one vector indicating the \(B\) activated coordinates at \(z\), so \(f(z)=\epsilon p_z\). The activated coordinates at \(z\) and \(-z\) are disjoint: on the same \(k\)-set they require opposite sign patterns. The simplex centroid formula already obtained from (1) gives
\[
\mathbf c_z=\epsilon p_z+\frac{dA}{d+1}\mathbf1_d,
\qquad
\mathbf c_{\mathbf0}=\frac d{d+1}\mathbf1_d,
\qquad
v_z:=\mathbf c_z+\mathbf c_{-z}-2\mathbf c_{\mathbf0}
=\epsilon\left(p_z+p_{-z}-\frac{2B}{d+1}\mathbf1_d\right).
\tag{6}
\]
The sum \(p_z+p_{-z}\) has exactly \(2B\) entries equal to one. Consequently
\(\|v_z\|_2^2+(\sum_i(v_z)_i)^2=\epsilon^2(2B-4B^2/(d+1))\).
Using (4)--(5) with \(r=dA\) proves the following two-sided actual gauge bound:
\[
\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{(d+1)(d+2)}}{dA_m}
\sqrt{2B_m\left(1-\frac{2B_m}{d+1}\right)}
\le\|v_z\|_{D_z}
\le\frac{6\epsilon\sqrt{(d+1)(d+2)}}{dA_m}
\sqrt{2B_m\left(1-\frac{2B_m}{d+1}\right)}.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(m<k\), (6) instead gives \(v_z=0\) and gauge zero. For \(m\ge k\), \(B_m\le\binom nk=d/2^k\), so \(1-2B_m/(d+1)\ge3/4\). Also \(\sqrt{(d+1)(d+2)}/d\le\sqrt6\) for \(d\ge1\). Hence (7) implies the simpler bounds
\[
\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{B_m}}{A_m}
\le\|v_z\|_{D_z}
\le24\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{B_m}}{A_m}
\qquad(m\ge k,\ A_m>0).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular a curved root with \(A_m\ge1/2\) must satisfy
\(\epsilon\sqrt{B_m}\ge\eta/48\).
Its mass therefore obeys
\[
\frac{a_z}{a_0}=A_m^d
\le e^{-\epsilon B_m}
\le e^{-\eta\sqrt{B_m}/48}.
\tag{9}
\]
The first inequality is \((1-t)^d\le e^{-dt}\), with \(t=\epsilon B_m/d\in[0,1)\). No lower bound on the slack is used outside this step.

We separately account for all positive fibers with \(0<A_m<1/2\). Their total mass is at most \(a_0\,3^n2^{-d}\), since there are at most \(3^n\) roots. Moreover,
\(d/n=2^k\binom nk/n\ge2^k/k\to\infty\): the binomial inequality follows from \(k\binom nk=n\binom{n-1}{k-1}\ge n\). Thus
\[
3^n2^{-d}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\sum_{z\in Z:\,0<A_{m(z)}<1/2}a_z}{W}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{10}
\]
where the second conclusion uses \(W\ge a_0\). Zero and negative slack were already included correctly in the ledger and carry no mass. In particular large curvature near empty fibers cannot invalidate the estimates below.

The dispersion screen rules out \(k\le n/16\) eventually. Suppose, to the contrary, this inequality holds on an infinite subsequence. The dispersion condition implies \(W/a_0\ge2^n\) for sufficiently large indices. For the roots with occupancy \(m<n/8\), a generating-function estimate gives
\[
\sum_{m<n/8}N_m
\le4^{n/8}\sum_{m=0}^nN_m4^{-m}
=\left(\frac32\,2^{1/4}\right)^n.
\tag{11}
\]
The base in parentheses is strictly less than two, since its fourth power is \(81/8<16\). Every fiber has mass at most \(a_0\), so (11) shows that the mass on these low-occupancy roots, divided by \(W\), tends to zero.

For \(m\ge n/8\) and \(k\le n/16\), one has \(m\ge2k\) and
\(B_m=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(m-i)/(k-i)\ge(m/k)^k\).
Uniformly over these occupancies, \(\sqrt{B_m}/n\to\infty\) along the subsequence. Here is an explicit verification covering slow as well as fast degree growth. If \(k\le\sqrt n\), then \(\sqrt{B_m}\ge(\sqrt n/8)^{k/2}\); for sufficiently large \(n\), \(\sqrt n/8\ge n^{1/3}\), so \(\sqrt{B_m}/n\ge n^{k/6-1}\to\infty\) because \(k\to\infty\). If \(k>\sqrt n\), then \(\sqrt{B_m}\ge2^{k/2}>2^{\sqrt n/2}\), giving the same conclusion. By (9), the total mass of curved roots with \(m\ge n/8\) and \(A_m\ge1/2\) is consequently at most
\(a_0\,3^n e^{-(\eta/48)\sqrt{\binom{\lceil n/8\rceil}{k}}}=o(a_0)\).
After dividing by \(W\ge a_0\), this tends to zero. Together with (10)--(11), this proves that all curved roots have mass \(o(W)\) on the subsequence, contradicting their mass being at least \(\delta W\). Therefore \(k>n/16\) eventually.

It remains to compare the curved layers with the unchanged layer of occupancy \(k-1\). For the remainder take \(k>n/16\). For every \(m\ge k\),
\(N_m/N_{m-1}=2(n-m+1)/m\le2n/k<32\).
Thus \(N_{k+j}\le32^{j+1}N_{k-1}\) for \(0\le j\le n-k\). The entire layer \(m=k\), curved or not, has mass at most \(32a_0N_{k-1}\).

For \(j\ge1\), \(B_{k+j}=\binom{k+j}{j}\), \(B_{k+1}=k+1\), and the successive ratios satisfy
\(B_{k+j}/B_{k+j-1}=1+k/j\ge1+k/n>17/16\).
It follows that \(B_{k+j}\ge(k+1)(17/16)^{j-1}\). Using (9), the total mass of curved roots with \(A_m\ge1/2\) in all layers \(m=k+j\), \(j\ge1\), is at most
\[
a_0N_{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}
32^{j+1}
e^{-\eta\sqrt{k+1}(17/16)^{(j-1)/2}/48}
\le a_0N_{k-1}E_k(\eta).
\tag{12}
\]
To verify the last bound, use
\((17/16)^{(j-1)/2}\ge1+(j-1)(\sqrt{17/16}-1)\)
and sum the resulting geometric series through infinity. Its ratio is
\(32e^{-\eta\sqrt{k+1}(\sqrt{17/16}-1)/48}\), which is less than one for sufficiently large \(k\); its initial term is \(1024e^{-\eta\sqrt{k+1}/48}\). This is exactly the displayed \(E_k(\eta)\), and it tends to zero for every fixed \(\eta>0\). If \(k=n\), the sum in (12) is empty, so the same bound holds.

All roots of occupancy below \(k\) have gauge zero by (6). Adding the \(m=k\) bound, (12), and the nearly depleted contribution yields
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathcal G_\eta}a_z
\le a_0N_{k-1}\bigl(32+E_k(\eta)\bigr)+a_0\,3^n2^{-d}
\le W_{\mathrm{inactive}}\bigl(32+E_k(\eta)\bigr)+a_0\,3^n2^{-d}.
\]
This is the asserted finite original-stock ledger. Divide by \(W\), use the curvature mass screen, \(a_0/W\le1\), (10), and \(E_k(\eta)\to0\). The resulting lower bound on \(W_{\mathrm{inactive}}/W\) has lower limit at least \(\delta/32\). The estimates cover every positive parameter sequence, including \(\epsilon\to0\), \(\epsilon\to\infty\), and slack tending to zero.

Finally prove the actual-depth charge from the original fibers directly. The moment estimate (2) also gives a uniform centroid cap bound for a simplex. Indeed, for a nonzero centered linear functional \(T\), \(\mathbb ET=0\) implies \(\mathbb ET_+=\mathbb E|T|/2\ge\sigma_T/6\). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives
\[
\mathbb P(T\ge0)
\ge\frac{(\mathbb ET_+)^2}{\mathbb ET_+^2}
\ge\frac1{36}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(T\) is identically zero, the probability is one instead. Every closed halfspace containing the simplex centroid contains a halfspace through that centroid, so (13) proves that such a halfspace captures at least \(1/36\) of the simplex volume. This proof uses the actual uniform simplex distribution, not a centroid-graph or curvature certificate.

The set \(\{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n:m(z)<k\}\) is invariant under \(z\mapsto-z\), and every fiber there equals \(d\Delta_d\), with centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf0}\). The point \(\mathbf p_0\) is in the zero-base fiber. Take a proper ambient halfspace through \(\mathbf p_0\), written as
\(\alpha\cdot\mathbf x+\beta\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf c_{\mathbf0})\ge0\).
At least half the inactive roots satisfy \(\alpha\cdot z\ge0\), by pairing \(z\) with \(-z\), including the fixed root zero. For every such root, its halfspace section contains the common centroid. If \(\beta\ne\mathbf0\), (13) contributes at least \(a_0/36\) from that fiber. If \(\beta=\mathbf0\), the section is the whole fiber and contributes \(a_0\). Summing only these original fibers gives mixed-integer volume at least \(W_{\mathrm{inactive}}/72\). Any halfspace containing \(\mathbf p_0\) contains its parallel sub-halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf p_0\), and the whole-space case is immediate. Thus the same lower bound holds for every containing halfspace, proving \(h_S(\mathbf p_0)\ge W_{\mathrm{inactive}}/72\).

Combining this with the inactive-stock bound proves \(\liminf h_S(\mathbf p_0)/W\ge\delta/2304\). In particular \(2^nh_S(\mathbf p_0)/W\to\infty\), so the screened sequence cannot make all actual point depths negligible relative to \(2^{-n}W\). No statement that the locked conjecture is proved for arbitrary mixed convex bodies is used or asserted.
