## Statement

Let $m\ge1$ be an integer and let $r$ be a continuous nonnegative concave function on $[a,b]$ with $a<0<b$ and $r(0)>0$. Write $R=\max r$ and $\{z:r(z)\ge v\}=[-B(v),A(v)]$ for $v\in[0,R]$. Both arms are continuous, concave and nonincreasing, with $A(0),B(0)>0$ and $A+B\ge0$. Equip $\{(x,z):a\le z\le b,\ |x|\le r(z)\}$ with the planar density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{m-1}/2$. Define $V$ as its total mass, $P(s)$ as the mass of $z+sx\ge0$, and $C(s),M(s)$ as the line integrals of $f,zf$ on $z+sx=0$ with line measure divided by $\sqrt{1+s^2}$. Then
$$
V=m\int_0^R v^{m-1}(A+B)\,dv .
$$
For $s>0$ define the even function
$$
j_s(t,v)=
\begin{cases}
m v^{m-1}|t|/2,& |t|\ge sv,\\
s(m+1)v^m/4+(m-1)v^{m-2}t^2/(4s),& |t|\le sv.
\end{cases}
$$
For $v>0$ off the branch boundaries put $c_s=\partial_t j_s$ and $k_s=-s\partial_s j_s$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(s)&=V/2+\int_0^R[j_s(A(v),v)-j_s(B(v),v)]\,dv,\\
C(s)&=\int_0^R[c_s(A(v),v)+c_s(B(v),v)]\,dv,\\
M(s)&=\int_0^R[k_s(A(v),v)-k_s(B(v),v)]\,dv.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $s=0$ one has $P(0)=V/2+(m/2)\int_0^R v^{m-1}(|A|-|B|)\,dv$, $C(0)=r(0)^m$, and $M(0)=0$. Terms with coefficient $m-1$ are omitted for $m=1$.

## Proof

The set $\{(z,v):a\le z\le b,\ 0\le v\le r(z)\}$ is compact and convex. Its horizontal intervals are nested, so each upper endpoint $A$ and reflected lower endpoint $B$ is concave and nonincreasing. Concavity gives continuity on $(0,R)$. At the top, compactness identifies the left limits with the endpoints at $R$. At zero, every $z\in(a,b)$ has $r(z)>0$ by concavity and $r(0)>0$, so the right limits are the original endpoints. This also gives $A(0),B(0)>0$ and nonnegative interval width.

For $v>0$ the layer representation has axial interval $[-B(v),A(v)]$ and transverse measure
$$
\frac{v^{m-1}}2(\delta_{-v}+\delta_v)
+\frac{(m-1)v^{m-2}}2\mathbf1_{[-v,v]}(x)\,dx .
$$
Indeed, integrating over levels, the two point masses contribute the density $|x|^{m-1}/2$ on $|x|\le r(z)$, and the second term adds $\int_{|x|}^{r(z)}(m-1)v^{m-2}/2\,dv$. For $m=1$ use only the first term. The layer mass per unit axial length is $m v^{m-1}$, proving the volume formula.

At each level, subtract half the transverse layer mass from the part included by $z+sx\ge0$. As a function of axial coordinate $z$, the difference is $m v^{m-1}\operatorname{sgn}(z)/2$ when $|z|>sv$ and $(m-1)v^{m-2}z/(2s)$ when $|z|<sv$. The continuous even primitive given in the statement follows by matching the values at $\pm sv$. Integrating from $-B(v)$ to $A(v)$, then integrating over $v$, proves the cap formula.

Each equation $A(v)=sv$ has at most one root because $A-sv$ strictly decreases from a positive value. For $A(v)=-sv$, the concave function $g(v)=A(v)+sv$ starts positive. If $g(t)=0$ at $t>0$, then for $v>t$ its chord slope bound gives $g(v)\le-(v-t)g(0)/t<0$. This gives at most one root for that sign as well, and the same argument applies to $B$. Hence the kink levels have zero measure. The threshold derivatives of $j_s$ are bounded by $m v^{m-1}/2$, and its slope derivative is bounded by a constant times $v^m$ since $|t|\le sv$ on the inner branch and the derivative is zero on the outer branch. The same bounds control the difference quotients by piecewise differentiability and continuity. Shifting the cap threshold to $\varepsilon$ changes the layer endpoints to $A-\varepsilon$ and $-B-\varepsilon$ in axial coordinates translated by $\varepsilon$. Thus the negative threshold derivative of the cap formula is $\int(c_s(A,v)+c_s(B,v))\,dv$.

For identification with the actual coarea, integrate first in $z$ with lower cutoff $z=\varepsilon-sx$. The line $z=-sx$ meets the interior at the source and its intersection with the body's boundary has at most two points. The density at the cutoff is continuous in $z$ for almost every $x$. It is bounded and $|x|\le R$ on the support, so dominated convergence gives the negative threshold derivative $\int f(x,-sx)\,dx=C(s)$ and the slope derivative $P'(s)=\int x f(x,-sx)\,dx$. The factor converting line measure to $dx$ is exactly $\sqrt{1+s^2}$. It follows that $M(s)=-sP'(s)$, giving the stated $k_s$ formula. On the inner branch $k_s=-s(m+1)v^m/4+(m-1)v^{m-2}t^2/(4s)$, and on the outer branch it is zero.

At zero slope the mass in each layer uses its positive axial interval length $(A+B+|A|-|B|)/2$. Direct integration on $z=0$ gives $C(0)=r(0)^m$ and $M(0)=0$. The same root argument applied to each arm itself shows that its first zero, if reached before the top, locates $r(0)$; if both arms stay nonnegative up to $R$, the source height is $R$. More explicitly, with a finite positive slope menu, partitioning each arm at the roots $A=\pm sv$ and at $A=0$ (and likewise for $B$) fixes the kernel branches on the open cells. For $m\ge2$, the arm-dependent integrals on each cell are $\int v^{m-2}A$, $\int v^{m-1}A$, and $\int v^{m-2}A^2$ for arm $A$, and the corresponding three for $B$. For $m=1$ only $\int A$ and $\int B$ remain, along with integrals of fixed powers of $v$.
