## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(d\geq n\), and \(R>0\). For \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), put \(a(x)=R-\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|\) and \(c(x)=(|x_1|,\ldots,|x_n|,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^d\). Define

\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
 \sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|\leq R,\quad
 |y_l-c_l(x)|\leq a(x)\quad(1\leq l\leq d)\right\},
\qquad
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]

Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Write \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(a_z=a(z)\), and \(c_z=c(z)\). Its complete positive integer support and actual volume weights are

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_+&=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_{i=1}^n|z_i|<R\right\}
     =\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
C_z&=c_z+[-a_z,a_z]^d
     =c_z+T_z[-1/2,1/2]^d\quad(z\in D_+),\\
T_z&=2a_z I_d,\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=(2a_z)^d,\\
W&=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z>0,\qquad
p_z=w_z/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q\in S\), let \(h_S(q)\) be the infimum of \(\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\) over closed ambient halfspaces \(H\) containing \(q\), where \(H_z=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\). This is exactly the locked actual mixed-volume depth. Let \(Z\) have the original volume law \(p\), and write \([t]_+=\max\{t,0\}\).

Here \(c_z\) is the actual fiber centroid. The symbols \(w_z,W,D_+\) have exactly the meanings in accepted fact 0bcc51d8cd216cf3. The radius \(a_z\) in this statement is not the volume denoted \(a_z\) in the centroid specialization of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78; that predecessor's volume \(a_z\) equals our \(w_z\). Our shape matrix \(T_z\) and the normalization \(C_z=c_z+T_z[-1/2,1/2]^d\) agree with the matrix convention in accepted fact 4e94470dc826efb6. The original root weights are exactly \(w_z^{1/d}=2a_z\), and \(p\) is not a normalized root law.

Define the fitted set for the zero affine map by

\[
E_0=\left\{z\in D_+:\|T_z^{-1}(0-c_z)\|_2\leq\frac1{\sqrt{48}}\right\}
   =\left\{z\in D_+:\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n z_i^2}{4a_z^2}\leq\frac1{48}\right\}.
\]

For every \(d\geq3\), the following exact moment and original-weight fit mass bounds hold:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\left[\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i^2}{a_Z^2}\right]
&\leq\frac{2n}{d(d-1)},\\
\sum_{z\in E_0}w_z
&\geq\left[1-\frac{24n}{d(d-1)}\right]_+ W.
\end{aligned}
\]

For all \(n,d,R\) in the statement, there exists \(y_0\in S\), fixed before the containing halfspace, with \(h_S(y_0)\geq(3/32)2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E_0}w_z\). In fact the symmetry of this family gives the stronger fixed-point bound \(h_S((0_n,0_d))\geq(3/64)\sum_{z\in E_0}w_z\). Consequently,

\[
h_S((0_n,0_d))
\geq\frac3{64}\left[1-\frac{24n}{d(d-1)}\right]_+W
\qquad(d\geq3).
\]

In the intrinsic residual-rank notation of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78, \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_+)=r_{\mathrm{aff}}(D_+)=n\) when \(R>1\); both quantities are zero when \(0<R\leq1\).

Finally, with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/(5088\log2)\in(0,1/e)\), for every \(n,d,R\) as above there exists \(y^\star\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni y^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq c_0 2^{-n}W\). When \(n\geq49\), one may choose \(y^\star=(0_n,0_d)\), and then \(h_S(y^\star)\geq3W/128\). When \(1\leq n\leq48\), the actual-body bound in accepted fact 3bcbf21f33edf564 supplies the required point.

## Proof

For \(1\leq l\leq n\), the fiber inequalities are equivalently

\[
-R+2|x_l|+\sum_{i\ne l}|x_i|
\leq y_l
\leq R-\sum_{i\ne l}|x_i|.
\]

The lower function is convex and the upper function is concave. For \(n<l\leq d\), the lower and upper functions are respectively \(-R+\sum_i|x_i|\) and \(R-\sum_i|x_i|\), again convex and concave. Their epigraph and hypograph constraints, together with \(\sum_i|x_i|\leq R\), are convex. These functions are continuous, so \(C\) is closed. Its base coordinates satisfy \(|x_i|\leq R\), and all continuous coordinates satisfy \(-R\leq y_l\leq R\). Thus \(C\) is bounded and compact. At \((0_n,0_d)\), the base constraint and every upper and lower fiber constraint are strict. Continuity gives an ambient open neighborhood contained in \(C\), proving full dimension.

Taking fibers gives the displayed cubes. They have positive volume exactly at the integer points with \(\sum_i|z_i|<R\). Integer fibers on the base boundary are singletons and have zero \(d\)-volume; outside the base they are empty. Hence \(D_+,w_z,W\) count every positive integer fiber with its original volume. In particular, \(w_{0_n}=(2R)^d>0\). The centroid and shape formulas follow from the symmetry of a cube about its center. The set \(D_+\) is finite. Its convex hull lies in \(\{x:\sum_i|x_i|<R\}\), since every convex combination of its points has \(\ell_1\)-norm at most the maximum of their finitely many norms, which is strictly less than \(R\). This proves its asserted completeness.

For integers \(s\geq0\), let \(A_n(s)\) count the integer vectors with \(\sum_i|z_i|=s\), and let \(B_n(s)\) be the sum of \(\sum_i z_i^2\) over those same vectors. All generating-series identities below are formal. A single signed integer coordinate has counting series \(1+2\sum_{k\geq1}t^k=(1+t)/(1-t)\) and second-moment series \(2\sum_{k\geq1}k^2t^k=2t(1+t)/(1-t)^3\). The latter follows by applying \(t\,d/dt\) twice to \(\sum_{k\geq0}t^k=(1-t)^{-1}\). Multiplication over the coordinates gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\geq0}A_n(s)t^s
&=\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^n,\\
\sum_{s\geq0}B_n(s)t^s
&=\frac{2nt}{(1-t)^2}\sum_{s\geq0}A_n(s)t^s,\\
B_n(s)&=2n\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}(s-r)A_n(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

These counts include all signs and zero coordinates, without restricting to a sublattice.

For every real \(b>0\) and integer \(d\geq3\), we have the exact analytic inequality

\[
\sum_{k\geq1}k(b-k)_+^{d-2}
\leq\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty(b-u-v)_+^{d-2}\,du\,dv
=\frac{b^d}{d(d-1)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, \((u,v)\mapsto(b-u-v)_+^{d-2}\) is convex because \(t\mapsto(t)_+^{d-2}\) is convex for \(d-2\geq1\). On the unit square \([i,i+1]\times[j,j+1]\), pair each point with its reflection through the square center. Convexity shows that the integral is at least the value at that center, \((b-1-i-j)_+^{d-2}\). Summing over all nonnegative integers \(i,j\) gives the left side of (1), because there are exactly \(k\) pairs with \(1+i+j=k\). The squares partition the nonnegative quadrant up to a null set, and successive integration gives \(b^d/[d(d-1)]\). This is an exact convexity inequality, not a Riemann-sum estimate.

In the moment ratio, the common factor \(2^d\) from every original weight cancels. The convolution identity and (1), applied with \(b=R-r>0\), give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\left[\frac{\sum_i Z_i^2}{a_Z^2}\right]
&=\frac{\sum_{\substack{s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\s<R}}B_n(s)(R-s)^{d-2}}
        {\sum_{\substack{s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\s<R}}A_n(s)(R-s)^d},\\
\sum_{\substack{s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\s<R}}B_n(s)(R-s)^{d-2}
&=2n\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\r<R}}A_n(r)
       \sum_{k\geq1}k(R-r-k)_+^{d-2}\\
&\leq\frac{2n}{d(d-1)}
       \sum_{\substack{r\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\\r<R}}A_n(r)(R-r)^d.
\end{aligned}
\]

There are only finitely many nonzero terms, and the denominator is positive. This proves the moment inequality for every \(R>0\), including radii arbitrarily close to integers. Terms at \(s=R\), when present as lattice shells, contribute zero and are not positive fibers.

The shape normalization gives \(\|T_z^{-1}(0-c_z)\|_2^2=\sum_i z_i^2/(4a_z^2)\). The moment bound and Markov's inequality yield \(p(D_+\setminus E_0)\leq24n/[d(d-1)]\). Together with nonnegativity of the fitted mass, this proves its positive-part bound. In every dimension, \(E_0\) contains the origin and is nonempty.

We verify the actual cube-cap transfer, using the normalization and cube calculation in accepted fact 4e94470dc826efb6. Our body is not identified with that predecessor's different body. Let \(U_1,\ldots,U_d\) be independent uniform variables on \([-1/2,1/2]\). For nonzero \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), put \(Q=b\cdot U\) and \(\sigma^2=\|b\|_2^2/12>0\). Direct integration and independence give

\[
\mathbb E Q^4
=\frac1{80}\sum_l b_l^4
 +\frac1{24}\sum_{l<m}b_l^2b_m^2
\leq3\sigma^4.
\]

Splitting the second moment at \(|Q|=\sigma/2\) and applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the remaining part yields \(\sigma^2\leq\sigma^2/4+(\mathbb E Q^4\,\mathbb P(|Q|\geq\sigma/2))^{1/2}\). Hence \(\mathbb P(|Q|\geq\sigma/2)\geq3/16\), and symmetry gives \(\mathbb P(Q\geq\sigma/2)\geq3/32\).

Fix \(z\in E_0\). Every coordinate of \(T_z^{-1}(0-c_z)\) has absolute value at most \(1/\sqrt{48}<1/2\), so \(0_d\in C_z\). For any nonzero fiber normal \(v\), take \(b=T_z^{\mathsf T}v\ne0\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
v\cdot(0-c_z)
\leq\|b\|_2\,\|T_z^{-1}(0-c_z)\|_2
\leq\frac{\|b\|_2}{\sqrt{48}}
=\frac{\sigma}{2}.
\]

Uniform volume on the actual cube is represented by \(c_z+T_zU\). Therefore the fiber halfspace \(v\cdot y\geq0\) has volume at least \((3/32)w_z\). Every closed fiber halfspace containing \(0_d\) contains a parallel one with boundary through \(0_d\), so the same lower bound holds for every such containing halfspace.

This cap estimate gives the stated affine-fit certificate. The finite weighted lattice centerpoint assertion recorded in the proof of accepted fact 0bcc51d8cd216cf3, applied to \(E_0\) with the original weights \(w_z\), supplies \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E_0)\) such that every proper closed base halfspace containing \(j\) captures at least \(2^{-n}\sum_{E_0}w_z\). Each \((z,0_d)\), \(z\in E_0\), belongs to \(C\), so convexity gives \(y_0=(j,0_d)\in S\). Shrink any containing ambient halfspace to a parallel one through \(y_0\), written \(\alpha\cdot(x-j)+v\cdot y\geq0\). On every fitted fiber with \(\alpha\cdot(z-j)\geq0\), its section contains \(0_d\). If \(\alpha\ne0\), these base points capture the stated centerpoint weight; if \(\alpha=0\), they capture all fitted weight. When \(v\ne0\), the cap bound gives at least \((3/32)w_z\) on each captured fiber. When \(v=0\), the entire fiber is captured. Summation and the infimum over containing halfspaces prove \(h_S(y_0)\geq(3/32)2^{-n}\sum_{E_0}w_z\). The whole space, if admitted as a halfspace, trivially satisfies this bound.

For this family we can select the origin and obtain a stronger result. Both \(E_0\) and its weights are invariant under \(z\mapsto-z\). Every base halfspace \(\alpha\cdot z\geq0\) captures at least half of \(\sum_{E_0}w_z\): each nonzero antipodal pair has equal weights, and at least one of its points is captured. The origin is captured as well. When \(\alpha=0\), all fitted weight is captured. Shrink any closed ambient halfspace containing \((0_n,0_d)\) to its parallel halfspace \(\alpha\cdot x+v\cdot y\geq0\). On captured fitted fibers its section contains \(0_d\), so the same cap and vertical-case argument gives at least \((3/32)(1/2)\sum_{E_0}w_z\). This proves the stronger fixed-point depth bound, with the point selected independently of all halfspaces.

If \(0<R\leq1\), the only positive integer fiber is at \(0_n\), whose centroid is zero, and both claimed ranks are zero. If \(R>1\), the support contains \(0_n\) and \(\pm e_i\) for every \(1\leq i\leq n\). Here \(e_i\) is a base coordinate vector, and let \(f_i\in\mathbb R^d\) be the corresponding continuous coordinate vector. The centroid graph contains \((0_n,0_d)\) and \((\pm e_i,f_i)\). Their sums and differences span \((0_n,f_i)\) and \((e_i,0_d)\), so its affine dimension is at least \(2n\). The graph lies in \(\mathbb R^n\times\operatorname{span}\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}\), so that dimension is exactly \(2n\); the base support has affine dimension \(n\). The residual-rank identity (16) of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78, with its centroid volume \(a_z\) identified as our \(w_z\), gives \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(D_+)=2n-n=n\).

If \(n\geq49\), then \(d\geq n\geq49\), and \(24n/[d(d-1)]\leq24/(n-1)\leq1/2\). The fitted original mass is at least \(W/2\), and the origin bound gives \(h_S((0_n,0_d))\geq3W/128\).

For \(1\leq n\leq48\), apply the actual-body conclusion (10) of accepted fact 3bcbf21f33edf564 to the full-dimensional compact convex body already verified above. Its hypotheses \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\) hold because the origin fiber has positive volume. That accepted conclusion supplies a single \(y^\star\in S\), before all containing halfspaces, with

\[
h_S(y^\star)
\geq\frac{1}{96(\log2)(n+5)}\,2^{-n}W
\geq\frac{1}{5088\log2}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

Finally, \(\log2=\int_1^2t^{-1}\,dt\geq1/2\), so \(0<c_0\leq1/2544<1/3<1/e\), using the elementary bound \(e<3\). Also \(c_0\leq1/2544<3/128\). The large-\(n\) estimate therefore implies \(h_S((0_n,0_d))\geq c_0 2^{-n}W\) as well. The two cases establish the absolute constant for every \(n,d,R\) in the statement. No assertion about arbitrary convex bodies beyond this common-cube family follows from the new moment calculation.
