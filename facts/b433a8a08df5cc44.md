## Statement

Consider an attained interior-scale local minimum of $\beta_\varepsilon(K,L)-\tau\log\varepsilon$ with $\tau>0$, where $\beta_\varepsilon=Q/M$ uses the actual logarithmically normalized finite-plane buffered profile. Assume the actual finite-contact continuation and conservative loss hypotheses of 5fa497a06b11cbcb for all proper groups, and the complete actual proper and possibly atomic old cover, strict off-cover gaps and physically addable test cone of 91ad59d2662839ca. Require the same old-contact localization and uniform first-order shape envelopes for the underlying body/parameter/normal paths, with a simultaneous perturbation of scale by $\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon e^{st}$, $s\in\mathbb R$. Atomic rows are fixed pure-base caps.

There is one allocated separator for this extended cone, with the same body, parameter and normal terms at $s=0$ as in 91ad59d2662839ca, and its total buffered force satisfies
$$
\Delta=\sum_{ia}\kappa_{ia}\varepsilon f_{ia}=M\tau.
$$
If $a\tau\le\varepsilon\le b_0\tau$ and $0<M_*\le M\le M^*$, then $M_*/b_0\le\Delta/\varepsilon\le M^*/a$. When the parameter directions required by ff01846e056fd4d4 are also in the same cone, this positive $\Delta$ is the one used in its gradient-labeled volume rows.

## Proof

Add $\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon e^{st}$ to each actual path. This preserves the bodies, the raw parameter and the freely shared normal velocities of the path, and is feasible for either sign of $s$ since the scale is interior. For each proper group, the normalized old normals have $\Gamma(u_{ij})=1$ and the weights $\rho_{ij}$ sum to one. The contact expression has buffer term $-\varepsilon f(w)\sum_j\rho_{ij}\Gamma(u_{ij})$. Thus the first-order budget of contact $a$ changes by $-s\varepsilon f_{ia}$ and its group budget is the minimum over the old contacts of these perturbed budgets. The uniform expansion used in 5fa497a06b11cbcb is preserved: the additional first-order term is continuous in $w$ on the contact neighborhoods, and the exponential Taylor error and products with old first variations are of higher order. Atomic pure-base caps have zero scale derivative.

For inward total volume speed $D$, a group with conservative raw loss $\ell_i^0$ gives objective derivative at most $(\beta_\varepsilon D-\ell_i^0)/M-\tau s$. Its conservative raw improvement is therefore $\ell_i^0-\beta_\varepsilon D+M\tau s$. This common penalty term also belongs to every atomic row. Strict improvement of every group would imply strict actual descent by the retained cover and off-cover gaps, contradicting minimality. The extended test cone is finitely addable, since actual body and parameter paths are addable in the hypothesis and the scalar jets add along the common scale line.

Use the compact branch and probability-mixture proof of 91ad59d2662839ca with these new improvements. The branches and all allocations are the same. The added terms are continuous evaluations on the same branch sets because contact choice is finite and every branch inverse coefficient $1/B_i$ is continuous there, with $B_i=\sum_j\rho_{ij}/T_{ij}$. For any finite set of tests the old strict separation argument applies on the product cone, and the finite intersection property gives a single probability mixture for all tests including the two scalar signs. Aggregation of body, normal and parameter terms is unchanged. In particular $\kappa_{ia}$ is the mixture integral of $1/B_i$ over branches with contact $a$ of group $i$. The coefficient of $s$ in the common inequality is therefore
$$
M\tau-\sum_{ia}\kappa_{ia}\varepsilon f_{ia}.
$$
Both signs force it to be zero. The raw body normalization term still uses $\beta_\varepsilon$, since the penalty is scale-only. The force-ratio bounds follow by division. Setting $s=0$ gives the fixed-scale inequalities with the same coefficients. If the extra parameter tests of ff01846e056fd4d4 are available, apply that theorem using this strictly positive $\Delta$.
