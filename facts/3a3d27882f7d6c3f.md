## Statement

For finite distinct Euclidean bases P with positive compact convex K_z satisfying all anchored Minkowski inclusions, set K_z(ε)=K_z+ε(R−|z|²)B_d, R>max|z|² and ε≥0. For each unit u and representation z=∑_w p_ww with p_z=0, its support gap grows exactly by ε∑_w p_w|w−z|²≥ε min_{w≠z}|w−z|² whenever such a representation exists. The family remains compatible. A common continuous-coordinate contraction with matching halfspaces multiplies each finite cap mass by the same factor s^d<1 and preserves its original cover property at that stage. A subsequent sufficiently small ball addition retains strict decreases in every originally positive cap budget and yields strict anchored compatibility.

Let P be a finite lattice-complete centrally symmetric spanning subset of ℤ^n, n≥1, and let the full-dimensional compact convex d-fibers K_z be compatible with K_{−z}=−K_z. For R>max_{z∈P}|z|² and ε>0 replace K_z by K_z+ε(R−|z|²)B_d, then scale all fibers by α_ε=(M/Σ_z|K_z+ε(R−|z|²)B_d|)^(1/d). The new families tend to the original, preserve central symmetry and total mass M and assign M/2 to every nonvertical origin-through cap. Every excluded-self anchored inclusion has ball slack at least α_ε ε min_{z≠w}|z−w|², and independent fiber Hausdorff changes smaller than one quarter of this slack still give actual compatible fibers on P.

## Proof

For each excluded-self representation $z=\sum_w p_w w$, the support of the rounded body is $h_z(u)+\varepsilon(R-|z|^2)$ on unit normals. Thus its support gap increases by exactly
$$
\varepsilon\bigl(\sum_w p_w|w|^2-|z|^2\bigr)=\varepsilon\sum_w p_w|w-z|^2.
$$
When the representation exists each positive weight has $w\ne z$, so this is at least $\varepsilon\Delta_P^2$ with $\Delta_P=\min_{w\ne z}|w-z|$. Representations with a selfweight in $(0,1)$ reduce to excluded-self ones by subtracting that weight and renormalizing. The pure self representation is an identity. This establishes all original inclusions, including infinitely many coefficient choices.

For the cap-budget application, common contraction in the continuous coordinates by $s\in(0,1)$, with matching transformation of the cap planes, preserves coverage at that stage and multiplies each cap mass by $s^d$. Keep these new halfspaces fixed during a subsequent ball addition. The possible increment in their masses is bounded by the sum of the volumes added to all fibers, which tends to zero by continuity of volume for these decreasing parallel bodies as the radii tend to zero. Since the menu is finite, a sufficiently small positive rounding preserves every originally positive strict budget decrease. Coverage after this rounding requires its own verification if the initial cover was only over the old mixed set.

For the symmetric assertion the radii are even in $z$, so the rounded family and then its common dilation by $\alpha_\varepsilon$ remain centrally symmetric. Every nonvertical through-origin cap has half the total mass by reflection and its volume-null boundary in each fiber. The dilation fixes the total at $M$ and makes the uniform support margin at least $\alpha_\varepsilon\varepsilon\Delta_P^2$. The rounded fibers converge to their original fibers and $\alpha_\varepsilon\to1$. An independent Hausdorff change smaller than one quarter of the stated margin changes each support gap by at most twice the Hausdorff bound, preserving all inclusions. For sufficiently small perturbations the fibers also stay positive, because each old fiber contains an interior ball and the support of a sufficiently close body remains above that of a slightly smaller ball with the same center. For a complete lattice base, grouping hull points by base verifies the actual fibers and lattice completeness excludes any additional integer base. The full base span makes this hull ambient full-dimensional.
