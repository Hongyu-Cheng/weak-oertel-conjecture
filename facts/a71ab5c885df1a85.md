## Statement

Use $K_0,t,D$ from the exact trapezoid depth lemma. Let $g_0=4/9$, $\Delta=t-g_0>0$, $c=g_0+\Delta/2$, and $K_1=[-\Delta/4,\Delta/4]\times[c-\Delta/4,c+\Delta/4]$. Form the actual hull $C=\operatorname{conv}((\{0\}\times K_0)\cup(\{1\}\times K_1))$. Under summed mixed area, $Y=(0,0,t)$ maximizes normalized depth with value $D/M$, where $M=3/2+\Delta^2/4$. Its cap $H=\{y\ge t\}$ is minimizing and its full ambient coarea centroid is $Y$. Its raw integer flux for $L=y$ at threshold $t$ is $-\Delta^3D/(8M)<0$. The conditional mean of the integer index decreases to zero immediately above the upper edge height of $K_1$ after being positive in its interior height range.

## Proof

The projection of the hull is $[0,1]$ and its extreme fibers are exactly $K_0,K_1$. Both are positive and their bases are strictly exposed in the positive support, by $-z$ and $z$. Thus 2acc8bc803acf1c0 identifies their pointwise raw depths with the own ordinary depths. The trapezoid depth lemma gives raw maximum $D$ on the first and the displayed mark attains it. On the second the raw depth is at most $|K_1|/2=\Delta^2/8$, since opposite through-point caps have area sum $|K_1|$. The square center attains this value by symmetry. The bounds $4/9<t<1/2$ give $\Delta<1/18$, so $\Delta^2/8<D$. This proves the global maximum.

The second fiber lies entirely below $t$, since its top height is $g_0+3\Delta/4$. In the first fiber the cap area is $D$ by the trapezoid lemma. Its only nonempty section is a horizontal segment centered at $(0,t)$ in that fiber, giving full ambient coarea centroid $Y$. The first-fiber area is $V_0=3/2$ and its scalar mean is $(\int_0^1 y(2-y)\,dy)/V_0=4/9=g_0$. Write $V_1=|K_1|=\Delta^2/4$. The second scalar mean is $c$, so the directed edge flow $0\to1$ is $V_0(m-g_0)=V_0V_1\Delta/(2M)$. By the closed-tail decomposition, the cap fractions $P_0(t)=D/V_0$, $P_1(t)=0$ give $J(t)=-V_1\Delta D/(2M)=-\Delta^3D/(8M)$. The section density of $K_1$ is positive on its interior height range and zero just above its top, where $K_0$ still has positive density. The conditional integer mean is $f_1/(f_0+f_1)$ there, proving the last claim.
