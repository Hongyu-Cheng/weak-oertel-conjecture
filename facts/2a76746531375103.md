## Statement

Let $T$ be a $4$-simplex with vertex $v$, let $B_r=v+r(T-v)$, and let
$$
C_r=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{-1\}\times B_r)\cup(\{0\}\times T)\cup(\{1\}\times B_r)\bigr)
\qquad(0\le r\le 1/10).
$$
Each $C_r$ is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^5$. At $r=1/10$ its maximum normalized mixed $4$-volume depth is strictly below $\gamma_4(1+10^{-4})/(1+2\cdot10^{-4})<\gamma_4$, where $\gamma_4=(4/5)^4$. At $r=0$ its maximum normalized depth is $\gamma_4$.

## Proof

The only integer bases are $-1,0,1$. The endpoint fibers are $B_r$, and the middle fiber is exactly $T$ because $B_r\subseteq T$ and projection onto the continuous coordinates sends the entire hull into $T$. A full-dimensional simplex in the middle plane and any point at an endpoint span $\mathbb R^5$. Set $V=|T|_4$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ and $t=\gamma_4(1+\epsilon)=160016/390625$. For $r=1/10$ the total mass is $(1+2\epsilon)V$.

Use the vertex-cap calculation of 8dd62757ee4f1859, reproduced here for $d=4$. Write $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_4$ for the barycentric coordinates of $T$, with $\lambda_0(v)=1$. The endpoint fibers satisfy $\lambda_i\le1/10$ for $i=1,\ldots,4$. If a point $(0,x)$ had raw depth at least $tV$, each ambient cap $\lambda_i(y)\ge\lambda_i(x)$ would have mass at least $tV$. Its middle contribution is $(1-\lambda_i(x))^4 V$ by homothety at vertex $i$. For $i\ge1$, if $\lambda_i(x)>1/10$ there is no endpoint contribution and therefore $\lambda_i(x)\le1-t^{1/4}$. If $\lambda_i(x)\le1/10$ this bound holds because $t<(9/10)^4$. For $i=0$ the endpoint contribution is at most $2\epsilon V$, so $\lambda_0(x)\le1-(t-2\epsilon)^{1/4}$. Put $a_*=8000199/10^7$ and $b_*=7999223/10^7$. The exact inequalities $t-a_*^4=2032792963819263760799/10^{28}>0$, $t-2\epsilon-b_*^4=664183074753367294559/10^{28}>0$, and $4a_*+b_*-4=19/10^7>0$ imply $4t^{1/4}+(t-2\epsilon)^{1/4}>4$. The five coordinate bounds sum to strictly less than one, a contradiction.

At either endpoint base the positive fiber is strictly exposed. By (7) of 2acc8bc803acf1c0 the raw depth equals its intrinsic depth, which is at most $\epsilon V/2$: opposite through-point halfspaces in the fiber have masses summing to $\epsilon V$ since their boundary is null. Thus the endpoint bound is strictly below $tV$ too. The maximum exists by the compact-body closed-depth attainment in the same fact, making the normalized inequality strict. At $r=0$ both endpoint marks have depth zero by the closed pure-base caps isolating those singleton fibers. All mixed mass lies on $T$, and (7) and (12) of 2acc8bc803acf1c0 give raw maximum $\gamma_4 V$. Division by $V$ proves the claim.
