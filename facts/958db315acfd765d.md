## Statement

For $N=3,r=2$, at bases $z=-1,0,1$ set $K_-=[-4,4]\times[-1,1]$, $K_+=[-1,1]\times[-4,4]$, $K_0=[-5/2,5/2]^2$, and $Q_z=K_z\times[-1/2,1/2]$. These are actual integer fibers with $Q_0=(Q_-+Q_+)/2$. The first-two-coordinate marginal densities are $\mathbf1_{K_z}$, with masses $W_-=W_+=16$ and $W_0=25$, unique radial centers zero and radial bodies $L_z=K_z$. The normalization $\widehat L_z=W_z^{1/N-1/r}L_z$ fails the midpoint inclusion. Consequently every lift whose exact projections are $\widehat L_z$ fails it as well. The same incompatibility occurs for marginals realized as simultaneous weak limits of common-normalization three-dimensional slices of one actual affine cell at positive rate and limiting support dimension $2<3$.

## Proof

The continuous half-widths $5/2-3z/2$ and $5/2+3z/2$ interpolate affinely, as does the constant width in the third direction. They define one convex body over $[-1,1]$ with the displayed integer census and exact Minkowski midpoint. The constant third thickness is one. For a uniform body and an interior mark $c$, its radial body is its translate by $-c$, so the only mark giving centroid zero is its centroid. A boundary or exterior mark cannot give a full-dimensional zero-centroid radial body, since its rays lie in a closed halfspace through zero. Thus $c=0$ for these rectangles. After normalization the endpoint midpoint is $16^{-1/6}K_0$, strictly larger than $25^{-1/6}K_0=\widehat L_0$. Linear projection commutes with Minkowski addition and preserves inclusion, giving the statement about all lifts. In particular appending the centered interval of length $W_z^{1/3}$ would restore volume $W_z$ but retain this projection defect.

For an integer $j\ge12$ set $\varepsilon_j=1/(57j)$. Use the actual convex body with the same affine rectangular widths and $-1\le z\le1$, now adding $|u|\le\varepsilon_j/2$ and $0\le v\le\varepsilon_j$ in two further coordinates. It is full-dimensional and uniformly bounded, with mixed four-volume $M_j=57\varepsilon_j^2$. For $A_j=v+\exp(-2j)$, rate $j$ and level $\exp(-j)$, the level has $0<v<\varepsilon_j$ because $\exp(j)>j^4/24>57j$. The continuous gradient has norm one and the common coarea factor is $(M_jj)^{-1}=1/\varepsilon_j$. Substitution $u=\varepsilon_j w$ in the integrals of continuous tests on the level gives the simultaneous weak limits $\mathbf1_{K_z}(x)\,dx\otimes\delta_0(du)\otimes\delta_0(dv)$.
