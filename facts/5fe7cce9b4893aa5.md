## Statement

Use the actual horizontal-section notation \(K_y=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:(x,y)\in C\}\) and \(N(y)=\#(K_y\cap\mathbb Z^2)\). A body is base-even here when it is invariant under \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)\), so each nonempty \(K_y\) is centrally symmetric about the origin.

For every real \(L\ge1\), put \(\varepsilon=1/(100L^2)\), \(\delta=\varepsilon/L=1/(100L^3)\), and introduce the orthonormal directions \(\mathbf u=(1,\sqrt2)/\sqrt3\) and \(\mathbf v=(-\sqrt2,1)/\sqrt3\). Define the two rectangles

\[
K^-_L=\{s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v:|s|\le L,\ |t|\le\varepsilon\},
\qquad
K^+_L=\{s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v:|s|\le\varepsilon,\ |t|\le L\},
\]

and the single actual body \(C_L=\operatorname{conv}((K^-_L\times\{-1\})\cup(K^+_L\times\{1\}))\). This is a compact full-dimensional base-even convex body. Its mixed-integer set \(S_L=C_L\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)\) has \(2\le\mathcal H_1(S_L)<\infty\).

Let \(I^-_L=(-1,-1+2\delta)\) and \(I^+_L=(1-2\delta,1)\). Each interval has length \(2\delta=1/(50L^3)>0\), and \(N(y)=1\) throughout both intervals, also at the two endpoint sections \(y=-1,1\). For every compact parallelogram \(Q\subset\mathbb R^2\) of area one, at least one of \(I^-_L,I^+_L\) satisfies

\[
\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\ge 2(1-\delta)L\ge L N(y)
\quad\text{for every }y\text{ in that interval}.
\]

In particular, this includes every translated lattice fundamental parallelogram with integer edge vectors of determinant \(\pm1\). Consequently, for every finite \(A\ge0\), choosing any \(L>\max\{A,1\}\) gives an actual \(C_L\) for which every such \(Q\) violates \(\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\le A N(y)\) on a set of positive one-dimensional measure.

## Proof

First audit the entire actual body and all its sections. The vectors \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) have norm one, have dot product zero, and satisfy \(\det(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)=1\). The endpoint rectangles are compact and convex. Because both lie at the two distinct heights, every point of their convex hull has height \(y\in[-1,1]\). At such a height put \(\lambda=(y+1)/2\). Grouping the lower-height terms and upper-height terms in any convex combination shows that its horizontal section is exactly \((1-\lambda)K^-_L+\lambda K^+_L\). Conversely, every point of this Minkowski combination is the base coordinate of the corresponding convex combination of two endpoint points. The endpoint cases \(\lambda=0,1\) have the same formula. The sum of the coordinate intervals gives the exact description

\[
K_y=\left\{s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v:
|s|\le(1-\lambda)L+\lambda\varepsilon,
\quad |t|\le(1-\lambda)\varepsilon+\lambda L\right\}
\quad(-1\le y\le1).
\tag{1}
\]

For \(y\notin[-1,1]\), \(K_y\) is empty. In particular, (1) describes the body globally, including its boundary. It exhibits \(C_L\) as a bounded closed set, since its defining bounds are continuous affine functions of \(y\). It is convex by its construction. Since \(\varepsilon\le L\), each bound in (1) is at least \(\varepsilon\). Thus \(C_L\) contains \(\{(s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v,y):|s|\le\varepsilon,\ |t|\le\varepsilon,\ |y|\le1\}\), which has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^3\). Hence \(C_L\) is a convex body. Each rectangle in (1) is invariant under \((s,t)\mapsto(-s,-t)\), proving base-evenness.

The origin belongs to \(K_y\) for every \(y\in[-1,1]\), so the actual integer fiber at the origin is the entire interval \([-1,1]\). Thus \(S_L\ne\varnothing\) and its mixed-integer volume is at least two. Both bounds in (1) are at most \(L\), so every base point of \(C_L\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt2 L\). Only finitely many integer base points can occur, and each of their actual fibers is contained in \([-1,1]\). This proves \(\mathcal H_1(S_L)<\infty\). The counts are measurable: (1) makes membership of each fixed integer base point a closed condition on \([-1,1]\), and only finitely many such points occur. Outside \([-1,1]\), both \(N(y)\) and \(\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\) are zero.

We next prove the exact lattice count on the two end intervals, with a margin that also covers the endpoint sections. Write an integer point as \((a,b)=s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v\). The inverse coordinate formulas and two exact identities are

\[
a=\frac{s-\sqrt2t}{\sqrt3},\qquad
b=\frac{\sqrt2s+t}{\sqrt3},\qquad
b^2-2a^2=t(2\sqrt2s-t),\qquad
a^2-2b^2=-s(s+2\sqrt2t).
\tag{2}
\]

If \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), each of \(b^2-2a^2\) and \(a^2-2b^2\) is a nonzero integer, and therefore has absolute value at least one. To check nonvanishing, an equality \(b^2=2a^2\) with a nonzero integer pair would make \(b\) even, then \(a\) even, and repeated division by two would contradict the existence of a nonzero integer pair; the other equality is the same argument with the variables interchanged.

For \(-1\le y\le-1+2\delta\), equation (1) gives \(|s|\le L\) and \(|t|\le\varepsilon+\delta L=2\varepsilon\). Since \(\sqrt2<3/2\) and \(L\ge1\), (2) implies

\[
|b^2-2a^2|
\le 2\varepsilon(2\sqrt2 L+2\varepsilon)
<\frac{3}{50L}+\frac{1}{2500L^4}
\le\frac{151}{2500}<1.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus the only integer point in every such section is the origin. Likewise, for \(1-2\delta\le y\le1\), equation (1) gives \(|s|\le2\varepsilon\) and \(|t|\le L\). The second quadratic identity in (2) gives the same bound (3) for \(|a^2-2b^2|\), so again only the origin is present. This proves \(N(y)=1\) on both closed end intervals, in particular on the positive-length open intervals in the statement. It uses the original integer lattice without a change of lattice or an approximate infeasibility conclusion.

Now fix any area-one parallelogram \(Q\), allowing its choice to depend arbitrarily on \(C_L\). Define its projection widths \(w_{\mathbf u}=\max_{q\in Q}\mathbf u\cdot q-\min_{q\in Q}\mathbf u\cdot q\) and \(w_{\mathbf v}=\max_{q\in Q}\mathbf v\cdot q-\min_{q\in Q}\mathbf v\cdot q\). In the orthonormal coordinates \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\), the set \(Q\) lies in its projection bounding rectangle, whose area is \(w_{\mathbf u}w_{\mathbf v}\). Monotonicity of area gives \(1\le w_{\mathbf u}w_{\mathbf v}\). Hence at least one of these widths is at least one. This argument is independent of the edge directions and translation of \(Q\).

We use the following elementary area lower bound. For \(r>0\), choose \(q_-\) and \(q_+\) in \(Q\) attaining its minimum and maximum \(\mathbf v\)-coordinates. The convex hull of the two segments \(q_-+[-r,r]\mathbf u\) and \(q_++[-r,r]\mathbf u\) is a parallelogram of area \(2r w_{\mathbf v}\), since its edge vectors are \(2r\mathbf u\) and \(q_+-q_-\). It is contained in \(Q+[-r,r]\mathbf u\), by convexity of this Minkowski sum. Therefore \(\operatorname{vol}_2(Q+[-r,r]\mathbf u)\ge2r w_{\mathbf v}\). Interchanging \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\) gives \(\operatorname{vol}_2(Q+[-r,r]\mathbf v)\ge2r w_{\mathbf u}\).

For \(y\in I^-_L\), we have \(0<\lambda<\delta\). Equation (1) contains the segment \([-(1-\lambda)L,(1-\lambda)L]\mathbf u\), so the area lower bound yields \(\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\ge2(1-\delta)L w_{\mathbf v}\). For \(y\in I^+_L\), we have \(1-\delta<\lambda<1\), and (1) contains \([-\lambda L,\lambda L]\mathbf v\). Thus \(\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\ge2(1-\delta)L w_{\mathbf u}\). If \(w_{\mathbf v}\ge1\), choose \(I^-_L\); otherwise \(w_{\mathbf u}\ge1\), so choose \(I^+_L\). On that entire interval the area is at least \(2(1-\delta)L\). Finally \(0<\delta\le1/100\), so \(2(1-\delta)L\ge(99/50)L\ge L\), and the exact count there is one.

For any proposed finite absolute \(A\ge0\), first choose \(L>\max\{A,1\}\) and construct this one \(C_L\). For every area-one unimodular \(Q\), the preceding argument then supplies one of the two positive-length intervals on which \(\operatorname{vol}_2(K_y+Q)\ge L>A=A N(y)\). Thus no choice of a single cell for this body can make the proposed inequality true almost everywhere. The failed claim is the common pointwise smoothing bound, and no inference about failure of actual mixed-integer depth is made.
