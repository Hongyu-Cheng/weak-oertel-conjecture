## Statement

Use the actual normalized closed-halfspace depth and entropy-row notation of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. For a positive law \(p\) on a finite support \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), and \(\mathbf j\in A\), the conventions here are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
  &=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^m\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
                    \mathbf j\in H}}p(H),\\
\mathcal M_3(p)
  &=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}\tau_p(\mathbf j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The third moment is exactly the original-law quantity \(\mathcal M_3(\pi)\) of accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c, with the exact alias \(\pi=p\), not a reweighted law. Write \(r_{A,\mathbf j}\) for the unique maximum-entropy law on \(A\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), exactly the predecessor's \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) with \(E=A\). For a support of intrinsic rank \(R\), define \(\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}(p)=2^R\max_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}\). This is the assignment's normalized heaviest-atom quantity \(\rho\), not the minimum positive entropy-row atom denoted \(\rho\) in bf1ff94487296374. Let \(a_{\mathrm{box}}>0\) be exactly the absolute constant of 8db3ba8c8761ea6c; explicitly,
\[
a_{\mathrm{box}}
=\frac{1}{8\cdot20288}
  \exp\!\left(
   -\frac{4\cdot20288
      \left(4+2\max\left\{16,\frac{131}{27}e^8,65e^3\right\}\right)}
      {\log(32/27)}\right).
\]

For each even integer \(n\ge4\), set \(L=2^{n/2}\), let \(\mathbf1_n=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb Z^n\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,1]^n\cup\{-L\mathbf1_n\}\bigr),\\
D&=\{0,1\}^n\cup\{-t\mathbf1_n:1\le t\le L\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(P_n\) is the assignment's geometric polytope \(P\), not a product law denoted \(P\) in either moment predecessor. Then \(D=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and its difference lattice is all of \(\mathbb Z^n\).

There is a unique \(q>1\) satisfying
\[
q(1+q)^{n-1}=\sum_{t=1}^{L}tq^{-nt},
\qquad
1<q<16^{1/(nL)}.
\tag{1}
\]
Set \(Q=\sum_{t=1}^Lq^{-nt}\), \(Z=(1+q)^n+Q\), and let the original law on \(D\) be \(p_q(\mathbf x)=q^{\sum_{i=1}^nx_i}/Z\). In bf1ff94487296374 its exact aliases are \(A=D\), \(m=r=n\), \(p=p_q\), and \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\log q)\mathbf1_n\). Its mean is \(\mathbf0\in D\), and \(r_{D,\mathbf0}=p_q\). Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac L{16}&\le Q\le L,
&L^2&\le Z\le2L^2+L,\\
\max_{\mathbf x\in D}p_q(\mathbf x)&\le\frac2{L^2},
&\tau_{p_q}(\mathbf0)&\le\frac{L+1}{L^2},\\
\mathcal M_3(p_q)&\ge\left(\frac45\right)^3 8^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
For every proper real affine hyperplane \(F\subset\mathbb R^n\),
\[
p_q(F)\le\frac12+\frac{\log16}{4nL}+\frac1L.
\tag{3}
\]

Now set \(I=\{0,\ldots,2L\}\), let \(p_I\) be its uniform probability law, and define \(\widetilde D=D\times I\), \(\widetilde p=p_q\otimes p_I\), \(R=n+1\), and \(\widetilde{\mathbf j}=(\mathbf0,L)\). The support \(\widetilde D\) is complete and has difference lattice \(\mathbb Z^R\). The law \(\widetilde p\) is positive and log-affine, and its mean is exactly \(\widetilde{\mathbf j}\in\widetilde D\). Its aliases in bf1ff94487296374 are \(A=\widetilde D\), \(m=r=R\), \(p=\widetilde p\), and \(\boldsymbol\ell=((\log q)\mathbf1_n,0)\); in particular \(r_{\widetilde D,\widetilde{\mathbf j}}=\widetilde p\). It satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\widetilde p}(\widetilde{\mathbf j})
  &\le\frac{L+1}{L^2}\longrightarrow0,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}(\widetilde p)
  &\le\frac4{2L+1}\longrightarrow0,\\
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^R\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\\c\in\mathbb R}}
 \widetilde p\bigl(\{\mathbf z:\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf z=c\}\bigr)
  &\le\frac12+\frac{\log16}{4nL}+\frac1L,\\
\mathcal M_3(\widetilde p)
  &\ge\left(\frac45\right)^3a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-R}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
All limits are through even \(n\to\infty\).

The subspace \(V=\{(\mathbf x,k):x_1=\cdots=x_n\}\subseteq\mathbb R^R\) has codimension \(n-1\) and saturated rank-two integer lattice. Its slice \(E=\widetilde D\cap V\), with conditional law \(\pi_E=\widetilde p(\,\cdot\mid E)\), satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{(s\mathbf1_n,k):s\in\{-L,\ldots,1\},\ k\in I\},\\
\widetilde p(E)&\ge\frac1{48L},
&\frac{\widetilde p(E)}{2^{-(n-1)}}&\ge\frac L{96}\longrightarrow\infty,\\
\mathcal M_3(\pi_E)&\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-2},
&\max_{\mathbf z\in E}\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf z)
  &\ge\frac{a_{\mathrm{box}}}{192L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the slice retains more than its codimension's dyadic mass by an unbounded factor and has a uniform rank-two conditional depth certificate.

Consequently, for every \(\varepsilon,\delta,\eta_0>0\), some complete full-rank finite integer support and positive log-affine original law have an integer mean \(\mathbf j\), \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)<\delta\), and \(\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}(p)<\eta_0\), while every proper real affine hyperplane has mass strictly less than \(1/2+\varepsilon\). In particular this holds for every primitive integer affine hyperplane, meaning \(\{\mathbf z:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z=c\}\) with \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^R\) primitive and nonzero and \(c\in\mathbb Z\). No fixed positive excess over one half can be forced by the stated small-depth and small-atom hypotheses.

## Proof

First verify the support without omitting any lattice points. Every \(\mathbf x\in P_n\) can be written as \(\mathbf x=(1-a)\mathbf b-aL\mathbf1_n\) with \(0\le a\le1\) and \(\mathbf b\in[0,1]^n\). If \(a=0\) and \(\mathbf x\) is integral, then \(\mathbf x\in\{0,1\}^n\). If \(a>0\), each coordinate-pair difference has absolute value at most \(1-a<1\). For integral \(\mathbf x\), these differences are integers and therefore vanish. Thus \(\mathbf x=s\mathbf1_n\) for an integer \(-L\le s\le1\). If \(s\ge0\), the point is one of the two diagonal cube vertices; if \(s<0\), it is a listed chain point. Conversely, every listed chain point lies on the segment from \(-L\mathbf1_n\) to \(\mathbf0\), and every cube point lies in \(P_n\). Hence \(D=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Since the cube vertices and the apex belong to \(D\), also \(P_n=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). The support contains \(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\), so its differences generate all of \(\mathbb Z^n\).

For (1), the function \(q\mapsto q(1+q)^{n-1}\) is strictly increasing for \(q>0\), and \(q\mapsto\sum_{t=1}^Ltq^{-nt}\) is strictly decreasing. At \(q=1\), the left side equals \(2^{n-1}=L^2/2\), while the right side equals \(L(L+1)/2>L^2/2\). Put \(\overline q=16^{1/(nL)}\). Since \(L\) is even, at \(\overline q\) the chain weights are \(16^{-t/L}\le1\) for \(t\le L/2\), and are strictly less than \(1/4\) for \(t>L/2\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^Lt\overline q^{-nt}
 &<\sum_{t=1}^{L/2}t
      +\frac14\sum_{t=L/2+1}^{L}t\\
 &=\frac7{32}L^2+\frac5{16}L
 <\frac12L^2
 \le\overline q(1+\overline q)^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate inequality follows from \(9L>10\), which holds for \(L\ge4\). Continuity and the strict monotonicities give exactly one solution in \((1,\overline q)\), and no other solution greater than one.

For this root, \(q^n<16^{1/L}\le2\). Each cube weight \(q^{\sum_i b_i}\) lies between one and two, and each chain weight \(q^{-nt}\) lies between \(1/16\) and one. There are \(2^n=L^2\) cube points and \(L\) chain points, so these weight bounds prove the bounds on \(Q\) and \(Z\) in (2). The largest weight is \(q^n\), at \(\mathbf1_n\); hence \(\max p_q=q^n/Z\le2/L^2\).

The unnormalized first moment in each coordinate is
\[
\sum_{\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^n}b_iq^{\sum_jb_j}
 -\sum_{t=1}^{L}tq^{-nt}
=q(1+q)^{n-1}-\sum_{t=1}^{L}tq^{-nt}=0.
\]
Thus the exact mean is \(\mathbf0\), not merely a limit of means. To verify the entropy-row identification in the convention of bf1ff94487296374, let \(\nu\) be any law on \(D\) with this mean. Its relative entropy against the positive log-affine law satisfies \(D(\nu\|p_q)=H(p_q)-H(\nu)\ge0\), with equality only for \(\nu=p_q\). Here \(H(\nu)=-\sum_{\mathbf x}\nu_{\mathbf x}\log\nu_{\mathbf x}\), with zero summands omitted, and \(D(\nu\|p_q)=\sum_{\mathbf x}\nu_{\mathbf x}\log(\nu_{\mathbf x}/p_q(\mathbf x))\) is relative entropy, not the support \(D\). The identity holds because the log-affine term has the same expectation under both laws. Nonnegativity and its equality case follow by Jensen applied to the strictly convex function \(u\log u\) with weights \(p_q(\mathbf x)\) and arguments \(\nu(\mathbf x)/p_q(\mathbf x)\). Hence \(p_q=r_{D,\mathbf0}\) exactly. The same argument applies to every positive log-affine law at its own mean, so it will also identify the padded law below. In the entropy-capacity convention of that accepted fact, both of these own-mean rows have capacity one; no capacity reweighting is being used.

The proper closed halfspace \(\{\mathbf x:\sum_i x_i\le0\}\) contains the mean. Among cube points it contains only \(\mathbf0\), including that boundary atom, and it contains every chain point. Its mass is \((1+Q)/Z\le(L+1)/L^2\), proving the depth bound in (2).

For the hyperplane bound, condition \(p_q\) on the cube. The conditional law is the product of \(n\) Bernoulli laws with probability \(q/(1+q)\) of the bit one. Given any proper real affine hyperplane \(F=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x=c\}\), choose \(i\) with \(u_i\ne0\). After fixing all other cube coordinates, at most one of the two values of the \(i\)-th bit can solve its equation. The conditional probability of that event is at most \(q/(1+q)\). Therefore the full law satisfies \(p_q(F)\le q/(1+q)+Q/Z\). The function \(s\mapsto e^s/(1+e^s)\) has derivative at most \(1/4\) and value \(1/2\) at zero. Since \(0<\log q<\log16/(nL)\), and \(Q/Z\le1/L\), this gives (3). This argument uses a hyperplane equality, not a halfspace inequality.

The convex hull of \(\widetilde D\) is \(P_n\times[0,2L]\): product convex coefficients show that the convex hull of a Cartesian product of finite supports is the product of their convex hulls. Its integer points are exactly \(D\times I\), by the already proved completeness of \(D\). The difference lattice is all of \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), because the base supplies every unit difference in the first \(n\) coordinates and the interval supplies the last unit difference. The law is explicitly
\[
\widetilde p(\mathbf x,k)
 =\frac{q^{\sum_i x_i}}{Z(2L+1)},
\qquad
(\mathbf x,k)\in\widetilde D.
\]
It is positive on the entire complete support, log-affine with the specified tilt, and has exact mean \((\mathbf0,L)\) because the uniform interval has mean \(L\). The preceding relative-entropy argument, with the padded aliases stated above, proves \(r_{\widetilde D,\widetilde{\mathbf j}}=\widetilde p\). Both means are interior to their convex hulls: a nonconstant supporting affine functional could not attain its boundary value at a mean of a positive law on a full-dimensional support.

The same closed cap on the first \(n\) coordinates, with the interval coordinate unrestricted, has mass \((1+Q)/Z\) and contains \((\mathbf0,L)\). It gives the first inequality of (4). Also,
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}(\widetilde p)
 =2^{n+1}\frac{q^n}{Z(2L+1)}
 \le2L^2\frac2{L^2(2L+1)}
 =\frac4{2L+1}.
\]
To bound any proper affine hyperplane in the padded space, write its equation as \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x+vk=c\). If \(v\ne0\), conditioning on \(\mathbf x\) leaves at most one interval value, so its mass is at most \(1/(2L+1)\). If \(v=0\), then \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\), and its mass is exactly the mass of a base hyperplane, bounded by (3). Since \(1/(2L+1)<1/2\), these cases prove the hyperplane inequality of (4).

We now verify the actual third moments without changing the original law. For every support point \(\mathbf x\), a closed halfspace containing it contains its atom, so \(\tau_{p_q}(\mathbf x)\ge p_q(\mathbf x)\). With \(N=|D|=L^2+L\le(5/4)L^2\), convexity gives
\[
\mathcal M_3(p_q)
 \ge\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_q(\mathbf x)^4
 \ge N^{-3}
 \ge\left(\frac45\right)^3L^{-6}
 =\left(\frac45\right)^3 8^{-n}.
\]
Apply accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c to the single interval factor with its exact parameters \(k=1\), \(m_1=2L+1\), \(\theta_1=0\), \(D_1=I\), \(p_1=p_I\), and product law \(P=p_I\). Its intrinsic rank is one, and its original-law third-moment estimate gives \(\mathcal M_3(p_I)\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-1}\).

For completeness, actual product depth is supermultiplicative here. Any proper closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf x,k)\) contains a parallel translate whose boundary passes through that point. Write the latter inequality as \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)+v(t-k)\ge0\). It contains the Cartesian product of \(\{\mathbf y\in D:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)\ge0\}\) and \(\{t\in I:v(t-k)\ge0\}\). Each block event has probability at least the corresponding block depth; when a block normal is zero its event has probability one. Independence under the original product law therefore gives \(\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf x,k)\ge\tau_{p_q}(\mathbf x)\tau_{p_I}(k)\). Cubing and summing with the product root probabilities yields \(\mathcal M_3(\widetilde p)\ge\mathcal M_3(p_q)\mathcal M_3(p_I)\), proving the final bound of (4).

Finally consider \(V\) and \(E\). The equations \(x_i-x_n=0\), \(1\le i<n\), have independent primitive integer normals, so \(V\) has codimension \(n-1\). Its integer points are exactly the image of the saturated chart \(\Phi:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^R\), \(\Phi(s,k)=(s\mathbf1_n,k)\): any integral point of \(V\) has integral common first coordinate and integral last coordinate. This proves the stated rank and saturation. The only diagonal cube points are \(\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf1_n\), and every chain point is diagonal. Hence the displayed description of \(E\) is exact, and
\[
\widetilde p(E)=\frac{1+q^n+Q}{Z}
 \ge\frac{Q}{Z}
 \ge\frac{L/16}{3L^2}
 =\frac1{48L},
\]
where \(Z\le2L^2+L\le3L^2\). As \(2^{-(n-1)}=2/L^2\), this proves the mass ratio in (5).

In the chart \(\Phi\), the conditional law is the product
\[
\pi_E(\Phi(s,k))
 =\frac{q^{ns}}{(Q+1+q^n)(2L+1)},
\qquad -L\le s\le1,\quad0\le k\le2L.
\]
It is a two-interval original log-affine law. The exact translated-box parameters in 8db3ba8c8761ea6c are factor lengths \(m_1=L+2\), \(m_2=2L+1\), tilts \(\theta_1=n\log q\), \(\theta_2=0\), intrinsic translation \(\mathbf a=(-L,0)\), and unnormalized prefactor \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}=1\). On that translated intrinsic support the weights are \(q^{ns}\). For the predecessor's unshifted reduced box, its chart is the present map \((h,k)\mapsto\Phi(h-L,k)=((h-L)\mathbf1_n,k)\): its ambient translation is \(\mathbf v=(-L\mathbf1_n,0)\), and its matrix \(B\) has columns \((\mathbf1_n,0)\) and \((\mathbf0,1)\). The predecessor's reduced prefactor is \(b_{\mathrm{red}}=q^{-nL}\). Choosing its ambient tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell'=((\log q)\mathbf1_n,0)\) gives \(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell'=(n\log q,0)\) and its ambient prefactor \(b'=b_{\mathrm{red}}e^{-\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf v}=1\). Thus the pushed-forward weights are exactly \(q^{ns}\), with total weight \((Q+1+q^n)(2L+1)\), and normalization is exactly \(\pi_E\). Both factors are nonsingleton, the intrinsic rank is two, and the chart is saturated as just checked. The accepted actual third-moment bound is therefore \(\mathcal M_3(\pi_E)\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-2}\), proving the conditional assertion of (5).

Since this moment is an average of depth cubes, some \(\mathbf z\in E\) has conditional depth at least \(a_{\mathrm{box}}/4\). Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\) intersects \(V\) in a containing intrinsic closed halfspace or in all of \(V\). Thus its original mass is at least \(\widetilde p(E)\tau_{\pi_E}(\mathbf z)\). Taking infima and using the slice mass bound gives \(\tau_{\widetilde p}(\mathbf z)\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}/(192L)\), completing (5).

The contrast with accepted counterexample 2fc4ffe8399d14ef is contextual only. That fact's tall-pyramid law has a base-facet mass tending to one, whereas (4) controls all proper hyperplanes by \(1/2+o(1)\). No construction or inequality from that predecessor is a premise for the new witness. The new family separates failure of a prescribed-integer-mean depth certificate from fixed hyperplane excess, while (5) exhibits a viable higher-codimension slice and (4) retains a uniform dyadic original-law third-moment bound.

To finish the quantified obstruction, choose an even \(n\) large enough that \((L+1)/L^2<\delta\), \(4/(2L+1)<\eta_0\), and \(\log16/(4nL)+1/L<\varepsilon\). All three expressions tend to zero. The padded support, law, and mean then satisfy the asserted hypotheses, and the hyperplane inequality in (4) excludes mass at least \(1/2+\varepsilon\) for every proper real affine hyperplane, hence for every primitive integer one. This proves exactly the fixed-excess bridge obstruction and no disproof of the locked target.
