## Statement

Let $K$ be a strictly convex compact $d$-body, $d\ge2$, with $x\in\operatorname{int}K$. For any finite list of affine hyperplanes there is a line through $x$ whose two boundary endpoints each have unique outer unit normal and avoid all the planes. If $rB_d\subseteq K-x\subseteq RB_d$, the radial parametrization from the unit sphere to the translated boundary is Lipschitz with constant at most $R+R^2/r$, and its inverse has constant at most $2/r$.

## Proof

Center $x$ at zero. The gauge $g$ is subadditive and bounded above by $\|\cdot\|/r$, so $|g(u)-g(v)|\le\|u-v\|/r$. On the sphere $g\ge1/R$ and the radial function is $\rho=1/g$. Thus $\rho$ has Lipschitz constant at most $R^2/r$, and $u\mapsto\rho(u)u$ has constant at most $R+R^2/r$. The inverse $w\mapsto w/\|w\|$ has constant at most $2/r$ on $\partial K$.

Nonregular points of a convex boundary form a surface-null set, by Lipschitz convex graph charts and differentiability almost everywhere. For any proper cutting plane the body-boundary intersection is the relative boundary of its section and therefore has zero $\mathcal H^{d-1}$; for a support plane strict convexity gives at most one point, also null since $d\ge2$. The union of all these exceptional boundary sets and its projection onto the sphere are null in surface measure. The antipodal image of the projection is null as well. A direction outside both projections gives the required two endpoints.
