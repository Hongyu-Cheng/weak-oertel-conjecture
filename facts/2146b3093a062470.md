## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
where \(a_z=0\) for an empty or lower-dimensional fiber. Define
\[
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad
N_+:=|D_+|,
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in D_+}a_z,
\]
and assume \(W>0\). Let
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\qquad
\mu(H):=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z),
\]
where \(H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}\). For \(y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(y):=\inf\{\mu(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\max_{y\in S}h_S(y).
\]
Finally, set
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
\theta_d:=1-\gamma_d,
\qquad
Q(t):=\sum_{z\in D_+}(a_z-\theta_dt)_+
\quad(t>0).
\]
Then \(D_+\) is finite, the displayed maximum defining \(h_{\max}(C)\) exists, and
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\sup_{t>0}
\min\left\{2^{-n}Q(t),\ \gamma_dt\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The terms with \(Q(t)=0\) are included in (1) and contribute the trivial lower bound zero. Moreover,
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d2^{-n}W}
{\gamma_d+(1-\gamma_d)2^{-n}N_+}.
\tag{2}
\]
If every point of \(D_+\) is a vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\), then
\[
N_+\leq2^n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
h_{\max}(C)>e^{-1}2^{-n}W.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

The base projection of \(C\) is compact and therefore meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points. Hence \(D_+\) is finite, \(N_+<\infty\), and every lattice sum below is finite when written over \(D_+\). Since \(W>0\), the set \(S\) is nonempty. Accepted fact 978e8555ec5bac42 proves that \(S\) is compact, that \(h_S\) is upper semicontinuous on \(S\), and that its maximum is attained; it also proves the boundary-reduction assertion used below.

Fix \(t>0\), and write
\[
q_t(z):=(a_z-\theta_dt)_+,
\qquad
D_t:=\{z\in D_+:q_t(z)>0\}.
\]
If \(Q(t)=0\), then
\[
\min\{2^{-n}Q(t),\gamma_dt\}=0\leq h_{\max}(C),
\]
so suppose henceforth that \(Q(t)>0\). The weighted lattice-centerpoint construction proved in accepted fact bb2b5f9519ae23e8, applied to the finite set \(D_t\) with positive real weights \(q_t(z)\), supplies
\[
z_t\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(D_t)
\tag{4}
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z_t\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}q_t(z)
\geq2^{-n}Q(t).
\tag{5}
\]
This application uses no integrality or rationality of the weights.

We verify that the selected lattice point has a positive-volume fiber. From (4), after discarding zero coefficients, write
\[
z_t=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i z_i,
\qquad
z_i\in D_t,
\qquad
\lambda_i>0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1.
\]
Fix \(i_0\), and choose \(x_i\in C_{z_i}\) for \(i\ne i_0\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\lambda_{i_0}C_{z_{i_0}}
+\sum_{i\ne i_0}\lambda_i x_i
\subseteq C_{z_t}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
a_{z_t}\geq\lambda_{i_0}^{\,d}a_{z_{i_0}}>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(z_t\in D_+\), its fiber \(C_{z_t}\) has a centroid \(x_t\), and \(p_t:=(z_t,x_t)\in S\).

By the boundary reduction in 978e8555ec5bac42, it suffices to bound every proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(p_t\). Write such a halfspace as
\[
H=\left\{(z,x):
\langle u,z-z_t\rangle+\langle v,x-x_t\rangle\geq0
\right\},
\qquad
(u,v)\ne(0,0).
\tag{7}
\]
We call \(H\) vertical when \(v=0\) and nonvertical when \(v\ne0\).

First suppose that
\[
a_{z_t}\leq t.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(H\) is vertical, then \(u\ne0\) and
\[
J:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\langle u,z-z_t\rangle\geq0\}
\]
is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(z_t\). Since \(0\leq q_t(z)\leq a_z\), (5) gives
\[
\mu(H)
=\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}a_z
\geq\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}q_t(z)
\geq2^{-n}Q(t).
\tag{9}
\]
If \(H\) is nonvertical, accepted fact c13409b4041cc35d applies to the distinguished fiber \(z_t\), its centroid \(x_t\), and the halfspace (7). It supplies a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni z_t\) and, already accounting for the zero mixed mass of the nonvertical boundary, gives
\[
\mu(H)
\geq
\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}
(a_z-\theta_da_{z_t})_+.
\tag{10}
\]
The sum in (10) is explicitly over the finite set \(D_+\cap J\); fibers outside \(D_+\) contribute zero and are never subjected to an infinite threshold subtraction. From (8), \(\theta_da_{z_t}\leq\theta_dt\), so monotonicity of the positive part and (5) imply
\[
\mu(H)
\geq
\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}(a_z-\theta_dt)_+
=\sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}q_t(z)
\geq2^{-n}Q(t).
\tag{11}
\]
Thus every boundary-through-\(p_t\) halfspace has mass at least \(2^{-n}Q(t)\) under (8).

Now suppose instead that
\[
a_{z_t}>t.
\tag{12}
\]
For a nonvertical \(H\), the conclusion of c13409b4041cc35d again gives a proper base halfspace \(J\ni z_t\) and hence includes the distinguished-fiber summand
\[
\mu(H)
\geq(a_{z_t}-\theta_da_{z_t})_+
=\gamma_da_{z_t}
>\gamma_dt.
\tag{13}
\]
For a vertical \(H\), the base boundary contains \(z_t\), so the entire distinguished fiber is contained in \(H\). Therefore
\[
\mu(H)\geq a_{z_t}>t>\gamma_dt.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus every boundary-through-\(p_t\) halfspace has mass at least \(\gamma_dt\) under (12).

The two cases (8) and (12), including the equality \(a_{z_t}=t\) in the first case, show that every proper boundary-through-\(p_t\) halfspace has mass at least
\[
\min\{2^{-n}Q(t),\gamma_dt\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Fact 978e8555ec5bac42 now extends (15) to every proper closed halfspace containing \(p_t\). If the convention for a closed halfspace also permits degenerate halfspaces, the empty halfspace does not contain \(p_t\), while the whole ambient space has mass \(W\). Since \(Q(t)\leq W\),
\[
\min\{2^{-n}Q(t),\gamma_dt\}\leq2^{-n}Q(t)\leq W,
\]
so the whole-space edge case also satisfies (15). Hence
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq h_S(p_t)
\geq\min\{2^{-n}Q(t),\gamma_dt\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Together with the already treated case \(Q(t)=0\), (16) holds for every \(t>0\). Taking the supremum proves (1). No condition relating \(t\) to the volume of a preselected weighted centerpoint was assumed.

We next derive (2). Put \(\delta:=2^{-n}\). Because the subtraction is performed only over the finite positive-mass support,
\[
Q(t)
=\sum_{z\in D_+}(a_z-\theta_dt)_+
\geq\sum_{z\in D_+}(a_z-\theta_dt)
=W-\theta_dN_+t
\tag{17}
\]
for every \(t>0\). Choose
\[
t_*:=\frac{\delta W}{\gamma_d+\delta\theta_dN_+}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Then
\[
W-\theta_dN_+t_*
=\frac{\gamma_dW}{\gamma_d+\delta\theta_dN_+}>0,
\tag{19}
\]
so (17) gives \(Q(t_*)>0\). Moreover,
\[
\delta\bigl(W-\theta_dN_+t_*\bigr)
=\gamma_dt_*
=\frac{\gamma_d\delta W}
{\gamma_d+\delta\theta_dN_+}.
\tag{20}
\]
Applying (1) at \(t_*\), then using (17) and (20), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C)
&\geq\min\{\delta Q(t_*),\gamma_dt_*\}\\
&\geq\min\{\delta(W-\theta_dN_+t_*),\gamma_dt_*\}\\
&=\frac{\gamma_d2^{-n}W}
{\gamma_d+(1-\gamma_d)2^{-n}N_+},
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (2).

It remains to prove (3). First observe that the positive-mass support is lattice-convex:
\[
\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_+.
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, the nontrivial inclusion follows by exactly the scaling argument used in (6): if \(z\in\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), choose a positive convex representation by points of \(D_+\); convexity of \(C\) then places a translated positive scaling of any one participating positive-volume fiber inside \(C_z\), so \(a_z>0\). This is also the positive-fiber argument established in bb2b5f9519ae23e8.

Suppose every point of \(D_+\) is a vertex of \(P:=\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\). If two distinct points \(z,z'\in D_+\) had the same parity class modulo \(2\mathbb Z^n\), then
\[
m:=\frac{z+z'}2\in\mathbb Z^n\cap P.
\]
By (21), \(m\in D_+\). But \(m\) is the strict midpoint of the two distinct points \(z,z'\in P\), so it is not a vertex of \(P\), contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore the points of \(D_+\) occupy distinct parity classes, and \(N_+\leq2^n\).

Using this in (2) gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d2^{-n}W}
{\gamma_d+\theta_d2^{-n}N_+}
\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\tag{22}
\]
Finally,
\[
d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1
\]
because \(\log(1+x)<x\) for \(x>0\). Hence
\[
\gamma_d
=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}
>e^{-1}.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (22) and (23) proves the strict inequality in (3).
