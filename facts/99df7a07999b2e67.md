## Statement

Use exactly the definitions of \(D,W,w_{\max},\phi,B,\mathcal E_{\mathbf J},G_E,m_{\log}\), and \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. In particular \(B\) is the potential range. Let \(R\ge e\) be real. In integer dimension \(n=2\), define finite factors
\[
u_1(i)=
\begin{cases}
R^{4i}e^{-(i-1)^2},&i\in\{0,1,2\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
u_2(j)=
\begin{cases}
R^je^{-(j-2)^2},&j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Set \(\mathbf a=(4,1)\), let the scalar budget be \(9\), and put
\[
w_{(i,j)}=u_1(i)u_2(j)\mathbf1_{\{4i+j\le9\}}.
\]
Both factors have finite-valued globally log-concave extensions, and both log potentials are strictly quadratic on their integer supports. The complete positive support and canonical normalization of the cut table are
\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=(\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,2,3,4\})
\cup(\{2\}\times\{0,1\}),\\
w_{\max}&=w_{(2,1)}=e^{-2}R^9,\\
\phi_{(i,j)}&=(9-4i-j)\log R+(i-1)^2+(j-2)^2-2,\\
B&=9\log R+3.
\end{aligned}
\]
For each \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), define the uncut tilted product
\[
\widetilde w^\lambda_{(i,j)}
=u_1(i)e^{-4\lambda i}\,u_2(j)e^{-\lambda j},
\qquad
\widetilde W_\lambda=\sum_{i,j}\widetilde w^\lambda_{(i,j)},
\]
and let \(\pi_1^\lambda,\pi_2^\lambda\) be its normalized one-dimensional marginals. An integer median of a probability table \(\pi\) means an integer \(k\) for which both \(\sum_{t\le k}\pi(t)\) and \(\sum_{t\ge k}\pi(t)\) are at least \(1/2\); a mode means an integer maximizing \(\pi\). Let \(\mathcal J_R\) consist of all \(\mathbf J\in D\) obtainable, for some common \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), by choosing each coordinate independently to be either an integer median or a mode of its corresponding \(\pi_i^\lambda\). All tie choices are allowed.

Then \(\mathcal J_R\) is nonempty and
\[
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_R\\L\ge0}}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{(1+L)W}
\le\frac{11e^2}{R},
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}W
\ge\frac1{1+11e^2/R}.
\]
Consequently no positive constant \(c\), even in dimension two, can guarantee that this selection prescription produces a feasible center and a cutoff with \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)/(1+L)\ge c2^{-2}W\) for every such one-cut product.

The tilted quantities above are separate-table quantities: applying the definitions of `488f0a583886b5fc` to \(\widetilde w^\lambda\) uses its rectangular support \(\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) and total mass \(\widetilde W_\lambda\), and does not replace the cut table's \(D,W,\phi\), or \(B\). Accepted fact `11cb4cde5378cfcd` applies to every one of these uncut tilted products and gives
\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(\widetilde w^\lambda)
\ge e^{-150}2^{-2}\widetilde W_\lambda.
\]
More specifically, at \(\lambda_0=\log R\) the unique marginal median and mode center is \(\mathbf J_0=(1,2)\in D\), and
\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J_0,0;\widetilde w^{\lambda_0})}
{\widetilde W_{\lambda_0}}\ge\frac1{15},
\qquad
\sup_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J_0,L;w)}{(1+L)W}
\le\frac{8e^2}{R^3}.
\]
This is an explicit failure of undoing the tilt at its feasible marginal center, as well as a failure of optimizing that center prescription over all tilts.

## Proof

The functions
\[
f_1(x)=e^{4x\log R-(x-1)^2}\mathbf1_{[0,2]}(x),
\qquad
f_2(y)=e^{y\log R-(y-2)^2}\mathbf1_{[0,4]}(y)
\]
are finite-valued and log-concave on the whole real line, since their logarithms are concave quadratics on convex positive supports and they vanish outside those supports. They agree with the factors at every integer, including all zeros. Their product multiplied by \(\mathbf1_{\{4x+y\le9\}}\) is likewise a finite-valued globally log-concave extension of the complete cut table. Thus accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` applies with the stated support.

Write \(Q(i,j)=(i-1)^2+(j-2)^2\), solely as an abbreviation in this proof. Every point of \(D\) except \((2,1)\) has budget \(4i+j\le8\). At \((2,1)\), \(Q=2\). For every other point, the logarithm of the ratio of its weight to \(w_{(2,1)}\) is
\[
-(9-4i-j)\log R+2-Q(i,j).
\]
If \(9-4i-j\ge2\), this is nonpositive since \(\log R\ge1\) and \(Q\ge0\). The only points with \(9-4i-j=1\) are \((1,4)\) and \((2,0)\), with \(Q=4\) and \(Q=5\), respectively, so their ratios are also less than one. This proves the stated \(w_{\max}\) and hence the formula for \(\phi\). The maximum of \(\phi\) is at \((0,0)\): for each \((i,j)\in D\),
\[
\phi_{(0,0)}-\phi_{(i,j)}
=(4i+j)\log R+5-Q(i,j)\ge0,
\]
since \(Q\le5\) throughout the rectangular support. Therefore \(B=9\log R+3\), with its canonical meaning preserved.

For the tilt selection, put \(\delta=\log R-\lambda\). Normalization does not affect medians or modes, so the first and second marginal weights can be written as
\[
v_1(i)=e^{4\delta i-(i-1)^2}\quad(0\le i\le2),
\qquad
v_2(j)=e^{\delta j-(j-2)^2}\quad(0\le j\le4).
\]
Suppose first that \(\delta\le0\). Then
\[
v_1(0)\ge v_1(2),\qquad
\frac{v_1(2)}{v_1(1)}=e^{4\delta-1}<1.
\]
The first inequality, together with \(v_1(1)>0\), shows that the first marginal's atom at \(2\) is strictly less than \(1/2\). Thus \(2\) is not a median. The displayed strict ratio shows that \(2\) is not a mode either. Consequently selecting first coordinate \(2\) requires \(\delta>0\).

When \(\delta>0\), for \(j=0,1\) one has
\[
\frac{v_2(4-j)}{v_2(j)}=e^{\delta(4-2j)}>1.
\]
Thus neither \(0\) nor \(1\) is a second-coordinate mode. Also \(v_2(0)+v_2(1)<v_2(3)+v_2(4)\), so the cumulative probability through \(1\) is strictly less than \(1/2\); no second-coordinate median is below \(2\). Any selected center with first coordinate \(2\) therefore has second coordinate at least \(2\), and budget at least \(10\). It is infeasible. This proves, simultaneously for all real tilts and all allowed choices, that
\[
\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
J_1\le1,\qquad 4J_1+J_2\le8.
\]
At \(\delta=0\), the two marginal sequences are symmetric about \(1\) and \(2\), with strictly larger central atom than any other atom. Their unique medians are their symmetry centers, because the probability strictly on either side is less than \(1/2\). Their unique modes are the same centers. Hence \(\mathbf J_0=(1,2)\in\mathcal J_R\), proving nonemptiness.

For any such feasible selected center and any \(L\ge0\), use in the closed lower-cap characterization (15) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` the horizontal normal \(-\mathbf a\) and last-coordinate normal \(t=0\). This is an admissible nonzero normal. It produces the closed vertical cap
\[
\{\mathbf I\in D:4I_1+I_2\le4J_1+J_2\},
\]
independently of \(L\). It excludes \((2,1)\) for every \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_R\). All its atoms have weight at most \(R^8\), since their quadratic factors are at most one. There are at most eleven such atoms. Since \(W\ge w_{(2,1)}=e^{-2}R^9\), it follows that
\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{(1+L)W}
\le\frac{11R^8}{e^{-2}R^9}
=\frac{11e^2}{R}.
\]
The cap is closed and no equality atom is discarded. This argument uses the full integral hitting identity, not a reflected-pair proxy or a restriction of barycentric supports.

For comparison, every hitting set at center \((2,1)\) and cutoff zero must contain \((2,1)\), because the singleton belongs to \(\mathcal E_{(2,1)}\) and has \(G_E=0\), with exactly the definitions in `488f0a583886b5fc`. Therefore
\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\ge m_{\log}((2,1),0;w)\ge e^{-2}R^9.
\]
The other eleven atoms have total mass at most \(11R^8\), giving
\[
W\le e^{-2}R^9+11R^8,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}W
\ge\frac1{1+11e^2/R}.
\]
Thus the unrestricted score tends to one after division by \(W\), whereas the supremum over the prescribed centers tends to zero. Given any \(c>0\), choosing \(R>44e^2/c\) refutes the prescription's proposed \(c2^{-2}\) guarantee, regardless of its chosen cutoff.

Finally, each tilted factor has a globally log-concave extension obtained by multiplying \(f_i\) by its corresponding exponential affine function. The hypotheses of accepted fact `11cb4cde5378cfcd` are therefore satisfied for \(\widetilde w^\lambda\), proving its asserted uncut product bound with the separate-table normalization specified in the statement. At \(\lambda_0=\log R\), the tilted central atom \(\widetilde w^{\lambda_0}_{(1,2)}\) equals one and
\[
\widetilde W_{\lambda_0}
=(1+2e^{-1})(1+2e^{-1}+2e^{-4})\le15.
\]
The singleton at \(\mathbf J_0\) forces \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J_0,0;\widetilde w^{\lambda_0})\ge1\), proving its normalized lower bound \(1/15\). For the original table the same vertical cap at \(\mathbf J_0\) has budget at most six. It consists of the five row-zero atoms and the three atoms \((1,0),(1,1),(1,2)\). Each has weight at most \(R^6\). For every \(L\ge0\), its original-weight contribution is consequently at most \(8R^6\), and division by \(W\ge e^{-2}R^9\) yields the stated \(8e^2/R^3\) bound. This completes the exact verification of the selection and transfer obstruction for nonlinear factors.
