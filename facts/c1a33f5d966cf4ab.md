## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $m=d+1$, $r=d/m$, $\kappa=(1-r^d)^{1/d}$, $\psi(q)=\min\{1,\max\{0,1-q\}\}$ and $\phi(q)=(1-\psi(q)^d)^{1/d}$.

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional simplex of centroid zero and volume $V>0$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i=1/m+\ell_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. The convex hull of the lifts of $A=aT+\delta$ at $-1$ and $D=bT-\delta$ at $1$, with $a,b>0$, has actual midpoint fiber $K=((a+b)/2)T$. Take ambient halfspaces $H_i=\{(z,x):\ell_i(x)+\beta_i z\ge0\}$, with $B=\sum_i\beta_i$. Their endpoint barycentric cutoffs on the reference $T$ are
$$
q_i^A=1/m-\ell_i(\delta)/a+\beta_i/a,\qquad
q_i^D=1/m+\ell_i(\delta)/b-\beta_i/b.
$$
They satisfy
$$
\sum_iq_i^A=1+B/a,\qquad\sum_iq_i^D=1-B/b,\qquad
a q_i^A+b q_i^D=(a+b)/m.
$$
The endpoint capture fractions are $\psi(q)^d$. The actual volume roots of the residuals are $aV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^A)$ and $bV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^D)$. The closed residual is empty for $q<0$ and tangent for $q=0$, while the capture is tangent for $q=1$ and empty for $q>1$. For $0<q<1$ both have positive volume.

Define the normalized residual-root overshoots
$$
\Delta_i=\frac{a\phi(q_i^A)+b\phi(q_i^D)-\kappa(a+b)}2,
\qquad \widehat\Delta_i=V^{1/d}\Delta_i,
$$
where $\widehat\Delta_i$ is the actual overshoot in volume-root units.

Put $\eta=m\kappa-d$ and let $E_d(h)=\max\{d,m\phi(h/m)\}$. For any real $B$,
$$
\sum_i\Delta_i\le
\frac{aE_d(1+B/a)+bE_d(1-B/b)-m\kappa(a+b)}2.
$$
For fixed $a,b,d$ and $-a\eta\le B\le b\eta$, the exact maximum of $\sum_i[a\phi(q_i^A)+b\phi(q_i^D)]$ over the actual paired vectors is
$$
m\left[a\phi\left(\frac{1+B/a}{m}\right)
+b\phi\left(\frac{1-B/b}{m}\right)\right].
$$
It is attained with $\delta=0$ and $\beta_i=B/m$. It is strictly below $m\kappa(a+b)$ if $d\ge2$ and $B\ne0$. For $d=1$ this central range is $B=0$.

## Proof

Apply the scalar envelope to each threshold sum to get the upper bound. Its branch point is $h_*=m(1-\kappa)=1-\eta$. Thus the specified range of $B$ is exactly the condition that both sums are on the Jensen branch, where each constant vector is optimal separately. The two constant vectors satisfy the paired identity, and setting $\delta=0,\beta_i=B/m$ realizes them in the same actual convex body. This gives the joint maximum.

The weighted mean of the two constant arguments is $1/m$. They are nonnegative in the indicated range since $h_*=m(1-\kappa)>0$. When $B\ne0$ they are distinct, and the interval between them has nonempty interior inside $(0,1)$ since it contains $1/m$ strictly between its endpoints. The nonnegative-domain concavity and strict concavity on that subinterval give strict Jensen even if an argument is at or above one. If $d=1$, $\eta=0$, so only $B=0$ is in the range and the formula remains valid.
