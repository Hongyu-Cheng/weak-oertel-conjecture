## Statement

Let \(n\ge3\) be an integer, let \(R>0\) be rational, and put \(b=1/2-2R/(3n+1)\). Indices on \(n\)-vectors are cyclic, so \(t_0=t_n\). Set \(d=2n\), and write the continuous coordinate of the locked problem as \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n\). Define

\[
C=\left\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t):
\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0,\quad
y_i\ge|z_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}|\ (1\le i\le n),\quad
\sum_i y_i+\sum_i t_i\le R\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

This is a full-dimensional rational simplex in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and its ambient centroid has integer-coordinate projection \((1/2)\mathbf1_n\).

Retain \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{2n})\), \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{(\mathbf y,\mathbf t):(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_{2n}(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\), the locked depth \(h_S\), and \(T=2^{-n}W\). These meanings and normalizations agree with accepted facts \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\) and \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\): their continuous variable \(x\) or \(\mathbf x\) is our ordered pair \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\), their \(d\) is \(2n\), and the latter fact's \(h_C\) is exactly \(h_S\). The radius \(R\) in (1) is not the comparison constant denoted \(R\) in consequence 4 of the latter fact; that comparison constant is not used here.

For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)
&=\sum_i t_i+\sum_i|z_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}|,\\
\sigma(\mathbf z)
&=\max\left\{
\sum_i(b-z_i)_+
+\max_{\substack{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
 I\text{ contains no two cyclic neighbors}}}
\sum_{i\in I}(z_i-b)_+,
\quad\frac12\sum_i(z_i-b)
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \((a)_+=\max\{a,0\}\), with the same positive-part convention as the accepted facts. The complete integer support is \(\{\mathbf z:\sigma(\mathbf z)\le R\}\), and the positive-volume support is exactly \(\{\mathbf z:\sigma(\mathbf z)<R\}\). Its section volumes and laws are

\[
w_{\mathbf z}
=\frac1{n!}\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}
\bigl(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\bigr)_+^n\,d\mathbf t.
\tag{3}
\]

For a uniform sample from a positive fiber, the marginal density of \(\mathbf t\) is \((R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t))_+^n/(n!w_{\mathbf z})\). Given a value with \(F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)<R\), the vector with coordinates \(y_i-|z_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}|\) is uniform on \(\{\mathbf s\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i s_i\le R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\}\). Every point of every nonempty fiber with \(\sigma(\mathbf z)=R\) has actual depth zero.

Now specialize to integers \(n\ge100\) and rational numbers \(1\le k\le n/100\), with \(R=n/2+k\). Put \(L_0=R-nb\). Then \(W>0\), and

\[
w_{\mathbf0}=\frac{L_0^{2n}}{3^n(2n)!},
\qquad
\frac{W}{w_{\mathbf0}}
\le 2en\left(\frac{199}{100}\right)^n.
\tag{4}
\]

The actual zero-fiber centroid is

\[
\mathbf p_0=
\left(\mathbf0,
\left(b+\frac{5L_0}{3(2n+1)}\right)\mathbf1_n,
\frac{L_0}{3(2n+1)}\mathbf1_n\right).
\tag{5}
\]

For every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf p_0\), including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing entire fibers,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_{2n}(S\cap H)
&\ge \left(\frac{2n}{2n+1}\right)^{2n}w_{\mathbf0},\\
\frac{h_S(\mathbf p_0)}{T}
&\ge\frac1{2e^2n}\left(\frac{200}{199}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

If \(n\ge10000\), then \(w_{\mathbf0}>T\), so \(h_S(\mathbf p_0)>e^{-1}T\). For all \(n\ge100\) in this parameter range, there exists \(\mathbf p^\star\in S\) with \(h_S(\mathbf p^\star)\ge(2880000)^{-1}T\). In particular this is one absolute positive constant smaller than \(1/e\) for the stated family. Along \(n=m4^m\), \(k=4^m\), the explicit bound in (6) applies for every integer \(m\ge100\) and tends to infinity as \(m\to\infty\).

## Proof

Start from \(R\Delta_{3n}=\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t)\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i(u_i+v_i+t_i)\le R\}\), and define \(z_i=b+u_i-v_i+t_i+t_{i-1}\) and \(y_i=u_i+v_i\). The inverse is

\[
u_i=\frac{y_i+z_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}}2,
\qquad
v_i=\frac{y_i-z_i+b+t_i+t_{i-1}}2.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus this is a rational invertible affine map onto (1). Its linear part has determinant of absolute value \(2^n\). In particular (1) is a full-dimensional rational simplex. Each of the \(3n\) coordinates at the centroid of \(R\Delta_{3n}\) equals \(R/(3n+1)\). Its image therefore has base coordinates \(b+2R/(3n+1)=1/2\), as asserted.

To calculate the support exactly, its base projection, translated by \(-b\mathbf1_n\) and divided by \(R\), is
\(P=\operatorname{conv}(\{\pm\mathbf e_i,\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_{i+1}:1\le i\le n\})\).
The extra zero vertex may be omitted because zero is already in the convex hull of the opposite unit vectors. The polar polytope is

\[
P^\circ=\{\mathbf a:-1\le a_i\le1,\quad a_i+a_{i+1}\le1\ (1\le i\le n)\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Every vertex of (8) has all entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), except that \((1/2)\mathbf1_n\) is an additional possible vertex when \(n\) is odd. Here is a direct verification. Form the subgraph of the cycle consisting of the active edge equalities \(a_i+a_{i+1}=1\). A path component with no active coordinate bound has a nonzero alternating perturbation preserving all active equalities; both sufficiently small signs of the perturbation preserve every inactive inequality. Such a point is not a vertex. A path component at a vertex therefore contains an active bound \(a_i=1\) or \(a_i=-1\). Propagation along its active edges from 1 gives the alternating values 1 and 0. Propagation from \(-1\) would give 2 at a neighbor, which is infeasible, so a component containing \(-1\) has no edge. If the active graph is the full cycle, an odd cycle forces every coordinate to be \(1/2\); an even cycle has an alternating free parameter unless a coordinate bound is also active, in which case its entries are again 1 and 0. This proves the vertex description.

Among feasible vectors with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), the positions assigned 1 form an independent set of the cycle. For a linear objective with coefficients \(p_i=z_i-b\), one never needs to assign 1 to a negative coefficient: replacing that entry by \(-1\) remains feasible and increases the objective. At every negative coefficient outside the selected independent set, the maximizing choice is \(-1\); all other unselected entries may be zero. Consequently the maximum of that linear objective on these integral feasible vectors is exactly the first expression inside the maximum in (2). The possible half-integral vertex gives its second expression. That second expression can also be retained for even \(n\), since \((1/2)\mathbf1_n\) is feasible in (8) and cannot increase the maximum over the polytope. Since a linear objective on a compact polytope attains its maximum at a vertex, \(\sigma(\mathbf z)=\max_{\mathbf a\in P^\circ}\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-b\mathbf1_n)\).

The usual supporting-hyperplane characterization of a compact convex set containing zero in its interior now gives \(\mathbf z-b\mathbf1_n\in RP\) exactly when \(\sigma(\mathbf z)\le R\). This characterization follows directly by separating a point outside \(RP\) and scaling the separating normal by its positive support value on \(P\). Thus it introduces no unverified assertion about the integer support. Equivalently, solving (7) with nonnegative \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) for each fixed \(\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0\) shows that this condition is \(\min_{\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0}F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\le R\). The minimum exists because \(\mathbf t\mapsto F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\) is continuous and at least \(\sum_i t_i\), so its sublevel sets in the nonnegative orthant are compact. With \(b\) held fixed, the same argument gives identical sublevel sets for every positive radius; hence the minimum equals \(\sigma(\mathbf z)\).

For a fixed \(\mathbf t\), substitute \(s_i=y_i-|z_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}|\). Its feasible \(\mathbf y\)-section is a translate of the \(n\)-simplex of radius \(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\), or is empty if this radius is negative. Its \(n\)-volume is the integrand of (3) divided by \(n!\), including radius zero. Fubini proves (3) and the stated conditional laws. If \(\sigma(\mathbf z)<R\), continuity allows a feasible \(\mathbf t\) with all coordinates strictly positive and \(F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)<R\); choosing all \(s_i>0\) with a strict sum inequality then gives an interior point of the \(2n\)-dimensional fiber. If \(\sigma(\mathbf z)=R\), every feasible \(\mathbf t\) has zero remaining radius, so (3) is zero. If \(\sigma(\mathbf z)>R\), the fiber is empty.

For a nonempty boundary fiber choose a maximizer \(\mathbf a\in P^\circ\) with \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-b\mathbf1_n)=R\). Since \(R>0\), this normal is nonzero. The closed vertical halfspace \(\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,\mathbf t):\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-b\mathbf1_n)\ge R\}\) contains every point of this fiber. Every nonempty fiber it meets satisfies \(\sigma(\mathbf x)=R\), and therefore has zero volume. There are only finitely many integer fibers because the projection is compact. The mixed volume of this halfspace is zero. This proves the claimed actual-depth statement for every point of every zero-volume boundary fiber.

For the remaining proof impose \(n\ge100\), \(1\le k\le n/100\), and \(R=n/2+k\). The parameter identities and bounds are

\[
\begin{aligned}
b-\frac16&=\frac{1/3-2k}{3n+1},
&0<\frac16-b<\frac1{150},\\
R&\le\frac{51n}{100},
&L_0=R-nb&=\frac{n^2+(5n+1)k}{3n+1}\ge\frac n3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular \(b>0\). At the zero base, all residuals \(-b-t_i-t_{i-1}\) are negative. The substitution \(s_i=y_i-b-t_i-t_{i-1}\) identifies its fiber with \(\{\mathbf s,\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i s_i+3\sum_i t_i\le L_0\}\). This shear has determinant one. Rescaling the \(\mathbf t\)-coordinates by 3 gives a \(2n\)-simplex of radius \(L_0\) with volume factor \(3^{-n}\). This proves the formula for \(w_{\mathbf0}>0\) in (4). The centroid in the rescaled simplex has every coordinate \(L_0/(2n+1)\). Undoing the rescaling and shear gives exactly (5).

It remains to bound the complete normalization \(W\). For every real \(a\), the exponential series gives \((a)_+^n/n!\le8^{-n}e^{8a}\). Define the positive 8-periodic function \(\Theta(r)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-|8j-r|}\). Apply this inequality inside (3), sum over every integer \(\mathbf z\), and use Tonelli and \(x_i=8t_i\). The resulting bound is

\[
W\le e^{8R}8^{-2n}
\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}e^{-\sum_i x_i}
\prod_{i=1}^n\Theta(8b+x_i+x_{i-1})\,d\mathbf x.
\tag{10}
\]

No fiber has been omitted in (10). The exponential bound also applies where the section radius is nonpositive.

Let \(\kappa_b(x,y)=e^{-(x+y)/2}\Theta(8b+x+y)\) for \(x,y\ge0\), and let \(\|\kappa_b\|_2\) be its Lebesgue \(L^2\)-norm on the quadrant. The cycle integral in (10) is at most \(\|\kappa_b\|_2^n\). To see this without assuming a spectral representation, compose kernels by integrating their product over their common variable. Pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz followed by integration gives \(\|f*g\|_2\le\|f\|_2\|g\|_2\). The cycle integral is the integral of the \((n-1)\)-fold composition of \(\kappa_b\) against its transpose, and one further Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the claimed bound. The kernels are nonnegative, so Tonelli justifies these compositions; finiteness follows from the norm estimate proved next. Thus

\[
W\le e^{8R}8^{-2n}\|\kappa_b\|_2^n,
\qquad
\|\kappa_b\|_2^2
=\int_0^\infty s e^{-s}\Theta(8b+s)^2\,ds.
\tag{11}
\]

Here is an explicit rational bound on this one-dimensional integral. First evaluate the comparison kernel at \(b=1/6\), and put \(a=20/3\), \(q=e^{-8}\), and \(h=e^{-20/3}\). These are auxiliary scalars in the present integral calculation; in particular \(q\) is not a table of thinned fiber weights. For \(0\le r\le8\), summing the two geometric tails gives \(\Theta(r)=(e^{-r}+e^{r-8})/(1-q)\). Split the integral in (11) at \(s=a\) and then at \(a+8j\) for integers \(j\ge0\). On the initial interval the numerator after multiplying by \((1-q)^2\) is

\[
A_0
=\frac{e^{-8/3}}9\bigl(1-(1+3a)e^{-3a}\bigr)
+2q\bigl(1-(1+a)h\bigr)
+h^2\bigl((a-1)e^a+1\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

On the remaining intervals, writing \(s=a+8j+x\), \(0\le x\le8\), and summing over \(j\) gives the numerator

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1
&=h\left[
\left(\frac a{1-q}+\frac{8q}{(1-q)^2}\right)J_0
+\frac{J_1}{1-q}\right],\\
J_0&=\int_0^8(e^{-3x}+2qe^{-x}+q^2e^x)\,dx
\le\frac13+3q,\\
J_1&=\int_0^8x(e^{-3x}+2qe^{-x}+q^2e^x)\,dx
\le\frac19+9q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The last two inequalities can be checked directly: the exact expressions are \(J_0=1/3+3q-3q^2-q^3/3\) and \(J_1=1/9+9q-17q^2-25q^3/9\). Therefore \(\|\kappa_{1/6}\|_2^2=(A_0+A_1)/(1-q)^2\).

All numerical bounds needed here have finite rational certificates. The exponential series satisfies

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{12}\frac{(8/3)^r}{r!}-\frac{100}{7}
=\frac{68530579}{645700815}>0,
\qquad
\sum_{r=0}^{20}\frac{(20/3)^r}{r!}-780
=\frac{298562335426150237381}{51776152168407487821}>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently \(e^{-8/3}<7/100\), \(h<1/780\), and \(q=(e^{-8/3})^3<1/2900\). Dropping negative terms in (12), then using these rational upper bounds in (12)-(13), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_0
&<\frac7{900}+\frac2{2900}+\frac{17}{3\cdot780}+\frac1{780^2}
=\frac{6169}{392080}<\frac{79}{5000},\\
A_1
&<\frac{1313893}{437018452}<\frac{31}{10000},
\qquad
\frac1{(1-q)^2}<\frac{1001}{1000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

For clarity, the displayed bound for \(A_1\) is obtained by substituting \(h=1/780\), \(q=1/2900\), \(a=20/3\), \(J_0=1/3+3/2900\), and \(J_1=1/9+9/2900\) into its positive expression in (13); its exact rational value is the fraction in (15). It follows that \(\|\kappa_{1/6}\|_2^2<189189/10000000<(69/500)^2\).

Termwise, the triangle inequality gives \(\Theta(r+\delta)\le e^{|\delta|}\Theta(r)\). Equation (9) and the elementary series bound \(e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}\) for \(0\le x<1\) therefore imply

\[
\|\kappa_b\|_2
\le e^{4/75}\|\kappa_{1/6}\|_2
<\frac{75}{71}\frac{69}{500}
<\frac{73}{500}.
\tag{16}
\]

The remaining factorial estimate is also elementary. For an integer \(N\ge1\), monotonicity of \(\log x\) gives \(\log(N!)\le\int_1^N\log x\,dx+\log N=N\log N-N+1+\log N\). Hence \((2n)!\le2en(2n/e)^{2n}\). Combine this with (9), (11), (16), and the exact formula for \(w_{\mathbf0}\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac W{w_{\mathbf0}}
&\le2en\left[
e^{8R/n-2}\,3\|\kappa_b\|_2
\left(\frac n{4L_0}\right)^2
\right]^n\\
&\le2en\left[\frac{1971}{8000}e^{52/25}\right]^n
\le2en\left(\frac{199}{100}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

To verify the last constant without decimal approximation, the series for \(e\) gives \(e<\sum_{j=0}^5 1/j!+(1/6!)/(1-1/7)=11743/4320<68/25\). Also \(e^{2/25}\le25/23\). Thus \(e^{52/25}=e^2e^{2/25}<4624/575\), and \((1971/8000)(4624/575)<199/100\). This proves (4).

We now use the accepted results with the alias map in the statement. In equation (1) of \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\), choose its set \(E=\{\mathbf0\}\) and its target \(\mathbf j=\mathbf0\). The positive volume just proved ensures that this is an allowed choice. Its marginal depth \(\tau_E(\mathbf0)\) is exactly \(w_{\mathbf0}\), since every closed base halfspace containing zero includes that singleton. Its barycentric root ratio \(\vartheta_{\mathbf0}(E)\) is exactly one. Its actual fiber centroid is (5), and its dimension is \(2n\). That accepted inequality consequently gives \(h_S(\mathbf p_0)\ge\gamma_{2n}w_{\mathbf0}\), where \(\gamma_{2n}=(2n/(2n+1))^{2n}>e^{-1}\). This proves the first part of (6); dividing (17) by \(2^n\) proves the second. The selected point is independent of the halfspace. The accepted result quantifies over all closed ambient halfspaces, and all other fiber contributions remain nonnegative.

For \(n\ge10000\), equation (17) gives
\(T/w_{\mathbf0}\le2en(199/200)^n\le2en e^{-n/200}<6n e^{-n/200}\).
The function \(x e^{-x/200}\) is decreasing for \(x\ge200\), so this last expression is at most \(60000e^{-50}<60000/2^{50}<1\). This proves \(w_{\mathbf0}>T\) and the stronger explicit-centroid bound stated after (6). For \(100\le n<10000\), apply the actual-body conclusion (4) of accepted fact \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\), with precisely this body and its full table. Its hypotheses hold because (1) is a convex body and \(w_{\mathbf0}>0\). It supplies one point with depth at least \(T/[288(n+1)]\ge T/2880000\). The large-dimensional centroid bound is also stronger than this last constant. This proves the asserted dimension-free family theorem. Finally \((200/199)^n/n\to\infty\), so (6) eliminates the proposed sequence for every sufficiently large \(m\), with an explicit point and every containing halfspace covered.

For a duplication check, the full section table in this proof is not an affine-root table of the already accepted profile specialization. On every integer base with all \(z_i\le0\), formula (3) gives \(w_{\mathbf z}=(L_0+\sum_i z_i)^{2n}/[3^n(2n)!]\) whenever \(L_0+\sum_i z_i>0\). The values at zero and at \(-\mathbf e_i\), all positive in the stated range, therefore force any purported positive affine root on the full positive support to be proportional to \(L_0+\sum_i z_i\). But for \(\mathbf z=\mathbf e_i\), writing \(a=t_i+t_{i-1}\) gives \(F_{\mathbf e_i}(\mathbf t)-F_{\mathbf0}(\mathbf t)=|1-b-a|-(b+a)\ge-1\), with a strict inequality for \(a<1-b\). Since \(L_0>1\), this strict region contains a positive-measure set on which both section radii are positive. Equation (3) thus gives \(w_{\mathbf e_i}<(L_0+1)^{2n}/[3^n(2n)!]\), contradicting the forced affine root. The substantive additional estimate here is (17), which controls the full forced normalization despite the changing positive-coordinate sections. It is then consumed by an existing accepted centroid theorem, rather than asserted to be a new general depth-transfer principle.
