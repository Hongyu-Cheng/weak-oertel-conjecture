## Statement

Let $L,E$ be finite nonnegative Borel measures supported on a common compact subset of a Euclidean space, with masses $A=L(\mathbb R^m)>0$ and $B=E(\mathbb R^m)$. The barycenters of the measures $L+\alpha E$, where $\alpha$ is Borel and $0\le\alpha\le1$, form a compact convex set $\mathcal B$. For any linear functional $u$, its support in direction $u$ is the unique real $R$ solving
$$
\int(u\cdot w-R)\,dL(w)+\int(u\cdot w-R)_+\,dE(w)=0.
$$
The minimum of $u\cdot w$ on $\mathcal B$ is the unique root obtained by replacing the positive part by $\min(u\cdot w-R,0)$. For $K=\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}L\cup\operatorname{supp}E)$,
$$
\operatorname{width}_u\mathcal B\le \frac{B}{A+B}\operatorname{width}_u K .
$$

## Proof

The measures $0\le\nu\le E$ form a weakly compact set of measures on the common compact support. Indeed their masses are bounded and the inequality is closed against nonnegative continuous tests. Each has a Borel Radon–Nikodym density in $[0,1]$ with respect to $E$. The barycenter of $L+\nu$ is continuous in the weak topology because the denominator is at least $A$ and the coordinate functions are continuous. Hence $\mathcal B$ is compact. For barycenters $x_1,x_2$ of $L+\nu_1,L+\nu_2$ with total masses $T_1,T_2$, use $\nu=a\nu_1+(1-a)\nu_2$ with $a=tT_2/[tT_2+(1-t)T_1]$. Its barycenter is $t x_1+(1-t)x_2$, so $\mathcal B$ is convex.

For any $R$, the maximum of the unnormalized numerator $\int(u\cdot w-R)\,d(L+\alpha E)(w)$ is the displayed left side, attained by $\alpha=\mathbf1_{\{u\cdot w>R\}}$. This function is continuous and decreases by at least $A$ times the increase in $R$, with opposite infinite limits, so it has one root. At the root all barycenter projections are at most $R$ and the threshold density attains equality. For the minimum use $\alpha=\mathbf1_{\{u\cdot w<R\}}$ and the same argument with the minimum numerator.

Every probability measure $(L+\alpha E)/(A+\int\alpha\,dE)$ dominates the same submeasure $L/(A+B)$. The residual measure has mass $B/(A+B)$ and support in $K$. Thus all barycenters lie in the translate of $(B/(A+B))K$ by the common first moment $\int w\,dL/(A+B)$, giving the width bound, including the singleton case $B=0$.
