## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with nonempty interior, and let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. For $a\in\mathbb R^d$ put $L_a(x)=1+a\cdot(x-y)$ and define
$$
E_K(y)=\inf_{\{a:L_a>0\ \text{on }K\}}\int_K L_a(x)^{-d-1}\,dx.
$$
The infimum is positive and attained at a unique admissible $a_*$, and $E_K(y)$ is the minimum $d$-volume of the images of $K-y$ under $w\mapsto w/(1+a\cdot w)$ over these $a$. For $L=L_{a_*}$ and $E=E_K(y)$,
$$
\int_K (x-y)L(x)^{-d-2}\,dx=0,\qquad
\int_K L(x)^{-d-2}\,dx=\int_K L(x)^{-d-1}\,dx=E.
$$
Thus $\rho=L^{-d-2}/E$ on $K$ is a probability density with barycenter $y$ and $\int_K L\rho\,dx=1$. Its restriction to $K$ extends to a smooth positive function on a neighborhood of $K$.

For every invertible linear $Q$ and every $b\in\mathbb R^d$, $E_{QK+b}(Qy+b)=|\det Q|E_K(y)$. If $K\subset H$ for another convex body $H$, then $E_K(y)\le E_H(y)$ with equality exactly when $K=H$. Writing $g_K=|K|^{-1}\int_K x\,dx$ for the ordinary centroid, $E_K(y)\le |K|$ with equality exactly when $y=g_K$.

## Proof

Translate the mark to the origin for the parameter analysis. The admissible set $\Omega=\{a:1+a\cdot x>0\text{ for all }x\in K\}$ is convex, contains $0$, and is open by compactness of $K$. Choose $r>0$ such that the closed ball of radius $r$ about $0$ lies in $K$. Testing the denominator at $-ra/\|a\|$ for $a\ne0$ gives $\|a\|<1/r$. For $V(a)=\int_K(1+a\cdot x)^{-d-1}\,dx$, differentiation on a parameter neighborhood with uniform positive lower bound on the denominator gives
$$
\nabla V(a)=-(d+1)\int_K x(1+a\cdot x)^{-d-2}\,dx,\qquad
D^2V(a)[b,b]=(d+1)(d+2)\int_K(b\cdot x)^2(1+a\cdot x)^{-d-3}\,dx.
$$
The Hessian is positive definite since a nonzero linear functional does not vanish on a set with nonempty interior.

Suppose $a_k\in\Omega$ tends to $a_0\in\partial\Omega$. The limiting affine function is nonnegative on $K$ and has a zero $x_0\in K$. In particular $a_0\ne0$. Put $\delta_k=1+a_k\cdot x_0>0$, which tends to zero. A fixed small $(d-1)$-dimensional disk $D$ about $0$ in $a_0^\perp$ lies in $K$, so $\operatorname{conv}(\{x_0\}\cup D)\subset K$. The parameterization $x=(1-t)x_0+tu$ for $u\in D$ has volume element $c\,t^{d-1}\,dt\,du$ with $c=\operatorname{dist}(x_0,a_0^\perp)>0$. In dimension one $D=\{0\}$ with unit zero-dimensional mass. Boundedness of $a_k$ and $D$ gives a constant $C>0$ with $1+a_k\cdot x\le\delta_k+Ct$ on the cone. For large $k$ the range $\delta_k\le t\le2\delta_k$ lies inside $(0,1)$, and its contribution to $V(a_k)$ is bounded below by a positive constant times $\delta_k^{d}\delta_k^{-d-1}=\delta_k^{-1}$. Thus $V(a_k)\to\infty$. Any minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence in the bounded closure of $\Omega$, and the limit is interior by this divergence. Continuity and strict convexity give the unique minimum, which is positive.

Restore the mark $y$. Stationarity gives the first integral identity. Multiplying it by $a_*$ and using $L=1+a_*\cdot(x-y)$ gives $\int_K(L-1)L^{-d-2}\,dx=0$, hence the other identities and the escort assertions. Positivity of $L$ on the compact body gives a neighborhood with the same positivity. For the volume identity, the inverse projective map is $v\mapsto v/(1-a\cdot v)$ on the image. The determinant of the derivative of $w\mapsto w/(1+a\cdot w)$ is $(1+a\cdot w)^{-d-1}$ by the rank-one determinant formula, giving the displayed objective by change of variables.

For affine covariance use $x'=Qx+b$ and the bijective parameter substitution $a=Q^{\mathsf T}a'$. For inclusion use the attained denominator on $H$, which is admissible on $K$. If $K\subsetneq H$, choose $x\in H\setminus K$ and an interior point of $H$. Points on the segment arbitrarily close to $x$ toward that interior point are interior to $H$ and lie outside the closed set $K$. Thus $H\setminus K$ has positive volume, and the strictly positive larger-body integrand has strictly larger integral on $H$ than on $K$. The integral on $K$ is at least $E_K(y)$. Finally $a=0$ has objective $|K|$, so equality $E_K(y)=|K|$ holds exactly when $a=0$ is the minimizer. The gradient at $a=0$ vanishes exactly when $y=g_K$, which is interior to $K$ because a nonconstant supporting functional has strict inequality from its maximum on a subset of positive volume.
