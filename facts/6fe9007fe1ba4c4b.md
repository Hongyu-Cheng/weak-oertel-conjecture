## Statement

Let \(r\ge1\), \(s\ge0\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers. Let \(D_{\rm core}\subset\mathbb Z^r\) be finite and nonempty, let \(h_{\rm core}:D_{\rm core}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) have minimum zero, let \(q_{\mathbf x}>0\) for every \(\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core}\), and fix a real \(\beta\ge0\). The notation \(q_{\mathbf x}\) denotes only these spatial weights. For each integer \(0\le U\le T\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{{\rm core},U}
&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t'): \mathbf x\in D_{\rm core},\ t'\in\mathbb Z,
\ h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)\le t'\le U\rbrace,\\
Z_{\rm core}(U)&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},U}}
q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t'},\qquad
p_{{\rm core},(\mathbf x,t')}^{(U)}
=\frac{q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t'}}{Z_{\rm core}(U)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For any finite probability law \(\rho\) in an ambient Euclidean space, write \(\tau_\rho(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\rho(H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed affine halfspaces with nonzero ambient normal, and write \(M_3(\rho)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\rho_{\mathbf z}\tau_\rho(\mathbf z)^3\).

Suppose \(\kappa_{\rm core}>0\) and, for every integer \(0\le U\le T\), the core depths in \(\mathbb R^{r+1}\) satisfy \(M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(U)})\ge\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+1)}\). For each \(i\in[s]\), let \(B_i\ge2\) be an integer and define \(g_i(0)=0\), \(g_i(1)=1\), \(g_i(2)=B_i\). Put \(\Gamma(\mathbf y)=\sum_{i=1}^s g_i(y_i)\), with the usual empty sum when \(s=0\). Define the combined original law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t):\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core},\
\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\\
&\hspace{8em}h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)+\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)\in A_T}q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t},
\qquad p_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)}=\frac{q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t}}Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Then its ambient closed-halfspace cubic moment in \(\mathbb R^{r+s+1}\) satisfies
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_{\rm ext}\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+s+1)},
\qquad c_{\rm ext}=\frac1{8\cdot9^4}=\frac1{52488}.
\tag{3}
$$
In fact, the core assumption is needed only at the finite cutoff set
$$
\mathcal U_T=\lbrace T-\Gamma(\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s,\ \Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T\rbrace
\subseteq\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace.
\tag{4}
$$
For \(s=0\), this set is \(\lbrace T\rbrace\). No convexity assumption on the abstract core or its weights is required.

As an application, let \(k\ge1\), \(s\ge0\), \(T\ge0\), and \(B\ge2\) be integers. For \(j\in[k]\), let \(C_{4,j}\ge2B-1\) be an integer and set
$$
g_j^{(4)}(0)=0,\quad g_j^{(4)}(1)=1,\quad
g_j^{(4)}(2)=B,\quad g_j^{(4)}(3)=C_{4,j}.
\tag{5}
$$
Take \(D_{\rm core}=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k\), \(r=k\), \(h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{j=1}^k g_j^{(4)}(x_j)\), and \(q_{\mathbf x}=1\) in (2), and adjoin any three-site potentials \(g_i=(0,1,B_i)\) as above. There is an absolute \(\kappa_{\rm mix}>0\), independent of all these parameters and of \(\beta\ge0\), such that \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\rm mix}8^{-(k+s+1)}\). One may take \(\kappa_{\rm mix}=c_{\rm ext}\kappa_{\rm four}\), where \(\kappa_{\rm four}>0\) is the universal coefficient specified in (7) of accepted fact `a764225201e4e7d7`. If all four-site potentials are \((0,1,2,4)\), one may instead take the explicit coefficient
$$
\kappa_{\rm mix,fixed}
=c_{\rm ext}\frac{2^{-96}}{448}
=\frac{2^{-96}}{3584\cdot9^4}.
\tag{6}
$$
These mixed laws are complete log-affine lattice tables of compact convex epigraph sets, including lower affine ranks. The symbols \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) keep their locked meanings and are not auxiliary notation in this theorem.

## Proof

Every core support in (1) is nonempty, because a site of energy zero contributes its atom at height zero. All supports and sums are finite, so their normalizers, and the combined \(Z\), are positive and finite. All depths below are the ambient depths just defined.

For each integer \(E\ge0\), define the core tail
$$
A_{\rm core}(E)
=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core}}q_{\mathbf x}
\sum_{t=h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)+E}^{T}e^{-\beta t}
=e^{-\beta E}Z_{\rm core}(T-E)\quad(0\le E\le T),
\tag{7}
$$
and set \(A_{\rm core}(E)=0\) for \(E>T\); an inner sum whose lower endpoint exceeds its upper endpoint is empty. This is a different tail from the scalar \(A(E)=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\) of accepted `cd9532b1d59c5977`: (7) also sums over the weighted core and shifts each site's own lower height. We use none of that predecessor's scalar-height moment conclusion. Identity (7) follows by the exact substitution \(t=t'+E\). In particular \(A_{\rm core}(E)>0\) for \(0\le E\le T\).

Use the following Boolean input from accepted `c13fd517ed177886`: for a downset \(\mathcal D\) of subsets of a finite ground set, the number of ordered disjoint pairs in \(\mathcal D^2\), including empty sets, is at least \(|\mathcal D|^{3/2}\). Its ground set will be the present \(L\), its downset the present \(\mathcal D_L(E)\), and its ordered count \(\mathfrak D(\mathcal D_L(E),\mathcal D_L(E))\) the present \(R_L(E)\). This includes the empty ground set. No actual-body assertion of that predecessor is used.

For \(L\subseteq[s]\), put \(\ell=|L|\), \(\mathbf y_L=\mathbf1_L\), and \(a_i=B_i-1\ge1\). For \(I\subseteq L\), put \(E_L(I)=\ell+\sum_{i\in I}a_i\). A three-site vector has the unique representation \(\mathbf y=\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I\), where \(L=\lbrace i:y_i>0\rbrace\) and \(I=\lbrace i:y_i=2\rbrace\subseteq L\), and its energy is exactly \(E_L(I)\). Thus the following partition uses all original mass exactly once:
$$
Z=\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}Z_L,\qquad
Z_L=\sum_{I\subseteq L}A_{\rm core}(E_L(I)).
\tag{8}
$$
The factor \(q_{\mathbf x}\) is retained in every term of this identity.

For an integer \(\ell\le E\le T\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_L(E)&=\lbrace I\subseteq L:\sum_{i\in I}a_i\le E-\ell\rbrace,
&m_L(E)&=|\mathcal D_L(E)|,\\
R_L(E)&=|\lbrace(P,Q)\in\mathcal D_L(E)^2:P\cap Q=\varnothing\rbrace|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The positivity of the increments makes this a downset. The accepted Boolean inequality gives \(R_L(E)\ge m_L(E)^{3/2}\). Associate to each pair its displacement \(\boldsymbol\delta=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q\). Its positive and negative coordinate supports recover \(P,Q\), so the displacement map is injective. Its image, denoted \(\mathcal R_L(E)\), therefore has cardinality \(R_L(E)\), is invariant under negation, and contains zero once. Directly from the three-site costs,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\boldsymbol\delta)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in P}a_i-|Q|\le E,\\
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L-\boldsymbol\delta)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in Q}a_i-|P|\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
Both endpoints are in the three-site box. These identities use the actual heterogeneous increments; no homogeneous-cost replacement is made.

We next prove the core-copy depth estimate. Fix \(L,E\) as in (9), put \(U=T-E\), and abbreviate \(\lambda_E=A_{\rm core}(E)/Z\). For each \(\boldsymbol\delta\in\mathcal R_L(E)\), the map
$$
\iota_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\mathbf x',t'')
=(\mathbf x',\mathbf y_L+\boldsymbol\delta,t''+E)
\qquad((\mathbf x',t'')\in A_{{\rm core},U})
\tag{11}
$$
has image in the original combined support. Indeed, (10) and \(t''\ge h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x')\) give the lower-height constraint, while \(t''+E\le U+E=T\). Distinct displacements give distinct three-site vectors, so these entire copies of the core support are pairwise disjoint. Their original atom masses satisfy exactly
$$
p_{\iota_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\mathbf x',t'')}
=\lambda_E p_{{\rm core},(\mathbf x',t'')}^{(U)}.
\tag{12}
$$
In particular, the zero displacement gives the sampled original root \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)\) for every \((\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},U}\), with the same mass formula.

Let \(H\) be any closed affine halfspace containing this root, written as \(H=\lbrace(\mathbf x',\mathbf y',t''):\boldsymbol\nu_x\cdot\mathbf x'+\boldsymbol\nu_y\cdot\mathbf y'+\nu_t t''\ge b_H\rbrace\) with nonzero ambient normal \((\boldsymbol\nu_x,\boldsymbol\nu_y,\nu_t)\). At least \(R_L(E)/2\) of the displacements satisfy \(\boldsymbol\nu_y\cdot\boldsymbol\delta\ge0\): negation exchanges positive and negative values, and all zero values, including the zero displacement, are retained. For every such displacement, all core atoms in
$$
K=\lbrace(\mathbf x',t''):
\boldsymbol\nu_x\cdot(\mathbf x'-\mathbf x)+\nu_t(t''-t')\ge0\rbrace
\tag{13}
$$
map under (11) into \(H\). Their defining functional at the image differs from its value at the root by the left side of (13) plus \(\boldsymbol\nu_y\cdot\boldsymbol\delta\), a nonnegative quantity. If \((\boldsymbol\nu_x,\nu_t)\ne0\), (13) is a closed core halfspace through \((\mathbf x,t')\), so its core mass is at least \(\tau_{p_{\rm core}^{(U)}}(\mathbf x,t')\). If this core normal is zero, (13) is the entire core ambient space and has core mass one, which is again at least that depth. This also covers normals that are constant on the core's affine hull.

Summing over the disjoint favorable copies, using (12), and then taking the infimum over \(H\), proves
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)
\ge\frac{R_L(E)}2\frac{A_{\rm core}(E)}Z
\tau_{p_{\rm core}^{(U)}}(\mathbf x,t').
\tag{14}
$$
No independence assertion about core coordinates or heights is used. All restrictions and favorable-displacement inequalities are weak, so boundary atoms belong to the counted halfspaces.

Cube (14), multiply by the exact original root mass (12), and sum over the entire capped core. The map from core atoms to these sampled roots is injective. It follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{(\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},T-E}}
p_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)}
\tau_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)^3\\
&\hspace{1em}\ge
\frac{R_L(E)^3A_{\rm core}(E)^4}{8Z^4}
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-E)})
\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+1)}}{8Z^4}
R_L(E)^3A_{\rm core}(E)^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
There are four factors of the same full original normalizer \(Z\), one from sampling the root and three from its depth.

For each \(L\), define its finite feasible threshold set and its stock by
$$
\mathcal E_L=\lbrace E_L(I):I\subseteq L,\ E_L(I)\le T\rbrace,
\qquad
V_L=\max_{E\in\mathcal E_L}R_L(E)^{3/4}A_{\rm core}(E),
\tag{16}
$$
with \(V_L=0\) if \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty. In the nonempty case choose one maximizing threshold and use (15) once. Its cutoff \(T-E\) belongs to (4), because \(E=E_L(I)=\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I)\) for some three-site vector. This proves the weaker cutoff hypothesis claimed in (4).

Distinct labels \(L\) have distinct sampled three-site roots \(\mathbf y_L\), even when their maximizing thresholds differ. Within a label only one threshold was chosen. Thus no sampled atom is counted twice when (15) is summed over the labels. Other original sampled atoms have nonnegative contributions. With \(\mathcal V_4=\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}V_L^4\), we conclude
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+1)}}{8Z^4}\mathcal V_4.
\tag{17}
$$
Copies used to bound different roots' depths may overlap; this causes no double counting of sampled roots in (17).

It remains to compare (8) with these stocks. For a fixed \(L\), list the subsets with \(E_L(I)\le T\) as \(I_1,\ldots,I_{N_L}\) in nondecreasing order of energy, breaking ties arbitrarily. All first \(j\) subsets belong to \(\mathcal D_L(E_L(I_j))\), including when energies tie. Hence (9) and (16) give
$$
V_L\ge R_L(E_L(I_j))^{3/4}A_{\rm core}(E_L(I_j))
\ge j^{9/8}A_{\rm core}(E_L(I_j))
\qquad(1\le j\le N_L).
\tag{18}
$$
The terms from other subsets in (8) have zero tail. As \(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-9/8}\le1+\int_1^{\infty}x^{-9/8}\,dx=9\), summing (18) yields \(Z_L\le9V_L\). If \(N_L=0\), both quantities are zero and this inequality still holds. Hölder's inequality on the \(2^s\) labels, together with (8), now gives
$$
Z^4\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}V_L\right)^4
\le9^4(2^s)^3\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}V_L^4
=9^4\,8^s\mathcal V_4.
\tag{19}
$$
The empty label has threshold zero, one ordered disjoint pair, and positive tail \(A_{\rm core}(0)=Z_{\rm core}(T)\). Therefore \(\mathcal V_4>0\). Combining (17)-(19) proves
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+1)}}{8\cdot9^4\cdot8^s}
=c_{\rm ext}\kappa_{\rm core}8^{-(r+s+1)},
\tag{20}
$$
as asserted.

For completeness, a label has no feasible threshold exactly when \(\ell>T\), since its least energy is \(E_L(\varnothing)=\ell\). Such a label contributes no mass and no sampled roots. The empty label always has a feasible threshold. When \(s=0\), it is the only label, (8)-(20) still apply, and the hypothesis directly also gives the stronger core bound at \(U=T\). When \(T=0\), the only feasible label is empty and the only core cutoff used is \(U=0\); the argument applies to all energy-zero core sites without requiring that there be just one. When \(\beta=0\), every exponential in the proof equals one and the same identities hold. No division by \(\beta\), strict energy comparison, or assumption of full affine dimension occurred. Thus singleton height intervals, lower affine ranks, all ties among the \(B_i\), and equality at every support or halfspace boundary are included.

We finish by checking the application. In accepted `a764225201e4e7d7`, replace its number of coordinates by the present \(k\), its spatial variable by the present \(\mathbf x\), its potential \(g_j\) by the present \(g_j^{(4)}\), and its top cap by \(U\). Its common \(B\), fourth costs \(C_{4,j}\), and \(\beta\) are exactly those in (5). Its original normalizer is then exactly \(Z_{\rm core}(U)\) in (1) with \(q_{\mathbf x}=1\), and its original law, ambient depths, and cubic moment are exactly \(p_{\rm core}^{(U)}\), \(\tau_{p_{\rm core}^{(U)}}\), and \(M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(U)})\). Its coefficient \(\kappa\), specified in its (7), is the present \(\kappa_{\rm four}\) and is independent of \(U\) and every displayed parameter. Its theorem therefore supplies all the core bounds required in (4). Equation (3) gives the claimed common-B mixed family.

For the fixed profile, apply accepted `dbcf5be1e541e0cf` with its \(k,T,\beta,g\) mapped respectively to the present \(k,U,\beta,g_j^{(4)}=(0,1,2,4)\). Again its full \(Z\) maps to \(Z_{\rm core}(U)\), and its original core law and depths are unchanged. Its stronger first inequality (3) gives
$$
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(U)})\ge\frac{2^{-96}}{3584}8^{-k}
=\frac{2^{-96}}{448}8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{21}
$$
Using this \(\kappa_{\rm core}\) in (3) proves (6). The \(Z\) throughout both mixed conclusions is still the single full mixed normalizer in (2); neither core normalizer replaces it.

Finally, the convex-geometric assertion in the application has a direct check. Linearly interpolate \(g_j^{(4)}\) on \([0,3]\); its slopes are \(1,B-1,C_{4,j}-B\), nonnegative and nondecreasing by (5). Linearly interpolate each \(g_i\) on \([0,2]\); its slopes are \(1,B_i-1\), also nonnegative and nondecreasing. On the product of these intervals the sum of the interpolated potentials is convex. Its epigraph cut by \(t\le T\) is a compact convex set whose lattice points are exactly the mixed support (2). At every such lattice point the weight is the same log-affine function \(e^{-\beta t}\). This establishes the complete convex epigraph interpretation for the mixed application, including degenerate caps. The abstract closure proved above required only its finite supports, positive weights, and specified core moment bounds, so it makes no convexity assertion about a general \(D_{\rm core},h_{\rm core},q_{\mathbf x}\).
