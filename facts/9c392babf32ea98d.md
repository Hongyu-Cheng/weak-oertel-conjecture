## Statement

In the barycentric affine space $\sum_i x_i=1$, let the reference $d$-simplex have vertex matrix $I$ and volume $V>0$. Put $m=d+1$ and fix caps $x_i\ge a_i$ with $0<a_i<1$. For a column-sum-one matrix $W$ near $I$, let $F_i(W)$ be the actual volume of its simplex in cap $i$. Then the total volume is $V\det W$, and
$$
F_i(W)=V\det W\frac{(W_{ii}-a_i)^d}{\prod_{j\ne i}(W_{ii}-W_{ij})}.
$$
For $d\ge2$, the Lagrangian $V\det W-\sum_i\ell_i F_i(W)$ is stationary at $I$ on the physical vertex tangent space, for real multipliers $\ell_i$, exactly when all $a_i$ equal one $a$ and $\ell_i=1/[d(1-a)^{d-1}]$ for every $i$. The caps cover the affine space exactly when $\sum_i a_i\le1$, and their boundaries are concurrent exactly when $\sum_i a_i=1$. In the concurrent stationary case $a_i=1/m$ and $\ell_i=1/(m\Gamma_d)$, with $\Gamma_d=(d/m)^d$. For $d=1$ all proper threshold pairs admit stationarity precisely with $\ell_0=\ell_1=1$.

In every dimension let $h_i=d(1-a_i)^{d-1}$. For $W=I+tB$ write $f_i(B)=F_i'(0)/V$. The map from column-sum-zero matrices $B$ to $f(B)$ has right inverse
$$
S(f)_{ii}=f_i/h_i,\qquad
S(f)_{ij}=f_i/h_i-\frac1d\sum_l f_l/h_l\quad(i\ne j).
$$
Its trace is $\sum_i f_i/h_i$. The cap derivative map consequently has no nonzero abnormal multiplier vector.

## Proof

A column-sum-one matrix fixes the quotient by the direction space $\sum_i x_i=0$, so its determinant on the direction space is $\det W$, positive near $I$. Near $I$ each fixed cap contains only vertex $i$. The cap is a simplex with edge ratios $(W_{ii}-a_i)/(W_{ii}-W_{ij})$ on edges from vertex $i$, which gives the volume formula.

For $W=I+tB$, let $T=\operatorname{tr} B$, $b_i=B_{ii}$, $s_i=\sum_{j\ne i}B_{ij}$, $\Gamma_i=(1-a_i)^d$ and $k_i=d/(1-a_i)-m$. Differentiating gives
$$
f_i(B)=\Gamma_i[T+s_i+(1+k_i)b_i].
$$
Each off-diagonal $B_{ij}$ is an independent velocity, and $b_j=-\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}$. Writing $\eta_i=\ell_i\Gamma_i$, the coefficient of $B_{ij}$ in the normalized Lagrangian derivative is $-1+\sum_l\eta_l-\eta_i+\eta_j(1+k_j)$. For $m\ge3$, comparing the zero coefficients for different $i$ at a fixed $j$, and varying $j$, makes all $\eta_i$ equal to some $\eta$. The equations become $1=\eta(m+k_j)=\eta d/(1-a_j)$, proving the common threshold and multiplier formula. Conversely those values make all coefficients zero. For $d=1$, the cap volume derivatives are $-V B_{10}$ and $-V B_{01}$, from the same formula, while the total volume derivative is $-V(B_{10}+B_{01})$. This gives the stated multipliers without any restriction on the threshold pair.

The cap boundaries meet exactly at the vector of thresholds when its sum is one. If their sum is at most one, no point can be strictly below all thresholds. If their sum exceeds one, subtract $(\sum_i a_i-1)/m$ from every threshold to obtain a point missing all caps. In particular equal thresholds $a<1/m$ give stationary nonconcurrent covers. When they concur at stationarity, $d(1-1/m)^{d-1}=m\Gamma_d$.

For the right inverse put $z_i=f_i/h_i$ and $Z=\sum_i z_i$. The column sums of $S(f)$ are zero, its trace is $Z$, and its off-diagonal row sums are $dz_i-Z$. Substitution into the derivative yields $\Gamma_i(m+k_i)z_i=h_i z_i=f_i$, proving the stated inverse and the abnormal-multiplier conclusion.
