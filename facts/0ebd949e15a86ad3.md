## Statement

Let $k,Q_i,\sigma_i>0$ for $1\le i\le3$, and put $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $T=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i^2$, $U=\prod_i Q_i/\sigma_i^2$, and $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_i M)$. Let $B$ be symmetric with zero diagonal and cyclic off-diagonal entries $f_i/2$, and let $J=\lambda_{\max}(B)$. For $r>0$ put $E_r=r^2M^2-kT$ and $R_r=r^2M^2E_r^2-4k^3U$. Then $J\le r$ exactly when $E_r\ge0$ and $R_r\ge0$, and $J<r$ exactly when both are positive.

For actual planar cap data from fixed nonzero normals through an interior point, along a quadrilateral path with one vertex moving affinely and all other vertices fixed, the two expressions are rational functions on each strict-incidence interval. On such intervals a fixed positive spectral level can be tested by polynomial inequalities after clearing denominators of known positive sign. Along a continuous positive-data path the boundary at level $r$ lies on $R_r=0$ with $E_r>0$.

## Proof

The determinant of $B$ is twice the product of its three positive off-diagonal entries, and its trace is zero. Thus it has one positive and two negative eigenvalues. For $r>0$, $\det(rI-B)$ has the sign of $r-J$. Direct expansion gives
$$M^3\det(rI-B)=rME_r-2k^{3/2}\sqrt U.$$
Since $U>0$, taking the nonnegative branch $E_r\ge0$ makes squaring equivalent, including strict signs. At equality the rightmost radical is positive, so $E_r>0$. For a clipping edge from $a$ to $b$ and normal $v$, the intersection with $v\cdot x=0$ is $a+[v\cdot a/(v\cdot(a-b))](b-a)$. On a fixed strict-incidence interval this expression is rational in the path parameter, as is the area by shoelace. A chord lies on a fixed line, so its length divided by $\lVert v\rVert$ is the absolute value of a rational signed displacement along a fixed perpendicular to $v$ divided by its squared norm. Its sign can be fixed on the interval because the chord is nondegenerate. This yields the rational tests, and continuity gives the crossing assertion.
