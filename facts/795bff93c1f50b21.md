## Statement

Let $n=d=1$, $\sigma=\sqrt{5/24}$, $\varepsilon,h>0$, $H=h\sigma$ and $Y=\sigma(\varepsilon+h)/2$, with $Y+H<1/2$. Take the actual hull of the intervals $K_{-1}=[-1,0]$, $K_0=[Y-1,Y+1]$, $K_1=[0,1]$ at bases $-1,0,1$. Its mixed mass is $4$ and its pooled within-fiber covariance is $\sigma^2$. For a source $(p,c+t)$ in a length-$L$ interval centered at $c$, use the tied profile $f_H=(L/4)\sqrt{1-(2t/L)^2}$. For continuous orientation $s=\pm1$ and arbitrary physical tilt $A$, the cap value is the mixed integral of
$$
\Psi\left(\frac{s(x-(c+t))+A(z-p)+\varepsilon\sigma f_H}{H}\right),\qquad
\Psi(q)=(q+1)_+-q_+ .
$$
Include closed pure-base caps with their ordinary masses. The unique globally deepest mark for the infimum over these caps is $(0,Y)$ with raw depth $2+Y$. Every positive-orientation tilt is minimizing there. For the untilted positive cap the lower and upper strip planes are $x=-H/2,+H/2$, and the normalized strip coarea masses on bases $-1,0,1$ are $\sigma/2,\sigma,\sigma/2$. The strip centroid has base zero. As $\varepsilon,h\to0$, including with $h=o(\varepsilon)$, the same cap and family tend to cut $x=0$ at source $(0,0)$, with outside support at base $-1$ and inside support at base $1$. The outside captured and inside missing boundary fractions are both $1/2$, the two balanced transition densities are both $1$, and the full normal quadratic comparison is equality in every direction.

## Proof

The midpoint of the endpoint intervals is $[-1/2,1/2]\subset K_0$, so the only three integer fibers of the hull are exactly those listed. Their lengths are $1,2,1$ and the within-fiber variances of intervals of length $L$ are $L^2/12$, giving $\Sigma=(1^3+2^3+1^3)/(12\cdot4)$. For the marked interval energy, with distances $l,r$ from an interior mark to the two endpoints, $\int_{-l}^r(1+a x)^{-2}dx=(l+r)/((1-al)(1+ar))$ on $-1/r<a<1/l$. Maximizing the denominator gives energy $4lr/(l+r)$ and the endpoint limit is zero. Thus the tied profile $\sqrt{L E}/4$ has the stated value, with $f_H(Y)=1/2$ and zero derivative at the middle center.

The outward ramp is the average of hard caps expanded by all displacements in $[0,H]$. At a source $c+t$, the orientation toward the nearer endpoint has own smoothed capture at most $L/2-|t|+\varepsilon\sigma f_H+H/2$ by the untruncated linear length bound, since the expansion starts from a cut at a mark in the interval. At the center, this is the exact value in both orientations, as $\varepsilon\sigma L/4+H<1/2$ for $L=1,2$. The strict dependence on $|t|$ makes it the unique own-depth maximum. At each outer base an exposing tilt isolates this own capture, strictly below $2+Y$. At a noncentral middle mark a sufficiently large finite tilt takes one outer interval whole, misses the other and leaves the chosen own capture unchanged. The resulting mass is strictly less than $2+Y$.

At $(0,Y)$ the positive-orientation margin in physical kernel coordinates is $x+Az-H/2$. The two outer arguments paired by $x\mapsto-x$ sum to $-1$, and $\Psi(q)+\Psi(-1-q)=1$. Thus outer capture is exactly $1$ for every positive tilt. The middle capture is $1+Y$. In negative orientation the margin is $-x+Az+Y+\varepsilon\sigma/2$; paired arguments sum to a nonnegative value, so their kernel values sum to at least one by monotonicity and the same reflection identity. Finite saturation attains one, and its middle capture is again $1+Y$. Pure-base caps contain the whole middle interval and one entire endpoint interval, of total mass at least $3$. This proves the all-ambient depth assertion. The untilted positive cap and a saturated negative tilt give a balanced two-cap menu, with opposite continuous normals and weights $1/2$; the profile has zero continuous gradient there.

At $x=0$ on both outer endpoint faces the positive untilted argument is $-1/2$. Thus its outside boundary capture and inside boundary omission are both $1/2$. The physical strip intersects the outer intervals in lengths $H/2,H/2$ and the middle in length $H$, so integration of $1/h$ over these portions gives the stated coareas, with base centroid zero. In fact the continuous strip centroid is also $0$, by reflection of the two outer strip pieces and the symmetry of the middle strip. At the lower endpoint plane the base $-1$ and middle sections are proper, and at the upper endpoint plane the middle and base $1$ sections are proper. Coarea per section point is $\sigma$ in covariance-normalized coordinates, and hence each outer transition density is one relative to this full coarea. The middle sections cancel. On the limiting cut plane every affine incidence centered at $(0,0)$ is $a'z$, where $a'$ is the base component of the variation; there is no transverse continuous normal in $d=1$. Both outer square contributions are $(a')^2$. Positive-width flatness in tilt proves equality for the actual normal Hessian directly: near the chosen positive normal, normalizing its positive continuous component leaves its orientation and converts every small variation to an arbitrary nearby tilt, whose mass is constant.
