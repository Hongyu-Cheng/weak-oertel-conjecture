## Statement

Let a bounded canonical polytope $C$ be full-dimensional in the relative base-ambient space, with finite lattice-complete projection sites $P$ and positive sampled fibers. Keep purely base facet inequalities fixed and apply arbitrary affine coefficient offsets and rotations to the other ambient facets, $(A_i+t\dot A_i)\cdot w\le b_i+t\dot b_i$ with $A_i=(a_i,u_i)$. For small signed $t$ this gives actual bounded compatible sampled fibers at precisely $P$, on a relative body at Hausdorff distance $O(|t|)$ from $C$. At a regular old sampled boundary point the right inward speed is the maximum of $(\dot A_i\cdot w-\dot b_i)/|u_i|$ over the active nonvertical constraints. Reversing the direction gives minus the minimum over the same active set.

For the paths above, set $n_i=u_i/|u_i|$, $g_i(w)=(b_i-A_i\cdot w)/|u_i|$ and $v_i(w)=(\dot A_i\cdot w-\dot b_i)/|u_i|$. Choose nonnegative continuous $\psi_g$ on the normal sphere interpolating the indicator of each distinct normal $n_g$ at the finitely many $n_i$. For $B>\max_{i,w\in C}|v_i(w)|$ and $T>0$, the fields
$$
\Phi_T(n,w)=\sum_g\psi_g(n)\max\bigl(-B,\max_{i:n_i=n_g}[v_i(w)-Tg_i(w)]\bigr)
$$
are globally finite and jointly continuous, signed and convex in $w$ for each $n$. Their evaluations at the actual old measured boundary normals are bounded by $B$ and converge almost everywhere, and in $L^1$ of the original summed boundary measure, to the exact right inward speed $v$. Hence every inequality $\int\Xi\Phi\,dS\le0$ for all such signed convex fields with one fixed integrable density $\Xi$ also gives $\int\Xi v\,dS\le0$. The same passage holds for each integrable linear boundary response density. Opposite directions give equality for that separator when the active owner is unique almost everywhere, and yield the two max/min inequalities in general.

## Proof

Work in the relative Euclidean ambient space. Boundedness of the original nonempty full-dimensional polytope means there is no common recession direction for its complete facet normals. Thus $\min_{|y|=1}\max_i A_i\cdot y>0$ and this positive bound persists for all sufficiently small $|t|$. The bounded perturbed thresholds then give a common bounding ball for the perturbed bodies. On a ball containing them and the old body the constraint coefficient errors are $O(|t|)$. With any old interior point as center, comparison with its finitely many strictly positive slacks gives inner and outer homotheties of the old body with ratios $1-O(|t|)$ and $1+O(|t|)$. For the outer inclusion use the old inequalities with errors bounded by $O(|t|)$ and dilate by more than those errors divided by the smallest old slack. The inner inclusion follows by contracting until the old slacks exceed the new errors. This proves the ambient Hausdorff bound.

At each $z\in P$ choose a continuous inball center. Every nonvertical row has strictly positive slack at this center, and vertical rows stay fixed, so the same argument gives two-sided $O(|t|)$ radial control on the sampled fiber about that center. All old sites persist. Each facet of the old base projection corresponds to an ambient purely base facet: the positive fibers above the affinely spanning vertices of the base facet give a supported face of dimension $(\dim\operatorname{aff}P-1)+d$. Keeping those inequalities and retaining the sites fixes the projection. At rank zero the projection is the fixed singleton. Lattice completeness gives exactly the old sampled sites. Convexity gives every anchored inclusion, and taking the lifted canonical hull keeps these same fibers.

At a regular old point $(z,x)$ all active nonvertical rows have continuous normal $n$ equal to the unique outward unit normal. Let $o_z$ be the old inball center of the fiber, and write its new radial endpoint as $x_t=x-s_t(x-o_z)$, where $|s_t|=O(t)$ for $t\downarrow0$. Each initially active row gives
$$
s_t\,n\cdot(x-o_z)\ge t\,v_i(z,x)+O(t^2).
$$
A new active nonvertical row exists at the endpoint, and for sufficiently small $t$ every such row was active at the old point, since the list is finite and the inactive slacks there are positive. Equality for this new row gives the opposite limiting bound with the largest old $v_i$. The denominator is positive by the old inball. Hence the radial limit is $v/[n\cdot(x-o_z)]$ with $v=\max_{i\in I(z,x)}v_i$. Negating the coefficient direction gives $-\min_{i\in I(z,x)}v_i$. The radial volume Jacobian is $n\cdot(x-o_z)$, so the uniform radial bound also gives the signed first inward volume loss $\int_{\partial K_z}v\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$ by dominated convergence. Lower-dimensional own faces have zero surface measure, and interval endpoints use counting measure.

The normal interpolants exist on the sphere because the distinct normals form a finite set. Each maximum in $\Phi_T$ is convex in $w$ as a maximum of affine functions and a constant. The resulting fields meet the hypotheses of 8820984cf6a6942e. At an old sampled regular boundary point only its normal group enters. All $g_i$ there are nonnegative, so the term is bounded between $-B$ and $B$. Terms from inactive rows tend to minus infinity as $T$ grows while each active term is exactly $v_i$. The limit therefore equals $v$ almost everywhere. Dominated convergence on the old finite surface measure gives the $L^1$ and integrable response conclusions. If there is a unique active row almost everywhere, opposite directions negate the speed and give equality under the fixed separator. For a tie the two values are the maximum and negative minimum, which gives the stated two inequalities.
