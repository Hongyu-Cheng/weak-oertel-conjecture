## Statement

For uniform probability on $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$, every closed halfspace containing the line $L=\{y=1-1/\sqrt2\}$ has mass at least $1/2$, optimal among all lines. Yet every point of $L$ has halfspace depth at most $(1-1/(2\sqrt2))^2=9/8-1/\sqrt2<4/9$. The same assertion holds for two identical copies of this measure.

## Proof

Put $h=1-1/\sqrt2$. The mass of $y\ge h$ is $(1-h)^2=1/2$ by similar triangles and the boundary is null, so $y\le h$ also has mass $1/2$. A halfspace containing the line contains one of these two halfspaces, and for any line its two opposite through-line halfspaces have combined mass one, proving optimality.

For $(x,h)\in T$ the barycentric coordinates $1-x-h$ and $x$ sum to $1/\sqrt2$, so some barycentric coordinate $\lambda_i$ is at least $1/(2\sqrt2)$. The closed vertex cap at that coordinate value has probability $(1-\lambda_i)^2$ by homothety. Outside $T$ strict separation gives depth zero. The claimed strict inequality is equivalent to $49/72<1/\sqrt2$ and follows by squaring, since $2401<2592$. The argument is unchanged for two identical copies.
