## Statement

Fix an integer \(T\ge1\). For each \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), let \(s_t\ge1\) be an integer, let \(P_t,Q_t\subseteq\mathbb R^{s_t}\) be nonempty compact convex sets, and suppose that
$$
C_t:=\operatorname{conv}(P_t\cup Q_t)\cap\mathbb Z^{s_t}
$$
is finite and nonempty. Put
$$
h:=\sum_{t=1}^T|C_t|,
\qquad
M:=\max\left\{2,\max_{1\le t\le T}|C_t|\right\},
$$
and define the positive constant
$$
c_M:=\frac14\exp\left(
-\frac{M^2\bigl((\log M)^2+2\bigr)}
{2\log\left(1+(2M^2)^{-2^M}/(4M^2)\right)}
\right).
\tag{1}
$$
This is exactly the constant \(c_M\) of accepted fact ad475d6d41d953b3.

For \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), define
$$
S_t(\lambda):=\lambda P_t+(1-\lambda)Q_t,
\qquad
A_t(\lambda):=S_t(\lambda)\cap\mathbb Z^{s_t},
\qquad
I_{t,x}:=\{\lambda\in[0,1]:x\in S_t(\lambda)\}
\quad(x\in C_t).
$$
Every \(I_{t,x}\) is a nonempty closed interval, with a singleton allowed. Let
$$
0=\eta_1<\eta_2<\cdots<\eta_m=1
$$
be the distinct points consisting of \(0,1\) and all endpoints of all intervals \(I_{t,x}\). Define
$$
\Gamma:=\{\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_m\}
\cup\left\{\frac{\eta_j+\eta_{j+1}}2:1\le j<m\right\},
\qquad
L:=|\Gamma|=2m-1\le4h+3.
\tag{2}
$$
The interval endpoints, the sample set \(\Gamma\), and \(L\) depend only on the fixed library.

For every integer \(k\ge1\) and every sequence \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), put
$$
N:=\sum_{i=1}^k s_{t_i},
\qquad
D:=\operatorname{conv}\left(
\left(\prod_{i=1}^kP_{t_i}\right)
\cup
\left(\prod_{i=1}^kQ_{t_i}\right)
\right)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
$$
Then the full census satisfies
$$
D=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_\gamma,
\qquad
E_\gamma:=\prod_{i=1}^k A_{t_i}(\gamma).
\tag{3}
$$
Components with an empty factor support may be discarded. Every remaining \(E_\gamma\) has nonempty complete factor supports, each of cardinality at most \(M\). The union is an ordinary set union and may overlap.

Assume \(D\ne\varnothing\). For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), retain the original law on every point of this entire support:
$$
Z:=\sum_{x\in D}e^{\ell\cdot x},
\qquad
p_x:=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}Z\quad(x\in D).
$$
For a set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), write \(p(B):=\sum_{x\in D\cap B}p_x\). For \(j\in D\), define the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its moments by
$$
\tau_p(j):=\inf\{p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\},
\qquad
M_r(p):=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^r.
$$
For every real \(r\ge2\),
$$
M_r(p)\ge c_M^{r/2}L^{-(r+1)}2^{-rN}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular,
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_M L^{-3}4^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_M^{3/2}L^{-4}8^{-N}.
\tag{5}
$$
Thus \(c_M(4h+3)^{-3}\) and \(c_M^{3/2}(4h+3)^{-4}\) are valid positive constants depending only on the library.

For the application, retain the standard basis vectors \(e_1,e_2,e_3\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(v=(1,1,3)\) from accepted fact 453e84ccd542fa05, and set
$$
C_0=\{0,e_1,e_2,e_3,v,(1,-1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\},
\qquad
P=\operatorname{conv}(C_0),
\qquad
Q=-P.
$$
For every \(k\ge1\), the entire coupled completion
$$
D_k:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(C_0^k\cup(-C_0^k)\bigr)
\cap\mathbb Z^{3k}
\tag{6}
$$
satisfies, under every original globally affine law on all of \(D_k\),
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_{15}\,31^{-3}4^{-3k},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{15}^{3/2}\,31^{-4}8^{-3k}.
\tag{7}
$$
Here \(c_{15}\) is (1) with \(M=15\). There is no restriction on \(k\) or the field. For every \(k\ge2\), \(D_k\) strictly contains the naive signed product \(C_0^k\cup(-C_0^k)\).

## Proof

For any two nonempty convex sets \(U,V\) in the same finite-dimensional space,
$$
\operatorname{conv}(U\cup V)
=\bigcup_{0\le\lambda\le1}\bigl(\lambda U+(1-\lambda)V\bigr).
\tag{8}
$$
Indeed, split a finite convex combination of points in \(U\cup V\) according to the two sets. If the total weight on \(U\) is \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), convexity averages the points in each set to points \(u\in U\) and \(v\in V\), giving \(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v\). If one total weight is zero, choose any point of its nonempty set for the unused term. This proves containment in the right-hand side, and the other containment follows from the definition of a convex hull.

For each library type, the set
$$
G_t:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{1\}\times P_t)\cup(\{0\}\times Q_t)\bigr)
$$
is convex. Applying the same splitting argument gives
$$
G_t=\{(\lambda,\lambda u+(1-\lambda)w):
0\le\lambda\le1,\ u\in P_t,\ w\in Q_t\}.
\tag{9}
$$
It is compact, since the displayed set is the continuous image of the compact set \([0,1]\times P_t\times Q_t\). For fixed \(x\in C_t\), the preimage of \(G_t\) under the affine map \(\lambda\mapsto(\lambda,x)\) is exactly \(I_{t,x}\); hence \(I_{t,x}\) is compact and convex in \([0,1]\). It is nonempty by (8) and the definition of \(C_t\). It is therefore a nonempty closed interval, possibly a singleton. This proves the interval assertion, including boundary and isolated-parameter membership.

There are at most \(2h\) interval endpoints before repetitions are removed, so \(m\le2h+2\). The \(m\) singleton endpoint states and \(m-1\) open interval states give (2). For every \(t\) and \(\lambda\),
$$
A_t(\lambda)=\{x\in C_t:\lambda\in I_{t,x}\},
\tag{10}
$$
because \(S_t(\lambda)\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(P_t\cup Q_t)\). On each open interval \((\eta_j,\eta_{j+1})\), membership in every \(I_{t,x}\) is constant: a change of membership would require an endpoint of that closed interval inside the open interval, contrary to the definition of the \(\eta_j\). Thus all the sets \(A_t(\lambda)\), simultaneously for all library types, are constant on each such open interval. At every singleton endpoint, \(\Gamma\) contains that endpoint itself. Consequently, for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), some \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) satisfies
$$
A_t(\lambda)=A_t(\gamma)\quad\text{for all }t.
\tag{11}
$$

The sets \(\prod_iP_{t_i}\) and \(\prod_iQ_{t_i}\) are nonempty and convex. For every fixed \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), coordinatewise selection gives
$$
\lambda\left(\prod_iP_{t_i}\right)
+(1-\lambda)\left(\prod_iQ_{t_i}\right)
=\prod_iS_{t_i}(\lambda).
\tag{12}
$$
For the reverse inclusion in (12), choose a representation in \(S_{t_i}(\lambda)\) separately for each coordinate block, and concatenate the chosen points in \(P_{t_i}\) and \(Q_{t_i}\). This uses the same fixed \(\lambda\) in every block and works also at \(0\) and \(1\). Combining (8), (12), and coordinatewise lattice membership gives
$$
D=\bigcup_{0\le\lambda\le1}\prod_iA_{t_i}(\lambda).
\tag{13}
$$
Equation (11) now reduces (13) exactly to (3). In particular, no point coming from an endpoint or an additional mixed block pattern has been omitted. Each \(S_t(\gamma)\) is compact and convex, and (10) gives \(|A_t(\gamma)|\le|C_t|\le M\). These are complete local lattice censuses. None of this argument assumes that the sets or their lattice supports have full affine dimension.

Discard the empty \(E_\gamma\) in (3), and let \(J\le L\) be the number left, with repetitions retained if they occur. Since \(D\ne\varnothing\), \(J\ge1\). Because their union is \(D\),
$$
\sum_{\gamma:E_\gamma\ne\varnothing}p(E_\gamma)
=\sum_{x\in D}p_x
\sum_{\gamma:E_\gamma\ne\varnothing}\mathbf 1_{E_\gamma}(x)
\ge\sum_{x\in D}p_x=1.
\tag{14}
$$
Thus some nonempty component \(E=E_\gamma\) has original-law mass
$$
\alpha:=p(E)\ge J^{-1}\ge L^{-1}.
\tag{15}
$$
Overlaps only increase the left-hand side of (14); no partition or disjoint mixture is used.

Decompose the original field according to the displayed coordinate blocks as \(\ell=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k)\), with \(\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{s_{t_i}}\). Write
$$
Z_i:=\sum_{z\in A_{t_i}(\gamma)}e^{\ell_i\cdot z},
\qquad
q_i(z):=\frac{e^{\ell_i\cdot z}}{Z_i}.
$$
Every factor is nonempty because \(E\ne\varnothing\), so these normalizers are positive. Let \(q=p(\,\cdot\mid E)\), and define \(\tau_q\) and \(M_r(q)\) by the same formulas with support \(E\) and law \(q\). This conditional law has the exact identities
$$
Z_E:=\sum_{x\in E}e^{\ell\cdot x}=\prod_{i=1}^kZ_i,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{Z_E}{Z},
\qquad
q_x=\frac{p_x}{\alpha}
=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z_E}
=\prod_{i=1}^kq_i(x_i)
\quad(x\in E).
\tag{16}
$$
In particular, the original law \(p\) still uses its original full-support normalizer \(Z\), and \(q\) is precisely its conditional law, with no alteration of the field.

Apply accepted fact ad475d6d41d953b3 with the following exact substitutions into its notation:
$$
n_i=s_{t_i},\quad Q_i=S_{t_i}(\gamma),\quad
A_i=A_{t_i}(\gamma),\quad
\boldsymbol\ell_i=\ell_i,\quad p_i=q_i,
\quad A=E,\quad p=q.
\tag{17}
$$
The predecessor's block count \(k\), total displayed dimension \(N\), and integer cardinality bound \(M\) are exactly ours. Its \(c_M\) is (1), and its depth uses exactly the same ambient closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\). The completeness, nonemptiness, compact convex factor sets, positive block dimensions, and cardinality hypotheses were all checked above. The theorem therefore gives
$$
M_2(q)\ge c_M4^{-N}.
\tag{18}
$$
For every real \(r\ge2\), Jensen's inequality applied under the probability law \(q\) to the convex function \(u\mapsto u^{r/2}\) on \([0,\infty)\) gives
$$
M_r(q)
=\mathbb E_q[(\tau_q^2)^{r/2}]
\ge M_2(q)^{r/2}
\ge c_M^{r/2}2^{-rN}.
\tag{19}
$$

For every \(j\in E\) and every ambient closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(j\),
$$
p(H)\ge p(E\cap H)=\alpha q(H).
$$
Taking the infimum over exactly those halfspaces yields
$$
\tau_p(j)\ge\alpha\tau_q(j)\quad(j\in E).
\tag{20}
$$
Consequently,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_r(p)
&\ge\sum_{j\in E}p_j\tau_p(j)^r\\
&\ge\alpha^{r+1}\sum_{j\in E}q_j\tau_q(j)^r\\
&=\alpha^{r+1}M_r(q)
\ge L^{-(r+1)}c_M^{r/2}2^{-rN},
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
by (15) and (19). This proves (4), and (5) and the stated library-only constants follow. Singleton factor supports and lower-dimensional factors require no separate treatment, since they satisfy all hypotheses of ad475d6d41d953b3.

For the seed application, accepted fact 453e84ccd542fa05 establishes the exact identities
$$
P\cap\mathbb Z^3=C_0,
\qquad
\operatorname{conv}(P\cup(-P))\cap\mathbb Z^3
=C_0\cup(-C_0),
\qquad
C_0\cap(-C_0)=\{0\}.
\tag{22}
$$
Thus this one-type library has local census cardinality \(15\), and \(M=15\). Only these accepted seed geometry conclusions are used from 453e84ccd542fa05; no suspension dimension restriction is needed here. The equality
$$
P^k=\operatorname{conv}(C_0^k)
\tag{23}
$$
follows directly by expanding products of convex-combination weights: if each \(u_i\in P\) is a convex combination of points of \(C_0\), the product of those coefficients expresses \((u_1,\ldots,u_k)\) as a convex combination of tuples in \(C_0^k\). The reverse containment follows from convexity of \(P^k\). Equation (23) and its negative show that (6) is exactly the complete coupled support \(D\) of the theorem for \(k\) repetitions of this library type. It is nonempty because it contains zero.

The interval-state count improves here without any new lattice census. For every nonzero \(x\in C_0\), \(I_{1,x}\) contains \(1\), so its upper endpoint is \(1\) and it has at most one endpoint in \((0,1)\). For every nonzero \(x\in-C_0\), it contains \(0\) and has at most one endpoint in \((0,1)\). Since \(0\in P\cap(-P)\), its membership interval is all of \([0,1]\) and has no interior endpoint. There are fourteen nonzero points in (22), so the endpoint set has at most sixteen points including \(0,1\). Hence \(L\le31\). Substituting \(N=3k\), \(M=15\), and this bound on \(L\) into (5) proves (7).

Finally, the additional mixed-sign points can already be certified for two blocks. Define
$$
c_+:=\frac{v+e_3}{2}=(1/2,1/2,2),
\qquad
c_-:=\frac{e_1+e_2}{2}=(1/2,1/2,0).
$$
Both belong to \(P\), and \((c_+-c_-)/2=e_3\). Therefore
$$
(e_3,-e_3)
=\frac12(c_+,c_-)+\frac12(-c_-,-c_+)
\in\operatorname{conv}(P^2\cup(-P)^2)\cap\mathbb Z^6
=D_2.
\tag{24}
$$
The point in (24) does not belong to \(C_0^2\cup(-C_0^2)\), since \(e_3\in C_0\) and \(-e_3\notin C_0\). For \(k>2\), append zero blocks to both tuples in this convex representation; zero belongs to \(P\) and \(-P\). The resulting point \((e_3,-e_3,0,\ldots,0)\in D_k\) is again outside both naive signed products. Thus every \(k\ge2\) has additional lattice points, and the bounds above apply to their full completion through the exact cover (3).
