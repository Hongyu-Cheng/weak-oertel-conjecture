## Statement

Use the meanings of the parity defect and selected-row quantities in accepted fact ee640ebdfbcb632d, extended by indexing selected unordered pairs individually when several pairs have the same midpoint. Explicitly, let \(A\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, put \(K=2^n\), and let \(p\) be a positive probability law on all of \(A\). Assume every parity class has at most two atoms and the midpoint of every distinct same-parity pair belongs to \(A\). Write \(\mathcal P\) for these unordered pairs. For \(e=\{u_e,v_e\}\in\mathcal P\), put
$$
j_e=\frac{u_e+v_e}{2},\qquad
s_e=\frac{\sqrt{p_{u_e}p_{v_e}}}{p_{j_e}},
$$
and require \(0<s_e\le1\) for every \(e\in\mathcal P\), including unselected pairs. For a selection \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal P\), retain the center and the endpoints of its selected pairs in each auxiliary reflected row, so that
$$
\widehat H(x)=p_x+2\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal S\\j_e=x}}\sqrt{p_{u_e}p_{v_e}}.
$$
Define, with the sum over all \(K\) coordinatewise parity classes,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=K\sum_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^n}p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)^2-1,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x(K\widehat H(x)-1)^2,\\
\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}&=4K\sum_{e\in\mathcal S}s_e(1-s_e)p_{j_e}^2,\\
\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}&=\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}+2\mathcal D+\mathcal T_{\mathcal S},\qquad
\beta_{\mathcal S}=K\sum_{e\in\mathcal S}s_ep_{j_e}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
There is no absolute finite \(C\) such that \(\beta_{\mathcal S}\le C\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\) for all these objects. More precisely, for each integer \(q\ge4\), the finite construction below satisfies every displayed hypothesis, has \(\mathcal D=\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}=0\), has \(s_e=1\) for every selected pair, and obeys
$$
\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}>0,\qquad
\frac{\beta_{\mathcal S}}{\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}>\frac{2q+1}{12}.
$$
The support is midpoint-closed in exactly the required sense: whenever the midpoint of two supported atoms is an integer point, it belongs to the support. No convex-completeness or affine-Gibbs assertion is made.

## Proof

All weights constructed first will be dimensionless numbers \(t_x\). After the geometry and its dimension are fixed, the original law will be \(p_x=t_x/K\). This is the same normalization \(t_x=Kp_x\) used in the scalar-energy step of ee640ebdfbcb632d.

Fix \(q\ge4\), and set
$$
t_0=\frac1{2q+1},\qquad
L=\left\lceil\frac{2\log(1/t_0)}{\log(3/2)}\right\rceil.
$$
For \(0<t\le1/9\), define
$$
a(t)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,\qquad
d(t)=\left\lfloor\frac{1/t-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$
Then
$$
a(t)(1-a(t))=t^2,\qquad
t^2\le a(t)\le2t^2\le\frac2{81}<\frac14.
$$
Here the bound \(a(t)\le2t^2\) follows by rationalizing its defining expression. Consequently
$$
a(t)=\frac{t^2}{1-a(t)}\le\frac43t^2,
\qquad
d(t)\le\frac1{2t},\qquad
d(t)a(t)\le\frac23t.
\tag{1}
$$
Also \(d(t)\ge4\), and the floor definition gives
$$
0\le1-(1+2d(t))t<2t.
\tag{2}
$$
Define recursively
$$
t_{\ell+1}=a(t_\ell),\quad d_\ell=d(t_\ell),\quad
B_0=1,\quad B_{\ell+1}=B_\ell d_\ell
\qquad(0\le\ell<L).
$$
All these are exact finite definitions. The numbers \(t_\ell\) are positive, decrease, and are at most \(t_0\le1/9\). In particular, every \(d_\ell\) is a finite positive integer. There will be \(B_\ell\) tiny atoms at level \(\ell\). Every tiny atom at a level \(\ell<L\) is the center of \(d_\ell\) selected pairs. Each such pair has one tiny endpoint of weight \(t_{\ell+1}\) and one large endpoint of weight \(1-t_{\ell+1}\). The initial level-zero tiny atom is itself one endpoint of one additional, unselected seed pair, whose other endpoint has weight \(1-t_0\) and whose center has weight one.

Here is an explicit lattice realization of this finite incidence construction. The total number of unordered pairs, including the seed, is
$$
m=1+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}B_\ell d_\ell.
$$
Choose
$$
n=\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil,\qquad K=2^n,
$$
and list distinct nonzero Boolean vectors \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\in\{0,1\}^n\). There are enough because \(K-1\ge m\). The vector \(0\) is the seed center, with weight one. Create the seed endpoints
$$
c_1\quad\hbox{with weight }t_0,\qquad
-c_1\quad\hbox{with weight }1-t_0.
$$
The tiny endpoint \(c_1\) is the single level-zero center. Process the tiny centers successively by level. For each level-\(\ell\) tiny center \(j\), create its \(d_\ell\) pairs using the next unused labels \(c_r\). The two endpoints of the pair using label \(c_r\) are
$$
c_r\quad\hbox{with weight }t_{\ell+1},\qquad
2j-c_r\quad\hbox{with weight }1-t_{\ell+1}.
\tag{3}
$$
The Boolean endpoint \(c_r\) is the new tiny atom. Stop after creating level \(L\). Include every remaining Boolean vector as a singleton atom of weight one. Thus the support consists of the full Boolean cube together with one reflected partner \(2j-c_r\) for each pair; for the seed \(j=0\).

This construction is injective and has exactly the claimed parity structure. At the time label \(c_r\) is used, its center \(j\) is either zero or a previously created Boolean tiny endpoint. Hence \(j\ne c_r\). The partner \(2j-c_r\) has parity \(c_r\), differs from \(c_r\), and is not Boolean: a Boolean vector of parity \(c_r\) would have to equal \(c_r\). Partners from different pairs have different parity, so they are distinct. Therefore the parity class \(c_r+2\mathbb Z^n\) contains exactly the two endpoints of its assigned pair, while every other parity class contains exactly its Boolean representative. These statements also show that every atom receives exactly one weight assignment.

Each assigned pair has its prescribed midpoint \(j\) in the support. Conversely, any distinct supported points with integer midpoint have equal parity and therefore constitute one of these assigned pairs. This proves finite midpoint closure and the bound of two atoms in each parity class, with no implicit additional midpoint pairs. Different selected pairs at the same tiny center are precisely the intended collisions.

Every parity class has total dimensionless weight one: the two weights of each used class sum to one, and an unused class has its single weight-one atom. Thus
$$
\sum_{x\in A}t_x=K.
$$
Define the full original law \(p_x=t_x/K\) on every atom of \(A\). It is positive and sums to one. Every parity mass is exactly \(1/K\), giving \(\mathcal D=0\).

Select all constructed pairs except the seed. For each selected pair centered at a level-\(\ell\) tiny atom,
$$
\sqrt{p_{u_e}p_{v_e}}
=\frac{\sqrt{t_{\ell+1}(1-t_{\ell+1})}}K
=\frac{t_\ell}K=p_{j_e}.
$$
Hence \(s_e=1\). The sole unselected seed pair also satisfies the required original midpoint inequality, since its center has dimensionless weight one and
$$
s_{\mathrm{seed}}=\sqrt{t_0(1-t_0)}<1.
$$
These are all distinct same-parity pairs, so the full original law satisfies every required midpoint-log-concavity inequality, including the unselected one. In particular \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}=0\).

At a level-\(\ell\) selected center \(j\), all its \(d_\ell\) incident pairs are selected and have ratio one. Thus
$$
K\widehat H(j)=(1+2d_\ell)t_\ell.
$$
Every other auxiliary row is its singleton, so \(K\widehat H(x)=t_x\) there. In particular this is so at the weight-one seed center: its genuine original seed pair is retained in the support and law, while the auxiliary row selection omits that pair.

Put \(M_\ell=B_\ell t_\ell\), the total dimensionless weight of the level-\(\ell\) tiny atoms. From (1),
$$
M_{\ell+1}\le\frac23 M_\ell,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}M_\ell\le3t_0,
\qquad
M_L\le t_0(2/3)^L\le t_0^3.
\tag{4}
$$
The energy can now be bounded by a direct disjoint accounting of all original atoms. Multiplying its deviation part by \(K\) gives
$$
K\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}
=\sum_{x\in A}t_x(K\widehat H(x)-1)^2.
\tag{5}
$$
Every selected center of dimensionless weight \(t\) contributes less than \(4t^3\), by (2). Its \(d(t)\) newly created large endpoints together contribute at most
$$
d(t)(1-a(t))a(t)^2
\le\frac1{2t}(2t^2)^2=2t^3.
$$
Summing these two contributions over all selected centers and using (4) gives
$$
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}6B_\ell t_\ell^3
\le6t_0^2\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}M_\ell
\le18t_0^3.
$$
The large endpoint of the seed contributes
\((1-t_0)t_0^2\le t_0^2\). The terminal tiny endpoints have singleton auxiliary rows and contribute in total at most \(M_L\le t_0^3\). The seed center and all padding singletons have dimensionless weight one and contribute zero. This exhausts the support, since selected centers are precisely nonterminal tiny endpoints and every large endpoint lies outside the Boolean cube. As \(\mathcal D=\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}=0\), (5) proves
$$
0<K\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}
=K\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}
\le t_0^2+19t_0^3
\le\frac{28}{9}t_0^2<4t_0^2.
\tag{6}
$$
Strict positivity follows already from the large seed endpoint.

On the other hand, all selected ratios are one, and \(d_0=q\). Therefore
$$
K\beta_{\mathcal S}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}B_\ell d_\ell t_\ell^2
\ge q t_0^2
=\frac{q}{2q+1}t_0
\ge\frac{t_0}{3}.
\tag{7}
$$
Combining (6) and (7) gives
$$
\frac{\beta_{\mathcal S}}{\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}
>\frac1{12t_0}=\frac{2q+1}{12},
$$
which tends to infinity.

For completeness, the unselected seed is fully consistent with the exact selected-energy identity. With the notation of ee640ebdfbcb632d, set
$$
A_{\mathcal S}=K^2\sum_xp_x\widehat H(x)^2,\qquad
\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}=K\sum_{e\notin\mathcal S}s_e^2p_{j_e}^2.
$$
The same identity remains true with repeated midpoints:
$$
A_{\mathcal S}=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}-4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
$$
Indeed,
\(\sum_\eta p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)^2=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathcal P}s_e^2p_{j_e}^2\),
\(\sum_xp_x\widehat H(x)=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathcal S}s_ep_{j_e}^2\), and expanding (5) with these two equalities proves it. Here the sole omitted pair gives exactly
$$
\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}=\frac{t_0(1-t_0)}K.
$$
Thus no original seed atom or unselected-pair effect was suppressed in computing the prescribed energy. The obstruction concerns this selection and this precise charge. It does not address a replacement charge that includes an omitted-pair penalty, or an approach that adds convex-completeness or affine-Gibbs restrictions. All construction parameters are finite explicit formulas; no computational search or geometric-realization assumption is used.
