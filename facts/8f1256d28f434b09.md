## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a centrally symmetric convex body with center $0$, and let $K=\{(X,z):a\le z\le b,\ X\in r(z)D\}$, with $a<0<b$ and $r$ positive and concave. For a fixed horizontal unit vector $e$, write $x=e\cdot X$. There is a probability law $\mu$ on radii $R>0$ under which the projection $e\cdot U$ of uniform $U$ on $D$ is a mixture of the uniform laws on $[-R,R]$. The layer of radius $R$, in coordinates $x=Ry$, has density
$$
f(y,z)=\frac12 r(z)^{m-1}\mathbf1_{\{a\le z\le b,\ |y|\le r(z)\}}
$$
after dividing ordinary volume on $K$ by $|D|$. Its mass is $V=\int_a^b r(z)^m\,dz$. Let $P(s)$ be the cap mass of $z+sy\ge0$ under this density. Let $C(s)$ and $M(s)$ be its section integrals of $f$ and $zf$ over $z+sy=0$, with line measure divided by $\sqrt{1+s^2}$. For any finite $t\in\mathbb R$, the cap mass, raw section coarea, and raw vertical section first moment of $z+tx\ge0$ in $K$, divided by $|D|$, are the $\mu$-averages of $P(tR),C(tR),M(tR)$. Raw section integrals in $K$ use $\mathcal H^m/\sqrt{1+t^2}$. In particular full centering of the actual section implies $\int M(tR)\,d\mu(R)=0$.

## Proof

The projection of uniform $U$ on $D$ has an even density $q$. When $m=1$ it is uniform on an interval. When $m\ge2$ its value at $x$ is the $(m-1)$-volume of the section at $x$, divided by $|D|$. Symmetry makes $q$ even, and Brunn–Minkowski makes its $1/(m-1)$ power concave on the projection, hence $q$ nonincreasing on the positive half. The density given by these actual sections is upper semicontinuous: for $x_j\to x$, the limsup of the section indicators in perpendicular coordinates is bounded by the indicator of the section at $x$ because $D$ is closed, and the indicators have a common bounded support. Also $q$ is continuous near $0$ by concavity of its positive root. For $0<v<q(0)$ the superlevel set $\{q\ge v\}$ is a centered interval $[-R_v,R_v]$ of positive radius. The layer-cake identity gives $q(x)=\int_0^{q(0)}\mathbf1_{\{|x|\le R_v\}}\,dv$. Integrating in $x$ shows that $2R_v\,dv$ has total mass one, and its pushforward by $v\mapsto R_v$ gives the stated law. For $m=1$ use the single radius of the interval.

After dividing by $|D|$, the projected volume density in $(x,z)$ is $r(z)^{m-1}q(x/r(z))$ on $a\le z\le b$. In radius $R$ it is $r(z)^{m-1}/(2R)$ on $|x|\le Rr(z)$. The change $x=Ry$ gives $f$ and converts the cap inequality to $z+tRy\ge0$. Integrating gives the mass identity and the cap average.

For $t\ne0$, the original section is the graph $x=-z/t$ over $z$ and $X-xe$. Its area element divided by $\sqrt{1+t^2}$ is $dz\,d(X-xe)/|t|$. Integrating over the perpendicular coordinates and dividing by $|D|$ yields the integral of $r(z)^{m-1}q(-z/(t r(z)))/|t|$ over $[a,b]$, with an extra factor $z$ for the moment. Layering $q$ puts $|z|\le |t|Rr(z)$ in the integration domain and changes the denominator to $2|t|R$. These are exactly the fixed-line section integrals for slope $tR$. Tonelli applies to the coarea and to the positive and negative parts of the moment. For $t=0$ the coarea divided by $|D|$ is $r(0)^m$ in the original section and in each layer, and the moment is zero. The last implication follows by integrating the vertical coordinate over a section whose centroid is zero.
