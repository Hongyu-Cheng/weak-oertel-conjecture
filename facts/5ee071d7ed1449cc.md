## Statement

Let $K_z$ be a finite complete compatible family of positive $d$-volume compact convex fibers over integer bases, with summed volume measure $\mu$, $d\ge1$. Let $Q>0$ be its attained maximum raw closed-halfspace depth over the mixed set, and let $D_p=\{x:h_\mu(p,x)\ge Q\}$ at a deepest base $p$. For $y\in D_p$ write $a$ for its unique minimizing projective parameter in
$$
E_{K_p}(y)=\min_{a:\,1+a\cdot(x-y)>0\ \forall x\in K_p}\int_{K_p}[1+a\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-1}\,dx.
$$
For each $b\in\mathbb R^n$ and unit continuous $u$, define the rightmost raw $Q$ quantile $\tau_{b,u}=\sup\{t:\mu\{b\cdot z+u\cdot x\ge t\}\ge Q\}$ and $c_{b,u}=\tau_{b,u}-b\cdot p-u\cdot y\ge0$. Then
$$
D_p-y=\bigcap_{b,u}\{h:u\cdot h\le c_{b,u}\}.
$$
The active indices $c_{b,u}=0$ are precisely the actual nonvertical minimizing caps through $(p,y)$ and have proper own sections. With the outward normal cone convention,
$$
y\in\operatorname{argmax}_{x\in D_p}E_{K_p}(x)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\in N_{D_p}(y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(a,0)\in\overline{\operatorname{cone}}\{(u,c_{b,u}):b\in\mathbb R^n,\ \|u\|=1\}.
$$
In particular an interior point of $D_p$ maximizes energy only at the own-fiber centroid; a singleton places no restriction on the optimizer vector.

The cone condition is equivalent to certificates with at most $d+1$ actual quantile caps for every $\varepsilon>0$:
$$
\lambda_j\ge0,\qquad
\left\|a-\sum_j\lambda_j u_j\right\|\le\varepsilon,\qquad
0\le\sum_j\lambda_j c_j\le\varepsilon.
$$
If a sequence of certificates with $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ has bounded total multiplier weight, then $a$ is a nonnegative combination of at most $d$ genuine critical normals. A Slater ball in $D_p$ ensures this boundedness. A finite local definition of $D_p$ by actual quantile inequalities also gives the exact representation. Conversely, if genuine critical continuous normals positively span $\mathbb R^d$, then $D_p=\{y\}$ and every vector has such an exact representation.

## Proof

At base $p$, the conditions imposed by pure-base halfspaces are independent of the continuous coordinate and already satisfied at $(p,y)$. Every nonvertical containing cap can be reduced to the cap through the tested point without increasing its measure. For a fixed direction and tilt the tail is continuous and nonincreasing from the total mass to zero, since all nonvertical boundaries are mixed-null. The through-point cap has mass at least $Q$ exactly when its threshold is at most the rightmost $Q$ quantile. This proves the claimed intersection for all continuous marks. A point outside $K_p$ has zero depth: the anchored convex hull of the family is compact, has $p$-fiber exactly $K_p$ by compatibility, and a separating cap through the exterior point misses it. Fact 55a57b1cf55d0c87 gives own-interiority at positive depth. Thus if a nonvertical through-point cap has mass $Q$, its own trace is proper and its tail strictly decreases locally at that level. It is therefore the rightmost quantile. Conversely any active quantile gives such a cap. The same proper-section argument forbids strict inclusion of the mark by any cap of mass $Q$.

The gradient of energy is $(d+1)E_{K_p}(y)a$ by a56f8711406a3870. Differentiating along feasible segments gives normal-cone necessity, and concavity of $E_{K_p}^{1/d}$ from 5aeb5916857f97a9 gives sufficiency by the tangent inequality. If $a=0$, the first derivative in the minimizing parameter at zero is proportional to the first moment about $y$, so $y$ is the centroid.

For the conic characterization, let $G=\{(u,c_{b,u})\}$. If $(a,0)$ is a limit of finite conic combinations of $G$, testing them on $(h,-1)$ for feasible $h$ gives $a\cdot h\le0$. Conversely, if $(a,0)$ is outside the closed cone, separation gives $(h,s)$ with $a\cdot h>0$ and $u\cdot h+s c_{b,u}\le0$ for all indices. For $s\ge0$ the point $h$ is feasible, and for $s<0$ the point $h/(-s)$ is feasible. Both contradict the normal-cone condition. Conic Carathéodory in $\mathbb R^{d+1}$ proves the approximate cardinality.

Take a sequence of bounded-weight certificates and subsequences of their coefficients and unit normals, padding with zero coefficients if needed. Every positive limiting coefficient has $c_j\to0$. For each such slot use 0a73ba4a9c7b24d1 on the sequence of actual quantile caps, with affine thresholds $\tau_j-b_j\cdot z$ and a fixed ball containing all sampled fibers. This produces a finite affine threshold with the same limiting mass $Q$ and the same limiting own threshold $u_j\cdot y$, because that finite evaluation is preserved by the compactification. Its own section is proper, so it is a genuine active quantile cap. The terms with coefficients tending to zero have vanishing normal contribution. Conic Carathéodory in $\mathbb R^d$ now gives at most $d$ normals. A Slater ball $w+rB_d\subseteq D_p$ gives $c_j-u_j\cdot(w-y)\ge r$ for each normalized inequality, and therefore $r\sum_j\lambda_j\le\sum_j\lambda_jc_j-(\sum_j\lambda_j u_j)\cdot(w-y)$, which stays bounded. In the finite local case, the normal cone is the conic hull of the finitely many active normals: if a vector is outside that closed polyhedral cone, a separating velocity satisfies all active inequalities to first order and is feasible along a short segment because all other finitely many inequalities are strictly slack. Finally if the active normals positively span, their inequalities already force $h=0$, and the full cone contains every vector.
