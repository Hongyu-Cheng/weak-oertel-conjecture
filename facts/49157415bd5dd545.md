## Statement

On the area-one triangle $C=\{(v,w):0\le v\le1,\ |w|\le1-v\}$, fix the continuous normal span to the $v$ axis and use exactly two caps with unit normals covering the entire line in that span. Let $h=1-1/\sqrt2$. The minimizing caps $v\ge h$, $v\le h$ have masses $1/2$ and hinges $\Psi_+=1/(6\sqrt2)$, $\Psi_-=2/3-5/(6\sqrt2)$, where a hinge is the area integral of the positive part of its cap margin. The minimum total hinge under individual mass budget $\tau$ is $(2-\sqrt2)/3$ for $\tau\ge1/2$, and smaller budgets are infeasible. At interior budgets $1/2<\tau<1$ these caps have objective-one stress with budget multipliers zero, coverage multiplier one, circuit weights $(1/2,1/2)$ and contact $(h,0)$.

The projected density is $2(1-v)$ on $[0,1]$, with layer representation $2\int_0^1\mathbf1_{[0,1-t]}(v)\,dt$. Retain the cap thresholds $h$ and normalize each proposed single nonzero uniform level to total mass one. None preserves both the zero cap-normal force and the scalar tensor $(2-\sqrt2)/3$. The same failure holds for any uniform probability on an interval contained in $[0,1]$.

## Proof

The $w$-section length is $f(v)=2(1-v)$ on $[0,1]$, and its integral is one. Its upper tail at $h$ is $(1-h)^2=1/2$, so $h$ is the unique median. Any two unit-normal caps covering the line have opposite normals and margins $v-a$, $b-v$ with $a\le b$. For every $v$, their two positive parts sum to at least $|v-(a+b)/2|$. Integrating gives a lower bound by the mean absolute deviation about the median, since its derivative as a function of the center is $2\Pr(V\le x)-1$. Taking $a=b=h$ attains this bound. A union bound rules out $\tau<1/2$. For the minimizing pair, integration gives $\Psi_+=\int_h^1 2(1-v)(v-h)\,dv=(1-h)^3/3=1/(6\sqrt2)$. Since $\Psi_+-\Psi_-=\mathbb E V-h=1/3-h$, their displayed values and sum follow.

For the objective-one stress, the signed coverage constraint is $(a-b)/2\le0$, since $\min_v\max(v-a,b-v)=(b-a)/2$. The objective derivatives in $(a,b)$ at $(h,h)$ are $(-1/2,1/2)$, so multiplier one balances them, and the budget multipliers vanish by slackness. The two normals average to zero with weights $1/2$, the contact has both margins zero, and each continuous cap moment is its mass times $(h,0)$ plus $\Psi_\pm u_\pm$, with $u_\pm=(\pm1,0)$. The second coordinate vanishes by reflection. These are the mass and moment balances of the specified objective-one stress, and the value is constant on the interior feasible budget range. In particular the force of the cap-labeled source is $0$ and its tensor in the $v$ coordinate is $\int v U\,d\nu=\Psi_++\Psi_-$, where $\nu$ is the two cap restrictions and $U$ is the label $+1$ or $-1$.

For $0\le v\le1$ the layer integral is $2\int_0^{1-v}dt=2(1-v)$. Any uniform interval probability with zero force has equal masses on the two sides of $h$, so its midpoint is $h$. A layer interval must then have length $2h=2-\sqrt2$. More generally an interval contained in $[0,1]$ with midpoint $h$ has length at most $2h$. Its scalar tensor at zero force equals its mean absolute deviation from $h$, equal to one quarter of its length. Thus it is at most $(2-\sqrt2)/4$, which is less than the original by $(2-\sqrt2)/12$. The two selected hinges of a bisected uniform interval are equal, whereas $\Psi_+-\Psi_-=\mathbb EV-h\ne0$. If total mass is free, multiplying the balanced level probability by $4/3$ keeps its force zero and restores the sum, though it changes the original cap masses.
