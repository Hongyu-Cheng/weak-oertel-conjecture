## Statement

Use the canonical definitions of positive support \(D\), total original mass \(W\), maximum original weight \(w_{\max}\), normalized potential \(\phi\), potential range \(B\), barycentric support family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\), gap \(G_E\), and full integral hitting mass \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) from accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The score at a prescribed center is \(\sup_{L\ge0}m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)/(1+L)\), using the complete original table. In particular, \(B\) remains the potential range; it is never a resource coefficient or a reflected-pair sum.

For every integer \(n\ge3\), set
\[
r=n^2,\qquad m=nr+1=n^3+1,\qquad N=n+2,\qquad
M=2nm,\qquad R=\exp(4nm).
\]
Here \(N\) is the full integer dimension, while \(n\) counts only the last factors. The symbol \(M\) is a resource scale, not a table amplitude. Let the first two factors be exactly those of accepted fact `99df7a07999b2e67`, with its integer coordinates \((i,j)\) mapped to \((x,y)\) and its \(R\) equal to the displayed \(R\):
\[
u_1(x)=R^{4x}e^{-(x-1)^2}\mathbf1_{\{0,1,2\}}(x),\qquad
u_2(y)=R^ye^{-(y-2)^2}\mathbf1_{\{0,1,2,3,4\}}(y).
\]
For \(1\le i\le n\), let
\[
g_i(k)=\exp\left(k-\frac{k^2}{4m^2}\right)\mathbf1_{\{0,1,\ldots,m\}}(k),\qquad
\beta_1=1,\qquad \beta_i=1+\frac{\sqrt2\,i}{4nm}\quad(2\le i\le n).
\]
The vector \(\beta\) is a new resource vector. Define the complete one-cut table on \(\mathbb Z^N\) by
\[
w_{(x,y,z)}=u_1(x)u_2(y)\prod_{i=1}^n g_i(z_i)
\mathbf1_{\{4Mx+My+\beta\cdot z\le9M+m\}}.
\tag{1}
\]
All factors, and the complete table, have finite-valued globally log-concave extensions. The resource coefficients are positive and pairwise distinct, and the coefficients \(\beta_i\), \(i\ge2\), are irrational.

Write
\[
D_{\mathrm{off}}^0=(\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,2,3,4\})\cup\{(2,0)\},\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{face}}=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:\sum_i z_i\le m-1\right\}\cup\{(m,0,\ldots,0)\}.
\]
Then the complete positive support is exactly
\[
D=(D_{\mathrm{off}}^0\times\{0,1,\ldots,m\}^n)
\cup(\{(2,1)\}\times Z_{\mathrm{face}}).
\tag{2}
\]
The equality set of the single resource cut in (1) is the singleton
\[
\mathbf J^\star=(2,1,m,0,\ldots,0).
\]
This point is the unique original maximum-weight center and an exposed vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}D\). The exact canonical normalization is
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{\max}&=R^9\exp\left(m-\frac94\right),\\
\phi_{(x,y,z)}&=(9-4x-y)\log R+(x-1)^2+(y-2)^2-2
+m-\frac14-\sum_i z_i+\frac{\sum_i z_i^2}{4m^2},\\
B&=9\log R+m+\frac{11}4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For each \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), consider the UNCUT tilted product
\[
\widetilde w^\lambda_{(x,y,z)}
=u_1(x)e^{-4M\lambda x}\,u_2(y)e^{-M\lambda y}
\prod_{i=1}^n\bigl(g_i(z_i)e^{-\lambda\beta_i z_i}\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]
Its normalized one-dimensional marginals are those of this rectangular product, not those of the cut table (1). An integer median has at least half the marginal mass on each of its two closed sides, and a mode maximizes the marginal mass. Let \(\mathcal J_n\) consist of all \(\mathbf J\in D\) obtained for some common \(\lambda\) by choosing each coordinate independently to be either a marginal median or a marginal mode of (4), including all ties and all mixtures of the two choices. This set is nonempty, and every member satisfies \(4J_x+J_y\le8\).

Let \(W_{\mathrm{off}}\) be the original mass of the first set on the right of (2), and put
\[
A_n=\binom{m+n-2}{n-1}.
\]
The following exact bounds hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0<W_{\mathrm{off}}&\le11e^{9/4}\exp\bigl(-(2n+1)m\bigr)w_{\max}<w_{\max},\\
e^{-1}A_nw_{\max}&\le W
\le\left(2+\frac{e^{1/4}}{e-1}\right)A_nw_{\max},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)&\le W_{\mathrm{off}}
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_n,\ L\ge0),\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J^\star,L;w)&=w_{\max}\quad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, if \(\mathcal U_n=\mathcal J_n\cup\{\mathbf J^\star\}\), which is exactly the prescribed union of feasible tilted marginal centers and original modes, then
\[
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf J\in\mathcal U_n\\L\ge0}}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}=w_{\max},\qquad
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf J\in\mathcal U_n\\L\ge0}}
\frac{2^Nm_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{(1+L)W}
\le\frac{4e\,2^n}{n^{2(n-1)}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
This rules out every positive universal coefficient for this center prescription on complete one-cut product tables.

For comparison, the explicit feasible balanced face center and closed cutoff
\[
\mathbf J^\dagger=(2,1,r,\ldots,r),\qquad L_0=\frac{nr^2}{4m^2}
\]
satisfy the full-score certificate
\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J^\dagger,L_0;w)}{1+L_0}
\ge c\,2^{-N}W,\qquad
c=\frac1{e\left(2+e^{1/4}/(e-1)\right)}\in(0,1/e).
\tag{7}
\]
All mass and score statements concern original weights (1), and the full barycentric hitting family of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`; no pair-only score is substituted for that family.

## Proof

The canonical notation and closed-cap identity used below are those of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The first-factor map to accepted fact `99df7a07999b2e67` is stated above; its scalar tilt parameter is \(M\lambda\) when the present tilt is \(\lambda\). We prove all construction, mode, support, tie, mass, and lower-certificate assertions directly.

Extend the first two factors to their real support intervals by the same concave quadratic exponential formulas, and extend every \(g_i\) by \(\exp(k-k^2/(4m^2))\) on \([0,m]\), zero elsewhere. Each logarithm has strictly negative second derivative on its positive interval. The product multiplied by the indicator of the single real affine cut in (1) is log-concave on its convex positive domain and zero elsewhere. When an endpoint is outside that domain, the required log-concavity inequality has zero right side. Thus these are globally defined finite-valued extensions agreeing with every lattice zero. No additional resource cut or independently chosen support restriction has been introduced. Distinctness and the stated irrationality of the resource coefficients follow from their formulas: \(4M>M>\max_i\beta_i\), and the \(\beta_i\) are distinct with \(\beta_1=1\).

For any \(z\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}^n\),
\[
\beta\cdot z\le\left(1+\frac{\sqrt2}{4m}\right)nm
=nm+\frac{\sqrt2 n}4<2nm=M.
\tag{8}
\]
Also \(M>m\). Every first-block point with \(4x+y\le8\) therefore retains the entire \(z\)-box, since its total resource is strictly less than \(9M\). Every first-block point with \(4x+y\ge10\) is excluded, since its resource is at least \(10M>9M+m\). The only first-block point with \(4x+y=9\) is \((2,1)\), and its remaining cut is exactly \(\beta\cdot z\le m\).

To determine this last support, write \(k=\sum_i z_i\). If \(k\le m-1\), then
\[
\beta\cdot z
\le k\left(1+\frac{\sqrt2}{4m}\right)
\le m-1+\frac{\sqrt2(m-1)}{4m}<m.
\]
If \(k=m\), the resource is at most \(m\) exactly when all \(z_i\) with \(i\ge2\) vanish, because their coefficient increments are strictly positive. This gives \((m,0,\ldots,0)\), with equality. If \(k\ge m+1\), then \(\beta\cdot z\ge k>m\). All points with \(k\le m-1\) already lie in the original product box. This proves (2) including every boundary point, and shows that \(\mathbf J^\star\) is the only equality atom of the original single cut. That cut's resource functional consequently exposes it as a unique vertex. In any positive barycentric representation of this vertex, every source would have to attain the same maximum resource, so the only possible source is \(\mathbf J^\star\) itself.

The weight at \(\mathbf J^\star\) is exactly the first value in (3). On its first-block face, every other point has \(\sum_i z_i\le m-1\), and hence last-factor weight at most \(e^{m-1}<e^{m-1/4}\). Thus the mode is unique within that face. Off the face, the first-factor weight is at most \(R^8\), because \((x-1)^2+(y-2)^2\ge0\), and every last-factor product is at most \(e^{nm}\). An off-face atom's ratio to \(w_{\max}\) is therefore at most
\[
\exp\left(-4nm+(n-1)m+\frac94\right)
=\exp\left(-(3n+1)m+\frac94\right)<1.
\]
This proves the unique global mode claim. Subtracting the original log weight from \(\log w_{\max}\) gives the displayed \(\phi\). The minimum original weight is \(e^{-5}\), attained at \((0,0,0,\ldots,0)\): throughout the first-factor box, the quadratic sum is at most five and \(R^{4x+y}\ge1\); for integer \(0\le k\le m\), one has \(k-k^2/(4m^2)\ge0\). These observations give \(w_I\ge e^{-5}\) everywhere on \(D\). Therefore \(B=\log(w_{\max}/e^{-5})\), proving the last formula in (3) with its canonical meaning.

For the tilt selection, set \(\delta=\log R-M\lambda\). The first two uncut marginal weights, up to their individual positive normalizations, are
\[
v_1(x)=e^{4\delta x-(x-1)^2}\quad(0\le x\le2),\qquad
v_2(y)=e^{\delta y-(y-2)^2}\quad(0\le y\le4).
\tag{9}
\]
These are indeed the original product marginals: the other factors in (4) contribute only positive constants to the marginal sums. If \(\delta\le0\), then \(v_1(0)\ge v_1(2)\) and \(v_1(2)/v_1(1)=e^{4\delta-1}<1\). The first inequality together with \(v_1(1)>0\) makes the probability at two strictly less than one half; hence two is not a median. The strict ratio excludes it as a mode. Thus selecting \(x=2\) requires \(\delta>0\).

For \(\delta>0\) and \(y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\frac{v_2(4-y)}{v_2(y)}=e^{\delta(4-2y)}>1.
\]
Neither zero nor one is a mode. Also \(v_2(0)+v_2(1)<v_2(3)+v_2(4)\), so the closed lower cumulative probability through one is strictly less than half; neither can be a median. Every possible second-coordinate median or mode then has \(y\ge2\). A selected first coordinate two would therefore force first-block resource at least ten, which is infeasible by (8) and \(M>m\). This argument covers every real tilt, every tie, and each coordinatewise mixture of median and mode choices. Every feasible selected center has \(x\le1\), and consequently \(4x+y\le8\).

For nonemptiness, choose \(\lambda=\log R/M=2\). Then \(\delta=0\), and the first two marginals have unique modes one and two. Every tilted last factor at \(k\ge1\) has logarithm
\[
(1-2\beta_i)k-\frac{k^2}{4m^2}\le-k<0,
\]
whereas its value at zero is one. Its unique mode is zero. Thus \((1,2,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathcal J_n\), since its first-block resource is six and the entire corresponding box survives.

There are eleven off-face first-block sites. The sum of each last factor is at most \((m+1)e^m\), so
\[
\frac{W_{\mathrm{off}}}{w_{\max}}
\le11e^{9/4}(m+1)^n\exp\bigl(-(3n+1)m\bigr)
\le11e^{9/4}\exp\bigl(-(2n+1)m\bigr),
\tag{10}
\]
where \(m+1\le e^m\) follows from the exponential series. Since \(n\ge3\) and \(m\ge28\), the last expression is at most \(11e^{-193}<1\). This last strict inequality follows, for example, from \(e>2\) and \(2^{193}>11\). It proves the first line of (5), including strict domination of the complete off-face mass by one mode atom.

There are exactly \(A_n\) nonnegative integer \(z\) with \(\sum_i z_i=m-1\), by the separator bijection for compositions into \(n\) parts. All are on the retained first-block face, and each satisfies \(\sum_i z_i^2\le(m-1)^2<m^2\). Its original weight is consequently at least
\[
e^{-2}R^9e^{m-1-1/4}=e^{-1}w_{\max}.
\tag{11}
\]
This proves the lower mass bound in (5). For the upper mass bound, the number of points in the layer \(\sum_i z_i=k\), \(0\le k\le m-1\), is \(\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}\le A_n\), and their last-factor weights are at most \(e^k\). Therefore the complete first-block face mass is at most
\[
w_{\max}+e^{-2}R^9 A_n\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e^k
\le w_{\max}\left(1+\frac{e^{1/4}}{e-1}A_n\right).
\tag{12}
\]
Adding (10) and using \(A_n\ge1\) proves the upper mass bound in (5). Thus all mass comparisons include the mode atom, every lower simplex layer, and every off-face atom.

For any \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_n\) and any \(L\ge0\), use the exact closed lower-cap characterization of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` with horizontal normal \((-4,-1,0,\ldots,0)\) and last lifting coordinate \(t=0\). It gives the closed cap
\[
\{(x,y,z)\in D:4x+y\le4J_x+J_y\}.
\]
This is a permissible nonzero normal at every cutoff, and the cap excludes the entire first-block face because \(4J_x+J_y\le8\). Its original mass is at most \(W_{\mathrm{off}}\), including all equality atoms in the cap. This proves the third line of (5) for the FULL integral hitting mass, uniformly in \(L\).

At \(\mathbf J^\star\), the singleton support has gap zero, so every hitting set at every \(L\ge0\) contains this atom and has mass at least \(w_{\max}\). Conversely, the original resource normal \((4M,M,\beta)\), with \(t=0\), gives a closed cap through \(\mathbf J^\star\) whose only atom is \(\mathbf J^\star\), by the already proved equality-set assertion. The same closed-cap identity gives \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J^\star,L;w)\le w_{\max}\). Equivalently, its full positive barycentric family has only its singleton. Thus the last line of (5) holds at every cutoff, not merely for reflected pairs. Since \(W_{\mathrm{off}}<w_{\max}\), the exact restricted score in (6) is obtained at this mode with \(L=0\).

For its asymptotics,
\[
A_n=\frac{m(m+1)\cdots(m+n-2)}{(n-1)!}
\ge\frac{(n^3)^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}=n^{2(n-1)}.
\]
Combining this with (11) and \(N=n+2\) proves the second inequality in (6). Its right side tends to zero, since its logarithm is \(\log(4e)+n\log2-2(n-1)\log n\), which tends to negative infinity. This is a simultaneous cutoff-uniform upper bound on the entire union specified in the statement.

It remains to prove (7), independently of any assertion about a pair-only score. The point \(\mathbf J^\dagger\) is feasible because its last-coordinate sum is \(nr=m-1\). Define the oriented tangent displacements
\[
\mathcal V_{n,r}=\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i v_i=0,\ -r\le v_i\le r\ (1\le i\le n)\right\}.
\]
For every \(v\in\mathcal V_{n,r}\), the two points \((2,1,(r,\ldots,r)+v)\) and \((2,1,(r,\ldots,r)-v)\) are nonnegative, have coordinate sums \(m-1\), and have individual coordinates at most \(2r\le m\). Hence both are in the exact support (2). For \(v\ne0\), they form an affinely independent two-point barycentric support of \(\mathbf J^\dagger\) with coefficients one half. Their original weights are equal, since \(r\sum_i v_i=0\), and are exactly
\[
e^{-2}R^9\exp\left(m-1-\frac{nr^2+\sum_i v_i^2}{4m^2}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
In particular each is at least \(e^{-1}w_{\max}\), by (11). From the original potential formula (3), their canonical barycentric gap is
\[
G_E=\frac{\sum_i v_i^2}{4m^2}\le\frac{nr^2}{4m^2}=L_0.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(v=0\), use the central singleton, whose gap is zero. The weak cutoff inequality in (14) is precisely the closed cutoff convention.

The nonzero displacements form disjoint pairs under \(v\mapsto-v\). The source pairs belonging to distinct such orbits are disjoint, and are disjoint from the central singleton. Every hitting set for the full family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J^\dagger}^{\log}(L_0)\) must hit each of these two-point supports and the singleton. Equal original endpoint weights in (13) therefore yield
\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf J^\dagger,L_0;w)
\ge\frac12\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{n,r}}
w_{(2,1,(r,\ldots,r)+v)}
\ge\frac{e^{-1}}2w_{\max}|\mathcal V_{n,r}|.
\tag{15}
\]
The central singleton actually contributes its full weight, so the first inequality is valid also with the zero displacement counted once. Additional barycentric supports only add hitting requirements; they cannot invalidate this lower bound for the full score.

We now prove the required cardinality comparison without assuming that a central layer maximizes its count. Let \(c_q(s)\) count \(q\)-tuples from \(\{-r,\ldots,r\}\) with sum \(s\). These counts are even and nonincreasing for \(s\ge0\). For \(q=1\) this is direct. Given the assertion at \(q\), convolution gives evenness at \(q+1\), and
\[
c_{q+1}(s)-c_{q+1}(s+1)
=c_q(s-r)-c_q(s+r+1)\ge0\quad(s\ge0),
\]
because \(|s-r|\le s+r+1\) and the asserted evenness and monotonicity apply. This proves the induction. Thus the central sum count is a largest one among the \(2nr+1\) possible sums, whose counts sum to \((2r+1)^n\). Consequently
\[
|\mathcal V_{n,r}|\ge\frac{(2r+1)^n}{2nr+1}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since
\[
A_n\le\frac{[n(r+1)]^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},\qquad
2nr+1\le2n(r+1),
\]
equation (16) implies
\[
\frac{|\mathcal V_{n,r}|}{A_n}
\ge2^{n-1}\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^n\frac{(n-1)!}{n^n}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(r=n^2\), the elementary Bernoulli inequality gives
\[
\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^n
\ge1-\frac n{n^2+1}\ge\frac12.
\]
Also \(4^{n-1}(n-1)!/n^n\ge1\) for every \(n\ge3\). To verify the latter explicitly, its value at three is \(32/27>1\), and the ratio of the value at \(n+1\) to the value at \(n\) is \(4(n/(n+1))^{n+1}>1\): indeed
\[
\left(1+\frac1n\right)^{n+1}
\le\left(1+\frac1n\right)e\le\frac43e<4.
\]
Here \(\log(1+t)\le t\) gives the first exponential bound, and \(e<3\) follows from its series, using \(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\), with a strict inequality for \(k\ge3\). Multiplying (17) by \(2^n\) therefore proves the concrete comparison
\[
|\mathcal V_{n,r}|\ge2^{-n}A_n.
\tag{18}
\]

Finally, \(L_0=nr^2/(4m^2)<1/(4n)<1\), so (15), (18), and the complete upper mass bound in (5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J^\dagger,L_0;w)}{1+L_0}
&\ge\frac{e^{-1}}4w_{\max}|\mathcal V_{n,r}|\\
&\ge\frac{2^{-n}}{4e\left(2+e^{1/4}/(e-1)\right)}W
=c\,2^{-N}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (7) at an explicit original center and a closed original-potential cutoff. The proof of the upper union bound used the full closed-cap characterization and the exact exposed-vertex family, while this lower bound used valid disjoint supports within the full family. All normalizations, support assertions, and asymptotics concern the same complete one-cut table (1).
