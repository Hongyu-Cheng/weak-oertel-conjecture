## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $T$ be a volume-one $d$-simplex with vertex $0$, centroid $g$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$, with $\lambda_0(0)=1$. Put $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$ and $b=1/(d+1)$. For a unit vector $u$ let $f(u)=|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge u\cdot g\rbrace|$ and $q(u)=|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge 2u\cdot g-\min_T u\cdot x\rbrace|$, and set $\rho_d=\min_u[f(u)-\Gamma+q(u)]$. Assume $\varepsilon>0$ and $a=\varepsilon^d<\rho_d$ and place $A=\varepsilon T,T,T$ at bases $-1,0,1$ in their actual lifted convex hull. With mixed measure the sum of the three fiber volumes, the unique deepest mixed-integer point is $(0,g)$ and its raw depth is $\Gamma+a$. The closed ambient halfspaces containing $(0,g)$ and attaining that depth are exactly
$$
\lbrace (z,x):\lambda_i(x)-Lz\ge b\rbrace,\qquad
0\le i\le d,\quad L\ge1-b,
$$
up to positive scaling of the defining margin. At $L=1-b$ the right trace is a vertex of $T$ and has zero $d$-volume.

## Proof

The profile lemma 53fe12b742a7608d gives $\rho_d\le(1-2b)^d<1$, so $A$ is compact convex and contained in $T$ with volume $a$. The exact mixed-depth theorem 98603e266680b22d applies to these actual fibers and gives the stated depth and uniqueness.

A halfspace containing $(0,g)$ whose continuous normal is zero includes the entire middle fiber and at least one endpoint. Its mass is at least $1+a>\Gamma+a$. For a halfspace with nonzero continuous normal containing $(0,g)$ in its interior, move its boundary through $(0,g)$. This removes positive mass from the middle fiber because $g$ is interior to $T$ and the continuous normal is nonzero. The moved cap already costs at least $\Gamma+a$, so every attaining cap passes through $(0,g)$.

Normalize its continuous normal to a unit vector $u$. The through-point cap bound from the proof of 98603e266680b22d is $f(u)+\min\lbrace a,q(u)\rbrace$. Indeed, the two endpoint thresholds in the $u$ direction sum to $2u\cdot g$. If the right one is at most $2u\cdot g-\min_T u\cdot x$, the right fiber contributes at least $q(u)$. Otherwise the left cap contains all of $A\subseteq T$. If $q(u)<a$, the bound is at least $\Gamma+\rho_d>\Gamma+a$. If $q(u)\ge a$, equality requires $f(u)=\Gamma$. By 53fe12b742a7608d this means $u$ points along $\nabla\lambda_i$ for some $i$. The cap then has equation $\lambda_i(x)-Lz\ge b$ for a real $L$.

Let $m_i=\min_A\lambda_i\ge0$. If $L<b-m_i$, the right threshold $b+L$ lies below $2b$, so the right trace has volume at least $(1-2b)^d\ge\rho_d>a$. This exceeds the entire endpoint mass allowed by equality. If $L\ge b-m_i$, the left trace is all of $A$ and equality requires the right trace to have zero volume. Since $\lambda_i$ ranges over $[0,1]$ on $T$, this is equivalent to $L\ge1-b$. Conversely $1-b\ge b-m_i$, so every such $L$ captures $A$ and zero right volume. The middle homothetic cap has volume $(1-b)^d=\Gamma$. At the right supporting threshold $b+L=1$ only the vertex with $\lambda_i=1$ remains.
