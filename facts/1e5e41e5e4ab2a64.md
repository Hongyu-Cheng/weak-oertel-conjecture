## Statement

Let $F\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a compact convex body with $x\in\operatorname{int}F$ and $p>1$. The minimum
$$
B=\min_a\int_F[1+a\cdot(z-x)]_+^p\,dz
$$
has a unique parameter $a_p$. For $h=[1+a_p\cdot(z-x)]_+$ one has
$$
\int_F(z-x)h^{p-1}\,dz=0,\qquad \int_Fh^p\,dz=\int_Fh^{p-1}\,dz=B>0.
$$
Among nonnegative probability densities on $F$ of mean $x$, $h^{p-1}/B$ uniquely minimizes the integral of the power $p/(p-1)$, with minimum $B^{-1/(p-1)}$. In dimension zero use the unit counting measure on the tangent point, the empty parameter and $B=1$.

## Proof

On the half of an inner ball at $x$ where $a\cdot(z-x)\ge0$, the objective is at least the integral of $[a\cdot(z-x)]_+^p$, giving a coercive lower bound $c|a|^p$. The objective is continuous, and strict convexity holds for any two distinct parameters on a common neighborhood of $x$ where both affine factors are positive and differ off a null plane. This gives unique attainment. Differentiate the $C^1$ hinge power at the optimizer to obtain the first moment identity; dotting with $a_p$ and using $h^p=h^{p-1}(1+a_p\cdot(z-x))$ gives the mass identity. For any competing probability density $\sigma$ with the stated mean, Hölder gives
$$
1=\int_F (1+a_p\cdot(z-x))\sigma\,dz
\le\int_F h\sigma\,dz
\le B^{1/p}\left(\int_F\sigma^{p/(p-1)}\,dz\right)^{(p-1)/p}.
$$
The asserted density satisfies both equalities, and equality in Hölder forces proportionality to $h^{p-1}$, hence uniqueness under normalization.
