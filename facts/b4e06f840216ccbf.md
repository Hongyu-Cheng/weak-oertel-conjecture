## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the exact canonical hypotheses, objects, and normalizations of accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70, 7ec63df101c30343, and c57ba4b9d987a404. In particular, put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
\]
and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-dominant inequalities
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\geq O.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained matching, let \(\mathcal E_I\) be the canonical family of \(I\)-bad opposite edges, let \(t_e\) be the actual opposite-edge triangle masses, and let \(\mathcal B\) be the exceptional family from 7ec63df101c30343, so
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal B}s_e\leq G,
 \qquad t_e\geq\frac18s_e\quad(e\notin\mathcal B).
\tag{2}
\]
For every physical target \(c\), put
\[
 T_c:=\sum_\xi
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
 \qquad
 E:=\{c\in\mathbb Z^n:T_c>0\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_I:=\sum_{c\in E}T_c.
\tag{3}
\]
At each \(c\in E\), choose one retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing its labelled contribution to \(T_c\), put
\[
 q_c:=q_{\xi(c),c}^{\circ},
\]
and define
\[
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\qquad
 r_E:=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-a(c):c\in E\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Assume
\[
 \rho_E=3,
 \qquad r_E=1.
\tag{5}
\]

Remove the exceptional edges and put
\[
 J:=\{e\in\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B:s_e>0\},
 \qquad S:=\sum_{e\in J}s_e.
\tag{6}
\]
For an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), use the canonical notation
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},
 \qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2},
\tag{7}
\]
and define
\[
 \sigma_M:=\sum_{e\in J}\frac{s_e}{16}
 \delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)},
 \qquad
 \sigma_F:=\gamma_d\sum_{e\in J}s_e
 \delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(\rho_M,r_M\) and \(\rho_F,r_F\) be the intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks of these two measures in the exact sense of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483. Then \(S>0\), both measures in (8) are nonzero, and
\[
 \rho_M\leq3,
 \qquad \rho_F\leq3,
 \qquad r_M,r_F\leq d.
\tag{9}
\]
Define
\[
 K_3:=
 \frac{\gamma_d}{32}
 +\frac{2^{n-\rho_M-4}}{r_M+1}
 +\max\left\{
 \frac{\gamma_d}{32},
 \frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_F}}{r_F+1}
 \right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Every configuration satisfying (1)--(9) obeys the strict joint escape
\[
 \boxed{K_3<\frac8{275}.}
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{2^{n-7}}{r_M+1}
 +\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-3}}{r_F+1}
 <\frac8{275}-\frac{\gamma_d}{32}
 =\frac{256-275\gamma_d}{8800}.}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 d+1>
 \frac{8800\,2^{n-7}(1+16\gamma_d)}
 {256-275\gamma_d}
 >3\,2^n.}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus the rank pair (5) is impossible whenever \(d+1\leq3\,2^n\). No contradiction is asserted after the strict escape (11)--(13) is allowed.

## Proof

All recurring objects above have exactly their accepted meanings. In particular, no target densities belonging to different parity labels are pooled: the points \(q_c\) in (4) are the one-label-per-physical-target selection used in c57ba4b9d987a404 and e2263a787ace07a4.

The hard alternative of 6b09aae189415c70, together with \(I\geq O\), gives
\[
 I>\frac Q4.
\tag{14}
\]
The same accepted fact gives the exact defect inequality
\[
 I+O\geq
 \frac{11}{16}Q-\frac{67}{4}R-\frac{51}{128}G.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(I\geq O\),
\[
 I\geq
 \frac{11}{32}Q-\frac{67}{8}R-\frac{51}{256}G.
\tag{16}
\]
By definition, \(I=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I}s_e\). Equations (2), (6), and (16) therefore give
\[
 \boxed{
 S+\frac{67}{8}R+\frac{307}{256}G
 \geq\frac{11}{32}Q.}
\tag{17}
\]
All summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\) are nonnegative, so (1) gives \(G<Q/100\). Equations (14) and (6) then give
\[
 S\geq I-G>\frac{6Q}{25}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
This proves the asserted nonzero cases without a limiting or empty-support convention.

For every \(e\in J\), equations (2) and (6) give \(t_e\geq s_e/8>0\). Hence \(m_e\in E\), and summing over \(J\) gives
\[
 \Theta_I\geq\sum_{e\in J}t_e\geq\frac S8.
\tag{19}
\]
Since all bases of both measures in (8) lie in \(E\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(E)\), equation (5) proves \(\rho_M,\rho_F\leq3\). Their residual ranks are at most \(d\), because the zero affine map is admissible in the defining minimizations. This proves (9).

Apply the heaviest-label selector theorem e2263a787ace07a4 to the complete positive support \(E\). Equations (4)--(5) and (19) give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{4\gamma_d}{2^3(1+1)}\Theta_I
 =\frac{\gamma_d}{4}\Theta_I
 \geq\frac{\gamma_d}{32}S.
\tag{20}
\]
The same accepted theorem gives
\[
 D\geq R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).
\tag{21}
\]

Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives \(s_e/16\leq r_e\) for every retained edge and makes every nonzero positive submeasure of its full reservoir-centroid measure admissible in \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\). Therefore \(\sigma_M\) is admissible, has mass \(S/16\), and satisfies
\[
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_M}\frac{S/16}{r_M+1}
 =\frac{2^{n-\rho_M-4}}{r_M+1}S.
\tag{22}
\]

Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 also proves that the full positive edge-point measure
\[
 \gamma_d\sum_es_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\). Its positive restriction \(\sigma_F\) is therefore halfspace-dominated as well. It has mass \(\gamma_dS\), base rank \(\rho_F\), and residual rank \(r_F\). The dominated-measure residual theorem be359f8a6c628483 yields
\[
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_F}}{r_F+1}S.
\tag{23}
\]

The capacity summands in the next accounting are distinct. Equation (20) is charged once to \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), equation (22) is charged once to \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and only the maximum of (21) and (23) is charged to the single \(D\)-summand. The terms \(R\) and \(G\) are then added once, while \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) is discarded. Thus (10) and (20)--(23) give
\[
 \mathcal A(C)\geq R+G+K_3S.
\tag{24}
\]

Put \(\lambda:=8/275\). If \(K_3\geq\lambda\), equations (17) and (24) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A(C)
 &\geq R+G+K_3S\\
 &\geq
 \lambda\left(S+\frac{67}{8}R+\frac{307}{256}G\right)
 +\frac{208}{275}R+\frac{8493}{8800}G\\
 &\geq
 \frac8{275}\frac{11}{32}Q
 =\frac Q{100}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Here the second line uses only \(K_3S\geq\lambda S\) and the exact identities
\[
 1-\lambda\frac{67}{8}=\frac{208}{275}>0,
 \qquad
 1-\lambda\frac{307}{256}=\frac{8493}{8800}>0.
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (25) contradicts the strict hard inequality (1). Hence (11) holds, including its strict endpoint.

The maximum in (10) is at least its second entry. Therefore (11) gives
\[
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_M-4}}{r_M+1}
 +\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_F}}{r_F+1}
 +\frac{\gamma_d}{32}
 <\frac8{275}.
\tag{27}
\]
Using \(\rho_M,\rho_F\leq3\) proves (12). The binomial theorem gives
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d\geq2,
 \qquad 0<\gamma_d\leq\frac12,
\tag{28}
\]
so the right side of (12) is positive. Since \(r_M,r_F\leq d\), equation (12) gives
\[
 \frac{2^{n-7}(1+16\gamma_d)}{d+1}
 <\frac{256-275\gamma_d}{8800},
\tag{29}
\]
and exact rearrangement proves the first inequality in (13).

It remains to audit the uniform strict comparison. The factorial-series estimate
\[
 e<\frac{163}{60}+\frac{1/720}{1-1/7}
 =\frac{11743}{4320}<\frac{68}{25}
\tag{30}
\]
and the strict inequality \((1+1/d)^d<e\) give
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac{25}{68}>\frac{2797}{7700},
\tag{31}
\]
where the last comparison is \(25\cdot7700=192500>190196=2797\cdot68\). Because the denominator in (13) is positive, the desired comparison with \(3\,2^n\) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{275}{4}\frac{1+16\gamma_d}{256-275\gamma_d}>3,
\]
which after cross multiplication is exactly \(7700\gamma_d>2797\). This follows strictly from (31) and completes (13).

Finally, let \(A:=\operatorname{aff}(E)\). If \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\), then \(m_e\in E\subseteq A\), and hence
\[
 v=2m_e-u\in\mathbb Z^n\cap(2A-u).
\tag{32}
\]
Under (5), the affine space \(2A-u\) has dimension three. Its occupied-fiber affine hull may also have rank three. The slice theorem 8aac56c49321466c assumes occupied rank at most two, so it is not applicable to (32). The one-dimensional continuous residual hypothesis in (5) concerns the selected target points \(q_c\), not the source-fiber masses on \(2A-u\), and supplies no domination relation between those two objects. Accordingly, no estimate \(S\leq64G\), no target-density pooling, and no synthetic source measure is used or asserted above. The exact unconditional conclusion available from the accepted interfaces is the joint escape (11).
