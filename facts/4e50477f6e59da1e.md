## Statement

Let $n,r\ge1$ and let $D\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^r$ be a compact convex body whose mixed $r$-volume $\nu$ has mass $W>0$, full affine span and centroid an actual mixed point. Translate that point to $0$, preserving the integer lattice. Define $R=\max_{X\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}|X|$ and $m=\min_{|v|=1}W^{-1}\int(v\cdot X)_+\,d\nu>0$. For continuous positive concave $g$ on $D$, put $G=\max_Dg$ and assume $L\ge GR/m$. The body
$$
C=\{(X,t):X\in D,\ -L\le t\le g(X)\}
$$
has mixed $(r+1)$-volume $LW+Q$ with $Q=\int_Dg\,d\nu$. The raw depth of the actual point $(0,0)$ is exactly $Q$, attained by $\{t\ge0\}$, and this cap cuts every positive-volume sampled fiber properly.

## Proof

The body is compact and convex by the roof and base assumptions, with nonempty interior. A vanishing positive moment for a unit $v$ would imply $v\cdot X=0$ almost everywhere since the mean is zero, contradicting full affine span. Continuity on the compact unit sphere gives $m>0$. Also $m\le R$, so $L\ge G>0$. Fubini gives both the total mass and the flat cap mass. The vertical thickness is uniformly positive and bounded, so each positive extruded fiber comes from a section fiber of positive $r$-volume. It is cut properly at $t=0$ since $L>0$ and $g>0$.

It suffices to bound caps through $(0,0)$, because parallel movement to this point only decreases the mass of a containing cap. For a cap $t+\ell(X)\ge0$ with linear $\ell$, its mass minus $Q$ is $\int\max(-g(X),\min(L,\ell(X)))\,d\nu$. This is bounded below by
$$
\int \min(k(v\cdot X)_+,L)\,d\nu-\int \min(k(-v\cdot X)_+,G)\,d\nu
$$
when $\ell(X)=k v\cdot X$ with $k>0$ and $|v|=1$. For $kR\le L$ the positive moment is untruncated and dominates the negative term by zero mean. For $kR>L$ the inequality $\min(kx,L)\ge Lx/R$ on $[0,R]$ gives a positive term at least $LWm/R\ge GW$, and the negative term is at most $GW$. For $\ell=0$ the original mass difference is zero.

For a cap $t\le\ell(X)$ with nonzero $\ell$, the entire lower interval of height $L$ is captured above $\ell\ge0$. The same holds for a cap $\ell(X)\ge0$ with zero $t$ coefficient. For the associated unit direction $v$, the bound $(v\cdot X)_+\le R\mathbf1_{\{v\cdot X>0\}}$ gives $\nu\{\ell\ge0\}\ge Wm/R$, so the captured mass is at least $LWm/R\ge GW\ge Q$. The cap $t\le0$ has mass $LW\ge Q$. These cases give the claimed lower bound over all closed ambient directions.
