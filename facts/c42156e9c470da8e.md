## Statement

Fix a finite nonempty base $P$ in its relative affine span and $d\ge1$. Let $K_{j,z}\to K_z$ in Hausdorff distance, with all the moving and limiting fibers positive convex $d$-bodies. Suppose each moving canonical hull $C_j=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_z(\{z\}\times K_{j,z})$ has actual sampled fibers $K_{j,z}$. Let $h_j$ be nonnegative continuous concave functions on $C_j$ with a common sampled upper bound. There is a subsequence along which $h_j(z,\cdot)$ converges locally uniformly on each $\operatorname{int}K_z$ to a nonnegative concave function $h_z$. Each $h_z$ is either positive throughout its interior or identically zero there. The upper-semicontinuous concave boundary closures of these functions are the sampled restrictions of a bounded nonnegative upper-semicontinuous concave roof $h$ on $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_z(\{z\}\times K_z)$.

Write $Z=\{z:h_z\equiv0\}$. For every $z\in Z$, $\max_{X\in K_{j,z}}h_j(z,X)\to0$ along this subsequence. If $p\in Z$ and $F_p$ is the smallest face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ containing $p$, then $P\cap F_p\subseteq Z$.

## Proof

By support convergence, each compact subset of a limiting fiber interior, together with a sufficiently small compact neighborhood there, is contained in the moving fiber eventually. On such a subset a uniform oscillation bound and concavity bound secant slopes by extending in either direction into that neighborhood. Compactness on an exhaustion and a diagonal subsequence over the finitely many fibers yield the locally uniform limits. At an interior point of a convex body, nonnegative concavity implies that if the value is zero then every interior value is zero: extend the segment from any candidate positive point through the zero point and apply concavity.

Compatibility passes to the limit by approximating each input point in its moving fiber. An anchored combination of own-interior inputs is own-interior at the target since the combined input balls contain a ball of positive radius. At such tuples the concavity inequality passes to the limit. For each $z$, close the truncated hypograph $\{(X,t):X\in\operatorname{int}K_z,\ 0\le t\le h_z(X)\}$. It is compact, convex, and projects onto $K_z$; its upper envelope is the claimed upper-semicontinuous closure. Approximate every anchored tuple of points of these closed hypographs by tuples from the interior hypographs. The corresponding outputs lie in the interior target hypograph and their limit lies in its closure. Thus the anchored inequalities hold for all the closed hypographs. Their lifted convex hull is compact, projects onto $C$, and has exactly the given hypograph at every sampled base by the anchored inequalities. Its upper envelope defines $h$.

For $z\in Z$, choose $g\in\operatorname{int}K_z$ with a common ball of radius $r>0$ about $g$ in the moving fibers for large $j$. Bound $|X-g|$ on these fibers by $D$. Express $g$ as a combination of $X\ne g$ and the point at distance $r$ on the opposite ray. Nonnegativity gives $h_j(z,X)\le(1+D/r)h_j(z,g)$, also valid at $X=g$. This tends uniformly to zero.

Finally, if $z\in P\cap F_p$ and $z\ne p$, relative interior of $p$ in $F_p$ lets the ray from $z$ through $p$ extend to a point of $F_p$ beyond $p$. Express that point by the vertices of $F_p$ to get an anchored combination at $p$ with positive weight at $z$. If $h_z$ is positive at an interior point, take it and arbitrary own-interior inputs at all other positive weights. Concavity makes the resulting own-interior value at $p$ positive, contradicting $p\in Z$.
