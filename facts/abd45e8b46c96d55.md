## Statement

For every \(L>0\) and \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), define

\[
\delta(L,\epsilon)
=\frac{e^{-1/\epsilon}}{96}
 \exp\!\left(
 -18e^{2/\epsilon}L^2
 -24\sqrt{6}\,e^{3/(2\epsilon)}L
 \right)>0.
\]

For every integer \(d\geq1\), let \(U\) be uniform on \([-1/2,1/2]^d\), and put
\(\tau_U(v)=\inf_{H\ni v}\mathbb P(U\in H)\), where the infimum is over proper closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^d\). Then every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) satisfying \(\|v\|_2\leq L\) and \(\|v\|_\infty\leq1/2-\epsilon\) belongs to this cube and satisfies \(\tau_U(v)\geq\delta(L,\epsilon)\). Equivalently, every closed halfspace containing this same \(v\) has original uniform cube probability at least \(\delta(L,\epsilon)\). The whole space, if included among halfspaces, also satisfies the bound.

The vector \(U\) has exactly the normalization of the independent uniform coordinates called \(U_i\) in the final affine-fit cap argument of accepted fact `4e94470dc826efb6`; \(d\) is the number of these coordinates. Here \(v\) denotes a normalized displacement, corresponding there to \(T_z^{-1}(\ell(z)-c_z)\), and never a halfspace normal. Halfspace normals in this proof are denoted by \(b\). The auxiliary tilted vector introduced below is not the original \(U\).

## Proof

First derive the required scalar bounds directly on the continuous interval. For real \(\theta\), let
\(\mathcal Z(\theta)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{\theta x}\,dx\), and let \(\nu_\theta\) have density \(f_\theta(x)=e^{\theta x}/\mathcal Z(\theta)\) on this interval with respect to Lebesgue measure. In scalar expectations, \(X\) denotes the identity random variable under \(\nu_\theta\). Denote its mean by \(\mu(\theta)\) and its variance by \(V(\theta)\). Direct integration and differentiation give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Z(\theta)
 &=\frac{e^{\theta/2}-e^{-\theta/2}}{\theta},
 &\mu(\theta)
 &=\frac12\coth(\theta/2)-\frac1\theta
 &&(\theta\ne0),\\
\mathcal Z(0)&=1,
 &\mu(0)&=0,
 &V(0)&=\frac1{12},\\
\mu(\theta)&=\frac{\mathcal Z'(\theta)}{\mathcal Z(\theta)},
 &\mu'(\theta)&=V(\theta)
 =\frac{\mathcal Z''(\theta)}{\mathcal Z(\theta)}
  -\left(\frac{\mathcal Z'(\theta)}{\mathcal Z(\theta)}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

All derivatives at zero in the last line are given by the integral, rather than by a formula containing \(1/\theta\). Differentiation under the integral is justified on every compact parameter interval because each differentiated integrand is continuous and bounded there. In particular all these quantities are continuous at zero. The value \(V(0)=1/12\), and the fourth centered moment \(\mathbb E_{\nu_0}X^4=1/80\), follow by integrating \(x^2\) and \(x^4\) on \([-1/2,1/2]\).

The density is positive throughout the interval, so \(V(\theta)>0\) for every real \(\theta\). Thus \(\mu\) is strictly increasing. Reflection in zero shows that \(\mathcal Z\) is even and \(\mu\) is odd. Set \(\Theta_\epsilon=1/\epsilon\). Since \(\coth(\Theta_\epsilon/2)>1\), the explicit mean formula yields \(\mu(\Theta_\epsilon)>1/2-\epsilon\), and hence \(\mu(-\Theta_\epsilon)<-1/2+\epsilon\). By continuity and strict monotonicity, for every \(t\in[-1/2+\epsilon,1/2-\epsilon]\) there is a unique \(\theta\in[-\Theta_\epsilon,\Theta_\epsilon]\) such that \(\mu(\theta)=t\). For \(t=0\) this parameter is exactly zero. Equality in either permitted coordinate margin causes no difficulty.

For \(|\theta|\leq\Theta_\epsilon\), bounding the integrand above by \(e^{|\theta|/2}\) gives \(\mathcal Z(\theta)\leq e^{|\theta|/2}\). Therefore \(f_\theta(x)\geq e^{-|\theta|}\geq e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}\) throughout the interval. The following variance bounds now follow from exact interval integrals and the minimizing property of the mean:

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(\theta)
 &=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}(x-\mu(\theta))^2f_\theta(x)\,dx\\
 &\geq e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}
       \left(\frac1{12}+\mu(\theta)^2\right)
 \geq\frac{e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}}{12},\\
V(\theta)
 &\leq\mathbb E_{\nu_\theta}X^2\leq\frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]

Integrating \(\mu'=V\) from zero to \(\theta\), and using oddness for negative parameters, gives \(|\mu(\theta)|\geq e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}|\theta|/12\). In particular, the parameter giving mean \(t\) satisfies \(|\theta|\leq12e^{\Theta_\epsilon}|t|\), including \(t=0\).

The mean of any \(\nu_\theta\) lies in \([-1/2,1/2]\), so \(|X-\mu(\theta)|\leq1\). Consequently, with \(\kappa_\epsilon=12e^{\Theta_\epsilon}\),
\(\mathbb E_{\nu_\theta}(X-\mu(\theta))^4\leq V(\theta)\leq\kappa_\epsilon V(\theta)^2\) whenever \(|\theta|\leq\Theta_\epsilon\). This establishes a uniform centered fourth-moment ratio directly for the tilted continuous interval; no lattice assertion is applied to it.

For \(|\theta|\leq\Theta_\epsilon\), the scalar log-density also has mean and variance controlled by the squared prescribed mean. Indeed, \((\log\mathcal Z)'=\mu\), and differentiation gives
\(\frac{d}{d\theta}[\theta\mu(\theta)-\log\mathcal Z(\theta)]=\theta V(\theta)\). At zero the expression is zero. It is even by the reflection identities. Integrating for nonnegative \(\theta\), using \(0<V\leq1/4\), and then using evenness proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\leq\mathbb E_{\nu_\theta}\log f_\theta(X)
 &=\theta\mu(\theta)-\log\mathcal Z(\theta)
 \leq\frac{\theta^2}{8}
 \leq18e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}\mu(\theta)^2,\\
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_\theta}(\log f_\theta(X))
 &=\theta^2V(\theta)
 \leq\frac{\theta^2}{4}
 \leq36e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}\mu(\theta)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

These identities and inequalities also hold when \(\theta=0\): the density is identically one and its log-density is zero. This proves all scalar estimates needed for both the mean cap and the change of measure.

Now fix arbitrary \(d\) and \(v\) satisfying the theorem's two norm bounds. For each coordinate choose the parameter \(\theta_i\) with \(\mu(\theta_i)=v_i\) just constructed. Let \(Q_v=\bigotimes_{i=1}^d\nu_{\theta_i}\), and write \(\widetilde U\) for a random vector with this auxiliary law. Its coordinates are independent and its mean is exactly \(v\). For an arbitrary nonzero normal \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), put \(F_b=b\cdot(\widetilde U-v)\) and \(\sigma_b^2=\mathbb E_{Q_v}F_b^2=\sum_i b_i^2V(\theta_i)>0\). Expanding the fourth power, independence and centering eliminate every term with an index appearing only once. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_v}F_b^4
 &=\sum_i b_i^4\mathbb E_{\nu_{\theta_i}}(X-v_i)^4
   +6\sum_{i<j}b_i^2b_j^2V(\theta_i)V(\theta_j)\\
 &\leq\kappa_\epsilon
   \left(\sum_i b_i^2V(\theta_i)\right)^2
 =\kappa_\epsilon\sigma_b^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The inequality uses \(\kappa_\epsilon\geq3\). In particular the product projection retains one fourth-moment constant, rather than paying a constant for each coordinate.

Apply the centered scalar cap argument proved in accepted fact `4e94470dc826efb6`, now with this \(\kappa_\epsilon\). To make its use explicit, Hölder's inequality gives
\(\sigma_b^2\leq(\mathbb E_{Q_v}|F_b|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E_{Q_v}F_b^4)^{1/3}\), hence \(\mathbb E_{Q_v}|F_b|\geq\sigma_b/\sqrt{\kappa_\epsilon}\). Since \(F_b\) is centered, its positive part has expectation \(\mathbb E_{Q_v}|F_b|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz and \(\mathbb E_{Q_v}(F_b)_+^2\leq\sigma_b^2\) therefore give
\(Q_v(F_b>0)\geq1/(4\kappa_\epsilon)\). It follows that every closed halfspace through \(v\) with nonzero normal has \(Q_v\)-probability at least \(\alpha_\epsilon=1/(4\kappa_\epsilon)=e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}/48\). The same estimate holds for every containing closed halfspace by inclusion of its parallel halfspace through \(v\). All normal directions are allowed.

Let \(P\) denote the original uniform law of \(U\) on the cube. The cube has Lebesgue volume one, so its density is one there. The likelihood ratio and its logarithm on the cube are exactly
\(dQ_v/dP(x)=\prod_i f_{\theta_i}(x_i)=e^{R_v(x)}\), where \(R_v(x)=\sum_i[\theta_i x_i-\log\mathcal Z(\theta_i)]\). Both laws give zero mass outside the cube and have strictly positive densities inside; thus also \(dP/dQ_v=e^{-R_v}\). The scalar estimates and independence imply the dimension-free bounds

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\leq\mathbb E_{Q_v}R_v(\widetilde U)
 &\leq18e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}\sum_i v_i^2
 \leq18e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}L^2,\\
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_v}(R_v(\widetilde U))
 &\leq36e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}\sum_i v_i^2
 \leq36e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}L^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set \(B_\epsilon(L)=18e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}L^2+24\sqrt{6}\,e^{3\Theta_\epsilon/2}L\). Since \(L>0\), the second summand is positive. Chebyshev's inequality and the preceding mean bound show

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_v(R_v(\widetilde U)>B_\epsilon(L))
 &\leq
 \frac{36e^{2\Theta_\epsilon}L^2}
      {(24\sqrt{6}\,e^{3\Theta_\epsilon/2}L)^2}\\
 &=\frac{e^{-\Theta_\epsilon}}{96}
 =\frac{\alpha_\epsilon}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This remains valid if the log-likelihood variance is zero, in particular if \(v=0\), when all parameters and the log-likelihood itself are zero.

Finally, let any proper closed halfspace containing \(v\) be written as \(H=\{x:b\cdot x\geq h\}\), with \(b\ne0\). The condition \(v\in H\) means \(h\leq b\cdot v\), so \(H\) contains \(A_b=\{x:b\cdot(x-v)\geq0\}\). The cap estimate gives \(Q_v(A_b)\geq\alpha_\epsilon\). Removing the event where the log-likelihood exceeds \(B_\epsilon(L)\) loses at most \(\alpha_\epsilon/2\). Changing back to the original uniform cube law therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(H)&\geq P(A_b)
 =\int_{A_b}e^{-R_v(x)}\,dQ_v(x)\\
 &\geq e^{-B_\epsilon(L)}
 Q_v\bigl(A_b\cap\{R_v\leq B_\epsilon(L)\}\bigr)\\
 &\geq\frac{\alpha_\epsilon}{2}e^{-B_\epsilon(L)}
 =\delta(L,\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
\]

All intersections in this integral are evaluated under the actual cube laws, so no auxiliary probability is substituted for the asserted original probability. The use of a closed mean cap covers equality in the halfspace boundary condition, and the scalar construction already covers zero coordinates and equality in the inward-margin condition. The coordinate bound places \(v\) in the cube, with positive margin, and the same fixed \(v\) works for every containing halfspace. Taking their infimum proves the theorem. The whole space has probability one and satisfies the same bound because \(0<\delta(L,\epsilon)<1\).
