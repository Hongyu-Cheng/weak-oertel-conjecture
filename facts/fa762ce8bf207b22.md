## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-3,-1),(3,3),(1,3),(-1,2)\}$ and $S=P\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Of the nine distinct unordered same-parity pairs, the only pairs $\{p,q\}$ for which $S\setminus\{p,q\}$ is the complete lattice set of its convex hull are
$$
\{(-3,-1),(1,3)\},\quad \{(-3,-1),(3,3)\},\quad \{(1,3),(3,3)\}.
$$
Each such deletion retains its integer midpoint. For uniform counting on the eight survivors the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth is exactly $3/8$, witnessed respectively at $(1,2),(0,2),(-1,1)$. The original maximum is $3/10$.

## Proof

Use the indexed support and cut table from the ten-site baseline fact. The parity partition yields nine unordered distinct same-parity pairs. Six fail: removing $\{s_1,s_5\}$ leaves $s_1$ in $[s_0,s_2]$, removing $\{s_4,s_8\}$ leaves $s_8$ in $[s_7,s_9]$, and removing $\{s_3,s_6\}$ leaves $s_6$ in $[s_4,s_8]$. For each of $\{s_0,s_2\},\{s_2,s_7\},\{s_2,s_9\}$, the removed $s_2$ belongs to the retained segment $[s_1,s_5]$.

For each other pair the table gives inequalities $ax+by\ge c$. All survivors satisfy the corresponding row and each deleted site violates at least one inequality in that row. This suffices for the complete lattice-set test: the new hull is contained in $P$, so the only possible extra lattice points are the two deleted sites.

| Removed indices | $(a,b,c)$ triples |
|---|---|
| $0,7$ | $(-1,2,2),(-2,3,3),(0,-1,-3),(1,-3,-7),(2,-1,-4)$ |
| $0,9$ | $(-1,2,2),(-1,1,1),(0,-1,-3),(1,-2,-5),(2,-1,-4)$ |
| $7,9$ | $(-2,3,3),(-1,1,1),(1,-3,-7),(3,-2,-7)$ |

The retained midpoints of the removed pairs are $s_2,s_4,s_8$ in row order. For the first depth witness, $s_6$ is the midpoint of both $\{s_2,s_9\}$ and $\{s_4,s_8\}$. For the second, $s_5$ is the midpoint of both $\{s_2,s_7\}$ and $\{s_3,s_6\}$. For the third, $s_2$ is the midpoint of $\{s_1,s_5\}$ and is $(s_0+2s_3+2s_4)/5$. In each case the two groups consist of distinct surviving sites and avoid the witness. A closed halfspace containing the witness must meet both groups because it contains a point in each group's convex hull. Together with the witness this gives a lower bound of three. Each survivor keeps its original containing cut, and removal cannot increase that cut's count. Thus the raw maximum is exactly three in all cases.
