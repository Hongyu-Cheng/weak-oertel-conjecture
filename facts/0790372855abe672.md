## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and write
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad
\gamma_m:=\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m.
\]
For every Borel set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define
\[
\mu(E):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(E_{\mathbf z}),\qquad
E_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in E\}.
\]
Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body. Suppose that, for some \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(R>3/2\),
\[
\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^n
\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C).
\]
Then there is a point \(\mathbf p\in C\cap\Lambda\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf p\) satisfies
\[
\mu(C\cap H)\ge
\gamma_m\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m\mu(C).
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
R\ge
\frac{3+2^{-n/m}}{2(1-2^{-n/m})},
\tag{2}
\]
then the same point satisfies
\[
\mu(C\cap H)\ge\gamma_m2^{-n}\mu(C)
>\frac{1}{e\,2^n}\mu(C)
\tag{3}
\]
for every such \(H\). A simpler sufficient condition for (3) is
\[
R\ge\frac72\,\frac{m}{n}.
\tag{4}
\]
The conclusions are conditional on the displayed large-box hypothesis. The coefficient in (3) is \(\gamma_m2^{-n}\); no claim of the sharp-style coefficient \(\gamma_d2^{-n}\) is made.

## Proof

Let
\[
\varepsilon:=\frac{3}{2R},\qquad
\lambda:=1-\varepsilon=\frac{2R-3}{2R},\qquad
r:=\frac1\varepsilon=\frac{2R}{3}.
\tag{5}
\]
The assumption \(R>3/2\) gives \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(\lambda>0\). Let
\(\mathbf c=(\mathbf c_I,\mathbf c_C)\) be the Lebesgue centroid of \(C\), with
\(\mathbf c_I\in\mathbb R^n\). Choose \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) by coordinatewise nearest-integer rounding so that
\[
\left\|\mathbf z_0-
\bigl(\lambda\mathbf c_I+\varepsilon\mathbf a\bigr)
\right\|_\infty\le\frac12,
\]
and define
\[
\mathbf u:=\frac{\mathbf z_0-\lambda\mathbf c_I}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\|\mathbf u-\mathbf a\|_\infty
\le\frac1{2\varepsilon}=\frac R3.
\]
Since \(r=2R/3\), it follows that
\[
\mathbf u+r[-1,1]^n
\subseteq\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^n
\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C).
\tag{7}
\]

For every \(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n\), choose a point
\(\mathbf v_{\boldsymbol\sigma}\in C\) whose base coordinate is
\(\mathbf u+r\boldsymbol\sigma\). Such points exist by (7). For
\(\mathbf t=\mathbf u+r\boldsymbol\xi\), where
\(\boldsymbol\xi\in[-1,1]^n\), define
\[
\mathbf s(\mathbf t):=
\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{1+\sigma_i\xi_i}{2}\right)
\mathbf v_{\boldsymbol\sigma}.
\tag{8}
\]
The coefficients in (8) are nonnegative and sum to one, so
\(\mathbf s(\mathbf t)\in C\). Their weighted average in the \(i\)-th base
coordinate equals \(u_i+r\xi_i=t_i\); hence
\[
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf s(\mathbf t)=\mathbf t.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathbf s\) is affine in each coordinate separately. Put
\[
\mathbf w:=\mathbf s(\mathbf u)\in C,\qquad
D:=\lambda C+\varepsilon\mathbf w.
\tag{10}
\]
This is a convex body contained in \(C\), with
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)=\lambda^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\tag{11}
\]
Its centroid is
\[
\mathbf p:=\lambda\mathbf c+\varepsilon\mathbf w.
\tag{12}
\]
By (6), (9), and the fact that the base coordinate of \(\mathbf w\) is
\(\mathbf u\), the base coordinate of \(\mathbf p\) is
\[
\lambda\mathbf c_I+\varepsilon\mathbf u=\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]
Also \(\mathbf p\in D\subseteq C\), so \(\mathbf p\in C\cap\Lambda\).

We next prove the cap-transfer inequality
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)\le\mu(C\cap H)
\tag{13}
\]
for every closed affine halfspace
\[
H=\{\mathbf y:L(\mathbf y)\ge b\},
\tag{14}
\]
where \(L\) is a nonzero linear functional. Define the scalar multi-affine
function
\[
f(\mathbf t):=L(\mathbf s(\mathbf t))
\quad\text{on }\mathbf u+r[-1,1]^n.
\]
Apply accepted fact \(92e80d28d7127cde\) to obtain a Borel map
\(\mathcal R_f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb Z^n\) such that, for every
\(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_i\in
\{\lfloor q_i\rfloor,\lceil q_i\rceil\},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
f\!\left(\mathbf u+r(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)-\mathbf q)\right)
\ge f(\mathbf u),
\tag{16}
\]
and, for every nonnegative \(\phi:\mathbb Z^n\to[0,\infty]\),
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\phi(\mathbf z).
\tag{17}
\]

Fix \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\), and set
\[
\mathbf z:=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q),\qquad
\mathbf t:=\mathbf u+r(\mathbf z-\mathbf q).
\tag{18}
\]
By (15), \(\|\mathbf z-\mathbf q\|_\infty\le1\), so \(\mathbf t\) lies in
the domain of \(\mathbf s\). For \(\mathbf y=(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)\in D\),
the point
\[
\mathbf c_{\mathbf y}:=\frac{\mathbf y-\varepsilon\mathbf w}{\lambda}
\tag{19}
\]
is the unique point of \(C\) with
\(\mathbf y=\lambda\mathbf c_{\mathbf y}+\varepsilon\mathbf w\). Define
\[
\Phi_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf y):=
\lambda\mathbf c_{\mathbf y}+\varepsilon\mathbf s(\mathbf t)
=\mathbf y+\varepsilon\bigl(\mathbf s(\mathbf t)-\mathbf w\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]
This point belongs to \(C\), since it is a convex combination of
\(\mathbf c_{\mathbf y}\) and \(\mathbf s(\mathbf t)\). Its base coordinate is
\[
\mathbf q+\varepsilon(\mathbf t-\mathbf u)
=\mathbf q+\varepsilon r(\mathbf z-\mathbf q)
=\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{21}
\]
where \(\varepsilon r=1\). From (16) and \(\mathbf w=\mathbf s(\mathbf u)\),
\[
L(\Phi_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf y))
=L(\mathbf y)+\varepsilon\bigl(L(\mathbf s(\mathbf t))-L(\mathbf w)\bigr)
\ge L(\mathbf y).
\tag{22}
\]
Thus \(\Phi_{\mathbf q}\) maps the \(\mathbf q\)-fiber of \(D\cap H\) into
the \(\mathbf z\)-fiber of \(C\cap H\).

For fixed \(\mathbf q\), the map in (20) acts on the continuous coordinate
\(\mathbf x\) by translation by a vector depending only on \(\mathbf q\).
It therefore preserves \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume. If
\[
g(\mathbf q):=\operatorname{vol}_d((D\cap H)_{\mathbf q}),\qquad
h(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d((C\cap H)_{\mathbf z}),
\]
then
\[
g(\mathbf q)\le h(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)).
\tag{23}
\]
The section-volume function \(g\) is Borel measurable because \(D\cap H\)
is compact; for example, the standard compact-section argument makes it
upper semicontinuous. Fubini's theorem, (23), and the exact pushforward
identity (17), applied to the nonnegative function \(h\), now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}g(\mathbf q)\,d\mathbf q\\
&\le\int_{\mathbb R^n}h(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}h(\mathbf z)
=\mu(C\cap H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
This proves (13). Notice that no global injectivity or disjointness of the
translated continuous fibers is required: (17) supplies exactly one unit of
base-parameter capacity at each target lattice point. The Borel construction
in accepted fact \(92e80d28d7127cde\) also includes tie sets and cell
boundaries, so (24) has no omitted boundary case.

Now let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing the point \(\mathbf p\) from
(12). Since \(\mathbf p\) is the centroid of \(D\), the sharp continuous
Grünbaum inequality in dimension \(m\) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)\ge
\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(D).
\tag{25}
\]
Combining (13), (25), and (11) yields
\[
\mu(C\cap H)\ge
\gamma_m\lambda^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\tag{26}
\]
Apply accepted fact \(4cfd86cd551df9e7\) to \(C\), with box center
\(\mathbf a\) and thickness parameter \(R>1/2\). Its upper
mixed-continuous comparison is
\[
\mu(C)\le
\left(1+\frac1{2R}\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C),
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(C)\ge
\left(\frac{2R}{2R+1}\right)^m\mu(C).
\tag{27}
\]
Substituting (27) and
\(\lambda=(2R-3)/(2R)\) into (26) proves (1).

It remains to verify the two radius consequences. Put
\[
q:=2^{-n/m}\in(0,1).
\]
The inequality
\[
\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\ge q
\]
is equivalent, because \(2R+1>0\), to
\[
R\ge\frac{3+q}{2(1-q)}.
\]
Thus (2) makes the \(m\)-th power of the profile factor at least \(2^{-n}\),
which proves the first inequality in (3). Moreover,
\[
\gamma_m=\left(1+\frac1m\right)^{-m}>e^{-1},
\]
because \(m\log(1+1/m)<1\). This proves the strict second inequality in (3).

For the simpler condition, put \(\theta:=n/m\in(0,1)\). The function
\((2R-3)/(2R+1)\) is increasing in \(R\). If
\(R\ge7/(2\theta)\), then
\[
\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\ge\frac{7-3\theta}{7+\theta}.
\tag{28}
\]
Define, continuously on \([0,1]\),
\[
\varphi(\theta):=
\log(7-3\theta)-\log(7+\theta)+\theta\log2.
\]
Then \(\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=0\), while
\[
\varphi''(\theta)=
-\frac9{(7-3\theta)^2}+\frac1{(7+\theta)^2}<0
\qquad(0\le\theta\le1).
\]
Hence \(\varphi\) is concave and lies above its zero endpoint chord, so
\(\varphi(\theta)\ge0\). Equivalently,
\[
\frac{7-3\theta}{7+\theta}\ge2^{-\theta}.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (28) and (29) imply that the \(m\)-th power of the profile factor is
at least \(2^{-\theta m}=2^{-n}\). This proves (4) as a sufficient condition
for (3), and completes the proof.

## External sources

Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace theorem: B. Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. The exact form used above is: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) is a convex body with centroid \(\mathbf g\), then every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf g\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(K\cap H)\ge
\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(K).
\]
Here \(K=D\) is a full-dimensional convex body because \(\lambda>0\), \(\mathbf g=\mathbf p\) by (12), and the halfspace in (25) is closed and contains \(\mathbf p\), so all hypotheses apply.
