## Statement

In the class of a367af5c0a8f6dc0, suppose a minimizing family over fixed lattice-convex $P$ has all fibers positive, total summed $d$-volume $M$, and normalized maximum closed-halfspace depth $\beta$. It is a global volume maximizer among compatible families with the same bound $N$ on positive-fiber facets, with budgets $\beta M$ for a fixed menu of at most $|P|(d+1)$ nonvertical caps covering $P\times\mathbb R^d$. The competing families may have any central volume.

For each fiber take the normal fan of its sufficiently small positive inner parallel bodies and a common simplicial refinement. Restrict the common-fan support chart to functions linear on each fiber's own chosen fan. Let $A_0$ be the active homogeneous support and compatibility rows in this restricted chart. For a facet $F$ of $K_z$ let $n_F$ be its unit outward normal and let $|F|$ be its $(d-1)$-area. There exist $\lambda_j,\eta\ge0$ with some $\lambda_j>0$ and with $\lambda_j=0$ for slack caps, such that for every direction in the restricted chart,
$$
\sum_{z,F}|F|\,\delta_z(n_F)=\sum_j\lambda_j L_j(\delta)-\eta^\top A_0\delta.
$$
Here $L_j(\delta)=\sum_{z,F}a_{jzF}\delta_z(n_F)$. The coefficient is the area strictly inside cap $j$ at noncoincident facets and $\theta_{jzF}|F|$, $0\le\theta_{jzF}\le1$, at coincident facets.

For cap $H_j=\{v_j\cdot z+u_j\cdot x\ge b_j\}$ with $u_j$ unit, let $t_j(z)=b_j-v_j\cdot z$. Let $s_{jz}$ be the proper section area, zero for strict full or empty cuts and lower-dimensional contact, $(1-\theta_{jzF})|F|$ at an inside supporting facet, or $\theta_{jzF}|F|$ at an outside supporting facet. The same trace choices satisfy
$$
\sum_j\lambda_j u_j\sum_z s_{jz}=0,\qquad
\sum_j\lambda_j u_j\bigl(\sum_z s_{jz}z\bigr)^\top=0,\qquad
1=\beta\sum_j\lambda_j+\frac{\sum_{j,z}\lambda_jt_j(z)s_{jz}}{dM}.
$$
In $d=1$ all facet and proper section measures are counting measures.

## Proof

Choose the whole-plane cover by 755cb08c379113cc at the positive compatible minimizing family. Its original cap masses are at most $\beta M$, and a positive fiber ball gives $\beta>0$. If a compatible competitor meets the same fixed budgets and has larger mass $M'$, every competitor point lies in a cap of mass at most $\beta M$, hence its normalized maximum depth is at most $\beta M/M'<\beta$. The fixed positive barycentric weights for the specified interior site show that its central fiber is positive. Common continuous dilation restores central unit volume while preserving depth and the facet bound. Thus the candidate is a global volume maximizer for the stated fixed constraints. This uses the covering comparison in 052e61fcdb0be10f in the restricted class; no premise of minimization over all compatible families is needed.

For a positive fiber, shift each of its original unit-normal facet offsets by $-t$. Each original facet persists for small $t$ by shifting a relative interior point inward by $tn_F$, retaining its strict slack in every other row and a relative neighborhood. Candidate vertices are obtained from independent original facet systems and vary affinely in $t$; finitely many affine feasibility and incidence conditions are constant for all sufficiently small $t>0$. Hence the normal fan of the inner parallel body is then fixed, with rays exactly the original facet normals, and refines the original fan: each persistent vertex of the inner bodies converges to a vertex of the old body and its active rows remain active there. In the common simplicial refinement, require linearity on each fiber's own small-erosion fan by linear equations on ray values. A support function linear on such a fan has no facet normal outside its rays. Indeed the normal cone of a facet is one-dimensional, and a refining fan must include that ray. Thus the chart stays in the original facet bound. By 074a5bdf8c59720e, the restricted finite support and compatibility rows have the radial cone $D=\{\delta:A_0\delta\ge0\}$ within the specified linear subspace, and small radial changes remain positive.

Common erosion preserves all anchored Minkowski inclusions. With weights summing to one, $\sum_z p_z(K_z\ominus tB)+tB=\sum_z p_z((K_z\ominus tB)+tB)\subseteq\sum_z p_zK_z$, and if the last sum lies in a target fiber, this puts the eroded sum in its erosion. The small-erosion path is affine in the common-fan supports because each relevant vertex varies affinely in $t$, so its direction $e$ lies in $D$ and $e_z(n_F)=-1$. Common dilation and affine fiber translations remain in the chosen linear subspace. For translations by $Bz+b$, the convexity and compatibility rows vanish in the translation direction because the translation is affine, so $A_0$ vanishes on it.

Apply the shared-plane-owner-derivatives local strip argument at each fiber with its own one-row-per-facet representation and fixed normals. The rows of these per-fiber representations can be changed independently for this derivative computation; compatibility is imposed separately by the support chart. Their offsets are precisely the chart values at $n_F$, and the restricted chart preserves this normal list. The objective has the linear derivative on the left. At a coincident facet the cap derivative density is $\min(\delta_z(n_F),0)$ for a body inside and $\max(\delta_z(n_F),0)$ for a body outside. Hence each actual cap derivative is a branch of the finite set of choices with coefficients $0$ or $|F|$ at all coincidences, and its convex hull gives the stated $\theta$ coefficients. Every branch of every active cap is strictly negative on $e$: a positive-volume cap contains a relatively open positive-area patch of a facet strictly inside its cap, found at an incident facet of a maximizing vertex, and no selected coefficient contributes positively under erosion.

If a direction in $D$ increases the objective and all active cap branches are nonpositive there, add a small positive multiple of $e$. The objective still increases and all those branches are strictly negative. A sufficiently small step then remains compatible, meets all budgets and increases mass, which is impossible. The finite Farkas alternative gives the claimed covector identity after grouping nonnegative cap coefficients into one convexified $L_j$ per cap. At the dilation direction $h$ itself the active homogeneous rows vanish, while the objective evaluates to $dM>0$, so some $\lambda_j>0$.

For a translation vector $c$ of fiber $z$, the contribution to $L_j$ is $s_{jz}u_j\cdot c$. At the support dilation direction the contribution is $d|K_z\cap(H_j)_z|+t_j(z)s_{jz}$. For a proper cut, apply the constant and radial divergence identities on the truncated polytope with its cap section's outward normal $-u_j$. For a body-inside coincident facet, omit the fraction $1-\theta_{jzF}$ of the original facet flux with normal $-u_j$, so the same identities hold with $s_{jz}=(1-\theta_{jzF})|F|$. For a body-outside coincident facet, only its coefficient $\theta_{jzF}|F|$ remains, with outward normal $u_j$ and support value $t_j(z)$. For the remaining cases the identities are those of the full or empty body. Evaluating the covector identity on all affine translations kills the $A_0$ term and the objective, proving both translation balances. Evaluating it on dilation kills $A_0$ and gives $dM=d\sum_j\lambda_j\mu(H_j)+\sum_{j,z}\lambda_jt_j(z)s_{jz}$. All stressed caps have mass $\beta M$, giving the displayed Euler identity. The cap parameters are fixed in this direction.
