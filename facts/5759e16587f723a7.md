## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $d=m+1$, $a<0<b$, and let $r:[a,b]\to(0,\infty)$ be finite and concave. In the planar layer with density $r(z)^{m-1}/2$ on $a\le z\le b,\ |x|\le r(z)$, let $V=\int_a^b r(z)^m\,dz$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $\sigma_s$ be the pushforward to the $z$-coordinate of the section measure obtained by integrating the layer density against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$ on $z+sx=0$. Set $C(s)=\int d\sigma_s$ and $M(s)=\int z\,d\sigma_s$. Let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a convex body centrally symmetric about $0$ and $K=\{(X,z):a\le z\le b,\ X\in r(z)D\}$. Consider any finite list of unit normals $u_i$, with caps $H_i=\{Y:u_i\cdot Y\ge0\}$, sections $K\cap u_i^\perp$ with centroid $0$ and area $S_i=\mathcal H^m(K\cap u_i^\perp)$, and weights $\theta_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i\theta_i=1$ and $\sum_i\theta_iS_i u_i=0$. Put $\gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. Suppose there are real $c,\lambda_L,\lambda_U$ satisfying
$$
\gamma V+\lambda_LM(s)\le P(s)-cC(s)\le(1-\gamma)V+\lambda_UM(s)
$$
for every $s\ge0$. Then $\sum_i\theta_i |K\cap H_i|/|K|\ge\gamma$. The same conclusion holds if one common $c$ satisfies both inequalities after averaging over every centered layer law arising in the menu, with the moment terms then zero.

## Proof

Write $b_i$ for the vertical component of $u_i$. For $b_i>0$, divide the cap inequality by $b_i$ and apply 8f1256d28f434b09 in the horizontal direction of the resulting coefficient, using slope zero when the coefficient is zero. The layer law is a probability, and its average $M$ is zero by actual section centering. The cap mass divided by $|D|$ is its average $P$, and the average $C$ is $b_i S_i/|D|$, because the normal with vertical component one has norm $1/b_i$. Averaging the lower inequality gives $|K\cap H_i|/|D|\ge\gamma V+c b_i S_i/|D|$.

For $b_i<0$, apply the upper inequality to the reverse cap and use complementation in ordinary volume. The reverse raw coarea is $-b_iS_i/|D|$, so the same lower bound holds for the original cap with the signed factor $b_i$. In particular each multiplier is canceled before caps of different orientations are combined. When $b_i=0$, horizontal central symmetry gives half of every fiber, and $1/2\ge\gamma$ since $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$. Multiply the inequalities by $\theta_i$, sum, use the vertical component of the response balance, and divide by $V=|K|/|D|$.
