## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\) be arbitrary positive integers, and let \(\theta\in\mathbb R\). Define
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
                    0\le x_i\le L-hy\text{ for every }i\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y)=
\frac{e^{\theta y}}{\sum_{(\mathbf u,v)\in D}e^{\theta v}}.
\]
For any probability law \(P\) on a nonempty finite support in \(\mathbb R^s\), put \(\tau_P(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}P(H)\) and \(M_3(P)=\sum_{\mathbf z}P(\mathbf z)\tau_P(\mathbf z)^3\), where \(H\) ranges over all closed halfspaces. On a singleton the depth is one. These are the actual depth and original-root-law moment used in ac4157e446425a3d, specialized here to ambient dimension \(s=m+1\), support \(D\), and probability \(p\).

There is one absolute \(\kappa>0\) such that \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-(m+1)}\) for all these parameters, without assuming \(L\ge h\). More explicitly, set \(c_1=60000\), the height-band constant of ac4157e446425a3d, and \(g=(32/27)^{3/2}>1\). One may take
\[
\kappa=\min\left\{\frac1{32},\;
             \frac1{512c_1^4g}\left(\frac{\log g}{8}\right)^8\right\}.
\]
The number \(32/27\) is the uniform reflection-second-moment gain obtained from the explicit statistic \(S\) of 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9, as mapped in the proof below. It is not a capacity-to-depth comparison.

## Proof

We first record two elementary rules for actual depths. If finite laws \(P,V\) in the same ambient space satisfy \(P(\mathbf z)\ge\rho V(\mathbf z)\) at every point for some \(\rho\in[0,1]\), extending \(V\) by zero when needed, then every containing halfspace has \(P\)-mass at least \(\rho\) times its \(V\)-mass. Retaining the original root weights gives
\[
M_3(P)\ge
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\operatorname{supp}V}
 P(\mathbf z)(\rho\tau_V(\mathbf z))^3
\ge\rho^4M_3(V).
\tag{1}
\]
Also, for finite laws \(P_1,P_2\) in separate coordinate blocks, \(\tau_{P_1\otimes P_2}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)\ge\tau_{P_1}(\mathbf z_1)\tau_{P_2}(\mathbf z_2)\), and hence \(M_3(P_1\otimes P_2)\ge M_3(P_1)M_3(P_2)\). Indeed, a containing halfspace contains its parallel translate through the root, whose inequality is the sum of one centered linear functional in each block being nonnegative. It contains the product event where both functionals are nonnegative. Each factor event has probability at least the corresponding actual depth, and independence proves the assertion. A zero functional has probability one. These are the same rules as (9) and (10) in the proof of ac4157e446425a3d.

Every finite probability law \(\pi\) on a line satisfies \(M_3(\pi)\ge1/32\). For completeness, order the atoms and write \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) for the cumulative mass strictly before and up to atom \(i\), respectively. Its depth is \(\min\{b_i,1-a_i\}\). For every \(v\in[a_i,b_i]\), \(\min\{v,1-v\}\le\min\{b_i,1-a_i\}\). Integrating the cubes over the cumulative intervals proves \(M_3(\pi)\ge\int_0^1\min\{v,1-v\}^3\,dv=1/32\). In particular this applies to every uniform consecutive integer interval, including a singleton, as used in ac4157e446425a3d.

Next let \(U_R\) be the uniform law on the integer cube \(\{0,\ldots,R\}^m\), for an integer \(R\ge2\). For \(0\le j\le R\), put \(k_j=2\min(j,R-j)+1\). Specialize 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9 to its scalar interval cardinality \(R+1\), scalar tilt zero, and probabilities \(p_j=1/(R+1)\). Its explicit statistic \(S\) then gives
\[
\frac1{R+1}\sum_{j=0}^{R}
       \left(\frac{k_j}{R+1}\right)^2
=\sum_{j=0}^{R}k_j^2p_j^3
=S(R+1,0)\ge\frac8{27}.
\tag{2}
\]
Only the explicit reflection statistic in that fact is used here.

For a cube root \(\mathbf x\), define its normalized reflection overlap by \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}=U_R(\{\mathbf v:2\mathbf x-\mathbf v\in\{0,\ldots,R\}^m\})\). This is the same type of normalized reflection overlap denoted \(\mathsf H\) in ac4157e446425a3d, now on the cube. The count in each coordinate is \(k_{x_i}\), so \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{k_{x_i}}{R+1}\). Reflection around \(\mathbf x\) pairs the overlap points, with \(\mathbf x\) its sole fixed point. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf x\) contains at least one point from each pair, since their midpoint is \(\mathbf x\). Uniformity gives \(\tau_{U_R}(\mathbf x)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}/2\). Independence of the uniform root coordinates and (2), followed by Jensen for \(v\mapsto v^{3/2}\), therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(U_R)
&\ge\frac18\mathbb E_{U_R}\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}^{3}
 \ge\frac18\left(\mathbb E_{U_R}\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}^{2}\right)^{3/2}\\
&\ge\frac18\left(\frac8{27}\right)^{3m/2}
 =\frac18g^m8^{-m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (3) is an actual-depth rank surplus for every \(R\ge2\). For \(R=0\), \(U_R\) is a singleton and \(M_3(U_R)=1\). For \(R=1\), all \(2^m\) cube vertices have depth exactly \(2^{-m}\): any containing halfspace contains the root atom, and a supporting halfspace can isolate that vertex. Consequently \(M_3(U_1)=8^{-m}\), with no strict surplus needed.

Return to the source law. Put \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\ge0\), \(\ell=L-hK\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\), \(t=K-y\), and \(R_t=\ell+ht\). The invertible affine map \((\mathbf x,y)\mapsto(\mathbf x,t)\) preserves every containing closed-halfspace trace, its probability, and the actual depth moment. Write \(P\) for the pushed-forward original law. Its support has \(0\le t\le K\) and \(\mathbf x\in\{0,\ldots,R_t\}^m\), and
\[
P(\mathbf x,t)=\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^{K}e^{-\theta t}(R_t+1)^m.
\tag{4}
\]
The canceled constant is \(e^{\theta K}\). Let \(q(t)=(R_t+1)^m e^{-\theta t}/\mathcal Z\) be the original height marginal. Conditional on height \(t\), the transverse law is exactly \(U_{R_t}\).

Because the positive integer \(h\) makes \(R_t\) strictly increasing, at most two heights satisfy \(R_t\le1\). If these heights have total \(q\)-mass at least \(1/2\), some such height \(t_0\) has \(q(t_0)\ge1/4\). The law \(P\) pointwise dominates \(q(t_0)\) times the conditional uniform slice law at height \(t_0\). Its actual depths equal those of \(U_{R_{t_0}}\): restricting an ambient affine inequality to the slice gives a transverse affine inequality, and every transverse inequality extends to the ambient space. Thus (1) and the exact singleton or binary value above yield
\[
M_3(P)\ge q(t_0)^4M_3(U_{R_{t_0}})
\ge\frac1{256}8^{-m}
=\frac1{32}8^{-(m+1)}.
\tag{5}
\]
This includes the case \(K=0\) with \(L=1\).

Otherwise the heights with \(R_t\ge2\) form a nonempty tail \(\{a_0,\ldots,K\}\) with original mass \(\mu>1/2\). Condition only for the extraction step on this tail, and call the resulting probability law \(P_{\mathrm{tail}}\). Put \(J=K-a_0\), \(s=t-a_0\), and \(b_s=(R_{a_0}+hs+1)^m\). Its atom probabilities and height normalization are
\[
P_{\mathrm{tail}}(\mathbf x,a_0+s)
=\frac{e^{-\theta s}}{Z_b},
\qquad
Z_b=\sum_{s=0}^{J}e^{-\theta s}b_s.
\tag{6}
\]
The original law satisfies \(P\ge\mu P_{\mathrm{tail}}\) pointwise, extending the conditional law by zero.

When \(J\ge1\), the sequence \(b_s\) is positive and nondecreasing and, for \(1\le u\le v\le J\), satisfies
\[
\frac{b_v}{b_u}
=\left(\frac{R_{a_0}+1+hv}{R_{a_0}+1+hu}\right)^m
\le(v/u)^m.
\tag{7}
\]
Apply the general height-band assertion proved at (1)--(2) of ac4157e446425a3d with its growth exponent \(\nu=m\), its height endpoint \(K=J\), its sequence equal to this \(b_s\), and its \(\eta=\theta\). It supplies a nonempty consecutive interval \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq\{0,\ldots,J\}\) such that \(\lambda=|I|b_a\min_{s\in I}e^{-\theta s}/Z_b\ge[c_1(m+1)^2]^{-1}\). If \(J=0\), take \(I=\{0\}\) and \(\lambda=1\), which satisfies the same bound directly.

Set \(R=R_{a_0+a}\ge2\) and let \(u_{a_0+I}\) be uniform on the translated height interval. The fixed cube \(\{0,\ldots,R\}^m\) is contained in every slice with \(s\in I\). Define the product law \(V=U_R\otimes u_{a_0+I}\), extended by zero elsewhere. On its support, the ratio of the atom in (6) to the atom of \(V\) is \(|I|b_a e^{-\theta s}/Z_b\), so \(P_{\mathrm{tail}}\ge\lambda V\) and hence \(P\ge\mu\lambda V\ge(\lambda/2)V\). Equations (1), (3), and the product and interval estimates give, under the original root law,
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(P)
&\ge(\lambda/2)^4M_3(V)
 \ge(\lambda/2)^4 M_3(U_R)M_3(u_{a_0+I})\\
&\ge\frac{g^m8^{-m}}{4096c_1^4(m+1)^8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The factor \((\lambda/2)^4\) retains one original-root factor and three original-depth factors; no conditioned law replaces the law in the asserted moment.

Finally, applying \(e^u\ge u\) to \(u=(m+1)\log g/8>0\) and raising to the eighth power gives \(g^m/(m+1)^8\ge g^{-1}(\log g/8)^8\). Substituting this into (8), and using \(8^{-m}=8\cdot8^{-(m+1)}\), proves
\[
M_3(P)\ge
\frac1{512c_1^4g}\left(\frac{\log g}{8}\right)^8
8^{-(m+1)}.
\]
Together with (5) and the affine invariance from (4), this proves the stated bound for \(M_3(p)\). Both constants in the minimum defining \(\kappa\) are absolute and positive. The proof covers every positive \(m,h,L\), every real \(\theta\), all singleton and binary slices, and every closed halfspace containing each original root.
