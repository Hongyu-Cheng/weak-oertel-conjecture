## Statement

There exists an absolute constant \(g>1\) such that, for every finite set \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^3\) with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^3\), \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(D)=3\), and \(|D|>8\), and every \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^3\), the law and capacities

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf x}&=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}{Z},
&Z&=\sum_{\mathbf y\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf y},\\
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)&=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^3}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},
&\mathcal B_2(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2
\end{aligned}
\]

satisfy \(64\mathcal B_2(p)\ge g\). Here \(D\) is the support denoted by \(A\) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, with intrinsic rank \(r=3\). Both \(\mathsf C_p\) and \(\mathcal B_2\) have exactly that fact's normalization. The quantifier order is \(\exists g>1\;\forall D\;\forall\boldsymbol\ell\); no restriction is placed on the geometry or the finite tilt. The proof establishes existence, not an explicit numerical value of \(g\).

## Proof

Extend each law by zero to all of \(\mathbb Z^3\), and retain the reflected Hellinger mass of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z^3}
\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}},\\
s(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(p),\\
v(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}p_{\mathbf j}
\bigl(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(p)-s(p)\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The scalar \(s(p)\) is the scalar called \(S\) in equation (36) of that fact, not the mixed-integer set \(S\) of the locked problem. Its equations (33)--(37) give

\[
s(p)=\sum_{\mathbf c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3}\pi_{\mathbf c}^2\ge\frac18,
\qquad
\mathcal B_2(p)\ge s(p)^2+v(p),
\tag{1}
\]

where \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\) is the original law's mass in parity class \(\mathbf c\). These assertions include all boundary centers.

We first prove a halfspace estimate. For any finite law, if a linear functional \(f\) takes distinct values on its support, then

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(p)
\le p_{\mathbf j}+2\sqrt{
 p\{f(\mathbf X)<f(\mathbf j)\}
 p\{f(\mathbf X)>f(\mathbf j)\}}.
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, every reflected pair other than the diagonal consists of one point below and one point above \(\mathbf j\) in this ordering. The two orientations contribute twice the square root of the product of their masses. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds their sum by the product of the square roots of the two corresponding tail masses, proving (2).

Fix \(\varepsilon=1/4096\). Suppose \(s(p)\ge1/8\), and a strict affine halfspace \(H\) contains mass at least \(\delta\), where \(0<\delta\le\varepsilon\), while every atom in \(H\) has mass at most \(\varepsilon\). Orient its defining functional so that the points in \(H\) come first. A sufficiently small generic perturbation makes all support values distinct and preserves this ordering between the points in \(H\) and those outside it: the two finite sets have a positive gap in the original functional values. If the complement is empty, only distinctness is required.

The shortest initial segment of mass at least \(\delta\) lies in \(H\), has mass at most \(\delta+\varepsilon\), and consists of atoms of mass at most \(\varepsilon\). For each of its centers, (2) bounds the reflected mass by \(\varepsilon+2\sqrt{\delta+\varepsilon}\). Since
\(\varepsilon+2\sqrt{2\varepsilon}=1/4096+\sqrt2/32<1/16\), its distance below \(s(p)\) exceeds \(1/16\). Consequently

\[
v(p)\ge\frac{\delta}{256}.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, if \(v(p)<\varepsilon/256\), every strict affine halfspace all of whose atoms have mass at most \(\varepsilon\) has mass at most \(256v(p)\). To see this, a mass at least \(\varepsilon\) contradicts (3) with \(\delta=\varepsilon\); for a positive mass less than \(\varepsilon\), apply (3) with \(\delta\) equal to that mass.

We also need the following elementary geometric bound. If \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) is the convex hull of \(M\) points and has full affine rank, its complement is covered by at most \(\binom M3\) strict affine halfspaces disjoint from \(Q\). Here is a proof independent of lattice volume. Let \(\mathbf b\) be the arithmetic mean of the \(M\) points. Four affinely independent points form a simplex whose barycenter has a small ball around it inside that simplex. This follows directly from continuity of its four barycentric coordinates, which are all positive at the barycenter. Expressing \(\mathbf b\) as \(4/M\) times this barycenter plus the average contribution of the remaining points shows that \(Q\) contains a ball of some radius \(a>0\) around \(\mathbf b\). For \(M=4\), use the simplex ball itself.

Consider the polar polytope
\(\mathcal P_Q=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf b)\le1\text{ for every generating point }\mathbf x\}\).
It is closed and bounded, because the ball in \(Q\) implies \(\|\mathbf u\|\le1/a\); it also contains a ball around zero. At any extreme point of \(\mathcal P_Q\), the vectors \(\mathbf x-\mathbf b\) of active constraints span \(\mathbb R^3\). Otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active vectors would allow small feasible perturbations in both directions, using the positive gaps in the finitely many inactive constraints, contrary to extremality. Three independent active vectors therefore determine that extreme point uniquely. Assigning one such triple to each extreme point is injective, so there are at most \(\binom M3\) extreme points.

For every \(\mathbf y\notin Q\), choose its closest point \(\mathbf z\in Q\) and put \(\mathbf h=\mathbf y-\mathbf z\). Differentiating squared distance along segments from \(\mathbf z\) into \(Q\) gives \(\mathbf h\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\le0\) for every \(\mathbf x\in Q\). Thus
\(\max_{\mathbf x\in Q}\mathbf h\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=\mathbf h\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf b)>0\), where positivity follows from the ball around \(\mathbf b\). Dividing \(\mathbf h\) by this maximum gives an element of \(\mathcal P_Q\) whose scalar product with \(\mathbf y-\mathbf b\) exceeds one.

The maximum of that scalar product over the compact polytope \(\mathcal P_Q\) is attained at an extreme point. For completeness, successively maximize the scalar product and then the three coordinate functions on their preceding maximizing sets. The resulting point is unique; any expression of it as a midpoint of two feasible points would force both points into each successive maximizing set and hence force them to coincide. It is therefore extreme. The strict halfspaces \(\{\mathbf y:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf b)>1\}\), indexed by the extreme points \(\mathbf u\) of \(\mathcal P_Q\), consequently cover \(\mathbb R^3\setminus Q\) and are all disjoint from \(Q\). This proves the geometric bound.

Now suppose, toward a contradiction, that a sequence of supports and tilts in the statement satisfies \(\mathcal B_2(p_k)\to1/64\). Equation (1) implies
\(s(p_k)\to1/8\) and \(v(p_k)\to0\). Let
\(T_k=\{\mathbf x\in D_k:p_{k,\mathbf x}\ge\varepsilon\}\).
It has at most \(4096\) points.

For all sufficiently large \(k\), \(T_k\) has full affine rank three. If it did not, it would lie in an integer affine plane \(L_k\): when \(T_k\) is nonempty, translate by one of its integer points and choose a nonzero integer vector orthogonal to the rational span of its differences; when it is empty, use the plane \(x_3=0\). Both strict sides of \(L_k\) contain only atoms of mass less than \(\varepsilon\). The consequence of (3) gives \(p_k(L_k)\ge1-512v(p_k)\to1\).

The conditional support \(E_k=D_k\cap L_k\) is complete in its intrinsic affine lattice and has rank at most two. In fact any lattice point in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_k)\) belongs to \(D_k\) by completeness and belongs to \(L_k\), and hence belongs to \(E_k\). Its conditional law is log-affine. Restricting every exponential sum to \(E_k\), for every \(\mathbf j\in E_k\), gives
\(\mathsf C_{p_k}(\mathbf j)\ge p_k(L_k)\mathsf C_{p_k(\cdot\mid L_k)}(\mathbf j)\).
The same statement applies in intrinsic coordinates, since ambient linear functionals restrict onto all intrinsic linear functionals. Equation (5) of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 therefore yields

\[
\mathcal B_2(p_k)\ge p_k(L_k)^3\mathcal B_2\bigl(p_k(\cdot\mid L_k)\bigr)
\ge\frac{p_k(L_k)^3}{16}.
\tag{4}
\]

The conditional support is nonempty for large \(k\), and ranks zero and one also satisfy this bound. Equation (4) contradicts the assumed limit \(1/64\), proving the full-rank assertion.

For the remaining indices put \(Q_k=\operatorname{conv}(T_k)\). Completeness and log-affinity give the exact census
\(Q_k\cap\mathbb Z^3=T_k\): every integer point of \(Q_k\) belongs to \(D_k\), and its logarithmic mass is a convex combination of logarithmic masses at points of \(T_k\), so its mass is at least \(\varepsilon\). Thus \(T_k\) is itself a complete full-rank support, including every boundary lattice point.

Apply the geometric bound to \(Q_k\). Each covering halfspace contains only atoms of mass less than \(\varepsilon\), so (3) and the union bound give

\[
1-q_k\le256\binom{4096}{3}v(p_k)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad q_k:=p_k(T_k).
\tag{5}
\]

Define \(\widehat p_k=p_k(\cdot\mid T_k)\). Every atom of \(\widehat p_k\) is at least \(\varepsilon\). Restriction of the capacity exponential sum again gives, for every center in \(T_k\),
\(\mathsf C_{p_k}(\mathbf j)\ge q_k\mathsf C_{\widehat p_k}(\mathbf j)\).
Consequently

\[
\frac1{64}\le\mathcal B_2(\widehat p_k)
\le\frac{\mathcal B_2(p_k)}{q_k^3}\longrightarrow\frac1{64}.
\tag{6}
\]

The restriction argument does not assume that \(Q_k\) is a face of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_k)\), or that any capacity infimum is attained. Every boundary center of \(T_k\) is included, and the omitted original summands are nonnegative. Throughout, \(p_k\) and \(\mathcal B_2(p_k)\) refer to the original support \(D_k\).

For all sufficiently large \(k\), \(|T_k|=8\). If \(|T_k|>8\), equation (7) of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 gives \(\mathcal B_2(\widehat p_k)\ge1/64+\varepsilon^3/4\), incompatible with (6) along any infinite subsequence. If \(|T_k|<8\), the diagonal term in the exponential sum implies \(\mathsf C_{\widehat p_k}(\mathbf j)\ge\widehat p_{k,\mathbf j}\), and convexity of the cube gives \(\mathcal B_2(\widehat p_k)\ge\sum_{\mathbf j}\widehat p_{k,\mathbf j}^3\ge|T_k|^{-2}\ge1/49\), again incompatible with (6).

Moreover, these eight points occupy all eight parity classes. Otherwise two distinct points \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in T_k\) have the same parity, and their midpoint \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) belongs to \(T_k\) by its completeness. Log-affinity gives \(\sqrt{\widehat p_{k,\mathbf x}\widehat p_{k,\mathbf y}}=\widehat p_{k,\mathbf j}\), so the diagonal and the two orientations of this pair give \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(\widehat p_k)\ge3\widehat p_{k,\mathbf j}\). At every other center the reflected mass is at least its atom mass. Therefore the reflected-pair capacity bound gives

\[
\mathcal B_2(\widehat p_k)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in T_k}\widehat p_{k,\mathbf z}^3
   +8\widehat p_{k,\mathbf j}^3
\ge\frac1{64}+8\varepsilon^3.
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (6) excludes such a duplicate along any infinite subsequence. Eight distinct classes are all the classes of \(\mathbb Z^3\) modulo two.

Finally \(|D_k|>8\), so \(D_k\setminus T_k\) is nonempty. Choose a point \(\mathbf w\) of maximum original mass there, and write \(b=p_{k,\mathbf w}\). Then \(0<b<\varepsilon\). There is a point \(\mathbf v\in T_k\) in the same parity class as \(\mathbf w\). The integer midpoint \(\mathbf m=(\mathbf w+\mathbf v)/2\) belongs to \(D_k\) by completeness, and the original law satisfies exactly
\(p_{k,\mathbf m}^2=b\,p_{k,\mathbf v}\).
If \(\mathbf m\notin T_k\), then
\(p_{k,\mathbf m}\ge\sqrt{b\varepsilon}>b\), contradicting the choice of \(\mathbf w\). Thus \(\mathbf m\in T_k\), and
\(b=p_{k,\mathbf m}^2/p_{k,\mathbf v}\ge\varepsilon^2\), since \(p_{k,\mathbf m}\ge\varepsilon\) and \(p_{k,\mathbf v}\le1\). It follows that \(1-q_k\ge\varepsilon^2\), contradicting (5). This is an exact obstruction to completing any eight-parity high-mass core by arbitrarily small-mass additional sites; it neither assumes primitive intermediate sites are absent nor selects an apex geometry.

We have excluded every sequence in the stated rank-three class with \(\mathcal B_2(p_k)\to1/64\). This class is nonempty, for example for \(D=\{0,1,2\}^3\) with zero tilt, and \(\mathcal B_2(p)\le1\) because the defining infimum is at most its value at \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\). Let \(\gamma\) be the infimum of \(\mathcal B_2(p)\) over exactly this class of supports and finite tilts. Accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 gives \(\gamma\ge1/64\). Equality would, by the definition of infimum, produce a sequence just excluded. Hence \(\gamma>1/64\), and \(g=64\gamma>1\) proves the claimed inequality simultaneously for every support and tilt.
