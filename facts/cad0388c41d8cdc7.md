## Statement

Fix an absolute constant \(C_{\rm SS}\ge1\) for which the following form of the Sárközy–Szemerédi theorem holds: for every integer \(r\ge1\), every \(r\) distinct integers \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\), and independent uniform signs \(\varepsilon_a\in\lbrace -1,1\rbrace\),
$$
\sup_{u\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb P\left(\sum_{a=1}^r v_a\varepsilon_a=u\right)
\le C_{\rm SS}r^{-3/2}.
\tag{1}
$$
Such an absolute constant exists by the external theorem specified below. Set
$$
C_0=4C_{\rm SS},\qquad
\eta=\frac{2^{-12}}{C_0^2},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2^{-37}}{C_0^2}.
\tag{2}
$$
Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer and let \(1\le h_1<\cdots<h_m\) be integers. Write \(\mathbf e_i\) for the \(i\)-th standard basis vector in the active coordinate block \(\mathbb R^m\). A label is a pair \(q=(b,i)\) with \(b\in\lbrace 0,1\rbrace^m\), \(1\le i\le m\), and \(b_i=0\). Its active vector and energy are
$$
d_q=b+2\mathbf e_i,\qquad
e_q=\sum_{a=1}^m h_ab_a+2h_i.
$$
Choose any nonempty subfamily \(\mathcal Q_0\) of these labels and any integer \(H\le\min_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}e_q\). For each \(q\in\mathcal Q_0\), take a private group \(G_q\) of
$$
n_q=2^{e_q-H+1}\ge2
$$
neutral coordinates, with these groups pairwise disjoint. Put
$$
L=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}n_q,\qquad k=m+L,\qquad N=k+1,\qquad Q=2^k.
$$
For a label \(q\), let \(E_q\) consist of all points of \(\mathbb Z^{m+L}\) whose active vector is \(d_q\), with exactly one neutral coordinate equal to two, that coordinate belonging to \(G_q\), and every other neutral coordinate arbitrary in \(\lbrace 0,1\rbrace\). Define exactly
$$
E=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}E_q,\qquad
D=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^k,\qquad
A=D\times\lbrace 0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N.
\tag{3}
$$
No Boolean points are added to (3). Use the original field and original law
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta&=((h_a\log2)_{a=1}^m,0,\ldots,0),
&w_x&=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),\\
Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
&p_{(x,0)}&=p_x=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Define the containing-closed-halfspace depth and the two original cubics by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed affine halfspace}\\z\in\mathcal H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap\mathcal H}p_u,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_cs_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
The sum defining \(s_p(c)\) is ordered, with its diagonal counted once. Halfspaces may be required to have a nonzero normal. Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-N},\qquad
T(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$
More precisely, \(D\) is complete, ternary, and every point has at most two coordinates equal to two. Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace a:x_a=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace,
&D_2&=D\setminus D_1,
&\alpha&=p(D_1),
&S_4&=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4,\\
t_0&=2^{-10}/Q,
&t_j&=t_02^{-j}\quad(j\ge0),\\
E_j&=\lbrace x\in D_2:t_j/2<p_x\le t_j\rbrace,
&n_j&=|E_j|,
&m_j&=p(E_j),\\
\mathcal J&=\lbrace j\ge0:n_j\ge2Q\rbrace,
&q_j&=|\lbrace x\bmod2:x\in E_j\rbrace|,\\
C_j&=\lbrace (x+y)/2:x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\rbrace,
&\omega_j&=t_j|C_j|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
If
$$
\alpha<1/4,\qquad S_4Q^3<2^{-40},
\tag{8}
$$
then \(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\), every \(\omega_j\) for \(j\in\mathcal J\) is positive, and the actual band quantities satisfy
$$
\Gamma:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^2Q}{q_j},\qquad
\Omega:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j>0,\qquad
\frac{\Gamma^3}{\Omega^2}\ge\eta.
\tag{9}
$$
The only accepted predecessor used is the complete one-step assertion of fact f27ee487069e27e7, applied to the actual conditional law on \(D_1\).

## Proof

All probabilities in this proof are the original probabilities (4), except for the explicitly specified conditional core law near the end.

First determine the actual support. For each label \(q\), let \(F_q\) consist of points with active vector \(d_q\), all neutral coordinates binary, and at least two neutral ones in \(G_q\). Let \(B_{\rm act}=D\cap\lbrace 0,1\rbrace^k\) denote the actual Boolean part of the convex hull. Then the exact disjoint census is
$$
D=B_{\rm act}\mathbin{\dot\cup}
\coprod_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}(E_q\mathbin{\dot\cup}F_q),\qquad
D_2=E,\qquad
D_1=B_{\rm act}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\coprod_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}F_q.
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed, all generating coordinates lie in \([0,2]\). If an integral convex combination has an active coordinate equal to two, every positive-coefficient generator has that same active two. Every other active coordinate of those generators is binary. Its integral value therefore forces all such generators to agree in that coordinate. Thus all representing generators have one and the same label \(q\). In particular two active twos are impossible. If its neutral coordinates are all binary, their sum over \(G_q\) is at least two, since that bound holds for every generator. Thus this binary neutral vector has at least two ones in \(G_q\). Conversely, any such binary vector is the midpoint of two points of \(E_q\): choose two of its private ones and replace their pair \((1,1)\) by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\), retaining every other coordinate. This proves precisely the claimed \(F_q\) part.

If an integral point has a neutral two at coordinate \(v\in G_q\), extremality of two forces every representing generator to have a two at \(v\). Disjointness of the private groups forces all those generators to lie in \(E_q\), with that same neutral two and the same active vector. Their other neutral coordinates are binary, so the integral point is in \(E_q\). This excludes a point with Boolean active vector and a neutral two, and excludes two neutral twos. Every remaining integral point is Boolean. Distinct labels have distinct active vectors, so all unions in (10) are disjoint. These arguments also exclude every higher coordinate or point with more than two twos. Finally, \(E\subseteq D\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E)\) implies \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=\operatorname{conv}(E)\), establishing completeness. No count of \(B_{\rm act}\) is required below.

Put
$$
M=|\mathcal Q_0|,\qquad
M_e=|\lbrace q\in\mathcal Q_0:e_q=e\rbrace|,\qquad
M_{\max}=\max_e M_e,\qquad
K=2^{L-H},\qquad \theta=K/Z.
\tag{11}
$$
Here \(e\) ranges over integers. The different symbol \(\theta\) is a label mass and does not denote the absolute constant \(\kappa\), nor the canonical support \(A\). For each label, all its endpoints have weight \(2^{-e_q}\) and
$$
|E_q|=n_q2^{L-1},\qquad
\sum_{x\in E_q}w_x=n_q2^{L-1-e_q}=K.
$$
Consequently
$$
p(E)=M\theta=1-\alpha,\qquad
\theta=\frac{1-\alpha}{M},\qquad
M\le m2^{m-1}.
\tag{12}
$$

We next bound actual label multiplicities. For \(r\ge1\), the substitution \(\varepsilon_a=2\xi_a-1\) gives
$$
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{a=1}^r v_a\xi_a=u\right)
=\mathbb P\left(\sum_{a=1}^r v_a\varepsilon_a=2u-\sum_{a=1}^r v_a\right)
\le C_{\rm SS}r^{-3/2}
\tag{13}
$$
for independent uniform bits \(\xi_a\). For \(m\ge2\), apply this to the \(m-1\) distinct integers \(h_a\) with \(a\ne i\), separately for each \(i\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_e
&\le\sum_{i=1}^m
\#\left\lbrace (b_a)_{a\ne i}\in\lbrace 0,1\rbrace^{m-1}:
\sum_{a\ne i}h_ab_a=e-2h_i\right\rbrace\\
&\le mC_{\rm SS}2^{m-1}(m-1)^{-3/2}
\le C_0\frac{2^m}{\sqrt m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
For the last inequality, the ratio of the preceding expression to \(C_{\rm SS}2^m/\sqrt m\) is \(\tfrac12(m/(m-1))^{3/2}\le\sqrt2<4\). If \(m=1\), there is just one possible label, so \(M_{\max}=M=1\le C_0 2^m/\sqrt m\). Thus (14) holds for all stated \(m\), including the case where the deletion would otherwise leave zero variables.

Here is the exact census within an endpoint energy. For an occupied integer energy \(e\), write
$$
\nu_e=2^{e-H+1},\qquad f(n)=1-(n+1)2^{-n}.
$$
Every endpoint at this energy has probability \(p_e=2^{-e}/Z\). Any band in (7) contains at most one occupied integer endpoint energy, because its probability interval is half-open with ratio two. If it contains energy \(e\), its ratio
$$
\gamma=t_j/p_e\in[1,2)
\tag{15}
$$
is the same for every occupied band: for any two such bands, their ratios differ by an integral power of two, and both ratios lie in \([1,2)\).

Two labels of the same energy with the same active parity have the same \(b\); their energies then imply \(2h_i=2h_{i'}\), so \(i=i'\). Hence distinct labels at energy \(e\) have distinct active parities. Within a single label, the occupied neutral parities are exactly the binary vectors having at least one zero in \(G_q\), since the neutral two must occur at a zero of that parity. Therefore the actual number of occupied parities at energy \(e\) is
$$
q_j=2^L\sum_{q:e_q=e}(1-2^{-n_q})
=2^LM_e(1-2^{-\nu_e})\le2^LM_e.
\tag{16}
$$
In particular this count is for actual endpoints and does not use hypothetical Boolean atoms.

Distinct same-parity endpoints of energy \(e\) must belong to one label. Within that label, their neutral twos must occur at different private coordinates, since with the same two position parity would fix every coordinate. The other endpoint has zero at each of those two positions, and all remaining neutral coordinates agree. Their midpoint consequently lies in \(F_q\). Conversely the midpoint construction used to prove (10) has same-parity distinct endpoints, so every point of \(F_q\) arises. Thus, for the band containing \(e\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_j&=\coprod_{q:e_q=e}F_q,\\
|F_q|&=2^{L-n_q}(2^{n_q}-n_q-1)=2^Lf(n_q),\\
m_j&=\theta M_e,\qquad
|C_j|=2^LM_ef(\nu_e),\\
\omega_j&=\gamma\theta\,2^{H-e}M_ef(\nu_e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
This also identifies the complete deduplicated midpoint image, rather than an enlargement of it.

Assume (8). Removing endpoints of probability greater than \(t_0\) removes mass at most
$$
\sum_{\substack{x\in D_2\\p_x>t_0}}p_x
\le t_0^{-3}S_4<2^{-10}.
$$
The mass in bands with \(n_j<2Q\) is at most
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}2Qt_j=4Qt_0=2^{-8}.
$$
Hence the retained dense mass satisfies
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j
>1-\frac14-2^{-10}-2^{-8}
=\frac{763}{1024}>\frac12.
\tag{18}
$$
All sums of occupied bands are finite. Let \(M_{\rm dense}\) be the number of labels whose endpoint energy lies in a dense retained band. Equation (17) gives \(\theta M_{\rm dense}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j>1/2\). Using (16), (12), and (8),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma
&\ge 2^m\theta^2 M_{\rm dense}
>2^{m-1}\theta
>\frac38\frac{2^m}{M}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
Every \(e_q\ge H\). Since \(0<f(\nu_e)\le1\) for \(\nu_e\ge2\), (15)--(17) imply
$$
\Omega
\le2\theta M_{\max}\sum_{e=H}^{\infty}2^{H-e}
=4\theta M_{\max}
\le\frac{4M_{\max}}{M}.
\tag{20}
$$
Every dense band has a nonempty midpoint image, as is also proved by the pair count below; therefore \(\Omega>0\). Combining (19)--(20), (14), and the label-count bound in (12) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Gamma^3}{\Omega^2}
&\ge\frac{27}{8192}\frac{2^{3m}}{M M_{\max}^2}\\
&\ge\frac{27}{8192}
\frac{2^{3m}}{(m2^{m-1})(C_0^22^{2m}/m)}
=\frac{27}{4096C_0^2}\ge\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
This proves the asserted refined stock bound using the actual parities and actual midpoint images.

For completeness, we now prove its original-law cubic consequence, with no unproved band reduction. The affine law gives
$$
p_{(x+y)/2}^2=p_xp_y
\tag{22}
$$
whenever \(x,y\in D\) have an integral midpoint. Completeness puts such a midpoint in \(D\). Distinct unordered reflected pairs about any center \(c\) have disjoint endpoints. Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains an endpoint of each pair: otherwise the two endpoints strictly fail the defining closed inequality and their average fails it too. The center itself is distinct from those endpoints. Consequently, if \(R(c)\) is the sum of \(\min(p_x,p_y)\) over any chosen collection of ordered distinct reflected pairs, closed halfspaces give
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\frac12R(c).
\tag{23}
$$
In particular \(M_3(p),T(p)\ge S_4\), because \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c\) and \(s_p(c)\ge p_c\). This argument remains valid at every halfspace boundary and every affine rank. A nonzero normal does not cause an exception; the extra zero coordinate supplies a nonzero-normal halfspace containing the full support.

For a dense band \(j\), let \(n_{b,j}\) be its count in occupied parity \(b\) and let
$$
v_j=\sum_b n_{b,j}(n_{b,j}-1)
$$
be its number of ordered distinct same-parity pairs. Cauchy's inequality and \(n_j\ge2Q\ge2q_j\) give
$$
v_j\ge\frac{n_j^2}{q_j}-n_j\ge\frac{n_j^2}{2q_j}>0.
\tag{24}
$$
Thus \(C_j\ne\varnothing\) and \(\omega_j>0\), as used above. At a center \(c\in C_j\), let \(R_j(c)\) sum \(\min(p_x,p_y)\) over just those ordered distinct pairs in \(E_j\) with midpoint \(c\). Put \(r_j=|C_j|\). Formula (22) and the band bounds imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr L_j
&:=\sum_{c\in C_j}p_c^{1/3}R_j(c)\\
&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}
\ge(t_j/2)^{4/3}v_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
Hölder's inequality on the actual \(r_j\) centers yields
$$
\mathscr L_j^3\le r_j^2\sum_{c\in C_j}p_cR_j(c)^3.
$$
Using (24), \(m_j\le t_jn_j\), and \(\omega_j=t_jr_j\), it follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c\in C_j}p_cR_j(c)^3
&\ge\frac{t_j^4v_j^3}{16r_j^2}
\ge\frac{t_j^4n_j^6}{128q_j^3r_j^2}
\ge\frac{m_j^6}{128q_j^3\omega_j^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
By (22), every \(c\in C_j\) has \(t_j/2<p_c\le t_j\), so the different \(C_j\)'s are disjoint, including at dyadic boundaries. Equations (23), (26), and the definition of the full ordered \(s_p\) therefore give
$$
\begin{aligned}
T(p)&\ge\frac1{128}\Psi,
&M_3(p)&\ge\frac1{1024}\Psi,
&\Psi&:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{q_j^3\omega_j^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
Indeed \(s_p(c)\ge R_j(c)\) and \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge R_j(c)/2\) at the selected centers. A final Hölder inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma
&=Q\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}
\left(\frac{m_j^6}{q_j^3\omega_j^2}\right)^{1/3}\omega_j^{2/3}
\le Q\Psi^{1/3}\Omega^{2/3},\\
\Gamma^3&\le Q^3\Psi\Omega^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$
Thus (21), (27), and (28) imply in regime (8) that
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\eta}{1024Q^3}
=\frac{2^{-19}}{C_0^2}\,8^{-N},\qquad
T(p)\ge\frac{\eta}{128Q^3}.
\tag{29}
$$

It remains to cover the complementary regimes. The actual subset \(D_1\) is complete: an integral point of its convex hull belongs to \(D\); if it had two twos, coordinate extremality would force every positive-coefficient core point to have those same two twos, which is impossible. Consequently the final complete one-step assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 applies to \(D_1\), whenever \(\alpha>0\). Its dimension, ambient support and field map to the present ones as \(k\), \(D_1\times\lbrace 0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), and \(\beta\); its normalizer is \(Z_1=\alpha Z\), and its probability is exactly \(\widehat p_x=p_x/\alpha\). Its occupied parity count is at most \(Q\). It yields
$$
M_3(\widehat p),T(\widehat p)\ge\frac1{512Q^3}.
$$
For centers in \(D_1\), every original containing halfspace has mass at least \(\alpha\) times its conditional-core mass. Restricting the original full ordered reflection sum to endpoint pairs in \(D_1\) likewise gives at least \(\alpha\) times the conditional reflection sum. In either cubic, multiplication by the original center mass supplies the fourth factor of \(\alpha\), so
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512Q^3}.
\tag{30}
$$
For \(\alpha=0\) the displayed lower bound is zero and remains valid. Thus \(\alpha\ge1/4\) implies both cubics are at least \(2^{-14}8^{-N}\). Independently, if \(S_4Q^3\ge2^{-40}\), the atomic bound after (23) gives both cubics at least \(2^{-37}8^{-N}\). Otherwise (8) holds and (29) applies. Since \(C_0\ge4\), the common coefficient \(\kappa=2^{-37}/C_0^2\) from (2) is no larger than each of these three coefficients. This proves (6) for every support and original law in the statement.

## External sources

Hoi H. Nguyen, A new approach to an old problem of Erdős and Moser, arXiv:1112.0755v1, [primary research paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0755), Theorem 1.1 on printed page 2 (PDF page index 1). The theorem states that a set of \(r\) distinct integers has signed-sum concentration \(O(r^{-3/2})\); the independent signs and concentration are defined on printed page 1. The implied constant is absolute. Enlarging it to cover any finite initial range and to be at least one gives the fixed \(C_{\rm SS}\) in (1). The source statement was inspected directly. The exact sign-to-bit conversion is (13). The coefficients used there are the distinct positive integers remaining after deleting \(h_i\), with no multiplicities, real approximation, or interval anti-concentration substitution. The separate \(m=1\) check in (14) avoids applying the theorem to zero coefficients.
