## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(d\ge2\). Let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be compact and convex, let \(\ell:P\to\mathbb R\) be convex, let \(u:P\to\mathbb R\) be concave, and let \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^{d-1}\) be a full-dimensional convex body. Put
\[
V:=\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(U)>0
\]
and let
\[
C:=\left\{(y,s,v):y\in P,\ \ell(y)\le s\le u(y),\ v\in U\right\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d
\]
be a full-dimensional convex body. Write
\[
h(y):=u(y)-\ell(y),
\qquad
D:=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n:h(z)>0\},
\qquad
h_z:=h(z).
\]
Then \(D\) is finite and lattice-convex:
\[
\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D.
\tag{1}
\]
The positive-volume lattice fibers are exactly
\[
C_z=[\ell(z),u(z)]\times U\qquad(z\in D),
\]
and, when \(D\ne\varnothing\),
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in D}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
=V\sum_{z\in D}h_z,
\qquad
M_{\mathrm{vol}}(C):=\max_{z\in D}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
=V\max_{z\in D}h_z.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(D=\varnothing\), then \(W=0\) and all capacity assertions below are vacuous. Hence assume \(D\ne\varnothing\).

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) and \(0<t\le\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), define
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\le f\le1\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=t
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) and \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\), put
\[
\Lambda_x(E):=
\left\{
\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=x
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Fix \(\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D\),
\(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E)\), and strictly positive scalar masses
\[
0<\tau_z\le h_z,
\qquad
t_z:=V\tau_z
\qquad(z\in E).
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
L_z:=\ell_z+\frac{\tau_z}{2},
\qquad
U_z:=u_z-\frac{\tau_z}{2},
\qquad
\ell_z:=\ell(z),\quad u_z:=u(z).
\tag{6}
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. Exact metronoid interval and fixed-centroid section. For every \(z\in E\), projection onto the scalar coordinate satisfies
\[
\operatorname{proj}_s\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)=[L_z,U_z],
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
[L_z,U_z]\times\{\operatorname{cg}(U)\}
\subseteq\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\tag{8}
\]

2. Complete-core interval criterion and affine dual. Put
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
A_m(L):=\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zL_z,
\qquad
B_m(U):=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zU_z.
\tag{10}
\]
The maxima and minima in (10) are attained, and
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_m(L)\le B_m(U).
\tag{11}
\]
When (11) holds, the complete core contains the entire fixed-centroid section
\[
[A_m(L),B_m(U)]\times\{\operatorname{cg}(U)\}
\subseteq\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t),
\tag{12}
\]
and its scalar projection is exactly \([A_m(L),B_m(U)]\).

Moreover, the exact attained LP dual formulas are
\[
A_m(L)=
\min\left\{p(m):p:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\text{ affine},\ p(z)\ge L_z\ (z\in E)\right\},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
B_m(U)=
\max\left\{q(m):q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\text{ affine},\ q(z)\le U_z\ (z\in E)\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently, (11) is also equivalent to the existence of affine \(p,q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
p(z)\ge L_z,
\qquad
q(z)\le U_z
\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
p(m)\le q(m).
\tag{15}
\]
These dual optima are attained even when \(E\) has affine rank below \(n\).

3. Exact finite convex-concave realizability. Let \(D_0\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, nonempty, and lattice-convex, and prescribe \(\ell_z<u_z\) for every \(z\in D_0\). There exist a convex \(\ell:Q\to\mathbb R\) and a concave \(u:Q\to\mathbb R\), where \(Q:=\operatorname{conv}(D_0)\), whose restrictions to \(D_0\) are the prescribed data if and only if
\[
\ell_x\le\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_z\ell_z,
\qquad
u_x\ge\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_zu_z
\tag{R}
\]
for every \(x\in D_0\) and every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_x(D_0)\). When (R) holds, exact extensions are
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(y):=
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_y(D_0)}\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_z\ell_z,
\qquad
u^{\mathrm{ccv}}(y):=
\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_y(D_0)}\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_zu_z.
\tag{16}
\]
They satisfy, for \(h_{\min}:=\min_{z\in D_0}(u_z-\ell_z)>0\),
\[
u^{\mathrm{ccv}}(y)-\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(y)\ge h_{\min}
\qquad(y\in Q).
\tag{17}
\]
In center-width variables
\[
c_z:=\frac{\ell_z+u_z}{2},
\qquad
h_z:=u_z-\ell_z,
\]
(R) implies the precise finite coupling
\[
2\left|c_x-\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_zc_z\right|
\le h_x-\sum_{z\in D_0}\lambda_zh_z
\tag{18}
\]
for every \(x\in D_0\) and \(\lambda\in\Lambda_x(D_0)\). In particular, the right-hand side is nonnegative.

4. Attained finite capacity. Let \(\mathscr R_n\) be the class of triples \((D_0,\ell,u)\) satisfying the hypotheses and the finite conditions (R) in part 3. For \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\), define \(\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\) as the supremum of
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\tau_z
\tag{19}
\]
over all \(\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D_0\), all
\[
m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E),
\qquad
0<\tau_z\le h_z\quad(z\in E),
\tag{20}
\]
such that
\[
\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\left(\ell_z+\frac{\tau_z}{2}\right)
\le
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\left(u_z-\frac{\tau_z}{2}\right)
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}\tau_z
\ge2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}\tau_z
\tag{22}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(J\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\).

For fixed \(E,m\), condition (21) is the finite family of linear inequalities
\[
\frac12\sum_{z\in E}(\lambda_z+\mu_z)\tau_z
\le
\sum_{z\in E}\mu_zu_z-
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\ell_z
\tag{23}
\]
over all pairs \(\lambda,\mu\) of vertices of \(\Lambda_m(E)\). Condition (22) is also a finite family of linear inequalities, one for each subset of \(E\) that occurs as \(E\cap J\). Thus each fixed \((E,m)\) gives a finite linear program after allowing \(0\le\tau_z\le h_z\). Globally, \(\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\) is attained by some admissible tuple satisfying the strict inequalities \(\tau_z>0\); in particular, it is a maximum, not merely a supremum.

5. Exact identity with geometric capacity. Define, exactly as in accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`,
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C):=
\sup\left\{
\sum_{z\in E}t_z:
\begin{array}{l}
\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D,\quad 0<t_z\le Vh_z,\\
\text{there is }m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E)
\text{ with }\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing,\\
\displaystyle
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z\ge2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
\text{ for every proper closed halfspace }J\ni m
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Then the exact geometric capacity identity is
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
=V\Phi_n(D,\ell|_D,u|_D).
}
\tag{25}
\]
The correspondence in (25) is exactly \(t_z=V\tau_z\); it preserves all barycentric representations, every proper closed base halfspace, strict positivity of every selected mass, and omission of all zero-width fibers.

6. Converse full-dimensional realization. Conversely, every \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\), including data whose support has affine rank below \(n\), is realized by a full-dimensional prismatic convex body with continuous dimension \(d=2\), fixed factor \(U=[0,1]\), \(V=1\), positive lattice support exactly \(D_0\), and fiber widths exactly \(h_z=u_z-\ell_z\).

7. Equivalent finite extremal problem. Therefore the prismatic metronoid-core assertion
\[
\exists\eta_{\mathrm{pr}}>0\quad
\forall n\ge3\quad
\forall\text{ full-dimensional prismatic }C,
\qquad
\max\left\{
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
2^nM_{\mathrm{vol}}(C)
\right\}
\ge\eta_{\mathrm{pr}}W
\tag{PMC}
\]
is equivalent, with exactly the same best constant, to the finite statement
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\exists\eta_{\mathrm{pr}}>0\quad
\forall n\ge3\quad
\forall(D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n,\\
\max\left\{
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u),
2^n\max_{z\in D_0}(u_z-\ell_z)
\right\}
\ge
\eta_{\mathrm{pr}}
\sum_{z\in D_0}(u_z-\ell_z).
\end{gathered}
}
\tag{FE}
\]
In particular, a sequence violating every positive constant in (FE) yields, by part 6, an exact full-dimensional \(d=2\) prismatic counterexample sequence. No assertion is made here that (FE) holds or fails.

8. Capped-excess certificate. For every \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\) and every \(q>0\), put
\[
\tau_z^{(q)}:=\min\{h_z,\,2(h_z-q)_+\},
\qquad
E_q:=\{z\in D_0:h_z>q\},
\qquad
T_q:=\sum_{z\in D_0}\tau_z^{(q)}.
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\max\left\{
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u),
2^n\max_{z\in D_0}h_z
\right\}
\ge
\min\{T_q,\,2^{n+1}q\}.
}
\tag{27}
\]
More precisely, if \(T_q>0\), there is a weighted lattice centerpoint \(m\in D_0\cap\operatorname{conv}(E_q)\) satisfying (22) for the positive masses \(\tau_z^{(q)}\). If \(h_m\le2q\), those masses satisfy the complete interval-core condition (21), so \(\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\ge T_q\). If \(h_m>2q\), then \(2^n\max_zh_z>2^{n+1}q\).

9. Width-band corollary. As a corollary of the same complete-core characterization, if \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\) and there are \(a>0\) and \(1\le\kappa<2\) such that
\[
a\le h_z\le\kappa a
\qquad(z\in D_0),
\tag{28}
\]
then
\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge(2-\kappa)a|D_0|
\ge\frac{2-\kappa}{\kappa}\sum_{z\in D_0}h_z.
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently, every geometric prismatic realization satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\ge\frac{2-\kappa}{\kappa}W.
}
\tag{30}
\]
Equivalently, widths in \([a,(1+\varepsilon)a]\), with \(0\le\varepsilon<1\), give
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}W.
\tag{31}
\]

## Proof

Because \(P\) is compact, \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and hence \(D\), is finite. The function \(h=u-\ell=u+(-\ell)\) is concave. If
\[
m=\sum_{z\in D}\lambda_zz\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
\lambda_z\ge0,\qquad\sum_z\lambda_z=1,
\]
then, after discarding zero coefficients,
\[
h(m)\ge\sum_z\lambda_zh_z>0.
\]
Thus \(m\in D\), proving (1). For \(z\in D\), the fiber is the Cartesian product of an interval of length \(h_z\) and \(U\), so (2) follows. Lattice fibers of zero width have zero \(d\)-volume and are not in \(D\).

We prove part 1. Fix \(z\in E\) and abbreviate \(a:=\ell_z\), \(b:=u_z\), \(\tau:=\tau_z\), and \(t:=t_z=V\tau\). Let \(f:[a,b]\times U\to[0,1]\) be an admissible metronoid density of mass \(t\), and define its scalar marginal
\[
\bar f(s):=\int_U f(s,v)\,dv,
\qquad
g(s):=\frac{\bar f(s)}V.
\]
Then
\[
0\le g\le1,
\qquad
\int_a^b g(s)\,ds=\tau,
\]
and the scalar coordinate of the normalized first moment of \(f\) is
\[
\frac1t\int_a^b s\bar f(s)\,ds
=\frac1\tau\int_a^b s g(s)\,ds.
\tag{32}
\]
Among all such \(g\), the first moment is minimized by
\(g_-:=\mathbf1_{[a,a+\tau]}\). Indeed,
\[
\int_a^b(s-a-\tau)(g-g_-)\,ds\ge0,
\]
because both factors have the same sign on each side of \(a+\tau\); since \(g\) and \(g_-\) have the same mass, this gives
\[
\int_a^b s g(s)\,ds\ge\int_a^{a+\tau}s\,ds.
\]
The symmetric argument with \(g_+:=\mathbf1_{[b-\tau,b]}\) gives the maximum. The two normalized extreme means are
\[
a+\frac\tau2=L_z,
\qquad
b-\frac\tau2=U_z.
\]
The admissible set of densities is convex and the first-moment map is affine, so every intermediate scalar mean is attained. This proves (7).

For any admissible scalar density \(g:[a,b]\to[0,1]\) of mass \(\tau\), use the product density
\[
f(s,v):=g(s).
\]
It has mass \(V\tau=t\). Its normalized \(v\)-coordinate is
\[
\frac1t\int_a^b\int_U v g(s)\,dv\,ds
=\frac{\tau}{V\tau}\int_U v\,dv
=\operatorname{cg}(U),
\]
while its scalar coordinate is (32). Letting that coordinate range over \([L_z,U_z]\) proves (8). The hypotheses \(z\in D\), \(h_z>0\), and \(0<t_z\le Vh_z\) ensure throughout that \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body and that no zero-mass metronoid is used.

For part 2, fix \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\). Linear projection commutes with weighted Minkowski addition, so (7) gives
\[
\operatorname{proj}_s
\left(\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\right)
=
\left[\sum_z\lambda_zL_z,\ \sum_z\lambda_zU_z\right].
\tag{33}
\]
If \(x\in\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\), its scalar coordinate belongs to every interval in (33), which proves the forward implication in (11).

Conversely, suppose \(A_m(L)\le B_m(U)\), and choose any \(r\in[A_m(L),B_m(U)]\). For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), part 1 gives
\[
\left[\sum_z\lambda_zL_z,\ \sum_z\lambda_zU_z\right]
\times\{\operatorname{cg}(U)\}
\subseteq
\sum_z\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]
The point \((r,\operatorname{cg}(U))\) therefore belongs to every weighted Minkowski sum, proving (11) and (12). Equation (33) also shows that no point in the core can have scalar coordinate outside \([A_m(L),B_m(U)]\), so the projection assertion is exact. Notice that the choices of points in the individual metronoids may depend on \(\lambda\); no common affine selector over \(E\) is assumed.

The set \(\Lambda_m(E)\) is a nonempty compact polytope, so the primal extrema in (10) are finite and attained. The linear program for \(A_m(L)\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}\quad&\sum_zL_z\lambda_z,\\
\text{subject to}\quad&\sum_z\lambda_z=1,\quad
\sum_z\lambda_zz=m,\quad\lambda_z\ge0,
\end{aligned}
\]
whose dual, with variables \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) and \(g\in\mathbb R^n\), is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{minimize}\quad&\alpha+g\cdot m,\\
\text{subject to}\quad&\alpha+g\cdot z\ge L_z\quad(z\in E).
\end{aligned}
\]
A sufficiently large constant is dual feasible. Finite-dimensional strong LP duality gives equality and dual attainment, which is (13) with \(p(x)=\alpha+g\cdot x\). Similarly, the dual of the minimization defining \(B_m(U)\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize}\quad&\beta+k\cdot m,\\
\text{subject to}\quad&\beta+k\cdot z\le U_z\quad(z\in E),
\end{aligned}
\]
and a sufficiently small constant is feasible. Strong duality gives (14) and attainment with \(q(x)=\beta+k\cdot x\).

If \(E\) has affine rank below \(n\), delete redundant equality rows and solve these primal-dual pairs in \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\). An affine dual optimizer on \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\) extends affinely to \(\mathbb R^n\). Thus rank deficiency only adds dual lineality and cannot destroy attainment. Equations (13) and (14) immediately give (15) from (11). Conversely, if affine \(p,q\) satisfy (15), then every \(\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_m(E)\) satisfy
\[
\sum_z\lambda_zL_z
\le\sum_z\lambda_zp(z)=p(m)
\le q(m)=\sum_z\mu_zq(z)
\le\sum_z\mu_zU_z,
\]
which gives (11).

We prove part 3. Necessity of (R) is Jensen's inequality. Conversely, suppose (R) holds. For every \(y\in Q\), \(\Lambda_y(D_0)\) is a nonempty compact polytope, so both extrema in (16) are finite and attained. Combining barycentric representations shows directly that \(\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}\) is convex and \(u^{\mathrm{ccv}}\) is concave. For \(x\in D_0\), the Dirac representation gives
\[
\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(x)\le\ell_x,
\qquad
u^{\mathrm{ccv}}(x)\ge u_x,
\]
while (R) gives the reverse inequalities for every representation. Thus the extensions match all prescribed values.

For \(y\in Q\), choose a minimizing representation \(\lambda\) for \(\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(y)\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^{\mathrm{ccv}}(y)
&\ge\sum_z\lambda_zu_z\\
&=\sum_z\lambda_z\ell_z+
\sum_z\lambda_z(u_z-\ell_z)\\
&\ge\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(y)+h_{\min},
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (17). Finally, writing \(\ell_z=c_z-h_z/2\) and \(u_z=c_z+h_z/2\), the two inequalities in (R) give
\[
2\left(c_x-\sum_z\lambda_zc_z\right)
\le h_x-\sum_z\lambda_zh_z
\]
and
\[
2\left(\sum_z\lambda_zc_z-c_x\right)
\le h_x-\sum_z\lambda_zh_z.
\]
Together these are (18).

For part 4, fix \(E,m\). A linear objective on a polytope attains its maximum at a vertex, so (21) holds exactly when it holds for every pair of vertices \(\lambda,\mu\) of \(\Lambda_m(E)\). Expanding that pairwise inequality gives (23). Only finitely many subsets of finite \(E\) can occur as \(E\cap J\), so (22) also amounts to finitely many linear inequalities. Hence the feasible closure
\[
0\le\tau_z\le h_z
\]
is a compact polytope for each fixed \((E,m)\).

There are finitely many subsets \(E\subseteq D_0\), and, for each one, the bounded polytope \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\) contains only finitely many lattice points \(m\). Thus there are only finitely many closed LP blocks. At least one has positive optimum: for any \(z_0\in D_0\), take \(E=\{z_0\}\), \(m=z_0\), and \(\tau_{z_0}=h_{z_0}\). The two endpoints in (21) then both equal \((\ell_{z_0}+u_{z_0})/2\), while every halfspace containing \(m\) contains the sole selected point. Therefore the maximum over all closed blocks is positive and attained.

Take a global maximizing closed-block tuple and let
\[
E_+:=\{z\in E:\tau_z>0\}.
\]
It is nonempty. Balance implies \(m\in\operatorname{conv}(E_+)\): otherwise strict separation of \(m\) from \(\operatorname{conv}(E_+)\) would give a proper closed halfspace through \(m\) containing no positive-mass point, contradicting (22). Delete \(E\setminus E_+\). The balance sums and the objective are unchanged. Moreover, extending every element of \(\Lambda_m(E_+)\) by zeros embeds it into \(\Lambda_m(E)\). Hence the maximum on the left of (21) can only decrease and the minimum on the right can only increase. The reduced tuple is still feasible, has the same globally maximal objective, and has strictly positive coordinates. This proves global attainment in the original definition of \(\Phi_n\).

We next prove part 5. The actual lattice endpoint data of \(C\) satisfy (R) by Jensen's inequality, and (1) gives the other defining properties of \(\mathscr R_n\). Thus \((D,\ell|_D,u|_D)\in\mathscr R_n\). Under the bijective change of variables
\[
t_z=V\tau_z,
\]
the bound \(0<t_z\le Vh_z\) is exactly \(0<\tau_z\le h_z\). Part 2 says that geometric complete-core nonemptiness is exactly (21). Dividing every selected mass by \(V\) turns the proper closed-halfspace inequalities in (24) exactly into (22), and
\[
\sum_zt_z=V\sum_z\tau_z.
\]
This proves (25), with no closure, zero-mass, rank, representation, or halfspace discrepancy.

For part 6, fix \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\), put
\[
Q:=\operatorname{conv}(D_0),
\qquad
A:=\operatorname{aff}(D_0),
\qquad
L:=A-A,
\]
and use the extensions in (16). Lattice-convexity says \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_0\). Compactness of \(Q\) and discreteness of \(\mathbb Z^n\) therefore give
\[
\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(Q,\mathbb Z^n\setminus D_0)>0.
\]
Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\), let \(B_{L^\perp}\) be the closed unit ball in \(L^\perp\), and set
\[
P:=Q+\varepsilon B_{L^\perp}.
\tag{34}
\]
If \(L=\mathbb R^n\), then \(P=Q\), which is already full-dimensional. If \(L\ne\mathbb R^n\), the relative interiors of \(Q\) in \(A\) and of \(B_{L^\perp}\) in \(L^\perp\) show that their Minkowski sum is full-dimensional. In both cases \(P\) is compact and convex. Also,
\[
P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_0,
\tag{35}
\]
because every point of \(P\) has distance at most \(\varepsilon<\delta\) from \(Q\).

Let \(\pi_A:A+L^\perp\to A\) be orthogonal projection onto the affine subspace \(A\). For \(y\in P\), \(\pi_A(y)\in Q\). Define
\[
\ell_P(y):=\ell^{\mathrm{cvx}}(\pi_A(y)),
\qquad
u_P(y):=u^{\mathrm{ccv}}(\pi_A(y)).
\tag{36}
\]
These functions are respectively convex and concave, match the prescribed values at every point of \(D_0\), and by (17) have gap at least \(h_{\min}>0\) throughout \(P\). They are finite polyhedral value functions, hence continuous on their polytopal domains. Take
\[
d=2,
\qquad
U=[0,1],
\qquad
V=1,
\]
and define
\[
C_0:=
\left\{(y,s,v):y\in P,\ \ell_P(y)\le s\le u_P(y),\ 0\le v\le1\right\}.
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (34), (36), and the uniform positive gap show that \(C_0\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^{n+2}\). Equation (35) shows that its positive lattice support is exactly \(D_0\), and its fiber at \(z\in D_0\) has width \(u_z-\ell_z=h_z\). This proves part 6, including lower affine rank.

For part 7, every geometric prismatic body with \(W>0\) produces a datum in \(\mathscr R_n\), and (2), (25) give
\[
\max\{\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),2^nM_{\mathrm{vol}}(C)\}
=V\max\{\Phi_n(D,\ell,u),2^n\max_zh_z\},
\qquad
W=V\sum_zh_z.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus (FE) implies (PMC) with the same constant. Conversely, part 6 realizes every datum in \(\mathscr R_n\) with \(d=2\) and \(V=1\), and (38) then runs in reverse. This proves exact equivalence and equality of the best constants. It is only a reduction; it does not decide whether a positive constant exists.

We prove part 8. If \(T_q=0\), the right side of (27) is zero and the result is immediate. Suppose \(T_q>0\). For \(z\in E_q\), \(\tau_z^{(q)}>0\), and
\[
\frac{\tau_z^{(q)}}2\le h_z-q.
\tag{39}
\]
Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c`, in its \(d=0\) weighted lattice form, to the finite positive measure
\[
\sum_{z\in E_q}\tau_z^{(q)}\delta_z.
\]
It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed halfspace \(J\ni m\) has selected mass at least \(2^{-n}T_q\). Strict separation shows that \(m\in\operatorname{conv}(E_q)\), since otherwise a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E_q\) would have zero mass. Lattice-convexity then gives \(m\in D_0\). Thus (22) holds.

For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_q)\), (39), concavity of the extension \(u\), and convexity of the extension \(\ell\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_z\lambda_z\left(\ell_z+\frac{\tau_z^{(q)}}2\right)
&\le\sum_z\lambda_z(u_z-q)
\le u_m-q,
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_z\lambda_z\left(u_z-\frac{\tau_z^{(q)}}2\right)
&\ge\sum_z\lambda_z(\ell_z+q)
\ge\ell_m+q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
If \(h_m=u_m-\ell_m\le2q\), then
\[
u_m-q\le\ell_m+q,
\]
so (40) and (41) imply (21) for all representations. The capped-excess tuple is admissible and has total mass \(T_q\), proving \(\Phi_n\ge T_q\). If \(h_m>2q\), then
\[
2^n\max_zh_z\ge2^nh_m>2^{n+1}q.
\]
The two cases prove (27).

Finally, assume the width-band hypothesis (28). Put
\[
\tau:=(2-\kappa)a>0.
\tag{42}
\]
Since \(2-\kappa\le1\) and \(h_z\ge a\), one has \(0<\tau\le h_z\) for every \(z\in D_0\). Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the counting measure on \(D_0\). As above, it supplies
\[
m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(D_0)=D_0
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) contains at least \(2^{-n}|D_0|\) points of \(D_0\). Hence the uniform selected masses \(\tau_z=\tau\) satisfy (22).

For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(D_0)\), write
\[
\bar c_\lambda:=\sum_z\lambda_zc_z,
\qquad
\bar h_\lambda:=\sum_z\lambda_zh_z.
\]
Equation (18) gives
\[
|c_m-\bar c_\lambda|
\le\frac{h_m-\bar h_\lambda}{2}.
\tag{43}
\]
The interval in (33) for the uniform mass \(\tau\) is centered at \(\bar c_\lambda\) and has radius
\[
\frac{\bar h_\lambda-\tau}{2}.
\]
The width band gives
\[
h_m+\tau
\le\kappa a+(2-\kappa)a
=2a
\le2\bar h_\lambda,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{h_m-\bar h_\lambda}{2}
\le\frac{\bar h_\lambda-\tau}{2}.
\tag{44}
\]
Equations (43) and (44) show that \(c_m\) belongs to every averaged shrunken interval. By part 2,
\[
(c_m,\operatorname{cg}(U))
\in
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(D_0)}
\sum_z\lambda_z\mathsf M_{V\tau}(C_z)
\]
for any geometric realization, and the corresponding finite tuple satisfies (21). It is admissible and has scalar mass \(\tau|D_0|=(2-\kappa)a|D_0|\). Since
\[
\sum_{z\in D_0}h_z\le\kappa a|D_0|,
\]
(29) follows. Multiplying by \(V\) and using (25) gives (30). Substituting \(\kappa=1+\varepsilon\) gives (31).
