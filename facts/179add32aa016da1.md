## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),\qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]
Use the free same-parity matching normalization of accepted facts
c81f02314cb34f8b and f23ff6dd60fbbc37: a free matching is a
nonnegative family \(p=(p_{uv})\) on unordered pairs of distinct
same-parity points, with
\[
 d_z(p):=\sum_{e\ni z}p_e\leq\frac{a_z}{2},
 \qquad |p|:=\sum_ep_e.
\tag{2}
\]
Call such a matching saturated when it is maximum and, with
\[
 t_z:=2d_z(p),\qquad U(p):=\{z\in Z:t_z<a_z\},
\tag{3}
\]
it satisfies
\[
 t_z=a_z\quad(z\notin U(p)),\qquad
 \sum_{z\in U(p)}a_z\leq G,
\tag{4}
\]
as in accepted fact f23ff6dd60fbbc37.

Let \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\) be the collection of all quadruples
\((p,m,\mathcal Q,i)\) in conclusion 3 of f23ff6dd60fbbc37:
\(p\) is saturated, \(m\) is any admissible integral center for its
selected profile, \(\mathcal Q\) is any support-minimal balanced negative
circuit supplied by the empty degree-saturated core, including any valid
choice of the exact affine minorants \(\ell_j\), and \(i\) is any circuit
label. Write \(w_i\ne0\) for that label's continuous normal and
\[
 A_i:=C\cap\{(z,x):\langle w_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Define the individual-cap retention capacity, excluding positive
same-base reweightings, by
\[
 R_{\mathrm{cap}}(C):=
 \sup\{W(A_i):(p,m,\mathcal Q,i)\in\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Fix \(0<\eta\leq1/4\), and assume
\[
 G<\frac W4,\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{cap}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W.
\tag{7}
\]
Then \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\ne\varnothing\), and one of the
following alternatives holds.

1. The complete-core capacity is terminally large:
\[
 G\geq\frac{\eta^2}{100}W.
\tag{8}
\]

2. All conclusions below hold simultaneously. For every nonempty parity
class \(\omega\), put
\[
 A_\omega:=\sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z,\qquad
 M_\omega:=\max_{z\equiv\omega}a_z,
\tag{9}
\]
and define the set of good parity classes by
\[
 \mathcal G:=
 \{\omega:A_\omega>0,\ M_\omega\leq A_\omega/4\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Define the canonical product matching and its centroid-capacity scaling by
\[
 p^\times_{uv}:=\frac{a_ua_v}{2A_\omega}
 \quad(u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\omega),\qquad
 q^\times:=2\gamma_dp^\times.
\tag{11}
\]
There is a single maximum free matching \(p^\star\), chosen independently
of every member of \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\), such that
\[
 p^\star_{uv}\geq\frac12p^\times_{uv}
 \quad\text{whenever }u,v\text{ lie in a class }\omega\in\mathcal G.
\tag{12}
\]
The weights
\[
 \lambda^\star_{uv}:=2\gamma_dp^\star_{uv},\qquad
 \bar b_z:=\gamma_da_z,
\tag{13}
\]
together with their unmatched weights, form an exact minimum-unmatched
centroid matching for the capacities \((\bar b_z)\) in the sense of
accepted fact be359f8a6c628483. Thus the fixed measure
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{off}}^{\lambda^\star}:=
 \sum_e\lambda_e^\star\delta_{(m_e,c_e)},\qquad
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},\quad c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},
\tag{14}
\]
where \(c_z\) is the centroid of \(C_z\), is one of the exact hosts
allowed in the definition of \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\) in accepted fact
c1e3b8400d5b5b10.

Fix arbitrarily
\((p,m,\mathcal Q,i)\in\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\).
For \(z\notin U(p)\), put
\[
 D_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z,\qquad
 \sigma_{i,z}:=\langle w_i,c_z\rangle-\ell_i(z),\qquad
 r_{i,z}:=h_{D_z}(w_i),
\tag{15}
\]
and define
\[
 \phi_{i,z}:=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&z\notin U(p),\ \sigma_{i,z}=0,\\
 \min\{1,r_{i,z}/\sigma_{i,z}\},
    &z\notin U(p),\ \sigma_{i,z}>0,\\
 0,&z\in U(p),
 \end{cases}
 \qquad b_{i,z}:=a_z\phi_{i,z}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then \(\sigma_{i,z}\geq0\) on \(Z\setminus U(p)\), and
\[
 \sum_zb_{i,z}\geq\eta W-G.
\tag{17}
\]
The hard halo
\[
 H_i:=
 \left\{z\in Z\setminus U(p):
 \sigma_{i,z}\leq\frac{2}{\eta}r_{i,z}\right\}
\tag{18}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in H_i}a_z>\frac\eta3W.
\tag{19}
\]
Define
\[
 \rho_i^{\mathrm{sel}}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(H_i)
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
 r_i^{\mathrm{sel}}:=
 \min\left\{
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_z-s(z):z\in H_i\}:
 s:\operatorname{aff}(H_i)\to\mathbb R^d
 \text{ is affine}
 \right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)>
 \frac{\gamma_d\eta}{3}\,
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_i^{\mathrm{sel}}}}
 {r_i^{\mathrm{sel}}+1}\,W.}
\tag{22}
\]

For every same-parity edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), define
\[
 s_{i,e}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \frac12q_e^\times\phi_{i,u}\phi_{i,v},
 &u,v\text{ lie in a class }\omega\in\mathcal G,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\tag{23}
\]
and the cap-specific product-centroid measure
\[
 \Sigma_i^\times:=\sum_es_{i,e}\delta_{(m_e,c_e)}.
\tag{24}
\]
It is a submeasure of the same fixed exact host, and its mass obeys
\[
 \boxed{
 0\leq\Sigma_i^\times\leq\nu_{\mathrm{off}}^{\lambda^\star},
 \qquad
 S_i:=|\Sigma_i^\times|>
 \frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{8}W.}
\tag{25}
\]
For a positive soft edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 D_e:=\frac{D_u+D_v}{2}.
\tag{26}
\]
Then the exact weighted slab relation is
\[
 \boxed{
 0\leq
 \phi_{i,u}\phi_{i,v}
 \bigl(\langle w_i,c_e\rangle-\ell_i(m_e)\bigr)
 \leq h_{D_e}(w_i).}
\tag{27}
\]

Let
\[
 \rho_i^\times:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}
 (\operatorname{supp}\Sigma_i^\times),
\]
\[
 r_i^\times:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\Sigma_i^\times)
 -\rho_i^\times.
\tag{28}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)>
 \frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{8}\,
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_i^\times}}{r_i^\times+1}\,W.}
\tag{29}
\]

For any integral midpoint \(m_0\), let
\(\Sigma_{i,m_0}^\times\) be the restriction of
\(\Sigma_i^\times\) to atoms with base coordinate \(m_0\), and put
\[
 S_{i,m_0}:=|\Sigma_{i,m_0}^\times|,
\]
\[
 r_{i,m_0}^{\mathrm{cloud}}:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{c_e:s_{i,e}>0,\ m_e=m_0\}.
\tag{30}
\]
Whenever \(S_{i,m_0}>0\), one has \(m_0\in Z\) and
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\geq
 2^n\frac{S_{i,m_0}}{r_{i,m_0}^{\mathrm{cloud}}+1}.}
\tag{31}
\]

Finally, let \(P_i^\times\) be the maximum of
\[
 \sum_{\{e,f\}}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
\tag{32}
\]
over nonnegative weights on unordered pairs of distinct adjacent positive
soft edges, subject to
\[
 \sum_{f:f\sim e}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\leq s_{i,e}
 \quad\text{for every positive soft edge }e.
\tag{33}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{P_i^\times>\frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{20}W.}
\tag{34}
\]
All assertions in alternative 2 hold for every saturated matching, every
admissible center, every support-minimal empty-core circuit, every valid
choice of its exact affine minorants, and every cap label, while the exact
host \(\lambda^\star\) is fixed once for \(C\).

## Proof

Because \(G<W/4<W/2\), accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b gives
\(a_z\leq2^{-n}G\) for every \(z\in Z\). Since \(W>0\), this also shows
\(G>0\). Accepted fact f23ff6dd60fbbc37 therefore applies and shows
that \(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\) is nonempty.

If (8) holds there is nothing more to prove. Assume henceforth that
\[
 G<\frac{\eta^2}{100}W.
\tag{35}
\]
Fix an arbitrary member
\((p,m,\mathcal Q,i)\in\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\).
By (6)-(7),
\[
 W-W(A_i)\geq\eta W.
\tag{36}
\]
The individual-cap loss estimate (35) of accepted fact
f23ff6dd60fbbc37, including its zero-slack convention, gives
\[
 W-W(A_i)\leq
 G+\sum_{z\notin U(p)}a_z\phi_{i,z}.
\tag{37}
\]
The exact-minorant property in that same accepted fact gives
\(\sigma_{i,z}\geq0\) on every saturated fiber.
Equations (36)-(37) prove (17).

If \(z\notin H_i\) and \(z\notin U(p)\), then
\(\sigma_{i,z}>2r_{i,z}/\eta\), so
\(\phi_{i,z}<\eta/2\). Hence
\[
 \sum_zb_{i,z}
 \leq\sum_{z\in H_i}a_z+\frac\eta2W.
\tag{38}
\]
Combining (17), (35), and (38) gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in H_i}a_z
 \geq\frac\eta2W-G
 >\left(\frac12-\frac1{100}\right)\eta W
 >\frac\eta3W,
\tag{39}
\]
where \(\eta\leq1\) was used in the middle inequality. This proves (19).

For every \(z\in H_i\), the full-mass metronoid of \(C_z\) is the
centroid singleton. Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, applied with mass
\(a_z\), gives
\[
 \delta_C(z,c_z)\geq\gamma_da_z.
\tag{40}
\]
Use the points \(c_z\), the weights \(\gamma_da_z\), and an affine map
attaining the minimum in (21) in the definition of
\(R_{\mathrm{sel}}\) from accepted fact be359f8a6c628483.
Equation (19) gives (22).

We next construct the fixed host. Accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b
gives \(\sum_\omega M_\omega\leq G\) and a maximum free matching
\(p^\star\) satisfying (12). Scaling by \(2\gamma_d\) bijects the free
matchings with endpoint capacities \(a_z/2\) and the matchings with
endpoint capacities \(\bar b_z=\gamma_da_z\). Thus
\(\lambda^\star=2\gamma_dp^\star\) is maximum under the latter
capacities. This is true separately in every parity class, since
otherwise one could improve \(p^\star\) in that class. With
\[
 u_z^\star:=
 \bar b_z-\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_e^\star\geq0,
\tag{41}
\]
the total unmatched mass in a parity class is its total capacity minus
twice the matching mass. Hence maximum matching mass is equivalent to
minimum unmatched mass in every class. This proves that
\((\lambda^\star,u^\star)\) is an exact minimum-unmatched centroid
matching in the sense of be359f8a6c628483.

For every edge whose endpoints lie in a class \(\omega\in\mathcal G\),
equations (11)-(13) give
\[
 \lambda_e^\star\geq\gamma_dp_e^\times
 =\frac12q_e^\times\geq s_{i,e}.
\tag{42}
\]
The soft weight is zero on all remaining edges. After aggregating edges
that yield the same atom, (42) proves the submeasure inequality in (25).

For \(\omega\in\mathcal G\), write
\[
 B_{i,\omega}:=\sum_{z\equiv\omega}b_{i,z},\qquad
 B_i^{\mathcal G}:=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}B_{i,\omega}.
\tag{43}
\]
If a nonempty parity class is not in \(\mathcal G\), then
\(A_\omega<4M_\omega\). Consequently the total physical mass of all
classes outside \(\mathcal G\) is less than
\(4\sum_\omega M_\omega\leq4G\). Since
\(0\leq b_{i,z}\leq a_z\), equations (17) and (35), together with
\(\eta\leq1/4\), imply
\[
 B_i^{\mathcal G}\geq\eta W-5G
 >\frac34\eta W.
\tag{44}
\]
For \(u,v\) in a class \(\omega\in\mathcal G\), equations
(11), (16), and (23) give
\[
 s_{i,\{u,v\}}=
 \frac{\gamma_d}{2A_\omega}b_{i,u}b_{i,v}.
\tag{45}
\]
Summing over unordered distinct pairs gives the exact identity
\[
 S_i=
 \frac{\gamma_d}{4}
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}
 \frac{
 B_{i,\omega}^2-\sum_{z\equiv\omega}b_{i,z}^2
 }{A_\omega}.
\tag{46}
\]
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and
\(\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}A_\omega\leq W\) give
\[
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}
 \frac{B_{i,\omega}^2}{A_\omega}
 \geq\frac{(B_i^{\mathcal G})^2}{W}.
\tag{47}
\]
Also \(b_{i,z}\leq a_z\leq M_\omega\) and
\(B_{i,\omega}\leq A_\omega\), so
\[
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}
 \frac{\sum_{z\equiv\omega}b_{i,z}^2}{A_\omega}
 \leq
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal G}
 \frac{M_\omega B_{i,\omega}}{A_\omega}
 \leq\sum_\omega M_\omega\leq G.
\tag{48}
\]
Equations (35) and (44)-(48) yield
\[
 S_i>
 \frac{\gamma_d}{4}
 \left(\frac9{16}-\frac1{100}\right)\eta^2W
 =\frac{221}{1600}\gamma_d\eta^2W
 >\frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{8}W.
\tag{49}
\]
This proves the mass assertion in (25).

Because \(\ell_i\) is affine,
\[
 \langle w_i,c_e\rangle-\ell_i(m_e)
 =\frac{\sigma_{i,u}+\sigma_{i,v}}2.
\tag{50}
\]
The definition (16) gives
\(\phi_{i,z}\sigma_{i,z}\leq r_{i,z}\). All factors are nonnegative, so
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \phi_{i,u}\phi_{i,v}
 \bigl(\langle w_i,c_e\rangle-\ell_i(m_e)\bigr)
 &\leq\frac12
 (\phi_{i,u}\sigma_{i,u}+\phi_{i,v}\sigma_{i,v})\\
 &\leq\frac{r_{i,u}+r_{i,v}}2
 =h_{D_e}(w_i).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{51}
\]
This proves (27).

The measure \(\Sigma_i^\times\) is nonzero by (49), and it is a positive
submeasure of the exact host (14). Its ranks in (28) are precisely the
ranks allowed in the definition of \(M_{\mathrm{off}}\) in accepted fact
c1e3b8400d5b5b10. Substitution of (49) into that definition proves (29).

Fix \(m_0\) with \(S_{i,m_0}>0\). Every edge in this slice has the form
\(e=\{m_0+v,m_0-v\}\) with \(v\ne0\). Positive-volume convex fibers
contain open balls, and convexity of \(C\) puts the Minkowski midpoint of
one such ball from each endpoint inside \(C_{m_0}\). Thus
\(a_{m_0}>0\), proving \(m_0\in Z\). For such an edge,
\[
 s_{i,e}\leq\frac12q_e^\times
 =\frac{\gamma_da_{m_0+v}a_{m_0-v}}{2A_\omega}
 \leq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
 \min\{a_{m_0+v},a_{m_0-v}\}.
\tag{52}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\gamma_{2d}}{\gamma_d}
 =\left(1-\frac1{(2d+1)^2}\right)^d
 \geq1-\frac{d}{(2d+1)^2}>\frac12,
\tag{53}
\]
where the middle inequality is Bernoulli's inequality. Hence the last
quantity in (52) is strictly less than
\(\gamma_{2d}\min\{a_{m_0+v},a_{m_0-v}\}\).

Choose in the reflected-cloud definition of accepted fact
c1e3b8400d5b5b10 the midpoint \(m_0\) and exactly the nonzero reflected
pairs occurring in this slice. Their cloud points are the corresponding
\(c_e\), their cloud rank is (30), and (52)-(53) show that their cloud
energy is greater than \(S_{i,m_0}\). The definition of
\(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}\) proves (31).

It remains to prove (34). The finite linear program (32)-(33) has an
optimum. Fix one and define its residual edge weights by
\[
 \zeta_e:=
 s_{i,e}-\sum_{f:f\sim e}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\geq0.
\tag{54}
\]
No two edges with positive residual weights are adjacent, since
otherwise their pairing weight could be increased. Thus the positive
residual edges are endpoint-disjoint.

Let \(F_0\) be any endpoint-disjoint family of positive soft edges. In a
class \(\omega\in\mathcal G\), equation (45) and
\(0\leq b_{i,z}\leq M_\omega\) give
\[
 2b_{i,u}b_{i,v}\leq M_\omega(b_{i,u}+b_{i,v}),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \sum_{\substack{e\in F_0\\e\subseteq\omega}}s_{i,e}
 \leq
 \frac{\gamma_dM_\omega}{4A_\omega}
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}\in F_0\\e\subseteq\omega}}
 (b_{i,u}+b_{i,v})
 \leq\frac{\gamma_dM_\omega}{4}.
\tag{55}
\]
Apply (55) to the endpoint-disjoint support of \((\zeta_e)\), and use
\(0<\zeta_e\leq s_{i,e}\) on that support. Summing over
\(\omega\in\mathcal G\) and using \(\sum_\omega M_\omega\leq G\) gives
\[
 \sum_e\zeta_e\leq\frac{\gamma_dG}{4}.
\tag{56}
\]
Each pairing weight is charged at both of its incident soft edges, so
\[
 \sum_e\zeta_e=S_i-2P_i^\times.
\tag{57}
\]
Equations (35), (49), and (56)-(57) imply
\[
 P_i^\times
 \geq\frac12\left(S_i-\frac{\gamma_dG}{4}\right)
 >\frac{49}{800}\gamma_d\eta^2W
 >\frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{20}W.
\tag{58}
\]
This proves (34). Since the member of
\(\mathscr H_{\mathrm{sat}}(C)\) was arbitrary and the host was chosen
before it, all universal quantifiers in alternative 2 follow. No step
uses or produces a dual stress or endpoint support-contact decomposition,
so none of the excluded stress or interaction conclusions is asserted.
