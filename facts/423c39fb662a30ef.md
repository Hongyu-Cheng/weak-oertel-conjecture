## Statement

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite oriented loopless multigraph, with parallel edges regarded as distinct elements of \(E\), and let \(N=|E|\). Write \(\deg_G(v)\) for the ordinary undirected degree, counting edge multiplicity. Let \(\widehat A\) be the full vertex-edge incidence matrix: an edge column has \(-1\) at its tail, \(+1\) at its head, and zero elsewhere. Let \(A\) be obtained by deleting exactly one vertex row in each connected component, including each isolated-vertex component.

Notation map: \(\widehat A,A,N,\deg_G(v),B_{ea},t_{ea},Y_e\) have exactly the meanings specified in the assignment. The task-local matrix \(A\) is unrelated to the measurable subset \(A\subseteq\Lambda\) in the locked problem. No accepted predecessor or legacy fact notation is used, and the locked problem's \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S,h_S\) are not redefined.

For every \(e\in E\) and \(a\in\{1,2\}\), let \(B_{ea}\) be Bernoulli with \(\mathbb P(B_{ea}=1)=t_{ea}\in[0,1]\), and assume that all \(2N\) variables are mutually independent. Put \(Y_e=B_{e1}+B_{e2}-1\), \(Y=(Y_e)_{e\in E}\), and assume \(A\mathbb E Y=0\).

For this graph, define

\[
F(G):=\frac{(2N)!}{(2N)^{2N}}
       \prod_{v\in V}\frac{\deg_G(v)^{\deg_G(v)}}{\deg_G(v)!},
\qquad 0^0:=1,\quad 0!:=1.
\]

Then \(\mathbb P(AY=0)\ge F(G)\). In particular, if \(N=0\), both sides equal one.

More precisely, index the connected components having at least one edge by \(\alpha\in\mathcal J\), write them as \(G_\alpha=(V_\alpha,E_\alpha)\), and put \(N_\alpha=|E_\alpha|\). Then

\[
\mathbb P(AY=0)
\ge \prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal J} F(G_\alpha)
\ge F(G).
\]

There is the stronger deterministic-component improvement

\[
\mathbb P(AY=0)
\ge
\prod_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathcal J:\\
\exists e\in E_\alpha,\ a\in\{1,2\}\ \text{with}\ 0<t_{ea}<1}}
F(G_\alpha).
\]

An empty product is one. In fact, under the mean hypothesis, \(\mathbb P(AY=0)=1\) if and only if every \(t_{ea}\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\).

For each nontrivial component there is the rigorous elementary envelope

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(G_\alpha)
&\ge \sqrt{4N_\alpha}\,\exp(-|V_\alpha|)
       \prod_{v\in V_\alpha}\deg_{G_\alpha}(v)^{-1/2}\\
&\ge \sqrt{4N_\alpha}\,\exp(-|V_\alpha|)
       \left(\frac{|V_\alpha|}{2N_\alpha}\right)^{|V_\alpha|/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These envelopes may also be multiplied over the components occurring in either probability bound above. They exhibit cancellation of edge-exponential terms, while retaining factors depending on the number of vertices and their degrees.

## Proof

For every vector \(x\in\mathbb R^E\), the sum of the coordinates of \(\widehat A x\) over any connected component is zero: every edge column has one \(-1\) and one \(+1\) in that component. Consequently, the omitted coordinate in each component is the negative sum of its retained coordinates. This proves \(Ax=0\) if and only if \(\widehat A x=0\), including isolated vertices, whose full coordinates are always zero. In particular, the mean hypothesis implies \(\widehat A\mathbb E Y=0\).

For each labeled edge-copy \((e,a)\), choose the head of \(e\) when \(B_{ea}=1\) and the tail when \(B_{ea}=0\). Let \(I_v\) count the copies choosing \(v\), and let \(I=(I_v)_{v\in V}\). At the head of \(e\), the two copies contribute \(B_{e1}+B_{e2}=1+Y_e\); at its tail they contribute \(2-B_{e1}-B_{e2}=1-Y_e\). Subtracting one, the contribution of this edge to each endpoint's ordinary degree, gives exactly its incidence column multiplied by \(Y_e\). Summing over all labeled edges gives

\[
\widehat A Y=I-(\deg_G(v))_{v\in V},
\qquad
\mathbb E I_v=\deg_G(v),
\qquad
\{AY=0\}=\{I_v=\deg_G(v)\ \text{for every }v\}.
\]

The expectation identity uses \(\widehat A\mathbb E Y=0\). These calculations do not require distinct endpoints across different edges, so they apply unchanged to parallel edges.

If \(N=0\), there are no Bernoulli variables, \(Y\) is the empty vector, and \(AY=0\) surely. All degrees are zero and the conventions in the statement give \(F(G)=1\). All component products are empty. Henceforth suppose \(N>0\).

Write \(M=2N\). Let the row set be \(\mathcal R=E\times\{1,2\}\). For \(r=(e,a)\) and \(v\in V\), let

\[
q_{rv}:=
\begin{cases}
t_{ea},&v\text{ is the head of }e,\\
1-t_{ea},&v\text{ is the tail of }e,\\
0,&v\text{ is neither endpoint of }e.
\end{cases}
\]

The graph is loopless, so these cases are disjoint. Thus \(q_{rv}\) is the probability that row-copy \(r\) chooses vertex \(v\), and \(\sum_v q_{rv}=1\).

Clone each positive-degree vertex \(v\) into \(\deg_G(v)\) labeled columns. The column set is \(\mathcal K=\{(v,j):v\in V,\ 1\le j\le\deg_G(v)\}\), and \(|\mathcal K|=\sum_v\deg_G(v)=2N=M\). An isolated vertex gives no column. Define the square nonnegative matrix \(T\), with rows \(\mathcal R\) and columns \(\mathcal K\), by \(T_{r,(v,j)}=q_{rv}/\deg_G(v)\). No division by zero occurs, because each column has \(\deg_G(v)>0\).

For a fixed row, summing over the clones of a vertex returns \(q_{rv}\), so every row sum is one. For a fixed column \((v,j)\), its sum is \(\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}q_{rv}/\deg_G(v)=\mathbb E I_v/\deg_G(v)=1\). Therefore \(T\) is doubly stochastic, including when some probabilities are zero or one.

We next compute its permanent exactly. A bijection from \(\mathcal R\) to \(\mathcal K\) induces a vertex assignment \(f:\mathcal R\to V\) with \(|f^{-1}(v)|=\deg_G(v)\) for all \(v\). Conversely, for every such assignment, independently bijecting its rows at each vertex to that vertex's labeled clones gives exactly \(\prod_{v:\deg_G(v)>0}\deg_G(v)!\) bijections. All those bijections have the same product of matrix entries, namely

\[
\left(\prod_{r\in\mathcal R}q_{r,f(r)}\right)
\left(\prod_{v:\deg_G(v)>0}\deg_G(v)^{-\deg_G(v)}\right).
\]

By mutual independence, \(\prod_r q_{r,f(r)}\) is exactly the probability of the labeled endpoint assignment \(f\). Assignments using a non-endpoint or a zero-probability choice contribute zero; no positive-probability assumption is needed. Summing over balanced assignments proves the identity

\[
\operatorname{per}(T)
=
\mathbb P(I_v=\deg_G(v)\ \text{for every }v)
\prod_{v:\deg_G(v)>0}\frac{\deg_G(v)!}{\deg_G(v)^{\deg_G(v)}}.
\]

This grouping explicitly treats all rows and all clones as labeled, so repeated edges and repeated values of probabilities cause no extra multiplicity factors. The isolated-vertex factors are one under the stated conventions.

The doubly stochastic permanent theorem stated and checked in EXTERNAL SOURCES gives \(\operatorname{per}(T)\ge M!/M^M\). Combining it with the exact identity and the established event equivalence gives \(\mathbb P(AY=0)\ge F(G)\).

The mean identity \(\mathbb E I_v=\deg_G(v)\) holds at every vertex and thus on each component separately. Each component event uses only Bernoulli variables on that component's edges. Mutual independence of the full family implies independence of these component events, while isolated vertices impose no condition. Applying the proved bound separately to each nontrivial component gives \(\mathbb P(AY=0)\ge\prod_\alpha F(G_\alpha)\).

To compare this product with the single global factor, let \(M_\alpha=2N_\alpha\), so \(\sum_\alpha M_\alpha=M\), and put \(p_\alpha=M_\alpha/M\). In the multinomial expansion of \((\sum_\alpha p_\alpha)^M=1\), the nonnegative term having counts \(M_\alpha\) is at most one. Therefore

\[
\frac{M!}{\prod_\alpha M_\alpha!}
\prod_\alpha\left(\frac{M_\alpha}{M}\right)^{M_\alpha}\le1,
\qquad
\frac{M!}{M^M}\le
\prod_\alpha\frac{M_\alpha!}{M_\alpha^{M_\alpha}}.
\]

The vertex-degree factors are identical in \(F(G)\) and \(\prod_\alpha F(G_\alpha)\), with isolated vertices contributing one. Multiplication proves the claimed comparison.

If all Bernoulli variables on a component are deterministic, then the component's divergence equals its expectation almost surely, and that expectation is zero. Its event has probability exactly one, so its factor may be replaced by one, equivalently omitted from the component product. This improves the bound because \(F(G_\alpha)\le1\), already implied by its proved probability bound.

For completeness, this describes all cases of probability one. Write \(\widehat A_{\cdot e}\) for the full incidence column of edge \(e\). Since \(\widehat A\mathbb E Y=0\),

\[
\widehat A Y
=\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{a=1}^2
  \widehat A_{\cdot e}(B_{ea}-t_{ea}).
\]

Independence makes every cross term vanish when taking the expectation of the squared Euclidean norm. Every loopless incidence column has squared norm two. Thus

\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat A Y\|^2
=2\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{a=1}^2 t_{ea}(1-t_{ea}).
\]

If \(\mathbb P(AY=0)=1\), the left side is zero. Every summand on the right is nonnegative, forcing \(t_{ea}\in\{0,1\}\) for every edge-copy. The converse follows from determinism and the zero-mean divergence hypothesis, as above. For \(N=0\), both directions hold vacuously.

Finally, we prove the factorial envelope rather than invoking asymptotic Stirling estimates. For every integer \(k\ge1\), concavity of \(\log x\) gives

\[
\log(k!)-\tfrac12\log k
=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{\log i+\log(i+1)}2
\le\int_1^k\log x\,dx
=k\log k-k+1.
\]

Also, Jensen's inequality on each unit interval centered at \(i\) gives \(\int_{i-1/2}^{i+1/2}\log x\,dx\le\log i\). Summing these inequalities yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(k!)
&\ge\int_{1/2}^{k+1/2}\log x\,dx\\
&=(k+\tfrac12)\log(k+\tfrac12)-k+\tfrac12\log2\\
&\ge(k+\tfrac12)\log k-k+\tfrac12\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Exponentiating proves, including \(k=1\),

\[
\sqrt{2k}\,k^k\exp(-k)
\le k!
\le\sqrt{k}\,k^k\exp(1-k).
\]

Apply the lower inequality to \(k=2N_\alpha\) in the numerator of \(F(G_\alpha)\), and the upper inequality to each positive degree in its denominator. Since \(\sum_{v\in V_\alpha}\deg_{G_\alpha}(v)=2N_\alpha\), this gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(G_\alpha)
&\ge\sqrt{4N_\alpha}\,\exp(-2N_\alpha)
\prod_{v\in V_\alpha}
  \deg_{G_\alpha}(v)^{-1/2}
  \exp(\deg_{G_\alpha}(v)-1)\\
&=\sqrt{4N_\alpha}\,\exp(-|V_\alpha|)
\prod_{v\in V_\alpha}\deg_{G_\alpha}(v)^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The displayed equality is the exact cancellation of all exponential terms proportional to the edge count. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives \(\prod_{v\in V_\alpha}\deg_{G_\alpha}(v)\le(2N_\alpha/|V_\alpha|)^{|V_\alpha|}\), which gives the second envelope inequality. All factors are positive, so multiplying component envelopes preserves the established bounds.

## External sources

Leonid Gurvits, “Van der Waerden/Schrijver-Valiant like Conjectures and Stable (aka Hyperbolic) Homogeneous Polynomials: One Theorem for all,” arXiv:0711.3496v2, primary-source PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.3496 . The source was read directly. Printed page 2 defines nonnegative doubly stochastic matrices and the permanent; the van der Waerden matrix bound is stated on printed page 3. Corollary 2.5 is on printed page 8. Example 2.6 starts there, and item (1), giving its doubly stochastic permanent application, is on printed page 9 in the retrieved PDF.

The exact matrix theorem used is: for every integer \(M\ge1\) and every entrywise nonnegative real \(M\)-by-\(M\) matrix \(T\) whose row sums and column sums are all one, \(\operatorname{per}(T)\ge M!/M^M\). No strictly positive entry condition, rationality condition, or support-connectivity condition is imposed.

For the underlying capacity statement, Corollary 2.5 says: if \(p\) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(M\) in \(M\) variables with nonnegative real coefficients, and is H-stable, meaning \(p(z_1,\ldots,z_M)\ne0\) whenever every \(\operatorname{Re}z_j>0\), then

\[
\frac{\partial^M p}{\partial x_1\cdots\partial x_M}(0,\ldots,0)
\ge \frac{M!}{M^M}\operatorname{Cap}(p),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cap}(p):=\inf_{x_1,\ldots,x_M>0}
\frac{p(x_1,\ldots,x_M)}{x_1\cdots x_M}.
\]

Example 2.6(1) applies this statement to the product of row linear forms of a doubly stochastic matrix and obtains the matrix theorem. The source's matrix-size symbol \(n\) maps to \(M\) here, its matrix symbol \(A\) maps to the cloned matrix \(T\), not to this lemma's reduced incidence matrix, and its \(\operatorname{Prod}_A\) maps to \(p_T(x)=\prod_{i=1}^M(\sum_{j=1}^M T_{ij}x_j)\).

Applicability can be checked directly: the cloned matrix in the proof is square, nonnegative, and has every row and column sum one. The polynomial \(p_T\) is homogeneous of degree \(M\) with nonnegative coefficients. Every row linear form has positive real part when every variable has positive real part, because its nonnegative row weights sum to one; hence \(p_T\) is H-stable even if some entries are zero. Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean gives \(\sum_j T_{ij}x_j\ge\prod_j x_j^{T_{ij}}\) for positive \(x_j\). Multiplying and using the column sums gives \(p_T(x)\ge\prod_j x_j\), while \(p_T(1,\ldots,1)=1\), so its capacity is one. Its mixed derivative at zero is its coefficient of \(x_1\cdots x_M\), exactly \(\operatorname{per}(T)\). Thus Corollary 2.5 has precisely the required applicability. The case \(M=0\) is handled separately in the proof and is not an application of the external theorem. The factorial inequalities used for the envelope are proved within this packet and need no external source.
