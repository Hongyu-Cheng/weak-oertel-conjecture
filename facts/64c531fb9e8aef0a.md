## Statement

For any integer $N\ge1$, the closed mean sublevel of the affine function $f(z)=-z_2$ on the lattice triangle $P_N=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(N,0),(0,1)\}$ has fraction $1/(N+2)$ under uniform counting measure. Under mixed measure, the same fraction holds on $P_N\times K$ for every positive-volume continuous convex body $K$, with $f$ independent of its continuous coordinates. Thus a positive universal mean-sublevel fraction fails already with two integer coordinates.

## Proof

The integer points at height zero are $(j,0)$ for $j=0,\ldots,N$. The only integer point at height one is $(0,1)$, and no other integer heights occur. Therefore the counting mean is $-1/(N+2)$. The sublevel contains the apex alone. In the product every integer base has the same continuous fiber volume $|K|$, so the mean stays $-1/(N+2)$ and the mass fraction stays $1/(N+2)$. This tends to zero at fixed integer and continuous dimensions.
