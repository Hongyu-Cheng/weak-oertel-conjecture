## Statement

Let $T$ be a unit-volume $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$, and let $S$ be the actual integer-fiber source of $[0,2m-1]\times T$, where $m\ge1$ is an integer. Use the canonical vertex caps at each of the two middle centroids with a sufficiently steep base tilt to capture every lower base and miss every higher base from the left source, and reflect the construction for the right source. Put $c=(1-(d/(d+1))^d)^{1/d}$ and $s=dc^{d-1}$. This is a cap cover with residual-root obstacles $e_{m-1}=e_m=c$ and $e_j=0$ elsewhere. For the program minimizing $\sum_j h_j^d$ subject to $h_j\ge e_j$ and nonnegative adjacent second differences, $h\equiv c$ is optimal, and is unique when $d>1$. The nearest-neighbor multipliers are $\beta_j/s=j(j+1)/2$ for $0\le j\le m-1$ and their reflection for the other indices, with $\lambda_{m-1}=\lambda_m=ms$ and all other $\lambda_j=0$.

For $m\ge2$, the original terminal row from $m-1$ is $(m+1)/(2m^2)$ at each site in the left half and $(m-1)/(2m^2)$ at each site in the right half. For any left-source step cap its residual roots $f$ satisfy
$$
\sum_j\theta_j f_j-c=\frac{(m-1)[m-(2m+1)c]}{2m^2}<0.
$$
In the signed Green expression the only possibly nonzero second differences at positive-weight sites are $c,1-2c,c-1$ at $m-2,m-1,m$, with corresponding weights $(m-2)(m-1)(m+1)/(4m^2),(m^2-1)/(4m),(m-1)^2/(4m)$. The right calculation is its reflection. When $m=1$ both rows are point masses and their residual losses are zero.

## Proof

The interval product has exactly the displayed integer fibers. At the left middle site the cap associated with vertex $i$ uses barycentric coordinate $\lambda_i(x)\ge1/(d+1)$ and the threshold increases sufficiently fast with $z$ that every earlier fiber is full and every later fiber is empty. Such a finite slope exists since $0\le\lambda_i\le1$. Its own captured fraction is $(d/(d+1))^d$ by similarity. The own caps cover that entire fiber because some coordinate is at least $1/(d+1)$. Reflection treats the right half and supplies a full trace in the later fibers. This proves the cover and obstacle assertions.

Average any feasible vector with its reflection. The objective does not increase by convexity, and the averaged vector is symmetric. Its central two heights agree and are at least $c$. Its differences are nondecreasing, so no other height is smaller. Hence the constant $c$ is optimal. For $d>1$ strict convexity gives uniqueness. At this chosen optimum the gradient is $s$ at every site. Use the nearest-neighbor stationarity of fc27a2d1671b7707, $\lambda_j=s+2\beta_j-\beta_{j-1}-\beta_{j+1}$, with endpoint and exterior $\beta=0$. Strict obstacle slack off the central pair enforces $\lambda_j=0$ there. Induction inwards on each half gives the displayed triangular numbers and substituting at the two central sites gives $ms$ there. All these nonnegative multipliers satisfy complementarity. For $m=1$ there are no adjacent second differences and both stopping probabilities are one.

For $m\ge2$, the positive-$\beta$ component is $\{1,\ldots,2m-2\}$. The accepted Green recurrence on that component has $W_j=(\beta_j/s)\theta_j$. If the left and right values of $\theta$ are $A,B$, the recurrence off the two middle sites is precisely the triangular-number recurrence. At the central pair, writing $a=m(m-1)/2$, it is $(a+m)A-aB=1$ and $(a+m)B-aA=0$. The displayed values solve this system. At either endpoint the stopping probability is the adjacent Green weight, $W_1=A$ and $W_{2m-2}=B$. This gives the complete row by uniqueness of the absorbing Green system, and the accepted theorem gives its barycenter $m-1$.

The left cap leaves roots $0$ on $m-1$ sites, $c$ at its own site and $1$ at the $m$ sites on its right. Thus the signed loss is $Ac+mB-c$, which simplifies to the formula in the statement. Bernoulli's inequality gives $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$, so $c\ge(1/2)^{1/d}\ge1/2>m/(2m+1)$, proving strictness. Substitution of the three second differences of the root array into the accepted signed Green identity gives exactly the displayed weights and the same loss. When $m=2$, the first of these weights is zero because $m-2$ is a stopping endpoint.
