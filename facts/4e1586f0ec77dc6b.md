## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
K_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in K\},\qquad
a_z(K):=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z),
\]
\[
I_+(K):=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z(K)>0\},\qquad
W(K):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z(K).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write \(H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}\), and define
\[
h_K(p):=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z\cap H_z),
\qquad
h_{\max}(K):=\sup_{p\in K\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}h_K(p).
\]
Assume \(W(K)>0\). For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(Q\) and \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(Q)\), define
\[
\mathsf M_t(Q):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_Qx f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_Qf\,dx=t
\right\}.
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq I_+(K)\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), write
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m
\right\}.
\]
For positive masses \(0<t_z\leq a_z(K)\), put
\[
\mathcal K_m^K(E,t):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(K_z).
\]
Define
\[
G(K):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(K),
\]
where \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}\) is the complete-core capacity of accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`; explicitly, \(G(K)\) is the supremum of \(\sum_{z\in E}t_z\) over the data above for which \(\mathcal K_m^K(E,t)\neq\varnothing\) and
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z
\geq2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
\tag{1}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\). Finally, put
\[
r(K):=\frac{G(K)}{W(K)},\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
\kappa_d:=\frac{\gamma_d}{2}.
\]
Then \(0<G(K)\leq W(K)\), and the following conclusions hold.

1. Inclusion monotonicity and affine covariance. If \(B\subseteq K\) is another positive-mass convex body in the same ambient space, then
\[
G(B)\leq G(K).
\tag{2}
\]
More generally, let
\[
F(z,x)=(Uz+c,\,Tx+Az+b),
\tag{3}
\]
where \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\), \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(T\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb R)\), \(A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) is linear, and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\). With \(q:=|\det T|\),
\[
W(FK)=qW(K),\qquad G(FK)=qG(K),\qquad r(FK)=r(K).
\tag{4}
\]

2. Robust full-support core emptiness. Suppose \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}I_+(K)\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in I_+(K)\cap J}a_z(K)\geq2^{-n}W(K)
\tag{5}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\). For \(0<\alpha\leq1\), define
\[
\mathcal K_m^{(\alpha)}(K):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I_+(K))}
\sum_{z\in I_+(K)}
\lambda_z\mathsf M_{\alpha a_z(K)}(K_z).
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\alpha>r(K)\), then
\[
\mathcal K_m^{(\alpha)}(K)=\varnothing.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, if \(r(K)<1/4\), then both the half-mass and quarter-mass cores in (6) are empty. The half-mass empty-core branch of accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85` is therefore legally available.

3. Exact one-step descendant bounds. Call \(B\) a legal balanced-cap descendant of \(K\) if it is any body produced by the half-mass empty-core construction of accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85`, and write \(\operatorname{Desc}(K)\) for the collection of all such choices. Every \(B\in\operatorname{Desc}(K)\) satisfies
\[
B\subseteq K,\qquad
W(B)\geq\kappa_dW(K),\qquad
G(B)\leq G(K),
\tag{8}
\]
and hence
\[
r(B)\leq\kappa_d^{-1}r(K)=\frac{2}{\gamma_d}r(K).
\tag{9}
\]
For
\[
\sigma_K(B):=\frac{W(B)}{W(K)},\qquad
\rho_K(B):=\frac{G(B)}{G(K)},
\]
one has
\[
\kappa_d\leq\sigma_K(B)\leq1,\qquad
0<\rho_K(B)\leq1,
\tag{10}
\]
and the exact pruning identities
\[
\frac{r(B)}{r(K)}=\frac{\rho_K(B)}{\sigma_K(B)},
\tag{11}
\]
\[
r(B)-r(K)
=
\frac{r(K)(W(K)-W(B))-(G(K)-G(B))}{W(B)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Whenever \(\operatorname{Desc}(K)\neq\varnothing\),
\[
-r(K)
\leq
\sup_{B\in\operatorname{Desc}(K)}(r(B)-r(K))
\leq
(\kappa_d^{-1}-1)r(K).
\tag{13}
\]
If a descendant satisfies \(r(B)\geq r(K)+\varepsilon\), then necessarily
\[
\rho_K(B)\geq\sigma_K(B)\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{r(K)}\right),
\qquad
\sigma_K(B)\leq\frac{r(K)}{r(K)+\varepsilon}.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus, in the branch \(r(K)<1/4\), it must discard more than \(4\varepsilon/(1+4\varepsilon)\) of the mass.

4. Uniform vanishing-capacity criterion. If \((K_j)\) is any sequence of positive-mass bodies with \(r(K_j)\to0\), then for all sufficiently large \(j\), \(r(K_j)<1/4\), \(\operatorname{Desc}(K_j)\neq\varnothing\), and
\[
\sup_{B\in\operatorname{Desc}(K_j)}
\bigl(r(B)-r(K_j)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently, the existence of such a sequence would refute every assertion demanding one fixed positive additive normalized-core gain from some legal descendant. This is conditional on the existence of the sequence.

5. Exact consequence of normalized-core progress. For \(\varepsilon>0\), let \(\mathrm{NCP}(\varepsilon)\) denote the assertion that every positive-mass \(K\) with \(r(K)<1/4\) has some \(B\in\operatorname{Desc}(K)\) satisfying \(r(B)\geq r(K)+\varepsilon\). If \(\mathrm{NCP}(\varepsilon)\) holds, then every positive-mass \(K\) satisfies
\[
r(K)\geq
\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\gamma_d}{2-\gamma_d}\varepsilon\right\}
\tag{16}
\]
and in all cases the non-strict universal estimate
\[
r(K)\geq
\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\varepsilon}{2e-1}\right\}
\tag{17}
\]
holds. By the master bound in accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`, this conditionally implies
\[
h_{\max}(K)
\geq
\gamma_d
\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\gamma_d}{2-\gamma_d}\varepsilon\right\}
2^{-n}W(K),
\tag{18}
\]
and hence the dimension-independent weaker constant
\[
h_{\max}(K)
\geq
\frac1e
\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\varepsilon}{2e-1}\right\}
2^{-n}W(K).
\tag{19}
\]

6. Length-\(L\) core-only barrier. Let \(L\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and
\[
K_0\supseteq K_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq K_L
\tag{20}
\]
be a chain in which \(K_{i+1}\) is a legal balanced-cap descendant of \(K_i\). Then
\[
G(K_L)\leq G(K_0),\qquad
W(K_L)\geq\kappa_d^LW(K_0),\qquad
r(K_L)\leq\kappa_d^{-L}r(K_0).
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, any terminal guarantee \(r(K_L)\geq\beta>0\) already forces
\[
r(K_0)\geq\beta\kappa_d^L
>\beta(2e)^{-L}.
\tag{22}
\]
In particular, a guarantee \(r(K_L)\geq r(K_0)+\eta\) forces
\[
r(K_0)\geq\frac{\eta}{\kappa_d^{-L}-1},
\tag{23}
\]
and a deficit contraction
\[
\frac14-r(K_L)\leq q\left(\frac14-r(K_0)\right),
\qquad 0\leq q<1,
\]
forces
\[
r(K_0)\geq
\frac{\kappa_d^L(1-q)}{4(1-q\kappa_d^L)}.
\tag{24}
\]
Moreover, the complete-core master-bound certificate obtained at the terminal body is never numerically stronger than the direct complete-core certificate at the root, because \(G(K_L)\leq G(K_0)\). Thus any universally bounded-length descent with a uniform terminal large-core bound already entails a universal positive root complete-core gap; this statement does not prove that such a gap exists.

## Proof

The bounds \(0<G(K)\leq W(K)\) follow directly from the definition in accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`. The upper bound holds because every admissible selected mass satisfies \(t_z\leq a_z(K)\). For positivity, choose any \(z\in I_+(K)\), take \(E=\{z\}\), \(m=z\), and \(t_z=a_z(K)\). The full-mass metronoid is the singleton consisting of the centroid of \(K_z\), its complete core is nonempty, and (1) is automatic.

Let \(B\subseteq K\). If \(0<t\leq a_z(B)\), every density on \(B_z\) admissible for \(\mathsf M_t(B_z)\) extends by zero to an admissible density on \(K_z\). Hence
\[
\mathsf M_t(B_z)\subseteq\mathsf M_t(K_z).
\tag{25}
\]
For every admissible tuple \((E,t,m)\) for \(G(B)\), (25) gives, for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\),
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(B_z)
\subseteq
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(K_z).
\]
Thus nonemptiness of the first complete core implies nonemptiness of the second. The base-balance inequalities are unchanged, so every selected total mass admissible for \(B\) is admissible for \(K\). Taking suprema proves (2).

We next prove (4). For \(z' = Uz+c\), the fiber of \(FK\) at \(z'\) is the image of \(K_z\) under
\[
F_z(x):=Tx+Az+b.
\]
Change of variables in the defining densities gives, for \(0<t\leq a_z(K)\),
\[
\mathsf M_{qt}((FK)_{Uz+c})=F_z(\mathsf M_t(K_z)).
\tag{26}
\]
The base map \(z\mapsto Uz+c\) bijects \(\mathbb Z^n\), so summing fiber volumes gives \(W(FK)=qW(K)\). It also bijects proper closed base halfspaces and preserves all barycentric coefficients. If \(E'=UE+c\), \(m'=Um+c\), and \(t'_{Uz+c}=qt_z\), then for every corresponding \(\lambda\),
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zF_z(\mathsf M_{t_z}(K_z))
=
T\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(K_z)+Am+b.
\tag{27}
\]
The affine map on the right is independent of \(\lambda\). Therefore the original complete core is nonempty if and only if the transformed complete core is nonempty, and the base-balance condition is preserved while total selected mass is multiplied by \(q\). This gives \(G(FK)\geq qG(K)\); applying the same argument to \(F^{-1}\) gives equality and proves (4).

Now suppose (5) holds and \(\alpha>r(K)\). If the core in (6) were nonempty, choose \(E=I_+(K)\) and \(t_z=\alpha a_z(K)\). Equation (5), multiplied by \(\alpha\), is exactly (1), so this tuple would be admissible for \(G(K)\) with total selected mass \(\alpha W(K)\). It would follow that \(G(K)\geq\alpha W(K)\), contrary to \(\alpha>r(K)\). This proves (7). If \(r(K)<1/4\), both \(\alpha=1/4\) and \(\alpha=1/2\) are allowed. In particular, the half-mass core at the weighted centerpoint used by accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85` is empty, so that fact supplies at least one legal descendant and proves the first two conclusions in (8), including \(W(B)\geq\kappa_dW(K)\). The third follows from (2).

Divide the inequalities in (8) by the appropriate positive masses to obtain (9) and (10). Equations (11) and (12) are algebraic identities:
\[
\frac{r(B)}{r(K)}
=
\frac{G(B)/G(K)}{W(B)/W(K)},
\]
and
\[
\frac{r(K)(W(K)-W(B))-(G(K)-G(B))}{W(B)}
=
\frac{G(B)}{W(B)}-\frac{G(K)}{W(K)}.
\]
Equation (13) follows from positivity of \(r(B)\) and (9). If \(r(B)\geq r(K)+\varepsilon\), then (11) gives the first inequality in (14); using \(\rho_K(B)\leq1\) gives the second. When \(r(K)<1/4\), the function \(s\mapsto s/(s+\varepsilon)\) is increasing, so
\[
\sigma_K(B)<\frac{1}{1+4\varepsilon},
\]
which is the asserted mass-discard bound.

For a sequence with \(r(K_j)\to0\), the robust emptiness result makes \(\operatorname{Desc}(K_j)\) nonempty for all sufficiently large \(j\). Applying (13) gives
\[
-r(K_j)
\leq
\sup_{B\in\operatorname{Desc}(K_j)}(r(B)-r(K_j))
\leq
(\kappa_d^{-1}-1)r(K_j).
\]
Both outer terms tend to zero, proving (15).

Assume \(\mathrm{NCP}(\varepsilon)\), and first suppose \(r(K)<1/4\). Choose its promised descendant. Equations (9) and the NCP inequality give
\[
r(K)+\varepsilon
\leq r(B)
\leq\kappa_d^{-1}r(K).
\]
Therefore
\[
r(K)\geq\frac{\kappa_d}{1-\kappa_d}\varepsilon
=\frac{\gamma_d}{2-\gamma_d}\varepsilon.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(r(K)\geq1/4\), the other branch of the minimum applies, proving the first estimate in (16). Since
\[
d\log(1+1/d)<1,
\]
one has \(\gamma_d=(1+1/d)^{-d}>e^{-1}\); the function \(s\mapsto s/(2-s)\) is increasing on \((0,1)\). These observations prove (17). The master bound
\[
h_{\max}(K)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}G(K)
\]
is part of accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`. Combining it with the exact lower bound for \(r(K)\) gives (18), and the elementary lower estimates just used give (19).

Finally, along (20), repeated application of (8) gives
\[
W(K_L)\geq\kappa_d^LW(K_0),
\]
while (2) and the nested inclusions give \(G(K_L)\leq G(K_0)\). Their quotient proves (21). If \(r(K_L)\geq\beta\), then (21) gives \(\beta\leq\kappa_d^{-L}r(K_0)\), proving (22); the strict comparison uses \(\kappa_d>1/(2e)\). The same inequality combined respectively with \(r(K_L)\geq r(K_0)+\eta\) and with
\[
r(K_L)\geq\frac{1-q}{4}+qr(K_0)
\]
gives (23) and (24) by rearrangement.

For the last assertion, the master bound at \(K_L\) yields the numerical certificate \(\gamma_d2^{-n}G(K_L)\). Any mixed-integer point of \(K_L\) is also a point of \(K_0\), and every closed-halfspace mass in \(K_0\) is at least the corresponding mass in \(K_L\); hence any depth certificate in \(K_L\) transfers to \(K_0\). But
\[
\gamma_d2^{-n}G(K_L)\leq\gamma_d2^{-n}G(K_0),
\]
and accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` already gives the right-hand certificate directly at \(K_0\). Thus a terminal complete-core certificate cannot improve the direct root complete-core bound. All conclusions are implications from accepted facts; neither NCP nor a vanishing-capacity sequence has been established.
