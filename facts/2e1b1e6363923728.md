## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
 \quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
 \varnothing\ne A\subseteq\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}
\]
be finite, put
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
\tag{2}
\]
and choose weights
\[
 0<w_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in A).
\tag{3}
\]
For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), set
\[
 A_\xi:=\{y\in A:y\equiv\xi\pmod2\},
 \qquad
 M_\xi:=\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y,
\tag{4}
\]
and define
\[
 B_{\xi,m}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac1{M_\xi}
 \sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
 w_uw_v,&M_\xi>0,\\[2ex]
 0,&M_\xi=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad m\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(M_\xi>0\), let
\[
 \mathcal H_{\xi,m}:=
 \bigl\{[h]:=\{h,-h\}:h\in\mathbb Z^n,
          \ m+h,m-h\in A_\xi\bigr\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Choose one representative \(h\in[h]\) for each orbit, with \(h=0\)
for \([h]=[0]\), and put
\[
 b_{\xi,m,[h]}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{w_m^2}{M_\xi},&[h]=[0],\\[2ex]
 \displaystyle\frac{2w_{m+h}w_{m-h}}{M_\xi},&[h]\ne[0].
 \end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Also put
\[
 \alpha_m:=\sum_\zeta B_{\zeta,m}.
\tag{8}
\]
Fix a total order on the parity classes. Let \(\mathcal L_\square\) be
the finite set of positive square labels
\[
 \lambda=(m;\xi,[h];\eta,[k]),
 \qquad
 \xi<\eta,
\tag{9}
\]
from accepted fact `17e91caf77284233`, where
\([h]\in\mathcal H_{\xi,m}\),
\([k]\in\mathcal H_{\eta,m}\), and
\[
 \theta_\lambda
 :=\frac{2b_{\xi,m,[h]}b_{\eta,m,[k]}}{\alpha_m}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
For each label retain the four tagged source occurrences, including
multiplicity,
\[
 \mathcal O_\lambda=[m+h,m-h,m+k,m-k],
\tag{11}
\]
and define
\[
 D_y:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L_\square}
 \theta_\lambda\operatorname{mult}_y(\mathcal O_\lambda),
 \qquad
 \Theta_\square:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L_\square}\theta_\lambda.
\tag{12}
\]
The occurrence ledger proved in accepted fact `17e91caf77284233` is
\[
 D_y\leq4w_y\leq a_y
 \qquad(y\in A).
\tag{13}
\]

Let
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{14}
\]
For a label \(\lambda=(m;\xi,[h];\eta,[k])\), define
\[
 r_\lambda:=\frac14
 \bigl(c_{m+h}+c_{m-h}+c_{m+k}+c_{m-k}\bigr),
 \qquad
 p_\lambda:=(m,r_\lambda),
\tag{15}
\]
and define the global square-centroid cloud
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_\square:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L_\square}
 \theta_\lambda\delta_{p_\lambda}.}
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{supp}\nu_\square
 \subseteq C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),}
\tag{17}
\]
and for every closed halfspace
\(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\),
\[
 \boxed{\nu_\square(H)\leq\mu_C(H),}
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
 \mu_C(E):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in E\}
\tag{19}
\]
for Borel \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Its mass is exactly
\[
 \boxed{|\nu_\square|=\gamma_d\Theta_\square.}
\tag{20}
\]
These conclusions include coincident atoms, labels sharing source points,
and labels for which \(h=0\) or \(k=0\); the repeated loop source in
(11) remains two tagged occurrences.

Only if \(\Theta_\square>0\), define
\[
 P_\square:=\operatorname{supp}\nu_\square,
 \qquad
 A_\square:=
 \operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(P_\square),
 \qquad
 \rho_\square:=\dim A_\square,
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
 r_\square:=
 \min_{s:A_\square\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{x-s(z):(z,x)\in P_\square\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Then there is \(p_\square^\star\in
C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) such that every closed halfspace
\(H\ni p_\square^\star\) satisfies
\[
 \mu_C(H)\geq\nu_\square(H)
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_d\Theta_\square}
 {2^{\rho_\square}(r_\square+1)}.
\tag{23}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}\mu_C(H),
\tag{24}
\]
one has the intrinsic-rank screen
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_d\Theta_\square}
 {2^{\rho_\square}(r_\square+1)}
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_d\Theta_\square}
 {2^n(r_\square+1)}.}
\tag{25}
\]
No assertion about \(\rho_\square\) or \(r_\square\) is made when
\(\Theta_\square=0\), and (25) does not bound \(r_\square\).

## Proof

Fix \(y\in A\). Since \(a_y>0\), the compact convex fiber \(C_y\)
is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. In accepted fact
`6769574fc1bd9f8d`, take \(K=C_y\) and metronoid mass \(a_y\). The
only admissible density of mass \(a_y\) with \(0\leq f\leq1\) is
\(f=1\) almost everywhere, so its normalized barycenter is \(c_y\).
That accepted fact therefore gives both \(c_y\in C_y\) and
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap J)\geq\gamma_da_y
\tag{26}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing
\(c_y\), including a halfspace whose boundary contains \(c_y\).

For each label \(\lambda\), introduce four tagged ambient source points
in the order displayed in (11):
\[
 q_{\lambda,1}:=(m+h,c_{m+h}),\quad
 q_{\lambda,2}:=(m-h,c_{m-h}),\quad
 q_{\lambda,3}:=(m+k,c_{m+k}),\quad
 q_{\lambda,4}:=(m-k,c_{m-k}).
\tag{27}
\]
The membership \(c_y\in C_y\) puts each point in \(C\). Moreover,
\[
 p_\lambda=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^4q_{\lambda,i}.
\tag{28}
\]
Convexity of \(C\) now gives \(p_\lambda\in C\), and its base
coordinate is the integer point \(m\). This proves (17). If a direction
is zero, the corresponding two entries of (27) are the same point, but
(28) still counts both tagged entries and remains valid.

We prove (18). Fix a proper closed ambient halfspace and write it as
\[
 H=\{(z,x):\langle u,z\rangle+\langle v,x\rangle\geq t\},
 \qquad (u,v)\ne(0,0).
\tag{29}
\]
Whenever \(p_\lambda\in H\), averaging the defining affine functional
in (28) shows that at least one tagged point \(q_{\lambda,i}\) belongs
to \(H\). This remains true when \(p_\lambda\) is on the boundary.
Orient this label to the first such tagged occurrence in the order
(27). For \(y\in A\), let
\[
 L_{H,y}:=
 \sum_{\substack{\lambda:p_\lambda\in H\\
 \text{the selected occurrence of }\lambda\text{ has source }y}}
 \theta_\lambda.
\tag{30}
\]
Every term in (30) is one of the tagged occurrences counted in (12).
Therefore, without identifying repeated sources or coincident labels,
\[
 0\leq L_{H,y}\leq D_y.
\tag{31}
\]
This is exactly where loops and repeated occurrences are handled: a loop
supplies two distinct available tags to \(D_y\), while the orientation
selects at most one of them for that label. Labels yielding the same
atom are still oriented separately, and labels reusing the same source
are all charged separately to the same \(D_y\).

If an occurrence with source \(y\) is selected, then
\((y,c_y)\in H\). The fiber section
\[
 H_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in H\}
\tag{32}
\]
contains \(c_y\). If \(v\ne0\), it is a proper closed halfspace, so
(26) applies. If \(v=0\), selection of a point over \(y\) implies
\(H_y=\mathbb R^d\), and then
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)=a_y\geq\gamma_da_y\).
Using (13) and discarding all unselected nonnegative fiber volumes gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu_\square(H)
 &=\gamma_d\sum_{\lambda:p_\lambda\in H}\theta_\lambda
 =\gamma_d\sum_{y\in A}L_{H,y}\\
 &\leq\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in A\\(y,c_y)\in H}}D_y
 \leq\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in A\\(y,c_y)\in H}}a_y\\
 &\leq
 \sum_{\substack{y\in A\\(y,c_y)\in H}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
The same argument covers the whole-space halfspace, by selecting the
first tag of every label and using \(H_y=\mathbb R^d\). The empty
improper halfspace has zero mass for both measures. Thus (18) holds for
every convention concerning improper closed halfspaces.

Since every Dirac measure in (16) has unit total mass and all
coefficients are nonnegative,
\[
 |\nu_\square|
 =\gamma_d\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal L_\square}\theta_\lambda
 =\gamma_d\Theta_\square.
\tag{34}
\]
Coincident locations do not change this identity: their coefficients add
at the common atom. This proves (20), including the empty-label case.

Assume finally that \(\Theta_\square>0\). Then (34) makes
\(\nu_\square\) a nonzero positive finite Borel measure carried by the
mixed-integer body, and (18) is precisely the halfspace-domination
hypothesis of the dominated-measure residual theorem in accepted fact
`be359f8a6c628483`. Applying that theorem with
\(\sigma=\nu_\square\) proves (23) and the first inequality in (25).
The base affine rank satisfies \(\rho_\square\leq n\), which proves the
second inequality. The theorem leaves \(r_\square\) as the actual
residual rank of this cloud and supplies no bound on it. No target-fiber
density or additive packet-energy assertion was used. This completes the
proof.
