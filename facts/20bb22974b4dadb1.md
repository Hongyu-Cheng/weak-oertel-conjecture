## Statement

Consider two fixed barycentric relation couplings of uniform points on actual compact positive integer fibers, with the same shared source marginal and one common target fiber, as in the shared-source cap credit setting. Fix closed ambient caps $H_1,\ldots,H_k$ and weights $\omega_i\ge0$. There is one joint gluing over the shared source maximizing the weighted sum of the cap credits $c_1 f_{1i}+c_2 f_{2i}-\rho o_i$, where the probabilities and coefficients are those of the shared-source credit. It preserves the two complete original relation couplings. More precisely, at each shared-source point $x$, let $q_1(\cdot\mid x),q_2(\cdot\mid x)$ be the conditional laws of the two output incidence vectors in $\{0,1\}^k$. The optimum is achieved by minimizing
$$
\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\{0,1\}^k}T_{\sigma,\tau}(x)\,\rho\sum_{i=1}^k\omega_i\sigma_i\tau_i
$$
over joint probability matrices with those conditional row and column marginals, and lifting the chosen matrices to the original coordinate laws.

## Proof

Use the two original conditional kernels given the shared source to obtain Borel versions of the conditional signature probabilities. For every gluing the marginal cap probabilities $f_{ri}$ are fixed, and its overlap is the expectation of the corresponding product of two signature coordinates. Thus its weighted cost term is the integral of the displayed conditional matrix cost. Conversely, select a Borel optimizing matrix as follows. The finite transportation polytope is nonempty and compact. Delete one redundant marginal equation, leaving a fixed full-row-rank matrix. A minimizing vertex exists and is a basic feasible solution, possibly degenerate. For each of finitely many column bases its coordinates are linear in the marginal probability vector. Feasibility and pairwise objective comparisons are Borel, so select the first feasible cost-minimizing solution in a fixed order.

Disintegrate each original relation law further by its own signature and the shared source. Given $x$ and the pair of signatures drawn from the selected matrix, sample the remaining relation coordinates from their own original conditional kernels. This is well defined up to null conditions, and entries corresponding to marginal probability zero are not sampled. Integrate over the common source law. The row and column marginals reconstruct both original relation laws exactly, and the new conditional signatures are distributed as the chosen matrix. The affine barycentric identities persist because the original relations are preserved. The resulting common gluing attains the weighted optimum.
