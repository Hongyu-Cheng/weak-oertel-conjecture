## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. For \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), put \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\) and \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), taking zero volume for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Assume \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0\). Put \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. For every positive-volume fiber, \(\lambda_z\) is the uniform probability measure on \(C_z\). These are the canonical objects of accepted facts `03f650d1c4f1b86d` and `15ec168ab4433041`.

Fix \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\). For every oriented displacement \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), put \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), and let \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\). For \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), let \(\nu_v\) be the probability law of \((X^++X^-)/2\) for independent \(X^+\sim\lambda_{j+v}\), \(X^-\sim\lambda_{j-v}\). In particular, \(\nu_0\) uses two independent points of \(C_j\), and \(\nu_v=\nu_{-v}\). No midpoint law is assigned to a zero-volume pair. Let \(\mathcal R_j\) contain one representative of each unordered pair \(\{v,-v\}\) in \(\mathcal U_j\setminus\{0\}\), and retain

\[
\kappa_j:=w_j\lambda_j+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}\alpha(v)\nu_v.
\]

All these objects have exactly their meanings in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`. In accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, the substitutions are \(m=j\), \(\alpha_v=\alpha(v)\) for \(v\in\mathcal R_j\), and \(M_\#(\lambda_{m+v}\otimes\lambda_{m-v})=\nu_v\), where its \(M(x,x')=(x+x')/2\) is the midpoint map.

Using natural logarithms, define \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) only for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). This is a finite, nonnegative, even function, with \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). Let \((q_v)_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be any even family satisfying \(0\le q_v\le\alpha(v)\) and \(\sum_vq_v>0\). Such a family has finite support, and \(q_v=0\) off \(\mathcal U_j\). Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_q&:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}q_v,
&\overline\Delta_{j,q}&:=\frac1{M_q}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}q_v\Delta_j(v),\\
P_{j,q}&:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\frac{q_v}{M_q}\nu_v,
&\sigma_{j,q}&:=\frac{M_q}{2}P_{j,q}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The scalar \(M_q\) is distinct from the predecessor's midpoint map \(M\), and the probability measure \(P_{j,q}\) is distinct from its scalar \(P_m=|\kappa_m|\). There is no assumption that \(q_0>0\).

For a finite measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), let \(\operatorname{TD}_\mu(x):=\inf_{G\ni x}\mu(G)\), over proper closed halfspaces \(G\subset\mathbb R^d\). This is the pointwise depth \(D_\mu\) in accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`; allowing the whole space does not change it. Put \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\). Then \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j,q}\|\nu_0)\le\overline\Delta_{j,q}\), and \(\sigma_{j,q}(G)\le\kappa_j(G)\) for every closed halfspace \(G\subset\mathbb R^d\), including the whole space if admitted. There exists \(x_{j,q}^\star\in C_j\) such that

\[
h_S((j,x_{j,q}^\star))
\ge\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x_{j,q}^\star)
\ge\frac{\ell M_q}{16(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2)}.
\]

The point is selected after \(j,q\) and before any halfspace: the same point satisfies the bound for every closed ambient halfspace containing it.

For \(L\ge0\), define \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\). There exists \(p^\star\in S\) such that

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{\ell}{16}
\max_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z^n,\ w_j>0\\L\ge0}}
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}.
\]

The displayed maximum is attained at one of finitely many choices of \(j\) and curvature values \(L\in\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\). Consequently, the additional hypothesis that there is an absolute \(a>0\) such that \(\max_{j:w_j>0,\,L\ge0}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\ge a\,2^{-n}W\) for every such \(n,d,C\) would prove the locked weak Oertel conjecture with \(c_0:=\min\{a\ell/16,1/(2e)\}\). This uniform selection hypothesis is not asserted by the theorem.

## Proof

The compact projection of \(C\) contains only finitely many lattice points. Thus there are finitely many positive-volume integer fibers, every \(\mathcal U_j\) is finite, and every sum above is finite. Since \(w_j>0\), the displacement zero belongs to \(\mathcal U_j\) and has \(\alpha(0)=w_j\). Positive-volume fibers are full-dimensional compact convex bodies; zero-volume fibers contribute no weight or probability law. Fix \(j\) and \(q\) as in the statement.

We first extract the exact comparison already proved for these midpoint laws. The midpoint volume inequality in the proof of accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d` gives \(w_j^{1/d}\ge(w_{j+v}^{1/d}+w_{j-v}^{1/d})/2\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality implies \(w_j^2\ge w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\). Hence \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\); finiteness, evenness and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\) follow directly from the definition. Also \(\Delta_j(v)\le2\log(w_j/\alpha(v))\), since \(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\ge\alpha(v)^2\).

The density construction in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`, with exactly the same \(j,v,C_z,w_z,\lambda_z,\nu_v\), proves that \(\nu_v\) has the continuous, compactly supported density

\[
f_v(x)=\frac{2^d}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\operatorname{vol}_d R_v(x),
\qquad R_v(x):=C_{j+v}\cap(2x-C_{j-v}).
\]

Every \(f_v\) is supported in \(C_j\). In particular, all these laws have finite first absolute moments. The same accepted proof establishes log-concavity of \(f_0\) by applying its stated convolution theorem to the two uniform probability densities on \(C_j\). It also proves \(\operatorname{vol}_d R_v(x)\le\operatorname{vol}_d R_0(x)\) for every \(x\): the reflected intersections satisfy \(R_{-v}(x)=2x-R_v(x)\) and \((R_v(x)+R_{-v}(x))/2\subseteq R_0(x)\), and that proof handles empty and zero-volume intersections separately. We use this exact intermediate inequality, before its coarser replacement by the minimum volume. Substituting into the density formula gives

\[
f_v(x)\le\frac{w_j^2}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}f_0(x)
=e^{\Delta_j(v)}f_0(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

Thus \(\nu_v\ll\nu_0\) and its density ratio is at most \(e^{\Delta_j(v)}\). The negative part of its relative-entropy integrand is integrable because \(t\log t\ge-1/e\) and \(\nu_0\) is a probability measure. The ratio bound controls the positive part and gives \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_v\|\nu_0)\le\Delta_j(v)\). Here and below \(0\log0=0\). Taking the finite mixture with weights \(q_v/M_q\), convexity of \(t\log t\), applied to the density ratios with respect to \(\nu_0\) and integrated, gives

\[
D_{j,q}:=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j,q}\|\nu_0)
\le\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\frac{q_v}{M_q}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_v\|\nu_0)
\le\overline\Delta_{j,q}<\infty.
\]

Terms with \(q_v=0\) may simply be omitted. The mixture has a continuous density \(\sum_v(q_v/M_q)f_v\), compactly supported in \(C_j\), and is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu_0\).

Apply accepted fact `5bd39b316ef3c194` with its probability measures \(P=P_{j,q}\), \(Q=\nu_0\), densities \(f=\sum_v(q_v/M_q)f_v\), \(g=f_0\), and relative entropy \(D=D_{j,q}\). Its \(\Delta\) denotes the supremum of the probability halfspace depth, not the curvature \(\Delta_j(v)\) defined here. The preceding paragraphs verify continuity and compact support of \(f\), log-concavity and a finite first absolute moment of \(g\), absolute continuity, and finite relative entropy. That accepted lemma therefore supplies one point \(x_{j,q}^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}P_{j,q})\subseteq C_j\) for which

\[
\operatorname{TD}_{P_{j,q}}(x_{j,q}^\star)
\ge\frac{\ell}{8(D_{j,q}+\log2)}
\ge\frac{\ell}{8(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2)}.
\]

Next, evenness of \(q\) and \(\nu_v=\nu_{-v}\) give the exact unordered expansion \(\sigma_{j,q}=(q_0/2)\nu_0+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}q_v\nu_v\). If the midpoint of two points belongs to a closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), at least one endpoint belongs to \(G\). Applying this observation to two independent points with law \(\lambda_j\) and taking a union bound gives \(\nu_0(G)\le2\lambda_j(G)\). It also holds for the whole space. Since \(q_0\le\alpha(0)=w_j\) and \(q_v\le\alpha(v)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{j,q}(G)
&=\frac{q_0}{2}\nu_0(G)+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}q_v\nu_v(G)\\
&\le q_0\lambda_j(G)+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}\alpha(v)\nu_v(G)
\le\kappa_j(G).
\end{aligned}
\]

This argument includes \(q_0=0\) and charges each noncentral unordered pair once. It is a comparison on halfspaces; domination on arbitrary Borel sets is not needed. Taking infima over halfspaces containing a fixed \(x\), and using the scalar normalization of \(\sigma_{j,q}\), gives \(\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x)\ge(M_q/2)\operatorname{TD}_{P_{j,q}}(x)\). At the point already selected, this proves \(\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x_{j,q}^\star)\ge\ell M_q/[16(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2)]\).

For completeness, the required lift is pointwise, so it preserves this same selected point. Use equations (7) through (11) in the proof of accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, with \(m=j\), \(y=x\), its \(a=a_H\), \(b=b_H\), and the coefficient and midpoint-law substitutions stated above. For any \(x\in C_j\), a proper ambient halfspace through \((j,x)\) can be written as \(H=\{(z,u):a_H^\top(z-j)+b_H^\top(u-x)\ge0\}\), with \((a_H,b_H)\ne(0,0)\). If \(b_H\ne0\), put \(G:=\{u:b_H^\top(u-x)\ge0\}\). If \(b_H=0\), put \(G:=\mathbb R^d\). The accepted pointwise inequality is \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\kappa_j(G)\), so in either case it is at least \(\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x)\). For \(b_H=0\), the entire central fiber lies on the ambient boundary and its full volume is included by the predecessor's equation (9). Thus no zero-boundary-mass assumption is made for vertical ambient halfspaces.

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing \((j,x)\) contains a parallel halfspace through that point, so monotonicity gives the same lower bound. The whole-space case also holds: its mixed mass is \(W\), whereas \(|\kappa_j|\le W\), because the central fiber and the endpoint fibers of the pairs in \(\mathcal R_j\) are all distinct and \(\alpha(v)\le w_{j+v}+w_{j-v}\). Consequently \(h_S((j,x))\ge\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x)\) for every \(x\in C_j\). Applying this to \(x_{j,q}^\star\) proves the claimed mixed-depth bound with the required order of quantifiers.

For the cutoff conclusion, fix \(L\ge0\), and choose \(q_v:=\alpha(v)\) when \(v\in\mathcal U_j\) and \(\Delta_j(v)\le L\), and \(q_v:=0\) otherwise. This is an even thinning. It has \(M_q=B_j^\Delta(L)\ge\alpha(0)=w_j>0\) and \(\overline\Delta_{j,q}\le L\). The proved bound therefore supplies a point in the fiber \(j\) of depth at least \(\ell B_j^\Delta(L)/[16(L+\log2)]\), which is at least \((\ell/16)B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\), since \(\log2<1\).

We justify maximizing over the unbounded parameter \(L\). For this fixed \(j\), the finite nonempty set \(\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\) contains zero. Given any \(L\ge0\), let \(t\) be its largest member not exceeding \(L\). The terms in the cutoff sum are unchanged when \(L\) is replaced by \(t\), while \(1+t\le1+L\). Hence \(B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\le B_j^\Delta(t)/(1+t)\). The maximum for this \(j\) is therefore attained at a member of that finite set. There are only finitely many positive-volume integer fibers, so the joint maximum over \(j,L\) is attained. Select one maximizing pair and the point furnished by its cutoff thinning to obtain the asserted \(p^\star\).

The curvature is the second difference \(2\log w_j-\log w_{j+v}-\log w_{j-v}\). Adding an affine function of the base to the log-volume profile leaves this expression unchanged, because its values at \(j+v\) and \(j-v\) sum to twice its value at \(j\). This is the claimed cancellation of affine tilt in the curvature loss.

Finally, under the additional uniform selection hypothesis stated in the theorem, the selected point has depth at least \((a\ell/16)2^{-n}W\). The number \(c_0=\min\{a\ell/16,1/(2e)\}\) is an absolute constant strictly between zero and \(1/e\), and decreasing the lower-bound constant to \(c_0\) gives the locked target for \(W>0\). When \(W=0\), that target holds at any point of the assumed nonempty mixed set \(S\). This proves the sufficient implication and makes no claim that its selection hypothesis holds.
