## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For a tuple \(\mathbf q=(q_1,\ldots,q_t)\) of positive real numbers, define the composition partition sums by
\[
h_k(\mathbf q):=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{t}\\\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_i=k}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}q_i^{n_i}
\quad(k\ge0),
\qquad h_k(\mathbf q):=0\quad(k<0).
\]
For the empty tuple, use \(h_0(\varnothing)=1\) and \(h_k(\varnothing)=0\) for \(k\ne0\). Here \(h_k\) is a partition sum, not the halfspace-depth function \(h_S\) in the locked problem. The \(q_i\) are exponential count weights, not parity probabilities or the face-conditioning masses denoted \(q_i\) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Multiplying all count weights by a common positive number leaves a fixed-total composition law unchanged. The normalized weight ratios for an entropy row are specified explicitly in the proof.

For every finite tuple of positive weights, \(h_k(\mathbf q)^2\ge h_{k-1}(\mathbf q)h_{k+1}(\mathbf q)\) for every integer \(k\ge0\). For integers \(m\ge0\) and \(s\ge1\), let \(\mathbf N=(N_1,\ldots,N_s)\) have the law
\[
\mathbb P_{\mathbf q}(\mathbf N=\mathbf n)
=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_i^{n_i}}{Z_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbf q)},
\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbf q):=h_m(\mathbf q),
\qquad
\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{s},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_i=m.
\]
Every coordinate marginal is log-concave on its consecutive positive support, and \(\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb P_{\mathbf q}}(N_i,N_j)\le0\) for every pair of distinct coordinates.

Now let \(m\ge1\) and \(r\ge0\) be integers and set \(D_m:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{r}:\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_i\le m\}\). Its intrinsic rank is \(r\). For each \(\mathbf j\in D_m\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}:=r_{D_m,\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy law of mean \(\mathbf j\), using the notation of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 with support argument \(E=D_m\). When \(r=2\), these \(D_m,\nu_{\mathbf j}\), and \(H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\) are exactly those of accepted fact 2a9ff24df711450b. Entropy has the same normalization as in that fact: \(H(\nu):=-\sum_{\mathbf x:\nu(\mathbf x)>0}\nu(\mathbf x)\log\nu(\mathbf x)=-\mathbb E_{\nu}\log\nu(\mathbf X)\).

Define \(N_i:=X_i\) and \(j_i:=\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}X_i\) for \(1\le i\le r\), and define the slack count and its mean by \(N_{r+1}:=m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}X_i\) and \(j_{r+1}:=m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}j_i\). Put \(\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}:=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}:j_i>0\}\) and \(s:=|\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}|\). If \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is the minimal face containing \(\mathbf j\), then \(\dim F_{\mathbf j}=s-1\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\!\left(\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)\right)
&=\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\!\left[\bigl(\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)+H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\bigr)^2\right]\\
&\le 2(s-1)\le2r.
\end{aligned}
\]
The logarithm is evaluated only on the positive support. The bound includes all proper faces; a vertex row has variance zero. Equivalently, the centered self-information \(-\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)-H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\) has the same variance and the same bound.

## Proof

First we prove log-concavity of the partition sums by a finite matrix argument. Fix a nonnegative truncation index \(L_{\mathrm T}\). For a positive real \(q\), form the matrix \(T(q)\), with rows and columns indexed by \(0,\ldots,L_{\mathrm T}\), whose entry in row \(u\), column \(v\) is \(q^{v-u}\) if \(v\ge u\), and zero otherwise. Thus \(T(q)\) is the upper-triangular Toeplitz matrix of the one-sided geometric sequence \(1,q,q^2,\ldots\).

Every two-by-two minor of \(T(q)\) with increasing row and column indices is nonnegative. Indeed, for rows \(u_1<u_2\) and columns \(v_1<v_2\), if \(v_1<u_2\), the lower-left entry is zero, so the determinant is the product of the diagonal entries and is nonnegative. If \(v_1\ge u_2\), all four entries are positive and both determinant products equal \(q^{v_1+v_2-u_1-u_2}\), so the determinant is zero.

This property is preserved by multiplication of these finite matrices. More generally, for two finite square matrices \(\mathsf U,\mathsf V\), direct expansion of a two-by-two determinant, cancellation of terms with equal intermediate indices, and pairing the two orders of distinct intermediate indices give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\det\bigl((\mathsf U\mathsf V)_{\{u_1,u_2\},\{v_1,v_2\}}\bigr)\\
&\qquad=\sum_{0\le w_1<w_2\le L_{\mathrm T}}
\det\bigl(\mathsf U_{\{u_1,u_2\},\{w_1,w_2\}}\bigr)
\det\bigl(\mathsf V_{\{w_1,w_2\},\{v_1,v_2\}}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, if both factors have nonnegative such minors, so does their product. This is the required finite two-by-two Cauchy--Binet identity, with no infinite-matrix assertion involved.

For \(\mathbf q=(q_1,\ldots,q_t)\), the product \(T(q_1)\cdots T(q_t)\) has entry \(h_{v-u}(\mathbf q)\) in row \(u\), column \(v\). To see this, multiplying two upper-triangular Toeplitz matrices convolves their coefficient sequences: the contributing intermediate indices are exactly \(u\le w\le v\), and hence none is lost at either truncation boundary. Induction gives the stated product entry. An empty product is the identity and gives the specified empty-tuple sequence. All the product's ordered two-by-two minors are nonnegative. For \(k\ge1\), choose \(L_{\mathrm T}\ge k+1\) and take rows \(0,1\), columns \(k,k+1\). The resulting minor is \(h_k^2-h_{k-1}h_{k+1}\ge0\). At \(k=0\), the same inequality follows from \(h_{-1}=0\). This proves the claimed log-concavity for every tuple. For a nonempty positive tuple, every \(h_k\), \(k\ge0\), is strictly positive, so the ratios \(h_{k+1}/h_k\) are nonincreasing.

We next prove negative covariance for the complete-composition law. If \(m=0\), all counts are zero. If \(s=1\), its sole count is deterministic. If \(s=2\), then \(N_j=m-N_i\) for the distinct pair, and \(\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,N_j)=-\operatorname{Var}(N_i)\le0\).

Suppose \(s\ge3\), fix distinct \(i,j\), and write \(h_k:=h_k((q_\ell)_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}})\) temporarily for the partition sum of the remaining coordinates. This tuple is nonempty, so \(h_k>0\) for all \(k\ge0\). The joint mass is
\[
f(a,b):=\mathbb P_{\mathbf q}(N_i=a,N_j=b)
=Z_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbf q)^{-1}q_i^a q_j^b h_{m-a-b}
\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}).
\]
It satisfies the reverse two-by-two inequality
\[
f(a,b)f(a',b')\le f(a,b')f(a',b)
\qquad(a<a',\ b<b').
\]
If the left-hand side is zero, the inequality is immediate. Otherwise put \(k=m-a'-b'\ge0\), \(d=a'-a>0\), and \(e=b'-b>0\). Decreasing successive ratios give
\[
\frac{h_{k+d+e}}{h_{k+d}}
=\prod_{v=1}^{e}\frac{h_{k+d+v}}{h_{k+d+v-1}}
\le\prod_{v=1}^{e}\frac{h_{k+v}}{h_{k+v-1}}
=\frac{h_{k+e}}{h_k}.
\]
Thus \(h_{k+d+e}h_k\le h_{k+d}h_{k+e}\). The \(q_i,q_j\) factors on the two sides of the desired joint inequality are identical, proving it, including the support boundary.

Each value \(a\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) has positive marginal probability. Let \(f_a(b):=\mathbb P_{\mathbf q}(N_j=b\mid N_i=a)\) and \(\mu(a):=\mathbb E_{\mathbf q}[N_j\mid N_i=a]\). Dividing the joint inequality by the two positive row probabilities shows, for \(a<a'\) and \(b<c\), that \(f_a(b)f_{a'}(c)\le f_a(c)f_{a'}(b)\). Therefore
\[
\mu(a)-\mu(a')
=\sum_{0\le b<c\le m}(c-b)
\bigl(f_a(c)f_{a'}(b)-f_a(b)f_{a'}(c)\bigr)\ge0.
\]
The equality follows by writing the difference of means as \(\sum_{b,c}(b-c)f_a(b)f_{a'}(c)\) and pairing the two orders. Thus \(\mu\) is nonincreasing. Conditional expectation gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,N_j)=\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,\mu(N_i))\). For an independent copy \(N_i'\) of the scalar \(N_i\),
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,\mu(N_i))
=\mathbb E\bigl[(N_i-N_i')(\mu(N_i)-\mu(N_i'))\bigr]\le0.
\]
This independent copy is used only for the scalar covariance identity; no independence of the composition coordinates, conditional or otherwise, is assumed.

For the marginal assertion, when \(s\ge2\) the mass of \(N_i=k\), for \(0\le k\le m\), is \(q_i^k h_{m-k}((q_\ell)_{\ell\ne i})/Z_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbf q)\). It is positive throughout this interval. Reversing a log-concave sequence and multiplying it by a geometric sequence preserve its defining adjacent inequalities. Hence this marginal is log-concave. Its zero extension satisfies the endpoint inequalities as well. When \(s=1\), the marginal is a point mass and the assertion is also true.

We now identify each entropy row with such a complete-composition law on its active face. If \(r=0\), \(D_m\) is a singleton, so all the entropy-row claims hold directly. Assume \(r\ge1\). Apply accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 to \(A=D_m\) and the uniform positive reference law. Its relative-entropy minimizer is the maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) law, namely \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\). That fact proves that it is positive throughout \(E_{\mathbf j}:=D_m\cap F_{\mathbf j}\), zero outside \(E_{\mathbf j}\), and log-affine on this face support, with a finite parameter in the face's affine hull. Every such parameter has a finite extension \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\in\mathbb R^r\), so we can write
\[
\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\cdot\mathbf x}}
       {Z_{E_{\mathbf j}}(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})}
\quad(\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf j}),
\qquad
Z_{E_{\mathbf j}}(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})
:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf j}}e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\cdot\mathbf x}.
\]
For \(r=2\) and a full row, \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\) and \(Z_{E_{\mathbf j}}\) are exactly \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\) and \(Z\), respectively, in accepted fact 2a9ff24df711450b. A boundary row needs only this finite face parameter, not a finite ambient parameter giving positive mass off the face. Moreover, \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{E_{\mathbf j},\mathbf j}\) as well as \(r_{D_m,\mathbf j}\), in the notation of bf1ff94487296374.

The map \(\mathbf x\mapsto(x_1,\ldots,x_r,m-\sum_i x_i)\) identifies \(D_m\) bijectively with the nonnegative integer compositions of \(m\) into \(r+1\) counts. Its real-valued extension identifies the simplex with the real nonnegative compositions. Faces are obtained by setting some count coordinates to zero. Since the barycentric coordinates of \(\mathbf j\) are \(j_i/m\), its minimal face has precisely the active coordinates \(\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\), and has dimension \(s-1\). On that face, the active integer counts range over all compositions of \(m\) into \(s\) parts. Equivalently, inactive counts are zero almost surely already because they are nonnegative with mean zero. All active means are positive integers, hence \(j_i\ge1\).

Put \(\lambda_{\mathbf j,r+1}:=0\), choose \(i_\star\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\) at which \(\lambda_{\mathbf j,i}\) is largest among the active coordinates, and define the row's normalized ratios by \(q_i:=\exp(\lambda_{\mathbf j,i}-\lambda_{\mathbf j,i_\star})\) for \(i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\). Thus \(q_{i_\star}=1\) and \(0<q_i\le1\). This is the exact map from the canonical exponential row parameter to the count weights used above. For a full row, the ungauged weights are \((e^{\lambda_{\mathbf j,1}},\ldots,e^{\lambda_{\mathbf j,r}},1)\), and each \(q_i\) is its ratio to the largest active weight. The fixed-total identity gives the normalization map
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{comp}}((q_i)_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}})
&=e^{-m\lambda_{\mathbf j,i_\star}}Z_{E_{\mathbf j}}(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}),\\
\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)
&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}}(\log q_i)N_i
  -\log Z_{\mathrm{comp}}((q_i)_{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the active-count law is exactly the complete-composition law already treated. Deleting the \(i_\star\)-th count makes it the slack coordinate: the inverse map restores that count as \(m\) minus the sum of the others. This is an integer affine bijection on the face lattice. Bijections preserve the probability masses, \(H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\), and the self-log-density variance. Inactive counts contribute the constant zero, and any change in a face-normal parameter or in the common gauge contributes only a constant to the log score, so neither affects its variance.

Fix an active \(i\ne i_\star\). For an integer \(t\ge1\), transfer \(t\) units from coordinate \(i\) to the slack \(i_\star\). This is a bijection from the compositions with \(N_i\ge t\) to those with \(N_{i_\star}\ge t\); the original weight is \(q_i^t\) times the image weight, since \(q_{i_\star}=1\). Empty sets when \(t>m\) cause no exception. Hence
\[
\mathbb P_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(N_i\ge t)
=q_i^t\mathbb P_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(N_{i_\star}\ge t)
\le q_i^t.
\]
If \(q_i<1\), summing these finite survival probabilities and then bounding by the infinite geometric series yields \(j_i\le q_i/(1-q_i)\). For \(q_i=1\) the resulting lower bound on \(q_i\) is immediate. In both cases,
\[
q_i\ge\frac{j_i}{j_i+1},
\qquad
|\log q_i|=-\log q_i\le\log(1+1/j_i)\le\frac1{j_i}.
\]

The marginal log-concavity proved above permits direct use of the scalar log-concavity tail argument in accepted fact 2a9ff24df711450b. Precisely, for every finite nonnegative integer-valued law \(Y\) with consecutive positive support, log-concave masses, and mean \(j\), that argument proves \(\mathbb E[Y(Y-1)]\le2j^2\), hence \(\mathbb E Y^2\le2j^2+j\). Subtracting \(j^2\) gives \(\operatorname{Var}Y\le j(j+1)\). Applied to each active count, this gives \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}N_i\le j_i(j_i+1)\). This use is only of the scalar moment consequence, not an inference of negative covariance from marginal log-concavity.

If \(s=1\), the row is deterministic, proving the stated zero bound. For \(s\ge2\), discard the constant normalizer and the zero coefficient of the slack in the log-density formula. Every remaining coefficient \(\log q_i\) is nonpositive, and every pairwise count covariance is nonpositive by the preceding complete-composition proof. Their products therefore make all cross terms nonpositive. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)
&=\sum_{\substack{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\\i\ne i_\star}}(\log q_i)^2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}N_i
 +2\sum_{\substack{i<j,\ i,j\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\\i,j\ne i_\star}}
 (\log q_i)(\log q_j)\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(N_i,N_j)\\
&\le\sum_{\substack{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\\i\ne i_\star}}
 \frac{j_i(j_i+1)}{j_i^2}
=\sum_{\substack{i\in\mathcal I_{\mathbf j}\\i\ne i_\star}}(1+1/j_i)
\le2(s-1)\le2r.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, the entropy normalization gives \(\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)=-H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\), so this variance is exactly the centered second moment stated above. All estimates are exact and hold on every minimal face.
