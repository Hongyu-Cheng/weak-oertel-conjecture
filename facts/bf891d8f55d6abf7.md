## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty and finite and let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$, $p\in P$, be compact convex bodies of positive $d$-volume, with $d\ge1$. Write $h_p(u)=\max_{x\in K_p}u\cdot x$, and mark each regular boundary point $x$ of $K_p$ by its unique outer unit normal $u=n_p(x)$. The physical coordinate is $w=(p,x)$ and the compact marked exposed-face relation is $N=\{(u,p,x):u\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ x\in K_p,\ u\cdot x=h_p(u)\}$. Suppose $K_p$ is the actual $p$-section of $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{q\in P}(\{q\}\times K_q)$ for every $p\in P$. Let $\nu,\eta$ be finite nonnegative Borel measures on the physical regular boundaries (or their lifts to $N$). The following are equivalent:

1. The physical measures satisfy $\nu\preceq_{\rm cx}\eta$.
2. The marked measures satisfy $\int\psi(u)A(w)\,d(\eta-\nu)=0$ and $\int\psi_+(u)\Phi(w)\,d(\eta-\nu)\ge0$ for all continuous $\psi$, nonnegative continuous $\psi_+$ on $\mathbb S^{d-1}$, affine $A$ and finite continuous convex $\Phi$ on the ambient physical space.
3. There is a marked coupling $\Pi$ from $\nu$ to $\eta$ with equal input and output normals almost everywhere and $\int f(u,p,x)(q-p,y-x)\,d\Pi=0$ for all bounded Borel $f$.

In fact every physical martingale coupling from $\nu$ to $\eta$ preserves the normal. For $\nu$-almost every input $(u,p,x)$ the output row is supported on $y$ in the $u$-exposed face of $K_q$, with $h_q(u)=h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(q-p)$ for the continuous touching slope $A_p$ of accepted fact 155691fc3768c06f. Its base probabilities $\alpha_q$ give an active anchored representation, meaning $\sum_q\alpha_q q=p$ and $\sum_q\alpha_q h_q(u)=h_p(u)$.

## Proof

By actuality, every Minkowski combination of the fibers with base barycenter $p$ lies in $K_p$: a choice of one point from each fiber with positive coefficient gives a point of $C$ at base $p$. This is the anchored inclusion of accepted fact 155691fc3768c06f. For each $p$ that fact gives a bounded continuous slope $A_p(u)$ such that
$$
u\cdot y\le h_q(u)\le h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(q-p)
\qquad (q\in P,\ y\in K_q).
$$
The rightmost affine majorant touches at $p$. For every $z\in\operatorname{conv}(P)$ the support $h_{C_z}(u)$ is the maximum of $\sum_q\alpha_q h_q(u)$ over convex representations $z=\sum_q\alpha_q q$: group a convex combination in $C$ by base, then choose a support maximizer in each nonzero-weight fiber for the reverse inequality. Actuality gives $h_{C_p}(u)=h_p(u)$.

The regular locus is Borel because the compact unit-normal multifunction has closed graph and the sets where two normals have separation at least $1/m$ are compact. On the regular locus the normal is relatively continuous by compactness and uniqueness. Thus both regular physical marginals lift measurably to $N$ and have finite mass. In $d=1$ regularity includes both endpoints. Assume (1) and use the compact physical martingale lemma to obtain a coupling $\pi$. The following argument applies to any such coupling. Write a regular input as $(p,x)$ and put $u=n_p(x)$. Then $u\cdot x=h_p(u)$ and the nonnegative gap on input-output pairs is
$$
h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(q-p)-u\cdot y
=A_p(u)\cdot(q-p)-u\cdot(y-x).
$$
All coefficient functions on the right are bounded Borel in the input, by the Borel normal and the finite list of bounded continuous slope choices. The row moment identities thus make the integral of the gap zero. Nonnegativity forces equality $\pi$-almost everywhere. The two preceding inequalities force $u\cdot y=h_q(u)=h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(q-p)$. Since the target is regular almost everywhere, its normal is exactly $u$. Lifting $\pi$ to both normal graphs gives (3); all its marked row identities follow by composing the test with the input graph.

For almost every input, the regular conditional probability of the lifted coupling is concentrated on the stated exposed faces with touching equality. Its base probabilities give $\sum_q\alpha_q q=p$ by the row mean. Taking the dot product of the continuous mean with the input normal gives $\sum_q\alpha_q h_q(u)=h_p(u)$, so the representation attains the roof at $p$.

Finally, (3) implies (2) by disintegrating over the input and applying affine equality and conditional Jensen to the full physical output, multiplied by the continuous tests of the preserved normal. Condition (2) implies (1) by using the constant parameter test and affine test $A=1$ for equal masses. All statements also hold with the zero coupling at zero common mass.
