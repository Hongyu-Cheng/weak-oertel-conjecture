## Statement

For a finite set $P$, its dependence components are generated by joining the target to every positive-coefficient source in each nontrivial anchored convex representation of a site of $P$ by sites of $P$. Let
$$
a=(0,0,1),\quad b=(0,0,3),\quad d=(0,2,1),\quad
f=(1,2,0),\quad h=(2,0,0),\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}\{a,b,d,f,h\}.
$$
The integer set $P=Q\cap\mathbb Z^3$ consists of the five displayed vertices and
$$
e=(0,0,2),\quad j=(0,1,1),\quad t=(0,1,2),\quad v=(1,0,1).
$$
Its dependence components are $P_1=P\setminus\{f\}$ and $\{f\}$, of affine ranks three and zero, so $\sum_i2^{\dim\operatorname{aff} P_i}=9>2^3$. More generally, for each integer $k\ge3$, the integer set of $Q\times[0,1]^{k-3}$ has $2^{k-3}$ copies of each component with the same ranks and rank cost $9\cdot2^{k-3}>2^k$.

## Proof

The five vertices satisfy
$$
2x+y+4z\ge4,\quad x+z\ge1,\quad x\ge0,\quad y\ge0,\quad
x+y+z\le3,\quad 6x+3y+4z\le12
$$
and lie in $[0,2]^2\times[0,3]$. Therefore their hull does too. At integral $x=0$, the possible $(y,z)$ are $(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)$ by these inequalities. At $x=1$ the possibilities are $(0,1),(2,0)$: if $z=0$ then $2+y\ge4$ forces $y=2$; if $z\ge1$ then $3y+4z\le6$ forces $z=1,y=0$. At $x=2$ the last inequality forces $y=z=0$. All nine points are in the hull because $e=(a+b)/2$, $j=(a+d)/2$, $t=(b+d)/2$ and $v=a/4+b/4+h/2$.

The five displayed vertices are uniquely exposed within their hull by $-x-y-z$, $z$, $2y-x$, $x+2y$, and $x$, respectively. Hence none can be the target of a nontrivial representation. The three midpoint representations join $a,b,d,e,j,t$ and the representation of $v$ joins $h,v$ to $a,b$. Each of the four nonvertex targets has $x=0$ or $y=0$. Since all these coordinates on $P$ are nonnegative and both coordinates of $f$ are positive, no such target admits $f$ as a positive source. Hence $f$ is isolated and the other eight points form a single component. The determinant of columns $b-a,d-a,h-a$ is $-8$, so the eight-point component spans dimension three. This gives the strict cost inequality.

The integer set of the product is $P\times\{0,1\}^{k-3}$. A convex representation at a fixed binary vertex in the last coordinates uses only that vertex in every positive source, since a weighted average of numbers in $[0,1]$ can be zero or one only when all its terms equal that endpoint. Conversely the old representations lift at any fixed cube vertex. Thus there are precisely the stated copies, and the rank cost multiplies by $2^{k-3}$.
