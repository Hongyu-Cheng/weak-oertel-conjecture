## Statement

Let \(d\geq 1\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume \(a>0\), and let \(0<m\leq a\). Define
\[
\mathsf M_m(K):=
\left\{
\frac1m\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=m
\right\}
\]
and, for \(t\geq0\),
\[
D_t(K):=
\left\{q\in K:
\inf_{H\ni q}|K\cap H|_d\geq t
\right\},
\]
where the infimum is over closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\). The convention as to whether the whole space is also called an improper closed halfspace does not affect the result. Then \(\mathsf M_m(K)\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(K\), and, with
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
one has
\[
D_m(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_m(K)\subseteq D_{\gamma_dm}(K).
\]
In particular, every \(q\in\mathsf M_m(K)\) satisfies
\[
|K\cap H|_d\geq\gamma_dm
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(q\), including halfspaces whose boundary contains \(q\).

## Proof

Let
\[
\mathcal F_m:=
\left\{f\in L^\infty(K):0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=m\right\}.
\]
This set is nonempty because the constant function \(m/a\) belongs to it. The closed unit ball of \(L^\infty(K)=(L^1(K))^*\) is compact in the weak-star topology by Banach-Alaoglu. Within that ball, the inequalities \(f\geq0\) and \(1-f\geq0\) are weak-star closed: they are respectively the intersections, over all nonnegative \(g\in L^1(K)\), of the closed conditions
\[
\int_Kfg\,dx\geq0,
\qquad
\int_K(1-f)g\,dx\geq0.
\]
The mass condition is also weak-star closed. Hence \(\mathcal F_m\) is weak-star compact and convex. Since \(K\) is compact, each coordinate function belongs to \(L^1(K)\), so
\[
T(f):=\frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx
\]
is weak-star continuous. Therefore \(\mathsf M_m(K)=T(\mathcal F_m)\) is nonempty, compact, and convex.

It is also contained in \(K\). Indeed, if \(T(f)\notin K\), strict separation of a point from the nonempty compact convex set \(K\) gives \(u\neq0\) such that
\[
\langle u,T(f)\rangle>
\max_{x\in K}\langle u,x\rangle.
\]
But \(f\,dx/m\) is a probability measure supported on \(K\), so
\[
\langle u,T(f)\rangle
=\frac1m\int_K\langle u,x\rangle f(x)\,dx
\leq\max_{x\in K}\langle u,x\rangle,
\]
a contradiction.

We next derive the support function by an exact bathtub argument. Fix \(u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), put \(\phi_u(x):=\langle u,x\rangle\), and define
\[
F_u(s):=\left|K\cap\{x:\phi_u(x)\geq s\}\right|_d.
\]
If \(s_j\to s\), the corresponding indicators converge pointwise outside the hyperplane \(\{x:\phi_u(x)=s\}\), which has \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Dominated convergence therefore shows that \(F_u\) is continuous. If
\[
\alpha_u:=\min_{x\in K}\phi_u(x),
\qquad
\beta_u:=\max_{x\in K}\phi_u(x),
\]
then \(F_u(\alpha_u)=a\), while \(F_u(\beta_u)=0\), because the exposed face at level \(\beta_u\) lies in a hyperplane. Thus, if \(m<a\), the intermediate value theorem gives \(s_u\in(\alpha_u,\beta_u)\) with \(F_u(s_u)=m\); if \(m=a\), take \(s_u=\alpha_u\). In both cases the closed top cap
\[
A_{u,m}:=K\cap\{x:\phi_u(x)\geq s_u\}
\]
has volume exactly \(m\). It is compact and convex, and its positive \(d\)-volume makes it a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Notice that when \(m=a\), this construction gives \(A_{u,a}=K\).

For every \(f\in\mathcal F_m\), equality of the two masses gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{A_{u,m}}\phi_u(x)\,dx-\int_K\phi_u(x)f(x)\,dx
&=\int_K(\phi_u(x)-s_u)
\bigl(\mathbf1_{A_{u,m}}(x)-f(x)\bigr)\,dx\\
&\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality is pointwise: on \(A_{u,m}\), both factors are nonnegative, while on its complement both are nonpositive. Since \(\mathbf1_{A_{u,m}}\in\mathcal F_m\), this comparison proves the exact support-function formula
\[
h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(u)
:=\max_{y\in\mathsf M_m(K)}\langle u,y\rangle
=\frac1m\int_{A_{u,m}}\langle u,x\rangle\,dx
=\langle u,c_{u,m}\rangle,
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
c_{u,m}:=\frac1m\int_{A_{u,m}}x\,dx
\]
is the centroid of the cap. For the remaining direction \(u=0\), the support function is simply \(h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(0)=0\). This proves the support formula in every direction relevant to separation, and (1) is the bathtub principle here rather than an invoked external result.

We prove the left inclusion. Let \(q\in D_m(K)\), and fix \(u\neq0\). The closed halfspace through \(q\)
\[
B_u:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle u,x\rangle\geq\langle u,q\rangle\}
\]
contains \(q\), so \(b_u:=|K\cap B_u|_d\geq m>0\). The function
\[
f_u:=\frac m{b_u}\mathbf1_{K\cap B_u}
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal F_m\), and every point of its support satisfies \(\langle u,x\rangle\geq\langle u,q\rangle\). Hence
\[
h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(u)
\geq\left\langle u,T(f_u)\right\rangle
\geq\langle u,q\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]
The same inequality is trivial for \(u=0\). If \(q\notin\mathsf M_m(K)\), strict separation of the point \(q\) from the nonempty compact convex set \(\mathsf M_m(K)\) would supply \(u\neq0\) with
\[
\langle u,q\rangle>
\max_{y\in\mathsf M_m(K)}\langle u,y\rangle
=h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(u),
\]
contradicting (2). Therefore \(D_m(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_m(K)\). This argument uses the closed halfspace \(B_u\) itself, so points on its boundary are included and no unproved boundary reduction is being assumed.

We prove the right inclusion. Let \(q\in\mathsf M_m(K)\), and let \(H\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(q\). Write
\[
H=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle u,x\rangle\geq r\}
\]
with \(u\neq0\). Since \(q\in H\), \(r\leq\langle u,q\rangle\). Use the exact top cap \(A_{u,m}\) constructed above. Its centroid \(c_{u,m}\) is itself in \(\mathsf M_m(K)\), because it is represented by the admissible density \(\mathbf1_{A_{u,m}}\). More importantly, (1) and \(q\in\mathsf M_m(K)\) give
\[
\langle u,c_{u,m}\rangle
=h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(u)
\geq\langle u,q\rangle
\geq r.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
A_{u,m}\cap
\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\geq\langle u,c_{u,m}\rangle\}
\subseteq K\cap H.
\tag{4}
\]
The set \(A_{u,m}\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body of volume \(m\), and \(c_{u,m}\) is its centroid. Grünbaum's centroid halfspace inequality, applied to the closed halfspace in (4), whose boundary contains that centroid, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|K\cap H|_d
&\geq
\left|A_{u,m}\cap
\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\geq\langle u,c_{u,m}\rangle\}
\right|_d\\
&\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d|A_{u,m}|_d
=\gamma_dm.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
This covers both the case in which \(q\) lies on the boundary of \(H\) and the case in which it lies in the interior, because only the non-strict inequalities in (3) were used. If the whole space is admitted as an improper closed halfspace, then
\[
|K\cap\mathbb R^d|_d=a\geq m\geq\gamma_dm,
\]
so it also satisfies (5). An empty improper halfspace cannot contain \(q\). Thus every allowed closed halfspace containing \(q\) has volume at least \(\gamma_dm\). Since \(\mathsf M_m(K)\subseteq K\), this proves \(\mathsf M_m(K)\subseteq D_{\gamma_dm}(K)\). The construction and argument include \(m=a\), when the exact cap is \(K\) and the conclusion is precisely Grünbaum's inequality at its centroid.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and its stronger centroid statement proved in Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. The exact result used in (5) is: if \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(g(L)\), then every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(g(L)\) satisfies
\[
|L\cap J|_d\geq\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d|L|_d.
\]
The primary proof states this as \(G_L\in\mathcal C(L,a_d)\) with \(a_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). Its hypotheses apply above because \(d\geq1\), the exact cap \(A_{u,m}\) is compact and convex, its volume is \(m>0\) and hence it is full-dimensional, \(c_{u,m}\) is its centroid by definition, and the halfspace used in (4) is closed and contains that centroid on its boundary.
