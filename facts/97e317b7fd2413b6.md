## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) constructed support of 604889294a17d7df and the rooted-tree construction of 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, set
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\quad(8\le\ell\le k),
\qquad Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\qquad N=n+1.
$$
Put \(a_0=0\), and put \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\).

The coordinate tree is \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\). Choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), with nonterminal set \(D_m\), injective successor \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), and \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\). For a Boolean source \(b\) on the tree coordinates, successively select the child \(v i_m(b[v])\) whenever its child block \(b[v]\) belongs to \(D_m\), stopping on failure or at depth \(Q\). Write its selected path as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and retain all original spatial points
$$
z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r},\qquad 0\le s\le h(b).
$$
Their union is \(A_{m,Q}\), and the level of a point is its number of coordinates equal to two. Retain the exact original support
$$
\widehat A=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
$$

Let a nonempty family of \(B\) full rooted paths of length \(Q\) be given, where \(B\le64(Q+1)^{11}\). Let \(P_j\) be its depth-\(j\) node sets, including \(P_0=\lbrace\varnothing\rbrace\), and define the coordinate sets
$$
S=\bigcup_{j=0}^{Q-1}\ \bigcup_{v\in P_j}\lbrace vi:1\le i\le m\rbrace,
\qquad
U=S\cup\lbrace u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}:|u|\le\min(7,Q)\rbrace,
\qquad n_U=|U|.
$$
Thus \(P_j\) consists of forest nodes, whereas \(S\) contains the whole child block of each forest node. These are exactly the forest objects of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3.

Let \(G:R_U\to(0,\infty)\) be arbitrary, where \(R_U=\lbrace z|_U:z\in A_{m,Q}\rbrace\). Let \(\lambda\) vanish on \(U\), and suppose
$$
P_\lambda:=\Pi(\lambda)=
\max_{v_0,\ldots,v_Q}
\sum_{j=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{v_ji}|\le1,
$$
where \(v_0=\varnothing\) and each \(v_{j+1}\) is a child of \(v_j\). In particular, \(\Pi\) counts all coefficients in the visited child blocks, as in 6edf2d6eb3514aa3.

For every real \(\zeta\le-\log2\), set
$$
w_{(z,t)}=G(z|_U)e^{\lambda\cdot z+\zeta t}\quad((z,t)\in\widehat A),
\qquad w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin\widehat A),
\qquad W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},
\qquad p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W.
$$
Define
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=\inf\left\lbrace p(H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I\right\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Then, with the absolute choice \(c_{\mathrm{down}}=2^{-200}\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{down}}8^{-N}.
$$
All original points, all original column heights, the full original normalizer and sampling law, and every containing closed halfspace in the original ambient space are retained. The constant is independent of all parameters, the chain partition, the forest, \(G\), \(\lambda\), and \(\zeta\).

## Proof

Write
$$
\eta=2^{-m}|D_m|=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2},\qquad \delta=1-\eta,
\qquad \varepsilon_h=-(\zeta+\log2)\ge0,
\qquad q_t=e^{\zeta t}=2^{-t}e^{-\varepsilon_h t}.
$$
The symbol \(\delta\) is used only for tree failure, not for the height deviation \(\varepsilon_h\). Let \(v_z=G(z|_U)e^{\lambda\cdot z}\), and introduce the new full column normalizers
$$
T_s(\zeta)=\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}q_t.
$$
At \(\zeta=-\log2\) these equal the quantities denoted \(T_s\) in 6edf2d6eb3514aa3. Since \(q_{t+1}/q_t\le1/2\),
$$
T_s(\zeta)\le2q_{a_s}.
\tag{1}
$$
Accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df give the unique Boolean source of each spatial point, the complete inverse-pair reconstruction, the exact original columns, and
$$
|\lbrace z:\operatorname{level}(z)=s\rbrace|=2^n\eta^s\le2^n,
\qquad 0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2},\qquad \eta^Q>\frac34.
\tag{2}
$$
Directly \(b_8=4\), \(b_9=7\). Consequently \(k=8\) gives \(Q=4\); otherwise \(Q\ge11\), and in the latter case \(a_7=9\).

For any nonempty spatial class \(E\), let \(\widehat E\) retain every original height of every point in \(E\). Let \(\nu_{\widehat E}\) be the restriction of the unnormalized weighted measure to this lift, let \(W_{\widehat E}>0\) be its full mass, and put \(p^{\widehat E}=\nu_{\widehat E}/W_{\widehat E}\). All its depths use all original ambient closed halfspaces. The exact atom and restriction inequalities are
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat E})=
W_{\widehat E}^{-4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat E}
w_{\mathbf I}h_{\nu_{\widehat E}}(\mathbf I)^3
\ge W_{\widehat E}^{-4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat E}w_{\mathbf I}^4,
\tag{3}
$$
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{\widehat E}\alpha_{\widehat E}^{4}M_3(p^{\widehat E}),
\qquad \alpha_{\widehat E}=W_{\widehat E}/W,
\tag{4}
$$
for every partition into nonempty lifted spatial classes. Indeed a containing halfspace contains the sample atom, and its full probability is at least \(\alpha_{\widehat E}\) times its conditional probability. Sampling on the same class contributes the fourth factor \(\alpha_{\widehat E}\). Hölder therefore gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge8^{-n}
\left(\sum_{\widehat E}\kappa_{\widehat E}^{-1/3}\right)^{-3}
\quad\text{if}\quad
M_3(p^{\widehat E})\ge8^{-n}\kappa_{\widehat E}>0.
\tag{5}
$$
Here one uses
\(1=\sum\alpha_{\widehat E}\le(\sum\kappa_{\widehat E}\alpha_{\widehat E}^4)^{1/4}(\sum\kappa_{\widehat E}^{-1/3})^{3/4}\).
Empty classes are always omitted before defining a conditional measure.

If \(E\) has one fixed level \(s\), has \(A_E\) points, and has spatial weight sum \(L_E=\sum_{z\in E}v_z\), its full mass is at most \(2q_{a_s}L_E\) by (1). Keeping only its actual entrance atoms in (3), and using \(\sum x_i^4\ge(\sum x_i)^4/A_E^3\), gives
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat E})\ge\frac1{16A_E^3}.
\tag{6}
$$
When \(Q<7\), partition into its at most seven levels and use (2), (5), and (6). This yields
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-n}}{16\cdot7^3}>2^{-10}8^{-N},
\tag{7}
$$
which includes \(k=8\). Assume henceforth \(Q\ge11\), so the first seven depths and the entrance \(a_7=9\) exist.

Use the spatial partition of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3. The seven early classes have levels \(0,\ldots,6\). GOOD consists of points of level \(s\ge7\) whose seventh selected node is outside \(P_7\). For \(7\le j\le Q\), STOP\(_j\) consists of level-\(j\) points with \(u_j\in P_j\). For \(7\le j<Q\), BRANCH\(_j\) consists of points of level at least \(j+1\) with \(u_j\in P_j\) and \(u_{j+1}\notin P_{j+1}\). STOP specifies the level of the point, not failure of the next source test. Ancestor closure of the forest makes these classes disjoint and exhaustive.

The following spatial facts are used from 6edf2d6eb3514aa3, with exactly its full coordinate sets \(S,U\). If \(\mathcal B_j\) is the set of level-\((j+1)\) anchors of BRANCH\(_j\), then
$$
|\mathrm{STOP}_j|\le2^nB\delta^j,
\qquad |\mathcal B_j|\le2^nB\delta^j.
\tag{8}
$$
To recall the count, the fraction of Boolean child blocks selecting a prescribed direction \(i\) is at most \(\delta\): each symmetric chain adds coordinate \(i\) at most once, and there are \(2^m\delta\) chains. The disjoint blocks on a prescribed length-\(j\) path therefore contribute a fraction at most \(\delta^j\); sum over at most \(B\) forest nodes. The additional exit test for an anchor costs a fraction at most one. Unique sources justify these counts.

The same source maps a branch point at any fixed later level injectively to its anchor. The first exit spike is retained in the anchor, even if it lies in \(S\). Every later spike has its parent outside the forest and has depth greater than seven. Hence it is outside \(U\). The point and anchor have the same full \(G\)-label, and their spatial field weight ratio is at most \(e^{2P_\lambda}\). With \(L_j^{\mathrm b}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal B_j}v_z\), (1) and monotonicity of \(q_t\) give
$$

\le2e^{2P_\lambda}(Q-j)q_{a_{j+1}}L_j^{\mathrm b}.
$$
Its actual entrance-height anchor atoms and (3), (8) give
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat{\mathrm{STOP}}_j})
\ge8^{-n}\frac1{16(B\delta^j)^3},
\qquad
M_3(p^{\widehat{\mathrm{BRANCH}}_j})
\ge8^{-n}\frac{e^{-8}}{16(B\delta^j)^3(Q-j)^4}.
\tag{9}
$$
The early classes have coefficient \(1/16\) by (6). These estimates already cover all downward height coefficients, without comparing weights at different labels.

In detail its \(s-7\) forward and \(Q-s\) inverse tests reveal exactly \(Q-7\) fresh child blocks outside \(U\). Their fair-bit success probability is \(\eta^{Q-7}\).
