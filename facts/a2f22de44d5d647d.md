## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and $0<a<1$. Let $H=(1-a)/a$, $r(z)=(H-z)/(H+1)$ and consider the cone $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, with planar density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$, and $C(s),M(s)$ the section integrals of $f,zf$, respectively, with respect to $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. Put $V_0=(H+1)/d$, $C_0=(1-a)^{d-1}$ and $p=(1-a)^d$. For $s>0$ define
$$
e_s=\frac1{2s}\int_{-s}^{\min(s,1)}(1-ab)^{-d}\,db,\qquad
m_s=\partial_a e_s,\qquad k_s=\frac{(s-1)_+}{2s},\qquad v_s=1-k_s.
$$
At $s=0$ set $(e_0,m_0,k_0)=(1,0,0)$. Thus $e_s=C(s)/C_0$ and $M(s)/C_0=-(1-a)m_s/d$. A centered law is a probability law with average section moment zero.

Suppose $0<a<1/d$ and let $s_*>1$ be the unique positive centered slope. Put $R=1+as_*$, $e_*=e_{s_*}$, $v_*=v_{s_*}$ and $t_*=p/R^d$. For a cutoff $T\ge s_*$, set
$$
t=\frac{p}{(1+aT)^d},\quad \kappa=\frac{v_*+t}{1+e_*},\quad B=1+t-\kappa,\quad
\eta=\frac{aR^{d+1}(\kappa/p-1)}{d(R-1)^2},\quad
L(s)=B-\kappa e_s-k_s+a\eta m_s.
$$
For $s\ge1$ and $q=1+as$, set $\Psi(q)=[(\kappa+d\eta)q-(d-1)\eta]q^{-d}$. Then
$$
2(q-1)L(s)=\frac{\Psi(q)-\Psi(R)-\Psi'(R)(q-R)}{d-1}.
$$
If $\kappa\le p$, then $L(s)\ge0$ for all finite $s\ge0$. If $\kappa\ge p$, the same conclusion holds provided
$$
B-\kappa-(\kappa-2\eta)_+[E(a)-1]\ge0,\qquad
E(z)=\frac{(1-z)^{1-d}-(1+z)^{1-d}}{2z(d-1)},\quad E(0)=1.
$$
In either proved case, $\mathbb E[\kappa e_s+k_s]\le B$ for all centered laws on $[0,T]$. In the proper frustum with $U=HT/(H+1+T)$ and volume $V=(1-t)V_0$ this is the upper corrected-cap bound $P_f-cC_f\le(1-\gamma)V$ on the averaged records of every centered frustum law on $[0,\infty]$, for $c=(V_0/C_0)(p-\kappa)$ and $\gamma=(\kappa-t)/(1-t)$.

For a fixed $d,a$ with $\kappa_1=(v_*+t_*)/(1+e_*)>p$, the only additional test needed by this sufficient condition over the whole cutoff range is its value at $\kappa=\kappa_1$, and only when $0<2\eta(\kappa_1)<\kappa_1$.

## Proof

Use the cone energy record fact 18e7bdf54588779d and the exact sign formula of the affine-cone centered slope lemma. For $s\ge1$, write $q=1+as$ and $F(q)=(dq-d+1)q^{-d}$. Then
$$
e_s=\frac{(1-a)^{1-d}-q^{1-d}}{2(d-1)(q-1)},\quad
k_s=\frac{q-1-a}{2(q-1)},\quad
m_s=\frac{F(q)-(1-da)(1-a)^{-d}}{2a(d-1)(q-1)}.
$$
The identity $B=\kappa e_*+1-v_*$ and the zero moment at $R$ give $L(s_*)=0$. Differentiating the displayed formulas in $q$ at $R$ and using $F(R)=(1-da)(1-a)^{-d}$ gives
$$
\frac{d}{dq}(\kappa e_s+k_s)\bigg|_R=\frac{a(1-\kappa/p)}{2(R-1)^2},\qquad
\frac{d}{dq}m_s\bigg|_R=-\frac{dR^{-d-1}}{2a}.
$$
The choice of $\eta$ makes the derivative of $L$ in $q$ zero there. Substituting the formulas for $e_s,k_s,m_s$ into $2(q-1)L$ yields $\Psi(q)/(d-1)$ plus a function affine in $q$. Its value and derivative vanish at $R$, so the affine part is exactly the negative of the tangent to $\Psi/(d-1)$ at $R$. Also $\Psi''(q)=d(d-1)q^{-d-2}[(\kappa+d\eta)q-(d+1)\eta]$.

Let $x=q/R$. The tangent remainder of $\Psi$ equals
$$
R^{1-d}D_{d-1}(x)
\left[\kappa+d\eta-\frac{(d-1)\eta}{R}\frac{D_d(x)}{D_{d-1}(x)}\right],
$$
where $D_r(x)=x^{-r}-1+r(x-1)$. Use the inverse-power tangent remainder ratio lemma, with the continuous interpretation at $x=1$. If $\eta\le0$, the bracket is nonincreasing in $q$, and its limit has the sign of $B-1/2$ because $D_{d-1}(q/R)\sim(d-1)q/R$ and $L(s)\to B-1/2$. The centered endpoint constants give $B\ge1/2$. Thus $L(s)\ge0$ for $s\ge1$. If $\eta\ge0$, the bracket is nondecreasing, so $L(1)\ge0$ implies the same conclusion. Here $1+a<R$, so the prefactor at $s=1$ is strictly positive.

For $0\le s\le1$, set $z=as$. Then $e_s=E(z)$ and $a m_s=zE'(z)$, so $L(s)=B-\kappa E(z)+\eta zE'(z)$. The binomial expansion of the even uniform average of $(1-zb)^{-d}$ on $-1\le b\le1$ gives $E(z)=1+\sum_{j\ge1}c_j z^{2j}$ with $c_j>0$. If $\eta\le0$, the residual is nonincreasing in $s$ and is bounded below by $L(1)\ge0$. If $\eta\ge0$, each nonconstant coefficient of $\kappa E(z)-\eta zE'(z)$ is $c_j(\kappa-2j\eta)\le c_j(\kappa-2\eta)_+$. Therefore this expression is at most $\kappa+(\kappa-2\eta)_+[E(a)-1]$, and the stated condition gives $L(s)\ge0$ on $[0,1]$, including the endpoint needed for $s\ge1$.

For the endpoint reduction extend $B$ by $B(\kappa)=\kappa e_*+1-v_*$. At $\kappa=p$, the endpoint identities give $B(p)=(1+t_*)/2\ge p$ and $B(p)\ge1/2$, so the $\eta=0$ branch just proved gives $L(1)\ge0$. At $\kappa_1$, the endpoint constants give $B(\kappa_1)\ge\kappa_1$. Affinity gives $B\ge\kappa$ on $[p,\kappa_1]$. On that interval the sufficient residual is piecewise affine, with a possible breakpoint at $\kappa=2\eta$. Its value is nonnegative at $p$ by $L(1)\ge0$, and at any breakpoint by $B\ge\kappa$. If $2\eta(\kappa_1)\ge\kappa_1$, it is also nonnegative there by $B\ge\kappa$. Otherwise its sign there is the single additional test. This argument applies even when the physical interval begins above $p$.

Averaging under a centered law cancels $m_s$. For $s\le T$, fact f4be2679c83c2fd1 and the cone cap record give $(P_f-cC_f)/V_0=\kappa e_s+k_s-t$. The upper target in these units is $(1-\gamma)(1-t)=1-\kappa=B-t$. The bounded representative from that same fact has a separately centered finite part. Its atom at infinity has corrected cap $V/2\le(1-\gamma)V$ by the centered endpoint constants, so averaging extends the upper bound.
