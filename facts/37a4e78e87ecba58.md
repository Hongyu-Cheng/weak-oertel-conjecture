## Statement

Let $A,B$ be positive-volume $d$-dimensional fibers of one actual convex body at integer bases $z_A,z_B$ with integer midpoint $z_0=(z_A+z_B)/2$, where $d\ge1$. Write $a=|A|_d$, $b=|B|_d$ and $B_*=\bigl((a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2\bigr)^d$. For every $t\ge0$ and $\varepsilon\in[0,1]$ there is a coupling of the two uniform probabilities whose mass-$t$ midpoint output lies in the actual $z_0$ fiber and has density at most
$$
t\left[\frac{1-\varepsilon}{B_*}+\frac{\varepsilon\,2^d}{\max(a,b)}\right].
$$
For every finite menu of ambient closed halfspaces $H_\ell$ with $\theta_\ell\ge0$ and $\sum_\ell\theta_\ell=1$, the same coupling has weighted captured source-minus-output mass at least
$$
\varepsilon t\sum_\ell\theta_\ell
\frac{|A\cap H_{\ell,z_A}|_d}{a}\frac{|B\cap H_{\ell,z_B}|_d}{b}.
$$
Here the two source masses are each $t$, the output mass is $t$, and $H_{\ell,z}$ denotes the section of $H_\ell$ at base $z$. The source cap masses use the whole original halfspace traces on each allocated marginal.

## Proof

Use 793210f8a4be3647, (1), at the two positive-volume bases with weights $1/2,1/2$ to take a coupling $\Pi_0$ with the prescribed marginals and midpoint density at most $1/B_*$. When the bases coincide, use the diagonal coupling of their common uniform law, which has this bound. Let $\Pi_1$ be the independent coupling. Its midpoint density at $x$ is $2^d|A\cap(2x-B)|_d/(ab)$ by convolution and scaling, bounded by $2^d/\max(a,b)$ since the overlap has volume at most $\min(a,b)$. Mix the joint laws as $(1-\varepsilon)\Pi_0+\varepsilon\Pi_1$. Both marginals stay uniform, and convexity of the original body places all realized midpoints in the actual target fiber. The asserted density bound follows by linearity and multiplication by $t$.

For any one closed halfspace $H$, the pointwise difference of the two endpoint indicators minus the midpoint indicator is nonnegative. Indeed a captured midpoint cannot have both endpoints outside, since the open complementary halfspace is convex. If both endpoints are captured, convexity of $H$ gives midpoint capture and the difference is one. Under $\Pi_1$ the probability of this latter event is the product of the two uniform cap fractions. Under $\Pi_0$ the difference remains nonnegative. Integrating, scaling by $t$, and summing with the fixed nonnegative $\theta_\ell$ proves the simultaneous lower bound.
