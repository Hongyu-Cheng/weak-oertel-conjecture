## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(J\in\mathbb Z^n\), and put \(Q:=\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, with locked notation \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\). For \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\), write \(C_{\mathbf j}:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf j,x)\in C\}\). Assume \(\lambda_d(C_{\mathbf j})=0\) whenever \(\mathbf j\notin J+Q\); these other fibers are allowed to be nonempty. In particular this assumption holds if the base projection of \(C\) lies in \(J+[-1,1]^n\).

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) be compact and full-dimensional, with \(v:=\lambda_d(K)>0\). Suppose uniform probability on \(K\) has centroid \(0\) and covariance matrix \(\Gamma\), and define its covariance radius by \(R_K:=\max_{u\in K}\|\Gamma^{-1/2}u\|_2\). The matrix \(\Gamma\) is positive-definite. Assume, explicitly, that a finite constant \(\kappa\ge1\) satisfies

\[
\frac1v\int_K(\beta\cdot u)^4\,du
\le\kappa(\beta^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\beta)^2
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

Let \(\epsilon\ge0\), and let \(f:Q\to\mathbb R^d\) be any function satisfying, for every \(z\in Q\),

\[
f(z)+K\subseteq C_{J+z}\subseteq f(z)+(1+\epsilon)K.
\]

No affinity, parity, or regularity of \(f\) is assumed. No convexity of \(K\) is needed for the depth conclusions below; they therefore apply in particular when \(K\) is a convex body.

Set \(V:=\mathcal H_d(S)\), the canonical total-mass notation of accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2; the total \(W\) in the assignment is the same quantity, \(W=V\). The restrictions of the original measures to \(f(z)+K\), at bases \(J+z\), have total mass \(M:=3^n v\) and satisfy

\[
0<M\le V\le(1+\epsilon)^dM,
\qquad \frac MV\ge(1+\epsilon)^{-d}.
\]

For every \(z,v'\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(z,z+v',z-v'\in Q\), put \(t:=(f(z+v')+f(z-v'))/2-f(z)\). Then \(t+K\subseteq(1+\epsilon)K\), \(t\in\epsilon\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K)\), and \(\|\Gamma^{-1/2}t\|_2\le\epsilon R_K\), where \(0\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K):=\{0\}\). If \(K\) is convex, the stronger inclusion \(t\in\epsilon K\) holds.

Let \(e_i\) be the standard basis vectors, set \(b_0:=f(0)\), and define \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) by \(Ae_i:=(f(e_i)-f(-e_i))/2\). This vector \(b_0\) is the vector denoted \(b\) in accepted fact 1b11e7e19d123883. With

\[
L:=\max_{z\in Q}\|\Gamma^{-1/2}(f(z)-Az-b_0)\|_2,
\qquad
\bar f:=3^{-n}\sum_{z\in Q}f(z),
\qquad
\mathbf y^\star:=(J,\bar f),
\]

one has \(L\le2(n-\tfrac12)\epsilon R_K\), \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
&\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(\kappa+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)}\\
&\ge\frac{W}{512(1+\epsilon)^d
 \bigl(\kappa+(n+1)^2[2(n-\tfrac12)\epsilon R_K]^4\bigr)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, fix any finite absolute constants \(a,b\ge0\) and \(\kappa_0\ge1\). Here \(a,b\) are scalar threshold constants, not halfspace normals; the normal components denoted \((a,b)\) in accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2 are denoted \((\alpha,\beta)\) below. If \(\epsilon d\le a\), \(\epsilon R_K\le b\), and \(\kappa\le\kappa_0\), then for every positive \(n,d\),

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c(a,b,\kappa_0)2^{-n}W,
\qquad
c(a,b,\kappa_0):=
\frac{e^{-a}}{256\kappa_0+8{,}352{,}100\,b^4}
\in(0,1/e).
\]

The kurtosis and the two bounds involving \(\epsilon\) are hypotheses, not universal estimates for uniform convex-body noise.

## Proof

The uniform law on \(K\) is the probability with density \(\mathbf 1_K/v\) with respect to \(\lambda_d\); let \(U\) have this law. It is compactly supported and absolutely continuous, has \(m_U:=\mathbb EU=0\), and has covariance \(\Gamma=v^{-1}\int_K uu^{\mathsf T}\,du\). For a nonzero \(\beta\), the identity \(\beta^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\beta=0\) would force \(\beta\cdot u=0\) almost everywhere on \(K\), contrary to \(v>0\), since a hyperplane has zero \(\lambda_d\)-measure. Thus \(\Gamma\) is positive-definite. Compactness makes \(R_K\) finite. The assumed directional fourth-moment inequality is exactly the hypothesis on \(U\) of accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2, with the same \(\Gamma\) and \(\kappa\).

For every integral base define the original measure \(\mu_{\mathbf j}(B):=\lambda_d(B\cap C_{\mathbf j})\) on Borel sets \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\). Define \(\rho(B):=\lambda_d(B\cap K)\); this is the finite density measure of mass \(v\) in accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2. Translation invariance of Lebesgue measure and the lower fiber inclusion give, simultaneously for every \(z\in Q\),

\[
(T_{f(z)})_\#\rho(B)
=\lambda_d(B\cap(f(z)+K))
\le\lambda_d(B\cap C_{J+z})=\mu_{J+z}(B).
\]

In fact this is exactly the restriction of \(\mu_{J+z}\) to the indicated stock set. Hence the retained measure on \(\Lambda\) is

\[
\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(E)
:=\sum_{z\in Q}\rho(E_{J+z}-f(z)),
\qquad
E_{\mathbf j}:=\{x:(\mathbf j,x)\in E\},
\]

and its mass is \(M=3^n v\). All summands are charged to their fixed, distinct original bases.

Each fiber with base \(J+z\) has volume between \(v\) and \((1+\epsilon)^d v\), by the two inclusions and the determinant of the scalar dilation. Fibers at other integral bases have zero volume by hypothesis. There are only finitely many nonempty integral fibers, since \(C\) is compact. Consequently the locked mixed-integer volume satisfies

\[
V=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}\lambda_d(C_{\mathbf j})
 =\sum_{z\in Q}\lambda_d(C_{J+z}),
\qquad
3^n v\le V\le3^n(1+\epsilon)^d v.
\]

Thus nonempty zero-volume fibers outside \(J+Q\) affect neither total mass nor any cap mass, and the asserted retained-fraction bound follows.

Next fix any legal reflected triple \(z,z+v',z-v'\in Q\). For each \(u\in K\), both \((J+z+v',f(z+v')+u)\) and \((J+z-v',f(z-v')+u)\) belong to \(C\). Their midpoint belongs to \(C\) by convexity, so

\[
\frac{f(z+v')+f(z-v')}{2}+u
\in C_{J+z}\subseteq f(z)+(1+\epsilon)K.
\]

Subtracting \(f(z)\) proves \(t+K\subseteq(1+\epsilon)K\). This argument applies to every legal triple, including triples centered on noncentral faces.

Write \(h_K(p):=\max_{u\in K}p\cdot u\) for the support function. Taking supports in the preceding inclusion yields, for every \(p\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
p\cdot t+h_K(p)\le(1+\epsilon)h_K(p),
\qquad p\cdot t\le\epsilon h_K(p).
\]

When \(\epsilon=0\), using \(p=t\) gives \(t=0\). When \(\epsilon>0\), these inequalities imply \(t/\epsilon\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K)\). To verify this implication, the closed convex hull is compact because it is closed and bounded. If \(t/\epsilon\) were outside it, take a nearest point \(p_0\) and set \(w:=t/\epsilon-p_0\ne0\). Comparing squared distances along segments from \(p_0\) shows \(w\cdot(u-p_0)\le0\) for every \(u\) in that hull. The support of the hull equals \(h_K\), so \(h_K(w)\le w\cdot p_0<w\cdot(t/\epsilon)\), a contradiction. For convex compact \(K\), its closed convex hull is \(K\) itself. For a general compact \(K\), the assertion \(t\in\epsilon K\) is not needed and is not inferred from support inequalities.

The norm estimate also follows directly from the same supports. If \(t\ne0\), set \(p:=\Gamma^{-1}t/\|\Gamma^{-1/2}t\|_2\). Then \(p\cdot t=\|\Gamma^{-1/2}t\|_2\), \(\|\Gamma^{1/2}p\|_2=1\), and Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(h_K(p)\le R_K\). Therefore \(\|\Gamma^{-1/2}t\|_2\le\epsilon R_K\). The estimate is immediate for \(t=0\). In particular the full midpoint defect satisfies

\[
\|f(z+v')+f(z-v')-2f(z)\|_\Gamma
\le2\epsilon R_K,
\qquad \|u\|_\Gamma:=\|\Gamma^{-1/2}u\|_2.
\]

Apply accepted fact 1b11e7e19d123883 with its \(Q,f,\Gamma\) equal to ours, its \(\eta=2\epsilon R_K\), and its vector \(b=b_0=f(0)\). Its matrix \(A\) is exactly the one defined in the statement, and its residual radius \(R=(n-\tfrac12)\eta\) is a bound for our \(L\), not the geometric radius \(R_K\). It gives
\(L\le(n-\tfrac12)\eta=2(n-\tfrac12)\epsilon R_K\).
For \(n=1\), this reads \(L\le\epsilon R_K\); indeed both endpoint residuals equal \((f(1)+f(-1)-2f(0))/2\). For \(\epsilon=0\), all defects vanish and this fact gives \(f(z)=Az+b_0\) throughout \(Q\), with \(L=0\).

Let \(Z\) be uniform on \(Q\), independent of the same \(U\) above, and put \(X:=f(Z)+U\). Normalizing the already constructed restrictions, not replacing or redistributing them, gives
\(\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}/M=\operatorname{Law}(J+Z,X)\).
Its expectation is \(\mathbf y^\star=(J,\bar f)\), because \(\mathbb EZ=0\) and \(\mathbb EU=0\). This law is carried by \(C\). For completeness its expectation belongs to \(C\): otherwise let \(p_0\in C\) minimize the distance to \(\mathbf y^\star\). Convexity and comparison along segments imply \((\mathbf y^\star-p_0)\cdot(x-p_0)\le0\) for every \(x\in C\). Taking expectation under the stock law would give \(\|\mathbf y^\star-p_0\|_2^2\le0\), a contradiction. The base \(J\) is integral, so \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\).

All hypotheses of the original-source conclusion of accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2 now hold: its \(\rho,v,J,f,A,b_0,L,\Gamma,\kappa\) are precisely ours, its normalized law \(\rho/v\) is our uniform \(U\), its \(m_U=0\), its barycenter is our \(\mathbf y^\star\), and its total mass \(V\) is our \(V=W\). Its normal variables \((a,b)\) are renamed \((\alpha,\beta)\). In particular its centered scalar noise denoted \(W\) internally is not the assignment's total mass \(W=V\).

To make the treatment of boundary normals and original capacities explicit, let any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) be represented, for a nonzero normal \((\alpha,\beta)\), by

\[
H=\{(\mathbf j,x):\alpha\cdot(\mathbf j-J)+\beta\cdot(x-\bar f)\ge s\},
\qquad s\le0.
\]

Accepted fact 2564a74c5939ade2 gives
\(\mathbb P(\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(X-\bar f)>0)\ge[512(\kappa+(n+1)^2L^4)]^{-1}\).
This strict positive event is contained in the stock event for \(H\). Thus the bound applies even when the boundary passes through \(\mathbf y^\star\), when either component of the normal is zero, and when a discrete boundary carries positive mass. A containing zero-normal halfspace, if admitted, is the whole space and causes no difficulty. The physical comparison is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&=\sum_{\mathbf j}\mu_{\mathbf j}(H_{\mathbf j})\\
&\ge\sum_{z\in Q}\lambda_d(H_{J+z}\cap(f(z)+K))\\
&=\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(H\cap\Lambda)\\
&\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(\kappa+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every term on the second line is a restriction of the original measure appearing on the first line. Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves the first depth inequality. Using \(M\ge V/(1+\epsilon)^d\) and the proved bound for \(L\) proves the stated inequality with \(W=V\). For \(\epsilon=0\), one has \(M=V\) and \(L=0\), so it gives \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge V/(512\kappa)\) without a limiting argument. For arbitrary nonlinear translations the same proof uses their actual mean \(\bar f\), not \(f(0)\), as the continuous coordinate of the source barycenter.

It remains to prove the claimed uniform calibration for all \(n\), including small \(n\). Under the threshold hypotheses, \((1+\epsilon)^d\le e^{\epsilon d}\le e^a\), and \(L\le(2n-1)b\). Hence

\[
\frac{h_S(\mathbf y^\star)}{V}
\ge\frac{e^{-a}}{512\bigl(\kappa_0+b^4(n+1)^2(2n-1)^4\bigr)}.
\]

Here is an exact bound on the exponential-to-polynomial ratio. Define \(P_n:=(n+1)^2(2n-1)^4\) for integers \(n\ge1\). The ratio

\[
\frac{P_{n+1}/2^{n+1}}{P_n/2^n}
=\frac12\left(\frac{n+2}{n+1}\right)^2
       \left(\frac{2n+1}{2n-1}\right)^4
\]

is strictly decreasing in \(n\), since each positive fraction on its right is strictly decreasing. The exact values
\(P_8=4{,}100{,}625\), \(P_9=8{,}352{,}100\), and \(P_{10}=15{,}768{,}841\)
satisfy \(P_9>2P_8=8{,}201{,}250\) and \(P_{10}<2P_9=16{,}704{,}200\). Thus \(P_n/2^n\) increases from \(n=1\) through \(n=9\) and decreases thereafter. This proves, for every \(n\ge1\),

\[
\frac{P_n}{2^n}\le\frac{P_9}{2^9}
=\frac{8{,}352{,}100}{512},
\qquad
\frac{\kappa_0+b^4P_n}{2^n}
\le\frac{\kappa_0}{2}+\frac{8{,}352{,}100}{512}b^4.
\]

In particular the exact universal lower bound needed here is

\[
\frac{2^n}{\kappa_0+b^4(n+1)^2(2n-1)^4}
\ge\frac{512}{256\kappa_0+8{,}352{,}100b^4}
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]

Combining this with the depth estimate yields
\(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge e^{-a}(256\kappa_0+8{,}352{,}100b^4)^{-1}2^{-n}V\), as claimed. The denominator is finite and at least \(256\), while \(e^{-a}>0\) and \(e^{-a}\le1\). Therefore \(0<c(a,b,\kappa_0)\le1/256<1/e\). All calibration estimates are exact and include \(n=1\), \(b=0\), and \(\epsilon=0\). No dimension-dependent noise estimate or near-homothetic representation beyond the stated assumptions was used.
