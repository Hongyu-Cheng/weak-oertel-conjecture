## Statement

Let $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body of area $a$. Write $u=|A\cap\{x\ge-1\}|$ and $v=|A\cap\{y\ge-1\}|$, with $u\le v$ and $0<v<a/2$. Put $t=-x-y$, $h=\max_A t>2$, $H=h+1$, $w=|A\cap\{t\ge-1\}|$, $e=a-w>0$, and $\Delta=\sqrt a-\sqrt e$. Then
$$
\sqrt{vH^2+8a^2}\ \ge\ H\sqrt e+3\Delta,
\qquad
(v-e)H^2-6\sqrt e\,\Delta H+8a^2-9\Delta^2\ \ge\ 0.
$$
The two inequalities are equivalent. In particular $v\le e$ and $8a^2<9\Delta^2$ cannot hold together.

## Proof

The level $-1$ is proper because there is positive area below it and $h>2$. Apply the common-slice quantile and width lemma to this same $A,x,y,t$. Its final upper-tail estimate, together with the area-zero level at $2$, bounds the lower-cap area there above by $v+8a^2/H^2$.

The two convex lower caps $A\cap\{t\le-1\}$ and $A\cap\{t\le h\}=A$ have positive areas $e$ and $a$. Their Minkowski interpolation with weight $3/H\in(0,1)$ on the latter is contained in $A\cap\{t\le2\}$. Planar Brunn–Minkowski therefore bounds the square root of this last area below by $\sqrt e+(3/H)\Delta$. Combine the two estimates and multiply by $H>0$ to obtain the first inequality. Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring and expanding gives exactly the second one. Since $\Delta>0$, when $v\le e$ and $8a^2<9\Delta^2$ the constant term of the quadratic is strictly negative and its other two terms are nonpositive.
