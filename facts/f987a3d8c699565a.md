## Statement

Use the canonical normals $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$ and $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. Let $A,K,B$ be planar convex bodies with $B\subseteq T$, $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, and $K=\{x:v_i\cdot x\ge-t_i\ \forall i\}$. Suppose every line $v_i\cdot x=-1$ meets $\operatorname{int}A$. With $m=\sum_i h_B(-v_i)/3$ and $f(m)=|T\cap(-mT)|/|T|$, the areas $k=|K|$ and $b=|B|$ satisfy
$$ k/b>(m+1)^2/[4f(m)]\ge3/2.$$
The ratio $3/2$ is the sharp infimum under these geometric hypotheses.

## Proof

Writing $\tau=\sum_i t_i$, the coordinates $X=x_1+t_1$ and $Y=x_2+t_2$ identify $K$ with $X,Y\ge0$, $X+Y\le\tau$. Full dimension gives $\tau>0$, all three facets are present, $h_K(-v_i)=t_i$, and $k=\tau^2/2$. Midpoint inclusion yields $2t_i\ge h_A(-v_i)+h_B(-v_i)>1+h_B(-v_i)$, with strictness from the proper cuts. Thus $2\tau>3+3m$. By the cyclic support-area lemma, $b\le(9/2)f(m)$, giving $k/b>(m+1)^2/[4f(m)]$. For $m\le1/2$ this bound is at least $9/4$. For $1/2\le m\le2$, $(m+1)^2-6f(m)=3(m-1)^2\ge0$.

For $e>0$ take $B=T\cap(-T)$, $A=(1+e)(-T)$, and $K=(1+e/2)(-T)$. Then $|B|=3$ by the envelope formula, and each line $v_i\cdot x=-1$ meets $\operatorname{int}A$ because the extrema there are $-(1+e)$ and $2(1+e)$. The containment $B\subseteq -T$ implies the midpoint inclusion by convexity. The ratio is $(3/2)(1+e/2)^2\to3/2$ as $e\downarrow0$.
