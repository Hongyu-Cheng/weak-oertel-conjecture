## Statement

For $n\ge1$ and $d=2$, take $S=\{0,1\}^n\times K_L$ where $K_L=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(L,0),(0,1)\}$ and $L\ge1$. Let $v=|K_L|_2$ and $M=2^n v$. At each binary anchor $p$, the largest mass of a submeasure of the mixed volume with base mean $p$ is $v$. Its continuous centroid body at mass $v/2$ is the ordinary half-mass metronoid of $K_L$, with inradius $r_L$ satisfying
$$
\frac{1-1/\sqrt2}{2+\sqrt2}\le r_L\le\frac12.
$$
For $N\ge 3\cdot 2^n$ and the sharp cap budget $\gamma_2 v$, the continuously unit-normal hinge minimum satisfies
$$
\frac{\Psi_{\min}}{M r_L}
=\frac{8}{81r_L}\left(L+1+\frac{L}{\sqrt{L^2+1}}\right)
\ge \frac{16L}{81}.
$$
Thus even after uniform continuous scaling to $r_L=1$ there is no dimension-only upper bound for $\Psi_{\min}/(M r_L)$.

## Proof

A nonnegative distribution on binary vertices with base mean $p$ is supported at $p$, since the mean of each coordinate equals that coordinate's extreme value. The full own fiber is an admissible submeasure of mass $v$. This also identifies the continuous centroid body at mass $v/2$ with the set of means of densities $0\le f\le1$ on $K_L$ of total mass $v/2$.

For each $y\in K_L$, the subset $(1-1/\sqrt2)y+K_L/\sqrt2$ lies in $K_L$ and has area $v/2$. Its centroid runs over a translate of $(1-1/\sqrt2)K_L$, giving the lower inradius bound since the triangle's inradius is $L/(L+1+\sqrt{L^2+1})\ge1/(2+\sqrt2)$. Every admissible mean belongs to $K_L$, whose vertical width is one, giving the upper bound. The three altitudes are $L/\sqrt{L^2+1},L,1$. The product hinge minimum gives the formula because $2\gamma_2/3^2=8/81$. Dilation by a factor $s>0$ multiplies the hinge minimum by $s^3$, the mass by $s^2$, and the moment-body inradius by $s$. The hinge scaling follows in both directions by carrying the covers through the dilation with unit-normal representatives.
