## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ be an integer and let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body with $0\in\operatorname{int}D$. Define $\ell(z)=z+1$ for $0\le z\le3$ and $r(z)=\min\{z+1,3\}$ there. The compact convex set
$$
C=\{(z,x):0\le z\le3,\ x\in r(z)D\}
$$
is the canonical hull of its positive integer fibers at $0,1,2,3$. The affine envelope $\ell(z)D$ equals the actual fiber throughout $[0,2]$, which contains the interior integer $1$, and every integer fiber has strictly less than half the original total mixed volume. Under $(z,x)\mapsto x/\ell(z)$, the original mixed volume has density
$$
f=(1+2^d+3^d)\mathbf 1_D+4^d\mathbf 1_{(3/4)D}.
$$
There is no positive continuous $g$ on $\operatorname{int}D$ with $f/2\le g\le f$ almost everywhere there. In particular there is no such bounded positive log-concave $g$, or such positive $g$ with a finite convex inverse power on $\operatorname{int}D$.

## Proof

Concavity of $r$, homothetic Minkowski addition, and nesting of positive multiples of $D$ prove convexity. Between consecutive integer bases $r$ is affine, so its fiber is the Minkowski interpolation of the two integer fibers. This gives equality with their canonical hull. The scales at the four bases are $1,2,3,3$, so the original masses are $|D|,2^d|D|,3^d|D|,3^d|D|$. Each is strictly less than half their sum.

After division by $\ell(z)$ the supports are $D,D,D,(3/4)D$. The change from pointed coordinates back to original coordinates has Jacobian $\ell(z)^d$ on each sampled fiber, giving the formula. Put $S=1+2^d+3^d$. At $d=2$, $4^d>S$. For larger $d$, division by $4^d$ makes each summand of $S/4^d$ strictly decrease. At any $\xi_0\in\partial((3/4)D)$, which lies in $\operatorname{int}D$, the open parts on both sides approach $\xi_0$. By continuity the almost-everywhere bounds hold throughout the respective open parts. Their limits would imply $(S+4^d)/2\le g(\xi_0)\le S$, a contradiction. A finite convex function is continuous on the interior of its domain. Apply this to $-\log g$ for a positive log-concave density and to the stated finite convex inverse power in the other case.
