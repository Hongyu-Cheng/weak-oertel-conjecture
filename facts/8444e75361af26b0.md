## Statement

Let \(q,r,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(n:=q+r\) and \(m:=n+d\), and write points of \(\mathbb R^m\) as \((\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^q\times\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\). Set \(\Lambda_q:=\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\) and \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d\). For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{\boldsymbol\eta}&:=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in A\},&
A_{\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t}&:=\{\mathbf x:(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in A\},\\
\mu_q(A)&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q}\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(A_{\mathbf j}),&
\mu_n(A)&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q}\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^r}
                 \operatorname{vol}_d(A_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here every volume is ordinary Euclidean Lebesgue measure in the displayed coordinates. Thus \(\mu_n(A)=\mathcal H_d(A\cap\Lambda)\), with the locked problem's fiberwise normalization; \(\mu_q\) is a different, explicitly specified measure.

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be a convex body and \(P\subseteq\{0_q\}\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\) a nonempty compact convex set. Denote projection onto the \(\mathbf t\) coordinates by \(\pi_r\). Suppose \(\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^r\subseteq\pi_r(P)\) for \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^r\) and \(R>0\). Put \(\alpha:=3/(2R)\), \(\beta:=1/(2R)\), \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(W:=\mu_n(C)\). For \(t>0\), use \(C+tP:=\{\mathbf c+t\mathbf v:\mathbf c\in C,\mathbf v\in P\}\) and \(C\ominus tP:=\{\mathbf v:\mathbf v+tP\subseteq C\}\), with either measure zero for an empty set. Then
\[
\mu_q(C\ominus\beta P)\le W\le\mu_q(C+\beta P).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be any convex body satisfying \(E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). For every prescribed \(\mathbf y\in E\cap\Lambda_q\), there exists \(\mathbf w\in P\), chosen independently of all halfspaces, such that, with \(D:=E+\alpha\mathbf w\) and \(\mathbf p:=\mathbf y+\alpha\mathbf w\),
\[
\mathbf p\in S,\qquad
\mu_q(D\cap H)\le\mu_n(C\cap H)
\quad\text{for every closed ambient halfspace }H.
\tag{2}
\]
The assertion also holds if the empty set or the whole ambient space is admitted as a degenerate halfspace. Define \(h_{q,E}(\mathbf y):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mu_q(E\cap H)\) and \(h_S(\mathbf p):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mu_n(C\cap H)\), with proper closed ambient halfspaces in both infima. Then the same point satisfies \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\). In particular, if \(c_q>0\) and
\[
h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\ge c_q2^{-q}\mu_q(E),
\tag{3}
\]
then, whenever \(\mu_q(C+\beta P)>0\),
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge c_q2^{-q}\mu_q(E)
\ge c_q2^{-q}\frac{\mu_q(E)}{\mu_q(C+\beta P)}\,W.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the ratio condition \(\mu_q(E)/\mu_q(C+\beta P)\ge2^{-r}\) gives \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge c_q2^{-n}W\). The positivity of the denominator is part of this ratio condition. If \(W=0\) and \(S\ne\varnothing\), the locked zero-mass conclusion is automatic for any point of \(S\), without using a ratio. Assertion (2) itself requires neither positive mass nor a centroid assumption on \(\mathbf y\).

For the genuine-summand specialization, assume in addition that \(C=K+P\) for a nonempty compact convex set \(K\), and \(R>3/2\). Put \(k:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(P)\) and \(E:=K+(1-\alpha)P\). Then \(r\le k\le r+d\), \(E\) is a convex body, and \(E+\alpha P=C\). If \(W>0\), then \(\mu_q(C)>0\), \(\mu_q(E)>0\), and
\[
\frac{\mu_q(E)}{\mu_q(C+\beta P)}
\ge\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently any \(\mathbf y\) satisfying (3) gives a single \(\mathbf p\in S\) with
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge
c_q2^{-q}\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^kW.
\tag{6}
\]
The profile factor in (5) is at least \(2^{-r}\) exactly when \(R\ge(3+2^{-r/k})/(2(1-2^{-r/k}))\). A simpler sufficient condition is \(R\ge7k/(2r)\).

The accepted constants supply (3) without an extra hypothesis on \(E\) when \(q=1\) or \(q=2\) and \(\mu_q(E)>0\). Namely, accepted fact 758103b1282d6b91 supplies
\(c_1:=e^{-1}-(1-e^{-1})\log(e/(e-1))\in(0,1/e)\), and accepted fact daef2580cdfec07b supplies \(c_2:=1/(64e)\in(0,1/e)\). Hence, for \(q\in\{1,2\}\), every genuine-summand instance above with \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(R\ge7k/(2r)\) has a point satisfying \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge c_q2^{-n}W\). In particular, for every \(n\ge3\), taking \(q=2\) and \(r=n-2\) gives the constant \(1/(64e)\) under the stated summand hypothesis and \(R\ge7k/(2(n-2))\).

All hypotheses may instead be checked after an integer unimodular change of the \(n\) integer coordinates, acting as the identity on \(\mathbb R^d\). In that case \(\mu_q\) means the partial mixed measure in the chosen coordinates. The full mixed-depth conclusion pulls back to the original coordinates.

## Proof

All mixed measures of compact sets here are finite, since only finitely many retained integer indices, and finitely many full integer indices, can meet a compact set. The erosion is compact and convex: it is the intersection of the closed convex sets \(C-t\mathbf v\), \(\mathbf v\in P\), and is contained in \(C-t\mathbf v_0\) for any fixed \(\mathbf v_0\in P\). Minkowski sums of the compact sets in use are compact. All sets and section-volume functions below are therefore measurable; the same conclusion for the explicitly constructed Borel sets follows from Tonelli's theorem.

We first prove (1), including all degenerate retained-coordinate sections. Write \(P':=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):(0_q,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in P\}\), identifying \(P\) isometrically with \(P'\subseteq\mathbb R^{r+d}\). Because every point of \(P\) has first \(q\) coordinates zero, for each \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q\) one has \((C+tP)_{\mathbf j}=C_{\mathbf j}+tP'\) and \((C\ominus tP)_{\mathbf j}=C_{\mathbf j}\ominus tP'\). These identities also hold for empty sections.

This is the retained-coordinate version of the shear construction in accepted fact a4e3dbc02619d36f. Its rounded dimension \(n\) is here \(r\), its continuous dimension \(d\) remains \(d\), its ambient dimension \(m\) on a section is \(r+d\), its body \(C\) becomes \(C_{\mathbf j}\), its buffer \(P\) becomes \(P'\), and its mixed measure becomes \(\sum_{\mathbf z}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\). Since a section need not be a convex body, we give the construction explicitly and do not assume that predecessor's body hypothesis for such a section.

Put \(Q:=[-1/2,1/2)^r\) and \(\overline Q:=[-1/2,1/2]^r\). For each \(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^r\), choose \(\mathbf b_{\boldsymbol\sigma}\in P'\) with \(\mathbf t\)-coordinate \(\mathbf a+R\boldsymbol\sigma\). Define
\[
\mathbf b(\boldsymbol\theta):=
\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^r}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{1+2\sigma_i\theta_i}{2}\right)
\mathbf b_{\boldsymbol\sigma},
\qquad \boldsymbol\theta\in\overline Q.
\tag{7}
\]
The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Their weighted sign average in coordinate \(i\) is \(2\theta_i\). Thus \(\mathbf b\) is continuous, takes values in \(P'\), and has \(\mathbf t\)-coordinate \(\mathbf a+2R\boldsymbol\theta\). Write \(\mathbf b_x\) for its \(\mathbb R^d\) component. Define coordinatewise \(N_i(\mathbf t):=\lfloor t_i+1/2\rfloor\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf t):=\mathbf t-\mathbf N(\mathbf t)\in Q\).

For a fixed \(\mathbf j\), let \(U_{\mathbf j}:=\bigcup_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^r}((\mathbf z+Q)\times C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\). Its base cells are disjoint and have volume one, so \(\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(U_{\mathbf j})=\sum_{\mathbf z}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\). On \(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\) consider
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_+(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)
&:=\bigl(\mathbf t+\beta\mathbf a,\,
\mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_x(\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf t))\bigr),\\
T_-(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)
&:=\bigl(\mathbf t+\beta\mathbf a,\,
\mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_x(-\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf t+\beta\mathbf a))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Each is a Borel bijection with Borel inverse: recover \(\mathbf t\) by subtracting \(\beta\mathbf a\), then subtract the indicated \(\mathbb R^d\) translation. For each fixed \(\mathbf t\) the continuous fiber is translated, and the base is translated by a constant. Fubini and translation invariance therefore show that each map preserves \((r+d)\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure of every Borel set.

If \((\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in U_{\mathbf j}\), write \(\mathbf t=\mathbf z+\boldsymbol\theta\) with \(\boldsymbol\theta\in Q\) and \(\mathbf x\in C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z}\). Since \(\beta(2R)=1\), equation (7) gives \(T_+(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)=(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)+\beta\mathbf b(\boldsymbol\theta)\in C_{\mathbf j}+\beta P'\). Hence \(\sum_{\mathbf z}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\le\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(C_{\mathbf j}+\beta P')\).

For the reverse comparison, suppose \((\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in C_{\mathbf j}\ominus\beta P'\), and write \(\mathbf t=\mathbf z-\beta\mathbf a+\boldsymbol\theta\) with \(\boldsymbol\theta\in Q\). The point \(-\boldsymbol\theta\) belongs to the closed cube \(\overline Q\), including all cell endpoints. Consequently \((\mathbf t,\mathbf x)+\beta\mathbf b(-\boldsymbol\theta)=(\mathbf z,\mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_x(-\boldsymbol\theta))\in C_{\mathbf j}\). This proves \(T_-(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in U_{\mathbf j}\), and measure preservation gives \(\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(C_{\mathbf j}\ominus\beta P')\le\sum_{\mathbf z}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\). Empty or lower-dimensional sections require no change in these inclusions or volume identities. Summing the two comparisons over \(\mathbf j\) proves (1). In particular, no assumption that \(P\) contains the origin or is symmetric was used: \(\mathbf b(-\boldsymbol\theta)\) is a selected point of \(P'\), not an assumed negative of \(\mathbf b(\boldsymbol\theta)\).

We now construct the single translate in (2). Write the prescribed point as \(\mathbf y=(\mathbf y_q,\mathbf y_r,\mathbf y_x)\), where \(\mathbf y_q\in\mathbb Z^q\). Put \(\rho:=\alpha^{-1}=2R/3\), and choose \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^r\) by coordinatewise nearest-integer rounding of \(\mathbf y_r+\alpha\mathbf a\), resolving ties by \(\lfloor t+1/2\rfloor\). Set \(\mathbf u:=(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf y_r)/\alpha\). Then \(\|\mathbf u-\mathbf a\|_\infty\le1/(2\alpha)=R/3\), so \(\mathbf u+\rho[-1,1]^r\subseteq\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^r\subseteq\pi_r(P)\).

For every \(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^r\), choose \(\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma}\in P\) with \(\pi_r(\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma})=\mathbf u+\rho\boldsymbol\sigma\). For \(\boldsymbol\xi\in[-1,1]^r\), define
\[
\mathbf s(\mathbf u+\rho\boldsymbol\xi):=
\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^r}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{1+\sigma_i\xi_i}{2}\right)
\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma}.
\tag{9}
\]
As in (7), this is a continuous multi-affine map into \(P\), and \(\pi_r(\mathbf s(\mathbf t))=\mathbf t\) on its closed domain. Its first \(q\) coordinates are zero. Set \(\mathbf w:=\mathbf s(\mathbf u)\), \(D:=E+\alpha\mathbf w\), and \(\mathbf p:=\mathbf y+\alpha\mathbf w\). Then \(\mathbf p\in D\subseteq E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). Its first \(q\) coordinates are \(\mathbf y_q\in\mathbb Z^q\), and its next \(r\) coordinates are \(\mathbf y_r+\alpha\mathbf u=\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^r\). Thus \(\mathbf p\in S\). No halfspace has entered these choices, and no property of \(\mathbf y\) beyond \(\mathbf y\in E\cap\Lambda_q\) has been used.

Fix now any proper closed ambient halfspace \(H=\{\mathbf v:L(\mathbf v)\ge b\}\), where \(L\) is a nonzero linear functional. Define \(f:=L\circ\mathbf s\) on \(\mathbf u+\rho[-1,1]^r\). Apply accepted fact 92e80d28d7127cde with its dimension \(n\) equal to the present \(r\), its scalar radius \(r\) equal to the present \(\rho=\alpha^{-1}\), its center \(\mathbf u\) unchanged, and its argument denoted here by \(\mathbf t\). Its Borel rounding map \(\mathcal R_f:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb Z^r\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t)_i&\in\{\lfloor t_i\rfloor,\lceil t_i\rceil\},\\
f\bigl(\mathbf u+\rho(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t)-\mathbf t)\bigr)&\ge f(\mathbf u),\\
\int_{\mathbb R^r}\phi(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t))\,d\mathbf t
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^r}\phi(\mathbf z)
\quad\text{for every }\phi:\mathbb Z^r\to[0,\infty].
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
These statements include ties and cell boundaries. Let \(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf t):=\mathbf u+\rho(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t)-\mathbf t)\). By the first line of (10), this point belongs to the closed domain of \(\mathbf s\).

For \(\mathbf v=(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in D\), define \(\Phi_H(\mathbf v):=\mathbf v+\alpha(\mathbf s(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf t))-\mathbf w)\). Writing \(\mathbf e:=\mathbf v-\alpha\mathbf w\in E\) shows \(\Phi_H(\mathbf v)=\mathbf e+\alpha\mathbf s(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf t))\in E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). This map keeps \(\boldsymbol\eta\) fixed. Its \(\mathbf t\)-coordinate is \(\mathbf t+\alpha(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf t)-\mathbf u)=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t)\), and
\(L(\Phi_H(\mathbf v))-L(\mathbf v)=\alpha(f(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf t))-f(\mathbf u))\ge0\).
It therefore maps each \((\mathbf j,\mathbf t)\)-fiber of \(D\cap H\), for \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q\), into the \((\mathbf j,\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t))\)-fiber of \(C\cap H\). Within that fiber the \(\mathbf x\)-coordinate is translated by a vector depending only on \(\mathbf t\), so \(d\)-dimensional volume is preserved.

For fixed \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q\), put \(g_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf t):=\operatorname{vol}_d((D\cap H)_{\mathbf j,\mathbf t})\) and \(h_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d((C\cap H)_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z})\). The fiber inclusion gives \(g_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf t)\le h_{\mathbf j}(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t))\) for every \(\mathbf t\). Tonelli and (10) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_q(D\cap H)
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q}\int_{\mathbb R^r}g_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf t)\,d\mathbf t\\
&\le\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q}\int_{\mathbb R^r}
       h_{\mathbf j}(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t))\,d\mathbf t\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^q}\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^r}
       h_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf z)
=\mu_n(C\cap H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
This argument needs no injectivity between different \(\mathbf t\)-fibers. The exact unit-volume basins in (10) supply the comparison. It also imposes no nonzero-coefficient condition on either the retained or the continuous coordinates of \(L\); \(f\) may be constant. For the whole ambient space, the same construction with \(f\equiv0\) and no cap restriction proves \(\mu_q(D)\le W\). The empty-set case is immediate. This proves (2) in the stated generality.

Translation by \(\alpha\mathbf w\) changes only the \(r+d\) continuous coordinates of the \(q\)-integer measure. It preserves \(\Lambda_q\) and every \((r+d)\)-dimensional retained-fiber volume, so \(\mu_q(D\cap H)=\mu_q(E\cap(H-\alpha\mathbf w))\). If \(H\) contains \(\mathbf p\), its translate \(H-\alpha\mathbf w\) is a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf y\). Equations (2) and this equality give \(\mu_n(C\cap H)\ge h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\) for every such \(H\). Taking the infimum proves the depth transfer. If (3) holds, its substitution and (1), after division by the stated positive denominator, prove (4). Zero partial-volume fibers, including fibers through the chosen point, create no exception to any inequality. If \(W=0\), nonnegativity proves the claimed zero-mass conclusion whenever \(S\ne\varnothing\).

Consider next the genuine decomposition \(C=K+P\) and \(R>3/2\). Put \(\lambda:=1-\alpha\in(0,1)\). The full \(r\)-dimensional projection of \(P\) gives \(k\ge r\), and its containment in \(\{0_q\}\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\) gives \(k\le r+d\). For any \(\mathbf k_0\in K\), convexity gives \(\lambda C+(1-\lambda)\mathbf k_0\subseteq K+\lambda P=E\). This inclusion proves that the compact convex set \(E\) has nonempty interior. Also \(\lambda P+\alpha P=(\lambda+\alpha)P=P\), so \(E+\alpha P=C\).

We compare partial mixed measures by applying the section-volume argument of accepted fact 95b9903fc7036094 inside each fixed retained fiber. In that argument, its \(K,P,C,m,k\) become respectively \(K_{\mathbf j},P',C_{\mathbf j},r+d,k\). We reproduce the scalar volume argument so that empty and lower-dimensional \(K_{\mathbf j}\) and \(C_{\mathbf j}\) are covered. Its global mixed-depth conclusion and its centroid coefficient are not used here.

Choose \(\mathbf p_0\in P'\), set \(P_0:=P'-\mathbf p_0\), and let \(\mathcal L:=\operatorname{span}(P'-P')\subseteq\mathbb R^{r+d}\), of dimension \(k\). For every \(\tau>0\),
\(\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(K_{\mathbf j}+\tau P')=\operatorname{vol}_{r+d}(K_{\mathbf j}+\tau P_0)\), since the two sets differ by the translation \(\tau\mathbf p_0\). These are translations within a fixed retained fiber; the retained integer indices and the measure \(\mu_q\) stay fixed.

For \(\boldsymbol\nu\in\mathcal L^\perp\), let \(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}:=\{\mathbf v\in\mathcal L:\boldsymbol\nu+\mathbf v\in K_{\mathbf j}\}\). The section of \(K_{\mathbf j}+\tau P_0\) above \(\boldsymbol\nu\) is \(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+\tau P_0\). If \(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}\) is nonempty, choose any \(\mathbf v_0\) in it. Convexity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+P_0)+(1-\tau)\mathbf v_0
&\subseteq A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+\tau P_0
&& (0<\tau\le1),\\
A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+\tau P_0
&\subseteq\tau(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+P_0)+(1-\tau)\mathbf v_0
&& (\tau\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
For the first inclusion, \(\tau A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+(1-\tau)\mathbf v_0\subseteq A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}\). For the second, write any \(\mathbf v+\tau\mathbf b\), with \(\mathbf v\in A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}\) and \(\mathbf b\in P_0\), as \(\tau(\mathbf v'+\mathbf b)+(1-\tau)\mathbf v_0\), where \(\mathbf v':=\tau^{-1}\mathbf v+(1-\tau^{-1})\mathbf v_0\in A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}\). Hence \(\operatorname{vol}_k(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+\tau P_0)\ge\tau^k\operatorname{vol}_k(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+P_0)\) for \(0<\tau\le1\), and \(\operatorname{vol}_k(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+\tau P_0)\le\tau^k\operatorname{vol}_k(A_{\mathbf j,\boldsymbol\nu}+P_0)\) for \(\tau\ge1\).

Empty sections give zero on both sides. No measurable selection of \(\mathbf v_0\) is needed, since (12) proves pointwise scalar inequalities for measurable section volumes. Integrating over \(\mathcal L^\perp\), using the translation identity, and then summing over \(\mathbf j\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_q(K+\tau P)&\ge\tau^k\mu_q(C)&& (0<\tau\le1),\\
\mu_q(K+\tau P)&\le\tau^k\mu_q(C)&& (\tau\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(C_{\mathbf j}=K_{\mathbf j}+P'\), since \(P\) has zero retained coordinates. If \(k=r+d\), the transverse space \(\mathcal L^\perp=\{0\}\) has zero-dimensional Lebesgue measure one, so the same proof applies. This establishes (13) even when some retained fibers have no \((r+d)\)-dimensional volume.

Before taking a ratio, use (1) and (13) at \(\tau=1+\beta\):
\[
W\le\mu_q(C+\beta P)=\mu_q(K+(1+\beta)P)
\le(1+\beta)^k\mu_q(C).
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore \(W>0\) implies \(\mu_q(C)>0\), and (13) at \(\tau=\lambda\) then gives \(\mu_q(E)\ge\lambda^k\mu_q(C)>0\). Equation (14) also makes the denominator in (5) positive. Applying (13) at \(\lambda\) and \(1+\beta\), and cancelling the positive \(\mu_q(C)\), proves
\[
\frac{\mu_q(E)}{\mu_q(C+\beta P)}
\ge\frac{\lambda^k\mu_q(C)}{(1+\beta)^k\mu_q(C)}
=\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k.
\]
Together with (4), this proves (5) and (6). The positivity argument uses the fixed retained fibers, so it does not infer positive partial mixed mass merely from positive ambient Lebesgue volume.

The positive function \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\), for \(R>3/2\), is increasing, with derivative \(8/(2R+1)^2\). Taking a positive \(k\)-th root shows that its \(k\)-th power is at least \(2^{-r}\) exactly when \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\ge2^{-r/k}\). Solving this inequality gives the exact radius condition in the statement.

For the simpler sufficient radius, set \(\theta:=r/k\in(0,1]\). Accepted fact 0790372855abe672, equation (29) of its proof, gives \((7-3\theta)/(7+\theta)\ge2^{-\theta}\) for every \(\theta\in[0,1]\). Only that scalar inequality is used; its original dimension ratio \(n/m\) is replaced by the present \(r/k\). If \(R\ge7/(2\theta)\), monotonicity therefore gives
\[
\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k
\ge\left(\frac{7-3\theta}{7+\theta}\right)^k
\ge2^{-\theta k}=2^{-r}.
\tag{15}
\]
This includes \(k=r\) and proves the sufficient condition \(R\ge7k/(2r)\).

It remains to justify the stated applications of the known small-integer-dimensional constants. Suppose first that \(W>0\). We have proved that \(E\) is a convex body and \(\mu_q(E)>0\). Regarding \((\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\) as a single continuous coordinate in \(\mathbb R^{r+d}\), it is exactly a body with \(q\) integer coordinates and \(r+d\) continuous coordinates.

For \(q=1\), apply accepted fact 758103b1282d6b91 with its body \(C=E\), its continuous dimension \(d\) set to our \(r+d\), its lattice \(\Lambda=\Lambda_q\), its mixed mass \(T=\mu_q(E)\), and its constant \(c_0=c_1\). Its universal consequence supplies \(\mathbf y\in E\cap\Lambda_q\) with \(h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\ge c_12^{-1}\mu_q(E)\), which is (3). For \(q=2\), apply the first assertion of accepted fact daef2580cdfec07b with its body \(C=E\), its continuous dimension \(d\) set to our \(r+d\), its mixed mass \(W=\mu_q(E)\), its mixed set \(S=E\cap\Lambda_q\), and its constant \(c_0=c_2=1/(64e)\). It supplies \(\mathbf y\in E\cap\Lambda_q\) with \(h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\ge c_22^{-2}\mu_q(E)\). All these identifications use \((r+d)\)-dimensional volume on precisely the retained fibers, so no change of measure is implicit.

For either value of \(q\), construct \(\mathbf p\) from this \(\mathbf y\) by (9). Equations (6) and (15) give \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge c_q2^{-(q+r)}W=c_q2^{-n}W\). The fixed-point quantifier follows from the construction before (10); although \(\mathcal R_f\) may vary with \(H\), \(\mathbf y,\mathbf w,D,\mathbf p\) do not. If \(W=0\) and \(S\ne\varnothing\), choose any \(\mathbf p\in S\) and use nonnegativity instead. This proves the applications, including \(q=2,r=n-2\) for every \(n\ge3\) under the displayed buffer assumptions.

Finally, if \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\), define \(F(\boldsymbol\zeta,\mathbf x):=(U\boldsymbol\zeta,\mathbf x)\) for \(\boldsymbol\zeta\in\mathbb R^n\), \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d\). This map is a bijection on \(\Lambda\), preserves the \(d\)-dimensional volume of every full integer fiber, and carries ambient proper closed halfspaces bijectively to ambient proper closed halfspaces. Consequently \(\mu_n(F(A))=\mu_n(A)\), and full mixed depth is preserved. The general buffer containment becomes \(F(E)+\alpha F(P)\subseteq F(C)\); a genuine decomposition becomes \(F(C)=F(K)+F(P)\). Apply the appropriate assertion in the transformed split, when its hypotheses hold, then pull its point back by \(F^{-1}\). Partial measure in this assertion is defined in the transformed split, while the final full mixed measure and depth are the original ones. This proves the coordinate-invariance claim without changing any ratio or lattice normalization used in the proof.
