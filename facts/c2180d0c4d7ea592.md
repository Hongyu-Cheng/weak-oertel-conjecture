## Statement

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and $P\subset E$ a nonempty finite set. For $z\in P$ let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body, where $d\ge1$, and write $w_z=|K_z|_d>0$ and $g_z$ for its volume centroid. Let $M=\sum_{z\in P}w_z$ and $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Give the graph $T=\{(z,g_z):z\in P\}$ the atomic weights $w_z$, and write $w(F)=\sum_{(z,g_z)\in T\cap F}w_z$. The measured family has raw cap mass $\mu(H)=\sum_{z\in P}|\{x\in K_z:(z,x)\in H\}|_d$.

Fix $0<\alpha\le M$ and $y=(p,g_p)\in T$ such that every closed ambient halfspace containing $y$ has atomic weight at least $\alpha$. Let $F$ be the minimal face of $\operatorname{conv}T$ containing $y$, allowing the whole hull. Then the raw halfspace depth $h_\mu(y)=\inf_{H\ni y}\mu(H)$ is at least $\gamma_d\alpha$. Equality implies $w(F)=\alpha$ and the existence of a single unit $u\in\mathbb R^d$ satisfying
$$
|K_z\cap\{x:u\cdot(x-g_z)\ge0\}|_d=\gamma_d w_z
\qquad\text{for every }(z,g_z)\in F\cap T.
$$
In particular, $w(F)>\alpha$ implies $h_\mu(y)>\gamma_d\alpha$.

If $g_z=G(z)$ for a single affine map $G:E\to\mathbb R^d$, these two conditions also imply equality and a through-$y$ closed halfspace attains it. In this case the atomic center assumption is equivalent to weighted closed base depth at least $\alpha$ at $p$, and $F$ projects to the minimal face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ containing $p$.

## Proof

Every centroid $g_z$ is in $\operatorname{int}K_z$. Indeed its average lies in $K_z$ by convexity, and at a boundary point a supporting linear functional would be strictly smaller than its value there on an open subset of $K_z$, contradicting the average. Let $L_z=K_z-g_z$. Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with $m=w_z$ applies to $g_z$ via the density $1$ on $K_z$, so every ordinary closed halfspace through $0$ cuts at least $\gamma_d w_z$ from $L_z$.

Moving a containing proper closed halfspace parallel to itself until its boundary passes through $y$ only decreases its mass. A through-$y$ halfspace with zero continuous coefficient keeps a whole fiber precisely when it retains its centroid mark, including when the fiber lies on its boundary. Its mass is therefore at least $\alpha$. The whole-space cap has mass $M$. Otherwise, divide both coefficients by the norm of the nonzero continuous coefficient to write the cap as $a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(x-g_p)\ge0$, with $\|u\|=1$. In the integration coordinates $\xi=x-g_z$ on each fiber the cutoff is $u\cdot\xi\ge t_z$, where $t_z=-a\cdot(z-p)-u\cdot(g_z-g_p)$. The centroid trace consists of the marks with $t_z\le0$ and has weight at least $\alpha$. Each such fiber contributes at least $\gamma_d w_z$, proving the lower bound.

Suppose $h_\mu(y)=\gamma_d\alpha$. Take an infimizing sequence of through-$y$ caps. Since $0<\gamma_d<1$ and $\alpha>0$, its late members all have nonzero continuous coefficient. Extract a subsequence along which $u_j\to u$ on the unit sphere, the trace index set $J=\{z:t_{j,z}\le0\}$ is constant, and each $t_{j,z}\to t_z$ in $[-\infty,\infty]$. This uses the compactness of the unit sphere, the finitely many traces on $P$ and the compactness of the finite product of extended real lines. For finite $t_z$ the volumes converge by dominated convergence, since the limiting ordinary hyperplane with unit normal $u$ is $d$-null. For $t_z=+\infty$ or $-\infty$ boundedness of $L_z$ makes the caps eventually empty or full, respectively.

For $z\in J$, the limit cutoff is nonpositive and the limiting volume is at least $\gamma_d w_z$. A finite negative cutoff adds positive volume to the through-$0$ cut because $L_z$ contains a ball about $0$. A cutoff of $-\infty$ gives full volume, which exceeds $\gamma_d w_z$. Since $w(J)=\sum_{z\in J}w_z\ge\alpha$, equality of the limiting total forces $w(J)=\alpha$, cutoff $t_z=0$ and ordinary cap volume $\gamma_d w_z$ for every $z\in J$, and zero limiting volume for every $z\notin J$. For such an excluded index, a finite limit giving zero volume must satisfy $t_z\ge\max_{\xi\in L_z}u\cdot\xi>0$. Indeed, a smaller cutoff has an interior point above it, obtained by moving a maximizer slightly toward $0$, and therefore cuts a positive-volume ball. The support value is positive since $0\in\operatorname{int}L_z$. A limit of $+\infty$ also forces $t_{j,z}$ to be eventually bounded below by a positive constant.

The point $y$ is in the relative interior of $F$. It is a convex combination of every point of $T\cap F$ with strictly positive coefficients. To see this, for each $v\in T\cap F$ choose $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that $(y-\varepsilon v)/(1-\varepsilon)\in F$, express this point as a convex combination of vertices of $F$, and average the resulting representations of $y$ over $v$. This also applies to a singleton face. In that combination the weighted sums of $z-p$ and $g_z-g_p$ both vanish, so the same coefficients give an exact zero weighted sum of the cutoffs $t_{j,z}$ for every $j$. Terms indexed by $J$ tend to zero, and each term with index outside $J$ is eventually bounded below by a positive constant before multiplication by its positive coefficient. Thus all points of $T\cap F$ have indices in $J$. If $F$ is proper, a closed exposing halfspace contains $y$ and meets $\operatorname{conv}T$ exactly in $F$, giving $w(F)\ge\alpha$. For the whole hull this follows from $w(F)=M$. Since all weights are positive and $w(J)=\alpha$, the indices of $T\cap F$ are exactly $J$. This proves both necessary conditions and the strict inequality if $w(F)>\alpha$.

For the affine case, the graph projection onto $\operatorname{conv}P$ is an affine isomorphism, and any halfspace containing $y$ restricts to either a containing base halfspace or the whole graph. Conversely every containing base halfspace extends to the ambient space with zero continuous coefficient. Let $F_p$ be the base face corresponding to $F$. If $F_p=\operatorname{conv}P$, the cap $u\cdot(x-G(z))\ge0$ passes through $y$ and has mass $\gamma_d w(F)$. If $F_p$ is proper, choose an affine functional $\ell$ that vanishes on $F_p$ and is strictly negative on $P\setminus F_p$. Use the cap $u\cdot(x-G(z))+R\ell(z)\ge0$. For a sufficiently large finite $R$, boundedness of each of the finitely many fibers over $P\setminus F_p$ makes its intersection with that cap empty. On $F_p$ the displayed ordinary cap equality applies. The resulting mass is $\gamma_d\alpha$.

When these fibers come from an actual mixed-integer convex body, take $P$ to be the positive-$d$-volume bases. Empty fibers and fibers of zero $d$-volume contribute zero to every cap even when a vertical boundary contains them. The graph and its faces use only the positive fibers, and the selected mark belongs to its actual fiber. If the total mixed volume is zero, every feasible point has raw depth zero and there is no positive-mass graph center to select.
