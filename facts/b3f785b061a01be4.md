## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\lbrace(2,-1),(-1,2),(-1,-1)\rbrace$ and use the actual product $[-1,1]\times T$ with its three integer fibers and original mixed area. For $h>0$, define the outward-smoothed capture at a mark $(p,y)$ with oblique cap direction $(a,u)$, $u\ne0$, by integrating
$$
\Psi\left(\frac{a(z-p)+u\cdot(x-y)}{h\Gamma(u)}\right),
\qquad \Psi(t)=\min(1,\max(0,1+t)),\quad
\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u},
$$
where $\Sigma$ is the covariance of uniform probability on $T$, equal to the pooled within-fiber covariance here. Include ordinary closed pure-base caps. For all sufficiently small positive $h$, the unique deepest mark for this smoothed depth is $(0,0)$. Although $(T+T)/2=T$ and the endpoint centroids average to zero, every coupling of the full uniform endpoint probabilities satisfies
$$
\mathbb E|(X_-+X_+)/2|^2\ge 1/216.
$$
In the same example the physical Jensen inequality $\Phi(0,0)\le[\int\Phi(-1,x)\,dU_T+\int\Phi(1,x)\,dU_T]/2$ holds for every finite convex $\Phi$ on the ambient physical space, so these inequalities do not characterize the existence of the prescribed deterministic coupling.

## Proof

The convex product has precisely the three stated fibers and $T$ has centroid zero and positive definite covariance. Normalize oblique directions to $\Gamma(u)=1$. This gives a compact ellipse, and at the centroid the own-fiber infimum $q_h$ is attained by continuity and is positive: every ordinary through-centroid cap has positive area and its capture is bounded above by the corresponding smoothed capture. For small $h$ the own ramp at the centroid is not identically one in any direction. Indeed $T$ contains a centered ball and the normalized covectors have Euclidean norm bounded below, so every such direction has a uniformly positive distance in its linear score from zero to the lower support value. Consequently the transition interval $(-h,0)$ at the centroid is interior to each projection support and has positive area in $T$. In particular $q_h<|T|$.

At either extreme base and any mark, an oblique direction minimizing the own capture at that mark exists by compactness. Choose a sufficiently large signed base tilt to miss both other fibers completely. Thus the smoothed depth there is at most the own maximum; we check this maximum is $q_h$. The linear cyclic vertex permutation preserves $T$, its volume and its covariance norm on pullback covectors. Its three powers average every vector to zero because they average the vertices to the centroid and hence do so on their linear span. The same holds for the dual orbit. For any nonzero normalized covector, its three cyclic images span the plane: otherwise their common invariant line has a real action of order dividing three, necessarily the identity, contrary to zero group average. The orbit of an own minimizing direction at the centroid consists of own minimizers. For any $y\ne0$ one of their projections on $y$ is positive, since the orbit sums to zero and spans the plane. Reusing this normal shifts the margin by a strictly negative constant. The centroid ramp strictly loses area under any positive threshold increment, since its strip has positive area and the capture is monotone thereafter. This shows the own depth is strictly less than $q_h$ away from zero.

At the middle centroid every oblique cap has own contribution at least $q_h$. One endpoint's base margin is nonnegative, so on its identical triangle the ramp contribution is at least that same own contribution. Every pure-base cap through zero has mass at least $2|T|$. Thus the middle depth there is at least $2q_h>q_h$. It is attained by an oblique cap: large $|a|$ saturates the two endpoints at one and zero, uniformly over normalized $u$ by boundedness, so the tilt can be clamped to a compact interval for minimization. A saturated cap with an own minimizing direction has mass $|T|+q_h<2|T|$, below the pure-base value. The cyclic orbit of any minimizing oblique cap at the middle centroid keeps the same tilt and consists of mixed minimizing caps. Its continuous directions span and sum to zero by the same argument. At any other middle mark $y$, a direction in that orbit has positive projection on $y$, and reusing the cap there strictly reduces the own contribution and cannot increase either endpoint contribution. This proves the claimed unique global deepest mark.

For any $x,x'\in T$ one has $x'_1\ge-1$, so $|(x+x')/2|^2\ge(x_1-1)_+^2/4$. Under uniform $T$, the first coordinate has density $(2/9)(2-t)$ on $[-1,2]$, by the lengths of its vertical sections and area $9/2$. Hence for every coupling the integral lower bound is $(1/4)\int_1^2(t-1)^2(2/9)(2-t)\,dt=1/216$. The full endpoint marginal measures cannot therefore have constant midpoint zero. The Jensen inequality in the statement follows by applying Jensen to the half-sum of the two lifted uniform laws, which has mean $(0,0)$ independently of any coupling.
