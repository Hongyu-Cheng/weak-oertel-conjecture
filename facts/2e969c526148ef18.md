## Statement

The tree depth \(Q\), the integer dimension \(n\), and the tree objects \(\mathcal T_{m,Q},A_{m,Q},P_{m,Q}\) retain their meanings from accepted fact `032423921efad6c8`. The supplied transcript's tree parameter \(q\) is the present \(Q\); its auxiliary parameter called \(Q\) is the present \(Q+1\). Boolean source vectors, called \(b\) in that predecessor, are written \(\beta\) here, leaving \(b\) for a scalar block size. The supplied transcript's continuous boxes \(K_r\) are written \(\mathcal B_r\).

For every integer \(k\ge1\), set
\[
b:=128(k+1)^3,\qquad B:=2^b,\qquad Q:=B^k-1,
\qquad m:=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad
n:=n_{m,Q}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{Q}m^\ell,\qquad d:=kb.
\]
Thus, under the preceding alias map, these are exactly the source parameters \(q=Q_{\mathrm{source}}-1\), \(Q_{\mathrm{source}}=B^k\), and \(m=2Q_{\mathrm{source}}^4\).

Here is the definition of the required tree base. Let \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}:=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{Q}[m]^\ell\) index the integer coordinates, with the empty word as root. For each node \(u\) of depth less than \(Q\), its children \(u1,\ldots,um\) constitute one coordinate block. Choose a partition of the Boolean lattice \(\{0,1\}^m\) into symmetric saturated chains, and let \(F_m:D_m\to\{0,1\}^m\) send each nonterminal chain element to its successor. Thus \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), where \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\). Given a Boolean vector \(\beta\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\), start at the root and, whenever its current child block belongs to \(D_m\), move to the child selected by \(i_m\) of that block. Stop at the first undefined successor or at depth \(Q\). If the resulting path is \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(\beta)}\), define
\[
z_s(\beta):=\beta+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}e_{u_\ell}
\quad(0\le s\le h(\beta)),\qquad
A_{m,Q}:=\{z_s(\beta):\beta\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},\ 0\le s\le h(\beta)\},
\qquad P_{m,Q}:=\operatorname{conv}(A_{m,Q}).
\]
The level of a point of \(A_{m,Q}\) is the number of its coordinates equal to two. Put \(\eta:=1-2^{-m}\binom{m}{m/2}\).

Index the continuous coordinates by \(y_{i,h}\), where \(1\le i\le k\) and \(1\le h\le b\), and define the actual body
\[
C_k:=\left\{(z,y):
\begin{array}{l}
z\in P_{m,Q},\quad 0\le y_{i,h}\le1\quad(1\le i\le k,\ 1\le h\le b),\\
z_u+2y_{i,h}\le3\quad
(1\le i\le k,\ 1\le h\le b,\ u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q},\ |u|\ge B^{i-1})
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
For \(0\le s\le Q\), let \(a(s):=|\{i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}:s\ge B^{i-1}\}|\). Define \(\mathcal B_r:=[0,1/2]^{rb}\times[0,1]^{(k-r)b}\) for \(0\le r\le k\), with the factors in increasing block order.

Use the actual fibers \(C_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C_k\}\), their volumes \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), the total \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), and the uniform probability measures \(\lambda_z:=w_z^{-1}\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{C_z}\) whenever \(w_z>0\). These have exactly the meanings of the corresponding objects in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; the present body is \(C=C_k\), and \(w\) is not rescaled. Put \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S:=C_k\cap\Lambda\), and let \(h_S\) be its actual mixed-integer halfspace depth from the locked problem.

For each \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), define \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and let \(\mathcal R_j\) contain exactly one representative of each unordered nonzero pair \(\{v,-v\}\) in \(\mathcal U_j\). For each \(v\in\mathcal R_j\), choose any probability coupling \(\pi_{j,v}\) whose marginals are exactly \(\lambda_{j+v}\) and \(\lambda_{j-v}\). With \(T(u,u'):=(u+u')/2\), put
\[
\kappa_j^\pi:=w_j\lambda_j+
\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}\alpha(v)T_\#\pi_{j,v}.
\]
The normalization agrees with \(\kappa_j\) in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` when the couplings are independent products. The central term is the actual central fiber measure, and each nonzero unordered pair is charged once. The present theorem permits arbitrary dependence between the two endpoints of each pair. For a finite Borel measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), write \(\operatorname{TD}_\nu(x):=\inf_{G\ni x}\nu(G)\), over proper closed halfspaces, as in that accepted fact, and \(\operatorname{HD}(\nu):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d}\operatorname{TD}_\nu(x)\).

Then \(C_k\) is a full-dimensional rational compact convex polytope, its integer base support is exactly \(A_{m,Q}\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^n\), and a lattice point of level \(s\) has fiber \(\mathcal B_{a(s)}\) and actual weight \(B^{-a(s)}\). Its normalized mixed volume and every uniform-marginal kernel obey
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W}{2^n}
&=1+\sum_{r=1}^{k}B^{-r}
\sum_{s=B^{r-1}}^{B^r-1}\eta^s,\\
1+\left(1-\frac1{Q+1}\right)k(1-B^{-1})
&<\frac{W}{2^n}\le1+k(1-B^{-1}),
\qquad \frac{W}{2^n}>1+\frac{k}{2},\\
\sup_{j:w_j>0}\ \sup_{\pi}\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j^\pi)
&\le1+\frac{k}{k+2}<2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular the ratio of the last supremum to \(2^{-n}W\) is less than \(2/(1+k/2)\) and tends to zero. Integer centers outside \(A_{m,Q}\) have no reflected positive fibers and can only give the zero kernel if it is extended to those centers.

Set \(j_\star:=\mathbf1_n\), \(x_\star:=\tfrac12\mathbf1_d\), and \(p_k:=(j_\star,x_\star)\in S\). At this center one has, for every choice of the uniform-marginal couplings,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\kappa_{j_\star}^\pi|&=1+k(1-B^{-1}),\\
\mathcal H_d\bigl((C_k\cap(2p_k-C_k))\cap\Lambda\bigr)
&=1+2k(1-B^{-1}),\\
h_S(p_k)&\ge\frac12+k(1-B^{-1})
\ge\frac12\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every distinct reflected pair at \(j_\star\), there also exists a finite coupling measure whose two marginals are dominated by the actual Lebesgue measures on its endpoint fibers and whose mass is the smaller fiber volume. Pooling its midpoint pushforward once per unordered pair, together with the central fiber measure, gives exactly
\[
\widetilde\kappa_{j_\star}
=\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{[0,1]^d}
+k(1-B^{-1})\delta_{x_\star},
\qquad
\operatorname{TD}_{\widetilde\kappa_{j_\star}}(x_\star)
=\frac12+k(1-B^{-1}).
\]
Here \(\delta_{x_\star}\) denotes unit point mass. These couplings use nonuniform submeasures on the larger endpoint fibers. Both they and the full-uniform kernels satisfy the pointwise mixed-depth lower-bound mechanism proved below; the difference is the endpoint marginal constraint.

## Proof

All tree parameters are positive integers, and \(m\) is even. Apply accepted fact `032423921efad6c8` with exactly the present \(m,Q\), so that its continuous interval-body example is not being used. Its Boolean-chain existence result justifies the chosen successor map. Its lattice-convexity and level census give \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^n=A_{m,Q}\), \(P_{m,Q}\subseteq[0,2]^n\), \([0,1]^n\subseteq P_{m,Q}\), and exactly \(2^n\eta^s\) points of each level \(s\), for \(0\le s\le Q\). Its complete reflected-pair reconstruction applies to every center \(j\in A_{m,Q}\): if \(j\) has level \(s\), every nonzero unordered reflected pair has endpoint levels \(s<t\), with at most one pair for each \(t\). At \(j_\star=\mathbf1_n\) there is exactly one for every \(t=1,\ldots,Q\). It also shows that there are no integer centers with positive reflected fibers outside \(A_{m,Q}\). These are the only geometric or counting claims taken from that predecessor.

The definition of \(C_k\) uses finitely many rational linear inequalities over the rational polytope \(P_{m,Q}\). It is closed, bounded, and convex. For \(z\in[0,1]^n\) and \(y\in[0,1]^d\), every additional inequality has left side at most three. Hence \([0,1]^n\times[0,1]^d\subseteq C_k\), proving full dimension. Moreover \(y=0\) is feasible above every \(z\in P_{m,Q}\), so its base projection is exactly \(P_{m,Q}\).

For a lattice point \(z\) of level \(s\), the coordinates equal to two form an initial path with depths \(1,\ldots,s\). A coordinate of value zero or one imposes no additional bound below \(y_{i,h}\le1\). A coordinate of value two forces \(y_{i,h}\le1/2\) exactly when its depth is at least \(B^{i-1}\). Such a path coordinate exists if and only if \(s\ge B^{i-1}\). Thus exactly the first \(a(s)\) continuous blocks are restricted, independently in every coordinate: \(C_z=\mathcal B_{a(s)}\). Its volume is \(2^{-ba(s)}=B^{-a(s)}\), which is positive. This also proves the exact integer base support and excludes any unspecified zero-volume boundary fiber.

Stage zero consists of level zero. For \(1\le r\le k\), stage \(r\) consists exactly of the integer levels in \(I_r:=\{B^{r-1},\ldots,B^r-1\}\), since the final level is \(Q=B^k-1\). Hence \(|I_r|=B^r-B^{r-1}\). Multiplying the accepted level counts by their actual volumes gives the asserted formula for \(W/2^n\).

For completeness, the mass estimate is elementary. For every integer \(N\ge1\),
\[
\frac{\binom{2N}{N}}{4^N}
=\prod_{h=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac1{2h}\right)
\le\exp\left(-\frac12\sum_{h=1}^{N}\frac1h\right)
\le(N+1)^{-1/2}.
\]
Here \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) and \(\sum_{h=1}^N1/h\ge\int_1^{N+1}dt/t\) prove the inequalities. With \(N=(Q+1)^4=m/2\), it follows that \(0<1-\eta<(Q+1)^{-2}\). Bernoulli's inequality then gives \(\eta^s\ge1-s(1-\eta)>1-(Q+1)^{-1}\) for every \(0\le s\le Q\). The strict lower bound for \(W/2^n\) follows on inserting this in every nonzero stage; the upper bound follows from \(\eta^s\le1\). In both bounds, the contribution obtained by replacing \(\eta^s\) by one in stage \(r\) is exactly \(B^{-r}|I_r|=1-B^{-1}\). Since \(B=2^{128(k+1)^3}>4\) and \(Q+1=B^k\ge B\), both factors \(1-B^{-1}\) and \(1-(Q+1)^{-1}\) exceed \(3/4\); their product exceeds \(9/16>1/2\). This proves \(W/2^n>1+k/2\).

Fix any positive-volume integer center \(j\), let \(s\) be its level, put \(r:=a(s)\), and set \(R:=k-r\). For a reflected pair with upper endpoint level \(t>s\), write \(\ell:=a(t)\). Its endpoint fibers are \(\mathcal B_r\) and \(\mathcal B_\ell\), and its coefficient is \(\alpha(v)=B^{-\ell}\). Partition the kernel into measures \(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_R\). Group zero consists of the central component and pairs with \(\ell=r\); group \(g\ge1\) consists of pairs with \(\ell=r+g\).

If \(r=0\), then \(s=0\), and group zero is just the central component, of mass one. If \(r\ge1\), at most \(B^r-1-s\) upper endpoint levels remain in the same stage. By the all-center uniqueness of a pair at each upper level,
\[
|\mu_0|\le B^{-r}+(B^r-1-s)B^{-r}
=(B^r-s)B^{-r}\le1.
\]
For \(1\le g\le R\), the same uniqueness and the exact stage size give \(|\mu_g|\le|I_{r+g}|B^{-(r+g)}=1-B^{-1}<1\). Thus every group has mass at most one, regardless of which pairs are present and regardless of their couplings. If \(R=0\), the full kernel has mass at most one and the required depth bound follows immediately.

Suppose \(R\ge1\). For \(1\le i\le R\), define the remaining-block averages \(T_i(y):=b^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^{b}y_{r+i,h}\) and the affine functions \(X_i(y):=8(T_i(y)-3/8)\). Set
\[
\Phi_0:=X_1,\qquad
\Phi_\ell:=X_{\ell+1}-X_\ell\quad(1\le\ell<R),
\qquad \Phi_R:=1-X_R.
\]
They satisfy \(\sum_{\ell=0}^R\Phi_\ell=1\) identically, and every \(\Phi_\ell\) is nonconstant. In the central component and every same-stage midpoint component, all remaining coordinates have mean \(1/2\), so their mean \(X_i\) values are all one. In a midpoint component of group \(g\ge1\), an endpoint uniform in \(\mathcal B_r\) has mean \(1/2\) in every remaining coordinate. An endpoint uniform in \(\mathcal B_{r+g}\) has mean \(1/4\) in the first \(g\) remaining blocks and mean \(1/2\) in the others. Thus the midpoint means are \(3/8\) and \(1/2\), respectively, and its mean \(X_i\) values are zero for \(i\le g\) and one for \(i>g\). Affinity uses only these marginals. It follows in every probability component of group \(g\), including the central one, that \(\mathbb E\Phi_\ell=\mathbf1_{\{\ell=g\}}\).

We next prove the required concentration bound without any endpoint-independence assumption. If a real random variable \(Z\) has range length at most \(c\), then its centered log moment-generating function \(\psi(t):=\log\mathbb E e^{t(Z-\mathbb EZ)}\) satisfies \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\) and \(\psi''(t)\le c^2/4\). Indeed \(\psi''(t)\) is a variance under exponential tilting; the support remains in the same interval, and the squared distance to that interval's midpoint is at most \(c^2/4\). A variance is no larger than that second moment. Integrating twice gives \(\psi(t)\le t^2c^2/8\) for \(t\ge0\).

For an endpoint \(U\) uniform in any one of \(\mathcal B_0,\ldots,\mathcal B_k\), its coordinates are independent. Each fixed \(\Phi_\ell(U)\) is a constant plus at most \(2b\) such coordinates with coefficients of magnitude \(8/b\). Each coordinate has range length at most one, so the sum of squared summand-range lengths is at most \(2b(8/b)^2=128/b\). Independence, the preceding log moment-generating-function bound, and exponential Markov give, for every \(\tau>0\),
\[
\Pr\{\Phi_\ell(U)-\mathbb E\Phi_\ell(U)\ge\tau\}
\le\inf_{t\ge0}\exp(-t\tau+16t^2/b)
=\exp(-b\tau^2/64).
\]
The same argument applies separately to either endpoint marginal, without conditioning on the other endpoint. For any joint coupling \((U,V)\) of the prescribed marginals, affinity gives
\[
\Phi_\ell((U+V)/2)-\mathbb E\Phi_\ell((U+V)/2)
=\tfrac12\bigl(\Phi_\ell(U)-\mathbb E\Phi_\ell(U)
+\Phi_\ell(V)-\mathbb E\Phi_\ell(V)\bigr).
\]
If this average is at least \(\tau\), at least one endpoint deviation is at least \(\tau\). A union bound therefore gives the upper-tail bound \(2\exp(-b\tau^2/64)\) for every midpoint component under arbitrary endpoint correlation. The central component satisfies the same bound, with an unnecessary factor two.

Take \(\tau=1/(R+1)\). Since \(R\le k\), one has \(b/[64(R+1)^2]\ge2(k+1)\). Also \(e^{2(k+1)}\ge1+2(k+1)+2(k+1)^2>2(k+2)\), so \(\varepsilon:=2e^{-2(k+1)}<1/(k+2)\). Every probability component of group \(g\ne\ell\) has \(\mathbb E\Phi_\ell=0\); hence
\[
\mu_g\{y:\Phi_\ell(y)\ge1/(R+1)\}
\le\varepsilon|\mu_g|\le\varepsilon
\quad(g\ne\ell).
\]
This follows by summing the component bounds with their nonnegative coefficients, so it also covers a group of zero mass.

For any \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), the identity summing the scores supplies an index \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,R\}\) with \(\Phi_\ell(x)\ge1/(R+1)\). The set \(G_x:=\{y:\Phi_\ell(y)\ge\Phi_\ell(x)\}\) is a proper closed halfspace through \(x\), because the score is nonconstant. The group with index \(\ell\) contributes at most one; all other groups contribute at most \(\varepsilon\) each. Consequently \(\kappa_j^\pi(G_x)\le1+R\varepsilon<1+R/(k+2)\le1+k/(k+2)<2\). Together with the \(R=0\) case this supplies the stated uniform bound after taking the supremum over all points, all couplings, and all positive-volume integer centers. All these quantifiers are covered by the same constant. The normalized ratio bound now follows from the already proved lower bound on \(W/2^n\).

At \(j_\star=\mathbf1_n\), the central fiber is \(\mathcal B_0=[0,1]^d\), and the accepted tree reconstruction gives one unordered pair with endpoint levels zero and \(t\), for each \(1\le t\le Q\). At a fixed center distinct reflected pairs cannot share an endpoint, since an endpoint uniquely determines its partner by reflection. Therefore the sum of their coefficients is exactly
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{Q}B^{-a(t)}
=\sum_{r=1}^{k}|I_r|B^{-r}=k(1-B^{-1}).
\]
Each midpoint probability has mass one. Adding the central component proves \(|\kappa_{j_\star}^\pi|=1+k(1-B^{-1})\), independently of the couplings.

Reflection about \(x_\star\) maps \(\mathcal B_{a(t)}\) to \(\mathbf1_d-\mathcal B_{a(t)}\subseteq\mathcal B_0\), and reflection maps \(\mathcal B_0\) to itself. Thus the mixed-integer intersection \((C_k\cap(2p_k-C_k))\cap\Lambda\) has central fiber \(\mathcal B_0\). At the level-zero endpoint of the pair indexed by \(t\), its fiber is exactly \(\mathbf1_d-\mathcal B_{a(t)}\); at the level-\(t\) endpoint it is exactly \(\mathcal B_{a(t)}\). The complete reconstruction excludes all other integer base fibers in the intersection. Hence the central fiber contributes one, and each unordered pair contributes twice its smaller volume. This proves the exact symmetric-overlap mass \(1+2k(1-B^{-1})\).

Reflection about \(p_k\) preserves \(\Lambda\), since \(2j_\star\) is integral, and preserves its mixed volume. The restriction of mixed volume to that intersection is therefore a centrally symmetric finite measure. For any closed halfspace containing \(p_k\), the halfspace and its reflection cover the whole ambient space, and their measures are equal. Each consequently has at least half the total symmetric-overlap mass, even if the boundary has positive mass. Since this measure is a submeasure of mixed volume on \(S\), it follows that \(h_S(p_k)\ge1/2+k(1-B^{-1})\). The upper bound \(W/2^n\le1+k(1-B^{-1})\) implies the asserted lower bound \(h_S(p_k)\ge2^{-n}W/2\).

The nonuniform construction realizes the same bound as a midpoint certificate. Label the pair indexed by \(t\) with its level-zero endpoint first and its level-\(t\) endpoint second; transpose the coupling below if the chosen representative orientation is opposite. Define a finite measure on their continuous-coordinate product by
\[
\gamma_t:=(y\mapsto(\mathbf1_d-y,y))_\#
\bigl(\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{\mathcal B_{a(t)}}\bigr).
\]
Its mass is \(B^{-a(t)}\). Its second marginal is the entire actual measure \(\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{\mathcal B_{a(t)}}\). Its first marginal is \(\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{\mathbf1_d-\mathcal B_{a(t)}}\), by the unit absolute determinant of reflection, and is dominated by \(\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{\mathcal B_0}\). Every midpoint equals \(x_\star\), so \(T_\#\gamma_t=B^{-a(t)}\delta_{x_\star}\). Summing once over unordered pairs gives the stated \(\widetilde\kappa_{j_\star}\).

Every proper closed halfspace containing \(x_\star\) captures at least half the Lebesgue mass of \([0,1]^d\) and the entire atom. A proper halfspace through \(x_\star\) captures exactly half this cube's Lebesgue mass: reflection exchanges its two sides, and its bounding hyperplane has \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Such a halfspace exists because \(d\ge1\). Thus the depth of \(\widetilde\kappa_{j_\star}\) at \(x_\star\) is exactly \(1/2+k(1-B^{-1})\).

To verify the capacity normalization and its actual-depth implication directly, fix any center \(j\) and let \(\gamma_v\) be finite measures on \(C_{j+v}\times C_{j-v}\), one for each \(v\in\mathcal R_j\), whose first and second marginals are dominated by the corresponding actual fiber Lebesgue measures. Put \(\widehat\kappa_j:=\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{C_j}+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}T_\#\gamma_v\). For \(x\in C_j\), write an ambient proper halfspace through \((j,x)\) as \(H=\{(z,y):a_H^\top(z-j)+b_H^\top(y-x)\ge0\}\), with \((a_H,b_H)\ne(0,0)\). If \(b_H\ne0\), put \(G:=\{y:b_H^\top(y-x)\ge0\}\); if \(b_H=0\), put \(G:=\mathbb R^d\). Whenever \(T(u,u')\in G\), at least one of \((j+v,u)\) and \((j-v,u')\) lies in \(H\), because the sum of their defining affine values is \(2b_H^\top(T(u,u')-x)\ge0\). For \(b_H=0\) that sum is zero, so the assertion still holds. Integrating this indicator inequality and using marginal domination bounds \((T_\#\gamma_v)(G)\) by the total actual halfspace mass of its two endpoint fibers. The central fiber contributes exactly \(\operatorname{Leb}_d(C_j\cap G)\). The central fiber and all endpoint fibers are distinct, so their costs do not overlap. Summing gives \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\widehat\kappa_j(G)\ge\operatorname{TD}_{\widehat\kappa_j}(x)\), also when \(G\) is the whole space. Any halfspace containing \((j,x)\) contains a parallel halfspace through it; hence monotonicity proves \(h_S((j,x))\ge\operatorname{TD}_{\widehat\kappa_j}(x)\). If the whole ambient space is allowed as a halfspace, its inequality follows as well, because every coupling's mass is at most either endpoint fiber volume and all the endpoint fibers are distinct, giving \(|\widehat\kappa_j|\le W\).

For the full-uniform kernel, take \(\gamma_v=\alpha(v)\pi_{j,v}\). Its endpoint marginal at \(j\pm v\) is exactly \((\alpha(v)/w_{j\pm v})\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{C_{j\pm v}}\), which is dominated by the actual measure because \(\alpha(v)\le w_{j\pm v}\). Thus arbitrary full-uniform coupling is admissible for this same pointwise lift; no independence theorem is being invoked beyond the normalization recorded in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`.

For \(\gamma_t\) at \(j_\star\), the larger fiber has volume one and the smaller has volume \(\alpha=B^{-a(t)}<1\). A scaled full-uniform coupling would have larger-endpoint marginal \(\alpha\operatorname{Leb}_d|_{\mathcal B_0}\), of density \(\alpha\) on all of \(\mathcal B_0\). The actual larger-endpoint marginal of \(\gamma_t\) has density one on \(\mathbf1_d-\mathcal B_{a(t)}\) and zero on its complement in \(\mathcal B_0\). Both have mass \(\alpha\) and satisfy the actual capacity bound, but they are different measures. The smaller-endpoint marginal of both constructions is the entire Lebesgue measure on the smaller box. There is no additional factor two in their midpoint kernels: one finite coupling is assigned to each unordered pair. The factor two appears only in the mass of the symmetric overlap, which contains both endpoint fibers. This establishes the claimed distinction between full uniform marginals and nonuniform capacity trimming and completes the counterexample verification.
