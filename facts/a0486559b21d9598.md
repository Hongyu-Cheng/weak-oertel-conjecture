## Statement

Use the logarithmic gap, original source capacities, and containing-closed-halfspace conventions of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, for a finite positive support \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), original weights \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\), and \(\mathbf J\in D\), a barycentric support is a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) with positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). Its gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). For \(L\ge0\), let \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) be the minimum original weight of a set meeting every such support with \(G_\lambda\le L\). The accepted fact proves equivalence with the definition using affinely independent supports. Both definitions will also be checked directly for this family.

For each integer \(k\ge1\), put \(A=k\log2\), \(N=2^{2k}\), \(r=2N\), and \(n=r+N\). Write a point of \(\mathbb R^n\) as \((x,y)\in\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^N\). Let \(\mathbf e_i\) and \(\mathbf f_j\) be the coordinate unit vectors of those two spaces, and let \(\mathbf1_r=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^r\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{\mathbf0\}
\cup\{(\mathbf e_i,\mathbf0):1\le i\le r\}
\cup\{(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}
\cup\{(\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf f_j):1\le j\le N\},\\
P&=\operatorname{conv}D,\qquad
\phi_{(x,y)}=A\sum_{j=1}^N|y_j|\quad((x,y)\in D),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=
\begin{cases}
e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}},&\mathbf I\in D,\\
0,&\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus D.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), every site of this complete support has positive weight, \(P\) has affine rank \(n\), and \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\). The potential is convex-admissible, and the whole table has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with every positive and zero lattice value. Its original maximum weight is one, so its displayed potential is exactly the normalized potential \(\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\) in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`.

Let \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), and use the original law \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). Its original mean and energy are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf J&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0,\qquad
W=r+2+2N2^{-k},\\
g&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}
=\frac{2N2^{-k}A}{r+2+2N2^{-k}},\qquad
0<g<A2^{-k}<1.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(g\to0\) as \(k\to\infty\). Define the original marginal depth by
\(\tau_{\mathbf0}=\min_{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D:\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf I\ge0}w_{\mathbf I}\). This also equals the minimum mass over all closed halfspaces containing the origin. The exact original depth and hitting profile are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\mathbf0}&=2+N2^{-k}=2+2^k,\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
&=\begin{cases}
2,&0\le L<A,\\
2+N2^{-k},&L\ge A.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

The set \(F=\{\mathbf0,(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}\) has original mass two. On \(D\setminus F\), the minimum mean potential of any barycentric law at the origin is exactly \(A\), and such a law exists. Consequently, for every \(C,c>0\), some finite member of this family satisfies
\(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,C(1+g);w)<c\tau_{\mathbf J}\), with \(\mathbf J\) the original integer mean. Every displayed halfspace and cutoff uses the closed convention and includes its equality atoms.

## Proof

First determine the complete support. Let \(R=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r,-\mathbf1_r\}\subseteq\mathbb R^r\). For any \(x\in R\), write its nonnegative barycentric coefficients as \(\lambda_0\) at \(-\mathbf1_r\) and \(\lambda_i\) at \(\mathbf e_i\). They satisfy \(x_i=\lambda_i-\lambda_0\), \(\sum_{i=0}^r\lambda_i=1\), and hence
\(\lambda_0=(1-\sum_i x_i)/(r+1)\) and \(\lambda_i=x_i+\lambda_0\).
If \(x\) is integral and some coordinate is negative, then \(x_i\le-1\) and \(\lambda_i\ge0\) force \(\lambda_0\ge1\). Therefore \(\lambda_0=1\), all other coefficients vanish, and \(x=-\mathbf1_r\). If all coordinates are nonnegative, then \(\lambda_0\ge0\) gives \(\sum_i x_i\le1\), so \(x\) is zero or one of the unit vectors. Conversely all these points belong to \(R\), with zero equal to the average of its \(r+1\) vertices. Thus
\(R\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r,-\mathbf1_r\}\).

For a point \((x,y)\in P\), a convex representation using the displayed vertices and the origin gives \(\sum_j|y_j|\le1\). If \(y\) is a nonzero integer vector, it must therefore be \(\pm\mathbf f_j\). The total coefficient assigned to the vertices \((\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf f_j)\) is at least \(\sum_j|y_j|=1\), so it is exactly one, all other coefficients vanish, and \(x=\mathbf0\). If \(y=\mathbf0\), the projection \(x\) belongs to \(R\), because every projected vertex belongs to \(R\) and zero belongs to \(R\). The preceding census then lists every possible integral \(x\). This proves \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) without omitting boundary points or replacing the ambient lattice.

The listed positive and negative coordinate blocks span \(\mathbb R^n\). More explicitly, the origin and the \(n\) standard unit vectors belong to \(P\), so \(P\) is full-dimensional. There is a convex representation of the origin assigning the positive coefficient \(1/(2(r+1))\) to every simplex vertex and \(1/(4N)\) to every one of the \(2N\) remaining vertices. A supporting hyperplane through the origin would consequently have to contain every vertex: otherwise its functional, of one sign at all vertices, would have a strictly nonzero average in this representation. That contradicts full dimension. Thus the origin lies in the ordinary interior of \(P\).

The function \((x,y)\mapsto A\sum_j|y_j|\) is finite and convex on all of \(\mathbb R^n\), so its values on \(D\) satisfy every convex-admissibility inequality. Define \(f(x,y)=\exp(-A\sum_j|y_j|)\) on \(P\) and \(f(x,y)=0\) outside \(P\). If both endpoints lie in \(P\), convexity of the potential and of \(P\) proves the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). If either endpoint is outside \(P\), the right side of that inequality is zero. Thus \(f\) is a finite-valued global log-concave extension. The census proves that it agrees with the entire prescribed lattice table, including all zeros. Every simplex site, including the origin, has weight one, and every remaining site has weight \(2^{-k}\). Therefore \(w_{\max}=1\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), as asserted.

Original stationarity holds separately in the two coordinate blocks. The simplex vertices have the same original weight one and vector sum \(\sum_i\mathbf e_i-\mathbf1_r=\mathbf0\). Opposite remaining vertices have equal original weights \(2^{-k}\) and cancel pairwise. The origin contributes no moment. Hence \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\) exactly, with no reweighting or conditional law. There are \(r+2\) sites of potential zero and \(2N\) sites of potential \(A\). Summing their weights and energies gives the claimed formulas for \(W\) and \(g\). Since \(W>r=2^{2k+1}\), one has
\(g<2N2^{-k}A/r=A2^{-k}\). For \(k\ge1\), \(k\le2^k\) and \(\log2<1\) give \(A2^{-k}<1\); also \(k2^{-k}\to0\), so \(g\to0\).

To compute the original marginal depth, consider any nonzero normal \(\mathbf b\). Its closed halfspace through zero contains the origin. Because the simplex vertices have vector sum zero, at least one of their normal evaluations is nonnegative, so the halfspace includes at least one simplex vertex of weight one. For each opposite pair \((\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf f_j)\), at least one endpoint is in the halfspace, contributing at least \(2^{-k}\). A zero normal evaluation includes the corresponding equality atom, and a zero evaluation on an opposite pair includes both endpoints. These disjoint contributions prove \(\tau_{\mathbf0}\ge2+N2^{-k}\).

Take \(\mathbf b=(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf1_N)\). Its evaluations are \(-1\) on every \((\mathbf e_i,\mathbf0)\), \(r>0\) on \((-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\), and \(\pm1\) on \((\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf f_j)\). The only equality atom is the origin. The resulting closed halfspace has exactly the lower-bound mass, proving \(\tau_{\mathbf0}=2+N2^{-k}\). A closed halfspace merely containing zero can be written \(\{z:\mathbf b\cdot z\ge a\}\) with \(a\le0\). Replacing \(a\) by zero gives a contained closed halfspace, so allowing such halfspaces does not decrease the minimum further.

Now check all barycentric supports. Extend any barycentric coefficient vector by zero to the full support \(D\). Its coordinate equations at the origin are exactly

\[
\lambda_{(\mathbf e_i,\mathbf0)}
=\lambda_{(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)}\quad(1\le i\le r),
\qquad
\lambda_{(\mathbf0,\mathbf f_j)}
=\lambda_{(\mathbf0,-\mathbf f_j)}\quad(1\le j\le N).
\]

Thus using any simplex vertex forces the use of every simplex vertex, and using any other noncentral vertex forces the use of its opposite endpoint. The gap of this arbitrary coefficient vector is
\(G_\lambda=A\sum_{j=1}^N(\lambda_{(\mathbf0,\mathbf f_j)}+\lambda_{(\mathbf0,-\mathbf f_j)})\), which lies in \([0,A]\). The origin singleton has gap zero. The \(r+1\) simplex vertices, with equal coefficients \(1/(r+1)\), are an affinely independent barycentric support of gap zero. Every opposite pair, with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), is an affinely independent barycentric support of gap exactly \(A\).

For \(0\le L<A\), every hitting set must include the origin to meet its singleton and must include some simplex vertex to meet the simplex support. These requirements cost at least two. The set \(F=\{\mathbf0,(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}\) attains that lower bound even for arbitrary dependent barycentric supports. Indeed, a support using the origin meets \(F\). A support omitting the origin and all simplex vertices has all its coefficient mass on the opposite pairs and therefore has gap exactly \(A\). Every remaining support of gap below \(A\) uses a simplex vertex, and the coordinate equations force it to contain \((-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\). Thus \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)=2\) throughout \(0\le L<A\).

For \(L\ge A\), all barycentric supports are eligible. In addition to the previous disjoint requirements, each opposite pair must be hit, costing at least \(N2^{-k}\). The set obtained by adjoining \((\mathbf0,\mathbf f_j)\) for every \(j\) to \(F\) has exactly the resulting mass. It hits every barycentric support: one using the origin or any simplex vertex meets \(F\), and any other support contains a complete opposite pair. Hence \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)=2+N2^{-k}\) for every \(L\ge A\), including \(L=A\). These lower bounds use only affinely independent supports, and the matching upper bounds hit all supports, verifying agreement of the two definitions directly.

The deletion also has an exact retained-roof certificate. Define its roof at the origin by minimizing \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\setminus F}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\) over nonnegative coefficients of sum one and barycenter zero. The affine function
\(\ell(x,y)=A(1-\sum_{i=1}^r x_i)\) equals the retained potential at every retained site: it is zero on each \((\mathbf e_i,\mathbf0)\) and \(A\) on each \((\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf f_j)\). Its value at zero is \(A\). Every retained barycentric law therefore has mean potential \(A\), and any one of the retained opposite pairs gives such a law. This proves attainment and exact retained roof gap \(A\). In particular, the deletion obstruction is not failure of the retained set to represent the target.

For an explicit closed lifted-cap witness using the convention of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, take \(t=1\) and \(\mathbf b=(-A\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\). The atom score \(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf I+\phi_{\mathbf0}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I}\) is \(L\) at the origin, \(rA+L\) at \((-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\), and \(L-A\) at every retained atom. For \(0\le L<A\), the closed lifted cap is exactly \(F\). At \(L=0\), it includes the origin as an equality atom; all retained scores are strictly negative. At \(L=A\), retained atoms become equality atoms for this particular normal and are included. The exact profile at that cutoff was proved separately above, and its mass is attained by the vertical lifted normal corresponding to the closed marginal halfspace \(\mathbf b=(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf1_N)\). Thus no boundary atom or cutoff equality is discarded.

Finally let \(C,c>0\) be arbitrary. Choose a finite integer \(k\ge1\) such that \(k\log2>2C\) and \(2^{1-k}<c\). Since \(g<1\), one has \(C(1+g)<2C<A\). The exact profile and depth therefore give

\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C(1+g);w)}{\tau_{\mathbf0}}
=\frac{2}{2+2^k}<2^{1-k}<c.
\]

Equivalently, the displayed deletion has original mass \(2<c\tau_{\mathbf0}\), but its retained roof gap is \(A>C(1+g)\). All assertions concern the original centered table. This completes the quantified counterexample to the registered comparison.
