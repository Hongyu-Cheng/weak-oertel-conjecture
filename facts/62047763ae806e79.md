## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), let \(b_i:\mathbb Z\to[0,\infty)\) be the restriction of a nonnegative log-concave function on \(\mathbb R\), and assume that \(b_i\) has between one and three positive integer values. Write \(W_i:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}b_i(k)>0\), \(w_z:=\prod_{i=1}^n b_i(z_i)\), and \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\prod_{i=1}^nW_i\).

For each \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), define \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\) for \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\). On this finite set define the midpoint curvature \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\), using natural logarithms. For \(L\ge0\), put \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\). Then there exist \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\) and \(L\ge0\) such that

\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}\ge e^{-1000}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

The joint maximum over \(j,L\) is attained, so the same estimate holds for that maximum. All constants are independent of \(n\), the factor values, their translations, and their affine tilts. In particular, the result applies to factors whose values at three consecutive integers are a positive scalar multiple of \((u_i,1,\varepsilon_i)\), where \(u_i,\varepsilon_i\ge0\) and \(u_i\varepsilon_i\le1\), and whose other integer values are zero, together with arbitrary nonzero one- or two-point log-concave factors.

Consequently, let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and suppose its integer fiber volumes \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\) have the product form just specified. Put \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(W:=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). There exists \(p^\star\in S\), selected before the halfspace, such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W\), where the absolute constant is \(c_0:=e^{-1000}[-\log(1-e^{-1})]/16\in(0,1/e)\).

## Proof

Every factor has consecutive positive integer support: positivity at two integers forces positivity at every integer between them by log-concavity. Also, for every positive pair, log-concavity in each coordinate gives \(b_i(j_i)^2\ge b_i(j_i+v_i)b_i(j_i-v_i)\). Multiplication shows \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\). In particular, \(0\in\mathcal U_j\), \(\alpha(0)=w_j\), and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). No logarithm of a zero weight is used.

The asserted normalized triples satisfy the factor hypothesis. When both endpoints are positive, interpolate the logarithms of \(u_i,1,\varepsilon_i\) linearly between the consecutive integers. The two slopes are nonincreasing exactly when \(-\log u_i\ge\log\varepsilon_i\), which is \(u_i\varepsilon_i\le1\). Exponentiate and extend the resulting function by zero outside that interval. A zero endpoint shortens the positive interval; two zero endpoints leave a singleton. The same construction on the shortened interval gives the required log-concave extension in those cases.

We first choose the coordinate centers. Put in \(F\) every coordinate for which \(\max_k b_i(k)\ge W_i/2\); choose a maximizing integer \(j_i\) there, and retain only displacement zero in that coordinate. This applies to every one- or two-point factor, including singleton factors of arbitrary positive size. Its contribution \(b_i(j_i)\ge W_i/2\) therefore pays its entire coordinate target without any additional loss.

Let \(A:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus F\). Every factor in \(A\) has exactly three positive consecutive values. Choose its middle integer as \(j_i\). Integer translations and coordinate reflections bijectively reindex centers and displacements and preserve the endpoint products, their minima, and \(W\). Multiplying a factor by a positive scalar multiplies \(w_z,W,B_j^\Delta(L)\) by the same scalar and leaves \(\Delta_j(v)\) unchanged. We may therefore translate, reflect if necessary, and divide each factor in \(A\) by its middle value. From now on its values at \(j_i-1,j_i,j_i+1\) are \(u_i,1,\varepsilon_i\), respectively, with \(u_i\ge\varepsilon_i>0\). These are exact normalizations of the original factors, and the eventual conclusion transfers back under these operations.

For \(i\in A\), write \(T_i:=W_i/2=(1+u_i+\varepsilon_i)/2\), and for \(I\subseteq A\) write \(T_I:=\prod_{i\in I}T_i\). Empty products are one. The definition of \(A\) and log-concavity give

\[
u_i+\varepsilon_i>1,\qquad
u_i<1+\varepsilon_i,\qquad
u_i\varepsilon_i\le1,\qquad
u_i\ge\varepsilon_i>0.
\]

It follows that \(u_i>1/2\), \(\varepsilon_i\le1\), and \(1<T_i<1+\varepsilon_i\le2\). Indeed the first follows from \(u_i+\varepsilon_i>1\) and \(u_i\ge\varepsilon_i\), and the second from \(\varepsilon_i^2\le u_i\varepsilon_i\le1\). All active middle values are now one, so \(w_j=\prod_{i\in F}b_i(j_i)>0\). The factors already frozen imply

\[
2^{-n}W=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{W_i}{2}
\le w_j T_A.
\]

Define the relative active weights \(\beta_i(-1):=u_i\), \(\beta_i(0):=1\), and \(\beta_i(1):=\varepsilon_i\). For \(I\subseteq A\) and \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}^I\), put \(\alpha_I(t):=\min\{\prod_{i\in I}\beta_i(t_i),\prod_{i\in I}\beta_i(-t_i)\}\) and \(\Delta_I(t):=\sum_{i\in I:t_i\ne0}\log(1/(u_i\varepsilon_i))\). In particular, the local zero vector has mass one and cost zero. Every local nonzero coordinate has the cost bound \(0\le\log(1/(u_i\varepsilon_i))\le\log(2/\varepsilon_i)\).

For disjoint active blocks \(I\), choose vectors independently from specified finite sets \(\mathcal V_I\subseteq\{-1,0,1\}^I\), and fix every other displacement coordinate at zero. These choices give distinct positive global pairs. Their global curvature equals the sum of their local curvatures, because \(w_j^2\) cancels the common scale of the two endpoint products. Their global mass is at least \(w_j\prod_I\alpha_I(t_I)\), by the elementary inequality \(\min\{\prod_I a_I,\prod_I b_I\}\ge\prod_I\min\{a_I,b_I\}\) for nonnegative numbers. Thus, if \(Z_I:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal V_I}\alpha_I(t)\), and the sum of valid block cost bounds is \(L\), then \(B_j^\Delta(L)\ge w_j\prod_I Z_I\). This includes empty collections of blocks.

Consider first \(A_0:=\{i\in A:\varepsilon_i>1/64\}\), and let \(N:=|A_0|\). On a single coordinate its three displacements have total local mass \(1+2\varepsilon_i\), and

\[
\frac{1+2\varepsilon_i}{T_i}
\ge\frac{1+2\varepsilon_i}{1+\varepsilon_i}
\ge\frac{66}{65}.
\]

The last inequality follows by subtracting one and using \(\varepsilon_i/(1+\varepsilon_i)\ge1/65\). Each local cost is less than \(\log128<5\). To verify the latter constant directly, the exponential series gives \(e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3\), so \(e^5>(8/3)^5>128\).

If \(N\ge1040\), retain \(A_0\) as one block with all \(3^N\) choices of displacements and put \(L_0:=5N\). The same minimum-of-products inequality shows \(Z_{A_0}\ge\prod_{i\in A_0}(1+2\varepsilon_i)\ge(66/65)^N T_{A_0}\). Moreover \((66/65)^N\ge1+5N\) for every integer \(N\ge1040\). At \(N=1040=16\cdot65\), Bernoulli's inequality gives \((66/65)^{1040}\ge2^{16}=65536>5201=1+5\cdot1040\). The induction step follows from \((66/65)(1+5N)-(1+5(N+1))=(5N-324)/65>0\). Therefore this retained block satisfies \(Z_{A_0}\ge(1+L_0)T_{A_0}\).

If \(N<1040\), retain no block from \(A_0\), fix all its displacements at zero, and put \(L_0:=0\). The discarded coordinates in this group have target product with logarithm less than \(1040\log2<780\). Here \(\log2<3/4\) follows, for example, by integrating the strict chord upper bound \(1/t<(3-t)/2\) for \(1<t<2\). In the retained case the discarded contribution of this group is zero, so the same bound applies in both cases.

The remaining positive parameters satisfy \(0<\varepsilon_i\le1/64\). Partition them into \(I_s:=\{i\in A\setminus A_0:2^{-(s+1)}<\varepsilon_i\le2^{-s}\}\), indexed by integers \(s\ge6\). There are only finitely many nonempty sets. Put \(k_s:=2^{\lceil s/2\rceil+2}\), and partition \(I_s\) into \(m_s\) full blocks of size \(k_s\) and fewer than \(k_s\) remaining coordinates.

On a full block \(I\subseteq I_s\), retain the zero vector and every vector with displacement \(+1\) at one coordinate \(p\in I\), displacement \(-1\) at a distinct coordinate \(q\in I\), and zero elsewhere. The ordered pairs \((p,q)\) give \(k_s(k_s-1)\) distinct nonzero vectors. The mass of this vector is \(\min\{\varepsilon_pu_q,u_p\varepsilon_q\}\ge(1/2)\min\{\varepsilon_p,\varepsilon_q\}\ge2^{-(s+2)}\), and its cost is at most \(2(s+2)\log2\le2(s+2)\). Hence

\[
Z_I\ge1+k_s(k_s-1)2^{-(s+2)},\qquad
T_I\le\exp(k_s2^{-s}),\qquad
\Delta_I(t)\le2(s+2)\quad(t\in\mathcal V_I).
\]

For the target estimate we used \(T_i\le1+\varepsilon_i\) and \(\log(1+x)\le x\). The chosen block size satisfies \(k_s2^{-s}=4\cdot2^{-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\le1/2\) and \(k_s^2 2^{-(s+2)}\ge4\). Consequently \(Z_I\ge1+4-1/8=39/8\), whereas \(T_I\le e^{1/2}<2\). The last strict inequality follows by comparing the exponential series with \(\sum_{r\ge0}(1/2)^r=2\). Thus \(Z_I/T_I>39/16>2\).

If \(m_s\ge3(s+2)\), retain all full blocks at scale \(s\), discard the remaining coordinates, and put \(L_s:=2m_s(s+2)\). Otherwise retain none of these blocks, discard all coordinates of \(I_s\), and put \(L_s:=0\). In either case fewer than \(3(s+2)k_s\) coordinates at scale \(s\) are discarded. In the retained case, \(2^{m_s}\ge1+L_s\). To check this, write \(t=s+2\ge8\). At \(m=3t\), the inequality \(2^t\ge t+1\) gives \(2^{3t}\ge(t+1)^3\ge1+6t^2\), because the difference in the last inequality is \(t(t^2-3t+3)>0\). From any \(m\ge3t\), the desired inequality for \(m+1\) follows from \(2(1+2mt)\ge1+2(m+1)t\). The retained blocks at this scale therefore have total product mass at least \((1+L_s)\) times their target product.

Let \(R\subseteq A\) be the union of all retained blocks, including \(A_0\) if retained, and put \(D:=A\setminus R\). The discarded target loss from scales \(s\ge6\) is bounded independently of the number of scales. Indeed, using \(\log T_i\le\varepsilon_i\), it is at most

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s=6}^{\infty}3(s+2)k_s2^{-s}
&=12\sum_{s=6}^{\infty}(s+2)2^{-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\\
&=12\sum_{t=3}^{\infty}(4t+5)2^{-t}
=63.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last equality, the geometric series and its derivative give \(\sum_{t=3}^{\infty}2^{-t}=1/4\) and \(\sum_{t=3}^{\infty}t2^{-t}=1\). Including the possible discarded coordinates of \(A_0\) gives \(\log T_D<780+63=843<1000\). In particular \(T_R=T_A/T_D\ge e^{-1000}T_A\).

Finally set \(L:=L_0+\sum_{s\ge6}L_s\). This is finite, and all independently selected block vectors have global curvature at most \(L\). Multiplying the mass bounds for the retained blocks gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)
&\ge w_j\prod_{I\text{ retained}}Z_I\\
&\ge w_jT_R(1+L_0)\prod_{s\ge6}(1+L_s)\\
&\ge w_jT_R(1+L)
\ge e^{-1000}2^{-n}W(1+L).
\end{aligned}
\]

The product inequality uses only the nonnegativity of all the \(L_s\), and only finitely many factors differ from one. If no block is retained, the selected vector is zero, \(L=0\), and exactly the same argument applies with empty products. Coordinates with zero endpoint weights were already included in \(F\), so none was omitted from the bound. This proves the selection theorem after undoing the normalizations.

For completeness, there are finitely many positive centers \(j\), and for each such center the finite nonempty set of curvature values contains zero. Given any cutoff \(L\), replacing it by the largest curvature value not exceeding it preserves the numerator and does not increase the denominator. Thus the joint maximum is attained. Multiplication of any original factor by \(e^{a_i k+c_i}\), for arbitrary real \(a_i,c_i\), preserves log-concavity and its integer support, so the proved theorem already includes every such affine tilt. At the level of a normalized three-point factor, the change is \((u_i,1,\varepsilon_i)\mapsto(e^{-a_i}u_i,1,e^{a_i}\varepsilon_i)\); its endpoint product stays \(u_i\varepsilon_i\le1\), while the above center selection handles any resulting endpoint dominance. No assumption that the middle value remains maximal has been made.

To obtain the mixed-integer conclusion, put \(C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\) and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Apply accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` to the actual convex body \(C\). Its objects \(C_z,w_z,W,S,h_S,j,v,\alpha(v),\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j(v),B_j^\Delta(L)\) have exactly these meanings; in particular, \(B_j^\Delta\) counts oriented displacements, including zero, with no change of normalization. That fact supplies one point \(p^\star\in S\) such that \(h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{-\log(1-e^{-1})}{16}\max_{j,L}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}\). Substitution of the selection theorem proves the stated inequality for every closed ambient halfspace containing this same point. Finally \(e>2\) implies \(0<-\log(1-e^{-1})<\log2<1\), so the displayed \(c_0\) is positive and less than \(1/e\). This consequence applies to the stated product class of fiber-volume tables, without requiring the fibers themselves to be Cartesian products.
