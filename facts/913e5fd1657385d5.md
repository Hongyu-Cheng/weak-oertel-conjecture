## Statement

Retain the canonical hard common-normal instance and notation of accepted fact a4f0338595a3a00b. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is the canonical convex body, \(Q>0\),
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
2^n h_{\max}(C)<\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{1}
\]
\(v\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\) is the common continuous normal, and
\[
F:=\mathcal R_H^\circ,
\qquad
R:=R_H^\circ=\sum_{t\in F}b_t>\frac{359}{1600}Q.
\tag{2}
\]
The set \(F\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) is finite and nonempty, \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a full-dimensional compact origin-symmetric convex body, and
\[
0<b_t\leq\frac{a_t}{4},
\qquad
q_t+\frac65K\subseteq\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)
\quad(t\in F).
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, every nonempty \(E\subseteq F\) for which there are an affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R^d\) and points
\[
x_t\in q_t+\frac65K,
\qquad
x_t-s(t)\in\operatorname{span}\{v\}
\quad(t\in E)
\tag{4}
\]
satisfies
\[
\sum_{t\in E}b_t<\frac R4.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
V:=\mathbb R^d/\operatorname{span}\{v\},
\qquad
\pi:\mathbb R^d\to V
\tag{6}
\]
be the quotient map, put \(A:=\operatorname{aff}F\), and define the common quotient body and projected tubes
\[
D:=\frac65\pi(K),
\qquad
L_t:=\pi(q_t)+D
=\pi\left(q_t+\frac65K\right)
\quad(t\in F).
\tag{7}
\]

If \(d=1\), then \(V=\{0\}\), every \(L_t=\{0\}\), and the constant quotient map hits every projected tube. Equivalently, \(F\) itself satisfies (4), contradicting (5) because \(R>0\). Hence the canonical hard common-normal instance cannot have \(d=1\).

Suppose \(d\geq2\). Then \(D\) is a full-dimensional compact origin-symmetric convex body in \(V\). Write \(\|\cdot\|_D\) for its Minkowski norm and
\[
\operatorname{dist}_D(y,L_t)
:=\inf_{\ell\in L_t}\|y-\ell\|_D.
\tag{8}
\]
Define the quotient affine defect and maximum quotient-affine hit mass by
\[
\Delta_H
:=\min_{\bar s:A\to V\ \mathrm{affine}}
\sum_{t\in F}b_t\operatorname{dist}_D(\bar s(t),L_t),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hit}}
:=\max_{\bar s:A\to V\ \mathrm{affine}}
\sum_{\substack{t\in F\\ \bar s(t)\in L_t}}b_t.
\tag{10}
\]
Both extrema are attained, and
\[
R_{\mathrm{hit}}
=\max\left\{
\sum_{t\in E}b_t:
E\subseteq F\text{ satisfies }(4)
\right\}
<\frac R4.
\tag{11}
\]

For a compact convex set \(B\subseteq V\) and \(\eta\in V^*\), write
\[
h_B(\eta):=\max_{y\in B}\eta(y).
\tag{12}
\]
There is the following exact attained duality:
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_H
=\max\left\{
-\sum_{t\in F}h_{L_t}(\eta_t):
\begin{array}{l}
\eta_t\in V^*\quad(t\in F),\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\sum_{t\in F}\eta_t=0,\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\sum_{t\in F}t\otimes\eta_t=0,\\[1mm]
h_D(\eta_t)\leq b_t\quad(t\in F)
\end{array}
\right\}.}
\tag{13}
\]
Here the tensor equation means \(\sum_t\eta_t(Bt)=0\) for every linear map \(B:\mathbb R^n\to V\). In particular, \(\Delta_H=0\) if and only if one affine map hits all the \(L_t\).

Choose a primal optimizer \(\bar s_*\) in (9). Among all dual optimizers in (13), choose \(\eta=(\eta_t)_{t\in F}\) minimizing the gauge load
\[
\omega_t:=h_D(\eta_t),
\qquad
\Omega:=\sum_{t\in F}\omega_t.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
0\leq\omega_t\leq b_t,
\qquad
\omega_t=b_t
\quad\text{whenever }\bar s_*(t)\notin L_t,
\tag{15}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
R-R_{\mathrm{hit}}\leq\Omega\leq R,
\qquad
\Omega>\frac{3R}{4}>\frac{1077}{6400}Q.}
\tag{16}
\]

There are quotient contact points \(\bar x_t\in L_t\) such that
\[
h_{L_t}(\eta_t)=\eta_t(\bar x_t)
\quad(t\in F),
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_H
=\sum_{t\in F}
\omega_t\|\bar s_*(t)-\bar x_t\|_D.}
\tag{18}
\]
If \(\bar s_*(t)\in L_t\), one may take \(\bar x_t=\bar s_*(t)\); otherwise \(\bar x_t\) may be taken to be a nearest point of \(L_t\).

For every \(t\in F\), choose one lift
\[
x_t\in q_t+\frac65K,
\qquad
\pi(x_t)=\bar x_t.
\tag{19}
\]
Then the single atomic contact cloud
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H
:=\gamma_d\sum_{t\in F}\omega_t\delta_{(t,x_t)}}
\tag{20}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\): for every closed ambient halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\),
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_H(G)\leq\mu_C(G).}
\tag{21}
\]
Its mass is exactly
\[
|\sigma_H|=\gamma_d\Omega
>\frac{1077\gamma_d}{6400}Q
>\frac{1077}{17600}Q
>\frac{3}{50}Q.
\tag{22}
\]

Finally, put
\[
J:=\{t\in F:\omega_t>0\},
\qquad
\rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}J,
\tag{23}
\]
and define the intrinsic continuous residual rank of the lifted contacts by
\[
r:=\min_{s:\operatorname{aff}J\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}\{x_t-s(t):t\in J\}.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(N:=|J|\), if \(\xi:\operatorname{aff}J\to\mathbb R^\rho\) is any affine coordinate isomorphism, if \(X\) is the \(N\times(\rho+1)\) matrix with row \(t\) equal to \((1,\xi(t)^{\mathsf T})\), and if \(Y\) is the \(N\times d\) matrix with row \(t\) equal to \(x_t^{\mathsf T}\), then
\[
\boxed{
r=\operatorname{rank}[X\ Y]-\operatorname{rank}X
=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{(t,x_t):t\in J\}-\rho,}
\tag{25}
\]
\[
0\leq r\leq\min\{d,N-\rho-1\},
\tag{26}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d\Omega}{2^\rho(r+1)}.}
\tag{27}
\]
Consequently the hard ledger forces the explicit screen
\[
\boxed{
\min\{d+1,N-\rho\}
\geq r+1
>\frac{100\gamma_d\Omega}{Q}\,2^{n-\rho}
>\frac{1077\gamma_d}{64}\,2^{n-\rho}
>\frac{1077}{176}\,2^{n-\rho}
>6\,2^{n-\rho}.}
\tag{28}
\]
Thus a canonical hard instance surviving the quotient dual cloud must have both large intrinsic continuous residual rank and many positive-load targets relative to its intrinsic base rank. No terminal common-normal absorption conclusion is asserted.

## Proof

Accepted fact a4f0338595a3a00b supplies (1)-(5), including the strict hard ledger, the lower bound for \(R\), the tube inclusions, the fact that \(K\) is a common full-dimensional origin-symmetric body in the retained canonical instance, and the quotient-affine screen (5).

We first prove the zero-dimensional assertion. If \(d=1\), then the nonzero vector \(v\) spans all of \(\mathbb R\), so \(V=\{0\}\). Every \(L_t\) is the singleton \(\{0\}\). Take the zero affine map on \(A\) and choose an arbitrary \(x_t\in q_t+(6/5)K\) for every \(t\in F\). Then \(x_t-0\in\operatorname{span}\{v\}\), so \(E=F\) satisfies (4). Equation (5) would give \(R<R/4\), contrary to \(R>0\). Hence the hard branch has no \(d=1\) instance.

Assume \(d\geq2\). Because \(K\) is full-dimensional, compact, origin-symmetric, and has \(0\) in its interior, its quotient image \(D=(6/5)\pi(K)\) has the same properties relative to \(V\). Thus \(D\) is the unit ball of the norm \(\|\cdot\|_D\), and \(h_D\) is its dual norm.

We establish (11). Suppose first that an affine \(\bar s:A\to V\) hits \(L_t\) for every \(t\in E\). Choose a linear right inverse \(\iota:V\to\mathbb R^d\) of \(\pi\), put \(s:=\iota\circ\bar s\), and choose \(x_t\in q_t+(6/5)K\) with \(\pi(x_t)=\bar s(t)\). Then \(x_t-s(t)\in\ker\pi=\operatorname{span}\{v\}\), so \(E\) satisfies (4). Conversely, projecting the data in (4) gives an affine map \(\pi\circ s\) on \(\operatorname{aff}E\) hitting every \(L_t\), and that affine map extends from \(\operatorname{aff}E\) to \(A\). Hence the two maxima in (11) agree. There are only finitely many subsets of \(F\), so the maximum is attained. Equation (5) gives its strict upper bound. The minimum in (9) is attained by the finite-dimensional distance argument below.

We now prove (13) without importing any matching-specific assertion. Let
\[
\mathcal A
:=\{(\bar s(t))_{t\in F}:\bar s:A\to V\text{ affine}\}
\subseteq V^F,
\qquad
\mathcal L:=\prod_{t\in F}L_t.
\tag{29}
\]
The evaluation set \(\mathcal A\) is a linear subspace and \(\mathcal L\) is compact and convex. On \(V^F\), define
\[
\mathcal N((y_t)_{t\in F})
:=\sum_{t\in F}b_t\|y_t\|_D.
\tag{30}
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_H
=\min_{y\in\mathcal A,\,\ell\in\mathcal L}
\mathcal N(y-\ell).
\tag{31}
\]
Distance to the closed subspace \(\mathcal A\) is attained for each \(\ell\), and the quotient-distance function is continuous on the compact set \(\mathcal L\). This proves primal attainment.

The dual norm of (30) is
\[
\mathcal N^*((\eta_t)_t)
=\max_{t\in F}\frac{h_D(\eta_t)}{b_t}.
\tag{32}
\]
Indeed, coordinatewise polar Hölder inequalities give the upper bound, and a norming vector concentrated in a coordinate attaining the displayed maximum gives equality. The annihilator \(\mathcal A^\perp\) consists exactly of the families satisfying the two moment equations in (13). To see this, every affine map on \(A\) extends to \(\mathbb R^n\); testing constants gives \(\sum_t\eta_t=0\), and testing all linear maps gives \(\sum_t t\otimes\eta_t=0\). The converse follows by direct substitution.

If \(\eta\in\mathcal A^\perp\) and \(\mathcal N^*(\eta)\leq1\), then for every \(y\in\mathcal A\) and \(\ell\in\mathcal L\),
\[
-h_{\mathcal L}(\eta)
\leq-\sum_t\eta_t(\ell_t)
=\sum_t\eta_t(y_t-\ell_t)
\leq\mathcal N(y-\ell).
\tag{33}
\]
This is weak duality. For the reverse inequality, let the value in (31) be \(\Delta\). If \(\Delta=0\), \(\eta=0\) attains equality. If \(\Delta>0\), separate the closed convex set \(\mathcal A-\mathcal L\) from the open \(\mathcal N\)-ball of radius \(\Delta\). The invariance of \(\mathcal A-\mathcal L\) under translation by \(\mathcal A\) forces the separating functional to lie in \(\mathcal A^\perp\). Normalizing it to have dual norm one gives \(-h_{\mathcal L}(\eta)\geq\Delta\). Together with (33), this is equality. Since \(h_{\mathcal L}(\eta)=\sum_t h_{L_t}(\eta_t)\) and (32) turns dual-norm feasibility into \(h_D(\eta_t)\leq b_t\), this proves (13). The feasible dual set is compact because \(D\) contains a neighborhood of \(0\), so the maximum is attained. It also proves that \(\Delta_H=0\) exactly when \(\mathcal A\cap\mathcal L\neq\varnothing\), equivalently when one affine map hits every tube.

The compact dual optimizer set contains a minimizer of the continuous load \(\Omega\). Fix such a minimizer and any primal optimizer. Put \(y_t:=\bar s_*(t)\) and \(d_t:=\operatorname{dist}_D(y_t,L_t)\). For each coordinate, dual feasibility gives
\[
b_t d_t
\geq\eta_t(y_t)-h_{L_t}(\eta_t).
\tag{34}
\]
The sum of the right sides is the dual value because the moment equations annihilate the affine tuple \(y\). Primal-dual equality therefore forces equality in (34) for every \(t\).

Choose \(\bar x_t\in L_t\) nearest to \(y_t\), taking \(\bar x_t=y_t\) when \(y_t\in L_t\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_td_t
&=\eta_t(y_t)-h_{L_t}(\eta_t)\\
&\leq\eta_t(y_t-\bar x_t)\\
&\leq h_D(\eta_t)\|y_t-\bar x_t\|_D\\
&\leq b_td_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
All inequalities are equalities. The first inequality being an equality gives the support relation (17), and summing the middle equality gives (18). If \(y_t\notin L_t\), then \(d_t>0\), so equality in the last line forces \(h_D(\eta_t)=b_t\). This proves (15).

The missed set of \(\bar s_*\) has \(b\)-mass at least \(R-R_{\mathrm{hit}}>3R/4\). Every missed coordinate is saturated by (15), while every load is at most its capacity. Hence
\[
R-R_{\mathrm{hit}}\leq\Omega\leq R,
\]
and (2) proves (16).

We next lift and dominate the contacts. Equation (7) makes every lift in (19) possible. By (3),
\[
x_t\in\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t).
\tag{36}
\]
Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d says both that \(x_t\in C_t\) and that every proper closed fiber halfspace containing \(x_t\) has \(C_t\)-volume at least \(\gamma_db_t\).

Let \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace and write \(G_t:=\{y:(t,y)\in G\}\). For each \(t\) whose atom \((t,x_t)\) lies in \(G\), either \(G_t\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(x_t\), in which case
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap G_t)
\geq\gamma_db_t
\geq\gamma_d\omega_t,
\tag{37}
\]
or \(G_t=\mathbb R^d\), in which case the same bound follows from \(a_t\geq b_t\geq\omega_t\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\). An empty section contains no contact atom. There is exactly one contact atom in each target fiber, so summing (37) once per target proves (21). Equation (20) is carried by the mixed-integer part of \(C\) by (36), and its exact mass is \(\gamma_d\Omega\). Accepted fact a4f0338595a3a00b also records \(\gamma_d>4/11\); combining this with (16) gives every strict comparison in (22).

It remains to prove the residual screen. The set \(J\) is nonempty because \(\Omega>0\). Equation (36) and accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d give the absolute fiber-depth bounds
\[
\delta_C(t,x_t)
\geq\gamma_db_t
\geq\gamma_d\omega_t
\quad(t\in J).
\tag{38}
\]
Apply the arbitrary-selector theorem of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78 to the points \(x_t\) and weights \(\gamma_d\omega_t\). Its exact rank identity gives (25)-(26), and its intrinsic selector bound gives (27). Comparing (27) with the strict hard ledger (1) yields
\[
r+1
>\frac{100\gamma_d\Omega}{Q}\,2^{n-\rho}.
\tag{39}
\]
The rank upper bounds in (26), the load estimate \(\Omega>1077Q/6400\), and \(\gamma_d>4/11\) now give
\[
\min\{d+1,N-\rho\}
\geq r+1
>\frac{1077\gamma_d}{64}\,2^{n-\rho}
>\frac{1077}{176}\,2^{n-\rho}
>6\,2^{n-\rho},
\]
which is (28). This completes the theorem.
