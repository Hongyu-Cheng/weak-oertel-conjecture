## Statement

Let $\sqrt2-1<h<1$, $A=1/(h^2+2h-1)$, and $s=A(1+h)-h$. Let $K_h$ be the planar trapezoid with vertices $(0,1),(-1,1),(-s,-h),(s+h,-h)$. The origin is interior. Consider the three closed caps cut out by $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$, and $x+y\le0$. The closure of the portion of the upper cap lying outside both other caps is the triangle with vertices $0,(-1,1),(0,1)$, formed by the origin and the two chord endpoints bounding that sector. All three caps have the same area fraction
$$
c(h)=\frac1{5-h^2-4/(1+h)}.
$$
The fraction has a unique minimum at the root of $h(1+h)^2=2$ in the stated interval. Its supremum on the interval is $1/2$, approached at both ends. For $h=7/10$ the three fractions all equal $1700/3667<2\sqrt3-3$. Thus one unique sector equaling its radial triangle does not force a cap at or above $2\sqrt3-3$. For this instance $(9/4)c-1=158/3667>0$ and $c-128/279=4924/1023093>0$.

## Proof

The denominator in $A$ is positive on the parameter interval, and the numerator of $s$ over that denominator is
$(1-h)(h^2+3h+1)>0$. The horizontal edges have positive lengths $1$ and $2s+h$. Interpolation along the two side edges gives the horizontal chord endpoints $(-A,0),(A,0)$. Since $-h<0<1$ and the horizontal chord has zero strictly between its endpoints, zero is interior. The chord of $x=0$ has endpoints $(0,-h),(0,1)$ and that of $x+y=0$ has endpoints $(h,-h),(-1,1)$. These follow from the bottom and top edge intersections, which lie on the indicated segments because $s>0$.

The sector of the upper cap outside the other two has $y>0$ and $-y<x<0$. Its closure inside $K_h$ is exactly the claimed triangle. Indeed, the inequalities $0\le y\le1$ and $-y\le x\le0$ give the triangle, and its three vertices belong to $K_h$. Its area is $1/2$. The upper double sector in $x\ge0$ is the triangle with vertices $0,(A,0),(0,1)$ of area $A/2$. The linear area-preserving reflection $(x,y)\mapsto(-x-y,y)$ maps $K_h$ to itself and swaps the two oblique sectors, so the other upper double sector also has area $A/2$. The lower double sector $0\le x\le-y$, $-h\le y\le0$ is the triangle with vertices $0,(0,-h),(h,-h)$ of area $h^2/2$. All these triangles are verified by their bounding inequalities and vertex containment. No triple sector has positive area. The two remaining unique sectors have a common area by the same reflection.

The trapezoid area is
$$
|K_h|=\frac{(1+h)(1+2s+h)}2
=\frac{1-h^2}2+A(1+h)^2
=\frac32-\frac{h^2}2+2A,
$$
using $A(h^2+2h-1)=1$. The upper cap has area $1/2+A$. Counting the two equal remaining unique sectors using the total area shows that either oblique cap has area $|K_h|/2-1/4+h^2/4=1/2+A$. Division by the total area and simplification give the stated fraction.

Put $D(h)=5-h^2-4/(1+h)>0$. Then
$$
D'(h)=\frac{2(2-h(1+h)^2)}{(1+h)^2},
\qquad c'(h)=-D'(h)/D(h)^2.
$$
The function $h(1+h)^2$ is strictly increasing here, with limiting values $2(\sqrt2-1)<2$ and $4>2$. Thus $c$ strictly decreases before the root and strictly increases after it. At both ends $D$ tends to $2$, giving the supremum.

At $h=7/10$, $A=100/89$, $s=1077/890$, and $|K_h|=62339/17800$. Their ratio gives $c=1700/3667$. Since $c+3>0$ and $12-(c+3)^2=47267/13446889>0$, the strict upper comparison follows. The other two comparisons follow by subtraction of rational numbers.
