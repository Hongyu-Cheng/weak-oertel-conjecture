## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\) be positive integers with \(L\ge h\), let \(\beta,\theta\in\mathbb R\) with \(\beta\ne0\), and define
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
                 \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i+hy\le L\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y)=
\frac{e^{\beta\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i+\theta y}}
     {\sum_{(\mathbf u,v)\in D}e^{\beta\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_i+\theta v}}.
\]
Use the actual closed-halfspace depth and moment of 01a4100542206b94 with its ambient dimension \(N=m+1\), support equal to this \(D\), and probability equal to this \(p\):
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^{m+1}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
                                      \mathbf z\in H}}p(H),
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]
There is one absolute \(\kappa>0\) such that \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-(m+1)}\) for all these parameters. More explicitly, let \(g>1\) be the absolute reflection-moment constant of 609407d6db81b18b, and use the same \(g_0=\min\{2,g^{3/2}\}>1\) and \(c_1=60000\) as 6b4d5db75549652a. One may take
\[
\kappa=\min\left\{
\frac{1}{4096c_1^4},
\frac{1}{128c_1^8g_0^2}
             \left(\frac{\log g_0}{16}\right)^{16}
\right\}.
\]
This extends 6b4d5db75549652a from zero to arbitrary nonzero common transverse tilt without changing its source-law quantifiers.

## Proof

We first prove a height-band assertion whose hypotheses require only monotonicity and a polynomial growth bound, rather than an exact polynomial partition sum.

Let \(\nu,K\ge1\) be integers, let \(b_0,\ldots,b_K\) be positive and nondecreasing, and suppose
\[
\frac{b_v}{b_u}\le\left(\frac vu\right)^\nu
                    \qquad(1\le u\le v\le K).
\tag{1}
\]
For arbitrary \(\eta\in\mathbb R\), put
\(Z_b=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\eta t}b_t\) and
\(q(t)=e^{-\eta t}b_t/Z_b\). We claim that a nonempty consecutive integer interval \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\) exists with
\[
\lambda_b:=\frac{|I|b_a\min_{t\in I}e^{-\eta t}}{Z_b}
\ge\frac{1}{c_1(\nu+1)^2}.
\tag{2}
\]

We will use monotone weighting on a line. Multiplication of a probability by a nonnegative nondecreasing function of positive expectation cannot decrease probabilities of upper intervals or the mean. Multiplication by a positive nonincreasing function cannot increase the mean. These assertions follow from the sign of the covariance, using independent copies \(T,T'\) and
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(A(T),B(T))
=\mathbb E[(A(T)-A(T'))(B(T)-B(T'))].
\tag{3}
\]
Here one takes \(A\) to be an upper-interval indicator or the identity function, with \(B\) the weighting function. All uses below either have finite support or an exponentially decreasing tail.

If \(\eta\le0\), write \(w=-\eta\ge0\), and set
\[
d_*=
\begin{cases}
K/[16(\nu+1)],&w=0,\\
\min\{K/[16(\nu+1)],1/w\},&w>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Take \(n=\max\{1,\lfloor d_*\rfloor\}\), \(a=K-n+1\), and \(I=[a,K]\cap\mathbb Z\). This interval is nonempty, \(a\ge K/2\ge1/2\), and in fact \(a\ge1\). Also \(n-1\le K/[16(\nu+1)]\) and \(w(n-1)\le1\). By (1),
\[
\frac{b_K}{b_a}\le(1+(n-1)/a)^\nu\le e^{1/8}.
\]
Consequently \(\lambda_b\ge e^{-9/8}q(I)\ge q(I)/9\). The weights \(e^{wt}b_t\) are nondecreasing, so \(q(I)\ge n/(K+1)\). If \(w>0\), comparison by (3) with the weights \(e^{wt}\) also gives
\[
q(I)\ge\frac{1-e^{-wn}}{1-e^{-w(K+1)}}\ge1-e^{-wn}.
\tag{4}
\]
If \(d_*<2\), either \(K<32(\nu+1)\), giving \(q(I)\ge1/[33(\nu+1)]\), or \(w>1/2\), giving \(q(I)\ge1-e^{-1/2}\ge1/3\). If \(d_*\ge2\), then \(n\ge d_*/2\). When \(d_*=K/[16(\nu+1)]\), this gives \(q(I)\ge1/[64(\nu+1)]\); when \(d_*=1/w\), (4) gives at least \(1/3\). Thus \(\lambda_b\ge1/[576(\nu+1)]\), which implies (2).

If \(\eta\ge1/4\), (1) gives, for every integer \(t\ge8\nu\) with \(t+1\le K\),
\[
\frac{e^{-\eta(t+1)}b_{t+1}}{e^{-\eta t}b_t}
\le e^{-\eta}(1+1/t)^\nu\le e^{-1/8}.
\]
Shifting each term of the tail by 32 proves
\(q(t\ge8\nu+32)\le e^{-4}q(t\ge8\nu)<1/16\), with the same assertion if either tail is empty. Therefore at least \(15/16\) of the probability is carried by at most \(33(\nu+1)\) heights. Some height has probability at least \(1/[36(\nu+1)]\). For the singleton interval at that height, \(\lambda_b\) equals this atom probability and (2) follows.

It remains to treat \(0<\eta<1/4\). If \(K<4\), a singleton height has probability at least \(1/4\), so assume \(K\ge4\) and put \(s_* =\min\{K,1/\eta\}\ge4\). Let \(a_* =\lceil s_*/2\rceil\). Since \(b_t\) is nondecreasing, (3) compares \(q\) from below on upper intervals with the truncated geometric weights \(e^{-\eta t}\). Those weights give probability at least \(1/9\) to \(t\ge a_*\). Indeed, if \(K\le1/\eta\), there are at least \((K+1)/2\) such heights, each of weight at least \(e^{-1}\), and the total weight is at most \(K+1\). If \(K\ge1/\eta\), their probability is
\[
\frac{e^{-\eta a_*}(1-e^{-\eta(K-a_*+1)})}{1-e^{-\eta(K+1)}}
\ge e^{-3/4}(1-e^{-1/2})>1/9,
\]
because \(\eta a_*\le3/4\) and \(K-a_*+1\ge1/(2\eta)\).

The mean of \(q\) is at most \(2(\nu+1)/\eta\). To prove this without assuming a polynomial expansion, (1) says that \(b_t/t^\nu\) is nonincreasing on \(1\le t\le K\). Hence (3) bounds the mean of \(q\), conditional on \(t\ge1\), by that of the truncated law with weights \(t^\nu e^{-\eta t}\). Truncating the latter law at \(K\) cannot increase its mean. For \(S_j=\sum_{t\ge1}t^j e^{-\eta t}\), integration over \([t-1,t]\) and \([t,t+1]\), respectively, gives
\[
S_\nu\ge e^{-\eta}\frac{\nu!}{\eta^{\nu+1}},
\qquad
S_{\nu+1}\le e^\eta\frac{(\nu+1)!}{\eta^{\nu+2}},
\qquad
\frac{S_{\nu+1}}{S_\nu}
\le e^{2\eta}\frac{\nu+1}{\eta}<\frac{2(\nu+1)}{\eta}.
\]
The integral values follow by repeated integration by parts. An atom at zero only decreases the unconditional mean, proving the bound.

Set \(B_* =\min\{K,\lceil72(\nu+1)/\eta\rceil\}\). Markov's inequality gives \(q(t>B_*)\le1/36\), or zero when \(B_*=K\). Thus \(q(a_*\le t\le B_*)\ge1/12\). Partition this integer interval into consecutive blocks of at most
\(n=\max\{1,\lfloor s_*/[16(\nu+1)]\rfloor\}\) heights. We have \(n\ge s_*/[32(\nu+1)]\) and \(B_*\le73(\nu+1)s_*\), so there are at most \(2369(\nu+1)^2\) blocks. One block \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\) has probability at least \(1/[28428(\nu+1)^2]\). On this block,
\[
\frac{b_b}{b_a}\le(1+(b-a)/a)^\nu\le e^{1/8},
\qquad
\eta(b-a)\le1/16,
\]
since \(a\ge s_*/2\) and \(b-a\le n-1\le s_*/[16(\nu+1)]\). It follows that
\(\lambda_b\ge e^{-3/16}q(I)\ge q(I)/2\ge1/[56856(\nu+1)^2]\), proving (2). The numerical estimates above follow, for example, from \(2<e<3\), \(e^{1/2}\ge3/2\), and \(e^{1/2}<2\). This completes the height-band proof.

We now put the source in coordinates suited to the sign of \(\beta\). Write \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\ge1\), \(\ell=L-hK\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\), \(t=K-y\), and \(R_t=\ell+ht\). Let \(k=m-1\), let \(\mathbf j=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\), and let
\(F(u)=\binom{u+m-1}{m-1}\) for nonnegative integers \(u\). This is the number of compositions of the total \(u\) into the \(m\) transverse coordinates, equivalently the number of \(\mathbf j\ge0\) with \(\sum_i j_i\le u\). For \(m=1\), the vector \(\mathbf j\) is empty and \(F(u)=1\).

For \(\beta<0\), use the total \(U=\sum_i x_i\) as the remaining transverse coordinate. The invertible affine map is \((\mathbf x,y)\mapsto(\mathbf j,U,t)\), with inverse \(x_m=U-\sum_i j_i\) and \(y=K-t\). In these coordinates the support is
\(0\le t\le K\), \(0\le U\le R_t\), and \(\sum_i j_i\le U\). The original probabilities are exactly
\[
p(\mathbf j,U,t)=\frac{e^{-\theta t}e^{\beta U}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\theta t}A(R_t),
\qquad
A(R)=\sum_{U=0}^R e^{\beta U}F(U).
\tag{5}
\]
The constant \(e^{\theta K}\) cancels from the original normalizing sum.

For \(\beta>0\), use the weighted budget slack \(s=L-\sum_i x_i-hy\) instead. The map \((\mathbf x,y)\mapsto(\mathbf j,s,t)\) is invertible affine, with inverse \(y=K-t\) and \(x_m=R_t-s-\sum_i j_i\). Its support is
\(0\le t\le K\), \(s\ge0\), and \(\sum_i j_i+s\le R_t\). Put \(\eta=\theta-h\beta\). The original probabilities in these coordinates are exactly
\[
p(\mathbf j,s,t)=\frac{e^{-\eta t}e^{-\beta s}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\eta t}B(R_t),
\qquad
B(R)=\sum_{s=0}^R e^{-\beta s}F(R-s).
\tag{6}
\]
Here the canceled constant is \(e^{\beta\ell+\theta K}\). These maps preserve every closed halfspace and its original probability, so they preserve the actual depths and \(M_3\).

Both partition functions needed in (5) and (6) are positive, nondecreasing, and satisfy
\[
\frac{A(R_2)}{A(R_1)}\le\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)^m
\quad(\beta<0),
\qquad
\frac{B(R_2)}{B(R_1)}\le\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)^m
\quad(\beta>0)
\quad(1\le R_1\le R_2).
\tag{7}
\]
To verify these bounds exactly, let \(N_R=\binom{R+m}{m}\), the number of points in the standard simplex \(\Delta_{R,m}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i x_i\le R\}\). Under its uniform law let \(V_R=\sum_i x_i\) and \(W_R=R-V_R\). Then
\(A(R)/N_R=\mathbb E e^{\beta V_R}\) and
\(B(R)/N_R=\mathbb E e^{-\beta W_R}\).
The total \(V_R\) is stochastically nondecreasing with \(R\), since for every integer \(a\ge1\) its lower-tail probability, when \(R\ge a\), is
\(\mathbb P(V_R<a)=\binom{a-1+m}{m}/N_R\); when \(R<a\) it is one. The slack \(W_R\) is also stochastically nondecreasing, since for \(R\ge a\),
\[
\mathbb P(W_R\ge a)
=\frac{\binom{R-a+m}{m}}{\binom{R+m}{m}}
=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{R-a+i}{R+i},
\]
and every factor is nondecreasing with \(R\); for \(R<a\) the probability is zero. Thus the two normalized expectations above are nonincreasing for their stipulated signs. Consequently each ratio in (7) is at most
\(N_{R_2}/N_{R_1}=\prod_{i=1}^m(R_2+i)/(R_1+i)\le(R_2/R_1)^m\). Monotonicity of \(A\) follows from its partial-sum formula, and monotonicity of \(B\) follows from the monotonicity of \(F\) and its displayed sum. This proves all assertions used about these partition functions.

In the negative case set \(b_t=A(R_t)\) and \(\eta=\theta\); in the positive case set \(b_t=B(R_t)\) and use \(\eta\) from (6). For \(1\le u\le v\le K\), (7) gives
\[
\frac{b_v}{b_u}\le\left(\frac{\ell+hv}{\ell+hu}\right)^m
\le(v/u)^m.
\]
Apply (2) with \(\nu=m\). Obtain a nonempty height interval \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\) and \(\lambda\ge[c_1(m+1)^2]^{-1}\). Let \(R=R_a\), and let \(Q_R\) be the conditional transverse law at height \(a\), in the corresponding affine coordinates. In (5) it is
\(Q_R(\mathbf j,U)=e^{\beta U}/A(R)\) on \(\sum_i j_i\le U\le R\); in (6) it is
\(Q_R(\mathbf j,s)=e^{-\beta s}/B(R)\) on \(\sum_i j_i+s\le R\).
The fixed support of \(Q_R\) is contained in the transverse support at every \(t\in I\). The atom formulas (5) and (6) therefore prove the pointwise comparison, in the original normalization,
\[
p\ge\lambda\,\bigl(Q_R\otimes u_I\bigr),
\qquad u_I=\operatorname{Unif}(I).
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed the ratio on this product support is \(|I|b_a e^{-\eta t}/\mathcal Z\), whose minimum is precisely the \(\lambda_b\) of (2). The product law is extended by zero outside its support.

For clarity, we give the actual-depth and rank estimates used on this product. For any two finite probability laws \(P_1,P_2\) in separate real coordinate blocks,
\[
\tau_{P_1\otimes P_2}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)
\ge\tau_{P_1}(\mathbf z_1)\tau_{P_2}(\mathbf z_2),
\qquad
M_3(P_1\otimes P_2)\ge M_3(P_1)M_3(P_2).
\tag{9}
\]
To prove the depth assertion, take any closed halfspace containing the root and replace it by the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through that root and which is contained in the given halfspace. Its centered inequality is the sum of one linear functional from each block being nonnegative. The intersection of the two events in which each block functional is nonnegative is contained in this halfspace. Each event has probability at least the corresponding actual depth, and independence gives their product. If a coefficient block vanishes, its event is the whole probability space, with probability one. Taking the infimum proves the depth assertion; averaging its cube with the product root law proves the moment assertion.

Likewise, if a probability law \(P\) dominates \(\rho V\) pointwise for a probability law \(V\) on a subset of its support, then every containing halfspace has \(P\)-mass at least \(\rho\) times its \(V\)-mass. Retaining original root weights gives
\[
M_3(P)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in\operatorname{supp}V}
             P(\mathbf z)(\rho\tau_V(\mathbf z))^3
\ge\rho^4M_3(V).
\tag{10}
\]
The fourth power has one factor from the original root probability and three from its original depth.

For every \(k\ge1\) and integer \(T\ge0\), let \(u_{T,k}\) be uniform on \(\Delta_{T,k}=\{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^k:\sum_i j_i\le T\}\). It satisfies
\[
M_3(u_{T,k})\ge\tfrac18g_0^k8^{-k}.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(T\ge2\), apply 609407d6db81b18b with its rank \(r=k\), its dilation parameter \(m=T\), and its tilt vector \(\mathbf0\). This is the normalization map from that fact's dilation notation. For a target \(\mathbf j\), its composition is \((T-\sum_i j_i,j_1,\ldots,j_k)\); the fact's composition count is exactly the number of endpoints \(\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,k}\) for which \(2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,k}\). Divide this count by \(|\Delta_{T,k}|\) to obtain \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}\). The accepted reflection-moment assertion, rather than a capacity-to-depth assertion, gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,k}(\mathbf j)\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2\ge g^k4^{-k}\).
Reflection pairs all these endpoints except the root; every closed halfspace containing the root contains at least one endpoint of each pair, and contains the root. Uniformity consequently gives \(\tau_{u_{T,k}}(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}/2\). Jensen's inequality for the function \(x^{3/2}\), applied to \(\mathsf H^2\), now proves
\[
M_3(u_{T,k})\ge\tfrac18\mathbb E\mathsf H^3
\ge\tfrac18(\mathbb E\mathsf H^2)^{3/2}
\ge\tfrac18g^{3k/2}8^{-k}
\ge\tfrac18g_0^k8^{-k}.
\]
For \(T=1\), the support consists of \(k+1\) vertices, each of which can be isolated by a closed halfspace and hence has actual depth \(1/(k+1)\). The inequality \((k+1)^3\le2\cdot4^k\) holds at \(k=1\) and persists by induction because \(((k+2)/(k+1))^3\le27/8<4\). This and \(g_0\le2\) imply (11). For \(T=0\) the law is a singleton, so its moment is one and (11) also holds.

Every uniform consecutive integer interval has third depth moment at least \(1/32\), by 01a4100542206b94 with \(N=1\), \(q=3\), and its uniform complete-support case; this includes a singleton interval. Thus (9) and (11) give
