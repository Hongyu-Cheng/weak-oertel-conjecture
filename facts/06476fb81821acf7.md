## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$, let $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact and convex, and let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ carry the summed $d$-volume $\mu$ of its fibers, with positive total mass. Write $D(w)=\inf_{H\ni w}\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces. Let $y_0,y_1\in S$ be distinct points of positive depth, and let $y=(1-t)y_0+ty_1\in S$ for $0<t<1$. If $D(y)=\min(D(y_0),D(y_1))$, every minimizing nonvertical cap through $y$ contains both endpoints on its boundary, and $D(y_0)=D(y_1)=D(y)$. The endpoints can have the same base.

## Proof

The actual nonempty fibers at lattice bases form a finite compatible family, since convex combinations of their lifts remain in $C$. The positive-depth characterization of fact 55a57b1cf55d0c87 puts both endpoints in the interiors of their own positive-volume fibers.

By the nonvertical through-point attainment in fact 1ec728c26c11b368, let $H=\{L\ge0\}$ be a minimizing nonvertical cap through $y$. The weighted average of $L(y_0),L(y_1)$ equals $L(y)=0$. If $L(y_e)>0$, the parallel cap $\{L\ge L(y_e)\}$ still contains $y_e$. It removes strictly positive volume from $H$ in the interior ball about $y_e$ in its own fiber, since the continuous normal is nonzero and the positive-width slab $0\le L<L(y_e)$ meets the interior of that ball. Its mass would be below $D(y)\le D(y_e)$, a contradiction. Hence both margins are nonpositive and their zero average forces both to vanish. This cap has mass $D(y)$ and contains each endpoint, giving $D(y_e)\le D(y)$. The equality hypothesis gives the reverse inequalities.
