## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, let $p\in P$, and place the same compact convex $d$-body $K$, $d\ge1$, at every base. Let $\phi(u,x)$ be a finite nonnegative Borel weight on the unit normal sphere times $\partial K$. Assume the own boundary cost is
$$
D=\int_{\partial K}\phi(n_K(x),x)\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}(x)\in(0,\infty),
$$
where $n_K$ denotes the outward unit normal at almost every boundary point. In dimension one use the two endpoints with their outward signs and counting measure. An admissible continuation is a finite nonnegative Borel function $\Psi(u,z,x)$ convex in $(z,x)$ for fixed $u$, satisfying $\Psi(n_K(x),p,x)\ge\phi(n_K(x),x)$ on the measured source boundary. Its total cost is the sum of the same boundary integrals at all $z\in P$.

Define
$$
T(P,p)=\max_{\substack{0\le w_z\le1\\\sum_z w_z(z-p)=0}}\sum_z w_z .
$$
Every continuation costs at least $T(P,p)D$. If $\phi(u,x)=h(u)$ is independent of $x$, the minimum is $T(P,p)D$. This includes the projective boundary weight when its source is the ordinary centroid. If $p$ is the arithmetic centroid of $P$, every continuation costs at least $|P|D$. This minimum is attained whenever $\phi$ has a finite nonnegative Borel extension convex in $x\in\mathbb R^d$ for each $u$, including nonconstant projective weights. On actual convex products this factor is $2m+1$ for the bases $\{-m,\ldots,m\}$, $m\ge1$, and $31$ for the integer points of $\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,-4),(4,0),(0,4)\}$, whose arithmetic centroid is zero and which has four occupied parities.

## Proof

A maximizing balanced vector exists by compactness of the constrained box, and $T(P,p)\ge1$ by taking the unit weight at $p$. For each regular boundary point fix its continuous coordinate $x$ and normal $u=n_K(x)$. By the balance equation $(p,x)$ is the convex combination of $(z,x)$ with weights $w_z/T(P,p)$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{z\in P}\Psi(u,z,x)
 \ge\sum_{z\in P}w_z\Psi(u,z,x)
 \ge T(P,p)\Psi(u,p,x)
 \ge T(P,p)\phi(u,x).
$$
The first inequality uses nonnegativity and $w_z\le1$. The identical product boundaries allow integration against the same measure and normal map, giving the cost bound.

In 08b344179e054c1c take $m_z=1$ for each of these same bases and the same $p\in\operatorname{conv}P$. The balanced maximum there is $T(P,p)$ and its formula gives $\inf_a\sum_z[1+a\cdot(z-p)]_+=T(P,p)$. This infimum is attained. Indeed, the projection onto the $t$ variables of the finite polyhedron $t_z\ge0$, $t_z\ge1+a\cdot(z-p)$ is a closed polyhedron by linear elimination. A nonempty objective sublevel $\sum_z t_z\le L$ in that projection is compact, so the minimum of the sum is attained. A lifted optimizer $a$ gives the hinge minimum since for fixed $a$ the smallest admissible $t_z$ is the displayed positive part. Now $\Psi(u,z,x)=h(u)[1+a\cdot(z-p)]_+$ is convex in $(z,x)$ for each fixed $u$, matches $h(u)$ at $p$, and integrates to $T(P,p)D$. For general nonconstant $\phi$ only the Jensen lower bound applies at an arbitrary $p$.

If $p=|P|^{-1}\sum_z z$, the all-one balanced vector gives $T(P,p)=|P|$. When a globally convex-in-$x$ extension as in the statement is available, use that extension as the base-independent $\Psi$ to attain the bound. In the projective example, the attained positive denominator is $L(x)=1+a\cdot(x-y)$ at an interior source $y$ by d90ba05577427854. As in the first part of the proof of f2b2f272355448fa, $\phi(u,x)=h_{K-y}(u)f(L(x))$, where $h_{K-y}$ is the support function and $f$ is the positive convex tangent extension of $t^{-d}$ from $[\min_K L,\infty)$, is finite, positive, jointly continuous and convex in $x$ for fixed $u$. Its own cost is finite and positive since the centered support is positive and the measured boundary is compact with finite positive surface area. If $p$ is an exposed base, the balance condition and a strict exposing functional imply $w_z=0$ at every $z\ne p$, so $T(P,p)=1$.
