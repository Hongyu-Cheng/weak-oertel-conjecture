## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body and let $\Xi$ be bounded Borel on $\partial K$. The inequality $\int_{\partial K}\Xi\Phi\,ds\le0$ for every finite continuous convex $\Phi:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ holds exactly when $\Xi=0$ arclength-almost everywhere off the relative interiors of the maximal nondegenerate line facets, and on each such facet $F$ the signed measure $\Xi\,ds$ has zero mass, zero arclength-coordinate first moment, and $\int_F(s-t)_+\Xi(s)\,ds\le0$ for every $0<t<\ell_F$, where $s\in[0,\ell_F]$ parametrizes $F$.

## Proof

Affine tests of both signs give equal mass and first moments to $\mu=\Xi_+\,ds$ and $\nu=\Xi_-\,ds$. Their compact supports and the convex test inequality allow ac53fe19fef9ec25, giving a martingale coupling with these full marginals. The two measures are mutually singular.

Every point of $\partial K$ outside the open maximal facets is extreme. Indeed, a nontrivial segment in $K$ with that point in its relative interior lies in a supporting line at the point. A probability on $K$ with mean at an extreme point is its point mass: otherwise a linear coordinate nonconstant under that probability splits it into two sets of positive mass with different conditional means in $K$. Thus all rows starting outside open facets stay fixed. Mutual singularity rules out such source mass. For a row starting in an open facet, equality in its supporting affine function forces the row's target to stay in its closed facet. The open facets are disjoint intervals of positive arclength in a finite-perimeter boundary, so there are at most countably many. Their endpoints have zero target measure. It follows that each open facet transports only to itself and no target mass remains outside the open facets. On each facet the retained coupling preserves mass and first moment, and conditional Jensen gives the hinge inequalities.

Conversely, each continuous convex function on a closed segment is uniformly approximated by its convex piecewise-linear interpolants, each the sum of an affine function and nonnegative multiples of the displayed hinges. The facet conditions therefore give the integral inequality for the restriction of every $\Phi$ to every facet. Summing is justified by the finite boundary variation and boundedness of $\Phi$ on $K$. The density vanishes on the remaining boundary up to a null set.
