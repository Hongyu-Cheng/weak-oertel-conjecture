## Statement

Use centered intervals $[-1,1]$ at $z=-1,0,1,2$ and the compatible max path of radii $1-e(4,2,2,4)_z$, whose original normalized maximum is $3/8$. At $e=0$ the four half-tilt caps
$$
\pm x-z/2\ge0,\qquad \pm x+(z-1)/2\ge0
$$
each attain the original raw depth $3$ at their corresponding inner center and their pairs cover the same whole sampled planes as the tilt-one pairs $\pm x-z\ge0$, $\pm x+(z-1)\ge0$. For $0<e<1/8$ each half-tilt cap has mass $3-8e$. Its first normalized payment is $-1$, while the tilt-one payment is $1$ on the same path and the actual global depth strictly decreases. The common overlap field $(0,1,1,0)$ has first total cost $4$, first tilt-one cap loss $1$, and first half-tilt cap loss $2$. Replacing sum by max therefore increases the tilt-one payment by $1/2$ and decreases the half-tilt payment by $1/2$.

## Proof

The predecessor pk-w36-abnormal-two-witness-max-and-sum applies to the stated intervals and gives the true depths, the total loss $24e$ for the max path, and the tilt-one payment. The two half-tilt paired margin sums are $-z$ and $z-1$, so the first and second pairs cover respectively all planes at $z\le0$ and $z\ge1$, just as the tilt-one pairs. For $x-z/2\ge0$ the four thresholds are $-1/2,0,1/2,1$. For $0<e<1/8$, the outer radius $R=1-4e$ is greater than $1/2$, the inner radius is $r=1-2e>1/2$, and the captured lengths are
$$
R+1/2,\quad r,\quad r-1/2,\quad 0.
$$
At $e=0$ these are $3/2,1,1/2,0$, so the cap contains the base-$0$ center and has its genuine depth three there. The sum along the path is $3-8e$. Height reflection and base reversal give these conclusions for the other three caps. The first normalized payment is $8-(3/8)24=-1$. In fact their normalized masses are greater than $3/8$ for every $e$ in the stated range, whereas the true normalized maximum decreases as in the predecessor.

The pointwise minimum of the two individual speed arrays is $(0,1,1,0)$, and the max removes exactly this array from the sum. It accounts for a length cost $2+2=4$ from the two inner fibers. The tilt-one cap captures a half of one moving inner interval and zero length from the other one, so this overlap contributes cap loss one. For a half-tilt cap both moving inner intervals lose length at rate one in its captured pieces, so the overlap contributes cap loss two. These statements can also be read from the exact cap formulas for the sum path: its inner radius is $1-3e$ in place of $1-2e$ with identical outer radii, and all the listed clipping regimes still hold for $e<1/8$. Subtracting the overlap changes payment by the negative of its signed contribution, which is $-(1-(3/8)4)=1/2$ or $-(2-(3/8)4)=-1/2$ respectively.
