## Proof

For a uniform point of a triangle the barycentric second moments are $1/6$ on the diagonal and $1/12$ off the diagonal, by integration on the standard triangle. Since the vertex sum is zero, the covariance equals one twelfth of the sum of the vertex outer products. It is
$$
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/4\\-1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
This verifies covariance unit length and the claimed quadratic, whose volume mean is $\sum_j\rho_j u_j^\mathsf{T}\Sigma u_j=1$. The three normals sum to zero. Their halfspaces are the centroid vertex caps: each affine vertex coordinate equals $1/3$ on its section plane. Each captures $4/9$ of the area by similarity, and all through-centroid cuts have fraction at least $4/9$ by ordinary Grünbaum. At every other mark one barycentric coordinate exceeds $1/3$, and its through-mark vertex cap has smaller fraction. The three through-centroid caps cover $K$ because one barycentric coordinate is at least $1/3$ at every point.

Every edge, in its affine parameter $0\le v\le1$, has cone probability $dv/3$. Substitution of its endpoint coordinates gives $Q=12v^2-12v+4$ on each edge. Its integral over the three full edges is $2$. Each $H_j$ intersects its two incident edges in the last two thirds when directed toward its vertex and misses the third. Thus $\nu_V(H_j)=4/9$ and the captured quadratic integral is $(2/3)\int_{1/3}^1(12v^2-12v+4)\,dv=64/81$. Accordingly the height measure of $q_H+Q/(2V)$ is $(1+Q)\nu_V$. The stated inequality follows from $64/81<2(4/9)$, and at $t=1$ the captured fraction is $100/243$.
