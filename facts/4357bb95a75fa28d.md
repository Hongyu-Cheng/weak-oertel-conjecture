## Statement

Fix $D\ge2$, $\tau>0$, and $\eta>0$. For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^D$ of volume $V$ covered by $D+1$ proper closed halfspaces, let $A_i$ be their cap masses, $\gamma=(D/(D+1))^D$, and $E=\sum_i A_i-V-(D+1-1/\gamma)\min_i A_i$. There exists $\varepsilon(D,\tau,\eta)>0$ such that $\min_i A_i\ge\tau V$ and $E\le\varepsilon V$ imply the following for every affine normalization carrying an inscribed maximum-volume simplex of $K$ to $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_D)$.

The normalized body $K^*$ satisfies $d_H(K^*,T)<\eta$ and $|A_i/V-\gamma|<\eta$ for all $i$. Write the transformed halfspaces as $\{u_i\cdot x\ge b_i\}$ with unit $u_i$ and replace any $b_i<-(D+1)$ by $-(D+1)$. Up to permutation, the resulting unit-normal/offset pairs are within Euclidean distance $\eta$ of the canonical pairs of $T$.

## Proof

A maximal inscribed simplex exists by compactness of vertex tuples and is nondegenerate. After normalization, replacing the $i$-th vertex by $x\in K^*$ gives a simplex of volume $|\lambda_i(x)|/D!$, where $\lambda_i$ is its barycentric coordinate relative to $T$. Hence $T\subseteq K^*\subseteq\{|\lambda_i|\le1\text{ for all }i\}$, in particular $K^*\subseteq[-1,1]^D$. The class with these inclusions and maximum inscribed simplex volume $1/D!$ is Hausdorff compact: bounded support functions are equicontinuous and have uniformly convergent subsequences, and the inclusions and maximal-volume constraint persist by approximating each vertex in the limit by points in the approximants. It has volume bounded below by $1/D!$.

A positive cap has $b_i\le\sqrt D<D+1$. Replacing smaller offsets below $-(D+1)$ preserves the full trace on every member of the class. Thus all cap parameters lie in a fixed compact product of unit spheres and intervals. Along Hausdorff body and cap-parameter convergence, the indicators of the bodies and capped bodies converge off the null limiting body boundary and cap hyperplanes. For the body indicator at interior points, an interior ball and uniform support-function convergence give eventual membership. Dominated convergence in the common box gives continuity of volumes and cap fractions. The covering condition is closed, because each point of the limiting body is approximated by points in the old bodies and a subsequence uses one fixed covering cap. The lower cap-fraction condition is closed by continuity. Together these conditions give a compact parameter class.

The discrepancy is the maximum of the Hausdorff distance, the $D+1$ cap-fraction deviations, and the least over permutations of the maximum distance to the canonical pairs. The latter pairs are $(e_i,1/(D+1))$ for $1\le i\le D$ and $(-\mathbf 1/\sqrt D,-\sqrt D/(D+1))$. All terms are continuous. The subset of discrepancy at least $\eta$ is compact. On the parameter class, $E/V$ is continuous and nonnegative. At a zero, positive minimal cap fraction permits the equality theorem, giving a simplex and its canonical cap pairs. This simplex contains $T$ and has volume $1/D!$, since it is itself its own largest inscribed simplex. Hence it equals $T$ and the discrepancy is zero. If the bad subset is nonempty, $E/V$ has a strictly positive attained minimum there, and half that minimum serves as $\varepsilon$. If it is empty, use $\varepsilon=1$. Every choice of a largest inscribed simplex produces a member of the same normalized class, so the argument covers all choices.
