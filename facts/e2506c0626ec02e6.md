## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), put \(K=2^n\), \(m=2K-1\), and
$$
Q_n=\lbrace x\in[-1,1]^n:x_i-x_j\le1\text{ for all }i,j\rbrace,
\qquad
A_n=Q_n\cap\mathbb Z^n
=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\cup\bigl(-\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\bigr).
$$
For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), retain the original weights, normalizer, and law
$$
w_x=e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad Z=\sum_{x\in A_n}e^{\ell\cdot x},
\qquad p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}Z\quad(x\in A_n),
$$
with \(p_x=0\) off \(A_n\). The support and its geometry are those of accepted fact 94d565fe7e8ad9a1, including the same support when \(n=1\), which is checked below.

Use exactly the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and canonical reflected quantities of accepted facts ce8cccfdbee6f1cc and 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
 \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},
&b(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,
&\chi(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j).
\end{aligned}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in \(H_p\), and zero occurs once. For the full row \(R_j=\lbrace x\in A_n:2j-x\in A_n\rbrace\), define, with precisely the normalization in 445a9e668ee1a705,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},
&Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,
&q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_jq_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
These auxiliary laws do not replace \(p\). Set \(A_0=K^2\sum_xp_x^3\), which is not the canonical ratio \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\), and use natural logarithms. Then
$$
A_0<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0\le\nu\le16384\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$

The following exact formulas hold for every field. With
$$
V_3=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+e^{3\ell_i})
     +\prod_{i=1}^n(1+e^{-3\ell_i})-1,

$$
one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+e^{\ell_i})
     +\prod_{i=1}^n(1+e^{-\ell_i})-1,\\
A_0&=\frac{K^2V_3}{Z^3},
&K^2b(p)&=\frac{K^2(V_3-1+m^2)}{Z^3},
&q_0&=\frac{m^2}{V_3-1+m^2},\\
V_0&=\frac{K}{2m}
 \left[\sum_i\ell_i^2+\left(\sum_i\ell_i\right)^2\right],
&\nu&=q_0V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Every nonzero row is a singleton. The row at zero is all of \(A_n\), its law \(\kappa_0\) is uniform on these \(m\) points, and \(Y_0(x)=\ell\cdot x\).

There is also an actual heterogeneous-product consequence. Let \(L\ge1\), choose arbitrary dimensions \(n_i\ge1\) and arbitrary fields \(\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\), and form the entire original laws \(p_i\) above with their original normalizers \(Z_i\). For
$$
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i,
\qquad c=\frac14e^{-16384},
$$
the original ambient depth in \(\mathbb R^N\) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\,4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{3}
$$
There is no restriction on \(L\), on the dimensions or fields, or on the maximum of the multiplicities \(2^{n_i}-1\). Formula (3) concerns this family and its products only.

## Proof

We first verify (2), retaining all original normalizers. The integer census of \(Q_n\) is immediate: coordinates lie in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\), and the difference inequalities forbid a coordinate (1) and a coordinate \(-1\) at the same point. Thus an integer point is Boolean or negative Boolean; conversely all such points lie in \(Q_n\). The two cubes intersect only at zero, so their union has \(m=2K-1\) points. This also proves the \(n=1\) statement directly. The body is convex, compact, and full-dimensional, since it contains a neighborhood of zero. These are the same objects as in 94d565fe7e8ad9a1.

The zero parity class consists of zero; every other parity class is the antipodal pair \(c,-c\) for a nonzero Boolean vector \(c\). Distinct reflected endpoints have the same parity and hence are such a pair, with midpoint zero. Therefore \(R_0=A_n\) and \(R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\) for \(j\ne0\), as in 94d565fe7e8ad9a1. Affineness gives \(\sqrt{p_xp_{-x}}=1/Z\). Consequently
$$
H_p(0)=\frac mZ,\qquad H_p(j)=p_j\ (j\ne0),
\qquad b(p)=\frac{V_3-1+m^2}{Z^3}.
$$
Multiplication of the finite Boolean sums proves both product formulas in (2), and \(\sum_xp_x^3=V_3/Z^3\). At zero each of the \(m\) geometric weights is \(1/Z\), so \(\kappa_0\) is uniform and its score is \(\ell\cdot x\). All singleton-row scores vanish. For uniform Boolean \(C\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), independence gives
$$
\mathbb E(\ell\cdot C)^2
=\frac14\left[\sum_i\ell_i^2+\left(\sum_i\ell_i\right)^2\right].
$$
The two antipodal Boolean sums coincide and their common zero term vanishes. This proves the \(V_0,q_0,\nu\) formulas in (2). These are the canonical objects of 445a9e668ee1a705, with the identity map on \(p,Z,R,H,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu\).

We next prove the quantitative field restriction supplied by the exact active condition. Set
$$
t_i=\ell_i/2,\qquad s=\sum_it_i,
\qquad (u_1,\ldots,u_{n+1})=(t_1,\ldots,t_n,-s),
$$
and define
$$
f(u)=\log(1+3\tanh^2u),\qquad h(u)=\log\cosh u,
\qquad U=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}h(u_j),
\qquad W=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}u_j^2.
$$
The list \(u_j\) has sum zero. Put \(B=2Ke^U\) and
$$
B_3=2K\left(\prod_i\cosh(3t_i)\right)\cosh(3s).
$$
The exact original sums satisfy \(Z=B-1\) and \(V_3=B_3-1\). Moreover \(B_3\ge B>1\). The identity \(\cosh(3u)/\cosh^3u=1+3\tanh^2u\) gives
$$
\frac{K^2B_3}{B^3}
=\frac14\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}f(u_j)\right).
$$
Since \(B_3\ge B\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0
&=\frac{K^2B_3}{B^3}
 \frac{1-1/B_3}{(1-1/B)^3}\\
&\ge\frac{K^2B_3}{B^3}(1-1/B)^{-2}
>\frac14\exp\left(\sum_jf(u_j)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the exact hypothesis \(A_0<1\), without any asymptotic replacement, implies
$$
\sum_jf(u_j)<\log4.
\tag{4}
$$

For \( |u|\le1\), two elementary estimates will be used:
$$
f(u)\ge2h(u),\qquad f(u)\ge\frac34u^2.
\tag{5}
$$
For the first, write \(y=\cosh^2u\in[1,3]\); then \(4-3/y\ge y\), since \((y-1)(y-3)\le0\). For the second, concavity of \(\tanh\) on \([0,1]\) and \(\tanh1>3/4\) give \(\tanh|u|\ge3|u|/4\). Concavity of \(v\mapsto\log(1+3v)\) gives \(\log(1+3v)\ge v\log4\) on \([0,1]\), and \(\log4>4/3\), proving (5). The elementary numerical inequalities here follow, for example, from the exponential series: \(e^2>7\), \(\cosh1<\sqrt3\), and \(2/3<\log2<3/4\).

Let \(M=\max_j|u_j|\). If \(M\le1\), (4)--(5) give \(U<\log2\) and \(W<(8/3)\log2<2\). Suppose instead \(M\ge1\), choose one maximizing index, and set
$$
\delta=\log4-f(M)>0.
$$
Since \(f(1)>\log2\), one has \(\delta<\log2\). The remaining \(n\) entries have total \(f\)-value less than \(\delta\), so each has absolute value less than one. Apply (5) to them. The zero-sum identity and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
$$
U<h(M)+\frac\delta2,\qquad
M^2\le n\sum_{j\ne j_{\max}}u_j^2<\frac{4n}3\delta,
\qquad W<\frac43(n+1)\delta<n+1.
\tag{6}
$$
Together with the \(M\le1\) case, this proves \(W<n+1\) for every \(n\ge1\).

There is a useful sharper bound for \(U\). In the \(M\ge1\) case put \(y=\cosh^2M>9/4\) and \(\alpha=3/(4y)<1/3\). Then \(\delta=-\log(1-\alpha)\). The power series gives
$$
\delta\le\alpha+\frac{\alpha^2}{2(1-\alpha)}
\le\frac54\alpha,
\qquad
y\delta e^\delta
\le\frac{15}{16(1-\alpha)}\le\frac{45}{32}.
$$
Using (6), and \(M\ge1\), consequently gives
$$
e^{2U}<ye^\delta\le\frac{45}{32\delta}
<\frac{15n}{8M^2}\le\frac{15n}{8}<2n.
$$
In the \(M\le1\) case, \(e^{2U}<4\le2n\) for \(n\ge2\). Therefore
$$
U\le\frac12\log(2n)\quad(n\ge2),
\qquad W<n+1\quad(n\ge1).
\tag{7}
$$

Write \(g=\log(K^2b(p))\). Since \(Z=2Ke^U-1<2Ke^U\), \(V_3-1+m^2>m^2\), and \(m\ge3K/2\), (2) and (7) give
$$
g>n\log2+\log(9/32)-\frac32\log(2n)
\quad(n\ge2).
\tag{8}
$$
For all real \(n\ge8\), the right side of (8) is at least \(n\log2/64\). To verify this explicitly, subtract \(n\log2/64\). At \(n=8\) the result is \(\log9-(25/8)\log2>0\), because \(9^8=43046721>33554432=2^{25}\). Its derivative is \((63/64)\log2-3/(2n)>0\) for \(n\ge8\). Thus
$$
g\ge\frac{n\log2}{64}\quad(n\ge8).
\tag{9}
$$
From (2), \(V_0=(2K/m)W\le(4/3)W\), and \(q_0\le1\). Hence
$$
\nu\le\frac43(n+1).
\tag{10}
$$
For \(n\ge8\), (9)--(10) give \(\nu\le(96/\log2)g<144g\).

For completeness, the finitely many smaller dimensions can be handled by an exact uniform gap, without numerical optimization or a compactness argument. The log-affine parity identity in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc gives \(\chi(p)=\sum_\eta r_\eta^2\), where \(r_\eta\) are the original parity-class masses. Here \(r_0=p_0\); each nonzero parity \(\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\) has mass \(p_0(e^{\ell\cdot\eta}+e^{-\ell\cdot\eta})\ge2p_0\), so \(0<p_0\le1/m\). Cauchy--Schwarz on those \(K-1\) classes gives
$$
\chi(p)\ge p_0^2+\frac{(1-p_0)^2}{K-1}
\ge\frac{4K-3}{m^2}.
$$
The last scalar function of \(p_0\) is decreasing for \(p_0\le1/m<1/K\), so its minimum on the required interval occurs at \(p_0=1/m\). Also \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\), by Jensen under the original law. Consequently, for every field,
$$
g\ge2\log\left(1+\frac{K-1}{m^2}\right)
\ge\frac{K-1}{m^2}\ge\frac1{8K}>0.
\tag{11}
$$
The middle inequality uses \(\log(1+x)\ge x/2\) for \(0\le x\le1\); the last follows from \(8K(K-1)\ge(2K-1)^2\) for \(K\ge2\). This also verifies that every logarithm on the right of (1) is positive. For \(1\le n\le7\), (10)--(11) imply
$$
\frac\nu g\le\frac{32}3(n+1)K
\le\frac{32768}3<16384.
$$
Combining the two dimension ranges proves (1).

We finish by proving (3), including its actual ambient-depth conversion. In factor \(i\), write \(K_i=2^{n_i}\) and \(A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\). If \(A_{0,i}<1\), retain its full rows; if \(A_{0,i}\ge1\), retain only the central point in every row. Denote these symmetric auxiliary rows by \(\widehat R_{i,j}\), and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}
 \sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat b_i&=\sum_jp_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=
 \frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_i(j)},
&\widehat q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2}{\widehat b_i}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Use the same score \(Y_{i,j}(x)=\frac12\log(p_{i,x}/p_{i,2j-x})\), and let \(\widehat V_{i,j}=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2\), \(\widehat\nu_i=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j}\), and \(g_i=\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i)\). For an active factor these are the full canonical objects, so (1) gives \(\widehat\nu_i\le16384g_i\). For an inactive factor, \(\widehat H_i(j)=p_{i,j}\), \(\widehat\nu_i=0\), and \(g_i=\log A_{0,i}\ge0\). Thus, with \(G=\sum_i g_i\),
$$
G\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\widehat\nu_i\le16384G.
\tag{12}
$$
None of these selections changes any original probability or \(Z_i\).

For a supported product target \(J=(j_i)\), the auxiliary row is \(\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}\), its geometric mass is \(\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i)\), and its conditional law is \(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}\). The score \(Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i)\) has conditional mean zero and conditional second moment \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}\). These identities follow from reflection symmetry and factorization, as in 445a9e668ee1a705. In particular, on this row the exact likelihood identity is
$$
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)},
\qquad P_X=\frac{\prod_i e^{\ell_i\cdot x_i}}{\prod_i Z_i}.
$$
Every closed halfspace in the original \(\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each distinct reflected pair in this auxiliary row and contains \(J\) itself. Counting both orientations and then dividing by two, using the likelihood identity, Jensen, and Cauchy--Schwarz, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}
 \min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
 \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The central atom is counted only to half its available mass, so the zero-inclusive convention is valid also for singleton rows. Squaring and averaging over the entire original \(P\), then using the auxiliary product center law \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here the factorization uses exactly \(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b_i)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}\). By (12) and \(\prod_iK_i^2=4^N\),
$$
4^NM_2(P)\ge\frac14\exp\bigl(G-2\sqrt{16384G}\bigr)
\ge\frac14e^{-16384},
$$
since \(G-2\sqrt{CG}=(\sqrt G-\sqrt C)^2-C\ge-C\). Finally Jensen under the original law \(P\) gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving (3). Every halfspace in this argument remains an original ambient containing closed halfspace. The proved field budget (4), and hence (1) and (3), used the special antipodal Boolean product formulas; it supplies no general \(H_q\) statement.
