## Statement

Use the locked problem's fibers, mixed-integer measure, and depth: for integers \(n,d\ge1\) and a convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), let \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(W(C)=\sum_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero. For \(\mathbf y\in S\), let \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. For \(W(C)>0\), define the normalized maximum depth by \(\mathcal D(C)=W(C)^{-1}\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)\). The supremum convention suffices throughout.

Fix \(n\ge1\) and a nonempty finite set \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) with positive weights \(p_{\mathbf z}\), \(\mathbf z\in D\), summing to one. Extend \(p\) by zero outside \(D\). Assume that \(p\) is the entire normalized lattice-volume table of at least one convex body with integer dimension \(n\) and positive continuous dimension. Thus \(D\) is exactly the set \(D_+\) of positive fibers in accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8`, and \(p_{\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}/W(C)\). In accepted fact `b563a1eb51a20cd2`, the same positive support is called \(A\).

All logarithms are natural. Define \(I\) on \(D\) and \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)\) for all \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(\mathbf j)
&=-\log\inf_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n}
  \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}
       e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)},\\
H_{\mathbf j}(p)
&=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}
  \sqrt{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set \(I_*=\min_{\mathbf j\in D}I(\mathbf j)\), \(E(p)=e^{-I_*}\), and \(H_*(p)=\max_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}H_{\mathbf j}(p)\). The Hellinger sum counts both orientations and includes zero displacement. If \(H_{\mathbf j}(w)\) and \(H_*(w)\) are defined by the identical formulas for unnormalized fiber weights, then \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)=H_{\mathbf j}(w)/W(C)\) and \(H_*(p)=H_*(w)/W(C)\). These are reflected Hellinger sums, not halfspace depths.

For every \(\mathbf j\in D\), exponential and entropy definitions agree exactly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(\mathbf j)
&=\sup_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n}
 \left[\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf j
 -\log\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z}\right]\\
&=\min_{\substack{\eta_{\mathbf z}\ge0\ (\mathbf z\in D),\;
                  \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\eta_{\mathbf z}=1\\
                  \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\eta_{\mathbf z}\mathbf z=\mathbf j}}
 \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\eta_{\mathbf z}
       \log\frac{\eta_{\mathbf z}}{p_{\mathbf z}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where a summand with \(\eta_{\mathbf z}=0\) is zero. In particular, \(0\le I_*\le-\log H_*(p)\le n\log2\), or equivalently \(1\ge E(p)\ge H_*(p)\ge2^{-n}\).

Let \(C_i\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d_i}\), \(i\ge1\), be any sequence of convex bodies, where every \(d_i\ge1\), with \(W_i:=W(C_i)>0\), and suppose their entire normalized tables equal \(p\). Thus \(\operatorname{vol}_{d_i}(C_{i,\mathbf z})=W_i p_{\mathbf z}\) at every integer base, including zeros. Write \(C^{[k]}=C_1\times\cdots\times C_k\), with its coordinates regrouped into \(kn\) integer coordinates and \(d_1+\cdots+d_k\) continuous coordinates, and let \(S^{[k]}\) be its mixed-integer set. Its mixed volume is \(\prod_{i=1}^k W_i\).

There are a choice of \(\mathbf j\in D\), a probability table \(r\) supported on \(D\), and constants \(\kappa\ge3\), \(\sigma^2\ge0\), all depending only on \(p\) and its lattice support, with the following property. Define \(\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf z}=\mathfrak c(C_{i,\mathbf z})\) to be the centroid of the positive fiber \(C_{i,\mathbf z}\), for \(\mathbf z\in D\), and select once for each factor
\(\mathbf m_i=(\mathbf j,\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_{\mathbf z}\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf z})\in C_i\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{d_i})\).
These points are chosen before the factor count or any halfspace. For every \(k\ge1\),

\[
\frac{\exp\!\left(-kI_*-\sqrt{8e\kappa\sigma^2k}\right)}{8e\kappa}
\le
\frac{h_{S^{[k]}}((\mathbf m_1,\ldots,\mathbf m_k))}
     {\prod_{i=1}^kW_i}
\le \mathcal D(C^{[k]})
\le e^{-kI_*}.
\]

Consequently \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal D(C^{[k]})^{1/k}=E(p)\ge2^{-n}\).

There is a constant \(c(p)>0\), depending only on the fixed normalized table and its lattice support, such that \(\mathcal D(C^{[k]})\ge c(p)2^{-kn}\) for every \(k\ge1\), uniformly over the bodies and dimensions above. One admissible choice is \(c(p)=e^{-1}\) when \(\max_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\ge2^{-n}\). Otherwise \(\delta:=n\log2-I_*>0\), and one may take
\(c(p)=(8e\kappa)^{-1}\exp(-2e\kappa\sigma^2/\delta)\).

There also exist points \(\mathbf m_i^0\) selected once from each factor and an integer \(k_0(p)\ge1\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf m_1^0,\ldots,\mathbf m_k^0)\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_{d_1+\cdots+d_k}(S^{[k]}\cap H)
\ge
\gamma_{d_1+\cdots+d_k}\,2^{-kn}\prod_{i=1}^kW_i
\qquad(k\ge k_0(p)),
\quad
\gamma_m=\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m.
\]

If \(\max p_{\mathbf z}\ge2^{-n}\), one can take \(k_0(p)=1\) and the centroids of a fixed heavy fiber. Otherwise one can take \(\mathbf m_i^0=\mathbf m_i\) and
\(k_0(p)=\left\lceil\max\{1,32e\kappa\sigma^2/\delta^2,2\log(8e\kappa)/\delta\}\right\rceil\).
In particular, for a fixed body \(C_i=C\), both the quantitative rate bound and the eventual sharp bound use repetitions of a single one-copy point, respectively \(\mathbf m^{\times k}\) and \((\mathbf m^0)^{\times k}\).

Finally, fix finitely many normalized tables \(p^{(1)},\ldots,p^{(s)}\) satisfying the same realization hypothesis, with possibly different integer dimensions \(n_1,\ldots,n_s\). There exists \(c_{\mathrm{fin}}>0\), depending only on these tables and their lattice supports, such that every nonempty finite product of bodies whose normalized tables belong to this collection has normalized depth at least \(c_{\mathrm{fin}}2^{-N}\), where \(N\) is the sum of the factors' integer dimensions. The factors' continuous dimensions and fiber shapes may vary freely.

## Proof

First establish the finite-support geometry. If \(\mathbf j\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), write \(\mathbf j=\sum_{t=1}^a\lambda_t\mathbf z_t\) with \(\mathbf z_t\in D\), \(\lambda_t>0\), and \(\sum_t\lambda_t=1\). In a realizing body of continuous dimension \(d\), choose \(\mathbf x_t\in C_{\mathbf z_t}\) for \(t\ge2\). Convexity gives \(\lambda_1 C_{\mathbf z_1}+\sum_{t=2}^a\lambda_t\mathbf x_t\subseteq C_{\mathbf j}\). Its volume is \(\lambda_1^d w_{\mathbf z_1}>0\), so \(\mathbf j\in D\). This proves \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), including supports of smaller affine dimension.

The weighted lattice centerpoint statement proved in accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8` applies to these weights: there is \(\mathbf j_0\in D\) such that \(\tau(\mathbf j_0)\ge2^{-n}\), where
\(\tau(\mathbf j)=\inf_{Q\ni\mathbf j}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap Q}p_{\mathbf z}\)
and \(Q\) ranges over proper closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^n\). We use this centerpoint conclusion, and later the continuous Grünbaum inequality from that same accepted fact; no affine selector is assumed here.

For \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\), the halfspace \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\ge0\) contains \(\mathbf j\), and the indicator of this inequality is at most \(e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}\). Therefore \(\tau(\mathbf j)\le\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}\). For \(\mathbf a=\mathbf0\), the same numerical inequality is \(\tau(\mathbf j)\le1\), requiring no zero-normal halfspace. Taking the infimum gives \(\tau(\mathbf j)\le e^{-I(\mathbf j)}\). The defining exponential infimum belongs to \([p_{\mathbf j},1]\), so \(0\le I(\mathbf j)<\infty\). At the centerpoint this proves \(I_*\le n\log2\).

We next construct the minimizing tilt at an arbitrary \(\mathbf j\in D\), with no interior assumption in the ambient space. Let \(B=\operatorname{conv}(D)\), let \(F\) be its smallest face containing \(\mathbf j\), and put \(V_F=\operatorname{span}(F-F)\). Then \(F=\operatorname{conv}(D\cap F)\), \(\mathbf j\in\operatorname{relint}(F)\), and there is a vector \(\mathbf b\) such that \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)=0\) on \(F\) and \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)<0\) at every \(\mathbf z\in D\setminus F\). These elementary polytope assertions also hold when \(B\) has smaller affine dimension: work in \(\operatorname{aff}(B)\), take a finite supporting facet description, and intersect the facets tight at \(\mathbf j\). The remaining inequalities are strict at \(\mathbf j\), so it is relatively interior in that intersection. Summing the outward normals of the tight inequalities exposes exactly this intersection, because every summand is nonpositive on \(B\), and equality in their sum requires equality in every tight inequality. Extend that normal to the ambient space. If \(F=B\), use \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), with no outside points to suppress.

On \(V_F\), consider \(Z_F(\mathbf a)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}\). If \(V_F\ne\{\mathbf0\}\), relative interior gives \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(F\) contains the relative closed Euclidean ball of radius \(\varepsilon\) centered at \(\mathbf j\). For every nonzero \(\mathbf a\in V_F\), some \(\mathbf z\in D\cap F\) consequently has \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\ge\varepsilon\|\mathbf a\|\), since a linear functional's maximum on a finite convex hull is its maximum on the generating set. Thus \(Z_F(\mathbf a)\ge(\min_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}p_{\mathbf z})e^{\varepsilon\|\mathbf a\|}\). The continuous function \(Z_F\) is coercive and has a minimizer \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\in V_F\). If \(V_F=\{\mathbf0\}\), take \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}=\mathbf0\).

At a minimizer, differentiating in every direction of \(V_F\) gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)=\mathbf0\).
Indeed the displayed vector belongs to \(V_F\), so vanishing of all directional derivatives makes the vector zero; in dimension zero it is already zero. Define

\[
r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}
=\frac{p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}}
       {Z_F(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j})}
\quad(\mathbf z\in D\cap F),
\qquad
r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}=0\quad(\mathbf z\in D\setminus F).
\]

These probabilities are strictly positive on \(D\cap F\) and have mean \(\mathbf j\). Every full exponential sum is at least its sum on \(F\), whose value is at least the minimum of \(Z_F\); the latter assertion follows by projecting its exponent vector onto \(V_F\). Conversely, substitute \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}+t\mathbf b\) into the full sum. As \(t\to\infty\), all terms outside \(F\) tend to zero, and the terms on \(F\) remain unchanged. Hence the full infimum is exactly \(Z_F(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j})=e^{-I(\mathbf j)}\), although it need not be attained by a finite ambient exponent. On the positive support of \(r^{(\mathbf j)}\), we have the exact identity
\(\log(r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}/p_{\mathbf z})=I(\mathbf j)+\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\).

This also proves entropy duality without an assumption of ambient attainment. If a probability table \(\eta\) on \(D\) has mean \(\mathbf j\), applying the exposing functional \(\mathbf b\) shows that \(\eta\) assigns zero mass to \(D\setminus F\): its mean score is zero, while each outside score is strictly negative and each score on \(F\) is zero. For \(F=B\) this support assertion is automatic. Therefore

\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\eta_{\mathbf z}
   \log\frac{\eta_{\mathbf z}}{p_{\mathbf z}}
=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}\eta_{\mathbf z}
   \log\frac{\eta_{\mathbf z}}{r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}}
+I(\mathbf j).
\]

The last sum is nonnegative. For completeness, \(\log x\le x-1\) gives
\(\sum_{\eta_{\mathbf z}>0}\eta_{\mathbf z}\log(r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}/\eta_{\mathbf z})\le\sum_{\eta_{\mathbf z}>0}r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}-1\le0\).
Taking \(\eta=r^{(\mathbf j)}\) makes the sum zero, so the entropy minimum is attained with value \(I(\mathbf j)\). The supremum formula in the statement follows algebraically from the positive exponential infimum. This proves both descriptions, including vertex and other boundary minimizers.

We prove the Hellinger comparison next. Accepted fact `b563a1eb51a20cd2` proves that the real fiber-volume function of an actual convex body, with zeros retained, is globally log-concave. Applied to any realizing table and divided by its total volume, its midpoint inequality says \(\sqrt{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}\le p_{\mathbf j}\). Thus a positive reflected pair has \(\mathbf j\in D\); in particular a positive \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)\) can occur only at one of the finitely many centers in \(D\). Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the reflected square-root sequences gives \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)\le1\), and \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)\ge p_{\mathbf j}\) on \(D\), so \(H_*(p)\in(0,1]\) is attained.

Counting ordered pairs with the same coordinatewise parity by their integer midpoint and displacement gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{-n}
&\le \sum_{\mathbf b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}
 \left(\sum_{\mathbf z\equiv\mathbf b\; (2)}p_{\mathbf z}\right)^2\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf j,\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}
        p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\\
&\le\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}p_{\mathbf j}H_{\mathbf j}(p)
\le H_*(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz for the \(2^n\) parity masses, which sum to one. The equality uses the bijection \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\leftrightarrow((\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2,(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)/2)\) for ordered same-parity pairs, including repeated endpoints. The next inequality multiplies each midpoint inequality by its nonnegative square-root term.

For a center \(\mathbf j\) attaining \(H_*(p)\), set \(r^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathbf z}=\sqrt{p_{\mathbf z}p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf z}}/H_*(p)\). This probability table is symmetric about \(\mathbf j\), hence has mean \(\mathbf j\), and its positive support consists of positive reflected endpoint pairs. On that support,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathbf z}
 \log\frac{r^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathbf z}}{p_{\mathbf z}}
&=-\log H_*(p)
 +\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z:r^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathbf z}>0}
 r^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathbf z}
 \log\frac{p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf z}}{p_{\mathbf z}}\\
&=-\log H_*(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last finite sum cancels under \(\mathbf z\mapsto2\mathbf j-\mathbf z\). Entropy duality gives \(I_*\le I(\mathbf j)\le-\log H_*(p)\). Combining this with the parity lower bound proves the claimed information-Hellinger comparison.

For the quantitative depth lower bound choose once a minimizer \(\mathbf j\in D\) of \(I\), and retain its face, exponent \(\mathbf a:=\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\), and probabilities \(r_{\mathbf z}:=r^{(\mathbf j)}_{\mathbf z}\). Define

\[
r_{\min}=\min_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}r_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
\kappa=\max\{3,r_{\min}^{-1}\},
\qquad
\sigma^2=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}
 r_{\mathbf z}\bigl[\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\bigr]^2.
\]

All these choices depend only on the fixed table. The score in the last expression has mean zero, because the tilted mean is \(\mathbf j\).

Let \(\mathbb P_i\) be normalized mixed-integer volume on \(C_i\), and define \(\mathbb Q_i\) by choosing the base with probabilities \(r\) and retaining the uniform conditional distribution on the actual positive fiber \(C_{i,\mathbf z}\). Convexity of \(C_i\) and the mean-base identity give
\(\mathbf m_i=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}r_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf z,\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf z})=(\mathbf j,\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}r_{\mathbf z}\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf z})\in C_i\).
In particular its base is an integer. These choices depend on the individual factor and the fixed table, not on \(k\) or a halfspace.

For fixed \(k\), write \(\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb Q_k\); the following moment and likelihood estimates take expectations under \(\mathbb Q\). We show that the product point has depth at least \(\beta:=1/(4e\kappa)\) under this law. A proper closed halfspace containing the point contains its parallel translate whose boundary passes through it: if its inequality is \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge t\), replace \(t\) by \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf m_1,\ldots,\mathbf m_k)\). It thus suffices to treat

\[
H_0=\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^k\left[
\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf z_i-\mathbf j)
+\mathbf v_i\cdot(\mathbf x_i-\overline{\mathbf c}_i)
\right]\ge0\right\},
\qquad
\overline{\mathbf c}_i=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}r_{\mathbf z}\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf z},
\]

with a nonzero combined ambient normal. For independent base labels \(\mathbf Z_i\) of law \(r\), put
\(R_i=\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf Z_i-\mathbf j)+\mathbf v_i\cdot(\mathbf c_{i,\mathbf Z_i}-\overline{\mathbf c}_i)\),
\(R=\sum_iR_i\), and \(V_i=\mathbb E R_i^2\). Each \(R_i\) has mean zero. If \(R_i\) takes value \(s_{\mathbf z}\) at a positive-probability label, then \(r_{\mathbf z}s_{\mathbf z}^2\le V_i\) and \(r_{\mathbf z}\ge r_{\min}\), so \(s_{\mathbf z}^2\le V_i/r_{\min}\). Consequently \(\mathbb E R_i^4\le r_{\min}^{-1}V_i^2\), including the case \(V_i=0\). Independence and centering now give

\[
\mathbb E R^4
=\sum_i\mathbb E R_i^4+6\sum_{i<t}V_iV_t
\le\kappa\left(\sum_iV_i\right)^2
=\kappa(\mathbb E R^2)^2.
\]

For \(V:=\mathbb E R^2>0\), Hölder gives \(V\le(\mathbb E|R|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E R^4)^{1/3}\). Since \(\mathbb ER=0\), \(\mathbb E R_+=\mathbb E|R|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz, followed by the fourth-moment bound, therefore gives

\[
\mathbb Q(R>0)
\ge\frac{(\mathbb E R_+)^2}{V}
=\frac{(\mathbb E|R|)^2}{4V}
\ge\frac{V^2}{4\mathbb E R^4}
\ge\frac1{4\kappa}.
\]

If \(V=0\), then \(R=0\) almost surely and \(\mathbb Q(R\ge0)=1\). Conditional on the base tuple, the continuous distribution is uniform on the positive-volume convex product fiber \(\prod_i C_{i,\mathbf Z_i}\). Fubini's theorem shows that its centroid is \((\mathbf c_{1,\mathbf Z_1},\ldots,\mathbf c_{k,\mathbf Z_k})\). Whenever \(R\ge0\), that centroid belongs to the section of \(H_0\). If at least one \(\mathbf v_i\) is nonzero, apply the continuous Grünbaum inequality recorded in accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8`, once to this entire product fiber, to obtain conditional probability at least \(\gamma_{d_1+\cdots+d_k}\ge e^{-1}\). The last inequality follows from \(\log(1+1/m)\le1/m\). If all \(\mathbf v_i\) vanish, the section contains the whole fiber whenever \(R\ge0\), giving probability one. Thus, in every case, every containing closed halfspace has product-tilted probability at least \(\beta\). The only fourth moments used were those of the finite base labels; this bound is independent of every continuous dimension and fiber shape.

On the support of \(\mathbb Q_i\), the density ratio with respect to \(\mathbb P_i\) is \(r_{\mathbf Z_i}/p_{\mathbf Z_i}=\exp(I_*+\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf Z_i-\mathbf j))\). Under \(\mathbb Q_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb Q_k\), let \(T_k=\sum_i\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf Z_i-\mathbf j)\); its mean is zero and its variance is \(k\sigma^2\). Set \(t_k=\sqrt{2k\sigma^2/\beta}=\sqrt{8e\kappa\sigma^2k}\). If \(\sigma^2>0\), Chebyshev gives \(\mathbb Q(T_k>t_k)\le\beta/2\). If \(\sigma^2=0\), use \(t_k=0\); then \(T_k=0\) almost surely and the exceptional probability is zero.

The event \(T_k\le t_k\) is fixed before the halfspace. Hence every containing closed \(H\) has \(\mathbb Q(H\cap\{T_k\le t_k\})\ge\beta/2\). On the subset where all base labels belong to \(D\cap F\), the restrictions of the original and tilted measures are mutually absolutely continuous, with inverse density \(\exp(-kI_*-T_k)\). The original law may have additional mass outside this subset, which only increases the following lower bound:

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathbb P_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb P_k)(H)
&\ge\int_{H\cap\{T_k\le t_k\}}
 e^{-kI_*-T_k}\,d(\mathbb Q_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb Q_k)\\
&\ge\frac1{8e\kappa}
       \exp\!\left(-kI_*-\sqrt{8e\kappa\sigma^2k}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Mixed volume and conditional uniform laws factor over a Cartesian product, so \(\mathbb P_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb P_k\) is exactly normalized mixed volume on \(C^{[k]}\). This proves the stated pointwise lower bound for every halfspace, with the required order of quantifiers.

For the upper bound, take any point of \(S^{[k]}\) with integer base tuple \((\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_k)\). If some \(\mathbf j_i\notin D\), lattice convexity puts it outside the compact convex set \(B=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). There is a vector \(\mathbf u\) with \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j_i)<0\) for every \(\mathbf z\in B\). For example, let \(\mathbf z_0\) be a nearest point of \(B\) to \(\mathbf j_i\) and take \(\mathbf u=\mathbf j_i-\mathbf z_0\); the nearest-point optimality inequality gives \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j_i)\le-\|\mathbf u\|^2<0\). The vertical halfspace \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z_i-\mathbf j_i)\ge0\) contains the chosen point and has mixed volume zero. This covers nonempty zero-volume fibers as well as any other base outside \(D\).

If every \(\mathbf j_i\in D\), choose arbitrary vectors \(\mathbf a_1,\ldots,\mathbf a_k\), not all zero. The vertical halfspace with inequality \(\sum_i\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf z_i-\mathbf j_i)\ge0\) contains the point, and its normalized mass is at most
\(\prod_i\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j_i)}\).
This follows from the same indicator bound and independence. For the all-zero tuple, the numerical depth bound by one is automatic, so the inequality also holds without introducing an improper normal. Taking independent infima in the finitely many factors gives the bound \(\exp(-\sum_i I(\mathbf j_i))\le e^{-kI_*}\). To justify factorization of the infimum, the product is bounded below by the product of the positive individual infima, and choosing each factor within an arbitrary positive error of its own infimum gives the reverse inequality in the limit. Taking the supremum over all mixed-integer points proves \(\mathcal D(C^{[k]})\le e^{-kI_*}\). The two bounds have the same \(k\)-th-root limit, proving the exact rate.

We now treat the possible critical prefactor. For every \(\mathbf j\in D\) that is not a vertex of \(B\), we prove the strict inequality \(\tau(\mathbf j)<e^{-I(\mathbf j)}\). Use its already constructed face \(F\), exponent \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\), and exposing normal \(\mathbf b\). Here \(V_F\) has positive dimension. If \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\ne\mathbf0\), its scores on \(D\cap F\) take both positive and negative values: they have mean zero under the strictly positive law \(r^{(\mathbf j)}\), and they cannot all vanish because \(D\cap F-\mathbf j\) spans \(V_F\) and \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\in V_F\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\). For sufficiently large finite \(t\), the normal \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}+t\mathbf b\) has strictly negative score at every point of \(D\setminus F\). On \(F\) its scores are unchanged. Its containing halfspace therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(\mathbf j)
&\le\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in D\cap F\\
 \mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\ge0}}p_{\mathbf z}\\
&<\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}
 p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}
=e^{-I(\mathbf j)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Strictness holds because each retained exponential term is at least its original weight, positive-score terms are strictly enlarged, and excluded negative-score terms still contribute positive amounts to the full exponential sum. If \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}=\mathbf0\), the last sum is \(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap F}p_{\mathbf z}\). Choose any nonzero \(\mathbf u\in V_F\). Relative interior ensures that \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)\) has both signs on \(D\cap F\). Adding a sufficiently large multiple of \(\mathbf b\) to \(\mathbf u\) excludes all of \(D\setminus F\) and at least one positive mass in \(F\), so again \(\tau(\mathbf j)<e^{-I(\mathbf j)}\). These normals are nonzero: the nonzero component in \(V_F\) is orthogonal to the exposing normal, which vanishes on \(V_F\).

At a vertex, \(F=\{\mathbf j\}\), so the tilt formula gives \(e^{-I(\mathbf j)}=p_{\mathbf j}\). Every containing halfspace includes that atom, and an exposing halfspace excludes every other point of \(D\), giving \(\tau(\mathbf j)=p_{\mathbf j}\). If \(B\) is a singleton, any proper halfspace through it gives the same equality.

Suppose \(I_*=n\log2\). At the earlier weighted lattice centerpoint,
\(2^{-n}\le\tau(\mathbf j_0)\le e^{-I(\mathbf j_0)}\le e^{-I_*}=2^{-n}\).
Equality throughout and the strict nonvertex inequality force \(\mathbf j_0\) to be a vertex with \(p_{\mathbf j_0}=2^{-n}\). Thus the critical case always has a heavy vertex; in particular \(\max p_{\mathbf z}<2^{-n}\) forces \(\delta=n\log2-I_*>0\).

Whenever \(p_{\mathbf j_h}\ge2^{-n}\), select the centroid of \(C_{i,\mathbf j_h}\) in each factor. The product of these fibers has volume \(p_{\mathbf j_h}^k\prod_iW_i\), and its centroid is the product of those selected centroids. Every containing ambient halfspace restricts to a containing proper halfspace of the product fiber, or to the whole fiber. Grünbaum therefore gives depth at least \(\gamma_{d_1+\cdots+d_k}p_{\mathbf j_h}^k\prod_iW_i\), which is at least \(e^{-1}2^{-kn}\prod_iW_i\). This proves both the stated prefactor and the sharp bound for every \(k\) in the heavy case, including the critical case.

In the absence of a heavy atom, \(\delta>0\). The quantitative rate lower bound and completion of the square give

\[
k\delta-\sqrt{8e\kappa\sigma^2k}
=\left(\sqrt{k\delta}-\sqrt{\frac{2e\kappa\sigma^2}{\delta}}\right)^2
-\frac{2e\kappa\sigma^2}{\delta}
\ge-\frac{2e\kappa\sigma^2}{\delta}.
\]

After multiplication by \(2^{kn}\), this is exactly the asserted lower bound with \(c(p)=(8e\kappa)^{-1}\exp(-2e\kappa\sigma^2/\delta)>0\). For \(k\ge k_0(p)\) specified in the statement, the two threshold inequalities give \(\sqrt{8e\kappa\sigma^2k}\le k\delta/2\) and \(\log(8e\kappa)\le k\delta/2\). Hence the same selected product point has normalized depth at least \(2^{-kn}\), which is at least \(\gamma_{d_1+\cdots+d_k}2^{-kn}\). This proves the eventual sharp assertion with a threshold depending only on the table. In the fixed-body case all centroids and tilted averages are identical from factor to factor, so both constructions are repetitions of a single fixed point.

It remains to prove the finite-collection extension with a constant that does not deteriorate when the numbers of different factors change. For each table \(p^{(t)}\), make the following fixed choices. If it has a heavy atom, choose a base \(\mathbf j_t\) with \(p^{(t)}_{\mathbf j_t}\ge2^{-n_t}\), take \(r^{(t)}\) concentrated there, and put \(\ell_t=-\log p^{(t)}_{\mathbf j_t}\), \(U_t(\mathbf j_t)=0\), and \(\sigma_t^2=0\). Otherwise use the face tilt at an information minimizer, put \(\ell_t=I_*^{(t)}\), and let \(U_t(\mathbf z)=\mathbf a_t\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j_t)\) and \(\sigma_t^2=\mathbb E_{r^{(t)}}U_t^2\). In both cases the tilted mean base is the integer \(\mathbf j_t\), \(\mathbb E_{r^{(t)}}U_t=0\), and on the positive tilted support
\(\log(r^{(t)}_{\mathbf z}/p^{(t)}_{\mathbf z})=\ell_t+U_t(\mathbf z)\).

Put \(\varepsilon_t=n_t\log2-\ell_t\). This is nonnegative for a heavy table and strictly positive for every other table, by the critical-case argument. Let \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fin}}\) be the maximum of \(3\) and the reciprocals of every positive probability in the finitely many \(r^{(t)}\). Set \(\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}=1/(4e\kappa_{\mathrm{fin}})\), and define \(R_{\mathrm{fin}}\) to be the maximum of \(\sigma_t^2/\varepsilon_t\) over tables without a heavy atom, with value zero when there are no such tables. These are finite constants depending only on the fixed collection. Every table satisfies \(\sigma_t^2\le R_{\mathrm{fin}}\varepsilon_t\), including heavy tables because their variance is zero.

Consider any finite product with table types \(t(1),\ldots,t(k)\). For each factor retain its actual conditional uniform fibers and the chosen base law \(r^{(t(i))}\), and choose the resulting integer-base mean point. The preceding fourth-moment argument applies word for word with the common lower bound on positive label probabilities; the labels need not have identical supports, probabilities, or ambient dimensions for that argument. It gives tilted halfspace mass at least \(\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}\) at this fixed product point. The log density ratio is \(\sum_i\ell_{t(i)}+\sum_iU_{t(i)}(\mathbf Z_i)\); its centered part has variance \(V=\sum_i\sigma_{t(i)}^2\). The same Chebyshev argument, including its zero-variance case, gives normalized original halfspace mass at least
\((\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}/2)\exp(-\sum_i\ell_{t(i)}-\sqrt{2V/\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}})\).

Let \(N=\sum_i n_{t(i)}\) and \(\Delta=\sum_i\varepsilon_{t(i)}\). Then \(\Delta\ge0\), \(V\le R_{\mathrm{fin}}\Delta\), and \(N\log2-\sum_i\ell_{t(i)}=\Delta\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^N\mathcal D\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^kC_i\right)
&\ge\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}}{2}
 \exp\!\left(\Delta-\sqrt{\frac{2R_{\mathrm{fin}}\Delta}
                                      {\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}}{2}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{R_{\mathrm{fin}}}{2\beta_{\mathrm{fin}}}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last step completes the square in \(\sqrt\Delta\), and remains valid when \(R_{\mathrm{fin}}=0\) or \(\Delta=0\). Taking the last expression as \(c_{\mathrm{fin}}\) proves the finite-collection assertion. Every transfer above estimates all containing halfspaces at a point fixed before the halfspace, so the conclusion concerns actual depth throughout.
