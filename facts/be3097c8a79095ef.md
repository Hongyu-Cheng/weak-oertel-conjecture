## Statement

Let \(X\) be a real random variable with a compactly supported log-concave Lebesgue probability density and \(\mathbb E X=0\). For \(t\in\mathbb R\), write \(F(t)=\Pr(X>t)\) and \(P(t)=\Pr(X\le t)=1-F(t)\). Then, for every \(h\ge0\),

\[
F(0)+2F(h)+F(2h)-P(-2h)\ge\frac13.
\]

## Proof

We first prove the inequality for every centered probability density that is log-affine on a nondegenerate compact interval and zero outside that interval. We then give the localization argument for the full class. No extremal reduction is assumed during the explicit profile calculation.

For \(\tau>0\), let \(Y_\tau\) have density \(g_\tau(y)=e^{-y}/(1-e^{-\tau})\) on \([0,\tau]\), and zero elsewhere. The exact integrals

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\tau e^{-y}\,dy&=1-e^{-\tau},\\
\int_0^\tau y e^{-y}\,dy&=1-(\tau+1)e^{-\tau}
\end{aligned}
\]

give its normalization and mean \(m(\tau)=1-\tau/(e^\tau-1)\). In calculations at a fixed \(\tau\), write \(m=m(\tau)\). Put \(q(\tau)=\Pr(Y_\tau>m)=(e^{-m}-e^{-\tau})/(1-e^{-\tau})\).

We will use \(1/e\le q(\tau)\le1/2\), \(0<m<\tau/2\), and two elementary monotonicity properties of \(m\). Here are complete verifications. The inequality \(\log u\ge1-1/u\), valid for \(u>0\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{\tau}{1-e^{-\tau}}-m
&=\int_0^\tau g_\tau(y)\log\bigl(\tau g_\tau(y)\bigr)\,dy\\
&\ge\int_0^\tau g_\tau(y)
\left(1-\frac1{\tau g_\tau(y)}\right)\,dy=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently \(\tau e^{-m}\ge1-e^{-\tau}\). Differentiating the displayed formula for \(q\) yields

\[
q'(\tau)=
\frac{e^{-\tau}\bigl(1-e^{-\tau}-\tau e^{-m(\tau)}\bigr)}
{(1-e^{-\tau})^3}\le0.
\]

As \(\tau\downarrow0\), the density of \(Y_\tau/\tau\) converges uniformly to the uniform density on \([0,1]\), so \(m(\tau)/\tau\to1/2\) and \(q(\tau)\to1/2\). As \(\tau\to\infty\), the exact formula gives \(m(\tau)\to1\) and \(q(\tau)\to1/e\). This proves the asserted bounds on \(q\).

Differentiation of the normalized integrals on \([0,1]\) gives
\((m(\tau)/\tau)'=-\operatorname{Var}(Y_\tau/\tau)<0\). The strict inequality holds because that density is positive on the whole nondegenerate interval. Together with the preceding limit at zero, this proves \(0<m<\tau/2\). Also,
\(m'(\tau)=((\tau-1)e^\tau+1)/(e^\tau-1)^2>0\): the numerator vanishes at zero and has derivative \(\tau e^\tau>0\).

Consider first \(X=Y_\tau-m\), with the notation \(F,P\) in the statement. Here \(F(0)=q(\tau)\). If \(h\ge m/2\), then \(-2h\le-m\), the left support endpoint, and \(P(-2h)=0\), including equality because the distribution has no atom there. The desired expression is at least \(q(\tau)\ge1/e>1/3\).

If \(0\le h<m/2\), then \(0<m-2h\le m\), and \(m+h<3m/2<\tau\). Thus both thresholds in the following calculation lie in the exact integration ranges:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2F(h)-P(-2h)
&=\frac{e^{-m}(2e^{-h}+e^{2h})-1-2e^{-\tau}}
{1-e^{-\tau}}\\
&\ge\frac{3e^{-m}-1-2e^{-\tau}}{1-e^{-\tau}}
=3q(\tau)-1>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The inequality uses the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on \(e^{-h},e^{-h},e^{2h}\), whose product is one. Adding \(F(0)+F(2h)\) proves the claim in this range as well. The elementary bound \(e<3\), used here, follows from the exponential series and \(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge1\), with strict inequality for \(k\ge3\).

Now consider the opposite orientation \(X=m-Y_\tau\). Its density is \(e^{x-m}/(1-e^{-\tau})\) on \([m-\tau,m]\), and zero outside. In particular it increases on its support and \(F(0)=1-q(\tau)\ge1/2\). To bound the desired expression it suffices to bound \(D(h)=F(0)+2F(h)-P(-2h)\), since the omitted term \(F(2h)\) is nonnegative.

The function \(D\) is absolutely continuous on every bounded interval. For almost every \(h\in(0,m)\), its derivative is twice the density at \(-2h\) minus twice the density at \(h\), hence is nonpositive. Indeed, \(h\) lies inside the support, while \(-2h\) either lies farther left inside that interval or lies outside it where the density is zero. For \(h\ge m\), one has \(F(h)=0\), including \(h=m\), so \(D(h)=F(0)-P(-2h)\) is nondecreasing. Therefore its global minimum on \([0,\infty)\) is attained at \(h=m\), and

\[
D(h)\ge D(m)=1-q(\tau)-\Pr(Y_\tau\ge3m).
\]

For \(0<\tau\le2\), the monotonicity of \(m(\tau)/\tau\) gives \(m(\tau)/\tau\ge m(2)/2>1/3\). To check the last strict inequality, \(m(2)=1-2/(e^2-1)>2/3\) because \(e^2>7\); the latter follows by summing the exponential series through degree four, whose sum is exactly seven, and retaining its positive remainder. Thus \(3m>\tau\) and the tail in the preceding display is zero, giving \(D(h)\ge1/2\).

For \(\tau>2\), the monotonicity of \(m\) gives \(m>m(2)>2/3\). If \(3m\ge\tau\), the same tail is again zero, including equality at the support endpoint. If \(3m<\tau\), direct integration gives

\[
\Pr(Y_\tau\ge3m)
=\frac{e^{-3m}-e^{-\tau}}{1-e^{-\tau}}
\le e^{-3m}<e^{-2}<\frac17.
\]

Consequently in both cases \(D(h)\ge1/2-1/7=5/14>1/3\). This proves the claim for the increasing orientation, with all values of \(h\), including zero and both support cutoffs, covered.

A centered uniform law on \([-a,a]\), \(a>0\), satisfies \(F(0)=1/2\) and \(P(-2h)=F(2h)\) for every \(h\ge0\). Its desired expression is \(1/2+2F(h)\ge1/2\).

These cases exhaust centered log-affine densities on compact intervals. Explicitly, if a density is proportional to \(e^{\lambda x}\) on \([a,b]\), \(a<b\), then for \(\lambda<0\) the variable \((-\lambda)(X-a)\) has the law of \(Y_\tau\) with \(\tau=(-\lambda)(b-a)\). The mean-zero assumption gives \(X=(Y_\tau-m(\tau))/(-\lambda)\). For \(\lambda>0\), use \(\lambda(b-X)=Y_\tau\), giving \(\tau=\lambda(b-a)\) and \(X=(m(\tau)-Y_\tau)/\lambda\). Positive scaling only replaces \(h\) by the correspondingly scaled nonnegative number, so the two calculations apply. The case \(\lambda=0\) is the centered uniform law already checked.

We now prove the reduction from an arbitrary density in the statement. Fix \(h\ge0\), and define the bounded nondecreasing function

\[
w_h(x)=\mathbf1_{\{x>0\}}+2\mathbf1_{\{x>h\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{x>2h\}}-\mathbf1_{\{x\le-2h\}}.
\]

Each positive open-ray indicator is lower semicontinuous, as is the negative closed-ray indicator. Therefore \(w_h\) is lower semicontinuous, including at all coincident thresholds when \(h=0\). Its expectation is exactly the expression in the statement. At every \(x>0\), one has \(w_h(x)\ge1\).

Suppose, for contradiction, that the statement fails for the chosen \(X,h\), so \(\mathbb E w_h(X)<1/3\). The law of \(X\) has a Lebesgue density, hence \(F\) and \(P\) are continuous at every real threshold. For \(\delta>0\), the exact translated expectation is

\[
\mathbb E w_h(X+\delta)
=F(-\delta)+2F(h-\delta)+F(2h-\delta)-P(-2h-\delta).
\]

This converges to \(\mathbb E w_h(X)\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\). Choose \(\delta>0\) so small that the translated expectation remains strictly below \(1/3\). Its mean is exactly \(\delta\), and its law \(\mu\) is still log-concave and compactly supported. Let \(K\) be a compact interval containing its support.

Apply the Fradelizi–Guédon theorem stated below with constraint \(f(x)=x-\delta\) and functional \(\Phi(\theta)=-\int_K w_h(x)\,d\theta(x)\). The constraint function is continuous. The functional is real-valued and affine, hence convex, on all probability measures on \(K\). It is upper semicontinuous for weak convergence: it is a sum of negative open-set masses and a positive closed-set mass. Thus all the theorem's topological and sign hypotheses hold, and \(\mu\) satisfies \(\int f\,d\mu=0\).

The theorem supplies an extremizing probability \(\nu\) with \(\Phi(\nu)\ge\Phi(\mu)>-1/3\). Its Dirac alternative is impossible. Such a Dirac must lie at a point \(x\ge\delta>0\), where \(\Phi(\delta_x)=-w_h(x)\le-1\). Hence \(\nu\) is a nondegenerate compactly supported log-affine probability and satisfies \(\int(x-\delta)\,d\nu(x)=0\).

Let \(Z\) have law \(\nu\). Then \(\mathbb E Z=\delta\), so \(Z-\delta\) is centered and has a compactly supported log-affine density. Since \(w_h\) is nondecreasing,

\[
\mathbb E w_h(Z-\delta)\le\mathbb E w_h(Z)
=-\Phi(\nu)<\frac13.
\]

This recentering retains the same nonnegative \(h\). More explicitly, at each of the four thresholds \(t\in\{0,h,2h,-2h\}\), one has \(\Pr(Z-\delta>t)=\Pr(Z>t+\delta)\) and \(\Pr(Z-\delta\le t)=\Pr(Z\le t+\delta)\). Thus every positive tail can only decrease and the subtracted lower tail can only increase. The exact mean-zero condition is restored, and the strict violation is preserved. This contradicts the preceding exhaustive log-affine calculation and proves the theorem.

The intermediate Dirac alternative is handled before recentering. No inequality for a point mass at zero is asserted or needed. The original law and every non-Dirac log-affine law used above assign zero mass to every threshold and support endpoint. The definitions of \(w_h\) retain the stipulated strict upper and closed lower tails throughout; no change of endpoint convention or passage through a noncompact probability is used.

## External sources

Matthieu Fradelizi and Olivier Guédon, *The Extreme Points of Subsets of s-Concave Probabilities and a Geometric Localization Theorem*, Discrete & Computational Geometry 31 (2004), 327–335, [publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00454-003-2868-y.pdf), Theorems 1–2 on page 330 (PDF page 4).

The precise specialization of Theorem 2 used here is: let \(K\subset\mathbb R\) be compact and convex, let \(f:K\to\mathbb R\) be upper semicontinuous, and let \(\mathcal P_f\) consist of log-concave probabilities on \(K\) satisfying \(\int f\,d\mu\ge0\). For a real-valued convex upper semicontinuous functional \(\Phi\) on all probabilities on \(K\), its supremum over \(\mathcal P_f\) is attained at either a Dirac mass \(\delta_x\) with \(f(x)\ge0\), or a log-affine probability \(\nu\) on a nondegenerate segment \([a,b]\subset K\) satisfying \(\int f\,d\nu=0\) and either \(\int_a^x f\,d\nu>0\) for every \(a<x<b\), or \(\int_x^b f\,d\nu>0\) for every \(a<x<b\). This is the case \(n=1,s=0\); the extra partial-integral property is not needed here. Page 329 identifies log-concave densities with nondegenerate \(0\)-concave probabilities and identifies \(0\)-affine densities as exponentials of affine functions. All applicability and atom checks are given in the proof.
