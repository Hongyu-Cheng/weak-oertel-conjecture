## Statement

Let L be a positive integer. For each block \(\ell\in\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), let \(n_\ell\ge1\) be an integer and let \(A_\ell\subset\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty. For every \(j\in A_\ell\), define
$$
R_{\ell,j}=\lbrace x\in A_\ell:2j-x\in A_\ell\rbrace.
$$
Assume that each such row is either \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) or
$$
R_{\ell,j}=\lbrace u_{\ell,j},j,v_{\ell,j}\rbrace,
\qquad u_{\ell,j}\ne v_{\ell,j},
\qquad u_{\ell,j}+v_{\ell,j}=2j.
\tag{1}
$$
For each fixed block, assume that the endpoint sets
\(\lbrace u_{\ell,j},v_{\ell,j}\rbrace\) of its nontrivial rows are pairwise disjoint as j varies. Centers may be endpoints of other rows. The set of nontrivial rows may be empty. There is no condition on reflected rows at unsupported centers.

Choose arbitrary weights \(w_{\ell,x}>0\) for all \(x\in A_\ell\), and set
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=\frac{w_{\ell,x}}{Z_\ell},\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell.
\tag{2}
$$
Each law is zero off its stated support. Retain exactly the quantities \(\tau_p,H_p,b(p),M_2(p)\) of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc, with the following explicit definitions for a law p on \(\mathbb Z^n\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}p}p_jH_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_2(p)=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}p}p_j\tau_p(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The sum defining \(H_p\) is oriented, so a nonzero displacement and its negative both occur, while zero occurs once. Define also the original cubic moment
$$
M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}p}p_j\tau_p(j)^3.
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14e^{-6\sqrt L}\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell),
\tag{4}
$$
and
$$
M_3(P)\ge\frac18e^{-9\sqrt L}
\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)^{3/2}.
\tag{5}
$$
Both depths range over all closed halfspaces in the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^{n_1+\cdots+n_L}\), including when some or all supports are singletons or lower-dimensional. The averaging law in both bounds is exactly P from (2). In particular its original atom probabilities are
$$
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}=\frac{\prod_\ell w_{\ell,x_\ell}}{\prod_\ell Z_\ell},
$$
and its reflected factors have the exact normalization
$$
b(p_\ell)=\frac1{Z_\ell^3}\sum_{j\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{\ell,j}}\sqrt{w_{\ell,x}w_{\ell,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{6}
$$

More precisely, introduce the auxiliary geometric-mean reflected row law
$$
\kappa_{\ell,j}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{p_{\ell,x}p_{\ell,2j-x}}}{H_{p_\ell}(j)}
\quad(x\in R_{\ell,j}),
$$
and the row score, its variance, and the auxiliary center weights
$$
Y_{\ell,j}(x)=\frac12\log\frac{p_{\ell,x}}{p_{\ell,2j-x}},\qquad
V_{\ell,j}=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\ell,j}}Y_{\ell,j}^2,\qquad
q_{\ell,j}=\frac{p_{\ell,j}H_{p_\ell}(j)^2}{b(p_\ell)}.
\tag{7}
$$
These are well-defined on every supported center. The row law has mean j, its score has mean zero, and
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\ell,j}}\log\frac{\kappa_{\ell,j}(X)}{p_{\ell,X}}
=-\log H_{p_\ell}(j),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_{\ell,j}}\left(\log\frac{\kappa_{\ell,j}(X)}{p_{\ell,X}}\right)
=V_{\ell,j},\qquad
\sum_{j\in A_\ell}q_{\ell,j}V_{\ell,j}\le9.
\tag{8}
$$
The laws \(\kappa_{\ell,j}\) and \(q_\ell\) are used only in the proof, without replacing the original laws in (2)--(6).

The hypotheses hold in particular for independently chosen widths \(m_\ell\ge1\) and arbitrary symmetric saturated chain partitions of \(B_{m_\ell}=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{m_\ell}\), with each \(A_\ell=T_\ell\) given by the following exact aliases of the block objects of accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d:
$$
(B_\ell,D_\ell,F_\ell,i_\ell,E_\ell,T_\ell,C_\ell^{\mathrm{bool}})
=(B_{m_\ell},D_{m_\ell},F_{m_\ell},i_{m_\ell},E_{m_\ell},T_{m_\ell},C_{m_\ell}^{\mathrm{bool}}).
$$
Thus D consists of the nonterminal elements of the chosen chains, and within each block
$$
F_\ell(c)=c+e_{i_\ell(c)},\qquad c_{i_\ell(c)}=0,\qquad
E_\ell=\lbrace c+2e_{i_\ell(c)}:c\in D_\ell\rbrace,
$$
$$
T_\ell=\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\cap\mathbb Z^{m_\ell}
=E_\ell\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_\ell^{\mathrm{bool}},\qquad
C_\ell^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\cap B_\ell.
\tag{9}
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector of the current block. The arbitrary weights in (2) may be chosen independently on every point of these complete block supports; no affine or concave condition on their logarithms is imposed.

## Proof

First fix one block and suppress its block index. Its support is the finite nonempty set A, and p is its original positive law. For \(j\in A\), the change of variables \(x=j+v\) in (3) gives
$$
H_p(j)=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\ge p_j>0.
\tag{10}
$$
Consequently \(b(p)\ge\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3>0\), and \(q_j=p_jH_p(j)^2/b(p)\) defines a probability law on A. Dividing each original weight by Z proves the normalization identity (6).

The involution \(x\mapsto2j-x\) preserves \(R_j\) and \(\kappa_j\), and reverses the sign of \(Y_j\). It follows that \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}X=j\) and \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j=0\). Directly from its definition,
$$
\log\frac{\kappa_j(x)}{p_x}=-\log H_p(j)-Y_j(x).
\tag{11}
$$
This proves the likelihood mean and variance identities in (8) for every row, without any condition on the weights beyond positivity.

Let \(\mathcal J\subseteq A\) be the set of nontrivial row centers. For \(j\in\mathcal J\), abbreviate its endpoints as u and v, and put
$$
s_j=\frac{\sqrt{p_up_v}}{p_j}>0,\qquad
z_j=\frac12\log\frac{p_v}{p_u}.
$$
Thus \(p_u=s_jp_je^{-z_j}\) and \(p_v=s_jp_je^{z_j}\). Its three kernel masses, at u, j, v respectively, are
$$
\frac{s_j}{1+2s_j},\quad\frac1{1+2s_j},\quad\frac{s_j}{1+2s_j},
$$
and its three score values are \(-z_j,0,z_j\). Therefore
$$
H_p(j)=(1+2s_j)p_j,\qquad
V_j=\frac{2s_jz_j^2}{1+2s_j},\qquad
p_jH_p(j)^2V_j=2s_j(1+2s_j)p_j^3z_j^2.
\tag{12}
$$
At each singleton row, the score and variance are zero.

For every real z, the nonnegative even terms of the power series of cosh give
$$
\cosh(3z)\ge\frac{(3z)^6}{720}
=\frac{729}{720}z^6\ge z^6,
\qquad \cosh(3z)\ge1.
$$
Taking nonnegative cube roots, and then using the second inequality, yields both
$$
z^2\le\cosh(3z)^{1/3},\qquad
z^2\le\cosh(3z)^{2/3}.
\tag{13}
$$
For arbitrary \(s>0\), set \(x=s\cosh(3z)^{1/3}\). Then
$$
2s(1+2s)z^2\le2x+4x^2\le6(1+x^3).
\tag{14}
$$
To verify the last inequality, if \(0\le x\le1\) its left side is at most 6; if \(x\ge1\) it is at most \(6x^3\). This covers all x, including the case z=0 in (13)--(14). No upper bound on s is used.

For a row centered at j, its endpoint cubes satisfy
$$
p_u^3+p_v^3=2s_j^3p_j^3\cosh(3z_j).
$$
Combining this identity with (12)--(14) gives the bound
$$
p_jH_p(j)^2V_j\le6p_j^3+3(p_u^3+p_v^3).
\tag{15}
$$
The centers in \(\mathcal J\) are distinct, and by hypothesis their endpoint sets are pairwise disjoint. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2V_j
&\le6\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}p_j^3
+3\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}(p_{u_j}^3+p_{v_j}^3)\\
&\le9\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3\le9b(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
An atom may occur once as a center and once as an endpoint in these two sums; the separate coefficients 6 and 3 account for this possibility. If \(\mathcal J\) is empty, the left side is zero. Dividing (16) by b(p) proves the last assertion in (8) for every block.

Now fix a supported product target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\). Its reflected row, its geometric-mean row law, and its score are exactly
$$
R_J=\prod_{\ell=1}^L R_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
\kappa_J=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
Y_J(X)=\sum_{\ell=1}^L Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(X^{(\ell)}).
\tag{17}
$$
The reflected-mass product identity of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc gives
$$
H_P(J)=\prod_{\ell=1}^L H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell).
\tag{18}
$$
The product expression for \(\kappa_J\) also follows directly by factoring its geometric-mean numerator and (18). Block independence, the score means already proved, and finiteness give
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,
\qquad
\sigma_J=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^L V_{\ell,j_\ell}\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{19}
$$
For every \(X\in R_J\) the exact likelihood identity is
$$
P_X=H_P(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}.
\tag{20}
$$
Indeed, in each block the geometric-mean numerator multiplied by \(e^{Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(X^{(\ell)})}\) is exactly \(p_{\ell,X^{(\ell)}}=w_{\ell,X^{(\ell)}}/Z_\ell\). Thus (20) retains precisely the original normalizer \(\prod_\ell Z_\ell\).

Let \(\mathcal H\) be any closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{n_1+\cdots+n_L}\) containing J. For each distinct reflected pair \(X,2J-X\) in \(R_J\), at least one endpoint lies in \(\mathcal H\), because a linear inequality excluding both endpoints strictly would exclude their midpoint J. The central atom J lies in \(\mathcal H\) as well. Summing the available original masses over these disjoint reflection orbits yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
The first line counts each distinct pair twice before the factor \(1/2\); at the central orbit it uses only half of the available central mass. For the equality, (20) and the reflection symmetry of \(\kappa_J\) give the paired minimum exactly, since the reflection reverses \(Y_J\). The final inequalities are Jensen's inequality for \(t\mapsto e^{-t}\) and Cauchy--Schwarz, using (19). In particular (21) is valid when \(\sigma_J=0\); it does not divide by a variance or discard a zero score. Taking the infimum over all the original ambient closed halfspaces gives the same lower bound for \(\tau_P(J)\).

Let \(q=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L q_\ell\), using (7) only as auxiliary center weights. Squaring (21) and summing against the original law P gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\sum_JP_JH_P(J)^2e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_q e^{-2\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
The equality is exact: by (7) and (18),
\(P_JH_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell))\prod_\ell q_{\ell,j_\ell}\).
Thus this change of averaging weights factors the original moment without changing its definition. Applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz again, then (16) in every block, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}
&\ge e^{-2\mathbb E_q\sigma_J}
\ge e^{-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2}},\\
\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2
&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\sum_{j\in A_\ell}q_{\ell,j}V_{\ell,j}
\le9L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
Equations (22)--(23) prove (4). All formulas remain valid for empty sets of nontrivial rows, singleton supports, and zero log-ratios. The halfspace argument used the original full ambient space throughout, with no assumption that a support spans it.

For the cubic consequence, apply Jensen's inequality to \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\) under the original probability P:
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}
\ge\left(\mathbb E_P\tau_P(J)^2\right)^{3/2}
=M_2(P)^{3/2}.
$$
Taking the power \(3/2\) of (4) proves (5).

It remains to check the asserted applicability to every block (9). Fix one such block and suppress its index. Accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d supplies its finite nonempty set E, the exact completion \(T=E\mathbin{\dot\cup}C^{\mathrm{bool}}\), and the injective successor map F with the stated formula. Every spike \(c+2e_{i(c)}\in E\) has exactly one coordinate equal to two and all other coordinates Boolean. Its coordinatewise reduction modulo two recovers its source c. Consequently, two Boolean points with equal parity coincide, and two points of E with equal parity coincide as well. If one point of T is Boolean and the other is a spike, equality of their parities forces the points to be
$$
c\quad\text{and}\quad c+2e_{i(c)}
\qquad(c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D).
\tag{24}
$$
Distinct integer endpoints have an integer midpoint only if their parities agree. Thus (24) exhausts all nontrivial reflected pairs in T. Conversely, for every \(c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D\), both endpoints in (24) lie in T, and their midpoint \(F(c)\) is an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\); completeness puts it in T. Injectivity of F shows that distinct pairs have distinct centers. Hence every supported row is a singleton or the triple
$$
R_{F(c)}=\lbrace c,F(c),c+2e_{i(c)}\rbrace
\qquad(c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D).
\tag{25}
$$
Finally, distinct c give distinct Boolean endpoints and distinct spike endpoints, the latter because their parities recover their sources. Boolean endpoints and spike endpoints lie in disjoint sets. The endpoint sets in (25) are therefore pairwise disjoint. This proves exactly the general support hypotheses for every chosen symmetric saturated chain partition, without any further property of a particular partition. Applying (4)--(8) with \(A_\ell=T_\ell\) and the arbitrary weights (2) proves all the claimed block consequences.
