## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(R>0\). Let \(E:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, convex, and coercive, with \(E(0_n)=0\), and suppose \(E\) is invariant under every individual coordinate sign flip. Let \(\gamma:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) be continuous and invariant under the same sign flips, with \(\gamma(0_n)=0_d\) and \(|\gamma_l(x)|\le E(x)\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\). The alias for the center map called \(c(x)\) in the subgoal input is \(\gamma(x)=c(x)\); it is not the scalar depth constant called \(c\) in accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973.

Define the actual body and its integer fibers by

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(x)&=R-E(x),\\
C&=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{n+d}: E(x)\le R,\ |y_l-\gamma_l(x)|\le a(x)\text{ for }1\le l\le d\},\\
S&=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:E(z)<R\},\\
a_z&=R-E(z),\qquad C_z=\gamma(z)+[-a_z,a_z]^d\quad(z\in D_+),\\
w_z&=(2a_z)^d,\qquad W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z,\qquad p_z=w_z/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Assume \(C\) is convex. A sufficient condition for this assumption is that both \(E+\gamma_l\) and \(E-\gamma_l\) are convex on \(\mathbb R^n\) for every \(l\). Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body, \(D_+\) is its positive-volume integer support, and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), with mixed volume and halfspace depth as in the locked problem. In these definitions, \(a_z,w_z,p_z\) have the same actual-radius, actual-volume, and original-volume meanings as in accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973; the current center map replaces that fact's \(c_B\), without assuming its separable-energy representation.

For \(Z\) with exactly the original law \(p\), the following tail holds for every real \(t\ge0\):

\[
\mathbb P_p\!\left(\frac{E(Z)}{a_Z}\ge t\right)
\le 3^n(1+t/2)^{-d}.
\tag{1}
\]

Set \(\kappa_1=\exp(-72e^8)/(2304e^8)\) and \(c_0=\kappa_1/4\). Then \(0<c_0<1/e\), and there exists one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), chosen before all containing closed halfspaces, with

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{2}
\]

More precisely, if \(d\ge16n^2\), the point \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,0_d)\) has depth at least \(c_0W\). If \(d<16n^2\), some \(m\in[-R,R]^d\) gives \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,m)\) of depth at least \(W/[2(d+1)]\ge2^{-n}W/64\).

## Proof

First, unconditional convexity implies coordinatewise monotonicity in absolute values. To see this for one coordinate, keep all other coordinates fixed and let that coordinate change from \(v_i\) to \(u_i\), where \(|u_i|\le|v_i|\). If \(v_i\ne0\), the resulting vector is a convex combination of the vector with coordinate \(v_i\) and its sign-flipped vector, with coefficient \((1+u_i/v_i)/2\in[0,1]\). These two vectors have equal energy. Convexity therefore shows that reducing the absolute value of this coordinate cannot increase \(E\). If \(v_i=0\), then \(u_i=0\). Applying this argument successively to the coordinates gives \(E(u)\le E(v)\) whenever \(|u_i|\le|v_i|\) for every \(i\).

A finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^n\) is continuous. Thus the displayed constraints defining \(C\) are closed. Coercivity makes its base \(\{x:E(x)\le R\}\) bounded. The domination assumption gives, throughout \(C\), \(|y_l|\le|\gamma_l(x)|+R-E(x)\le R\). Consequently \(C\) is compact. All inequalities are strict at \((0_n,0_d)\), and continuity gives a neighborhood of that point in \(C\); hence it is full-dimensional. Under the stated sufficient condition, the lower endpoint in coordinate \(l\) is \(-R+(E+\gamma_l)(x)\), a convex function, while the upper endpoint is \(R-(E-\gamma_l)(x)\), a concave function. Their epigraph and hypograph constraints, together with \(E(x)\le R\), prove convexity of \(C\).

Coercivity makes the integer support finite. When \(E(z)<R\), its actual fiber is the cube \(C_z\) with volume \(w_z\). When \(E(z)=R\), its fiber is a singleton and has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume because \(d>0\). When \(E(z)>R\), the fiber is empty. Since \(0_n\in D_+\), \(a_{0_n}=R\) and \(w_{0_n}=(2R)^d>0\). The locked mixed-volume definition therefore gives \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\).

Let \(U\) be independent of \(Z\sim p\) and uniform on the normalized cube \([-1/2,1/2]^d\), and put \(Y=\gamma(Z)+2a_ZU\). The actual fiber map is exactly \(T_z=2a_z I_d\), so \(C_z=\gamma(z)+T_z[-1/2,1/2]^d\), with the same normalization of \(T_z,U\) as in accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973. Conditional on \(Z=z\), \(Y\) is uniform on \(C_z\). Hence for every measurable ambient set \(A\),

\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in A)=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)}{W}.
\tag{3}
\]

Every subsequent probability uses this original law. The sign assumptions give \(E(-z)=E(z)\) and \(\gamma(-z)=\gamma(z)\), so the joint law is invariant under \((Z,Y)\mapsto(-Z,Y)\). The marginal law of \(Y\) is absolutely continuous, as a finite mixture of positive cubes, and is supported on \([-R,R]^d\). This support cube is the entire origin fiber \(C_{0_n}\).

For the global contraction, define \(Q:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^n\) by \((Qz)_i=\operatorname{sgn}(z_i)\lfloor|z_i|/2\rfloor\), taking the value zero at \(z_i=0\). This \(Q\) is distinct from the actual fiber map \(T_z\). Since \(|(Qz)_i|\le|z_i|/2\), the monotonicity just proved and convexity at the origin imply

\[
E(Qz)\le E(z/2)\le E(z)/2,
\qquad a_{Qz}\ge a_z+E(z)/2.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus \(Q\) maps \(D_+\) into \(D_+\). A coordinate image zero has exactly the possible preimages \(-1,0,1\); a positive coordinate image \(k\) has possible preimages \(2k,2k+1\), and a negative image \(-k\), \(k>0\), has possible preimages \(-2k,-2k-1\). Every image under \(Q\) therefore has at most \(3^n\) preimages, including after restriction to \(D_+\).

For \(t\ge0\), let \(A_t=\{z\in D_+:E(z)/a_z\ge t\}\). On this set (4) gives \(a_{Qz}\ge a_z(1+t/2)\). Summing the original fiber weights and then grouping by images gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in A_t}(2a_z)^d
&\le(1+t/2)^{-d}\sum_{z\in A_t}(2a_{Qz})^d\\
&\le3^n(1+t/2)^{-d}\sum_{u\in D_+}(2a_u)^d.
\end{aligned}
\]

Division by \(W\) proves (1), without any exchangeability, separability, or covariance assertion.

We next record precisely the two purely continuous inputs used from accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973. Its proof, equation (3), gives for the normalized uniform \(U\) above, every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0_d\}\), and every \(\eta\in\mathbb R^d\) satisfying \(\|\eta\|_\infty\le1/4\) and \(\|\eta\|_2\le1\),

\[
\mathbb P(v\cdot(U-\eta)\ge0)
\ge\frac{\exp(-72e^8\|\eta\|_2^2)}{2304e^8}
\ge\kappa_1.
\tag{5}
\]

The same accepted proof gives a continuous centerpoint assertion: an absolutely continuous probability measure supported on \([-R,R]^d\) has a point \(m\) in that cube at which every containing closed halfspace has probability at least \(1/(d+1)\). These statements depend only on the uniform cube normalization, respectively on the compact marginal support and absolute continuity. They do not require the accepted fact's energy or center representation. The normalizations in (5) and the support and absolute-continuity hypotheses for the centerpoint were checked above.

Assume now \(d\ge16n^2\), so \(d\ge16\). For the new total-energy ratio \(\rho_z=E(z)/a_z\), take \(t=2/\sqrt d\). Since \(\log(1+s)\ge s/2\) for \(0\le s\le1\), (1) gives

\[
\mathbb P_p(\rho_Z\ge2/\sqrt d)
\le3^n(1+1/\sqrt d)^{-d}
\le\exp(n\log3-\sqrt d/2)
\le(3/e^2)^n<1/2.
\tag{6}
\]

The logarithm inequality follows by differentiating \(\log(1+s)-s/2\), whose derivative is nonnegative on \([0,1]\) and whose value at zero is zero. For the last strict inequality in (6), \(e^2>6\) and \(n\ge1\).

Let \(G=\{z\in D_+:\rho_z<2/\sqrt d\}\), which by (6) has original mass greater than \(1/2\). The normalized displacement from the fiber center to the single selected origin is \(\eta_z=-\gamma(z)/(2a_z)\). This is the same geometric displacement used in accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973, with \(\gamma(z)\) replacing \(c_B(z)\). Coordinatewise domination gives

\[
\|\eta_z\|_\infty\le\rho_z/2<1/\sqrt d\le1/4,
\qquad
\|\eta_z\|_2\le\sqrt d\,\rho_z/2<1
\quad(z\in G).
\tag{7}
\]

For every nonzero \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\) and every \(z\in G\), the actual fiber relation \(Y=2a_z(U-\eta_z)\) and (5) show that \(\mathbb P(\beta\cdot Y\ge0\mid Z=z)\ge\kappa_1\). Averaging over \(G\) under \(p\) gives \(\mathbb P(\beta\cdot Y\ge0)\ge\kappa_1/2\). For \(\beta=0_d\), this probability equals one.

To transfer a marginal bound to ambient depth, fix \(m\in[-R,R]^d\). For every \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), the pointwise indicator inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf 1_{\{\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf 1_{\{-\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}\\
&\hspace{12mm}\ge\mathbf 1_{\{\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

holds because when the right-hand scalar is nonnegative at least one of the two left-hand scalars is nonnegative. The joint sign symmetry makes the expectations of the two left-hand indicators equal. Thus each ambient halfspace through \((0_n,m)\) has probability at least one half of the corresponding marginal halfspace through \(m\). Every containing closed halfspace contains its parallel halfspace through the point, so this conclusion covers all containing halfspaces as well.

In the current regime, choose \(m=0_d\). The marginal lower bound and (8) give probability at least \(\kappa_1/4=c_0\) to every containing ambient halfspace. The point \((0_n,0_d)\) belongs to the origin fiber and is fixed independently of the halfspace. Equation (3) gives \(h_S((0_n,0_d))\ge c_0W\).

For every \(n,d\), the continuous centerpoint assertion from accepted fact ecd93b0b05cc8973, applied to the actual marginal law of \(Y\), gives some \(m\in[-R,R]^d\) with marginal depth at least \(1/(d+1)\). Fix that \(m\) before selecting any halfspace. The actual point \((0_n,m)\) belongs to \(S\). Equations (8) and (3) give \(h_S((0_n,m))\ge W/[2(d+1)]\). If \(d<16n^2\), integrality gives \(d+1\le16n^2\). For all positive integers \(n\), \(n^2\le2^{n+1}\): verify \(n=1,2,3\), and for \(n\ge3\) the induction step follows from \((n+1)^2\le2n^2\). Consequently,

\[
\frac{h_S((0_n,m))}{W}
\ge\frac1{32n^2}\ge\frac{2^{-n}}{64}.
\]

Finally, \(0<c_0\le1/(9216e^8)<1/64\) and \(c_0<1/e\). The high-dimensional estimate \(c_0W\) is at least \(c_0 2^{-n}W\), and the complementary estimate \(2^{-n}W/64\) is also at least \(c_0 2^{-n}W\). This proves (2) with the locked quantifier order for this entire actual-body class.

To verify that the class includes nonseparable energies with nonlinear centers of unrestricted affine rank, let \(n\ge2\) and \(d\ge1\) be arbitrary and define

\[
E(x)=\sum_{l=1}^d\|x\|_2^{2(l+1)},
\qquad \gamma_l(x)=\|x\|_2^{2(l+1)}\quad(1\le l\le d).
\tag{9}
\]

Each power of the Euclidean norm in (9) is convex: the triangle inequality first bounds the norm of a convex combination, and convexity and monotonicity of the scalar power \(s\mapsto s^{2(l+1)}\) then give the convexity inequality. All functions in (9) are finite, continuous, and invariant under individual coordinate sign flips. The energy is coercive, vanishes at the origin, and dominates each coordinate of the center map. Both \(E+\gamma_l\) and \(E-\gamma_l\) are sums of these convex norm powers with nonnegative coefficients, so the actual body is convex by the endpoint check above.

This energy is nonseparable. Indeed, for \(s>0\) and the first two standard basis vectors \(e_1,e_2\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
E(se_1+se_2)-E(se_1)-E(se_2)+E(0_n)
=\sum_{l=1}^d(2^{l+1}-2)s^{2(l+1)}>0,
\]

whereas this difference would vanish for a sum of functions of individual coordinates. The center map is nonlinear. Choose any \(R>E(de_1)\). The origin and all integer points \(ke_1\), \(1\le k\le d\), belong to \(D_+\). Their centers are \(0_d\) and the row vectors of the matrix \(A\) with entries \(A_{kl}=k^{2(l+1)}\). Factoring \(k^4\) from each row leaves the Vandermonde matrix with entries \((k^2)^{l-1}\), whose determinant is \(\prod_{1\le i<j\le d}(j^2-i^2)\ne0\), with the empty product equal to one when \(d=1\). Thus these \(d\) nonzero center vectors are linearly independent, and together with \(0_d\) their affine hull has dimension \(d\). This verifies unrestricted affine rank even for the actual positive-volume integer-fiber centers.
