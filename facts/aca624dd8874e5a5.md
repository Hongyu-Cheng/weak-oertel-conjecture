## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and put
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(\mu_z\) be \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure on the fiber \(\{z\}\times C_z\), put
\[
w_z:=|\mu_z|=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
\mu:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\mu_z,\qquad
W:=|\mu|=\sum_z w_z,
\]
and assume \(W>0\). For \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), define
\[
\mu_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}\mu_z,\qquad
W_r:=|\mu_r|,
\qquad
R_+:=\{r:W_r>0\},\qquad N_+:=|R_+|.
\]
Let
\[
T(p,q):=\frac{p+q}{2},\qquad
K_r:=\frac1{W_r}T_\#(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r)\quad(r\in R_+),
\]
and
\[
\mathsf A\mu:=2^{-n}\sum_{r\in R_+}K_r.
\]
Then:

1. \(\mathsf A\mu\) is carried by \(S\), has total mass \(2^{-n}W\), and has the same normalized barycenter as \(\mu\).
2. Every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) satisfies
   \[
   \mathsf A\mu(H)\leq2^{1-n}\mu(H).
   \]
3. For every \(r\in R_+\) and every \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\),
   \[
   K_r(\{c\}\times\mathbb R^d)
   =
   \frac1{W_r}\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z w_{2c-z}
   \leq w_c.
   \]
   Consequently, every target fiber satisfies
   \[
   \mathsf A\mu(\{c\}\times\mathbb R^d)
   \leq2^{-n}N_+w_c
   \leq w_c.
   \]

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that its projection onto the first \(n\) coordinates is compact. It therefore meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points. Hence only finitely many fibers \(C_z\) are nonempty, all sums below are finite, and all measures have finite first moments.

Apply accepted fact `3ac39fef94fcc0da` to \(\rho=\mu\). Its normalized parity-midpoint measure is
\[
\nu
=
\sum_{r\in R_+}\frac1{2W_r}T_\#(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r).
\]
Thus
\[
\mathsf A\mu=2^{1-n}\nu.
\]
The cited fact proves that \(\nu\) is carried by \(S\), that \(|\nu|=W/2\), that \(\nu(H)\leq\mu(H)\) for every closed halfspace \(H\), and that the normalized barycenters of \(\nu\) and \(\mu\) agree. Multiplication by the positive scalar \(2^{1-n}\) preserves the carrier and normalized barycenter and gives
\[
|\mathsf A\mu|=2^{1-n}\frac W2=2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\mathsf A\mu(H)\leq2^{1-n}\mu(H).
\]
This proves assertions 1 and 2, including closed halfspaces whose boundary carries mass.

It remains to prove the new paritywise estimate. Fix \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
F_c:=\{c\}\times\mathbb R^d,
\]
and fix \(r\in R_+\). Expanding the finite product measure shows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_\#(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r)(F_c)
&=
\sum_{\substack{z,z'\equiv r\pmod2}}
T_\#(\mu_z\otimes\mu_{z'})(F_c)\\
&=
\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z w_{2c-z}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the midpoint of the base coordinates \(z,z'\) lies in the target fiber \(F_c\) exactly when \(z+z'=2c\). If \(z\equiv r\pmod2\), then \(2c-z\equiv z\equiv r\pmod2\), so the surviving partner belongs to the same parity class.

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(\phi_c(z):=2c-z\). Convexity of \(C\) gives the fiber containment
\[
\frac12 C_z+\frac12 C_{\phi_c(z)}\subseteq C_c.
\]
If \(w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}=0\), then
\[
\sqrt{w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}}\leq w_c
\]
is immediate. If \(w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}>0\), the two endpoint fibers are nonempty compact measurable sets. Monotonicity of volume, the Brunn-Minkowski inequality at parameter \(1/2\), and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_c^{1/d}
&\geq
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(
\frac12 C_z+\frac12 C_{\phi_c(z)}
\right)^{1/d}\\
&\geq
\frac12 w_z^{1/d}
+\frac12 w_{\phi_c(z)}^{1/d}\\
&\geq
\bigl(w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}\bigr)^{1/(2d)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore, for every \(z\),
\[
\sqrt{w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}}\leq w_c.
\]

The reflection \(\phi_c\) is a bijection of each parity class onto itself. Using the preceding pointwise estimate once and then Cauchy-Schwarz over that parity class gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}
&=
\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}
\sqrt{w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}}
\sqrt{w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}}\\
&\leq
w_c\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}
\sqrt{w_z w_{\phi_c(z)}}\\
&\leq
w_c
\left(\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_{\phi_c(z)}\right)^{1/2}\\
&=w_cW_r.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(W_r>0\), division by \(W_r\) proves
\[
K_r(F_c)\leq w_c.
\]
Summing this inequality over \(r\in R_+\) yields
\[
\mathsf A\mu(F_c)
\leq2^{-n}N_+w_c
\leq w_c,
\]
because \(N_+\leq2^n\).

Empty parity classes never enter the normalized sum. Empty and nonempty lower-dimensional fibers have \(w_z=0\) and contribute zero to every displayed fiber sum. If \(w_c=0\), the same argument forces every reflected product \(w_z w_{2c-z}\) to vanish. Thus all zero-volume and lower-dimensional cases are included.

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, p. 362, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: if \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets, \(0<\lambda<1\), and \((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\) is measurable, then
\[
|(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B|_d^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)|A|_d^{1/d}
+\lambda|B|_d^{1/d}.
\]
Here it is applied only when \(w_z w_{2c-z}>0\), with \(A=C_z\), \(B=C_{2c-z}\), and \(\lambda=1/2\). These fibers are nonempty compact sets, their Minkowski midpoint is compact, and convexity gives \((C_z+C_{2c-z})/2\subseteq C_c\), so every hypothesis and the required applicability check hold.
