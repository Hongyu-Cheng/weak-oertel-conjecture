## Statement

Use the actual centered three-interval family of lengths $1/8,1,1$ at $-1,0,1$ and original measure $\mu$ of mass $17/8$. For a direct signed concave buffer of original mean one and physical shifts $\varepsilon f_\varepsilon(z,x)$ in unit continuous normals, the affine choice
$$
f_\varepsilon(z,x)=1+\frac{z-7/17}{8\varepsilon}
$$
has global raw buffered maximum $39/68+\varepsilon$ for every $0<\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$, whereas its ordinary raw maximum is $5/8$. The difference of these limits is $7/136$. With a fixed positive interval norm one can rescale the slope to get the same limiting gap. Replacing the constant term by a fixed bounded nonnegative concave mean-one buffer leaves the signed limit unchanged.

## Proof

The original base mean is $7/17$ and the positive body is the compatible family in c680a307d3f26cec with $a=1/8$, $b=U=1$ and no middle displacement. That fact gives ordinary maximum $5/8$. The chosen affine function has mean one and gives shifts $(\varepsilon-3/17,\varepsilon-7/136,\varepsilon+5/68)$ in base order. On either extreme fiber, finite outward base tilts isolate the two own continuous orientations from the other fibers. The minimum of these two cap lengths, and the own contribution to any nonvertical cap, is the clip of $v/2-|y|+\delta$ to $[0,v]$ for length $v$ and shift $\delta$. Pure-base isolation attains full own mass and cannot do better. The maximum on the smallest endpoint is therefore zero and on the largest it is $39/68+\varepsilon$.

At the middle center the endpoint clip formula from c680a307d3f26cec applies with common height $-\delta=7/136-\varepsilon<7/32$. Thus the endpoint infimum is $1/8$ in either orientation, and the own interval contributes $1/2+\delta$. Every purely base cap through this base has mass at least $9/8$. This proves the exact middle-center value $39/68+\varepsilon$. At every other middle mark a finite steep tilt can capture the smaller endpoint and miss the larger; use the orientation with own threshold $|y|-\delta$ to bound the value by $1/8+\max(0,1/2-|y|+\delta)$. It does not exceed the center value. This accounts for all candidates and proves the global formula.

For a different fixed interval norm its positive value on unit covectors is a common scalar. Divide the signed slope by that scalar to retain the nonvanishing offsets; the common shift is still $O(\varepsilon)$. Replacing the constant term by any fixed bounded mean-one concave profile changes all cap masses uniformly by $O(\varepsilon)$, since each of three clipped interval lengths is 1-Lipschitz in the shifted threshold. The same bound survives all cap infima and mark maxima.
