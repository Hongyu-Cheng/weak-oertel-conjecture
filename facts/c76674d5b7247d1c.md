## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $\gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, and $\kappa=(1-\gamma)^{1/d}$. Take $2\kappa<b<2$, $a=2-b$, and an integer $N\ge1$ with $2N+1>3b^d$. Let $T$ be a centroid-zero $d$-simplex of volume one. Over the diamond $P=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z_1|+|z_2|/N\le1\}$ put $C_z=(1+(b-1)z_1)T$. This defines a full-dimensional compact convex body with affine fiber-volume roots. The complete integer base consists of $(0,j)$ for integers $-N\le j\le N$, and the two endpoints $(-1,0),(1,0)$. Its mixed mass is $M=2N+1+a^d+b^d$, and its largest fiber volume is $b^d<M/4$.

At $y=((0,0),0)$ the raw depth equals the global maximum $Q=(2N+1)\gamma+a^d$. If $\lambda_i$ are the barycentric coordinates on $T$ and $\ell_i=\lambda_i-1/(d+1)$, every cap $\{\ell_i(x)-2z_1\ge0\}$ is critical at $y$ and has own captured fraction $\gamma$. On the primitive line with direction $e_1=(1,0)$, the original strict residual roots at $-e_1,0,e_1$ are $(0,\kappa,b)$, so the midpoint comparison fails strictly. Admissible rational choices are $a=1/(2d)$, $b=2-1/(2d)$, and $N=3\cdot2^{d-1}$. The normalized depth $Q/M$ is strictly greater than $\gamma/4$.

## Proof

The binomial expansion gives $1/3<\gamma\le4/9$, using $\sum_{k\ge0}1/k!<3$ for the lower bound. Consequently $\kappa>d/(d+1)>1/2$. For the stated rational choice, Bernoulli's inequality gives $(b/2)^d=(1-1/(4d))^d\ge3/4>1-\gamma$, while $2N+1=3\cdot2^d+1>3b^d$. Also $0<a<1<b<2$ for every admissible choice.

The scale $r(z)=1+(b-1)z_1$ is positive and affine on $P$. A convex combination of points in $r(z)T$ and $r(z')T$, divided by the combined scale, is a convex combination of points of $T$. This proves convexity of the bundle. Its compactness follows from compactness of $P,T$ and continuity of the scale, and its interior is nonempty over the interior of $P$. For an integer $z\in P$ one has $z_1\in\{-1,0,1\}$, and $|z_1|=1$ forces $z_2=0$. At $z_1=0$ the allowed integers are precisely the displayed column. Thus there are $2N+1$ copies of $T$ and the endpoint fibers $aT,bT$. The total mass and affine roots follow, and $M>4b^d$ by the parameter inequality.

To bound depth at $y$ from below, tighten any containing halfspace to one through $y$ of the form $\alpha_1 z_1+\alpha_2 z_2+u\cdot x\ge0$. If $u\ne0$, fact 6a7d56e450b3126f applied at zero shift in $T$ gives at least $\gamma$ from the central copy. At the reflected column pairs $(0,j),(0,-j)$ the same continuous normal has opposite thresholds $\pm j\alpha_2$, so that fact gives at least $2\gamma$ per pair. All copies are full-dimensional centroid-zero bodies and use ordinary $d$-volume. For the horizontal endpoints, the identity $\lambda_i(-x/d)=(1-\lambda_i(x))/d$ for $x\in T$ gives $-T\subset dT$. Since $b>2\kappa>2d/(d+1)$, we have $b>da$ and hence $-aT\subset bT$, using $0\in T$. The captures at the two endpoints therefore sum to at least
$$
|aT\cap\{u\cdot x\ge\alpha_1\}|+
|-aT\cap\{u\cdot x\ge-\alpha_1\}|=a^d.
$$
The equality is reflection and the fact that a continuous hyperplane has zero $d$-volume. When $u=0$, the source fiber is included in full and at least one whole fiber of each reflected pair is included. Each column pair then pays at least $1\ge2\gamma$, and the horizontal pair pays at least $a^d$. This checks all closed ambient halfspaces, including boundary fibers of pure base-normal caps, and gives the lower bound $Q$.

The canonical caps in the statement capture the entire $aT$ and miss $bT$ strictly. Indeed $-1/(d+1)\le\ell_i\le d/(d+1)$ on $T$, and the linear parts scale by $a$ or $b$. At each column fiber the captured cap has fraction $\gamma$ by the simplex homothety at barycentric threshold $1/(d+1)$. The caps thus attain $Q$ at $y$. Since the sum of the $\ell_i$ is zero, these same caps cover every column fiber as well as the left endpoint. Every such point therefore has raw depth at most $Q$. At the exposed endpoints use the caps $\ell_i(x)+3(z_1-1)\ge0$ on the right and $\ell_i(x)+3(-z_1-1)\ge0$ on the left. They isolate their respective vertex traces, since the additional margin is at most $-3$ on every other integer fiber and $\ell_i(x)<2$ throughout. They cover their own endpoint fibers and have masses $\gamma b^d$ and $\gamma a^d$, both below $Q$. Thus the census gives the global upper bound everywhere.

For a canonical critical cap the left strict residual is empty, the middle root is $\kappa$, and the right residual is the full $bT$, so $b/2>\kappa$. Tilting in $z_1$ fixes all column traces at once. The fixed term $(2N+1)\gamma$ in the cap budget is therefore a whole hyperplane of integer bases, whereas a one-dimensional base tilt fixes only one base. Finally $4Q-\gamma M=3(2N+1)\gamma+(4-\gamma)a^d-\gamma b^d>0$. For a planar calibration, $a=2/5$, $b=8/5$, $N=4$ and $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/3,-2/3),(2/3,-2/3),(-1/3,4/3)\}$ give $Q=104/25$, $M=293/25$ and residual roots $(0,\sqrt5/3,8/5)$. The strict comparison is $4/5>\sqrt5/3$ since $144>125$.
