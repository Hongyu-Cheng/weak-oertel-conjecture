## Statement

Let $Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(3,0),(0,3),(-3,-3)\}$, let $\lambda_i(z)$ be its three barycentric coordinates, and let $J$ be an area-one triangle with centroid zero and a vertex $P$. At each real $z\in Q$ take
$$
K_z=P+\left(1+\frac{13}{2}\min_i\lambda_i(z)\right)(J-P).
$$
These are the real fibers of an actual convex body in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2$ with nineteen positive integer fibers, total original mixed area $M=219/4$, and a nonaffine original centroid graph. At $Y=(0,0)$ choose the constant affine center zero and common template $J$. The maximal centered radii are all one. The atomic-core bound alone is $4$, and the sum of the exact ordinary own depth and exact independent reflected-pair depths is $52/9$. The additive reflected-shell bound is $20/3$, exceeding the original target $\Gamma_2 M/4=73/12$ by $7/12$. In particular the actual normalized depth at $Y$ is at least $80/657$.

## Proof

Write $R(z)=1+(13/2)\min_i\lambda_i(z)$. It is continuous, concave, and at least one. For each normal $u$ the support of $K_z$ is $u\cdot P+R(z)(h_J(u)-u\cdot P)$, with nonnegative bracket since $P\in J$. Its concavity in $z$ proves every required Minkowski inclusion for the fibers. Continuity and boundedness give a compact convex body, and it contains $Q\times J$ because these expansions about $P$ contain $J$. Its original centroid graph is $(1-R(z))P$, zero at the three vertices of $Q$ and nonzero at zero, hence nonaffine.

The coordinate formulas are $\lambda_1=(3+2x-y)/9$, $\lambda_2=(3-x+2y)/9$, and $\lambda_3=(3-x-y)/9$. The nonnegative conditions give the following integer census, where each row lists the possible second coordinates at the indicated first coordinate.
$$
\begin{array}{c|l}
x&y\\ \hline
-3&-3\\
-2&-2,-1\\
-1&-2,-1,0,1\\
0&-1,0,1,2,3\\
1&-1,0,1,2\\
2&0,1\\
3&0
\end{array}
$$
Substituting these coordinates shows that $\min_i\lambda_i$ takes values $0,1/9,2/9,1/3$ with multiplicities $9,6,3,1$. The mixed area is therefore $9+6(31/18)^2+3(22/9)^2+(19/6)^2=219/4$. The largest area $(19/6)^2=361/36$ is below $M/4=219/16$, so the ordinary centroid lower bound of any own fiber alone does not pay the target.

A normal exposing $P$ on $J$ satisfies $h_J(u)=u\cdot P>0$ since zero is interior. The support of every $K_z$ in that direction is still $h_J(u)$, so $rJ\subseteq K_z$ implies $r\le1$. Thus the maximal weights are one. The nonzero reflected pairs about zero are represented by $(-2,-1),(-1,-2),(-1,-1),(-1,0),(-1,1),(0,-1)$ and their negatives, giving $R_0(w)=6$. The unpaired nonzero bases are the two triples $\{(-3,-3),(3,0),(0,3)\}$ and $\{(-2,-2),(2,0),(0,2)\}$. Each triple sums to zero, so each closed base halfspace through zero captures at least one point from each. The normal $(-5,-4)$ captures exactly the negative diagonal member of each triple, so their combined atomic depth is two. The atomic identity of the additive reflected-shell theorem gives $D_0(w)=1+6+2=9$. Thus the atomic-core bound is $\Gamma_2\cdot9=4$, and the additive bound is $\Gamma_2(9+6)=20/3$.

For the exact separate budgets, let $\ell$ be the affine height on $J$ that is one at $P$ and zero on the opposite edge. The centroid has $\ell(0)=1/3$. For any $R\ge1$ the trace of $\{\ell\ge1/3\}$ on $P+R(J-P)$ is precisely $P+(2/3)(J-P)$: in the preimage in $J$ the required height is at least $1-2/(3R)$. Hence this cap captures area $\Gamma_2=4/9$ in every original fiber. The contained $J$ gives the matching lower bound on the ordinary depth of the own fiber at zero. Every pair of equal contained $J$ copies gives lower bound $2\Gamma_2$ on its endpoint-only depth at $Y$ by 6a7d56e450b3126f, with pure-base caps capturing at least one full copy. The same continuous cap with zero base tilt attains $2\Gamma_2$ on each original pair. Thus the exact sum is $\Gamma_2(1+2\cdot6)=52/9$. Both separate bounds are below $73/12=\Gamma_2 M/4$, while $20/3-73/12=7/12$ and $(20/3)/M=80/657$.
