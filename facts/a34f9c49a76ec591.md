## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$, let $Q=\{0,1\}^n$, and let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of volume $V>0$. Every nonempty integer fiber of a compact convex body whose fibers at $Q$ all equal $K$ is contained in $K$. In particular, adding a nonempty compact convex fiber $A$ at $p\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus Q$ to $[0,1]^n\times K$ and taking the convex hull preserves the core fibers if and only if $A\subseteq K$. The new projection has just one outside integer base $p$ if and only if $p\in[-1,2]^n$. At each such $p$ one can realize arbitrarily small positive added volume.

For any body with this fixed core, let $E$ be the sum of the $d$-volumes of its outside integer fibers. If $E<2^{-d}V$, every nonempty integer fiber, including any of zero volume, has base in $[-1,2]^n$. The constant $2^{-d}$ is the sharp infimum of $E/V$ in the presence of a farther nonempty base. For $0\in K$, adding $tK$ at $-2e_1$ for $0<t<1$ gives just the two extra fibers $tK$ and $((1+t)/2)K$ and has $E/V=t^d+((1+t)/2)^d$.

For an outside shell base $p$, let $q(p)$ be its coordinatewise clamp to $[0,1]$. Its opposite partner $2q(p)-p$ is in $Q$ and differs from $q(p)$. Define a group for each anchor $q\in Q$ by collecting the outside shell bases with $q(p)=q$, allowing empty groups. Within each group the partners are distinct. Under $E<2^{-d}V$, some group has total mass at least $E/2^n$.

## Proof

Convexity of the fixed-core body implies it contains the product $[0,1]^n\times K$. Let $p$ be an outside integer base with nonempty fiber $A$. Set $q_i=0$ if $p_i\le0$ and $q_i=1$ if $p_i\ge1$. These exhaust the possibilities. For sufficiently small $\alpha>0$, $(q-\alpha p)/(1-\alpha)\in[0,1]^n$, as seen in each coordinate. Convexity gives $\alpha A+(1-\alpha)K\subseteq K$ at base $q$. For every support direction, this implies $\alpha h_A+(1-\alpha)h_K\le h_K$ and hence $h_A\le h_K$. Therefore $A\subseteq K$. Conversely, if $A\subseteq K$ every generator of the single-addition hull has continuous coordinates in $K$ and all core fibers remain $K$.

If $p\in[-1,2]^n$, any outside integer point of the projection of the single-addition hull has some coordinate $-1$ or $2$. Attaining that coordinate forces all convex weight onto the single generator $p$, so this point is $p$ itself and its fiber is $A$. Shrinking a positive homothet of $K$ inside $K$ realizes all sufficiently small positive masses at $p$.

For arbitrary outside $p$, let $v\in Q$ have the same coordinatewise parity as $p$. Then $w=(p+v)/2$ is integral. If some $p_i\le-2$ or $p_i\ge3$, then $w$ is outside $Q$ and differs from $p$. In the single-addition hull it is a second outside base. In any body with a point $x$ in the fiber at $p$, the fiber at $w$ contains $(x+K)/2$. Its volume is at least $2^{-d}V$, even if the fiber at $p$ itself has zero volume. This proves both the necessity in the census and the mass barrier.

For the sharpness family at $-2e_1$, let $\alpha$ be the weight of the added generator at an outside base in a convex combination. A negative first coordinate forces $\alpha>0$, and the other coordinates are in $[0,1-\alpha]$ and thus vanish when integral. Only the first-coordinate levels $-2$ and $-1$ are possible. The first forces weight one, giving $tK$. At $-1$ all weights satisfy $\alpha\ge1/2$, with $\alpha=1/2$ possible at the core point of base zero. The continuous set for each feasible weight is $(\alpha t+1-\alpha)K$, and these are nested because $0\in K$ and $t<1$. The largest is $((1+t)/2)K$. This proves the formula and the limit.

On the shell the same parity representative obeys $q(p)=(p+v)/2$, which follows by checking $p_i=-1,0,1,2$. Hence $v=2q(p)-p$ is binary and differs from $q(p)$ because $p$ is outside. The map is injective in each fixed group. The $2^n$ groups, including empty ones of mass zero, partition the outside mass. Their maximum mass is at least $E/2^n$.
