## Statement

Let \(d,m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a compact
convex body, and let
\[
 (t_j,p_j)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq m.
\tag{1}
\]
Write \(\lambda_K:=\mathcal L^d|_K\).  The following conditions are
equivalent.

(i) Simultaneous packing.  There are Lebesgue-measurable densities
\(f_j:K\to[0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m f_j\leq1\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.},
 \qquad
 \int_K f_j\,d\lambda_K=t_j,
 \qquad
 \int_K x f_j(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)=t_jp_j
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(j\).

(ii) Max-affine dual inequalities.  For every choice of affine functions
\[
 \ell_j(x)=a_j+\langle v_j,x\rangle,
 \qquad a_j\in\mathbb R,\quad v_j\in\mathbb R^d,
\tag{3}
\]
one has the max-affine packing inequality
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j=1}^m t_j\ell_j(p_j)
 \leq
 \int_K\max\{0,\ell_1(x),\ldots,\ell_m(x)\}\,d\lambda_K(x).}
\tag{4}
\]

(iii) Nonnegative convex order.  With
\[
 \nu:=\sum_{j=1}^m t_j\delta_{p_j},
\tag{5}
\]
one has the nonnegative convex-order inequalities
\[
 \boxed{
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\Phi\,d\nu
 =
 \sum_{j=1}^m t_j\Phi(p_j)
 \leq
 \int_K\Phi(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)}
\tag{6}
\]
for every finite-valued nonnegative convex function
\(\Phi:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)\).  Thus (6) is the exact
sub-convex order \(\nu\preceq_{\mathrm{cx},+}\lambda_K\), with the order
defined by (6).

For comparison, the mass-\(t\) metronoid used below is
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kxf(x)\,d\lambda_K(x):
 0\leq f\leq1,\ \int_Kf\,d\lambda_K=t
 \right\}.
\]

Now let \(T\) be a finite rooted tree.  Give every node \(v\) a packet
\((t_v,p_v)\), with \(t_v>0\), and suppose every nonleaf node satisfies
\[
 t_v=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}t_w,
 \qquad
 t_vp_v=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}t_wp_w.
 \tag{7}
\]
Call \(T\) physically realizable in \(K\) if there are nonnegative
measurable densities \(f_v\) with the prescribed mass and first moment,
\[
 f_v=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}f_w
 \quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.}
 \tag{8}
\]
at every nonleaf node, and \(f_{\mathrm{root}}\leq1\) almost everywhere.
If \(L(T)\) denotes the leaf set, then \(T\) is physically realizable if
and only if, for every family of leaf-affine functions
\(\ell_\ell(x)=a_\ell+\langle v_\ell,x\rangle\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\ell\in L(T)}t_\ell\ell_\ell(p_\ell)
 \leq
 \int_K
 \max\{0,\max_{\ell\in L(T)}\ell_\ell(x)\}\,d\lambda_K(x).}
 \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in L(T)}t_\ell\Phi(p_\ell)
 \leq\int_K\Phi\,d\lambda_K
 \tag{10}
\]
for every finite-valued nonnegative convex \(\Phi\).

The criterion cannot be replaced by individual metronoid membership, even
when one retains exact mass and first-moment conservation, reflection
symmetry, equal child masses, and balanced exposed directions.  Indeed, fix
\[
 d\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq2},\qquad
 t:=\frac1{4N},\qquad K:=[0,1]^d,
 \tag{11}
\]
and, with the empty tail omitted when \(d=1\), put
\[
 p_-:=\left(\frac t2,\frac12,\ldots,\frac12\right),\qquad
 p_+:=\left(1-\frac t2,\frac12,\ldots,\frac12\right),\qquad
 p_0:=\left(\frac12,\ldots,\frac12\right).
 \tag{12}
\]
Take one parent packet \((1/2,p_0)\), \(N\) child packets \((t,p_-)\), and
\(N\) child packets \((t,p_+)\).  The parent and its children have the same
total mass and first moment:
\[
 2Nt=\frac12,\qquad
 Nt(p_-+p_+)=\frac14(1,\ldots,1)=\frac12p_0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The parent is represented by
\[
 A_0:=[1/4,3/4]\times[0,1]^{d-1},
 \tag{14}
\]
and the two child types are represented by
\[
 A_-:=[0,t]\times[0,1]^{d-1},\qquad
 A_+:=[1-t,1]\times[0,1]^{d-1}.
 \tag{15}
\]
More precisely, among all \(0\leq f\leq1\), the unique representation of
\((t,p_-)\) is \(f=\mathbf1_{A_-}\) almost everywhere, and the unique
representation of \((t,p_+)\) is \(f=\mathbf1_{A_+}\) almost everywhere.

For any collection of nonnegative densities satisfying the masses and
first moments of all \(2N\) children, define its congestion by
\[
 \kappa:=\left\|\sum_j f_j\right\|_{L^\infty(K)}\in[0,\infty].
 \tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\kappa\geq N.}
 \tag{17}
\]
The slab indicators attain equality, so the minimum congestion is exactly
\(N\).  In particular, the children cannot be packed under unit physical
capacity.

An explicit certificate for (17), and for the failure of (4), assigns
\[
 \ell_-(x):=t-x_1
\quad\text{to every bottom child},\qquad
 \ell_+(x):=x_1-(1-t)
\quad\text{to every top child}.
 \tag{18}
\]
For the aggregate child measure
\[
 \beta_N:=Nt\delta_{p_-}+Nt\delta_{p_+}
 =\frac14\delta_{p_-}+\frac14\delta_{p_+},
 \tag{19}
\]
any coefficient \(c_N\geq0\) satisfying the common halfspace domination
\[
 c_N\beta_N(H)\leq\lambda_K(H)
 \quad\text{for every closed halfspace }H\subseteq\mathbb R^d
 \tag{20}
\]
must obey
\[
 \boxed{c_N\leq\frac1{2N}.}
 \tag{21}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the equivalence of (2) and (4), including attainment of the
moment image.  Regard \(L^\infty(K)\) as the dual of \(L^1(K)\), with its
weak-star topology \(\sigma(L^\infty,L^1)\), and define
\[
 \mathcal F:=
 \left\{(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\in L^\infty(K)^m:
 f_j\geq0,\ \sum_{j=1}^m f_j\leq1\ \lambda_K\text{-a.e.}\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Every coordinate of an element of \(\mathcal F\) lies in the closed unit
ball of \(L^\infty(K)\).  For nonnegative \(g\in L^1(K)\), the inequalities
\[
 \int_K f_jg\,d\lambda_K\geq0,\qquad
 \int_K\left(1-\sum_jf_j\right)g\,d\lambda_K\geq0
\tag{23}
\]
are weak-star closed conditions.  Conversely, (23) for all nonnegative
\(g\) is equivalent to the corresponding almost-everywhere inequalities.
Thus \(\mathcal F\) is a weak-star closed subset of the finite product of
closed unit balls.  Banach-Alaoglu, followed by finite-product compactness,
shows that \(\mathcal F\) is weak-star compact.  It is plainly convex and
nonempty.

Define the finite-dimensional moment map
\[
 \mathcal T(f_1,\ldots,f_m):=
 \left(\int_Kf_j\,d\lambda_K,\int_Kxf_j(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)\right)_{j=1}^m.
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(K\) is bounded and has finite measure, the functions \(1,x_1,
\ldots,x_d\) restricted to \(K\) belong to \(L^1(K)\).  Hence every
coordinate of \(\mathcal T\) is weak-star continuous.  Its image
\[
 \mathcal C:=\mathcal T(\mathcal F)\subseteq\mathbb R^{m(d+1)}
\tag{25}
\]
is therefore a nonempty compact convex set.

For affine functions (3), let
\[
 M(x):=\max\{0,\ell_1(x),\ldots,\ell_m(x)\}.
\tag{26}
\]
At every \(x\), nonnegativity and \(\sum_jf_j(x)\leq1\) give
\[
 \sum_jf_j(x)\ell_j(x)\leq M(x).
\tag{27}
\]
The resulting upper bound is attained measurably.  Order the labels and set
\[
 E_j:=
 K\cap\{\ell_j>0\}
 \cap\bigcap_{i<j}\{\ell_j>\ell_i\}
 \cap\bigcap_{i>j}\{\ell_j\geq\ell_i\}.
\tag{28}
\]
These are pairwise disjoint Borel sets.  They assign every point where
\(M>0\) to its first maximizing label, including all positive tie and
boundary sets.  Taking \(f_j=\mathbf1_{E_j}\) gives
\(\sum_jf_j\leq1\) and
\[
 \sum_jf_j(x)\ell_j(x)=M(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in K.
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently the support function of \(\mathcal C\) in the direction
\((a_j,v_j)_j\) is exactly
\[
 \sup_{(f_j)\in\mathcal F}
 \sum_j\left(a_j\int_Kf_j+\left\langle
 v_j,\int_Kxf_j\right\rangle\right)
 =
 \int_KM\,d\lambda_K.
\tag{30}
\]

If (2) holds, integrating (27) proves (4).  Conversely, suppose (2) fails.
Then the target moment vector
\[
 y:=((t_j,t_jp_j))_{j=1}^m
\tag{31}
\]
does not belong to the compact convex set \(\mathcal C\).  Strict
finite-dimensional separation of a point from a compact convex set gives
coefficients \((a_j,v_j)_j\) such that
\[
 \sum_jt_j(a_j+\langle v_j,p_j\rangle)
 >
 \sup_{z\in\mathcal C}\sum_j(a_j,v_j)\mathbin{\cdot}z_j.
\tag{32}
\]
By (30), the right side is the integral in (4), contradicting (4).
Therefore (4) implies (2).  This also proves that no closure or unattained
moment-image case is hidden in the criterion.

Assume (2), and let \(\Phi:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)\) be finite-valued and
convex.  Such a function is continuous, hence bounded on the compact set
\(K\).  For each \(j\), the probability measure
\(t_j^{-1}f_j\lambda_K\) has barycenter \(p_j\).  Jensen's inequality gives
\[
 t_j\Phi(p_j)\leq\int_Kf_j(x)\Phi(x)\,d\lambda_K(x).
\tag{33}
\]
After summing, nonnegativity of \(\Phi\) and \(\sum_jf_j\leq1\) give (6).
Thus (2) implies (6).

Conversely, assume (6) and fix affine functions (3).  The function \(M\)
in (26) is finite-valued, nonnegative, and convex.  Since
\(\ell_j(p_j)\leq M(p_j)\) for each \(j\), (6) applied to \(M\) yields
\[
 \sum_jt_j\ell_j(p_j)
 \leq\sum_jt_jM(p_j)
 \leq\int_KM\,d\lambda_K.
\tag{34}
\]
This is (4), so (6) implies (2).  The three finite-packet conditions are
equivalent.

We next prove the tree criterion.  If nested node densities exist, repeated
use of (8) gives
\[
 f_{\mathrm{root}}=\sum_{\ell\in L(T)}f_\ell\leq1
\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.}
\tag{35}
\]
Thus the leaf packets are jointly realizable and satisfy (9) and (10).
Conversely, suppose the leaf packets have a joint realization.  For every
node \(v\), define
\[
 f_v:=\sum_{\substack{\ell\in L(T)\\\ell\text{ descends from }v}}f_\ell.
\tag{36}
\]
These finite sums are measurable and nonnegative, they satisfy (8), and
\(f_{\mathrm{root}}\leq1\).  Induction from (7) gives
\[
 t_v=\sum_{\ell\text{ below }v}t_\ell,\qquad
 t_vp_v=\sum_{\ell\text{ below }v}t_\ell p_\ell.
\tag{37}
\]
Therefore (36) has the prescribed mass and first moment at every node.
Applying the already proved finite-packet equivalences to the leaves proves
(9) and (10).

It remains to verify every assertion in the cube family.  The sets in
(14)-(15) have respective volumes \(1/2,t,t\).  Their product structure
gives barycenters \(p_0,p_-,p_+\), including when \(d=1\).  This proves the
claimed individual feasibility and, together with (11)-(12), proves (13).

We prove uniqueness of the child representations.  Let \(0\leq f\leq1\)
almost everywhere on \(K\), suppose \(\int_Kf=t\), and define its first
coordinate marginal
\[
 g(s):=\int_{[0,1]^{d-1}}f(s,y)\,dy,\qquad 0\leq s\leq1,
\tag{38}
\]
where for \(d=1\) this means \(g=f\).  Fubini gives
\[
 0\leq g\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\qquad \int_0^1g(s)\,ds=t.
\tag{39}
\]
Using equality of the two masses,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^1sg(s)\,ds-\frac{t^2}{2}
 &=
 \int_0^1s\bigl(g(s)-\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(s)\bigr)\,ds\\
 &=
 \int_0^1(s-t)
 \bigl(g(s)-\mathbf1_{[0,t]}(s)\bigr)\,ds
 \geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
The integrand in the last line is nonnegative: both factors are
nonpositive below \(t\), while both are nonnegative above \(t\).
Equality forces \(g=1\) almost everywhere on \((0,t)\) and \(g=0\) almost
everywhere on \((t,1)\).  If the barycenter is \(p_-\), its first moment is
\(t^2/2\), so equality holds.  For almost every \(s<t\), a function taking
values in \([0,1]\) on a set of volume one and having integral one equals
one almost everywhere; for almost every \(s>t\), integral zero forces it to
equal zero.  A second use of Fubini gives
\[
 f=\mathbf1_{A_-}\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.}
\tag{41}
\]

Similarly,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t-\frac{t^2}{2}-\int_0^1sg(s)\,ds
 &=
 \int_0^1s\bigl(\mathbf1_{[1-t,1]}(s)-g(s)\bigr)\,ds\\
 &=
 \int_0^1(s-(1-t))
 \bigl(\mathbf1_{[1-t,1]}(s)-g(s)\bigr)\,ds
 \geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
Equality holds only for \(g=\mathbf1_{[1-t,1]}\) almost everywhere.
The first moment of a packet at \(p_+\) is
\(t(1-t/2)=t-t^2/2\), so the same Fubini argument yields
\[
 f=\mathbf1_{A_+}\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.}
\tag{43}
\]
This proves exact slab uniqueness, with all endpoint differences confined
to null sets.

We now compute the max-affine certificate.  Each bottom label contributes
\[
 t\ell_-(p_-)=\frac{t^2}{2},
\tag{44}
\]
and each top label contributes
\[
 t\ell_+(p_+)=\frac{t^2}{2}.
\tag{45}
\]
Thus the left side of (4) is
\[
 Nt^2.
\tag{46}
\]
Since \(t\leq1/8\), the positive supports of \(\ell_-\) and \(\ell_+\) are
disjoint except for irrelevant boundary sets, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_K\max\{0,\ell_-(x),\ell_+(x)\}\,dx
 &=
 \int_0^t(t-s)\,ds
 +\int_{1-t}^1(s-(1-t))\,ds\\
 &=t^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
The same two scores are assigned to repeated labels, so the pointwise
maximum is still the function in (47).  Because \(N>1\), (46) is strictly
larger than (47).  This explicitly separates the child moment vector from
the unit-capacity moment image.

The certificate also gives the exact congestion bound without assuming
that the competing densities are individually bounded by one.  If
\(\kappa<\infty\) in (16), then pointwise
\[
 \sum_jf_j(x)\ell_j(x)
 \leq
 \left(\sum_jf_j(x)\right)
 \max\{0,\ell_-(x),\ell_+(x)\}.
\tag{48}
\]
Integrating (48), then using the prescribed moments and (47), gives
\[
 Nt^2\leq\kappa t^2.
\tag{49}
\]
Hence \(\kappa\geq N\); the case \(\kappa=\infty\) is immediate.  Taking
all bottom densities equal to \(\mathbf1_{A_-}\) and all top densities
equal to \(\mathbf1_{A_+}\) gives total density \(N\) on
\(A_-\cup A_+\) and zero elsewhere, so equality is attained.

The reflection
\[
 R(x):=(1,\ldots,1)-x
\tag{50}
\]
preserves \(K\), fixes \(p_0\), sends \(p_-\) to \(p_+\), and sends \(A_-\)
to \(A_+\).  Assigning direction \(-e_1\) to every bottom packet and
\(+e_1\) to every top packet gives
\[
 Nt(-e_1)+Nt(e_1)=0.
\tag{51}
\]
Thus reflection and balanced directions do not remove the packing
obstruction.

Finally, consider the proper closed halfspace
\[
 H_+:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_1\geq1-t/2\}.
\tag{52}
\]
It contains \(p_+\) on its boundary and excludes \(p_-\).  Therefore
\[
 \beta_N(H_+)=Nt=\frac14,\qquad
 \lambda_K(H_+)=\frac t2.
\tag{53}
\]
Applying (20) to this boundary-containing halfspace gives
\[
 \frac{c_N}{4}\leq\frac t2,
\qquad
 c_N\leq2t=\frac1{2N},
\tag{54}
\]
which proves (21).

We finish by recording the exact scope of the result.  Individual
feasibility means only that each \(p_j\) lies in its
mass-\(t_j\) metronoid, equivalently that one density can be chosen after
all other labels are discarded.  Simultaneous packing means that all
labels share one pointwise capacity constraint, and its exact tests are
(4) and (6).  The cube family satisfies the former and fails the latter.
Mass and first-moment conservation in (7) do not bridge this gap.

An actual roundwise no-reuse ledger is stronger than individual
feasibility: it already supplies simultaneous densities under the
available density at that round.  Such ledgers compose across rounds only
when the selected parent density itself becomes the next available
capacity, so that every child family satisfies
\[
 \sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}f_w\leq f_v
\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.}
\tag{55}
\]
Under the mass conservation in (7), the nonnegative difference between the
two sides of (55) has integral zero, so (55) forces equality and is exactly
the nested tree condition above.  Rechecking a descendant only in the
metronoid of the full body \(K\), rather than in the relative metronoid of
the retained density \(f_v\lambda_K\), does not establish (55).  Unique
exposed-slab representations show that changing witnesses cannot repair
that reset.  Randomization cannot repair it either, because expected
allocation densities would still satisfy the same linear moment and
capacity conditions and hence would contradict (4).

In particular, the counterexample does not challenge any accepted
single-round common-density or simultaneous-transport ledger.  Such a
ledger proves its stated one-round capacity conclusion.  What does not
follow merely from one-round feasibility is a cumulative leaf inequality
after later rounds reset to the original full fibers.  For any finite
canonical horizon, cumulative physical no-reuse must instead be proved by
applying (9), or equivalently (10), to all leaves attributed to each
original physical source fiber.  No assertion here says that the cube
packet family occurs in the canonical construction, closes a canonical
hard branch, or disproves the locked weak Oertel statement.
