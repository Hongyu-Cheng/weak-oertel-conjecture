## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be nonempty and finite, with $n\ge0$ and $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$. Let $d\ge1$ and let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies with nonempty interior for $z\in P$. Put $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$ and $C=\operatorname{conv}S$, and assume $C\cap(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d)=\{z\}\times K_z$ for each $z\in P$. All hulls and affine coefficients are relative to $\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d$. Write $dm=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes dX|_{K_z}$ and $M=m(S)$. Let $L$ be positive, continuous, and concave on $C$, with $\int_S\log L\,dm/M=0$. Fix $k\ge1$ marks $Y_i=(p_i,y_i)$ with $p_i\in P$ and $y_i\in\operatorname{int}K_{p_i}$, and write $l_i=L(Y_i)$ and $l=(l_i)_{i=1}^k$.

Assume there is at least one contact certificate. This means weights $\sigma_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i\sigma_i=1$ and, for each $\sigma_i>0$, a probability measure $\rho_i$ on $S$ with
$$
\int_S w\,d\rho_i=Y_i,\qquad
\int_S L\,d\rho_i=l_i,\qquad
\sum_{i:\,\sigma_i>0}\frac{\sigma_i}{l_i}\rho_i=\frac{dm}{ML}.
\tag{1}
$$
Let $\mathcal W$ be the set of all weight vectors of such certificates. For $b\in(0,\infty)^k$, let $H_C(b)$ be the supremum of $M^{-1}\int_S\log U\,dm$ over positive continuous concave functions $U$ on $C$ with $U(Y_i)\le b_i$ for all $i$.

For every such $U$ and every $\sigma\in\mathcal W$ one has
$$
\frac1M\int_S\log(U/L)\,dm
\le\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i\log\frac{U(Y_i)}{l_i}.
\tag{2}
$$
This also holds for continuous nonnegative concave $U$ on $C$ that are positive in the interior of every sampled fiber and have integrable sampled $\log U$. If $U(Y_i)\le l_i$ for all $i$ and the left-hand side is zero, then $U=L$ on every closed sampled fiber.

The function $H_C$ is finite, concave, and coordinatewise nondecreasing on the positive orthant, with $H_C(cb)=H_C(b)+\log c$ for $c>0$. One has $H_C(l)=0$, attained by $L$, and
$$
\partial^+H_C(l)=\left\{\left(\frac{\sigma_i}{l_i}\right)_{i=1}^k:\ \sigma\in\mathcal W\right\},
\tag{3}
$$
where $\partial^+$ consists of the supergradients for the concave function. In particular $\mathcal W$ is compact and convex, and for every $h\in\mathbb R^k$ and $t\downarrow0$,
$$
H_C(l+th+o(t))=t\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal W}\sum_i \frac{\sigma_i h_i}{l_i}+o(t).
\tag{4}
$$

## Proof

For each positive row the tilted probability $d\bar\rho_i=(L/l_i)\,d\rho_i$ has $\sum_i\sigma_i\bar\rho_i=dm/M$. Also
$\int (U/L)\,d\bar\rho_i=l_i^{-1}\int U\,d\rho_i\le U(Y_i)/l_i$ by concave Jensen. Applying logarithmic Jensen in each tilted row and summing gives (2). The same argument works in the stated nonnegative case: each row is absolutely continuous with respect to $m$, each fiber boundary is null, and integrability for $m$ implies integrability for each positive tilted row. If all marked bounds are $l_i$ and the left-hand side is zero, all positive-weight row inequalities are equalities. Strict concavity of $\log$ makes $U/L$ constant in each positive tilted row, and equality with the bound zero makes that constant one. Their mixture is $dm/M$. Every relatively open nonempty subset of a full-dimensional closed convex fiber has positive Lebesgue volume, so continuity gives equality everywhere on the sampled fibers.

A positive constant competitor gives a finite lower bound for $H_C$. Formula (2) gives the upper bound $\sum_i\sigma_i\log(b_i/l_i)$ for each certificate. Convex combinations of feasible functions at two different bounds are feasible at the same combination of bounds, and concavity of $\log$ then gives concavity of the value by taking approximating competitors. Monotonicity follows from inclusion of feasible sets and scaling follows by multiplying and dividing competitors by $c$. Formula (2) and the feasible $L$ give $H_C(l)=0$ and the maximizing sampled restriction. The inequality $\log x\le x-1$ for $x>0$ gives one inclusion in (3).

For the converse, take $g\in\partial^+H_C(l)$, so $H_C(b)\le g\cdot(b-l)$ for every $b>0$. Testing a positive increment in coordinate $i$ and using monotonicity gives $g_i\ge0$. Testing $b=cl$ and differentiating the two signs at $c=1$ gives $\sum_i g_i l_i=1$. For any continuous $f$ on $C$ with $f+aL$ concave for some $a\ge0$, the function $L+tf=(1-ta)L+t(f+aL)$ is positive and concave for all sufficiently small positive $t$. Use its own marked values as bounds and divide the resulting supergradient inequality by $t$. Since $L$ has a positive minimum and $f$ is bounded, differentiation of the logarithmic integral gives
$$
\sum_i g_i f(Y_i)\ge \frac1M\int_S \frac{f}{L}\,dm .
\tag{5}
$$
Both signs of every affine $f$ give equal masses and equal full-space first moments for $\mu=\sum_i g_i\delta_{Y_i}$ and $\nu=dm/(ML)$. The choice $f=-\varphi$ for continuous convex $\varphi$ gives $\int\varphi\,d\mu\le\int\varphi\,d\nu$.

To construct the rows, discard $g_i=0$. Consider the moment vectors $\big(\int q_i\,d\nu,\int w q_i\,d\nu\big)_i$ of measurable allocations $0\le q_i\le1$, $\sum_i q_i=1$ almost everywhere for $\nu$. The allocation set is bounded and weakly closed in the finite product of $L^2(\nu)$, hence weakly compact. The moment maps are weakly continuous because $1$ and the coordinates of $w$ are bounded on $S$. Thus the moment image is compact and convex. If $\big(g_i,g_iY_i\big)_i$ were outside the image, strict separation would give affine scores $A_i$ with
$$
\sum_i g_i A_i(Y_i)>\sup_{(q_i)}\int_S\sum_i q_i A_i\,d\nu
=\int_S\max_i A_i\,d\nu .
$$
The equality assigns each point to the first maximizing score, a measurable allocation. Applying the convex test $\max_i A_i$ gives the opposite inequality, so a desired allocation exists. Its probability rows $d\rho_i=(q_i/g_i)\,d\nu$ have the prescribed barycenters. Concave Jensen gives $\int L\,d\rho_i\le l_i$. These inequalities are equalities row by row because $\sum_i g_i\int L\,d\rho_i=\int L\,d\nu=1=\sum_i g_i l_i$. Thus $\sigma_i=g_i l_i$ and the rows satisfy (1), completing (3).

The finite concave value on an open orthant is locally Lipschitz with nonempty, locally bounded superdifferentials and closed supergradient graph there. These properties and (3) give compactness and convexity of $\mathcal W$. For $b=l+th$, the upper difference quotient is at most $\min_{g\in\partial^+H_C(l)}g\cdot h$. A supergradient $g_t$ at $b$ bounds the quotient below by $g_t\cdot h$. Every convergent subsequence of these locally bounded $g_t$ has its limit in $\partial^+H_C(l)$, so the lower limit is at least the same minimum. Local Lipschitz continuity permits the $o(t)$ perturbation of $b$ in (4).
