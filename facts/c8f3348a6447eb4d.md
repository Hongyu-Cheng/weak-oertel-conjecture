## Statement

Consider a continuous variation of the sampled endpoints of an affine planar interval roof, with affine centers on the sampled sites. Near a parameter value assume the retained sampled bases are fixed and their total length tends to a positive value. Suppose the only additional sampled site, when present, is an exposed extreme site and its length tends to zero. Then the global maximum normalized closed mixed halfspace depth is continuous through this entry or loss.

## Proof

At each parameter value center the fibers by the affine height shear, which preserves the sampled depth problem. Missing intervals are assigned length zero for purposes of the cap mass. The centered midpoint at each retained base maximizes that fiber's depth by height reflection and convexity of depth superlevel sets. For an oblique through-midpoint cap at base $j$, the mass is $\sum_i [w_i/2+v(i-j)]_0^{w_i}$ for a real slope $v$, where the brackets clip between $0$ and $w_i$. Each summand changes by at most $3|w_i-w_i'|/2$ when its width changes from $w_i$ to $w_i'$, uniformly in $v$, by the two successive clipping operations. Therefore the infimum over slopes is continuous for each retained base. The opposite oblique orientation gives the same family of masses, and a purely base cap has mass at least that of a corresponding sufficiently steep oblique cap. An exposed endpoint site has maximum depth half its own length, since a sufficiently steep through-point cap isolates the corresponding side of its interval. A zero-width exposed point has depth zero by the exposing base cap. The maximum over the retained sites and this vanishing endpoint contribution is continuous, and division by the positive limiting total length preserves continuity.
