## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and retain one common instantiation of the canonical zero-fresh hard system of accepted fact `6bfe50fed9a5540b` and its self-saturated switch atlas from accepted fact `cb96640c07ffd5fa`. Write
\[
 C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
put \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and define
\[
 \mu_C(A):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in A\},
\]
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in S}
 \inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For a nonempty compact convex set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), write
\[
 h_K(u):=\max_{x\in K}\langle u,x\rangle,
 \qquad
 F_K(u):=\{x\in K:\langle u,x\rangle=h_K(u)\}.
\]
For \(0<s\leq a_z\), define
\[
 \mathsf M_s(C_z):=
 \left\{
 \frac1s\int_{C_z}y f(y)\,dy:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \int_{C_z}f\,dy=s
 \right\}.
\]
Let \(Q>0\) be the canonical reservoir mass, let
\[
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
and retain the canonical residual-selector capacity \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. In particular, if
\[
 \delta_C(z,y):=
 \inf\left\{
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
 J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace},\ y\in J
 \right\},
\]
then \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) is the supremum of
\[
 2^{n-\dim\operatorname{aff}E}
 \frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}
 {1+\dim\operatorname{span}\{y_z-s(z):z\in E\}}
\tag{1}
\]
over finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\), choices \(y_z\in C_z\), weights \(0<w_z\leq\delta_C(z,y_z)\), and affine maps \(s:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R^d\). The hard ledger gives
\[
 G<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 D+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]

The switch atlas has a finite nonempty label set \(I\), positive self-saturated loads \(q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}>0\), and
\[
 \Omega_b:=\sum_{j\in I}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
 >\frac{97}{100}Q.
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(\pi_i:=q_i^{\mathrm{sat}}/\Omega_b\), so \(\sum_i\pi_i=1\), and retain its parameter \(0<t\leq1\). Every label \(j\in I\) has an integral physical target \(m_j\), a mass \(0<\vartheta_j\leq a_{m_j}\), and a nonempty compact convex body
\[
 L_j\subseteq\mathsf M_{\vartheta_j}(C_{m_j}).
\tag{4}
\]
For every physical target \(m\), the shared budget is
\[
 \sum_{j\in I:m_j=m}
 \frac{q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\vartheta_j}\leq1.
\tag{5}
\]

Retain the canonical directions \(u_i\). For every arbitrary permitted simultaneous choice of contacts
\[
 p_{ij}\in F_{L_j}(u_i)\subseteq L_j
 \qquad(i,j\in I),
\tag{6}
\]
put
\[
 b_{ik}^{(j)}:=
 \frac{tq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}{2}\pi_i\pi_k,
 \qquad
 c_j^{\mathrm{sw}}:=\sum_{i\in I}\pi_ip_{ij}.
\tag{7}
\]
For every physical target used by the atlas, define
\[
 I_m:=\{j\in I:m_j=m\},
 \qquad
 \eta_m:=\sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}>0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
 c_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 :=\frac1{\eta_m}
 \sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}c_j^{\mathrm{sw}},
 \qquad
 g_{ik}^{(j,m)}
 :=\frac{b_{ik}^{(j)}}{\eta_m}(p_{ij}-p_{kj}),
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 :=c_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 +\sum_{j\in I_m}\sum_{i,k\in I}
 [-g_{ik}^{(j,m)},g_{ik}^{(j,m)}].
\tag{10}
\]
Set
\[
 E_{\mathrm{sw}}:=\{m:\eta_m>0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 :=\frac1{\eta_m}
 \sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}L_j
 \quad(m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}),
\tag{11}
\]
where the sum in (11) is the full weighted Minkowski set, with one independent choice from each labelled copy of \(L_j\). Then, for every \(m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m).}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, \(0<\eta_m\leq a_m\), and every \(x\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\delta_C(m,x)\geq\gamma_d\eta_m.}
\tag{13}
\]

For every affine map \(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), define its pooled-body hit mass by
\[
 H_{\mathrm{sw}}(a):=
 \sum_{\substack{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\\
 a(m)\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}}}\eta_m.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{H_{\mathrm{sw}}(a)\leq G,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\\
 a(m)\notin\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}}}\eta_m
 >\frac{24}{25}Q.}
\tag{15}
\]

Finally, put
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{sw}}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}E_{\mathrm{sw}}.
\]
For every simultaneous independent selection
\[
 x_m\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}
 \qquad(m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}),
\]
define
\[
 r(x):=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}E_{\mathrm{sw}}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{x_m-s(m):m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then the two distinct hard summands obey
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_d\Omega_b
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}}{r(x)+1},}
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq
 \gamma_d\Omega_b
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}}{r(x)+1}.}
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 r(x)+1
 >200\gamma_d\frac{\Omega_b}{Q}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}
 >194\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}.}
\tag{18}
\]
All conclusions hold for every permitted contact choice in (6), including all collisions among physical target labels and all collisions among contact points. They make no assertion that the stationary switch energy is absorbed by \(G\), \(D\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), or any other terminal capacity.

## Proof

Fix the retained canonical instance and an arbitrary permitted simultaneous contact choice (6). We first prove the switch-to-pooled inclusion by collecting every coefficient exactly. Fix \(m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\) and an arbitrary \(x\in\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\). By the definition of the Minkowski sum of the symmetric segments in (10), there are numbers
\[
 \varepsilon_{ik}^{(j)}\in[-1,1]
 \qquad(j\in I_m,\ i,k\in I)
\]
such that
\[
 \eta_mx
 =\sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}c_j^{\mathrm{sw}}
 +\sum_{j\in I_m}\sum_{i,k\in I}
 \varepsilon_{ik}^{(j)}b_{ik}^{(j)}(p_{ij}-p_{kj}).
\tag{19}
\]
For \(j\in I_m\) and \(\ell\in I\), define the collected coefficient
\[
 \zeta_{\ell j}:=
 q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\pi_\ell
 +\sum_{k\in I}\varepsilon_{\ell k}^{(j)}b_{\ell k}^{(j)}
 -\sum_{i\in I}\varepsilon_{i\ell}^{(j)}b_{i\ell}^{(j)}.
\tag{20}
\]
The exact product coefficients in (7) give
\[
 \sum_{k\in I}b_{\ell k}^{(j)}
 +\sum_{i\in I}b_{i\ell}^{(j)}
 =tq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\pi_\ell.
\tag{21}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \zeta_{\ell j}
 \geq q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\pi_\ell
 -\sum_kb_{\ell k}^{(j)}
 -\sum_ib_{i\ell}^{(j)}
 =(1-t)q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\pi_\ell\geq0.
\tag{22}
\]
On summing (20) over \(\ell\), the outgoing and incoming double sums are identical and cancel, so
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in I}\zeta_{\ell j}=q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\tag{23}
\]
Since every \(p_{\ell j}\) lies in the convex set \(L_j\), equations (22)-(23) show that
\[
 \widehat p_j:=\frac1{q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}
 \sum_{\ell\in I}\zeta_{\ell j}p_{\ell j}
 \in L_j.
\tag{24}
\]
Using the definition of \(c_j^{\mathrm{sw}}\), collecting the coefficient of each \(p_{\ell j}\) in (19) gives exactly
\[
 \eta_mx
 =\sum_{j\in I_m}\sum_{\ell\in I}
 \zeta_{\ell j}p_{\ell j}
 =\sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\widehat p_j.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\). This argument permits \(t=1\), zero collected coefficients, degenerate generators, repeated labels at the same physical target, and repeated contact points. Hence it proves the first inclusion in (12) for every allowed contact choice.

For the second inclusion, fix arbitrary independent points \(y_j\in L_j\) for \(j\in I_m\). By (4), each \(y_j\) lies in \(\mathsf M_{\vartheta_j}(C_m)\), and (5) is the exact common-measure budget
\[
 \sum_{j\in I_m}\frac{q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\vartheta_j}\leq1.
\]
Apply accepted fact `43849354cffec2b9` to Lebesgue measure restricted to the single fiber body \(C_m\), with index set \(I_m\), metronoid masses \(\vartheta_j\), weights \(q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\), and subsets \(L_j\). It gives both
\[
 0<\eta_m=\sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\leq a_m
\]
and
\[
 \frac1{\eta_m}\sum_{j\in I_m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}L_j
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m).
\]
This is the full Minkowski-set inclusion, not an inclusion for one preselected tuple. It proves the second inclusion in (12) simultaneously across every label collision at \(m\).

Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied to \(K=C_m\) and metronoid mass \(\eta_m\), says that every point of \(\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m)\) has fiber halfspace depth at least \(\gamma_d\eta_m\). Together with (12), this proves (13), including halfspaces whose boundary contains the selected point.

We next prove the affine hit screen. The label sets \(I_m\) partition the finite nonempty set \(I\), so
\[
 E_{\mathrm{sw}}\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \sum_{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}}\eta_m
 =\sum_{j\in I}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
 =\Omega_b.
\tag{26}
\]
Fix an affine map \(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and let
\[
 J_a:=\{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}:a(m)\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\}.
\]
If \(J_a\ne\varnothing\), then (12) gives
\(a(m)\in\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m)\) for every \(m\in J_a\). Apply accepted fact `10f1feebe06e22d0` with \(L=\mathbb R^n\), \(E=J_a\), masses \(\eta_m\), and the affine selector \(a\). Its full-rank conclusion gives
\[
 G\geq\sum_{m\in J_a}\eta_m=H_{\mathrm{sw}}(a).
\tag{27}
\]
If \(J_a=\varnothing\), the same inequality is immediate from the nonnegativity of \(G\). This proves the first part of (15). Equations (2), (3), (26), and (27) then give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}\\
 a(m)\notin\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}}}\eta_m
 &=\Omega_b-H_{\mathrm{sw}}(a)\\
 &\geq\Omega_b-G\\
 &>\frac{97}{100}Q-\frac1{100}Q
 =\frac{24}{25}Q,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
which proves the second part.

It remains to prove the rank screen. Fix an arbitrary simultaneous independent selection \(x_m\in\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\). Define the finite positive atomic measure on \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\)
\[
 \sigma_x:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{m\in E_{\mathrm{sw}}}
 \eta_m\delta_{(m,x_m)}.
\tag{29}
\]
We claim that \(\sigma_x\) is halfspace dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any closed halfspace and, for an integer base point \(z\), write
\[
 H_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in H\}.
\]
This section is a proper closed halfspace, all of \(\mathbb R^d\), or empty. If \((m,x_m)\in H\) and \(H_m\) is proper, (13) gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_m\cap H_m)\geq\gamma_d\eta_m.
\]
If \(H_m=\mathbb R^d\), then
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_m\cap H_m)=a_m
 \geq\eta_m\geq\gamma_d\eta_m.
\]
An empty section cannot contain \(x_m\). Summing these fiberwise inequalities and then allowing all other fibers yields
\[
 \sigma_x(H)
 \leq\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 =\mu_C(H).
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(\sigma_x\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C\), including the whole-space and empty-halfspace conventions.

The measure in (29) has total mass \(\gamma_d\Omega_b\). Because every \(\eta_m\) is positive, its projected support is exactly \(E_{\mathrm{sw}}\). Hence its intrinsic base rank is \(\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}\), and its continuous residual rank in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` is exactly \(r(x)\). The dominated-measure theorem in that fact gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_d\Omega_b}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}(r(x)+1)}.
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) proves the first lower bound in (17).

For the second lower bound, (13) makes the data
\[
 E=E_{\mathrm{sw}},
 \qquad
 y_m=x_m,
 \qquad
 w_m=\gamma_d\eta_m
\]
admissible in the residual-selector supremum (1). The same accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` guarantees attainment of the affine-map minimum defining \(r(x)\), including \(\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}=0\) and \(r(x)=0\). Choosing such a minimizing affine map in (1) gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}
 \frac{\gamma_d\sum_m\eta_m}{r(x)+1}
 =\gamma_d\Omega_b
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}}{r(x)+1},
\]
which is the second lower bound in (17).

These are lower bounds for two separately defined nonnegative summands of the hard ledger, \(D\) and \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\). Therefore (2) and (17) give
\[
 2\gamma_d\Omega_b
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}}{r(x)+1}
 \leq D+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 <\frac Q{100}.
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(Q,\Omega_b,\gamma_d\) are positive, rearranging (31) yields
\[
 r(x)+1
 >200\gamma_d\frac{\Omega_b}{Q}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{sw}}}.
\]
Finally, (3) gives
\[
 200\frac{\Omega_b}{Q}>194,
\]
which proves the second strict inequality in (18). The proof used the switch zonotope only through exact coefficient collection into the pooled bodies. It did not convert the stationary generator-normal energy into any hard-ledger summand and makes no claim of branch closure.
