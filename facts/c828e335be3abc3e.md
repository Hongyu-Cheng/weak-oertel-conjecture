## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $A,K,B$ be compact convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interiors at the only sampled bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use summed $d$-volume. Suppose $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and the cap $u\cdot(x-y)+\alpha z\ge0$, $u\ne0$, locally minimizes capture under all small normal changes through $(0,y)$. Suppose its $A$ capture is proper and its $B$ capture has volume zero. Write $E_A,E_K$ for the proper sections, $t,s>0$ for their $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ areas, $c_A,c_K$ for their centroids, and $F$ for the possibly empty section on $B$. For every $w\in\mathbb R^d,\beta\in\mathbb R$,
$$
t[w\cdot(c_A-y)-\beta]+s\,w\cdot(c_K-y)+\int_F[w\cdot(x-y)+\beta]_+\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}(x)\ge0.
$$
Consequently $F$ is a positive-area supporting facet, $M=\mathcal H^{d-1}(F)\ge t$, and
$$
s^{1/(d-1)}\ge\frac{t^{1/(d-1)}+M^{1/(d-1)}}2\ge t^{1/(d-1)}.
$$

## Proof

On fiber $z$ the perturbation $(u,\alpha)\mapsto(u+\epsilon w,\alpha+\epsilon\beta)$ changes the original margin by $\epsilon[w\cdot(x-y)+\beta z]$. Use the fixed-fiber slice calculation in the proof of b319e7dcda1486e9. Dividing the old margin and the perturbing margin by $\|u\|$ makes the continuous normal unit. The signed integral of the perturbation on each proper section contributes the $A$ and $K$ terms divided by $\|u\|$. The zero-volume $B$ cut is at or beyond its maximum; the positive-part integral on the empty-side supporting section gives its contribution, with zero for an empty or null section. This slice calculation uses one convex body and its local affine increment. The distinct-mark hypothesis of the final flux inequality of that fact is unnecessary here. Since the right derivative at the local minimum is nonnegative, multiplying it by $\|u\|$ gives the inequality. At $w=0,\beta=1$ it reads $M\ge t>0$, forcing an actual supporting facet. The three thresholds on $A,K,B$ are $u\cdot y+\alpha,u\cdot y,u\cdot y-\alpha$. Midpoint compatibility and this threshold identity give $(E_A+F)/2\subseteq E_K$. Translate the sections into the common space $u^\perp$ and use Brunn–Minkowski in dimension $d-1$.
