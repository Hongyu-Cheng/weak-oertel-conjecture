## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\ge3\), and put
\[
 A_K:=\{0,\ldots,K-1\}^n,
 \qquad B:=[0,2K-2]^n.
\]
Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_x:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(x,y)\in C\},
 \qquad a_x:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)
\]
whenever the fiber has positive volume. Assume that there is \(A>0\) such that
\[
 a_{2a}=A\qquad(a\in A_K).
\tag{1}
\]
Then every \(x\in B\) has \(a_x=A\), there are a convex body \(K_0\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), a linear map \(T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\) such that
\[
 C_x=K_0+Tx+b\qquad(x\in B),
\tag{2}
\]
and the fiber centroids satisfy
\[
 c_x=c(K_0)+Tx+b\qquad(x\in B).
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(q=(q_e)\) be any nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching supported on pairs
\[
 e=\{2a,2a'\},\qquad a,a'\in A_K,\quad a\ne a',
\]
and assume the endpoint bounds required by accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d,
\[
 d_{2a}(q):=\sum_{e\ni2a}q_e\le \frac A2.
\tag{4}
\]
Put \(Q_q:=|q|\). Then
\[
 R_C(q)=Q_q,
 \qquad \Delta_C(q)=0,
\tag{5}
\]
and the gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d is the zero stress:
\[
 v_e=0,\qquad \omega_e=0,\qquad \Omega=0.
\tag{6}

In particular, let \(M:=K^n\) and take the exact masses and edge weights proposed in the finite product-output model,
\[
 A=\frac{4M}{M-1},
 \qquad q_{\{2a,2a'\}}=\frac1{M-1}
 \quad(a\ne a').
\tag{7}
\]
Then (4) holds with \(d_{2a}(q)=1\), and
\[
 Q_q=\frac M2,\qquad R_C(q)=\frac M2,\qquad \Omega=0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus no genuine convex body satisfying (1) can realize the formal assignments
\(\omega_e=q_e\), \(\beta_{2a}=1\), and \(\Omega=Q_q\). If this \(q\) is the canonical reservoir \(r\) in the normalization of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, then its terminal capacity is already
\[
 R=Q.
\tag{9}
\]

There is also a scale-invariant robust centroid obstruction. Let
\(\{h_a:a\in A_K\}\) be an orthonormal family, let
\(\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) be affine, let \(\lambda>0\), and suppose one prescribes
\[
 c_{2a}=\ell(2a)+\lambda h_a+\varepsilon_a,
 \qquad \|\varepsilon_a\|_2\le\delta\lambda
 \quad(a\in A_K).
\tag{10}
\]
If
\[
 \delta<\frac{\sqrt6}{4},
\tag{11}
\]
then no convex body can satisfy both (1) and (10). In particular, the archive's exact choice \(c_{2a}=h_a\), and every nonzero uniform rescaling of that choice, is impossible.

## Proof

For \(x\) in the base projection of \(C\), define
\[
 g(x):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)^{1/d}.
\]
Convexity of \(C\) gives, for \(x,y\) in its base projection and \(0<t<1\),
\[
 (1-t)C_x+tC_y\subseteq C_{(1-t)x+ty}.
\tag{12}
\]
The Brunn--Minkowski inequality applied to (12) shows that \(g\) is concave wherever the fibers have positive volume.

Every vertex of \(B\) is one of the points \(2a\) from (1). Hence concavity and (1) give
\[
 g(x)\ge A^{1/d}\qquad(x\in B),
\tag{13}
\]
because every point of \(B\) is a convex combination of its vertices. The point
\[
 x_0:=(2,\ldots,2)
\]
lies in the interior of \(B\) because \(K\ge3\), and (1) gives
\(g(x_0)=A^{1/d}\). Fix any \(y\in B\). Since \(x_0\) is interior, there are \(z\in B\) and \(0<t<1\) such that
\[
 x_0=(1-t)y+tz.
\]
Concavity and (13) now imply
\[
 A^{1/d}=g(x_0)
 \ge(1-t)g(y)+tg(z)
 \ge A^{1/d}.
\]
Therefore \(g(y)=A^{1/d}\). Since \(y\) was arbitrary,
\[
 a_x=A\qquad(x\in B).
\tag{14}
\]

Fix \(x,y\in B\) and \(0<t<1\). By (14), both endpoint fibers and the target fiber in (12) have volume \(A\). Brunn--Minkowski gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d((1-t)C_x+tC_y)^{1/d}
 \ge (1-t)A^{1/d}+tA^{1/d}=A^{1/d}.
\]
Inclusion (12) gives the reverse volume inequality. Thus equality holds. The equality case of Brunn--Minkowski for positive-volume convex bodies makes \(C_x\) and \(C_y\) homothetic; their equal volumes make them translates. Moreover, the inclusion in (12) is between convex bodies of equal volume, so it is equality:
\[
 C_{(1-t)x+ty}=(1-t)C_x+tC_y.
\tag{15}
\]

Fix one \(x_*\in B\). Since all fibers are translates, write
\[
 C_x=K_0+u(x),
 \qquad K_0:=C_{x_*}.
\]
The translation vector is uniquely determined, for example by the centroid. Since \((1-t)K_0+tK_0=K_0\), equation (15) gives
\[
 u((1-t)x+ty)=(1-t)u(x)+tu(y).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(u\) is affine on the box. Write \(u(x)=Tx+b\), after absorbing a fixed translation into \(K_0\). This proves (2), and translation covariance of the centroid proves (3).

We next prove (5)--(6). For an endpoint \(z=2a\), put
\[
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{A/2}(C_z).
\]
The constant density \(1/2\) on \(C_z\) shows directly that
\[
 c_z\in\mathcal K_z.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(e=\{2a,2a'\}\), accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d uses
\[
 m_e=a+a',
 \qquad L_e=\frac12(\mathcal K_{2a}+\mathcal K_{2a'}).
\]
The affine centroid map from (3), extended to \(\mathbb R^n\), satisfies
\[
 c(m_e)=\frac{c_{2a}+c_{2a'}}2\in L_e
\tag{18}
\]
by (17). Thus one affine map hits every \(L_e\). Equations (6)--(8) of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d give
\[
 R_C(q)=Q_q,\qquad \Delta_C(q)=0.
\]
The zero dual stress is then an optimizer and has gauge load zero. Since the canonical choice minimizes the nonnegative gauge load among all dual optimizers, it has \(\Omega=0\). Every \(D_e\) in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d is full-dimensional, so \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm on the dual space. Hence \(\Omega=\sum_eh_{D_e}(v_e)=0\) also forces \(v_e=0\) and \(\omega_e=0\) for every edge, proving (6).

For (7), each vertex has \(M-1\) incident edges of weight \(1/(M-1)\), so \(d_{2a}(q)=1\). Since
\[
 \frac A2=\frac{2M}{M-1}>1,
\]
condition (4) holds. Also
\[
 Q_q={M\choose2}\frac1{M-1}=\frac M2.
\]
Equations (8)--(9) follow from (5)--(6) and the exact canonical alias in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70.

Finally assume (10). Choose any \(a\in A_K\) and coordinate \(i\) with
\(a_i=1\); the other coordinates may be arbitrary, and \(K\ge3\) ensures that \(a-e_i,a,a+e_i\in A_K\). Affinity of the actual centroid field in (3) forces
\[
 c_{2(a-e_i)}-2c_{2a}+c_{2(a+e_i)}=0.
\tag{19}
\]
The affine term \(\ell\) cancels in (19). The three relevant members of the orthonormal family are distinct, so
\[
 \|h_{a-e_i}-2h_a+h_{a+e_i}\|_2=\sqrt6.
\tag{20}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
 \|\varepsilon_{a-e_i}-2\varepsilon_a+\varepsilon_{a+e_i}\|_2
 \le4\delta\lambda.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (19)--(21) require \(\sqrt6\lambda\le4\delta\lambda\), contradicting (11). This proves the robust obstruction and completes the theorem.

## External sources

R. J. Gardner, "The Brunn--Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355--405, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact result used: for positive-volume convex bodies \(K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t<1\),
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d((1-t)K+tL)^{1/d}
 \ge(1-t)\operatorname{vol}_d(K)^{1/d}
   +t\operatorname{vol}_d(L)^{1/d},
\]
with equality if and only if \(K\) and \(L\) are homothetic. Applicability: every fiber used after (13) is a compact convex set of positive \(d\)-volume by (14), and every Minkowski combination is compact and measurable.
