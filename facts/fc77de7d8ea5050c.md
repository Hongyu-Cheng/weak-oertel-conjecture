## Statement

For each integer \(n\ge3\), set \(d=n\) and let

\[
T=\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:x_j\ge0\ (1\le j\le n),\ \sum_{j=1}^n x_j\le1\right\},
\qquad
L=T\cap\{x_1\ge1/3\}=\tfrac13\mathbf e_1+\tfrac23T,
\]

where \(\mathbf e_1\) is the first standard basis vector of the continuous space \(\mathbb R^n\). Write \(|K|\) for volume in that continuous space. Define \(C=\operatorname{conv}([0,1]^n\times T,[-1,2]^n\times L)\), \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^n\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(W=\mathcal H_n(S)\). Here \(T,L\) are convex bodies with \(L\subseteq T\), and \(C\) is a convex body of the stipulated form, with

\[
|T|=\frac1{n!},
\qquad
\mathbf g_T=\left(\frac1{n+1},\ldots,\frac1{n+1}\right),
\qquad
W=\frac{2^n}{n!}\left(1+(2^n-1)\left(\frac23\right)^n\right).
\tag{1}
\]

The closed ambient halfspace

\[
H=\left\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^{n+n}:\sum_{i=1}^n t_i+3n x_1\le\frac{3n}{n+1}\right\}
\tag{2}
\]

contains \((\mathbf0,\mathbf g_T)\) on its boundary. Its exact actual mass is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal H_n(S\cap H)}{|T|}
={}&\sum_{j=0}^2\binom nj\left[1-\left(\frac n{n+1}+\frac j{3n}\right)^n\right]\\
&+\sum_{j=0}^2\binom{n+j-1}{j}\left[\left(\frac23\right)^n-\left(\frac23-\frac1{n+1}+\frac j{3n}\right)^n\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For every \(\mathbf w\in\{0,1\}^n\), a coordinate-reflected copy of \(H\) contains \((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T)\) and has the same mass. In particular,

\[
\frac{2^n h_S((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T))}{W}
\le
\frac{\dfrac{n^2+n+2}{2}+\dfrac{n^2+3n+2}{2}\left(\dfrac23\right)^n}
{1+(2^n-1)\left(\dfrac23\right)^n}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(n\longrightarrow\infty).
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, for every \(c>0\) there is an integer \(n=d\ge3\) for which this one body satisfies \(h_S((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T))<c\,2^{-n}W\) for every \(\mathbf w\in\{0,1\}^n\). Already \(n=d=24\) gives \(h_S((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T))<W/(3\cdot2^{24})\) at every such centroid, disproving the registered fixed-point assertion.

## Proof

Write \(|K|\) for ordinary \(n\)-dimensional volume in the continuous space. The simplex \(T\) is compact and convex, has nonempty interior, and has volume \(1/n!\) by iterated integration. For every \(0\le b\le1\), the affine map \(\mathbf y\mapsto b\mathbf e_1+(1-b)\mathbf y\) maps \(T\) onto \(T\cap\{x_1\ge b\}\). Its volume multiplier is \((1-b)^n\), with the formula also valid at \(b=1\), when the image has zero \(n\)-dimensional volume. Hence

\[
|T\cap\{x_1\ge b\}|=(1-b)^n|T|,
\qquad
|T\cap\{x_1\le b\}|=\bigl(1-(1-b)^n\bigr)|T|.
\tag{5}
\]

Every level set \(\{x_1=b\}\) has zero \(n\)-dimensional volume. This justifies all closed inequalities in (5) and all slab calculations below. Integrating the first formula in (5) over \(0\le b\le1\) shows that the first centroid coordinate is \(\int_0^1(1-b)^n\,db=1/(n+1)\). Coordinate permutations preserve \(T\), so every centroid coordinate is \(1/(n+1)\). The displayed affine formula for \(L\) is (5)'s geometric map at \(b=1/3\); it proves that \(L\) is a convex body contained in \(T\), with \(|L|=(2/3)^n|T|\).

The set \(C\) is compact and convex and contains \([0,1]^n\times T\), which has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{2n}\). It is therefore a convex body. For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(A_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}\). The complete actual fiber census is

\[
A_{\mathbf z}=\begin{cases}
T,&\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^n,\\
L,&\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^n\setminus\{0,1\}^n,\\
\varnothing,&\mathbf z\notin\{-1,0,1,2\}^n.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, convexity and \(L\subseteq T\) give \(C\subseteq[-1,2]^n\times T\), and the defining inner product supplies the entire fiber \(T\) at every inner integer site. Every other integer site in this box has a coordinate equal to \(-1\) or \(2\). Since both defining products are convex, each point of their convex hull is a convex combination of one point from each product. Such a combination cannot attain that extreme coordinate while assigning positive weight to the inner product, whose corresponding coordinate belongs to \([0,1]\). Its inner weight is therefore zero, and its continuous coordinate belongs to \(L\). The defining outer product gives the reverse fiber inclusion. No site outside the box can meet \(C\). This proves (6), including every outer site with any combination of face, edge, or corner coordinates.

There are \(2^n\) inner sites and \(4^n-2^n=2^n(2^n-1)\) outer sites. The lattice fibers are disjoint isometric copies of \(\mathbb R^n\), so their Hausdorff volumes add. Thus \(W=2^n|T|+(4^n-2^n)|L|\), proving the last formula in (1) and in particular \(W>0\).

The halfspace (2) is closed and proper. At \((\mathbf0,\mathbf g_T)\), its defining left side equals \(3n/(n+1)\), so it contains the specified point. At an integer base with coordinate sum \(q\), its continuous trace is

\[
x_1\le\frac1{n+1}-\frac q{3n}.
\tag{7}
\]

For an inner site, \(q\) is a nonnegative integer and there are \(\binom nq\) such sites. Since \(3n/(n+1)<3\), every inner site with \(q\ge3\) has empty trace. The remaining sums are \(q=j\) for \(j=0,1,2\). All three thresholds in (7) lie strictly between zero and one: the smallest is \((n-2)/(3n(n+1))>0\), because \(n\ge3\). Formula (5) gives the contribution at each such site as
\(|T|[1-(n/(n+1)+j/(3n))^n]\). This proves the first sum in (3) and accounts for every inner fiber.

For an outer site, the actual fiber is \(L\subseteq\{x_1\ge1/3\}\). Positive volume in (7) requires
\(q<3n/(n+1)-n=-n+3-3/(n+1)\). The number on the right lies strictly between \(-n+2\) and \(-n+3\), since \(n\ge3\). As \(q\) is an integer and no site in the full box has sum less than \(-n\), exactly the sums \(q=-n+j\), \(j=0,1,2\), can contribute. These sums are all negative, so none of the corresponding sites is an inner site. All other actual outer fibers have empty trace.

At these sums, put \(u_i=z_i+1\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). Then \(\sum_i u_i=j\). For \(j\le2\), the upper bounds \(u_i\le3\) impose no restriction, so the respective multiplicities are
\(1,n,\binom{n+1}{2}\), or uniformly \(\binom{n+j-1}{j}\). The upper endpoint of the slab (7) is now \(1/3+1/(n+1)-j/(3n)\). It lies strictly between \(1/3\) and one for all three \(j\). Subtracting the upper-tail volumes in (5) at the two slab endpoints gives the per-site fraction
\((2/3)^n-(2/3-1/(n+1)+j/(3n))^n\). This proves the second sum in (3). The exact formula counts every actual fiber, and its volume formulas preserve the original closed-halfspace definition.

Each inner trace has volume at most \(|T|\), and each outer trace has volume at most \(|L|\). Summing the multiplicities just proved yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal H_n(S\cap H)}{|T|}
&\le \sum_{j=0}^2\binom nj+\left(\frac23\right)^n\sum_{j=0}^2\binom{n+j-1}{j}\\
&=\frac{n^2+n+2}{2}+\frac{n^2+3n+2}{2}\left(\frac23\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Combining (8) and (1) gives the right side of (4) for the containing-halfspace mass at \(\mathbf w=\mathbf0\). This ratio tends to zero. For an explicit bound, its denominator is at least \((2^n-1)(2/3)^n\); using \(2^n-1\ge2^{n-1}\), the ratio is at most
\((n^2+n+2)(3/4)^n+(n^2+3n+2)2^{-n}\). Each summand tends to zero, as is seen, for example, by taking the ratio of consecutive terms, which tends respectively to \(3/4\) and \(1/2\).

Here is a fully rational instance of the requested strict violation. At \(n=d=24\), (1) and (8) give

\[
\frac{\mathcal H_{24}(S\cap H)}{|T|}\le301+325\left(\frac23\right)^{24},
\qquad
\frac{W}{3\cdot2^{24}|T|}=\frac{1+(2^{24}-1)(2/3)^{24}}3.
\tag{9}
\]

The second quantity is strictly greater than the first bound, because this comparison is equivalent to

\[
902\cdot3^{24}=254751441905862
<281458602147840=(2^{24}-976)\,2^{24}.
\tag{10}
\]

The second quantity in (9) minus its first upper bound is exactly \(26707160241978/(3\cdot3^{24})>0\). Thus the upper bound in (9) alone is already strictly below the required halfspace mass. For completeness, the actual mass (3) specializes in this same example to the explicit six-term rational formula

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal H_{24}(S\cap H)}{|T|}
={}&301+325\left(\frac23\right)^{24}\\
&-\frac{1728^{24}+24\cdot1753^{24}+276\cdot1778^{24}
+1128^{24}+24\cdot1153^{24}+300\cdot1178^{24}}{1800^{24}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(|T|=1/24!\), \(L=T\cap\{x_1\ge1/3\}\), and the actual closed halfspace is \(\sum_{i=1}^{24}t_i+72x_1\le72/25\). Formulas (1) and (11) provide the complete actual mass and actual containing-halfspace mass without a limiting body or an asymptotic substitute for the strict comparison.

Finally, fix any \(\mathbf w\in\{0,1\}^n\). Reflect each base coordinate with \(w_i=1\) by \(t_i\mapsto1-t_i\), leaving the other base coordinates and all continuous coordinates fixed. This affine isometry preserves both defining boxes, hence \(C\), and preserves \(\Lambda\). It sends \((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T)\) to \((\mathbf0,\mathbf g_T)\) and preserves Hausdorff volume. The preimage of (2) is the closed halfspace

\[
H_{\mathbf w}=\left\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):
\sum_{i:w_i=0}t_i+\sum_{i:w_i=1}(1-t_i)+3n x_1\le\frac{3n}{n+1}\right\}.
\]

It contains \((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T)\) on its boundary and has exactly the mass (3). Therefore \(h_S((\mathbf w,\mathbf g_T))\le\mathcal H_n(S\cap H)\), which proves (4), the failure of every positive uniform selector coefficient, and the strict n=d=24 counterexample at every inner-fiber centroid. No conclusion about other possible choices of points in \(S\) is asserted.
