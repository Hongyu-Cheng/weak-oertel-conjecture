## Statement

In $\mathbb R^2$ put $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$, and $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. For any nondegenerate triangle $P$, let $g$ be its centroid and let $u_j$ be the three outward facet normals scaled so that $P-g=\{x:u_j\cdot x\le1\ \forall j\}$. For a convex body $B$ put $\rho=\sum_j h_B(u_j)/3>0$. There is a unique $z$ with $u_j\cdot z=h_B(u_j)-\rho$. For $s>0$ and $z_0\in\mathbb R^2$ the full erosion of $2(z_0+sP)$ by $B$, if full-dimensional, and the enclosing homothet for $B$ are
$$
\{x:x+B\subseteq 2(z_0+sP)\}=2z_0+2sg-z+(2s-\rho)(P-g),
\qquad B\subseteq z+\rho(P-g).
$$

Assume $h_P(-v_i)=1$ for every $i$, $B\subseteq T$, $A$ is a convex body with $(A+B)/2\subseteq z_0+sP$, and all three lines $v_i\cdot x=2v_i\cdot y-1$ meet $\operatorname{int} A$ for one $y\in\mathbb R^2$. Write $p=|P|$, $a=|A|$, $b=|B|$, $S=\sum_i h_P(v_i)$ and $W=\max_i[h_P(v_i)+1]$. Then $0<\rho<2s-1$ and
$$
\frac bp\le F(\rho)<F(2s-1),\qquad F(r)=r^2-\frac{(rS/3-1)_+^2}{W^2}.
$$
In particular, at $s=1,z_0=0$ put $\zeta=(S/3-1)_+/W$. Then $b/p<1-\zeta^2$. If $p\le a+b$ and $a<b/32$, this forces $\zeta^2<1/33$, so $S\ge3+9/\sqrt{33}$ is impossible.

## Proof

The barycentric coordinate functions of $P-g$ are $(1-u_j\cdot x)/3$. Thus $\sum_j u_j=0$, and this is their sole dependence. The defining right sides of the equations for $z$ sum to zero, so there is a unique solution. Positivity of $\rho$ follows by evaluating each support against a common interior point of $B$, which makes the three inequalities strict before summing. The enclosure of $B$ follows from its supports in these three normals. Erosion by $B$ is equivalent to imposing all three inequalities
$$
u_j\cdot x+h_B(u_j)\le 2u_j\cdot(z_0+sg)+2s.
$$
Subtracting the support equations proves the exact identity if $2s-\rho>0$. Summing the inequalities shows that the intersection is empty if $2s-\rho<0$ and a point at equality. Thus positive area of the erosion is equivalent to $2s-\rho>0$.

Under the matched support hypothesis, $\sum_i h_{P-g}(-v_i)=3$. Properness gives $h_A(-v_i)>1-2v_i\cdot y$. Since $A$ lies in the erosion, summing these support inequalities cancels the translations and source terms, giving $3<3(2s-\rho)$. Hence $\rho<2s-1$. Now let $D=z+\rho(P-g)$, so $B\subseteq D\cap T$ and $|D|=\rho^2p$. For some $i$, $h_D(v_i)\ge\rho S/3$ because the sum of the three supports of $D$ is $\rho S$. Its width in that coordinate is at most $\rho W$. If the level $v_i\cdot x=1$ is below the maximum, contract $D$ about a maximizing point with ratio equal to the excess above one divided by its width. The level is above the minimum whenever there is an excess, because $D\cap T$ has positive area. The homothet lies in the removed cap and the boundary line has zero area. Consequently that cap occupies at least $(\rho S/3-1)_+^2p/W^2$. This proves $b/p\le F(\rho)$, including the case of zero excess.

We have $S>0$ by translating to $g$ and $W\ge 1+S/3$ by averaging the widths. Thus $F$ increases strictly for $r\ge0$: below the hinge it is $r^2$, and above it the derivative is $2r(1-(S/3)^2/W^2)+2S/(3W^2)>0$. Evaluate at $2s-1$, or at one in the unexpanded case. If $p\le a+b$, then $a/b\ge p/b-1>\zeta^2/(1-\zeta^2)$. As $P\subseteq -T$, each $h_P(v_i)\le2$ and hence $W\le3$, proving the simpler shape test.
