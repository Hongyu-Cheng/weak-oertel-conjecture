## Statement

For integers $k,m\ge1$, let $X_k\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)$ and $Y_m\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1)$. Let $E_+,E_-$ be unit-mean exponentials, with all four variables independent. For $p\in[0,1]$ and $b=(k+p-1)/m\in[0,1]$, set
$$
V_{k,m,p}=X_k+pE_+-E_--bY_m,\qquad
G_{k,p}=\Pr(X_k+pE_+\ge E_-+k+p-1).
$$
The variance of $V_{k,m,p}$ is $\sigma^2=k+p^2+1+mb^2$ and lies in $[k+1,2(k+1)]$. As $k\to\infty$, the probability $\Pr(V_{k,m,p}\ge0)$ tends to $1/2$ uniformly over the admissible $m,p$. For fixed $k$, if $m_j\to\infty$ and $p_j\to p\in[0,1]$ along admissible parameters, then $\Pr(V_{k,m_j,p_j}\ge0)\to G_{k,p}$.

## Proof

**Diverging positive count.** Write $V=V_{k,m,p}$ as $\sum_i a_i(E_i-1)$ with independent unit exponentials and signed coefficients in $[-1,1]$. The two coefficient magnitudes have total $k+p$ on each side, so $\sigma^2=\sum_i a_i^2\le 2(k+p)\le2(k+1)$. The $k$ positive units and fixed negative unit give the lower bound. Let $Z=V/\sigma$ with characteristic function $\varphi_Z(t)$. For $|u|\le1/2$, the power series of $-\log(1-iu)$ gives
$$
\log\!\left(\frac{e^{-iu}}{1-iu}\right)
=-\frac{u^2}{2}+R(u),
\qquad |R(u)|\le\frac23|u|^3.
$$
For fixed $t$ and large $k$ we can use this for every $u=ta_i/\sigma$. Since $\sum_i|a_i|^3\le\sigma^2$, the sum of remainders has absolute value at most $2|t|^3/(3\sigma)$. Therefore $\varphi_Z(t)\to e^{-t^2/2}$ for any admissible sequence with $k\to\infty$.

To pass to the sign probability directly, use the modulus bound from the $k$ unit positive phases,
$$
|\varphi_Z(t)|\le (1+t^2/\sigma^2)^{-k/2}.
$$
For $0\le t\le\sigma$, $\log(1+t^2/\sigma^2)\ge t^2/(2\sigma^2)$, and $k/(4\sigma^2)\ge1/16$, so the right-hand side is at most $e^{-t^2/16}$. For $t\ge\sigma$, substitute $u=t/\sigma$ and use $1+u^2\ge2u$ to get
$$
\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}\frac{|\varphi_Z(t)|}{t}\,dt
\le \int_1^\infty(1+u^2)^{-k/2}\frac{du}{u}
\le \frac2k\,2^{-k/2}.
$$
Near zero $|\operatorname{Im}\varphi_Z(t)|/t\le\mathbb E|Z|\le1$. These bounds give absolute integrability of $\operatorname{Im}\varphi_Z(t)/t$. The damped sine integral $\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon t}\sin(tz)\,dt/t=\arctan(z/\epsilon)$ follows by differentiating in $z$ and using the value zero at $z=0$. Fubini applies to the damped integral by $\mathbb E|Z|<\infty$ near zero and exponential damping at infinity. Taking the expectation and letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ by dominated convergence gives
$$
\Pr(Z\ge0)=\frac12+\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\operatorname{Im}\varphi_Z(t)}{t}\,dt,
$$
since the independent term $-E_-$ precludes an atom at zero. On $[0,\sigma]$ the integrand, extended by zero beyond $\sigma$, tends pointwise to zero and is dominated by $1$ for $t\le1$ and $e^{-t^2/16}/t$ for $t\ge1$. The remaining integral tends to zero by the tail bound. This works along every admissible sequence with $k\to\infty$, which proves uniform convergence in the other parameters, including sequences where $m$ also tends to infinity.

**Fixed positive count.** For fixed $k$, the negative gamma term $b_jY_{m_j}$ has mean $k+p_j-1$ and variance $(k+p_j-1)^2/m_j$. It converges in probability to $k+p-1$. Couple $X_k,E_+,E_-$ identically along the sequence, so the full difference converges in probability to $D=X_k+pE_+-E_--k-p+1$. For any $\delta>0$, the indicators of a nonnegative sign can differ only where the differences deviate by more than $\delta$ or $|D|\le\delta$. The former event tends to probability zero, and the latter probability tends to zero as $\delta\downarrow0$ by independence and continuity of $E_-$. This proves the second limit, including $p=0$ and $p=1$.
