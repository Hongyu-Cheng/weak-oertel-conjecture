## Statement

There is a full-dimensional planar convex body $K$ of area $V$ with centroid zero, an axial normalized closed tail $F(q)=|\{x\in K:x_1\ge q\}|/V$, and positive scales $r,s$ for which
$$
r^2 F(t/r)+s^2 F(-t/s)<\min\{\min(r^2,s^2),2(4/9)((r+s)/2)^2\}.
$$
For $r=1,s=11/10$, a supporting cut gives cost $91/100<49/50$ and a cut with both endpoint traces proper gives cost $41189/45000<49/50$. Both are costs divided by $V$ from actual endpoint fibers at integer bases $-1,1$ of a convex hull, cut through the centroid of its middle fiber.

## Proof

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(1,1)\}-(2/3,1/3)$, of area $V=1/2$. Before centering the normalized first-coordinate density is $2x$ on $[0,1]$, giving $F(q)=1-(q+2/3)^2$ for $-2/3\le q\le1/3$, with the saturated values one and zero outside. The closed upper-support cap is area zero. Set $r=1,s=11/10$ and $t=-11/30$. Then $-t/s=1/3$, so the endpoint sum is $1-(3/10)^2=91/100$. The proposed lower bound is $\min\{1,2(4/9)(21/20)^2\}=49/50$.

Set instead $t'=-109/300$. The two uncentered thresholds are $91/300$ and $329/330$, both strictly between zero and one. Their respective contributions are $81719/90000$ and $659/90000$ after weighting by $r^2,s^2$. The sum is $41189/45000$, with gap $2911/45000$ below $49/50$.

Take the convex hull of $\{-1\}\times rK$ and $\{1\}\times sK$. These are its actual endpoint fibers. Its fiber at zero is $(rK+sK)/2=((r+s)/2)K$, with centroid zero. The cap $x_1+t z\ge0$ through $(0,0)$ has exactly the specified endpoint costs, and likewise with $t'$. The measured sum here uses the two endpoints only.
