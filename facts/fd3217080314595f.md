## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, let \(D\) be a nonempty subset of \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) invariant under every coordinate permutation, and let \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb R^k\). Choose an ordering \(i_1,\ldots,i_k\) of the coordinate indices such that \(\beta_{(j)}:=\beta_{i_j}\) is nondecreasing. Assume

$$
\beta_{(j+2)}-\beta_{(j)}\ge\log2
\qquad(1\le j,\ j+2\le k).
$$

Set \(N:=k+1\) and \(A:=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\), viewed as a subset of \(\mathbb R^N\). Define the original spatial weights, normalization, and probabilities by

$$
w_{\mathbf x}:=\exp(-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x),
\qquad
Z:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,0)}:=\frac{w_{\mathbf x}}Z.
$$

For every original atom \(\mathbf z\in A\), define

$$
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=
\inf_{\substack{
H=\lbrace\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\ge b\rbrace\\
\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^N\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace,\ b\in\mathbb R,\ \mathbf z\in H
}}
\sum_{\mathbf v\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},
\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
$$

Thus the infimum uses all closed affine halfspaces of the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^N\), and the moment samples the original atoms with their original probabilities.

For every coordinate-permutation orbit \(\mathcal O\subseteq D\), let \(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}\) be its unique word that is nonincreasing when read in the coordinate order \(i_1,\ldots,i_k\). Then

$$
\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}w_{\mathbf x}
\le\exp(14)w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}=\max_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}w_{\mathbf x}.
$$

In particular,

$$
\max_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}
\ge\frac{\exp(-14)}{(k+1)^2},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\exp(-56)}{(k+1)^8}
\ge\frac{\exp(-56)}{16}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$

No accepted predecessor is used as a premise.

## Proof

All sums below are finite unless an infinite geometric series is explicitly displayed. Write \([r]:=\lbrace1,\ldots,r\rbrace\) for integers \(r\ge1\) and \([0]:=\varnothing\). Reading coordinates in the chosen order does not alter the spatial atoms, the original weights, the embedding, or any ambient halfspace.

First, for \(1\le r<s\le k\), monotonicity and the assumed two-step differences imply

$$
\beta_{(s)}-\beta_{(r)}
\ge\left\lfloor\frac{s-r}{2}\right\rfloor\log2.
\tag{1}
$$

Indeed, put \(h:=\lfloor(s-r)/2\rfloor\). Summing the \(h\) assumed inequalities from indices \(r,r+2,\ldots,r+2h-2\) gives

$$
\beta_{(r+2h)}-\beta_{(r)}\ge h\log2.
$$

If \(h=0\), this is the zero inequality. Since \(r+2h\le s\), monotonicity finishes the proof of (1).

For an integer \(a\) with \(0\le a\le k\), define the normalized binary partition sum

$$
T_a:=\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[k]\\|I|=a}}
\exp\left(-\sum_{j\in I}\beta_{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^a\beta_{(j)}\right).
$$

We claim that

$$
T_a\le\exp(7)\qquad(0\le a\le k).
\tag{2}
$$

If \(a=0\) or \(a=k\), there is a single possible \(I\) and \(T_a=1\). For the remaining cases \(1\le a<k\), put \(\mu:=\beta_{(a)}\). For each subset \(I\) of size \(a\), let \(L:=[a]\setminus I\) and \(U:=I\setminus[a]\). The cardinality equality \(|L|=|U|\) follows from \(|I|=a\). Cancelling the indices in \(I\cap[a]\), and then using this equality, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\exp\left(-\sum_{j\in I}\beta_{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^a\beta_{(j)}\right)
&=\exp\left(\sum_{j\in L}\beta_{(j)}-\sum_{j\in U}\beta_{(j)}\right)\\
&=\prod_{j\in L}\exp(\beta_{(j)}-\mu)
  \prod_{j\in U}\exp(\mu-\beta_{(j)}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

The correspondence \(I\mapsto(L,U)\) is a bijection onto pairs \(L\subseteq[a]\), \(U\subseteq[k]\setminus[a]\) satisfying \(|L|=|U|\). All factors are positive. Dropping this cardinality restriction in the sum of (3), and expanding the resulting finite products, proves

$$
T_a\le
\prod_{j=1}^a\bigl(1+\exp(\beta_{(j)}-\mu)\bigr)
\prod_{j=a+1}^k\bigl(1+\exp(\mu-\beta_{(j)})\bigr).
\tag{4}
$$

For the first product, (1) bounds \(\exp(\beta_{(j)}-\mu)\) by \(2^{-\lfloor(a-j)/2\rfloor}\). For the second product, (1) bounds \(\exp(\mu-\beta_{(j)})\) by \(2^{-\lfloor(j-a)/2\rfloor}\). These bounds also hold at distance zero. Therefore

$$
\sum_{j=1}^a\exp(\beta_{(j)}-\mu)
\le\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}2^{-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
=2\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}2^{-h}=4,
\tag{5}
$$

and

$$
\sum_{j=a+1}^k\exp(\mu-\beta_{(j)})
\le\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}2^{-\lfloor r/2\rfloor}=3.
\tag{6}
$$

The last equality in (6) removes the \(r=0\) term, which is one, from the series evaluated in (5). For \(t\ge0\), the power series for \(\exp(t)\) gives \(1+t\le\exp(t)\). Applying this inequality to every factor in (4), and using (5)-(6), proves (2). In particular, the contributions of indices at increasing distance from the boundary \(a\) are summable uniformly in \(k\); no positive lower bound on any individual adjacent field gap was used.

Fix a coordinate-permutation orbit \(\mathcal O\subseteq D\). Let \(m_0,m_1,m_2\) be its letter counts. Put \(a:=m_1+m_2\) and \(c:=m_2\). For \(\mathbf x\in\mathcal O\), define

$$
B_1(\mathbf x):=\lbrace j\in[k]:x_{i_j}\ge1\rbrace,
\qquad
B_2(\mathbf x):=\lbrace j\in[k]:x_{i_j}=2\rbrace.
$$

This is a bijection between \(\mathcal O\) and the pairs \((B_1,B_2)\) with \(B_2\subseteq B_1\subseteq[k]\), \(|B_1|=a\), and \(|B_2|=c\). To check surjectivity, a pair specifies \(x_{i_j}=2\) on \(B_2\), \(x_{i_j}=1\) on \(B_1\setminus B_2\), and \(x_{i_j}=0\) elsewhere; its counts are exactly \(m_0,m_1,m_2\), so coordinate-permutation invariance puts it in \(\mathcal O\). Injectivity follows from the same reconstruction.

The nonincreasing word \(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}\) has \(B_1(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O})=[a]\) and \(B_2(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O})=[c]\). Also

$$
x_{i_j}=\mathbf1_{\lbrace j\in B_1(\mathbf x)\rbrace}
       +\mathbf1_{\lbrace j\in B_2(\mathbf x)\rbrace},
$$

so its original energy is

$$
\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x
=\sum_{j\in B_1(\mathbf x)}\beta_{(j)}
 +\sum_{j\in B_2(\mathbf x)}\beta_{(j)}.
\tag{7}
$$

For every subset \(I\) of size \(r\), list its indices as \(j_1<\cdots<j_r\). Then \(j_\ell\ge\ell\), and monotonicity gives \(\beta_{(j_\ell)}\ge\beta_{(\ell)}\) for each \(\ell\). Consequently

$$
\sum_{j\in I}\beta_{(j)}\ge\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_{(j)}.
$$

Apply this fact to \(B_1(\mathbf x)\) and \(B_2(\mathbf x)\) in (7). It proves \(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x\ge\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}\) and hence \(w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}=\max_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}w_{\mathbf x}\), including when some letter counts vanish.

The bijection and (7) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}\frac{w_{\mathbf x}}{w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}}
&=\sum_{\substack{B_2\subseteq B_1\subseteq[k]\\|B_1|=a,\ |B_2|=c}}
  \exp\left(-\sum_{j\in B_1}\beta_{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^a\beta_{(j)}\right)
  \exp\left(-\sum_{j\in B_2}\beta_{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^c\beta_{(j)}\right)\\
&\le T_aT_c\\
&\le\exp(14).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

The first inequality drops only the nesting constraint \(B_2\subseteq B_1\), so it holds by positivity. This proves the claimed uniform orbit partition estimate. There are precisely two binary partition factors in (8), regardless of the number of coordinates.

Let \(R\) be the number of coordinate-permutation orbits present in \(D\). Each orbit is determined by its nonnegative count triple of sum \(k\). Thus

$$
1\le R\le\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}2\le(k+1)^2.
\tag{9}
$$

For example, the number of triples is obtained by summing \(k-m_2+1\) over \(m_2=0,\ldots,k\), which gives the displayed formula. Choose \(\mathbf x^\star\in D\) maximizing the original weight; this is possible because \(D\) is finite and nonempty. Sum (8) over the disjoint present orbits and bound each \(w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}\) by \(w_{\mathbf x^\star}\). The result is

$$
Z\le\exp(14)R w_{\mathbf x^\star},
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x^\star,0)}\ge\frac{\exp(-14)}R
\ge\frac{\exp(-14)}{(k+1)^2}.
\tag{10}
$$

For any original atom \(\mathbf z\) and any original closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing it, the mass sum over \(A\cap H\) includes \(p_{\mathbf z}\). Therefore \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge p_{\mathbf z}\). This statement uses every halfspace admitted in the definition, including normals with a nonzero last component. Restricting the original nonnegative sampling sum to \(\mathbf z^\star:=(\mathbf x^\star,0)\), equation (10) gives

$$
M_3(p)\ge p_{\mathbf z^\star}\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)^3
\ge p_{\mathbf z^\star}^4
\ge\frac{\exp(-56)}{(k+1)^8}.
\tag{11}
$$

To obtain the stated exponential form with one absolute constant, for every integer \(m\ge2\) we have

$$
m^8\le16\cdot8^m.
\tag{12}
$$

For \(m=2,3,4\), this reads respectively \(256\le1024\), \(6561\le8192\), and \(65536=65536\). For \(m\ge4\),

$$
\left(\frac{m+1}{m}\right)^8
\le\left(\frac54\right)^8<8,
$$

where the last inequality is equivalent to \(390625<524288\). Induction from \(m=4\) proves (12) for all larger integers. Substituting \(m=k+1\) into (11)-(12) proves

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\exp(-56)}{16}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$

All arguments apply when \(k=1\): the two-step condition is empty, \(T_0=T_1=1\), and the orbit, sampling, and scalar estimates remain valid. This completes the proof under exactly the registered hypotheses.
