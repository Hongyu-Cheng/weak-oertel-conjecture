## Statement

Let \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\) be finite, nonempty, and complete,
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N,
\]
and let
\[
p_{\mathbf z}=\frac{e^{\bm\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Z_D(\bm\theta)}
\qquad(\mathbf z\in D)
\]
for some \(\bm\theta\in\mathbb R^N\). Use actual closed-halfspace depth
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}p(H)
\]
and
\[
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]

Fix \(L>0\) and \(a\in\mathbb R\), and partition the log-weights into slabs
\[
D_j=\{\mathbf z\in D:a+jL\leq\log p_{\mathbf z}<a+(j+1)L\}
\qquad(j\in\mathbb Z).
\]
For each nonempty slab, let \(\alpha_j=p(D_j)\), let \(u_j\) be the uniform law on \(D_j\), and let \(U_j=M_3(u_j)\), with depth computed by ambient closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\). Then
\[
M_3(p)\geq e^{-4L}\sum_j\alpha_j^4U_j. \tag{1}
\]
For every collection \(J\) of nonempty slabs with total mass \(\rho=\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_j>0\),
\[
M_3(p)\geq
e^{-4L}\frac{\rho^4}
{\left(\sum_{j\in J}U_j^{-1/3}\right)^3}. \tag{2}
\]

If \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p_{\mathbf X})\), then
\[
M_3(p)\geq
\frac{81}{1024e^4(1+4\sigma)^3}8^{-N}, \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
M_3(p)\geq
\frac{81}{4096e^4(1+4\sigma)^3}8^{-N}
+\frac34\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^4. \tag{4}
\]

Writing \(\varepsilon=8^NM_3(p)\), every interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\) of length \(L\), with any endpoint convention, satisfies
\[
p\{\log p_{\mathbf X}\in I\}\leq(4e^{4L}\varepsilon)^{1/4},
\]
and every slab collection \(J\) of mass at least \(\rho>0\) satisfies
\[
2^{-N}\sum_{j\in J}U_j^{-1/3}
\geq e^{-4L/3}\rho^{4/3}\varepsilon^{-1/3}.
\]
Finally, if the log-weights of \(p\) take exactly two distinct values, then
\[
M_3(p)\geq\frac14\,8^{-N}.
\]

## Proof

Each \(D_j\) is complete in its intrinsic affine lattice
\(\Lambda_j=\operatorname{aff}(D_j)\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Indeed, a lattice point in \(\operatorname{conv}(D_j)\) belongs to \(D\), its log-weight is at least the lower slab endpoint, and it is strictly below the upper endpoint. For the last assertion, all vertices used in a convex representation belong to the finite set \(D_j\) and hence have log-weight bounded by a common number strictly below the upper endpoint.

Let \(q_j=p(\,\cdot\mid D_j)\). Restricting any containing halfspace to \(D_j\) gives
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)\geq\alpha_j\tau_{q_j}(\mathbf z)
\qquad(\mathbf z\in D_j).
\]
All conditional probabilities in this slab have ratio at most \(e^L\), so
\(q_j\geq e^{-L}u_j\) pointwise and therefore
\(\tau_{q_j}\geq e^{-L}\tau_{u_j}\). The center weight and three depth factors give
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_j}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
\geq\alpha_j^4M_3(q_j)
\geq e^{-4L}\alpha_j^4U_j.
\]
Summing proves (1). Hölder's inequality gives
\[
\left(\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_j\right)^4
\leq
\left(\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_j^4U_j\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in J}U_j^{-1/3}\right)^3,
\]
which together with (1) proves (2).

We now record precisely how accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\) applies to each \(U_j\). Put
\(r_j=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_j)\). If \(r_j=0\), then \(D_j\) is a singleton and \(U_j=1\). If \(r_j>0\), choose an affine lattice isomorphism from \(\mathbb Z^{r_j}\) onto \(\Lambda_j\). It sends a finite complete subset of \(\mathbb Z^{r_j}\) onto \(D_j\). Affine halfspaces correspond under this map, and every relative halfspace in \(\operatorname{aff}(D_j)\) is the restriction of an ambient halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\); hence the intrinsic and ambient depths, and thus their cubic moments, agree.

The uniform law on this complete support satisfies the midpoint hypothesis in Statement part 5 of accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\): for every distinct coordinatewise same-parity pair \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\), completeness puts \((\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) in the support, and all three uniform weights are equal. Applying that part with ambient dimension \(r_j\) and \(q=3\) gives
\[
U_j\geq\frac{2^{-3r_j}}{3+1}=\frac{8^{-r_j}}4.
\]
Together with the singleton case, this implies the common bound
\[
U_j\geq\frac{8^{-N}}4 \tag{5}
\]
for every nonempty slab.

Suppose first that \(\sigma>0\), and write
\(\mu=\mathbb E_p\log p_{\mathbf X}\). Chebyshev's inequality gives mass at least \(3/4\) to the open interval
\((\mu-2\sigma,\mu+2\sigma)\). Choose unit slabs with offset
\(a=\mu-2\sigma\). That open interval is covered by
\(K=\lceil4\sigma\rceil\leq1+4\sigma\) consecutive slabs, including when \(4\sigma\) is an integer because the right endpoint is not part of the open interval. Applying (1), (5), and
\[
\sum_{j=1}^K\alpha_j^4
\geq\frac{(\sum_{j=1}^K\alpha_j)^4}{K^3}
\]
gives
\[
M_3(p)
\geq e^{-4}\frac{8^{-N}}4\frac{(3/4)^4}{(1+4\sigma)^3}
=\frac{81}{1024e^4(1+4\sigma)^3}8^{-N},
\]
which is (3). If \(\sigma=0\), then \(p\) is uniform on \(D\). Its weights satisfy the same midpoint hypothesis, so Statement part 5 of accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\), now with dimension \(N\) and \(q=3\), gives
\(M_3(p)\geq8^{-N}/4\), which is stronger than (3).

Every containing halfspace contains its center atom, so
\(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\geq p_{\mathbf z}\) and hence
\[
M_3(p)\geq\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^4. \tag{6}
\]
Taking one quarter of (3) plus three quarters of (6) proves (4).

For an interval \(I\) of length \(L\), let
\(E=\{\mathbf z\in D:\log p_{\mathbf z}\in I\}\) and \(\beta=p(E)\). If \(E\) is empty there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, the same convexity argument used for slabs shows that \(E\) is complete in its intrinsic affine lattice, and the conditional probabilities on \(E\) have ratio at most \(e^L\). Repeating the conditional-law comparison above and using Statement part 5 of accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\) with \(q=3\), or the singleton case, gives
\[
M_3(p)\geq e^{-4L}\beta^4\frac{8^{-N}}4.
\]
After multiplying by \(8^N\), this is exactly
\[
\beta\leq(4e^{4L}\varepsilon)^{1/4}.
\]
If a slab collection \(J\) has mass \(\rho_J\geq\rho\), rearranging (2) gives
\[
\sum_{j\in J}U_j^{-1/3}
\geq e^{-4L/3}\rho_J^{4/3}M_3(p)^{-1/3}
\geq e^{-4L/3}\rho^{4/3}2^N\varepsilon^{-1/3},
\]
which proves the inverse-moment budget.

Finally, suppose the log-weights take exactly two values. Since
\(\log p_{\mathbf z}=\bm\theta\cdot\mathbf z-\log Z_D(\bm\theta)\), exact two-level behavior implies \(\bm\theta\neq0\), and each level set is a complete support of intrinsic affine dimension at most \(N-1\). Its conditional law is uniform and satisfies the midpoint hypothesis from Statement part 5 of accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\). The intrinsic-coordinate argument above with \(q=3\) therefore gives cubic moment at least \(8^{-(N-1)}/4\) for each nonsingleton level; a singleton has cubic moment \(1\) and satisfies the same lower bound. If the two level masses are \(b\) and \(1-b\), the direct conditional-depth comparison, with no within-level distortion, yields
\[
M_3(p)
\geq\frac{8^{-(N-1)}}4\bigl(b^4+(1-b)^4\bigr)
\geq\frac{8^{-(N-1)}}4\cdot\frac18
=\frac14\,8^{-N}.
\]
