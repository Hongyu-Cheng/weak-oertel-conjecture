## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$ and $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of positive summed full mixed volume $\mu$. Put $\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$, let $h(y)$ denote its ambient raw closed-halfspace depth, and suppose $h(y)<T$ for every $y\in C\cap\Lambda$, where $T>0$. If $D_T=\{y:h(y)\ge T\}$ is nonempty, then for every $\eta>0$ there is a bounded full-dimensional polytope $Q_\eta$ disjoint from $\Lambda$, with
$$
D_T\subset\operatorname{int}Q_\eta\subset Q_\eta
\subset\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,D_T)<\eta\},
$$
whose defining outward closed caps are nonvertical and have mass $<T$. Along $\eta\downarrow0$, every sequence of points in these polytopes, including attained projected-facet contacts, has all its cluster points in $D_T$.

## Proof

A point with depth $<T$ has a containing closed halfspace of mass $<T$. Slightly decrease its threshold, keeping the bound by continuity from above, to put the point in its open interior. The new threshold can be chosen with a mass-null boundary: for a nonzero continuous component this follows from absolute continuity on each fiber, and for a pure-base direction only finitely many thresholds meet sampled fibers. A small perturbation of the normal then preserves the strict membership and mass bound by dominated convergence, and can make its continuous component nonzero. The open bad caps so obtained cover the complement of $D_T$, and every outward closed cap of mass $<T$ misses $D_T$. Thus $D_T$ is closed; it is contained in $C$ by strict separation, so it is compact.

Choose a bounded parallelepiped $B$ containing $C$ in its interior, with all facet normals nonvertical (choose a basis of such normals and sufficiently distant opposite facets). Its outward closed caps have zero mass. Both $B\cap\Lambda$ and $\{y\in B:\operatorname{dist}(y,D_T)\ge\eta\}$ are compact and disjoint from $D_T$; for the mixed-lattice part use the depth hypothesis and strict separation off $C$. Cover their union by finitely many of the open bad caps constructed above. Intersect their closed complements with $B$. The result has the asserted exclusions and lies within the prescribed neighborhood. Each point of $D_T$ satisfies every defining inequality strictly, so it is interior and the result is full-dimensional. The final cluster conclusion follows from closedness and the distance estimate.
