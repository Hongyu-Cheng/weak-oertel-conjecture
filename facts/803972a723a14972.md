## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, $d\ge1$, and let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of volume one containing zero in its interior. Over the integer points of $2\Delta_n$, assign $D$ at the vertex zero and at all edge midpoints, and assign $3^{-1/d}D$ at every other vertex. The lifted convex hull is a full-dimensional body with precisely these assigned integer fibers. Its volume array attains the sharp complete-graph bound for the scalar menu
$$
B=\max\left\{\max_i v_i,\max_{i<j}k_{ij},\tfrac34\max_{i<j}(k_{ij}+\min(v_i,v_j))\right\}.
$$
In $n=2$ its total mass is $14/3$ and $B=1$, so the unaugmented scalar level $\Gamma_d B$ is $\Gamma_d/6$ below $\Gamma_d M/4$.

## Proof

All generating fibers are compact and their convex hull is compact. Its projection is the full simplex, and a full-dimensional continuous fiber together with the spanning projected vertices gives full affine dimension. A projection to an exposed vertex can use only the fiber assigned there. The midpoint of any edge is on that edge, a face of the projected simplex, so a lifted convex combination above it uses only the three assigned sites of that edge. Each of their fibers is contained in $D$, and the middle site already carries all of $D$. This proves the exact fibers, since the integer points of the simplex have coordinate sum at most two and are exactly the listed sites.

Here $v_1=1$, $v_2=\cdots=v_{n+1}=1/3$ and $k_{ij}=1$ for every edge. Thus $B=1$, and summing the volumes gives $(3N^2-N+4)/6$ with $N=n+1$. For $n=2$, $M=1+2/3+3=14/3$ and $M/4-B=1/6$. This compares only the displayed scalar level with the target. The actual depth may exceed this certificate level.
