## Statement

Let $P$ be a nondegenerate triangle in $\mathbb R^2$ with an interior point $p$. Let $n_1,n_2,n_3$ span $\mathbb R^2$ and satisfy $\sum_{i=1}^3\omega_i n_i=0$ for some $\omega_i>0$. Define $H_i=\{w:n_i\cdot(w-p)\ge0\}$ and let $q_i=|P\cap H_i|/|P|$, using ordinary planar area. If $q_i\le c<2\sqrt3-3$ for all $i$, each $H_i$ strictly captures one vertex and strictly excludes the other two, and the three captured vertices are distinct. In particular this holds when $q_i\le128/279$ for all $i$.

## Proof

Under an invertible affine map $w\mapsto Aw+b$, the normals transform by $A^{-T}$. Thus rank, the same positive dependence, and all vertex incidences are preserved, and both areas defining each $q_i$ scale by $|\det A|$. We use barycentric coordinates on $P$. Every pair of normals is independent, since a collinear pair and the positive dependence would put all three normals in a line. For a nonzero affine margin vanishing at the interior point $p$, the vertex values have both signs: their strictly positive barycentric weighted sum is zero, and they cannot all vanish. Each $q_i$ is positive, so $c>0$. Also $2\sqrt3-3<1/2$.

**Reducing to a cut through a vertex.** Suppose one cap strictly captures $P_1,P_2$ and strictly excludes $P_0$. Let $\alpha,\beta>0$ be the coordinates of $p$ at $P_1,P_2$, respectively, and let $\lambda_j$ denote the barycentric coordinate function of $P_j$. The boundary intercepts on $[P_0,P_1]$ and $[P_0,P_2]$, as fractions from $P_0$, are $u,v\in(0,1)$. The margin $\lambda_1/u+\lambda_2/v-1$ vanishes at $p$. The full two-vertex family is parameterized by
$$
\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}<u<1,\qquad v=\frac{\beta u}{u-\alpha}.
$$
Its cap fraction is $Q(u)=1-uv=1-\beta u^2/(u-\alpha)$, because the omitted triangle at $P_0$ has area fraction $uv$ and its boundary has zero area. On this interval
$$
Q''(u)=-\frac{2\beta\alpha^2}{(u-\alpha)^3}<0.
$$

Hold the other two caps fixed. The moving margin's gradient must lie in the open cone of strictly positive combinations of the negatives of the two fixed normals to give a positive circuit. This condition is open in $u$ and holds at the initial $u$. Take its interval component within the two-vertex parameter range. Move to its left endpoint if $Q'(u)\ge0$ initially and to its right endpoint if $Q'(u)<0$ initially. Strict concavity gives a strict decrease of $Q$ up to the endpoint, including when the initial derivative is zero. On the closed two-vertex parameter range, $u-\alpha>0$ and the margin remains nonzero, since it is $-1$ at $P_0$ and zero at $p$. If its endpoint gradient were on the boundary of the circuit cone, it would be a negative multiple of a fixed normal. The two opposing caps would then have fractions summing to one, contradicting the bound by $c<1/2$ on both. Thus the endpoint remains in the circuit cone and must be an endpoint of the two-vertex parameter range. At that endpoint exactly one of $u,v$ equals one, since $\alpha+\beta<1$. Exactly one formerly positive vertex now lies on the boundary, and the other remains strictly captured.

Rotate each two-strict-vertex cap in this way while keeping the other caps fixed. A cap with only one strictly captured vertex is left as it is. Each vertex must be strictly captured by at least one cap: if all its margins were nonpositive, the positive dependence would make them all zero, which by rank would place the vertex at $p$. Hence when all three caps have one strictly captured vertex, those vertices are distinct. If the original menu failed the stated incidence, the resulting menu has a cut through a vertex, either from a rotation or from a cap that already had a vertex on its boundary. All its fractions are at most $c$.

**Bounds for a single corner.** For a cap at just one strictly captured vertex, its two edge intercepts from that vertex have fractions $u,v\in(0,1]$, and the other two source coordinates $\alpha,\beta$ satisfy $\alpha/u+\beta/v=1$. Its area fraction is $uv$. Apply the one-vertex minimization in fact f75e1f526e17ab44 with $d=2$. The source is interior, and its two relevant coordinates are positive with $\alpha+\beta<1$, so the present intercepts are feasible and the fact's unique water level applies. If $\alpha,\beta\le1/2$, the level $2$ gives $uv\ge4\alpha\beta$. If $\alpha\ge1/2$, the level $1/(1-\alpha)$ gives intercepts $(1,\beta/(1-\alpha))$ and $uv\ge\beta/(1-\alpha)$. Indeed $\beta<1-\alpha\le\alpha$, and the two terms of the water-level equation are then $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$. These two formulas agree at $\alpha=1/2$. In the reduced menu, any coordinate $\alpha\ge1/2$ would therefore force the fractions of the two caps at the other vertices to sum to at least $(1-\alpha)/(1-\alpha)=1$, which is impossible. Thus all coordinates are below $1/2$ and the product bound applies to every cap.

**The boundary obstruction.** Relabel the reduced menu so the first cap strictly captures $P_1$ and has $P_2$ on its boundary. Write $x,y,z$ for the source coordinates at $P_1,P_2,P_3$. The intercept toward $P_2$ is one and the intercept toward $P_3$ is $z/(x+z)$, by the through-source intercept equation. Consequently
$$
q_1=\frac{z}{x+z},\qquad q_2\ge4xz,\qquad q_3\ge4xy.
$$
Set $t=z/x$ and $r=c/(1-c)$. Then $0<t\le r<1$, $y=1-(1+t)x$, and $1/[2(1+t)]<x<1/2$ by the strict coordinate bounds. If $t\le1/2$, the concave function $4x-4(1+t)x^2$ has values $1/(1+t)$ and $1-t$ at the endpoints of the corresponding closed interval. Both values are at least $1/2$, contradicting $q_3\le c<1/2$.

For $t>1/2$, the functions $4tx^2$ and $4x-4(1+t)x^2$ meet at $x_*=1/(1+2t)$, which lies strictly inside the interval. Their common value is $J(t)=4t/(1+2t)^2$. When $x\ge x_*$ the first function is at least $J(t)$. For $x\le x_*$ the second function is at least the smaller of its values at $1/[2(1+t)]$ and $x_*$. These values are $1/(1+t)>J(t)$ and $J(t)$. Thus $c\ge J(t)\ge J(r)$, since $J'(t)=4(1-2t)/(1+2t)^3<0$ for $t>1/2$. Substitution gives
$$
c\ge \frac{4c(1-c)}{(1+c)^2}.
$$
Since $c>0$, this implies $c^2+6c-3\ge0$, or $c\ge2\sqrt3-3$. The contradiction proves the strict incidence. Finally $128/279+3=965/279>0$ and $12\cdot279^2-965^2=2867>0$, so $128/279<2\sqrt3-3$.
