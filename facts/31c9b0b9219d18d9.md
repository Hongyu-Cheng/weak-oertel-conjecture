## Statement

Fix a positive integer \(M\) and a real number \(T\ge0\). There is a finite constant \(C=C(M,T)\ge0\) with the following property. Put \(c=\frac14e^{-C}\) and \(\gamma=\frac18e^{-3C/2}=c^{3/2}\).

Let \(L\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(n_\ell\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A_\ell\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty, and choose positive weights \(w_{\ell,x}\) on its entire support. Assume that every coordinatewise parity class in \(A_\ell\) has at most \(M\) atoms. For every unordered pair \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\) of distinct same-parity atoms, assume that \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\) belongs to \(A_\ell\). Retain the original normalizers and probability laws
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=w_{\ell,x}/Z_\ell,\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,\qquad
N=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell.
\tag{1}
$$
Extend these laws by zero off their supports. Require, for every such pair in every factor,
$$
p_{\ell,j_e}^{\,2}=p_{\ell,u_e}p_{\ell,v_e},\qquad
\left|\frac12\log\frac{p_{\ell,v_e}}{p_{\ell,u_e}}\right|\le T.
\tag{2}
$$
No bound is assumed on the number of pairs sharing a midpoint, the number of centers, the ambient dimensions, or the ratio of masses from different parity classes.

For one factor, suppress \(\ell\) and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P(A)&=\lbrace\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n:
A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\ne\varnothing\rbrace,
&K&=|\mathcal P(A)|,\\
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x)^2,
&\chi(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x),\\
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)}\quad(y\in R_x),
&Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,
&q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},\qquad
\nu=\sum_{x\in A}q_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Every row is the full reflected row: both orientations of each noncentral reflected pair occur, and its central atom occurs once. Then
$$
K^2b(p)\ge1,\qquad
\nu\le C(M,T)\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{4}
$$
For a finite lattice probability law \(p\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of its original mass over all ambient closed halfspaces containing \(j\), and put \(M_k(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^k\). With this convention the original product in (1), taking halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\), satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\gamma\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3}.
\tag{5}
$$
Since \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\) and \(0<\gamma\le c\), one common coefficient gives \(M_2(P)\ge\gamma4^{-N}\) and \(M_3(P)\ge\gamma8^{-N}\). The coefficient is paid once for the entire product. Singleton and lower-dimensional supports are included. One may take \(C(M,T)=0\) if \(M=1\) or \(T=0\).

## Proof

The quantities in (1) and (3) retain exactly their meanings and normalizations in accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705, c00bd51b9e53801c, and df930bf328963eef. In particular, \(K\) is the number of occupied parity classes, not the ambient capacity \(2^n\). We give the new conservation estimate and the original-product conversion in full.

Fix one factor. Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of all unordered pairs of distinct same-parity atoms, and let \(\mathcal J=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr Q\rbrace\). Retain the midpoint counts, full-row multipliers, and occupied scaling of c00bd51b9e53801c and df930bf328963eef:
$$
m_x=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q:j_e=x\rbrace|,\qquad
h_x=2m_x+1,\qquad t_x=Kp_x.
\tag{6}
$$
Thus \(h_x\) is an odd positive integer, with no upper bound imposed. The quantity \(m_x\) counts pairs at a midpoint; it is not a parity-class cardinality. The latter will be denoted \(m_\eta\). The parameter called \(D\) in c00bd51b9e53801c bounded midpoint multiplicity, and the parameter called \(D_i\) in 445a9e668ee1a705 measured endpoint incidence. Neither is imposed here.

A noncentral reflection orbit in \(R_x\) is exactly a pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(x\). Distinct such orbits at one center are disjoint, and the remaining orbit is the singleton \(\lbrace x\rbrace\). The exact midpoint equality in (2) makes each geometric-mean summand equal to \(p_x\). Consequently \(|R_x|=h_x\), \(H_p(x)=h_xp_x\), and \(\kappa_x\) is uniform on its full row. In particular \(b(p)>0\).

Write \(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\), \(m_\eta=|\mathcal C_\eta|\), \(r_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x\), \(R_\eta=Kr_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}t_x\), and \(d_\eta=(R_\eta-1)^2\). Here \(1\le m_\eta\le M\), and the meanings of \(r_\eta,R_\eta,d_\eta\) are exactly those in c00bd51b9e53801c and df930bf328963eef. Expanding each occupied parity square and using (2) counts every unordered pair once before its factor two:
$$
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}p_{u_e}p_{v_e}
=\sum_xh_xp_x^2=\chi(p).
\tag{7}
$$
Retain the full-row energies of those predecessors,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),
&\mathcal D&=K\sum_\eta r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_\eta d_\eta\ge0,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\sum_xp_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2,
&\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}&=\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The symbol \(\mathcal D\) is a parity-mass energy, distinct from both incidence parameters just discussed. Its identity and nonnegativity follow because there are exactly \(K\) occupied class masses, with sum one. Expanding the square in \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\), then applying (7), gives
$$
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}=A_{\mathcal J}-2K\chi(p)+1,
\qquad A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D
=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}\ge1.
\tag{9}
$$
For \(h\ge1\) and \(t>0\), put \(\Psi_h(t)=t(ht-1)^2\), as in df930bf328963eef, and abbreviate \(\Psi_x=\Psi_{h_x}(t_x)\). Then \(\sum_x\Psi_x=K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\).

We first control the sum of all squared pair scores by these energies. Choose either endpoint ordering for each pair and set \(z_e=\frac12\log(t_{v_e}/t_{u_e})\). For each class define the integer excess, its energy, and its total squared pair score by
$$
F_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}h_x-m_\eta^2,
\qquad f_\eta=d_\eta+\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\Psi_x,
\qquad S_\eta=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q\\u_e,v_e\in\mathcal C_\eta}}z_e^2.
\tag{10}
$$
Thus \(F_\eta\) is a new class excess, not the pair-local quantity called \(F_e\) in the two cited predecessors. Complete row counting gives a global conservation identity:
$$
\sum_xh_x=|A|+2|\mathscr Q|
=\sum_\eta\left(m_\eta+2\binom{m_\eta}{2}\right)
=\sum_\eta m_\eta^2,
\qquad \sum_\eta F_\eta=0.
\tag{11}
$$
Indeed, each unordered same-parity pair has exactly one midpoint in \(A\), and contributes two to the sum of row cardinalities. This does not require distinct pairs to have distinct midpoints.

If \(M=1\), there are no such pairs, so \(\nu=0\). If \(T=0\), all pair scores vanish, which also gives \(\nu=0\). Together with (9) this proves (4) with \(C=0\) in either case. For the construction of a finite constant in the remaining cases, assume \(M\ge2\) and \(T>0\). Put \(E=e^{2T}\), \(\rho=(2ME)^{-1}\), and \(\mathsf B=\binom M2T^2\). Every class has \(0\le S_\eta\le\mathsf B\). Since every pair in a class is among the pairs in (2), its scaled masses satisfy \(t_x\le Et_y\) for all \(x,y\) in that class. In particular, if \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\), each \(t_x\) is at least \(R_\eta/(m_\eta E)\ge\rho\), and each is at most two.

For \(1\le m\le M\), define the nonempty compact set
$$
\mathcal K_{m,T}=\left\{(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in[\rho,2]^m:
\frac12\le\sum_{i=1}^m t_i\le2,
\ t_i\le Et_j\text{ for all }i,j\right\}.
\tag{12}
$$
It is closed in a compact box, and the vector \((1/m,\ldots,1/m)\) belongs to it. Every actual balanced class belongs to this set after its atoms are enumerated. For a vector \(\mathbf h=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\) of odd positive integers, write
$$
f_{\mathbf h}(\mathbf t)=
\left(\sum_{i=1}^m t_i-1\right)^2
+\sum_{i=1}^m t_i(h_it_i-1)^2.
\tag{13}
$$
This polynomial is continuous and nonnegative. On (12), a zero forces \(\sum_i t_i=1\) and \(t_i=1/h_i\) for every \(i\), because all coordinates are positive. Therefore a zero forces \(\sum_i1/h_i=1\).

For a negative-excess class the multiplier vector satisfies \(\sum_i h_i<m^2\). There are finitely many such vectors as \(m\le M\), since each entry is a positive integer less than \(M^2\). None has a zero of (13) on (12): Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\left(\sum_i h_i\right)\left(\sum_i\frac1{h_i}\right)\ge m^2,
\tag{14}
$$
which precludes \(\sum_i1/h_i=1\) when \(\sum_i h_i<m^2\). For every such vector, the minimum of (13) on the nonempty compact set (12) is consequently strictly positive. Define \(\delta_->0\) to be the minimum of \(1/4\) and all these finitely many positive minima; if there are no such vectors, define \(\delta_-=1/4\). For any actual negative-excess class with balanced mass this gives \(f_\eta\ge\delta_-\). If instead \(R_\eta<1/2\) or \(R_\eta>2\), then \(d_\eta\ge1/4\), so the same lower bound holds. Since \(0<-F_\eta\le M^2\), in all cases
$$
\sum_{F_\eta<0}(-F_\eta)
\le\frac{M^2}{\delta_-}\sum_{F_\eta<0}f_\eta.
\tag{15}
$$
The endpoints \(R_\eta=1/2,2\) are included in (12). A scaled coordinate \(t_x<\rho\) cannot occur in a balanced class under the score bound; unbalanced classes are covered by \(d_\eta\ge1/4\), however small their positive coordinates are. No boundary mass has been discarded.

We next prove a quadratic score estimate for zero-excess classes. Their multiplier vectors satisfy \(\sum_i h_i=m^2\), so again there are only finitely many vectors for \(m\le M\). If (13) vanishes in (12), then \(\sum_i1/h_i=1\), and equality holds in (14). The equality condition in Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \((\sqrt{h_i})_i\) and \((1/\sqrt{h_i})_i\) forces all \(h_i\) to be equal. Thus necessarily \(h_i=m\) and \(t_i=1/m\) for every \(i\). Such a multiplier vector exists among odd vectors only when \(m\) is odd. Conversely, that vector and that point do give a zero.

For each zero-excess multiplier vector, take the whole set (12) if \(\mathbf h\ne(m,\ldots,m)\). If \(\mathbf h=(m,\ldots,m)\), take instead its closed subset
$$
\left\{\mathbf t\in\mathcal K_{m,T}:
\max_i|t_i-1/m|\ge1/(2m)\right\}.
\tag{16}
$$
Discard empty subsets. Every remaining set is compact and contains no zero of its polynomial (13), by the zero characterization just proved. Define \(\delta_0>0\) as the minimum of one and all their positive polynomial minima, with \(\delta_0=1\) if no set remains. This is a finite minimum depending only on \(M,T\). On any of these sets the score sum is at most \(\mathsf B\), hence at most \((\mathsf B/\delta_0)f_{\mathbf h}\).

It remains to give the required local estimate in the omitted neighborhood of a zero. There \(h_i=m\) and \(|t_i-1/m|<1/(2m)\) for every \(i\), in particular \(t_i>1/(2m)\). Put \(a_i=t_i-1/m\) only in this estimate. The derivative of \(\log t\) is at most \(2m\) on every interval joining two such coordinates, while
\(\Psi_m(t_i)=m^2t_i a_i^2\ge(m/2)a_i^2\). Hence, for every distinct pair \(i,j\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac14\left(\log\frac{t_j}{t_i}\right)^2
&\le m^2(t_j-t_i)^2
\le2m^2(a_i^2+a_j^2)\\
&\le4m\bigl(\Psi_m(t_i)+\Psi_m(t_j)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
Summing over all unordered pairs counts each \(\Psi_m(t_i)\) exactly \(m-1\) times, so the total is at most \(4m(m-1)\sum_i\Psi_m(t_i)\). This proves a quadratic bound at the zero itself as well as throughout its neighborhood; no inference of quadratic domination from mere compactness is used.

For a zero-excess class outside the balanced mass interval, \(d_\eta\ge1/4\) and \(S_\eta\le\mathsf B\). Combining this with (16)--(17), define
$$
L_0=\max\left\{4\mathsf B,\ \frac{\mathsf B}{\delta_0},\ 4M(M-1)\right\}.
\tag{18}
$$
Then \(S_\eta\le L_0 f_\eta\) for every zero-excess class, including singleton classes.

For every nonzero integer excess, \(|F_\eta|\ge1\). Therefore (11) and (15) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{F_\eta\ne0}S_\eta
&\le\mathsf B\sum_{F_\eta\ne0}|F_\eta|
=2\mathsf B\sum_{F_\eta<0}(-F_\eta)\\
&\le\frac{2\mathsf B M^2}{\delta_-}
\sum_{F_\eta<0}f_\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
Thus positive-excess classes are paid for by the negative ones through the exact global conservation law. They need no bound on their individual multipliers. Set
$$
L_\ast=\max\left\{L_0,\frac{2\mathsf B M^2}{\delta_-}\right\}.
\tag{20}
$$
The classes partition the atoms and the unordered pairs. From (18)--(20) we obtain the dimension-independent estimate
$$
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}z_e^2
=\sum_\eta S_\eta
\le L_\ast\sum_\eta f_\eta
=L_\ast\left(\sum_x\Psi_x+\sum_\eta d_\eta\right).
\tag{21}
$$
All minima used above are over explicit finite families of compact sets, all their zero sets have been characterized, and (17) supplies the local quadratic comparison wherever a zero is possible.

We now convert (21) into the full-row price without repeating a center deviation once per pair. Uniformity of \(\kappa_x\) gives zero score at its center and the scores \(\pm z_e\) for each pair with midpoint \(x\). Define \(G_e=2h_{j_e}t_{j_e}^3z_e^2\). Then the exact oriented-row identities are
$$
V_x=\frac2{h_x}\sum_{e:j_e=x}z_e^2,
\qquad A_{\mathcal J}\nu=\frac1K\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}G_e.
\tag{22}
$$
For a center \(x\) with \(h_xt_x\notin[1/2,2]\), write \(a=h_xt_x\) temporarily. Its entire contribution satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e:j_e=x}G_e
&\le2T^2h_xt_x^3m_x
=T^2h_x(h_x-1)t_x^3\\
&\le T^2t_xa^2
\le4T^2t_x(a-1)^2=4T^2\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
Here \(a^2\le4(a-1)^2\) for \(0<a<1/2\) and for \(a>2\). This charges each such center once, however large \(m_x\) is.

Every pair remaining after (23) has a center with \(h_xt_x\in[1/2,2]\), and that center has \(h_x\ge3\) because it has a noncentral pair. Hence \(t_x\le2/h_x\le2/3\), and
\(2h_xt_x^3=2(h_xt_x)t_x^2\le16/9\). By (21)--(23),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}G_e
&\le4T^2\sum_x\Psi_x+\frac{16}{9}\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}z_e^2\\
&\le\left(4T^2+\frac{16}{9}L_\ast\right)\sum_x\Psi_x
+\frac{16}{9}L_\ast\sum_\eta d_\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
The use of all pairs in the second term only enlarges a nonnegative sum. Every atom's \(\Psi_x\) appears once in the class partition in (21), and once at most as an entire-center charge in (23); (24) displays their combined coefficient. This includes centers that are also endpoints of other pairs, without any midpoint-degree cutoff.

Define the finite constant \(C(M,T)=4T^2+(16/9)L_\ast\) in the nontrivial cases under consideration. Divide (24) by \(K\) and use (8) and (22). Since \((16/9)L_\ast\le C(M,T)\),
$$
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
\le C(M,T)\bigl(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+\mathcal D\bigr)
\le C(M,T)\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{25}
$$
Together with (9) and \((a-1)/a\le\log a\) for \(a\ge1\), this gives (4). If \(A_{\mathcal J}=1\), (25) says \(\nu=0\) directly. If there are no pairs, (22) already says \(\nu=0\), regardless of any multiplier estimates. This also verifies every degeneracy used when \(M=1\) or \(T=0\).

For completeness, we carry the price through the original heterogeneous product, using exactly the reflection conventions of 445a9e668ee1a705 and df930bf328963eef. For any supported product center \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), its full row factors, and so do its geometric row mass and law:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_J&=\prod_\ell R_{\ell,j_\ell},
&H_P(J)&=\prod_\ell H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell),
&\kappa_J&=\bigotimes_\ell\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\\
Y_J(X)&=\sum_\ell Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),
&\sigma_J^2&=\sum_\ell V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
Reflection preserves each factor row law and negates its score, giving mean zero. Independence therefore yields \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2\). Direct substitution gives the exact likelihood identity
\(P_X=H_P(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}\) on \(R_J\), where \(P_X=\prod_\ell w_{\ell,x_\ell}/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\) is still the original law.

Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral orbit \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\), since excluding both endpoints strictly would exclude their midpoint. It contains the central atom \(J\) as well. Summing the smaller original mass in each noncentral orbit and half the central mass, and then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral orbit twice before multiplying by \(1/2\), and counts its central atom only once before that factor. Thus this estimate uses only half of the available central mass and is valid for singleton rows, lower-dimensional supports, and containing halfspaces whose boundary includes supported atoms.

Let \(q=\bigotimes_\ell q_\ell\) be the auxiliary center law. Its exact relation to the original law is
\(P_JH_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell))q_J\). In particular, each reflected factor has the original unnormalized expression
$$
b(p_\ell)=\frac1{Z_\ell^3}
\sum_{j\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{\ell,j}}
\sqrt{w_{\ell,x}w_{\ell,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{28}
$$
Squaring (27), summing under the original probability \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q\) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2}\right)
=\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
No original law has been conditioned or replaced; \(q\) is only the center law in this exact factorization. Put \(s_{\log}=\sum_\ell\log(K_\ell^2b(p_\ell))\ge0\). This scalar is distinct from the mixed-integer set \(S\) in the locked problem. By (4), \(\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\le C(M,T)s_{\log}\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2}\right)
\exp\bigl(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C(M,T)s_{\log}}\bigr)\\
&\ge\frac14e^{-C(M,T)}\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
$$
because \(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{Cs_{\log}}=(\sqrt{s_{\log}}-\sqrt C)^2-C\ge-C\), including \(C=0\). This pays the coefficient once for the whole product. Finally, Jensen under the same original probability \(P\) gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving (5). The comparison \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\) gives both ambient bounds with the common coefficient \(\gamma\). All constants depend only on \(M,T\).
