## Statement

Let $E$ be a three-dimensional real vector space, let $c_X,c_Z$ be independent, put $U=\operatorname{span}\{c_X,c_Z\}$ and $c_Y=-c_X-c_Z$, and let $Q_X,Q_Y,Q_Z$ be symmetric bilinear forms on $E$ with respective kernel vectors $c_X,c_Y,c_Z$. Assume $Q_Y$ has a positive direction and $A_X=-Q_X,A_Z=-Q_Z$ are positive semidefinite. For each $v\in E$ let the funding cost be the minimum of $A_X(u_X,u_X)+A_Z(u_Z,u_Z)$ over $u_X+u_Z=v$. If either $A$-kernel is not contained in $U$, the minimum is attained at zero for every $v$. Otherwise choose a nonzero functional $\ell$ with kernel $U$, represent the forms and functional in Euclidean coordinates, and put
$$
D=\ell^\top A_X^+\ell+\ell^\top A_Z^+\ell>0,
$$
where $+$ denotes the Moore–Penrose inverse. The minimum is attained and equals $\ell(v)^2/D$. Thus the maximum funded curvature for prescribed $v$ is $Q_Y(v,v)-\ell(v)^2/D$.

In the latter case the funded form descends to $E/\operatorname{span}\{c_Y\}$. If the descended $Q_Y$ has signature $(+,-)$, the funded form has a positive direction exactly when $D>\ell^\top Q_Y^{-1}\ell$ on that quotient. In a quotient basis where $\ell=(0,1)$ and $Q_Y=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c&b\end{pmatrix}$, it has a positive direction if $a>0$ or if $a=0$, $c\ne0$; when $a=c=0$ this is equivalent to $bD>1$, and when $a<0$ it is equivalent to $b-c^2/a>1/D$.

## Proof

If either kernel has a vector outside $U$, that vector and the two independent kernel vectors $c_X,c_Z$ span $E$. Hence the two kernels sum to $E$ and a zero-cost decomposition exists for each $v$.

Otherwise $\ell$ annihilates both kernels. In Euclidean coordinates each positive semidefinite $A$ satisfies $A A^+\ell=\ell$. Cauchy–Schwarz on the quotient by its kernel gives
$$
A(u,u)\ge \frac{\ell(u)^2}{\ell^\top A^+\ell}.
$$
The denominator is positive because $\ell\ne0$ and lies in the range of $A$. Equality for a prescribed value of $\ell(u)$ is obtained at a multiple of $A^+\ell$. Put $d_X=\ell^\top A_X^+\ell$ and $d_Z=\ell^\top A_Z^+\ell$. Minimizing $s^2/d_X+(\ell(v)-s)^2/d_Z$ gives $\ell(v)^2/(d_X+d_Z)$, at $s=d_X\ell(v)/(d_X+d_Z)$. The corresponding equality vectors sum to $v$ modulo $U$. Adding appropriate multiples of $c_X,c_Z$ to their respective vectors fixes their sum without changing the costs. This proves attainment and the exact formula.

Since both $Q_Y$ and $\ell$ annihilate $c_Y$, the rank-one subtraction is well defined on the quotient. If $Q_Y$ has signature $(+,-)$ there, the determinant lemma gives
$$
\det(Q_Y-\ell\ell^\top/D)=\det(Q_Y)\bigl(1-\ell^\top Q_Y^{-1}\ell/D\bigr).
$$
The subtraction leaves a strictly negative direction. A positive direction exists exactly when the determinant is negative, giving the strict comparison. In the stated quotient basis, the funded form on $(u,v)$ is $a u^2+2cuv+(b-1/D)v^2$. For $a>0$ choose $v=0$. If $a=0$ and $c\ne0$ this form is indefinite, and if also $c=0$ its only possible positive coefficient is $b-1/D$. If $a<0$, completing the square gives $a(u+cv/a)^2+(b-1/D-c^2/a)v^2$, establishing the last equivalence.
