## Statement

Let J,R partition N barycentric coordinates with R nonempty and let H be a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix from J to R. Every (N-1)-simplex in {lambda=(y,x)>=0,sum lambda=1,y<=Hx} has volume ratio at most product_j sqrt(m_j)/(1+sqrt(m_j))<=2^(-|J|), where m_j=max_r H_jr. An explicit sum-preserving linear map gives the bound and exact nonnegative determinant defects. For an irreducible stochastic P, the bounds apply to the genuine missed-row cone lambda_J<=(P lambda)_J using its proved absorption matrix. The factor2^(-|J|) is sharp; strictly positive off-diagonal transitions give strict inequality for nonempty properJ,N>=3. Weighted missed-pattern budgets and fixed centroid sign-fan surplus in d2,d3 are proved. No cap-excess bridge or arbitrary-fan conclusion is assumed.

## Proof

# Stochastic sign cones: sharp simplex capacity and absorption-sensitive bounds

Research theorem, 6 November 2026. This concerns genuine common-matrix
halfspace intersections in a simplex. It proves the proposed 2^(-|J|)
capacity bound, an absorption-sensitive improvement, and exact determinant
defect budgets. It makes no arbitrary-fan or OC conclusion.

## 1. Abstract dominated-coordinate theorem

Let N>=2, let J,R be a partition of {1,...,N} with R nonempty, and write
k=|J|. Let H be a nonnegative k by |R| matrix with every row sum equal1.
In the standard barycentric simplex Delta={lambda>=0:sum lambda=1}, write
lambda=(y,x), where y uses J coordinates and x uses R coordinates. Put

    D_H={lambda in Delta:y<=Hx componentwise}.

For each j in J define

    m_j=max_(r in R) H_jr,    t_j=1/sqrt(m_j),
    A_H=product_(j in J)(1+t_j).                               (1)

The maxima m_j are positive and at most1. Every inscribed (N-1)-simplex
Q in D_H satisfies the exact volume-ratio bound

    |Q|/|Delta|<=1/A_H
        =product_(j in J) sqrt(m_j)/(1+sqrt(m_j))
        <=2^(-k).                                              (2)

For J empty, empty products equal1 and this is the ordinary containment
bound. No irreducibility or Markov assumption is needed for this abstract
statement.

### A linear ellipsoid certificate

For k>0 define the linear map

    T_H(y,x)=((1+t_j)y_j : j in J,
               x-H^T(t_j y_j : j in J)).                       (3)

It preserves the sum of coordinates because each row of H sums to1, and
its determinant is A_H by block triangularity. Some coordinates in its
R block can be NEGATIVE; no positive-orthant claim is used.

For a feasible lambda put X=sum x_r, Y=sum y_j, and
z=H^T(t_j y_j). Then X+Y=1, y_j<=(Hx)_j<=m_j X, and

    ||H_j||_2^2<=m_j sum_r H_jr=m_j,
    ||z||_2<=sum_j t_j y_j ||H_j||_2<=Y.                       (4)

The first inequality below uses the actual domination y<=Hx:

    ||T_H lambda||_2^2
      =sum_j(1+2t_j+t_j^2)y_j^2+||x||_2^2
             -2sum_j t_j y_j(Hx)_j+||z||_2^2
      <=sum_j(1+t_j^2)y_j^2+||x||_2^2+||z||_2^2
      <=XY+XY+X^2+Y^2=1.                                     (5)

Indeed sum y_j^2<=XY because y_j<=X; and
sum t_j^2 y_j^2=sum y_j^2/m_j<=X sum y_j=XY. All vectors appearing in
these two scalar inequalities are nonnegative.

Let Lambda be the N by N matrix of barycentric vertex columns of Q.
Its absolute determinant is |Q|/|Delta|: the column-sum-one condition
identifies its affine determinant on the barycentric hyperplane with
its full linear determinant. One can also subtract its last column from
the others and expand to obtain the usual affine determinant formula.
Every column of T_H Lambda has Euclidean norm at most1. Hadamard's
inequality therefore gives

    A_H |det Lambda|=|det(T_H Lambda)|<=1,

proving(2). This is a containing ellipsoid argument with its entire
normalization proved directly; it is not a bound for simplices in a
positive orthant of a Euclidean ball.

## 2. Application to one common stochastic sign matrix

Let P be an irreducible nonnegative N by N row-stochastic matrix. The
intended fan case also has P_jj=0, but that extra condition is not needed
for this section. Fix a proper set J of rows and put R=J^c. Define

    K_J={lambda in Delta:lambda_j<=(P lambda)_j for j in J}.

For nonempty J put Q=P_JJ and

    H=(I-Q)^(-1) P_JR.                                        (6)

This inverse exists, is nonnegative, and H is row-stochastic. To justify
all of these claims elementarily, irreducibility gives from every state
j in J a finite path of positive transition product to R. Choose m at
least the maximum of those path lengths and let delta>0 be the minimum
of their positive products. The total mass remaining in J after m steps
is at most1-delta, so ||Q^m||_infinity<=1-delta. Consequently sum_(l>=0)Q^l
converges to a nonnegative inverse of I-Q. Also

    (I-Q)1_J=P_JR 1_R,

so multiplication by that inverse proves H1_R=1_J.

Every lambda=(y,x) in K_J satisfies (I-Q)y<=P_JR x. Multiplying by the
nonnegative inverse gives y<=Hx. Thus K_J subset D_H and Section1 proves

    max_(simplex Q' subset K_J)|Q'|/|Delta|
      <=product_(j in J) sqrt(m_j)/(1+sqrt(m_j))
      <=2^(-|J|),                                             (7)

with m_j defined from the actual absorption matrix H in(6). The maximum
exists by compactness of K_J and continuity of the determinant on its N
vertex tuples. No limiting or generic-position hypothesis is used.

When absorption is uniform on R, H_jr=1/|R|, equation(7) becomes

    |Q'|/|Delta|<=(1+sqrt(|R|))^(-|J|).                        (8)

For J empty, K_J=Delta and the capacity is1. For completeness, if all rows
are imposed, the maximum principle and irreducibility force lambda to be
uniform: start at a maximal coordinate and propagate equality to every
state reachable by a positive transition. Thus K_all is a single point,
and its (N-1)-dimensional simplex capacity is zero.

An affine identification with any physical (N-1)-simplex B preserves
volume ratios, so these statements apply directly to barycentric sign
cones in B. This is an ordinary continuous-coordinate identification and
does not transform any integer lattice.

## 3. Exact sharpness of the universal factor

Fix any proper J and choose r_0 in R. For j in J set P_j,r0=1 and all its
other entries zero. For each row r in R set P_r,s=1/(N-1) for s!=r and
P_r,r=0. This is an irreducible row-stochastic matrix with zero diagonal:
every constrained state reaches r_0, and r_0 reaches every other state.
For J empty the same root-row rule is simply the complete off-diagonal
chain.

The imposed constraints are exactly lambda_j<=lambda_r0, j in J. The N
vertices

    e_r                         for r in R,
    (e_j+e_r0)/2                for j in J                     (9)

belong to K_J and form a simplex of determinant2^(-|J|), by expansion in
its J coordinates. Hence the final constant in(7) is sharp for every N
and every proper number of imposed rows. In this example H_j,r0=1, so
m_j=1, and T_H sends the vertices(9) to the standard orthonormal basis.

## 4. Exact nonnegative defect terms

The proof retains more information than the cardinality bound. For every
feasible lambda=(y,x), define z,X,Y as in Section1 and put

    delta_H(lambda)=1-||T_H lambda||_2^2.

The following is an EXACT identity into nonnegative terms:

    delta_H(lambda)
      =(X^2-||x||_2^2)
       +(Y^2-||z||_2^2)
       +(XY-sum_j y_j^2)
       +(XY-sum_j t_j^2 y_j^2)
       +2sum_j t_j y_j[(Hx)_j-y_j].                            (10)

Nonnegativity follows respectively from x>=0, equation(4), y_j<=X,
y_j<=m_j X, and feasibility. The identity follows by expanding the
right side and using X+Y=1; there is no discarded sign or unspecified
remainder. Since T_H is invertible and lambda is nonzero,

    0<=delta_H(lambda)<1.

For a simplex Q' with barycentric vertex columns lambda^(1),...,lambda^(N)
and ratio rho>0, Hadamard now gives the sharper inequality

    A_H rho<=product_l sqrt(1-delta_H(lambda^(l))).             (11)

In particular

    log A_H
       +(1/2)sum_l[-log(1-delta_H(lambda^(l)))]
        <=log(1/rho),                                         (12)

and the same assertion remains valid if each -log(1-delta) is replaced
by the smaller delta.

The unweighted choice t_j=1 also defines a valid map T_0 with determinant
2^|J|, even when m_j<1. Its defect decomposes as

    delta_0(lambda)
      =(X^2-||x||_2^2)+(Y^2-||H^T y||_2^2)
       +2[XY-||y||_2^2]+2[y dot Hx-||y||_2^2].                (13)

This records exactly the concentration, saturation, and domination losses
of the original 2^(-|J|) argument.

## 5. A weighted missed-pattern budget

Consider a simplex B of volume vV in dimension d=N-1, and simplex sources
P_i subset B of volume at least2^(-d)V, carrying nonnegative weights e_i.
Suppose their missed rows J_i are interpreted using ONE irreducible
row-stochastic P, so each source is contained in the corresponding K_Ji.
Positive-volume sources cannot miss all rows. Delete zero weights.
For each remaining source form H_i,T_i,A_i^abs from its own missed set by
(1),(3),(6); the superscript distinguishes A_i^abs from endpoint bodies.
Let delta_il be the defects(10) at its N barycentric vertices.

Then (12), with rho_i=|P_i|/|B|>=2^(-d)/v, proves

    sum_i e_i log A_i^abs
       +(1/2)sum_i e_i sum_l[-log(1-delta_il)]
       <=(sum_i e_i)(d log2+log v).                            (14)

This is a genuine weighted geometric constraint on missed patterns, not
an unweighted count or a proposed compatibility principle. Keeping only
the universal map(13) gives, with E=sum e_i and h_i=N-|J_i|,

    sum_i e_i h_i
      >=E[1-log(v)/log2]
         +(1/(2log2))sum_i e_i sum_l[-log(1-delta_0,il)].       (15)

All defect terms are computed from the actual source vertices and the
common absorption matrix for their missed set. Equation(14) is stronger
when absorption is diffuse. No lower bound relating these exact defect
terms to the ordinary cap excess has been proved here; in particular
(14)-(15) alone are not asserted to yield the sharp Gamma_d fan score.

## 6. Strict capacity for strictly positive off-diagonal transitions

Assume N>=3, P_jr>0 whenever j!=r, and J is nonempty and proper. Then

    max_(simplex Q' subset K_J)|Q'|/|Delta|<2^(-|J|).            (16)

If |R|>=2, every absorption entry H_jr is positive, because the direct
entry P_jr is positive and the Neumann inverse contains I. Every row of
H then has maximum m_j<1. The strict inequality follows immediately from
the absorption-sensitive product in(7).

If R consists of one root r, then H is the all-ones column. Use the
unweighted map T_0 of(13). Put X=lambda_r. Feasibility implies every
other coordinate y_j<=X; also X>0, since X=0 would force all coordinates
zero. Its exact norm defect reduces to

    delta_0(lambda)=4sum_j y_j(X-y_j).                         (17)

Thus a norm-one image can occur only when every y_j is0 orX. If all are
zero, lambda=e_r. If some y_j=X>0, the original inequality at that j gives

    X<=sum_l P_jl lambda_l<=X.

Every off-diagonal coefficient is positive, so every coordinate other
than j must also equalX. Hence lambda is the uniform vector. The only
norm-one feasible points are therefore e_r and the uniform vector.
For N>=3 they cannot supply N linearly independent vertex columns. Any
full-dimensional simplex either has a transformed column of norm below1
or has dependent transformed columns; Hadamard is consequently strict.
The maximum is attained by compactness, so its strict inequality is(16).
The restriction N>=3 matters: for N=2, the root and uniform vector attain
the ratio1/2. J empty remains the equality case K_J=Delta.

## 7. A fixed uniform-centroid sign-fan consequence in d=2 and d=3

Let B be a d-simplex of volume vV, 2^(-d)<=v<=1, and g its centroid.
Consider N=d+1 affine cuts through g forming a full positive fan. Suppose
each row of vertex values has one strictly positive entry and d strictly
negative entries. A positive linear dependence shows that every vertex
column has a positive entry; since there are exactly N positive entries,
permuting rows puts them on the diagonal. Dividing each row by its positive
diagonal gives I-P, where P has zero diagonal, strictly positive off-
diagonal entries, and row sums1. This follows because the sum of a row's
vertex values is zero at the uniform barycentric centroid.

Let simplex sources P_i subset B have volumes at least2^(-d)V, with
nonnegative normalized weights e_i summing to E=1-v. A strict hit means
the source has some point where the row is positive; a contact alone is
a miss. Every source hits at least TWO rows when d>=2. Indeed, missing
all rows is impossible for a positive-volume source, and missing d rows
would put it in a cone with capacity strictly below2^(-d) by(16). Its
volume would then be less than2^(-d)|B|<=2^(-d)V, a contradiction. The
strict-capacity statement handles the endpoint v=1 as well.

The own-cap fraction of row j is the exact vertex-cap product

    p_j=product_(l!=j)(1+P_jl)^(-1)>=Gamma_d,

where the last inequality is arithmetic-geometric mean and sum_(l!=j)
P_jl=1. Thus the fixed fan's scores satisfy

    max_j [v p_j+sum_i e_i 1{P_i strictly hits row j}]
      >=Gamma_d v+2E/(d+1)
      =Gamma_d+[2/(d+1)-Gamma_d]E.                            (18)

In dimension2 this is 4/9+(2/9)E; in dimension3 it is
27/64+(5/64)E. Both are at least the sharp ordinary constant. The weights
may vanish, including E=0, when the assertion reduces to the ordinary
own-cap bound. Every boundary contact uses the same closed missed-cone
convention as Sections1-6.

This proves a fixed uniform-centroid sign-fan statement, not an all-
halfspace center theorem. Formula(18) holds as written in higher dimensions
but its coefficient of E becomes negative starting at d=4; no sharp
higher-dimensional conclusion is inferred from the bare two-hit count.
