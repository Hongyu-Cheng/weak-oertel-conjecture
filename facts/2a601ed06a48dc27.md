## Statement

Let $n\ge3$, $d\ge1$, and $\Delta_n=\{z:z_i\ge0,\ \sum_i z_i\le1\}$. For any $d$-simplex $K$ of volume $V>0$, the actual integer bases of $C=2\Delta_n\times K$ are its $n+1$ simplex vertices in the base and all their edge midpoints. The largest parity-class mixed mass is $(n+1)V$. Put $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. At every integer base there is a menu of $d+1$ closed ambient caps covering its whole continuous plane, each of mass $\gamma_dV$ at vertex bases and $3\gamma_dV$ at edge-midpoint bases. In particular the maximum raw mixed depth is at most $3\gamma_dV$, and its ratio to $\gamma_d$ times the largest parity-class mass is at most $3/(n+1)$.

## Proof

The integer vectors in $2\Delta_n$ are the nonnegative integer vectors with coordinate sum at most two: $0$, $2e_i$, $e_i$, and $e_i+e_j$ for $i<j$. The first two types are the $n+1$ all-even vertices, and the last two types are edge midpoints and have pairwise distinct nonzero parity vectors. Every fiber equals $K$, so the largest parity mass is $(n+1)V$ and the total mass is $(n+1)(n+2)V/2$.

Let $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_n$ be the affine barycentric coordinates on the base simplex. For a sampled base $p$, write $I$ for the vertices of its minimal face (one vertex or two edge vertices) and $\ell_I(z)=\sum_{i\notin I}\lambda_i(z)$. At the sampled points of that face it is zero, and at any other sampled base it is at least $1/2$, because all sampled barycentric coordinates are nonnegative integer multiples of $1/2$. Let $L_0,\ldots,L_d$ be the affine barycentric coordinates of the continuous simplex. Consider
$$
H_{I,r}=\{(z,x):L_r(x)-2\ell_I(z)\ge 1/(d+1)\},\qquad 0\le r\le d.
$$
For each base of the face the margins have the through-centroid thresholds, and the caps cover the entire continuous plane because $\sum_rL_r(x)=1$ for all $x\in\mathbb R^d$. Outside the face the left side is at most $1-2(1/2)=0$ on $K$, so no other fiber contributes. Each cap captures in each retained fiber the homothetic copy of $K$ about vertex $r$ with ratio $d/(d+1)$, hence mass $\gamma_dV$. The face retains exactly one sampled base in the vertex case and three in the edge case. Every point above $p$ lies in one of these caps, proving the raw bound and the ratio. Since the ratio tends to zero, a uniform positive multiplier of the largest parity-class mass times $\gamma_d$ cannot lower-bound the maximum depth.
