## Statement

For every $d\ge2$, choose two full-dimensional simplices $A,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ that are not translates of positive homothets of one another. Their ordinary normalized maximum depths both equal $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, but
$$
\min_U (|P_U(A)|+|P_U(B)|)-|A|-|B|>0.
$$
They are realized as the complete integer fiber family of $\operatorname{conv}((\{0\}\times A)\cup(\{1\}\times B))$. Thus no finite constant times the sum of individual ordinary depth deficits from $\Gamma_d$ bounds the minimum common-orientation excess even for actual compatible sampled fibers.

## Proof

There are nonhomothetic simplices in every $d\ge2$, for example the standard simplex and its image under the linear map that doubles its first coordinate. The two opposite-origin facets have different normal rays, which rules out positive homothety. Their normalized maximum depths are $\Gamma_d$ by accepted fact 03bc771bddd39a8a. The weighted orientation theorem applies with two full-dimensional members and weights one. If the minimum excess were zero, then each of $A\subset P_U(A)$ and $B\subset P_U(B)$ would be an equality in volume at an attaining orientation. A proper inclusion of compact convex bodies of full dimension has strict volume increase: a point of the larger outside the smaller and the interior of the larger give interior points outside the smaller, and hence a positive-volume region. Therefore both bodies would be their envelopes at a single orientation, making them positive homothets up to translation by the envelope lemma. Finally, a convex combination of bases $0,1$ has base $0$ or $1$ only if supported on the corresponding endpoint. These are the only integer points of $[0,1]$, so the displayed hull has exactly the claimed fibers.
