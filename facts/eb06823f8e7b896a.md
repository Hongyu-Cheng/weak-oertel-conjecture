## Statement

In the setting and subsequential limit of the sampled concave roof compactness lemma, suppose $h_j=\varepsilon_j^{d+1}L_j$, where $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ and $L_j>0$ are continuous concave with
$$
\frac1{M_j}\sum_{z\in P}\int_{K_{j,z}}\log L_j(z,X)\,dX=0,\qquad
M_j=\sum_{z\in P}|K_{j,z}|.
$$
Then the sampled zero set $Z$ is nonempty.

## Proof

For a positive continuous concave function $f$ on a positive convex $d$-body $K$, put $m=\max_K f$ and choose a maximizer. Homotheties about the maximizer give $|\{f\ge tm\}|\ge (1-t)^d|K|$ for $0<t<1$. Applying the layer-cake formula to $\log(m/f)$ and substituting $t=e^{-s}$ gives
$$
\frac1{|K|}\int_K\log(m/f)\,dX
\le\int_0^1\frac{1-(1-t)^d}{t}\,dt=\sum_{i=1}^d\frac1i,
$$
using the finite geometric sum after substituting $1-t$. If $Z$ were empty, a fixed interior point of each fiber would have a positive limit. The moving fiber maxima would therefore all be bounded away from zero along this subsequence. The displayed estimate bounds the mixed average of $\log h_j$ from below, since that average is a convex combination of the fiber averages. Its exact value is $(d+1)\log\varepsilon_j\to-\infty$ by normalization, a contradiction.
