## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$, $H>0$ and $0<U<H$. Put $B=H+1$ and $r(z)=(H-z)/B$. On $-1\le z\le U$, $|x|\le r(z)$, use the measure of density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$ with respect to $dx\,dz$. Set $a=r(U)$, $T=U/a$, $t=a^d=[H/(B+T)]^d$, $V_0=B/d$, and $V=(1-t)V_0$. The associated cone has the same density formula on $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, and mass $V_0$.

For a finite slope $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $C(s)$ and $M(s)$ be the integrals of $f$ and $zf$, respectively, over the section $z+sx=0$ inside the support, against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. A law's record is the average of $(P,C,M)$, and a law is centered if its averaged $M$ is zero.

Given any probability law on finite slopes $s\ge0$, let $\phi$ be the mixture of the uniform probabilities on $[-s,s]$, with the layer at $s=0$ interpreted as $\delta_0$. Put $v=\phi((-\infty,1])$, $w=\phi([-T,\infty))$, $C_0=(H/B)^{d-1}$, $p_0=(H/B)^d$, and $a_0=1/B$. For $0<q<1$ put $e(q)=\int_{[-T,1]}(1-qb)^{-d}\,d\phi(b)$. The averaged frustum records satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{P}{V_0}&=p_0e(a_0)+1-v-tw,\\
\frac{C}{C_0}&=e(a_0),\\
\frac{M}{C_0}&=-\frac HB\int_{[-T,1]} b(1-b/B)^{-d-1}\,d\phi(b).
\end{aligned}
$$

The energy $e$ is differentiable and convex on $(0,1)$. Centering is equivalent to $e'(a_0)=0$, or equivalently to $a_0$ minimizing $e$ on $(0,1)$.

If $T\ge1$ and the slope law is supported on $[0,T]$, the corresponding full-cone energy at $a_0$ is the same integral and satisfies $e(a_0)\ge (2v-1)+(t/p_0)(1-v)$. This last bound applies in particular to centered cone laws.

## Proof

Use $x=r(z)u$, so the measure becomes $r(z)^{d-1}\,du\,dz/2$ on $|u|\le1$. At each slope set $b=su$, giving the stated conditional law of $b$. The frustum occupies $a\le r\le1$, and the margin along a fixed ray is $H-(B-b)r$. For $b\ge1$ its values at both axial endpoints are nonnegative, so the whole ray is captured. For $b\le -T$ both are nonpositive, and the captured mass is zero. In between, the captured radii range from $a$ to $H/(B-b)$. Integrating $B r^{d-1}\,dr$ gives the three contributions in units of $V_0$ as $1-t$, $0$, and $p_0(1-b/B)^{-d}-t$, respectively. The measure $\phi$ has zero mass at $1$ and $-T$, since neither is zero and positive finite layers have no atoms. Thus averaging gives the first identity, with the subtraction $t$ on the whole event $b\ge -T$. The mass formulas for the cone and frustum follow by integrating $B r^{d-1}$ over $[0,1]$ and $[a,1]$.

For $s>0$, parametrize the section by $x=-z/s$. Its coarea integrand becomes $r(z)^{d-2}\,dz/(2s)$ on the set where $|z|\le sr(z)$. Along this section $b=-z/r(z)$ runs from $1$ to $-T$ as $z$ runs from $-1$ to $U$. Restrict to $b\in[-s,s]$ to enforce the section condition. Since $db/dz=-H/(B r(z)^2)$, the coarea weight in the $b$ variable is $(B/H)r(z)^d\,db/(2s)$ with the integration limits put in increasing order. Here $r(z)=H/(B-b)$ and $z=-br(z)$. The weight and its product by $z$ give the last two identities. At $s=0$, the section is $z=0$, giving $C=C_0$, $M=0$, as does the atom at $b=0$. The integrands are bounded on $[-T,1]$ at $a_0$, so averaging holds even for laws of unbounded finite slopes.

Differentiation under the integral is justified locally in $q$ by the compact $b$ interval and a positive lower bound for $1-qb$. The second derivative is an integral of $d(d+1)b^2(1-qb)^{-d-2}$, which is nonnegative. The moment formula gives $M/C_0=-(H/B)e'(a_0)/d$, proving the characterization.

For the full cone the captured ray energy integrates over $b\le1$, because the upper axial endpoint is the apex at $z=H$ and the margin there is positive. When the slope law is supported on $[0,T]$, the base law is supported on $[-T,T]$, so that full-cone integral equals $e(a_0)$. By symmetry, $[-1,1]$ has mass $2v-1$. Convexity of $b\mapsto (1-b/B)^{-d}$ gives a contribution at least $2v-1$ on this symmetric interval, with the claim immediate for a zero-mass interval. The remaining included interval $[-T,-1)$ has mass $1-v$ and integrand at least $(1+T/B)^{-d}=t/p_0$. Adding the two contributions proves the bound.
