## Statement

Let $\nu$ be a nonatomic probability on $[0,1]$ symmetric about $1/2$. On $\{0,1,2,3\}\times[0,1]$, give the bases unnormalized weights $(a,b,b,a)$ with $b\ge a>0$ and use the common conditional law $\nu$. The maximum closed-halfspace depth, normalized by $2a+2b$, is $(2a+b)/(4(a+b))$. It is attained at $(1,1/2)$ and $(2,1/2)$.

## Proof

Every closed halfspace containing a candidate contains the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through it, so it suffices to use through-point cuts. Reflection of the continuous coordinate about $1/2$ preserves the law and each base. At a fixed base, the midpoint of a candidate and its reflected point has depth at least their common depth, since every closed halfspace containing the midpoint contains at least one of the two. Thus it suffices to evaluate depth at the four heights $1/2$.

Let $q(u)=\nu\{x:x-1/2\ge u\}$. Symmetry and nonatomicity give $q(-u)=1-q(u)$ and $q(0)=1/2$. At $(1,1/2)$ a through-point cut with nonzero continuous normal can be reflected, if necessary, to $x-1/2\ge u(z-1)$ for some real $u$. Its raw mass is
$$
a q(-u)+b/2+b q(u)+a q(2u)
=a+b/2+(b-a)q(u)+a q(2u)\ge a+b/2.
$$
For $u>1/2$ the two terms containing $q(u),q(2u)$ vanish. Both pure-base orientations retain the boundary fiber and have raw masses $a+b$ and $a+2b$, respectively. Hence the depth at $(1,1/2)$ is exactly $a+b/2$ before normalization. Base reflection gives the same at $(2,1/2)$. At base zero the cut $x-1/2\ge u z$ with $u>1/2$ takes just half the extreme fiber's mass. At base three reflect the same cut. Their depths at height $1/2$ are thus at most $a/2$, strictly below $a+b/2$.
