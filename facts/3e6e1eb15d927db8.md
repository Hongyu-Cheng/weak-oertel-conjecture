## Statement

For $d\ge2$, let $E_0,\ldots,E_d$ be independent unit exponentials, $V=\sum_{j=0}^d c_jE_j$, and $P(c)=\Pr(V\ge0)$. If $c$ has at least two strictly positive coefficients and at least one strictly negative coefficient, $P$ is jointly $C^2$ near $c$, allowing repeated or zero entries. Set $g=\nabla P(c)$ and $H=\nabla^2P(c)$. Then $g\cdot c=0$, $Hc=-g$, and $g_j=d f_V(0)b_j$, where $b$ is the actual barycentric centroid of the section $c\cdot u=0$ of the uniform simplex and $f_V$ is the density of $V$.

## Proof

Select two positive coefficients $a,b$ and a negative coefficient $-r$, with $a,b,r$ bounded away from zero on a neighborhood. For $W=aE_1+bE_2-rE_3$ (using those three selected phases), the survival probability is
$$
\Pr(W\ge z)=
\begin{cases}
1-e^{z/r}r^2/[(a+r)(b+r)],& z\le0,\\
\big[a^2e^{-z/a}/(a+r)-b^2e^{-z/b}/(b+r)\big]/(a-b),& z\ge0.
\end{cases}
$$
For $z\le0$ this follows by conditioning on the two positive phases and using the exponential tail. For $z\ge0$, the two-positive-phase survival function at $y\ge0$ is $(a e^{-y/a}-b e^{-y/b})/(a-b)$ by convolution, and averaging at $y=z+rE_3$ gives the second expression. At $a=b$ use the integral of the derivative of $q\mapsto q^2e^{-z/q}/(q+r)$ along the interval joining $a$ to $b$ for its divided difference. At $z=0$, the two pieces have the same value and the same first and second $z$ derivatives, with derivative values $-r/[(a+r)(b+r)]$ and $-1/[(a+r)(b+r)]$. Their parameter derivatives match as needed by differentiating these matching identities. Thus the survival probability is jointly $C^2$. Its derivatives through order two are uniformly bounded for real $z$ and parameters in a compact positive neighborhood: differentiating the exponential formulas and divided-difference integral gives polynomials in $|z|$ times a decaying exponential, plus the constant term on $z\le0$.

Condition on the remaining phases, whose weighted sum is $Y$. The cap probability is the expectation of this survival probability at $z=-Y$. Its derivatives through order two are dominated by a constant times $(1+\sum_jE_j)^2$, permitting differentiation and continuity by dominated convergence even as coefficients in $Y$ pass through zero. Positive homogeneity of degree zero gives $g\cdot c=0$ and, on differentiating, $Hc=-g$.

For the centroid identity, let $G=\sum_jE_j$, $U_j=E_j/G$ and $L=c\cdot U$. The change of variables to $(G,U)$ has Jacobian $G^d$ and gives independent uniform $U$ and shape-$(d+1)$ unit-scale gamma $G$. The proper-cut first-variation lemma applied to $F_{-c}(0)$ gives $g_j=f_L(0)b_j$. The projection density $f_L$ is bounded by the uniform bound on parallel section volumes from coarea, and continuous at zero by proper-cut regularity. Since $V=GL$, its density at zero is $f_L(0)\mathbb E[G^{-1}]=f_L(0)/d$. This follows by conditioning on $G$ and dominated convergence at zero, identifying the resulting density with the continuous density in accepted fact 511d89569241b0d6. This proves the identity.
