## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a compact full-dimensional convex body. For every real \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), define \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\) and \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), taking the volume to be zero for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Put \(K:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0\), and \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). Keep the canonical depth \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces.

For every positive-volume fiber, let \(\lambda_z\) be its uniform probability measure, so \(\lambda_z(A)=\operatorname{vol}_d(A\cap C_z)/w_z\). For each \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), define \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\) for \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\). For \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), let \(\nu_v\) be the law of \((X^++X^-)/2\) for independent \(X^+\sim\lambda_{j+v}\) and \(X^-\sim\lambda_{j-v}\), including independent copies when \(v=0\). Choose one representative from each unordered pair \(\{v,-v\}\) in \(\mathcal U_j\setminus\{0\}\), forming \(\mathcal R_j\), and put

\[
\kappa_j:=w_j\lambda_j+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_j}\alpha(v)\nu_v,
\qquad
S_j:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\alpha(v),
\qquad
\Lambda_j:=\kappa_j(\mathbb R^d)=\frac{S_j+w_j}{2}.
\]

Thus \(S_j\) counts both orientations of every nonzero reflected pair; it is a scalar, not the mixed set \(S\). Define \(E_j:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}(\alpha(v)/S_j)\log(w_j/\alpha(v))\) and \(r_j:=\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\mathcal U_j\). The rank concerns base displacements, not midpoint centroids. For a finite Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), write \(\operatorname{TD}_\mu(x):=\inf_{G\ni x}\mu(G)\), with \(G\) ranging over proper closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^d\), and \(\operatorname{HD}(\mu):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d}\operatorname{TD}_\mu(x)\).

These objects have exactly the meanings and normalizations in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`. In accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, the lattice center denoted by \(m\) is our \(j\), not our ambient dimension \(m=n+d\). Its \(\lambda_z\), \(\mathcal R_m\), \(\alpha_v\), and \(\kappa_m\) are respectively our \(\lambda_z\), \(\mathcal R_j\), \(\alpha(v)\), and \(\kappa_j\); its pushforward under \(M(u,u')=(u+u')/2\) is \(\nu_v\). Its scalar \(P_m\) in equation (12) is \(\Lambda_j\), and its \(D_\mu(x)\) is our \(\operatorname{TD}_\mu(x)\).

Use natural logarithms and set \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\), \(\kappa_{\mathrm{ent}}:=5\log5/3\), and \(c_\star:=\ell/(32\kappa_{\mathrm{ent}})=3\ell/(160\log5)\), as in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`.

Suppose that, for some \(a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(R>1\), one has \(w_{a+v}=w_{a-v}\) for every real \(v\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(a+R[-1,1]^n\subseteq K\). Then every coordinatewise nearest integer point \(j\) to \(a\) has \(w_j>0\) and

\[
S_j\ge\left(\frac{2R-2}{2R+1}\right)^m W.
\]

A maximizer \(x\in C_j\) of \(\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}\) exists. Choosing a single such maximizer gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((j,x))
&\ge\max\left\{
\frac{\ell S_j}{16(2E_j+\log2)},
\frac{S_j+w_j}{2(d+1)}
\right\}\\
&\ge\frac{c_\star S_j}{\min\{r_j,d\}+1}\\
&\ge\frac{c_\star}{\min\{n,d\}+1}
\left(\frac{2R-2}{2R+1}\right)^m W.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, if \(R\ge(5/2)m/n\), that same point satisfies \(h_S((j,x))\ge c_0 2^{-n}W\), where \(c_0:=3c_\star/100=9\ell/(16000\log5)\in(0,1/e)\) is absolute.

There is also a radius-free corollary with different hypotheses: assume only that some \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies \(w_{j+v}=w_{j-v}\) for every \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\). Then \(w_j>0\), \(S_j=W\), and a single maximizer \(x\in C_j\) of the same Tukey depth satisfies

\[
h_S((j,x))
\ge\frac{c_\star W}{\min\{r_j,d\}+1}
\ge\frac{c_\star W}{n+1}
\ge c_\star 2^{-n}W.
\]

This corollary requires neither a projected box nor symmetry at noninteger base points.

## Proof

All lattice sums are finite, because the compact projection \(K\) meets the lattice in finitely many points. No probability law is assigned to a zero-volume fiber.

We first establish the depth bound for any \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), without symmetry or a projected-box assumption. Accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`, with all objects identified above, gives \(0\le E_j\le\kappa_{\mathrm{ent}}r_j\), \(0\le r_j\le n\), and a point whose \(\kappa_j\)-Tukey depth is at least \(\ell S_j/[16(2E_j+\log2)]\). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j)
\ge\frac{\ell S_j}{16(2E_j+\log2)}
\ge\frac{\ell S_j}{32\kappa_{\mathrm{ent}}(r_j+1)}
=\frac{c_\star S_j}{r_j+1}.
\]

The second inequality uses \(\log2\le2\kappa_{\mathrm{ent}}\). In particular, it retains \(S_j\) in the numerator of the predecessor's actual-entropy bound.

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite nonzero measure \(\kappa_j\) on \(\mathbb R^d\). In its mixed-integer specialization, its integer dimension \(n\) is zero, its continuous dimension \(d\) is our \(d\), and its ambient dimension \(m\) in the general statement is our \(d\), not our \(n+d\). Its support constraint set \(S\) and its lattice set \(\Lambda\) are both \(\mathbb Z^0\times\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R^d\), not our mixed set \(S\). Its measures \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) are \(\kappa_j\), and its total mass \(M\) is \(\Lambda_j\). Thus its Helly number is \(d+1\), and the conclusion is

\[
\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j)\ge\frac{\Lambda_j}{d+1}
=\frac{S_j+w_j}{2(d+1)}.
\]

Assertion 2 of accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, at its \(m=j\), shows that \(\kappa_j\) is supported in \(C_j\) and that its depth supremum is attained at some \(x\in C_j\). Fix one such \(x\). Its Tukey depth equals \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j)\), so both preceding bounds hold at this one point.

The same accepted fact supplies the pointwise lift in its proof, equations (7) through (11). Besides the aliases in the statement, map its \(y\) to our fixed \(x\), and its halfspace coefficients \(a,b\) to \(a_H,b_H\), leaving its symmetry-unrelated coefficient \(a\) distinct from our real symmetry center. For a proper ambient halfspace through \((j,x)\), write

\[
H=\{(z,u):a_H^\top(z-j)+b_H^\top(u-x)\ge0\},
\qquad (a_H,b_H)\ne(0,0).
\]

Set \(G:=\{u:b_H^\top(u-x)\ge0\}\) if \(b_H\ne0\), and \(G:=\mathbb R^d\) if \(b_H=0\). The predecessor's mixed measure \(\mu_C(H)\) is exactly \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), and its lift gives \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\kappa_j(G)\ge\operatorname{TD}_{\kappa_j}(x)\). This includes vertical halfspaces: when \(b_H=0\), the entire central fiber is on the ambient boundary and is counted, and the reflected-pair comparison still applies to closed halfspaces. A proper closed halfspace containing \((j,x)\) contains a parallel closed halfspace through that point, so the same bound holds for all containing halfspaces. The whole-space case follows from \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j)\le\Lambda_j\le S_j\le W\); the last inequality follows by summing \(\alpha(v)\le w_{j+v}\). Consequently \(h_S((j,x))\ge\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_j)\), proving the displayed maximum bound at the fixed point.

The elementary inequalities \(2<e<3\) give \(0<\ell<\log2<1<\log5\), so \(0<c_\star<3/160<1/2\). Hence the centerpoint bound is at least \(c_\star S_j/(d+1)\), while the entropy bound is at least \(c_\star S_j/(r_j+1)\). Taking their maximum proves

\[
h_S((j,x))\ge\frac{c_\star S_j}{\min\{r_j,d\}+1}
\ge\frac{c_\star S_j}{\min\{n,d\}+1}.
\]

This derivation also covers \(r_j=0\); the accepted entropy statement then gives \(E_j=0\).

We now prove the scalar mass comparison under the real symmetry and box hypotheses. On \(K\), put \(g(z):=w_z^{1/d}\). The regularity argument in accepted fact `4cfd86cd551df9e7`, before its use of a box hypothesis, proves that \(g\) is bounded, upper semicontinuous, and concave. Here its body \(D\) is \(C\), its projection \(K\) is ours, its \(f(z)\) is \(w_z\), its \(g(z)\) is ours, and its dimensions \(n,d\) are unchanged.

Every point of \(\operatorname{int}K\) has positive fiber volume. Indeed, choose \(z_0\in K\) with \(g(z_0)>0\), which exists because \(W>0\). For \(z\in\operatorname{int}K\), choose sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) so that \(q:=(z-\varepsilon z_0)/(1-\varepsilon)\in K\). Concavity gives \(g(z)\ge\varepsilon g(z_0)+(1-\varepsilon)g(q)>0\). In particular, any coordinatewise nearest integer \(j\) to \(a\) satisfies \(\|j-a\|_\infty\le1/2<R\), lies in \(\operatorname{int}K\), and has \(w_j>0\).

Let \(v_d:=\operatorname{vol}_d\{u\in\mathbb R^d:\|u\|_2\le1\}\), the same unit-ball constant as in accepted fact `4cfd86cd551df9e7`, and form

\[
E:=\{(v,u)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
a+v\in K,\ \|u\|_2\le g(a+v)\}.
\]

This is its fiberwise centered-ball set for \(D=C\), translated by \(-(a,0)\). In particular, the base is restricted to \(K-a\); no radius-zero fibers outside that projection are included. Upper semicontinuity and boundedness of \(g\) give compactness, while concavity and the triangle inequality give convexity. The volume identity established in that accepted fact, equations (1) and (5), gives \(\operatorname{vol}_m(E)=v_d\operatorname{vol}_m(C)>0\). Thus \(E\) is full-dimensional, and its projection is \(K-a\supseteq R[-1,1]^n\).

Put \(t:=j-a\), \(\delta:=1/(2R)\), and \(F:=(E-(t,0))\cap(E+(t,0))\). Since \(\|t/\delta\|_\infty\le R\), the points \((\pm t/\delta,0)\) belong to \(E\). For every \(y\in E\), convexity gives \((1-\delta)y\pm(t,0)=(1-\delta)y+\delta(\pm t/\delta,0)\in E\). Therefore

\[
(1-\delta)E\subseteq F,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_m(F)\ge(1-\delta)^m v_d\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\]

In particular, \(F\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its projection contains \((1-\delta)R[-1,1]^n=(R-1/2)[-1,1]^n\).

For a real base \(v\), write \(F_v:=\{u:(v,u)\in F\}\). If both \(a+v+t\) and \(a+v-t\) belong to \(K\), this fiber is the intersection of concentric balls of radii \(g(a+v+t)\) and \(g(a+v-t)\). If either base is outside \(K\), the fiber is empty, and the corresponding fiber volume of \(C\) is zero. Thus, including empty and radius-zero cases because \(d>0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_d(F_v)
&=v_d\min\{w_{a+v+t},w_{a+v-t}\}\\
&=v_d\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equality uses real symmetry to replace \(w_{a+v-t}\) by \(w_{a-v+t}=w_{j-v}\). Hence \(\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(F_v)=v_dS_j\).

Apply the lower bound of accepted fact `4cfd86cd551df9e7` with its \(D=F\), \(k=R-1/2>1/2\), \(z_D=0\), unchanged dimensions \(n,d\), projection equal to \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}F\), and \(S_D=F\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), whose mixed mass is \(v_dS_j\). Apply its upper bound with \(D=C\), \(k=R\), \(z_D=a\), projection \(K\), and \(S_D=S\), whose mixed mass is \(W\). These applications give

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_dS_j
&\ge\left(1-\frac1{2R-1}\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(F)\\
&\ge\left(1-\frac1{2R-1}\right)^m
\left(1-\frac1{2R}\right)^m v_d\operatorname{vol}_m(C)\\
&=\left(\frac{2R-2}{2R}\right)^m v_d\operatorname{vol}_m(C),\\
W&\le\left(\frac{2R+1}{2R}\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\end{aligned}
\]

The upper bound on \(W\) is a lower bound on \(\operatorname{vol}_m(C)\) in terms of \(W\). Substitution into the preceding lower bound on \(S_j\), and cancellation of \(v_d>0\), prove \(S_j\ge((2R-2)/(2R+1))^mW\). Combining this with the already proved depth estimate gives the complete chain in the statement. The auxiliary bodies \(E,F\) were used only for this scalar comparison; the depth estimate uses the original fibers of \(C\).

For the threshold, the function \((2R-2)/(2R+1)\) increases for \(R>1\). Put \(\theta:=n/m\in(0,1)\). At \(R=(5/2)m/n\), the ratio equals \((5-2\theta)/(5+\theta)\). Define \(\Phi(\theta):=\log(5-2\theta)-\log(5+\theta)+\theta\log2\) on \([0,1]\). Then \(\Phi(0)=\Phi(1)=0\), and

\[
\Phi''(\theta)
=-\frac4{(5-2\theta)^2}+\frac1{(5+\theta)^2}
\le-\frac4{25}+\frac1{25}
=-\frac3{25}.
\]

It follows that \(\Phi(\theta)-(3/50)\theta(1-\theta)\) is concave with zero endpoint values, so \(\Phi(\theta)\ge(3/50)\theta(1-\theta)\). Put \(s:=\min\{n,d\}\). Since \(nd/(n+d)\ge s/2\), for every \(R\ge(5/2)m/n\) we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{2R-2}{2R+1}\right)^m
&\ge 2^{-n}\exp\bigl(m\Phi(\theta)\bigr)\\
&\ge 2^{-n}\exp\left(\frac{3nd}{50m}\right)
\ge 2^{-n}\exp\left(\frac{3s}{100}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(s\ge0\), \(\exp(3s/100)\ge1+3s/100\ge3(s+1)/100\). The previously proved bound with denominator \(s+1\) therefore gives \(h_S((j,x))\ge(3c_\star/100)2^{-n}W=c_0 2^{-n}W\). Finally, \(0<c_0<c_\star<3/160<1/3<1/e\), using \(e<3\), verifies the required constant range without changing the coefficient.

For the radius-free corollary, choose \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_z>0\), using \(W>0\). The integer symmetry hypothesis gives \(w_{2j-z}=w_z>0\). Their midpoint \(j\) lies in \(K\). The unconditional concavity assertion from the proof of accepted fact `4cfd86cd551df9e7`, with \(D=C\), \(f=w\), and \(g=w^{1/d}\) exactly as above, gives \(w_j^{1/d}\ge(w_z^{1/d}+w_{2j-z}^{1/d})/2=w_z^{1/d}>0\). Thus all the definitions at \(j\) apply. For each lattice \(v\), \(\alpha(v)=w_{j+v}\), so reindexing gives \(S_j=W\). The depth argument at the start of the proof did not use real symmetry or a box, and now yields the stated bound \(c_\star W/(\min\{r_j,d\}+1)\). Since \(r_j\le n\) and \(n+1\le2^n\) for positive integers \(n\), the two remaining inequalities follow. The latter elementary inequality follows directly from the first two terms in the binomial expansion of \((1+1)^n\).
