## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), let
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad
\mathbb B_\infty^n:=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n:\|\mathbf u\|_\infty\leq1\},
\qquad
\gamma_m:=\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m,
\]
and, for every Borel set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
\mu(E):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d\!\left(E\cap(\{\mathbf z\}\times\mathbb R^d)\right).
\]
Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, let \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\), and suppose that for some \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(k>3/2\),
\[
\mathbf z_0+k\mathbb B_\infty^n
\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C).
\]
Then there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq L_m(k)\,\mathcal H_d(S),
\]
where
\[
L_m(k):=
\min\left\{
\gamma_m\left(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\right)^m,\,
\left(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\right)^m-(1-\gamma_m)
\right\}.
\]
In particular, if \(0<\tau<\gamma_m\), the stronger conclusion
\(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq\tau\mathcal H_d(S)\) follows whenever
\[
k\geq
\max\left\{
\frac{3+(\tau/\gamma_m)^{1/m}}
     {2\left(1-(\tau/\gamma_m)^{1/m}\right)},\,
\frac{2+(1-\gamma_m+\tau)^{1/m}}
     {2\left(1-(1-\gamma_m+\tau)^{1/m}\right)}
\right\}.
\]

## Proof

Apply accepted predecessor \(48f7ad59d388c8c8\) to \(C\). With
\(\epsilon=1/(2k)\), it gives \(\mathbf w\in C\) and the convex body
\[
C':=(1-\epsilon)C+\epsilon\mathbf w\subseteq C
\]
such that
\[
\mathbf y^\star:=\mathfrak c(C')\in C'\cap\Lambda\subseteq S,\qquad
\mathcal H_m(C')=\left(1-\frac1{2k}\right)^m\mathcal H_m(C),
\]
and \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C')\) contains a translate of
\((k-\tfrac12)\mathbb B_\infty^n\).

By accepted predecessor \(978e8555ec5bac42\), it is enough to prove the claimed bound for closed halfspaces \(H\) whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y^\star\): for an arbitrary containing halfspace, translating its boundary inward to \(\mathbf y^\star\) produces a contained halfspace and mixed-volume monotonicity gives the result. Fix such an \(H\), let \(\bar H\) be the complementary closed halfspace, and put
\[
A:=C'\cap H,\qquad B:=C'\cap\bar H.
\]
Sharp Grünbaum gives
\[
\mathcal H_m(A)\geq\gamma_m\mathcal H_m(C'),
\qquad
\mathcal H_m(B)\geq\gamma_m\mathcal H_m(C').
\]
Both volumes are positive; since \(A\) and \(B\) are compact and convex, they are convex bodies.
The common boundary has zero \(m\)-dimensional volume, so
\[
\mathcal H_m(B)
=\mathcal H_m(C')-\mathcal H_m(A)
\leq(1-\gamma_m)\mathcal H_m(C'). \tag{1}
\]
Accepted predecessor \(b80d9c776d7d7d77\) shows that either \(A\) or \(B\) contains a translate of \(\tfrac12C'\). Thus the projection of that set contains a translate of
\[
\frac{k-\frac12}{2}\mathbb B_\infty^n.
\]
This radius exceeds \(1/2\) exactly because \(k>3/2\), so accepted predecessor \(4cfd86cd551df9e7\) applies to the relevant cut piece. It also applies to \(C\), with radius \(k\), and to \(C'\), with radius \(k-\tfrac12\).

First apply its upper comparison bound to \(C\), and then use the exact homothetic volume of \(C'\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_m(C')
&=\left(\frac{2k-1}{2k}\right)^m\mathcal H_m(C)\\
&\geq
\left(\frac{2k-1}{2k}\right)^m
\left(\frac{2k}{2k+1}\right)^m\mu(C)
=\left(\frac{2k-1}{2k+1}\right)^m\mathcal H_d(S).
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that \(A\) contains the half-scale copy. The lower comparison bound, sharp Grünbaum, and (2) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&\geq\mu(A)\\
&\geq
\left(\frac{2k-3}{2k-1}\right)^m\mathcal H_m(A)\\
&\geq
\gamma_m\left(\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\right)^m\mathcal H_d(S). \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose instead that \(B\) contains the half-scale copy. Let
\[
H_-:=\mathbb R^{n+d}\setminus H,
\qquad S':=C'\cap\Lambda.
\]
The sets \(S'\cap H\) and \(S'\cap H_-\) are disjoint and cover \(S'\), while
\(S'\cap H_-\subseteq B\cap\Lambda\). Hence
\[
\mu(S'\cap H)\geq\mu(S')-\mu(B). \tag{4}
\]
The lower comparison bound for \(C'\), the upper comparison bound for \(B\), and (1) yield
\[
\mu(S'\cap H)\geq q_m(k)\mathcal H_m(C'), \tag{5}
\]
where
\[
q_m(k):=
\left(\frac{2k-2}{2k-1}\right)^m
-(1-\gamma_m)\left(\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}\right)^m.
\]
If \(q_m(k)>0\), multiplying (2) by this positive coefficient gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&\geq\mu(S'\cap H)\\
&\geq
\left[
\left(\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\right)^m-(1-\gamma_m)
\right]\mathcal H_d(S). \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(q_m(k)\leq0\), then the coefficient in square brackets in (6) equals
\[
q_m(k)\left(\frac{2k-1}{2k+1}\right)^m\leq0.
\]
Therefore (6) follows instead from
\(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq0\). No lower conversion bound is multiplied by a nonpositive number.

Combining (3) and (6) proves the bound with \(L_m(k)\) for every boundary-through-\(\mathbf y^\star\) halfspace, and predecessor \(978e8555ec5bac42\) extends it to every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\).

For the stated target-depth threshold, the two component inequalities needed for \(L_m(k)\geq\tau\) are respectively equivalent to
\[
\frac{2k-3}{2k+1}\geq(\tau/\gamma_m)^{1/m},
\qquad
\frac{2k-2}{2k+1}\geq(1-\gamma_m+\tau)^{1/m}.
\]
Because \(0<\tau<\gamma_m\), both right-hand sides lie in \((0,1)\); solving the two linear inequalities in \(k\) gives exactly the displayed threshold. Its first term is strictly greater than \(3/2\).

Finally, \(\gamma_m\geq1/e\) because \((1+1/m)^m\leq e\). Since \(k>3/2\), both bases below are nonnegative. For the first term, replace \(\gamma_m\) by \(1/e\) before applying Bernoulli's inequality; applying Bernoulli directly to the second base gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_m\left(1-\frac4{2k+1}\right)^m
&\geq
\frac1e-\frac{4m}{e(2k+1)}
\geq\frac1e-\frac{3m}{2k},\\
\left(1-\frac3{2k+1}\right)^m-(1-\gamma_m)
&\geq
\gamma_m-\frac{3m}{2k+1}
\geq\frac1e-\frac{3m}{2k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the claimed relation to the coarse theorem.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, DOI 10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF: https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. On p. 1257 the paper defines \(\mathcal C(K,\lambda)\) as the set of points \(P\) for which every closed halfspace containing \(P\) captures at least \(\lambda V(K)\). Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, proves for the centroid \(G_K\) of every convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) that
\[
G_K\in\mathcal C\!\left(K,\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m\right).
\]
Equivalently, every closed halfspace containing \(G_K\) has intersection with \(K\) of \(m\)-volume at least \((m/(m+1))^mV(K)\). Remark 4(i), p. 1260, records that this bound is best possible. Here \(K=C'\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^m\) by predecessor \(48f7ad59d388c8c8\), \(G_K=\mathbf y^\star\), and both \(H\) and \(\bar H\) are closed halfspaces containing that centroid. Thus the dimension, convex-body, centroid, measure, and halfspace hypotheses match exactly.
