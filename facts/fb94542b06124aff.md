## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be convex and $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ nonempty and compact. Let $\mu$ be any probability on $S$ and $Q$ any positive definite ambient quadratic form. Write $\nu\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}\mu$ when $\int f\,d\nu\le\int f\,d\mu$ for every finite convex $f$ on the ambient space. The minimum of $\int Q\,d\nu$ over such probabilities on $S$ is attained. Each minimizer has at most one physical point per base parity. The base set $P_*$ of its positive atoms is precisely both the vertex set and the integer point set of $\operatorname{conv}P_*$. An atom of mass at least $2^{-n}$ is the barycenter of a submeasure of $\mu$ of that same mass.

## Proof

The feasible set is nonempty because it contains $\mu$. It is weakly compact because the space of probabilities on $S$ is compact and each finite convex test is continuous on the ambient Euclidean space, so each inequality defines a closed set. The continuous objective attains a minimum.

If distinct support points have same-parity bases, choose separated neighborhoods in $S$ with those respective bases fixed. Their restrictions under an optimal law have positive mass. Scale the two restrictions to equal mass $m>0$ and couple them by their product divided by $m$. Replace the two restrictions by twice the midpoint law of this coupling. Midpoints lie in $S$ by convexity and parity, even when the two bases coincide. Jensen shows that the new law lies below the old law in convex order. The objective drops by the integral of $Q(x-y)/2$ under the coupling, strictly positive because the two neighborhoods are separated and $Q$ is positive definite. This contradiction gives at most $2^n$ support points, each a positive atom.

If distinct positive-atom bases admit a convex combination with positive coefficients $\lambda_i$, at least two terms, and integral mean $p$, take the same combination $y=\sum_i\lambda_i y_i$ of the physical atoms. Then $y\in S$. For sufficiently small $s>0$ remove masses $s\lambda_i$ at $y_i$ and put their sum at $y$. The replacement again decreases every convex test and strictly decreases the quadratic test. Such an integer combination cannot occur. Every nonvertex in $P_*$ or extra integer point of its hull would have such a representation. The count gives an atom of mass $m_*\ge 2^{-n}$. Apply ac53fe19fef9ec25 to the optimal law and $\mu$, which are compactly supported equal-mass laws in full physical convex order. Restrict the coupling to this source atom. Its second marginal is dominated by $\mu$, has mass $m_*$, and has barycenter at the atom.
