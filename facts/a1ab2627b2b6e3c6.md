## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex with positive mixed volume $M$ on $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$, measured by summed $d$-volume. Let the interiors $U_i$ of $k$ closed halfspaces with nonzero continuous normals cover $S$, and let their closed cap masses be $\tau M$. Write $E_i=S\cap U_i\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}U_j$ and $E=\sum_i\mathcal H_d(E_i)$. Then $E\ge 2M-k\tau M$; if $d=2$, $k\le 3m$, $m=2^n$, and $\tau<4/(9m)$, then $E>2M/3$.

Choose compact convex positive-volume pieces $A_i\subset E_i$ over pairwise distinct integer bases $z_i$ for some cap indices, and put $V_i=\mathcal H_d(A_i)$. The selected pieces and all midpoint sets $B_{ij}=(A_i+A_j)/2$ for $i<j$, $z_i\equiv z_j\pmod 2$, are pairwise disjoint in $S$, with $B_{ij}$ covered only by caps from $\{i,j\}$ in the open cover. In particular
$$
\sum_i V_i+\sum_{\substack{i<j\\z_i\equiv z_j\;(\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}}
\left(\frac{V_i^{1/d}+V_j^{1/d}}2\right)^d\le M,\qquad
\sum_i V_i+\sum_{\substack{i<j\\z_i\equiv z_j\;(\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}}
\left(\frac{V_i^{1/d}+V_j^{1/d}}2\right)^d\le k\tau M,
$$
where the sums use the selected indices.

If each positive-volume $E_i$ has positive volume in only one fiber and those bases are distinct, then $E\le m\tau M$. Thus if $k\le m(d+1)$, then $\tau\ge 2/[m(d+2)]$. A strict planar cover with $\tau<4/(9m)$ and $k\le3m$ therefore has two positive-volume exclusive colors sharing a fiber or one exclusive color of positive volume in multiple fibers.

## Proof

The boundaries are volume-null on every fiber because the continuous normals are nonzero. The integral of open coverage multiplicity is therefore $k\tau M$, at least $E+2(M-E)$. If $d=2$ and $k\tau<4/3$, this gives $E>2M/3$.

A same-parity midpoint lies in $S$ by convexity and integrality of its base. The endpoints belong to the closed complement of $U_\ell$ for any $\ell\notin\{i,j\}$, so the midpoint also misses $U_\ell$. Two midpoint sets indexed by disjoint pairs cannot intersect, since such an intersection would miss every open cap. Two distinct pairs sharing an endpoint index cannot land on the same base: equality of midpoint coordinates would imply equality of the other endpoint bases. A midpoint set misses each $A_\ell$ of a third color by open coverage and misses $A_i,A_j$ by its different base. The $A_i$ are mutually disjoint. Ordinary Brunn–Minkowski for compact positive-volume subsets of the continuous $\mathbb R^d$ gives $\mathcal H_d(B_{ij})\ge ((V_i^{1/d}+V_j^{1/d})/2)^d$. Sum over the disjoint sets to bound the displayed left side by $M$; open coverage also gives $M\le k\tau M$.

For the one-fiber condition, denote by $e_i$ the full exclusive volume of each positive color. In each occupied parity choose an $i$ maximizing $e_i$ there. Approximate each selected exclusive fiber from inside by compact convex pieces whose volumes tend to its $e_j$: intersect the fiber and all closed complements with the defining open inequality of $U_j$ tightened by a positive amount tending to zero. For every other $j$ of the same parity, $B_{ij}$ has a base different from both endpoint bases and is covered only by $i,j$. Its points not in $U_i$ lie in $E_j$ and its points not in $U_j$ lie in $E_i$. Both sets are null on that midpoint fiber under the one-fiber assumption. Thus $B_{ij}$ is charged to $U_i$ almost everywhere. The pieces $B_{ij}$ for this fixed $i$ and its own $A_i$ are disjoint. In the limit the cap mass is at least $e_i+\sum_{j\ne i}((e_i^{1/d}+e_j^{1/d})/2)^d\ge\sum_j e_j$ in that parity. At most $m$ parities contribute. Now $2M\le k\tau M+E\le m(d+2)\tau M$. In dimension two the lower bound is $1/(2m)>4/(9m)$. In dimension three the corresponding coefficient $2/5$ is below $(3/4)^3$, so this last comparison does not give a general-dimensional contradiction.
