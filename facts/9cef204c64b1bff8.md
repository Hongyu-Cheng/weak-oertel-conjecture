## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and $m\ge0$ be integers. Put $T_d=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, $V_d=1/d!$ and $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Take finite radii $R=r_0\ge r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_m\ge0$ with $R>0$ and with the drops $r_{h-1}-r_h$ nondecreasing in $h$. At each of the integer bases $-h$ and $h+1$, for $0\le h\le m$, take the fiber $r_hT_d$. Let $\mu$ denote the sum of the $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measures of these fibers.

Fix $u\in[0,\infty)^d$ with $\sum_i u_i=1$ and fix $\beta\ge0$. With $t=R/(d+1)$, the closed halfspace $H=\{(z,x):-\beta z+u\cdot x\ge t\}$ passes through $y=(0,t,\ldots,t)$, the actual centroid at base $0$. For $r\ge0$, $s\in\mathbb R$ and $h\ge0$, put
$$
F_r(s)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(rT_d\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge s\})}{V_d},
\qquad
G_h(r)=F_r(t-\beta h)+F_r(t+\beta(h+1))-\Gamma_d r^d .
$$
For $m\ge1$ there is a slope $a\in[0,R/m]$ such that $\ell_h=R-ah$ satisfies
$$
G_h(\ell_h)\le G_h(r_h)\qquad(0\le h\le m).
$$
The slope can be chosen with $\ell_h$ between $r_h$ and the unique minimizer of $G_h$ on $[0,\infty)$ for each $1\le h\le m$. For every such choice, equality in the comparison at an index holds exactly when $\ell_h=r_h$. Equality in the sum for such a choice is attainable exactly when the original radii are already affine in $h$. For $m=0$ leave the radius unchanged.

The replacement is realized by a compact convex body with the indicated fibers at the same integer bases and no other integer fibers. It retains $y$ and the very same $H$. The original signed cap surplus relative to the fraction $\Gamma_d/2$ is $V_d\sum_{h=0}^m G_h(r_h)$, with the identical formula using $\ell_h$ for the replacement. Hence an original cap below that fraction yields a replacement cap below the same fraction, measured against the replacement's own total mass.

## Proof

At $z=-h$ the inequality in $H$ is $u\cdot x\ge t-\beta h$, and at $z=h+1$ it is $u\cdot x\ge t+\beta(h+1)$. Thus the mass of $H$ is $V_d\sum_h[F_{r_h}(t-\beta h)+F_{r_h}(t+\beta(h+1))]$ and the total mass is $2V_d\sum_h r_h^d$. Both formulas apply to any replacement with the same bases. The nonzero normal $u$ gives a boundary of zero $d$-volume in every fiber. A radius-zero fiber also has zero $d$-volume, whether or not its point is in $H$.

**Radial minimizers.** Let $\Theta$ be uniform on the facet $\Delta=\{\theta\ge0:\sum_i\theta_i=1\}$ of $T_d$, let $U=u\cdot\Theta$ and write $T(v)=\mathbb P(U\ge v)$. For $d=1$ take $\Theta=1$. Since $\{x\in rT_d:\sum_i x_i\le w\}=wT_d$ for $0\le w\le r$, the radial variable for uniform volume has density $d w^{d-1}/r^d$ when $r>0$. At each positive radial level $w$ the uniform slice is the dilation of the same facet, so its direction has law $\Theta$. Consequently for $s>0$,
$$
F_r(s)=d\int_0^r w^{d-1}T(s/w)\,dw.
$$
For $s\le0$, $F_r(s)=r^d$ by $u\ge0$. These formulas give $F_0(s)=0$ for all $s$.

Temporarily let $h\ge0$ be real, and put $q=t-\beta h$, $p=t+\beta(h+1)$, so $p\ge q$ and $p>0$. If $q\le0$, then $G_h(r)=(1-\Gamma_d)r^d+F_r(p)$ is strictly increasing since $F_r(p)$ is nondecreasing. Its unique minimizer is $\rho_h=0$. If $q>0$ and $u$ is constant, then $U=1/d$ and for $s>0$ we have $F_r(s)=(r^d-(ds)^d)_+$, where $v_+=\max(v,0)$. Before $r=dq$ both cap terms vanish. From $r=dq$ onward the first cap and the mass term together increase strictly and the second cap is nondecreasing. Thus $G_h$ strictly decreases up to $\rho_h=dq$ and strictly increases thereafter. This includes $d=1$.

For $q>0$ and $u$ nonconstant, $d\ge2$. The law of $U$ is atomless because its level sets are proper hyperplane sections of $\Delta$. Its support is $[\min_i u_i,\max_i u_i]$, and its tail is continuous and strictly decreasing between the endpoints since every nonempty open subinterval of the projection has positive facet volume in its inverse image. Differentiating the radial integral for $r>0$ gives
$$
G_h'(r)=d r^{d-1}[T(q/r)+T(p/r)-\Gamma_d].
$$
The bracket is continuous and nondecreasing, starts at $-\Gamma_d$ for sufficiently small $r$, and tends to $2-\Gamma_d$ as $r\to\infty$ because $\mathbb P(U=0)=0$. At any zero, $T(q/r)\in[\Gamma_d/2,\Gamma_d]$ since $q\le p$. This tail is strictly between $0$ and $1$, so every strict increase of the radius from that zero increases the first tail, and every strict decrease of the radius decreases it. The zero $\rho_h$ is therefore unique, and the derivative has the strict signs on either side that give strict decrease up to $\rho_h$ and strict increase thereafter.

The facet $\Delta$ is a full-dimensional convex body in its $(d-1)$-dimensional affine hull. Its centroid is $(1/d,\ldots,1/d)$, where $u\cdot\Theta$ takes the value $1/d$. For nonconstant $u$, the closed facet halfspace with that threshold is proper. Apply the full-mass case of accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d in orthonormal coordinates on the affine hull. At full mass its metronoid consists of the centroid, so it gives $T(1/d)\ge\Gamma_{d-1}>\Gamma_d$. For the strict inequality, the derivative of $x\log(x/(x+1))$ is $\log(1-1/(x+1))+1/(x+1)<0$ for $x>0$. At the root $T(q/\rho_h)\le\Gamma_d$, so $q/\rho_h>1/d$ in this nonconstant case. Together with the explicit constant case and $q\le0$, this gives $0\le\rho_h\le d(t-\beta h)_+<R$. Whenever $q>0$ we have $\rho_h>0$ and $q/\rho_h\ge1/d>t/R$.

**Ordering of the minimizing secants.** For $h>0$ put $b(h)=(R-\rho_h)/h>0$. Take $0<h_1<h_2$. If $\rho_{h_1}=0$, then $t-\beta h_1\le0$, so $\rho_{h_2}=0$ and $b(h_2)=R/h_2<R/h_1=b(h_1)$. Otherwise let $a=b(h_1)$ and $\ell(x)=R-ax$. At $h_1$, $\ell(h_1)=\rho_{h_1}$ and $(t-\beta h_1)/\ell(h_1)>t/R$ gives $at>\beta R$. If $\ell(h_2)<0$, then $\ell(h_2)<\rho_{h_2}$. If $\ell(h_2)=0$, then $t-\beta h_2=t-\beta R/a>0$, again giving $\ell(h_2)<\rho_{h_2}$.

When $\ell(h_2)>0$, both normalized thresholds increase strictly from $h_1$ to $h_2$. Their derivatives, with the common positive denominator $\ell(x)^2$, have numerators $at-\beta R$ and $\beta R+a(t+\beta)$, respectively. The smaller normalized threshold remains positive. In the nonconstant case the first tail drops strictly from its value at the root for $h_1$, and the second tail does not increase, so the derivative bracket for $h_2$ is strictly negative at $\ell(h_2)$. Hence $\ell(h_2)<\rho_{h_2}$. In the constant case the smaller normalized threshold grows from $1/d$, so $\ell(h_2)<d(t-\beta h_2)=\rho_{h_2}$ as well. Thus $b(h_2)<b(h_1)$ in all cases.

**Affine replacement.** For integers $1\le h\le m$ let $a_h=(R-r_h)/h$ and $b_h=b(h)$. The $a_h$ are nonnegative and nondecreasing because they average the first $h$ successive drops. The $b_h$ are positive and strictly decreasing. The indices with $a_h\le b_h$ form an initial segment $1,\ldots,k$. If $k=m$, choose $a=a_m$, giving $a_h\le a\le b_h$ for all $h$. If $k=0$, choose $a=b_1$, giving $b_h\le a\le a_h$ for all $h$. If $0<k<m$, choose
$$
\max(a_k,b_{k+1})\le a\le\min(b_k,a_{k+1}).
$$
The interval is nonempty because $a_k\le b_k$, $b_{k+1}<a_{k+1}$, $a_k\le a_{k+1}$ and $b_{k+1}<b_k$. The resulting $a$ lies between $a_h$ and $b_h$ on both sides of $k$. Hence in every case $\ell_h$ is between $r_h$ and $\rho_h$. Each $\ell_h$ is nonnegative, so $0\le a\le R/m$. Strict monotonicity of $G_h$ on the segment between $r_h$ and $\rho_h$ gives the comparison and gives equality exactly at the unchanged radii. The index zero is unchanged. Equality in the sum forces equality at every index, and if the original radii are affine their own slope gives the unchanged sequence and satisfies the betweenness condition.

Finally interpolate the paired radii linearly on $[-m,m+1]$. For the original radii the interpolation is nonnegative and concave because its slopes are the successive drops in reverse order, then zero, then their negatives in forward order. The same applies to the replacement, whose successive drops are constant. With $L(z)$ the corresponding interpolation, the set of $(z,x)$ with $-m\le z\le m+1$, $x\ge0$ and $\sum_i x_i\le L(z)$ is compact and convex. It has nonempty interior because $L(z)=R>0$ on $[0,1]$. Its actual integer fibers are precisely those listed. This also realizes the unchanged case $m=0$ and completes the exact mass transfer.
