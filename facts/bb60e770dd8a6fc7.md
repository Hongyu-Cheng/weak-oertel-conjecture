## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), let \(N=k+1\), and put \(Q=2^k\). Let \(D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) be finite and nonempty, with
$$
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad
\#\lbrace a:x_a=2\rbrace\le2\quad(x\in D).
\tag{1}
$$
There is no assumption that any Boolean point belongs to \(D\). Set \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\). For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), use exactly the original affine law
$$
w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad
p_{(x,0)}=p_x=w_x/Z.
\tag{2}
$$
Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_cs_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The reflection sum is ordered and includes its diagonal once. Halfspaces in (3) may be required to have a nonzero normal.

Use the actual one-step core and actual double-point bands
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace a:x_a=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace,
&D_2&=D\setminus D_1,\\
\alpha&=\sum_{x\in D_1}p_x,
&S_4&=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4,\\
t_0&=2^{-10}/Q,
&t_j&=t_02^{-j}\quad(j\ge0),\\
E_j&=\lbrace x\in D_2:t_j/2<p_x\le t_j\rbrace,
&n_j&=|E_j|,
&m_j&=\sum_{x\in E_j}p_x,\\
\mathcal J&=\lbrace j\ge0:n_j\ge2Q\rbrace,
&q_j&=|\lbrace x\bmod2:x\in E_j\rbrace|,\\
C_j&=\left\lbrace\frac{x+y}{2}:x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\right\rbrace,
&r_j&=|C_j|,
&\omega_j&=t_jr_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Empty bands have all their cardinalities and masses equal to zero. Suppose that a real constant \(C\ge1\) satisfies the per-band hypothesis
$$
r_j\le C Q\qquad(j\in\mathcal J).
\tag{5}
$$
Then, without any affine-rank assumption,
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{2^{-40}}{C^2}\,8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge\frac{2^{-40}}{C^2}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$

One sufficient field condition is obtained by setting \(\lambda_a=\beta_a/\log2\) for every coordinate and
$$
R_\beta=\sup_{u\in\mathbb R}
\left(\mathbf1_{\lbrace0\in[u,u+1)\rbrace}
+\sum_{a=1}^k\mathbf1_{\lbrace2\lambda_a\in[u,u+1)\rbrace}\right).
$$
The indexed offsets in this definition count repeated values separately. Then \(1\le R_\beta\le k+1\), the hypothesis (5) holds with \(C=R_\beta\), and consequently
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{2^{-40}}{R_\beta^2}8^{-N}.
$$
In particular, if \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k\) are distinct nonzero integers, then \(R_\beta=1\) and both moments are at least \(2^{-40}8^{-N}\) for every support (1).

Here is an unconditional source corollary of (6). Let \(m\ge1\), and let \(h_1,\ldots,h_m\) be pairwise distinct positive integers, in any order. A label is a pair \(q=(b,i)\), where \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), \(1\le i\le m\), and \(b_i=0\). Write \(\mathbf e_i\) for the \(i\)-th standard basis vector of \(\mathbb R^m\), and define the active vector and integer energy of a label by
$$
d_q=b+2\mathbf e_i,
\qquad
e_q=\sum_{a=1}^m h_ab_a+2h_i.
\tag{7}
$$
Choose any nonempty subfamily \(\mathcal Q_0\) of labels. For every \(q\in\mathcal Q_0\), choose an arbitrary integer \(n_q\ge2\) and a private group \(G_q\) of \(n_q\) neutral coordinates, with the groups pairwise disjoint. Put
$$
L=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}n_q,
\qquad k=m+L,\qquad N=k+1,\qquad Q=2^k.
$$
Let \(E_q\subseteq\mathbb Z^{m+L}\) consist of the points with active vector \(d_q\), exactly one neutral coordinate equal to two, that coordinate belonging to \(G_q\), and every other neutral coordinate arbitrary in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\). Define exactly
$$
E=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}E_q,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace,
\tag{8}
$$
with no added Boolean points. In (2), use the active coefficients \(h_a\log2\) and all neutral coefficients zero, that is,
$$
\beta=((h_a\log2)_{a=1}^m,0,\ldots,0).
\tag{9}
$$
Then \(D\) satisfies (1), every actual band (4) satisfies \(r_j\le Q\), and therefore
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N},\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N}.
\tag{10}
$$
No relation between \(n_q\) and \(e_q\), and no equality of the masses of different labels, is required.

## Proof

First prove the generic assertion. For \(x,y\in D\) with integral midpoint \(c=(x+y)/2\), completeness places \(c\) in \(D\), and the original affine law gives
$$
p_c^2=p_xp_y.
\tag{11}
$$
For any fixed center \(c\), distinct unordered reflected pairs \(\lbrace x,y\rbrace\) with \(x\ne y\) and \(x+y=2c\) have disjoint endpoints, since the partner of \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\). No such endpoint equals \(c\). Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains an endpoint of each pair. Indeed, if \(H=\lbrace u:v\cdot u\ge a\rbrace\) and both endpoints were outside, their average would satisfy \(v\cdot(c,0)<a\). Thus, for any collection of these pairs counted in both orders, with sum \(R(c)\) of their \(\min(p_x,p_y)\),
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\frac12R(c).
\tag{12}
$$
This includes halfspaces whose boundaries pass through the center. It uses no full-dimensionality. Requiring a nonzero normal does not affect the proof, and the halfspace \(\lbrace u:u_N\ge0\rbrace\) contains the whole support and has nonzero normal. In particular
$$
M_3(p)\ge S_4,\qquad T(p)\ge S_4,
\tag{13}
$$
using respectively \(\tau_p((x,0))\ge p_x\) and the diagonal term \(s_p(x)\ge p_x\).

The subset \(D_1\), if nonempty, is complete. An integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) belongs to \(D\) by (1). If it had two coordinates equal to two, coordinate extremality in the cube \([0,2]^k\) would force every positive-coefficient point in a representing convex combination from \(D_1\) to have those same two coordinates equal to two. This contradicts the definition of \(D_1\). Consequently the point belongs to \(D_1\).

Apply the final complete-one-step assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 to this actual \(D_1\) when \(\alpha>0\). The notation map is precisely its \(k,N,\beta\) equal to the present \(k,N,\beta\), its support equal to \(D_1\times\lbrace0\rbrace\), and its normalizer equal to
$$
Z_1=\sum_{x\in D_1}w_x=\alpha Z.
$$
Its original law is therefore the conditional law \(\widehat p_x=p_x/\alpha\) on \(D_1\). Its occupied parity count is at most \(Q\), so that accepted assertion yields
$$
M_3(\widehat p)\ge\frac1{512Q^3},\qquad
T(\widehat p)\ge\frac1{512Q^3}.
\tag{14}
$$
For each center in \(D_1\), every original containing halfspace has mass at least \(\alpha\) times its conditional-core mass. Taking the infimum gives \(\tau_p\ge\alpha\tau_{\widehat p}\) at those centers. Restricting the original ordered reflection sum to pairs from \(D_1\) similarly gives \(s_p\ge\alpha s_{\widehat p}\). The original center mass equals \(\alpha\widehat p_c\). Restriction of the two original cubics to centers in \(D_1\) consequently gives the exact fourth-power conditioning bound
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512Q^3}.
\tag{15}
$$
For \(\alpha=0\) this lower bound is zero and holds without conditioning. If \(\alpha\ge1/4\), (15) is at least \(2^{-17}Q^{-3}=2^{-14}8^{-N}\). If \(S_4Q^3\ge2^{-40}\), (13) is at least \(2^{-40}Q^{-3}=2^{-37}8^{-N}\). Both bounds imply (6), since \(C\ge1\).

It remains to consider
$$
\alpha<\frac14,\qquad S_4Q^3<2^{-40}.
\tag{16}
$$
Removing the double points of probability greater than \(t_0\) removes mass at most
$$
\sum_{\substack{x\in D_2\\p_x>t_0}}p_x
\le t_0^{-3}S_4<2^{-10}.
$$
Every remaining double point lies in exactly one of the half-open bands (4). The mass of bands with \(n_j<2Q\) is at most
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}2Qt_j=4Qt_0=2^{-8}.
$$
The total dense-band mass therefore satisfies
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j
>1-\frac14-2^{-10}-2^{-8}
=\frac{763}{1024}>\frac12.
\tag{17}
$$
There are only finitely many nonempty bands, because \(D\) is finite and all its probabilities are positive. In particular \(\mathcal J\) is finite and nonempty in regime (16).

Fix \(j\in\mathcal J\). Let \(n_{b,j}\) count the endpoints in \(E_j\) of an occupied parity \(b\). There are \(q_j\le Q\) such parities, with total \(n_j\ge2Q\ge2q_j\). The number of ordered distinct same-parity pairs in this band is
$$
v_j=\sum_b n_{b,j}(n_{b,j}-1)
\ge\frac{n_j^2}{q_j}-n_j
\ge\frac{n_j^2}{2q_j}>0,
\tag{18}
$$
where the first inequality is Cauchy's inequality. Thus \(r_j>0\). The actual midpoint set \(C_j\) lies in \(D_1\): if such a midpoint had two coordinates equal to two, both endpoints would have two in those positions. Every other coordinate of the endpoints would then be binary by their max-two budget, so equal parity would force equality of every remaining coordinate and hence equality of the endpoints. This contradicts their distinctness.

For \(c\in C_j\), let \(R_j(c)\) sum \(\min(p_x,p_y)\) over the ordered distinct pairs from \(E_j\) with midpoint \(c\). Identity (11) gives the exact grouping
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr L_j
&:=\sum_{c\in C_j}p_c^{1/3}R_j(c)\\
&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}
\ge(t_j/2)^{4/3}v_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
Hölder's inequality on the \(r_j\) distinct actual centers gives
$$
\mathscr L_j^3\le r_j^2\sum_{c\in C_j}p_cR_j(c)^3.
$$
Combining this with (18), (19), and \(m_j\le t_jn_j\) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c\in C_j}p_cR_j(c)^3
&\ge\frac{t_j^4v_j^3}{16r_j^2}
\ge\frac{t_j^4n_j^6}{128q_j^3r_j^2}\\
&\ge\frac{m_j^6}{128q_j^3t_j^2r_j^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Every center in \(C_j\) has \(t_j/2<p_c\le t_j\), by (11) and the same bounds on both endpoint probabilities. Consequently the sets \(C_j\) for different bands are disjoint, including at dyadic boundaries. This allows direct summation of (20) in the original moments. Indeed, \(s_p(c)\ge R_j(c)\), while (12) gives \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge R_j(c)/2\). Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)&\ge\frac1{1024}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{q_j^3t_j^2r_j^2},\\
T(p)&\ge\frac1{128}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{q_j^3t_j^2r_j^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
All pair sums here are actual ordered-pair sums, and all center sets are deduplicated actual images.

Using (5) and \(q_j\le Q\), the common smaller bound in (21) is
$$
M_3(p),T(p)
\ge\frac1{1024C^2Q^5}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{t_j^2}.
\tag{22}
$$
Apply Hölder with exponents \(6\) and \(6/5\) directly to
\(m_j=(m_j^6/t_j^2)^{1/6}t_j^{1/3}\). It gives
$$
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j\right)^6
\le
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{t_j^2}\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}t_j^{2/5}\right)^5.
\tag{23}
$$
The full geometric sum obeys
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}t_j^{2/5}
=\frac{t_0^{2/5}}{1-2^{-2/5}}
<5t_0^{2/5}.
\tag{24}
$$
For the strict numerical inequality, \((4/5)^5=1024/3125>1/4\) implies \(2^{-2/5}<4/5\). From (17), (23), and (24),
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{t_j^2}
>\frac{2^{-6}}{5^5t_0^2}
=\frac{2^{14}}{3125}Q^2.
$$
Substitution in (22) proves, in regime (16), the stronger bounds
$$
M_3(p),T(p)
>\frac{16}{3125C^2}Q^{-3}
=\frac{128}{3125C^2}8^{-N}.
\tag{25}
$$
The rational number \(128/3125\) exceeds \(2^{-40}\). Equations (13), (15), and (25) cover all cases and establish (6). No aggregation involving a separate stock ratio is used.

To prove the sufficient field criterion, write \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\), so that \(p_c=2^{-\lambda\cdot c}/Z\). For a dense band \(j\), every \(c\in C_j\) belongs to \(D_1\), as proved above, and its center probability lies in \((t_j/2,t_j]\). With \(u_j=-\log_2(t_jZ)\), the latter condition is exactly
$$
\lambda\cdot c\in[u_j,u_j+1).
$$
Fix a midpoint parity vector \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\). A point of \(D_1\) of this parity is either \(b\) itself or \(b+2\mathbf e_a\) for one index \(a\) with \(b_a=0\), where here \(\mathbf e_a\) is the standard basis vector of \(\mathbb R^k\). These are distinct points uniquely indexed by the Boolean case or by the position of their two. Their energies minus \(\lambda\cdot b\) are the corresponding indexed offsets \(0\) and \(2\lambda_a\). The energy interval condition therefore permits at most \(R_\beta\) such centers of parity \(b\). The definition counts repeated offset values separately, so this conclusion also holds when several different coordinates have the same offset. Summing over the \(Q\) possible midpoint parities gives \(r_j\le R_\beta Q\). Also \(1\le R_\beta\le k+1\), since an interval can contain zero and there are only \(k+1\) indexed offsets in total. Applying (6) proves the displayed field bound. If the \(\lambda_a\) are distinct nonzero integers, all indexed offsets are distinct integers, and an interval \([u,u+1)\) contains at most one of them. Hence \(R_\beta=1\), which proves its stated special case. No approximation of arbitrary fields by fields with this property is used.

Now verify every hypothesis and the actual band census for (8). For each label \(q\), define \(F_q\) as the points with active vector \(d_q\), every neutral coordinate binary, and at least two neutral ones in \(G_q\). Let
$$
B_{\rm act}=D\cap\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k
$$
be the actual Boolean part of (8), without asserting that any other Boolean point is present. We claim the exact disjoint census
$$
D=B_{\rm act}\mathbin{\dot\cup}
\coprod_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}(E_q\mathbin{\dot\cup}F_q),
\qquad D_2=E,
\qquad D_1=B_{\rm act}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\coprod_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}F_q.
\tag{26}
$$
All generating coordinates lie in \([0,2]\). Suppose an integral convex combination of generators has an active coordinate equal to two. Coordinate extremality forces every positive-coefficient generator to have its active two in that same position \(i\). All other active coordinates of these generators are binary. Their integral averages therefore force agreement at every one of these other coordinates. Thus every generator used has the same active vector \(d_q\) and the same label \(q=(b,i)\). In particular the point cannot have two active twos. If its neutral coordinates are all binary, their sum over \(G_q\) is at least two, since this inequality holds for every generator of \(E_q\). The integral point consequently lies in \(F_q\). Conversely, every point of \(F_q\) is the midpoint of two generators in \(E_q\): choose two private coordinates where it is one, and replace the pair \((1,1)\) by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\), retaining every other coordinate.

If an integral point of the convex hull has a neutral two at coordinate \(v\), coordinate extremality forces every generator used to have a two at \(v\). The private groups are disjoint, so \(v\) belongs to exactly one \(G_q\), and all those generators lie in \(E_q\) with that same two position. Their active vector is \(d_q\) and their other neutral coordinates are binary. Their integral average is therefore an element of \(E_q\). This also shows that a neutral two cannot occur with Boolean active vector, and that two neutral twos cannot occur. All remaining integral points are Boolean. Distinct labels have distinct active vectors, so the indicated unions are disjoint. These observations prove (26), the ternary property, and the at-most-two-twos property. Finally \(E\subseteq D\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E)\) implies \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=\operatorname{conv}(E)\), which proves completeness. The support is finite and nonempty.

The field (9) gives, for every endpoint of a label \(q\), the original weight \(2^{-e_q}\). The exact endpoint count and label mass are
$$
|E_q|=n_q2^{L-1},
\qquad
p(E_q)=\frac{n_q2^{L-1-e_q}}{Z}.
\tag{27}
$$
There is no equality imposed on the expressions on the right. By (26), every double point is an endpoint in one of these labels. All endpoint probabilities have the form
$$
p_e=2^{-e}/Z\qquad(e\in\mathbb Z).
$$
A band \((t_j/2,t_j]\) contains at most one occupied endpoint energy: two different integer energies give probabilities with ratio at least two, whereas any two positive numbers in this half-open band have ratio strictly less than two. Thus each nonempty actual band \(E_j\) is exactly
$$
E_j=\coprod_{q:e_q=e}E_q
\tag{28}
$$
for one occupied energy \(e\).

Fix that energy. Two labels with the same active parity have the same \(b\). If they also have the same energy, (7) gives \(2h_i=2h_{i'}\), whence \(i=i'\) by distinctness of the \(h_i\). They are the same label. Therefore labels at fixed energy have distinct active parities. Writing
$$
M_e=|\lbrace q\in\mathcal Q_0:e_q=e\rbrace|,
$$
this proves \(M_e\le2^m\).

Distinct same-parity endpoints in (28) must consequently belong to one label \(q\). They must have their neutral twos in different positions: with the same two position, equal parity would force agreement of all remaining binary neutral coordinates and make the endpoints identical. If their two positions are \(v,w\in G_q\), the other endpoint has zero at each of these positions, by equal parity. All remaining neutral coordinates agree. Their midpoint therefore has neutral ones at \(v,w\), is binary elsewhere in the neutral block, and has active vector \(d_q\). It lies in \(F_q\). Conversely, the two-endpoint construction proving membership of each point of \(F_q\) above uses distinct same-parity endpoints, both of energy \(e_q\). Hence the exact actual midpoint image is
$$
C_j=\coprod_{q:e_q=e}F_q.
\tag{29}
$$

For completeness, put \(f(n)=1-(n+1)2^{-n}\). The neutral binary choices give
$$
|F_q|=2^{L-n_q}(2^{n_q}-n_q-1)=2^Lf(n_q),
\qquad 0<f(n_q)<1.
$$
The occupied neutral parity vectors of a label are exactly those having at least one zero in \(G_q\): a two must occur at such a zero, and any such zero can be chosen as the two position. At a fixed energy there is no overlap between the active parities of different labels. Thus the entire actual band census is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n_j&=2^{L-1}\sum_{q:e_q=e}n_q,
&m_j&=p_e n_j,\\
q_j&=2^L\sum_{q:e_q=e}(1-2^{-n_q}),
&r_j&=2^L\sum_{q:e_q=e}f(n_q),
&\omega_j&=t_jr_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
$$
In particular (29), or equivalently (30), gives
$$
r_j\le2^LM_e\le2^{L+m}=Q.
\tag{31}
$$
This holds for every actual band, hence for every dense actual band required by (5). Applying (6) with \(C=1\) proves (10). The proof permits arbitrary private group sizes, arbitrary nonempty label selections, arbitrary ordering of the distinct positive integer energies, and every affine rank of the resulting support.
