## Statement

Use exactly the canonical reflected quantities of accepted 445a9e668ee1a705. For a positive law \(p\) on a finite support \(A\subset\mathbb Z^n\), extended by zero elsewhere, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=2^n,&R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,&
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},\\
\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j,&
A_0&=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3,\qquad A_{\mathrm{full}}=K^2b(p).
\end{aligned}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in \(H_p\), and the central atom occurs once. All logarithms are natural. The symbol \(A_0\) denotes the displayed atom quantity, not the canonical ratio \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\), where \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\).

For every integer \(n\ge7\), put \(m=K-1\), using the convention of accepted 5a434b8dc317dfaf. Thus our support cardinality is \(2m+1\), the quantity denoted by \(m\) in e2506c0626ec02e6; no parameters from its field-budget proof are imported here. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=m^{-1/3},&
a_+&=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,&
a_-&=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,\\
B&=\frac{a_+}t,&z&=\log B,&M&=K^2,\qquad \delta=\frac zM.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(\mathcal C=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\). For \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\in\mathcal C\), set
$$
u_c=(M+c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_n),\qquad
A=\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\cup\lbrace u_c,-u_c:c\in\mathcal C\rbrace,
\qquad \ell=\frac zM e_1.
$$
Take the genuine global affine Gibbs weights, their entire original normalizer, and their original law:
$$
w_x=e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in A}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}Z\quad(x\in A).
$$
Every parity class contains at most two atoms. The support is closed under taking an integral midpoint, and every supported pair \(x,y\) with integral midpoint \(j\) satisfies \(\sqrt{p_xp_y}=p_j\). Nevertheless, the full canonical rows satisfy
$$
A_0<1,\qquad 1\le A_{\mathrm{full}}<12,\qquad
\nu\ge\frac1{81}\log^2(2^n-1).
$$
Thus no finite positive absolute constant \(C\) makes \(\nu\le C\log(K^2b(p))\) valid throughout this affine, midpoint-closed, parity-at-most-two active class.

The support is not complete in any convex set: \(e_1\in\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) but \(e_1\notin A\). This counterexample therefore does not contradict the complete antipodal Boolean affine theorem e2506c0626ec02e6. It neither refutes the price under the conjunction of complete convex support and global affine weights nor asserts a counterexample to actual depth moments or the locked mixed-integer-volume statement.

## Proof

The parameters \(t,a_+,a_-\) are those of accepted 5a434b8dc317dfaf. Since \(m\ge127\), they are positive and satisfy \(a_++a_-=1\), \(a_+a_-=t^2\), \(a_+>t\), \(a_+\ge1/2\), and \(t^3=1/m\). Consequently \(B>1\), \(z>0\), and \(B^{-1}=a_-/t\).

The integer \(M=K^2\) is even. Hence \(u_c\equiv-u_c\equiv c\pmod2\). The positive first coordinates of the \(u_c\), and their remaining Boolean coordinates, show that all the points listed in \(A\) are distinct. The zero parity class contains only \(\mathbf0\), and each nonzero parity class \(c\) contains exactly \(u_c,-u_c\). Two integer points have an integral midpoint precisely when they have the same parity. Thus the only distinct supported pairs with integral midpoint are \(u_c,-u_c\), whose midpoint is \(\mathbf0\). This proves midpoint closure and gives exactly
$$
R_{\mathbf0}=A,\qquad |R_{\mathbf0}|=2m+1,\qquad
R_x=\lbrace x\rbrace\quad(x\ne\mathbf0).
\tag{1}
$$

For each \(c\in\mathcal C\), set \(s_c=z+\delta c_1\). The original weights are exactly
$$
w_{\mathbf0}=1,\qquad w_{u_c}=e^{s_c}=Be^{\delta c_1},
\qquad w_{-u_c}=e^{-s_c}=B^{-1}e^{-\delta c_1}.
\tag{2}
$$
In particular \(w_{u_c}w_{-u_c}=1=w_{\mathbf0}^2\), so all the asserted midpoint equalities hold after division by the same original \(Z\). There are \(K/2-1\) vectors \(c\in\mathcal C\) with \(c_1=0\) and \(K/2\) with \(c_1=1\). Therefore the entire original normalizer is
$$
Z=1+\left(\frac K2-1\right)(B+B^{-1})
 +\frac K2\left(Be^\delta+B^{-1}e^{-\delta}\right).
\tag{3}
$$
No atom has been omitted from this sum.

For comparison only, define reference weights on the same new support by \(w^*_{\mathbf0}=1\), \(w^*_{u_c}=B\), and \(w^*_{-u_c}=B^{-1}\). Write \(Z^*=\sum_xw^*_x\) and \(p^*_x=w^*_x/Z^*\). Under the label map \(\mathbf0\mapsto\mathbf0\), \(c\mapsto u_c\), \(-c\mapsto-u_c\), these are exactly the weights of accepted 5a434b8dc317dfaf divided by \(t\). Thus \(Z^*=(m+t)/t=1+m/t\), and \(p^*\) is exactly its normalized law transported by that label map. Equation (1) shows that the full reflected rows are transported as well. Hence its \(H,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu,A_0,A_{\mathrm{full}}\) are the corresponding canonical reference objects, with no change of their definitions. Superscripts \(*\) below denote this reference law, not a replacement of the original law \(p\).

We need a quantitative margin in the accepted active inequality. Put \(\varepsilon=m^{-2/3}\). The reference formula from 5a434b8dc317dfaf is
$$
A_0^*=\frac{(1+1/m)^2}{(1+t/m)^3}
 \left(1-3\varepsilon+m^{-2}\right).
\tag{4}
$$
For \(m\ge64\), the factor \(f=1-3\varepsilon+m^{-2}\) lies strictly between zero and one, and \((1+1/m)^2\le1+3/m\). Moreover
$$
\frac{m^{-2}+3/m}{\varepsilon}
=m^{-4/3}+3m^{-1/3}
\le\frac1{256}+\frac34<1.
$$
It follows directly from (4) that
$$
A_0^*<(1+3/m)f\le f+3/m<1-2\varepsilon.
\tag{5}
$$
The reference full-row bound \(A_{\mathrm{full}}^*<6\) is also supplied by accepted 5a434b8dc317dfaf for all our \(n\ge7\).

We now bound the perturbation explicitly. Since \(a_+\le1\), one has \(z\le(\log m)/3\). The elementary inequality \(\log m\le m\), together with \(m\ge8\), yields
$$
0<3\delta\le\frac{\log m}{(m+1)^2}
\le\frac1m\le\frac{\varepsilon}2.
\tag{6}
$$
For \(0\le x\le1/2\), the exponential series gives \(e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}\le1+2x\). Since \(\varepsilon\le1/16\), equation (6) therefore implies
$$
e^{3\delta}\le1+\varepsilon<2.
\tag{7}
$$
The weights in (2) satisfy \(e^{-\delta}w^*_x\le w_x\le e^\delta w^*_x\). More specifically, \(e^{s_c}+e^{-s_c}=2\cosh(s_c)\ge2\cosh z\), because \(s_c\ge z>0\). Consequently
$$
Z^*\le Z\le e^\delta Z^*,\qquad
e^{-2\delta}\le\frac{p_x}{p_x^*}\le e^\delta
\quad(x\in A).
\tag{8}
$$
These inequalities provide the required quantitative comparison without a continuity argument.

Write \(h=2m+1\) for the cardinality in (1), and define only for the following finite sums
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_3&=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}(e^{3s_c}+e^{-3s_c})\\
&=\left(\frac K2-1\right)(B^3+B^{-3})
 +\frac K2\left(B^3e^{3\delta}+B^{-3}e^{-3\delta}\right),\\
T_3^*&=m(B^3+B^{-3})=m^2(1-3\varepsilon).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The last identity follows from \(a_+^3+a_-^3=1-3t^2\) and \(t^3=1/m\). Equation (2) gives \(T_3\le e^{3\delta}T_3^*\). Using (1) and the exact reciprocal weights, the original canonical quantities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(\mathbf0)&=\frac hZ,&H_p(x)&=p_x\quad(x\ne\mathbf0),\\
A_0&=\frac{K^2(1+T_3)}{Z^3},&
A_{\mathrm{full}}&=\frac{K^2(h^2+T_3)}{Z^3},\\
q_{\mathbf0}&=\frac{h^2}{h^2+T_3},&
\kappa_{\mathbf0}(x)&=\frac1h\quad(x\in A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
In particular the canonical row law is uniform at zero, with its central atom counted once. The same formulas with \(T_3^*,Z^*\) give the reference quantities. From (8)--(10), including the fixed central contributions in their numerators, we obtain
$$
A_0\le e^{3\delta}A_0^*
<(1+\varepsilon)(1-2\varepsilon)<1,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{full}}\le e^{3\delta}A_{\mathrm{full}}^*
<6(1+\varepsilon)<12.
\tag{11}
$$
Thus the active inequality survives with a proved margin at every \(n\ge7\).

For the lower bound on \(A_{\mathrm{full}}\), retain exactly \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\) from 445a9e668ee1a705. All integral midpoints of supported same-parity pairs are supported, and their midpoint equalities have already been proved. The bijection between ordered same-parity pairs and their reflected triples therefore gives
$$
\chi(p)
=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
 p_{(x+y)/2}\sqrt{p_xp_y}
=\sum_{\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}
 p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)^2
\ge\frac1K.
\tag{12}
$$
The final inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz for the \(K\) parity masses, whose sum is one. Jensen under the original law gives \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\), and hence \(A_{\mathrm{full}}\ge1\). This argument uses the already verified midpoint closure and does not assume convex completeness.

It remains to check the price. The canonical scores at the origin are \(Y_{\mathbf0}(\mathbf0)=0\), \(Y_{\mathbf0}(u_c)=s_c\), and \(Y_{\mathbf0}(-u_c)=-s_c\). Every nonzero row is a singleton and has score zero. Thus, with no change of the canonical normalization,
$$
V_{\mathbf0}=\frac2h\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}s_c^2
\ge\frac{2m}{2m+1}z^2\ge\frac23z^2,
\qquad \nu=q_{\mathbf0}V_{\mathbf0}.
\tag{13}
$$
Equations (7) and (9) show \(T_3<2m^2\), whereas \(h^2\ge4m^2\). Consequently (10) gives \(q_{\mathbf0}\ge2/3\). Also \(a_+\ge1/2\) and \(m\ge64\) imply
$$
z=\log(a_+/t)\ge\frac13\log m-\log2
\ge\frac16\log m.
$$
Combining these bounds with (13) proves
$$
\nu\ge\frac49z^2\ge\frac1{81}\log^2m.
\tag{14}
$$
Since \(1\le A_{\mathrm{full}}<12\), for each proposed finite \(C>0\), sufficiently large \(n\) makes (14) exceed \(C\log A_{\mathrm{full}}\). This proves the claimed obstruction for genuine affine weights with the original normalizer (3).

Finally, \(u_{e_1}=(M+1)e_1\) belongs to \(A\), and \(e_1=(M+1)^{-1}u_{e_1}+(1-(M+1)^{-1})\mathbf0\) is an integer point on its segment with \(\mathbf0\). But every nonzero point of \(A\) has first coordinate of absolute value at least \(M>1\), so \(e_1\notin A\). Any convex set containing \(A\) contains that segment, and therefore cannot have \(A\) as its entire lattice intersection. The new support is not the complete Boolean support covered by e2506c0626ec02e6, whose conclusion is accordingly unaffected. Accepted 5a434b8dc317dfaf retains completeness and drops global affinity; this construction retains global affinity and drops completeness. Neither result resolves the conjunction of those premises or provides an actual-depth or mixed-integer-volume disproof.
