## Statement

For d>=2, N=d+1, Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d and kappa=1/2-Gamma, set eta_d=min(1/(4N),kappa log N/(4N)) and E_d=kappa Gamma^2/(144dN). In the simplex strict-hitting model with |B|=(1-E)V, source simplex volumes>=2^(-d)V and weights summing to E, if 0<E<=E_d there is one deterministic point with all barycentric coordinates within 6E/Gamma of 1/N and score at least Gamma+E kappa/4 for every affine form within oscillation eta_d of a positive canonical barycentric form. The radius is independent of E; arbitrary remaining orientations and a full OC center are not asserted.

## Proof

# A fixed normal neighborhood and an explicit small-deficit center

This theorem upgrades the normal-neighborhood radius from order E to a dimension-dependent constant in the true simplex-source strict-hitting model. It combines the proved quantile/minimax mechanism with the exact quadratic vertex-cap bound. It still does not control directions outside this neighborhood or prove OC.

## 1. Statement

Fix d>=2 and put

    N=d+1, Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d, kappa=1/2-Gamma>0,
    eta_d=min{1/(4N), kappa log N/(4N)},
    E_d=kappa Gamma^2/(144dN).

Let V>0 and let B be a d-simplex of volume vV, where v=1-E and 0<E<=E_d. Let P_i subset B be finitely many d-simplices of volumes at least 2^(-d)V, and let e_i>=0 sum to E. Denote the barycentric functions of B by lambda_j and its centroid by g.

There is one deterministic point y_* in B such that

    |lambda_j(y_*)-1/N|<=6E/Gamma  for every j,      (1)

and, for every affine f satisfying

    osc_B(f-lambda_j)<=eta_d for at least one j,

the following normalized closed-cap/strict-hitting inequality holds:

    |B intersect {f>=f(y_*)}|/V
      +sum_i e_i 1{max_(x in P_i)f(x)>f(y_*)}
       >=Gamma+E*kappa/4>Gamma.                    (2)

The same is true for all containing halfspaces in these positive orientations. The size eta_d is independent of E. The explicit admissible deficit E_d is proportional to 1/[d(d+1)] times the displayed exact Gamma/kappa factor.

## 2. Continuous threshold minimax

Normalize each allowed f by an additive constant so that f(g)=1/N, and let F be the resulting compact family. No f is constant. Put

    T=Gamma+E*kappa/4,
    G_f(t)=|B intersect {f>=t}|/V
             +sum_i e_i 1{max_(P_i)f>t},
    r_T(f)=sup{t:G_f(t)>=T}.

As proved directly in the companion nearby-normal note, r_T(f) is continuous on F. To recall the exact mechanism, for a source subset J set q_J=T-sum_(i in J)e_i. Omit J if q_J>v. Let b_q(f) be the continuous ordinary B-cap quantile of normalized mass q when 0<q<=v, and put b_q=+infinity for q<=0. Then

    r_T(f)=max_(J:q_J<=v)
          min{min_(i in J) max_(P_i)f, b_(q_J)(f)},

where the empty minimum is +infinity. Each included term is finite and continuous. This formula handles source-support jumps exactly. Moreover

    t<r_T(f) => G_f(t)>=T,
    t>=r_T(f) => G_f(t)<=T.                         (3)

The second inequality at equality follows from right continuity and the supremum definition; no attainment of a strict-hitting depth maximum is assumed.

Minimize the continuous convex function

    Phi(y)=max_(f in F)[f(y)-r_T(f)]

over compact B. Choose a minimizer y_0 and put alpha=Phi(y_0). We will first localize every minimizer quantitatively, without assuming alpha<0.

## 3. An upper bound on the minimax value

Write a=Gamma+kappa/4. Then 0<a<=1/2 and T=v Gamma+Ea. Since E<=E_d, one has v>=1/2 and

    E<=d Gamma/(4N),   E<=Gamma/4.                 (4)

These follow immediately from kappa Gamma<1 and the denominator 144dN.

Set

    sigma=2Ea/(dv Gamma)<=2E/(d Gamma)<=1/(2N).     (5)

For every f in F, select its canonical label j. At g its positive cap contains only vertex j: after the normalization f(g)=1/N, the other vertex values are at most eta_d<=1/(4N), while vertex j is higher. The threshold gap above every other vertex is at least 3/(4N). Thus lowering the threshold by any amount up to sigma remains in this vertex-cap region.

Let A=f(v_j)-f(g). Then 0<A<2. The exact vertex-cap formula is proportional to (A+t)^d when its threshold is lowered by t. At t=0 its normalized own-cap mass is at least v Gamma by AM-GM. Its derivative at zero is therefore at least dv Gamma/A>=dv Gamma/2, and this derivative increases as t increases. Consequently lowering the threshold by sigma increases its own mass by at least Ea. Its own cap alone then has mass at least T. Hence

    r_T(f)>=f(g)-sigma,
    alpha<=Phi(g)<=sigma.                          (6)

All source credits were ignored in this upper bound.

## 4. Canonical constraints localize every minimizer

For a canonical f=lambda_j, source credits are at most E, so

    r_T(lambda_j)
       <=1-((T-E)/v)^(1/d).                        (7)

Indeed a larger threshold has own cap less than T-E and hence total score less than T. Let b=1-Gamma-kappa/4=1-a. Then

    (T-E)/v=Gamma-Eb/v,
    Gamma/2<=(T-E)/v<=Gamma

by (4) and v>=1/2. The derivative of x^(1/d) on [Gamma/2,Gamma] is at most 2/(d Gamma). Since Gamma^(1/d)=d/N, (7) gives

    r_T(lambda_j)<=1/N+4E/(d Gamma).

Combining this with (6) and the defining minimax inequalities yields

    lambda_j(y_0)<=1/N+U,   U=6E/(d Gamma).          (8)

Because the coordinates sum to one,

    lambda_j(y_0)>=1/N-dU.

This proves (1) for any minimizer. In addition,

    6E/Gamma<=1/(8N),                              (9)

since E<=E_d and kappa Gamma<=3d. Thus every minimizer is strictly interior and lies in the near-centroid region required by the quadratic vertex-cap estimate.

The asymmetric bounds in (8) give a sharper variance estimate than bounding each deviation separately. Write z_j=lambda_j(y_0)-1/N. Then sum z_j=0 and -dU<=z_j<=U. The inequality (z_j+dU)(U-z_j)>=0 gives, after summation,

    sum_j z_j^2<=NdU^2
       =36N E^2/(d Gamma^2).                       (10)

## 5. Quadratic cap loss and active-fan contradiction

The exact vertex-cap calculation gives, for any f labeled j at this near-centroid point,

    |B intersect {f>=f(y_0)}|/|B|
       >=(1-lambda_j(y_0))^d-d^2 sum_l z_l^2.       (11)

For completeness, the exact cap fraction is (sum_(i!=j)r_i s_i)^d/product r_i, which is at least d^d product_(i!=j)s_i by AM-GM. With m the average of the other s_i, the deviations (s_i-m)/m have absolute value at most 2/7 by (9). Taylor's inequality for log(1+x) bounds the difference between (sum_(i!=j)s_i)^d and d^d product_(i!=j)s_i by d^2 sum_(i!=j)(s_i-m)^2, which is at most d^2 sum_l z_l^2. The normal bound eta_d<=1/(4N) ensures that each cap stays in the single-vertex region, as verified in the quadratic companion note.

Suppose alpha>=0. Because y_0 is interior, the active gradients of Phi have a finite positively balanced subfamily. Every active f has f(y_0)>=r_T(f), hence its score is at most T by (3). Balance and eta_d<1/N imply that every canonical label occurs in the active family: if label m were missing, evaluation on v_m-g would make all positively weighted gradients strictly negative. Choose one active f per label.

The hit-row determinant argument with perturbed thresholds t_j=lambda_j(y_0)+eta_d, combined with (11), gives

    max_(chosen active f) Q_f(y_0)
       >=Gamma+E*kappa
            -E*N*eta_d/log N-v*d^2 sum_l z_l^2.    (12)

This is the same logarithmic budget calculation as in the quadratic note, but retaining the sharper variance term. Each source has a hit row because otherwise it fits in an inverted simplex of relative volume (N eta_d)^d<=4^(-d); centered Hadamard then bounds its missed log-weight by (N/2)log N+log v. Chebyshev/Jensen control the canonical own caps, and sum log(1/t_j)>=N log N-N^2 eta_d prices the perturbation.

By the definition of eta_d, the first loss in (12) is at most E*kappa/4. By (10) and E<=E_d, the second loss is at most

    36dN E^2/Gamma^2<=E*kappa/4.

Therefore an active cap has score at least Gamma+E*kappa/2>T, a contradiction. It follows that alpha<0. Equations (3) then prove (2) for y_0 simultaneously for all f in F.

Select the lexicographically first point of the compact minimizer set to obtain a deterministic choice y_*. The localization estimates hold for this point, and adding back arbitrary affine constants does not alter its containing halfspaces.

## 6. Scope and unresolved extension

The theorem uses an attained continuous quantile-minimax problem and proves its selected point is within the explicit O(E) barycentric neighborhood. The quadratic cap identity then permits a fixed dimension-dependent normal neighborhood, an improvement over the earlier radius proportional to E.

It remains a theorem for a simplex middle and simplex sources in the strict-hitting model. It gives no assertion that all other oblique normals are harmless, no substitution of an arbitrary convex partner by a simplex, and no mixed-integer realization or global mass allocation. In particular this is not a resolution of the general hitting-selection problem or OC.

# Appendix: complete quadratic vertex-cap calculation

# Exact vertex-cap optimization and a quadratic near-centroid loss

This note strengthens the own-cap estimate in the nearby-normal determinant argument. It remains a scoped cap-fan theorem, with an explicit near-centroid hypothesis. It does not assert that an arbitrary obstructing fan or selected center satisfies that hypothesis.

## 1. Exact cap formula and orientation-independent lower bound

Let B be a d-simplex, d>=2, with vertices v_1,...,v_N, N=d+1, and barycentric coordinates lambda_i. Let y have strictly positive barycentric coordinates s_i. Fix j and an affine function f such that

    f(v_j)>f(y)>=max_(i!=j) f(v_i).

Thus the positive cap through y contains only vertex j, permitting other vertices on its boundary. Put r_i=f(v_j)-f(v_i)>0 for i!=j. The edge from v_j to v_i meets the cap boundary at the fraction

    [f(v_j)-f(y)]/r_i

of its full length from v_j. These fractions are in (0,1]. The cap is a simplex, so

    |B intersect {f>=f(y)}|/|B|
       =[f(v_j)-f(y)]^d/product_(i!=j) r_i
       =[sum_(i!=j) r_i s_i]^d/product_(i!=j) r_i
       >=d^d product_(i!=j) s_i.                    (1)

The last inequality is AM-GM. Equality in (1) holds exactly when all r_i s_i are equal.

If max_(i!=j) s_i<=1/d, this lower bound is attainable by a vertex-j cap. Indeed choose the vertex-value gaps r_i=1/s_i, so f(v_j)-f(y)=d. The condition d<=r_i ensures that the cap boundary remains beyond all other vertices in the required orientation. Thus (1) gives the exact minimum over that vertex-cap region whenever this condition holds.

At the centroid, s_i=1/N and (1) is exactly Gamma_d. There is no first-order orientation error at that point.

## 2. Explicit quadratic loss from the canonical cap

Suppose

    |s_i-1/N|<=epsilon for every i,
    0<=epsilon<=1/(8N).

Write S_j=sum_(i!=j)s_i=1-s_j and m=S_j/d. Then m>=7/(8N), and |s_i-m|<=2epsilon. The normalized deviations x_i=(s_i-m)/m satisfy

    sum_(i!=j)x_i=0,  |x_i|<=2/7.

Taylor's theorem for log(1+x), using its second derivative, gives for |x|<=r<1

    log(1+x)>=x-x^2/[2(1-r)^2].

Apply this with r=2/7 and sum. Since 1/[2(1-2/7)^2]=49/50<1,

    product_(i!=j)(1+x_i)>=exp(-sum_(i!=j)x_i^2).

Using 1-exp(-z)<=z and that the mean minimizes the sum of squared deviations,

    S_j^d-d^d product_(i!=j)s_i
       <=S_j^d sum_(i!=j)x_i^2
       =d^2 S_j^(d-2) sum_(i!=j)(s_i-m)^2
       <=d^2 sum_(i!=j)(s_i-1/N)^2
       <=d^3 epsilon^2.                            (2)

Here S_j<=1 and d>=2. Consequently every cap in the vertex-j region satisfies

    |B intersect {f>=f(y)}|/|B|
       >=(1-s_j)^d-d^3 epsilon^2.                  (3)

The lower bound (3) is independent of the angular distance from the canonical normal, provided the cap remains in the stated vertex region.

## 3. A uniform neighborhood is inside the vertex region

Suppose f is affine and

    osc_B(f-lambda_j)<=eta<=1/(4N).

Subtract a constant so that min_(i!=j) f(v_i)=0. Then the other vertex values lie in [0,eta], while

    1-eta<=f(v_j)<=1+eta.

Under the near-centroid hypothesis of Section 2,

    f(y)>=s_j f(v_j)>=(7/(8N))(1-eta)>eta
          >=max_(i!=j)f(v_i).

Also f(y)<f(v_j), since y is interior and all other vertex values are strictly smaller than f(v_j). Thus all these nearby normals lie in the vertex-j cap region and (3) applies. The comparison (7/(8N))(1-1/(4N))>1/(4N) is immediate for N>=3.

## 4. A determinant fan bound with no E-dependent angular tolerance

Let V>0, |B|=vV with 0<v<1, and let P_i subset B be d-simplices of volume at least 2^(-d)V. Let their nonnegative weights e_i sum to E=1-v. Set

    Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d, kappa=1/2-Gamma>0.

For each j choose an affine f_j with osc_B(f_j-lambda_j)<=eta, and define the normalized closed-cap/strict-hitting scores Q_j as in the nearby-normal note. Suppose y satisfies Section 2 and

    0<=eta<=1/(4N).

Then

    max_j Q_j(y)
       >=Gamma+E*kappa
                 -E*N*eta/log N-v*d^3*epsilon^2.   (4)

In particular,

    eta<=min{1/(4N), kappa*log N/(4N)},
    epsilon<=min{1/(8N), sqrt(E*kappa/(4v*d^3))}      (5)

imply

    max_j Q_j(y)>=Gamma+E*kappa/2>Gamma.             (6)

The angular tolerance in (5) is independent of E. The hypothesis on the point permits displacement of order sqrt(E), with the explicit constants displayed.

Proof of (4). Put t_j=s_j+eta and L_j=log(1/t_j). They are positive because s_j<=1/N+epsilon and eta<=1/(4N), with N>=3. Write L=sum_j L_j.

A source missing cap j has barycentric row maximum at most t_j. No source can miss all caps: it would fit in the inverted simplex lambda_j<=t_j of relative volume (N eta)^d<=4^(-d), giving source volume at most v4^(-d)V<2^(-d)V. Thus an actual hit row exists for each source. The hit-row Hadamard argument proves

    sum_(j missed) L_j <=(N/2)log N+log v
                       <=(N/2)log N.

For clarity, that argument uses the barycentric source matrix M with |det M|>=2^(-d)/v, replaces a hit row by the all-ones row, centers every other row in its entry range, and applies Hadamard to get |det M|<=N^(N/2)2^(-d) product_(j!=hit) r_j. Discarding hit factors at most one yields the asserted missing-log bound.

The sequences L_j and (1-s_j)^d have the same ordering. Chebyshev and Jensen give sum_j L_j(1-s_j)^d>=Gamma L. Apply (3) to each own cap, add source hit weights, and divide by L:

    max_j Q_j(y)
       >=v Gamma-vd^3 epsilon^2
          +E[1-((N/2)log N)/L].

Finally L>=N log N-N^2 eta. Since x=N eta/log N<=1/(4log N)<1/2,

    ((N/2)log N)/L<=1/[2(1-x)]<=1/2+x.

Substitution gives (4), and (5) gives (6).

## 5. Scope

This is a quantified improvement of a finite-direction certificate. Positive balanced fans entirely within these neighborhoods also have a sufficiently large score at such a near-centroid point: the missing-label argument from the nearby-normal note forces every canonical label to occur, and (4) applies to one representative per label.

It does not establish near-centroid localization for a global optimizer, control normals outside the vertex-cap neighborhoods, or prove that a full strict-hitting maximum is attained. The exact AM-GM cap formula identifies the source of the quadratic improvement without importing any such unproved global step.
