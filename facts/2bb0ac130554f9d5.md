## Statement

Let a finite actual cap menu have nonzero continuous normals forming a minimal positive circuit of rank $r_c$. It has $r_c+1$ normals. In rank one they form an antipodal pair after normalization, and in rank at least two at most one cap supports a fixed positive-area own-fiber facet. On a sampled incidence piece in one nonvertical ambient facet supported by the same menu cap $j$ with fixed orientation, use summed own-facet area $dA$. Suppose the actual section selector $\tau\in[0,1]$ is the vertical length of a genuine convex unit-height lift $L$ in orthonormal tangent and height coordinates. It is extended by zero as a length off the lift projection. Assume rank at least two and the usual limit allocation, namely $\tau$ is body capture for outside support and $1-\tau$ is body capture for inside support. Each other actual cap $H_k$ pulls back to $\widetilde H_k$ independent of height. If $A_0=\int dA$, $L_0=\int\tau\,dA$, and $\omega_k$ are the menu weights, the original weighted body load is
$$
\omega_j L_0+\sum_{k\ne j}\omega_k\left(|L\cap\widetilde H_k|+\int(1-\tau)\mathbf 1_{H_k}\,dA\right)
$$
at outside support, with $\omega_j L_0$ replaced by $\omega_j(A_0-L_0)$ at inside support. Here $|\cdot|$ sums lifted $d$-volume over the piece's sampled bases, and the lift has its sampled projection within this piece. The same split holds with a bounded nonnegative physical weight pulled back independently of height.

The complement can be nonconvex. For $\tau(x)=1-x^2$ on $[-1,1]$, the original trace, selected load and complementary load on $x\ge0$ are $1$, $2/3$ and $1/3$. Even for convex selector lifts the ratio $L_0/A_0$ can be arbitrarily small.

## Proof

If a minimal positive kernel vector had an independent kernel direction, change the latter's sign to make at least one entry positive. Subtract a positive multiple of that direction from the positive vector until the first entry becomes zero. The result is a nonzero nonnegative dependence on a proper subset, a contradiction. The kernel thus has dimension one. Repeated positive directions could be merged into one coefficient, and an opposite pair would itself be a positive circuit. Consequently in rank at least two the normals are pairwise nonparallel. In rank one the cardinality is two and the directions are opposite. Supporting an own facet requires the continuous normal to be parallel to its normal. For $k\ne j$ the cut restricted to the facet has nonzero tangential component and its zero plane has zero $dA$ measure. Thus the limiting capture is the ordinary indicator of $H_k$ there, including the cases of a full or empty trace.

Parameterize each own facet by orthonormal tangent coordinates. The pullback of the original inequality ignores the new height variable and retains its original threshold. Fubini gives $|L\cap\widetilde H_k|=\int\tau\mathbf1_{H_k}\,dA$. Add its complement $\int(1-\tau)\mathbf1_{H_k}\,dA$ and the stated allocation of cap $j$ to obtain the displayed identities. The proof with a physical weight uses the same Fubini argument. If computing cap-$j$ coarea in the original continuous covector $u_j$, multiply its own area-based section allocation by $1/\lVert u_j\rVert$. A genuine lift is taken only on its convex domain, with its actual zero-height points.

For the example, the lift below $\tau$ is convex by concavity. In its complement the points $(\pm1,1/2)$ occur and $(0,1/2)$ does not. The integrals over $[0,1]$ are $1$, $\int_0^1(1-x^2)\,dx=2/3$ and $\int_0^1x^2\,dx=1/3$. Constant $\tau=\epsilon>0$ gives a convex product lift with ratio $\epsilon$.
