## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge 1$, a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb R^n$, and nonempty compact convex sets $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ for $z\in P$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_z})$, and $M=\mu(S)$. Let $h_\mu(z,x)$ be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(z,x)$.

The family is compatible if the fiber of $C=\operatorname{conv}(S)$ at every $z\in P$ is exactly $K_z$. Equivalently, whenever a convex combination of bases in $P$ equals a base $z\in P$, the same Minkowski combination of the corresponding fibers lies in $K_z$. For a lattice application require $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, so $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$.

Assume compatibility and $M>0$. A point $(z,x)\in S$ has positive depth if and only if $x\in\operatorname{int}K_z$. If $\mathcal L^d(K_z)=0$, every site of $P$ on the minimal face of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ containing $z$ also has zero fiber volume.

Put $P_+=\{z\in P:\mathcal L^d(K_z)>0\}$ and let $C_+$ be the convex hull of the lifted fibers over $P_+$. The fibers of $C_+$ at the sites in $P_+$ are the original $K_z$, and the bases of $C_+$ in $P$ are exactly $P_+$. Deleting the null fibers therefore preserves $\mu$ and the maximum depth over the remaining mixed set. If $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, then $P_+=\operatorname{conv}(P_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ and $C_+\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ consists exactly of these retained fibers.

## Proof

Fix $z\in P$, let $F$ be the minimal face of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ containing $z$, and choose an affine exposer $g$ that is zero on $F$ and strictly negative at every site of $P\setminus F$. The face $F$ may be all of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, in which case take $g=0$. Since $z\in\operatorname{relint}F$, it is a convex combination of $P\cap F$ with all weights strictly positive. Indeed, a sufficiently small displacement from $z$ away from the average of the sites of $P\cap F$ stays in $F$; express the displaced point as a convex combination and mix it with that average. Compatibility and any one positive-volume fiber on $F$ imply that $K_z$ contains a translate of a positive multiple of that full-dimensional fiber. Thus a null fiber at $z$ forces every fiber above $F$ to be null.

If all these fibers are null, $M>0$ implies $P\setminus F\ne\varnothing$, and the vertical cap $g(w)\ge0$ through $(z,x)$ has mass zero for every $x\in K_z$. If some fiber on $F$ is positive, let $C_F$ be the convex hull of the lifted fibers over $P\cap F$. Its affine hull is $\operatorname{aff}(F)\times\mathbb R^d$: its projection spans $F$ and one of its fibers supplies every continuous direction. Its fiber at $z$ equals $K_z$ by compatibility. For $x\in\partial K_z$, the point $(z,x)$ is on the relative boundary of $C_F$, since a relative neighborhood would contain a neighborhood in the $z$-fiber. Choose a nonconstant relative supporting functional there, written as an affine margin $L(w,y)$ with $L(z,x)=0$ and $L\le0$ on $C_F$. Its continuous component is nonzero. A pure base functional supporting at $z\in\operatorname{relint}F$ would be constant on $F$ and hence on the relative ambient space. Therefore the outward cap $L\ge0$ meets each $F$-fiber only in a $d$-volume-null hyperplane. The margin $L+Tg$ for sufficiently large finite $T>0$ excludes every off-face fiber, by boundedness and finiteness, giving a nonvertical closed cap through $(z,x)$ of mass zero.

For $x\in\operatorname{int}K_z$, a ball of positive radius centered at $x$ is contained in $K_z$. Every ambient halfspace through or containing $(z,x)$ keeps at least half the ball's $d$-volume. This proves the depth characterization.

Every point $w\in P\cap\operatorname{conv}(P_+)$ has a convex representation by positive-volume sites. Compatibility puts a full-dimensional Minkowski combination inside $K_w$, so $w\in P_+$. Thus $P\cap\operatorname{conv}(P_+)=P_+$. The projection of $C_+$ is $\operatorname{conv}(P_+)$, and at each retained site its fiber is sandwiched between $K_z$ from the original lift and $K_z$ from inclusion in $C$. When the lattice completeness condition holds, every integer point in $\operatorname{conv}(P_+)$ belongs to $P$, hence to $P_+$. Deletion leaves the measure itself unchanged. Deleted points have zero depth and there is positive depth at an interior point of a positive fiber. Therefore the maximum depth is unchanged. These statements concern the original family and its reduced support.
