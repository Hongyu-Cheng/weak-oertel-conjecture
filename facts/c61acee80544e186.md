## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge4\), put \(r:=n^2\), \(N:=n^3\), \(\beta:=1/r\), and \(q_{\mathrm{geom}}:=e^{-\beta}\). Let \(P_n:=[0,N]^n\), \(A_n:=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and \(\boldsymbol\lambda_n:=-\beta(1,\ldots,1)\). Define the auxiliary model weights and their reflected masses by

\[
\widetilde w_{z,n}:=
\begin{cases}
e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot z},&z\in A_n,\\
0,&z\notin A_n,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widetilde W_n:=\sum_z\widetilde w_{z,n},
\qquad
\widetilde S_{j,n}:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\min\{\widetilde w_{j+v,n},\widetilde w_{j-v,n}\},
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n:=\max_{j\in A_n}\widetilde S_{j,n}.
\]

Every displacement sum is oriented and includes zero unless explicitly excluded. A nonzero integer vector \(v\) is primitive when \(\gcd(|v_1|,\ldots,|v_n|)=1\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde T_n
&:=\sum_{\substack{j\in A_n,\ v\in\mathbb Z^n\\j+v,j-v\in A_n}}
\widetilde w_{j+v,n}\widetilde w_{j-v,n},&
\widetilde Q_n&:=2^{-n}\widetilde W_n^2,\\
\widetilde D_n&:=\sum_{j\in A_n}\widetilde w_{j,n}^2,&
\widetilde R_n&:=\sum_{j\in A_n}\widetilde w_{j,n}\widetilde S_{j,n},\\
\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)
&:=\sum_{\substack{j\in A_n,\ v\in\mathbb Z^n\\
j+v,j-v\in A_n,\ v\ \mathrm{primitive}\\
|\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot v|>L}}
\widetilde w_{j+v,n}\widetilde w_{j-v,n},&
\widetilde G_n(L)&:=\widetilde T_n-\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)
\quad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\]

The quantities \(\widetilde w_{\cdot,n},\widetilde W_n,\widetilde S_{\cdot,n},\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n,\widetilde T_n,\widetilde D_n,\widetilde R_n,\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)\) are the respective auxiliary objects of accepted fact 3bd24ddc8a30dd00 with the subscript \(n\) added: log-affinity makes its midpoint-square summand equal to the endpoint product used here. The new symbols \(\widetilde Q_n\) and \(\widetilde G_n(L)\) abbreviate the baseline and retained difference displayed above. Thus the source quantities \(T_n,Q_n,D_n,R_n,G_n(L)\) are respectively \(\widetilde T_n,\widetilde Q_n,\widetilde D_n,\widetilde R_n,\widetilde G_n(L)\) here. The quantity \(\widetilde G_n(L)\) is retained product mass, not a Hellinger gain; \(\widetilde Q_n\) is the scalar parity baseline, not a quadratic form.

For the specialization \(w=\widetilde w_{\cdot,n}\) of accepted fact c382ad0f37a86dd8, one has
\(\Delta_j(v)=0\), \(\tau_j(v)=\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot v\), \(E_j=\widetilde S_{j,n}\), \(K(w)=\widetilde{\mathcal M}_n\), \(\mathscr R=\widetilde R_n\), \(D_w=\widetilde D_n\), \(P_{\mathrm{tot}}=\widetilde T_n\), and \(P_{\mathrm{bad}}(L)=\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)\). In particular, the source's \(T_n\) is \(P_{\mathrm{tot}}\) in that accepted fact. Its parity-collision scalar \(q\) is denoted here by \(q_{\mathrm{par},n}:=\widetilde T_n/\widetilde W_n^2\), and is distinct from the geometric ratio \(q_{\mathrm{geom}}\). Retain its \(\gamma(L):=\max\{4,2+e^L\}\).

For \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\), let \(\widetilde W_{n,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{z\in A_n,\ z\equiv\varepsilon\pmod2}\widetilde w_{z,n}\). There is an absolute constant \(C_*>0\) such that, for every \(n\ge4\) and every real \(L\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n}
\left|\frac{2^n\widetilde W_{n,\varepsilon}}{\widetilde W_n}-1\right|
&\le\frac2n,\\
1\le\frac{\widetilde T_n}{\widetilde Q_n}
&\le1+\frac2{n^3},

\le(2n^{-2})^n,\\
\frac{\widetilde G_n(L)}{\widetilde Q_n}
&\le C_*\left(\frac{L+1}{\sqrt n}+n^3\,2^{-n}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(\widetilde G_n(L_n)/\widetilde Q_n\to0\) for every sequence \(L_n\ge0\) with \(L_n=o(\sqrt n)\). With \(x_+:=\max\{x,0\}\), the following five quantities each equal \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\), with positive absolute upper and lower constants:

\[
\begin{aligned}

{(2+e^L)\widetilde Q_n},\\

\frac{\widetilde G_n(L)+(\gamma(L)-1)\widetilde D_n}
{\gamma(L)\widetilde Q_n},\\
&\sup_{L\ge0}
\frac{(\widetilde Q_n-\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)
+(\gamma(L)-1)\widetilde D_n)_+}

## Proof

The box \(P_n\) is compact and convex, and its lattice support is finite and nonempty. Any positive reflected pair has its integer midpoint in \(A_n\), so the reflected mass is zero outside \(A_n\). Log-affinity gives \(\widetilde w_{j+v,n}\widetilde w_{j-v,n}=\widetilde w_{j,n}^2\) whenever both endpoints belong to \(A_n\). The function equal to \(e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot z}\) on the real box and zero outside it is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension of the model table: interpolation within its convex positive support gives equality, and a zero endpoint makes the log-concavity inequality automatic. This checks the global-extension hypothesis of c382ad0f37a86dd8. Its zero-curvature specialization and every alias in the statement now follow directly from its definitions. In particular, \(K_j(w)=\widetilde S_{j,n}\), since every feasible curvature is zero and the cutoff denominator is smallest at zero.

First compute the parity masses, keeping the finite endpoint. Write
\(a_n:=q_{\mathrm{geom}}^{N+1}=e^{-n-n^{-2}}\),
\(E_n:=\sum_{0\le k\le N,\ k\ {\rm even}}q_{\mathrm{geom}}^k\),
\(O_n:=\sum_{0\le k\le N,\ k\ {\rm odd}}q_{\mathrm{geom}}^k\),
and \(\delta_n:=(E_n-O_n)/(E_n+O_n)\). The two finite geometric sums give

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_n+O_n&=\widetilde W_{1,n},\\
\delta_n
&=\frac{1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}}{1+q_{\mathrm{geom}}}
\frac{1-(-q_{\mathrm{geom}})^{N+1}}{1-a_n}
=\tanh(\beta/2)\,
\frac{1-(-1)^{N+1}a_n}{1-a_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Here \(a_n<1/32\): indeed \(n\ge4\) and the exponential series gives \(e^4>32\). The last fraction in (6) lies between one and \((1+a_n)/(1-a_n)<2\). Since \(0\le\tanh x\le x\) for \(x\ge0\), we obtain \(0\le\delta_n\le\beta\). For the more precise source expansion, \(0\le x-\tanh x=\int_0^x\tanh^2 t\,dt\le x^3/3\), and the last fraction in (6) differs from one by at most \(2a_n/(1-a_n)\). Therefore \(\delta_n=\beta/2+O(\beta^3+\beta a_n)\), with an absolute implied constant.

The product structure gives \(\widetilde W_n=\widetilde W_{1,n}^n\) and
\(2^n\widetilde W_{n,\varepsilon}/\widetilde W_n
=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+(-1)^{\varepsilon_i}\delta_n)\).
This product lies between \((1-\delta_n)^n\) and \((1+\delta_n)^n\). Bernoulli's inequality bounds its possible downward deviation from one by \(n\delta_n\). Its upward deviation is at most \(e^{n\delta_n}-1\le2n\delta_n\), because \(n\delta_n\le1/n\le1/4\) and \(e^t-1=\int_0^t e^s\,ds\le2t\) on that interval. This proves the first inequality of (1).

The map \((j,v)\mapsto(j+v,j-v)\) is a bijection onto ordered endpoint pairs of equal coordinatewise parity. Its inverse takes the integer midpoint and half-difference; the midpoint belongs to the convex box. It includes every diagonal exactly once. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde T_n
&=\sum_{\varepsilon}\widetilde W_{n,\varepsilon}^{\,2}
=(E_n^2+O_n^2)^n,\\
\frac{\widetilde T_n}{\widetilde Q_n}
&=(1+\delta_n^2)^n,\\
\frac{\widetilde D_n}{\widetilde Q_n}
&=\left[
2\frac{1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}}{1+q_{\mathrm{geom}}}
\frac{1+a_n}{1-a_n}
\right]^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The middle expression is between one and \(e^{n\beta^2}\le1+2n\beta^2=1+2/n^3\), using \(n\beta^2=n^{-3}\le1/64\) and the same exponential bound. The bracket in the last expression is at most \(2\beta\), because \(2\tanh(\beta/2)\le\beta\) and \((1+a_n)/(1-a_n)<2\). This proves both remaining mass estimates in (1). In particular, \(q_{\mathrm{par},n}=2^{-n}(1+\delta_n^2)^n\), with the stated distinction from \(q_{\mathrm{geom}}\).

We next construct the probability law used to estimate retained product mass. Start with independent endpoint coordinates \(X_i,Y_i\), each with geometric probabilities \((1-q_{\mathrm{geom}})q_{\mathrm{geom}}^k\), \(k\ge0\), and condition each pair on \(X_i\equiv Y_i\pmod2\). Denote the resulting law of all pairs by \(\mathbb P_\infty\). The \(n\) coordinate pairs are still independent, since the conditioning events involve disjoint independent coordinates. The half-displacements \(V_i:=(X_i-Y_i)/2\) are integers. For one coordinate, a fixed half-displacement \(k\) permits the midpoint to be any integer at least \(|k|\), with unnormalized product mass \(q_{\mathrm{geom}}^{2|k|}/(1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2)\). Summing this mass over all \(k\) gives \((1+q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2)/(1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2)^2\). Hence

\[
\mathbb P_\infty(V_i=k)
=\frac{1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2}{1+q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2}
q_{\mathrm{geom}}^{2|k|}
=\tanh(\beta)e^{-2\beta|k|}
\quad(k\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(\mathcal E_n\) be the event that all \(2n\) endpoint coordinates are at most \(N\). The law \(\mathbb P_n:=\mathbb P_\infty(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal E_n)\) is exactly the finite law that assigns probability \(\widetilde w_{j+v,n}\widetilde w_{j-v,n}/\widetilde T_n\) to every feasible ordered pair \((j,v)\). This follows by restricting the same product weights to finite equal-parity endpoint pairs, then using the bijection above. Thus no limiting support has replaced the finite support.

Before conditioning one coordinate pair on equal parity, each endpoint exceeds \(N\) with probability \(a_n\), and the equal-parity probability is
\((1+q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2)/(1+q_{\mathrm{geom}})^2\ge1/2\).
The conditional probability that either endpoint exceeds \(N\) is therefore at most \(4a_n\). The union bound gives
\(\mathbb P_\infty(\mathcal E_n^c)\le4na_n\).
The function \(t e^{-t}\) decreases for \(t\ge1\), so \(4na_n\le16e^{-4}<1/2\). In particular,
\(\mathbb P_\infty(\mathcal E_n)\ge1/2\), and conditioning multiplies the probability of any event, or the expectation of any nonnegative random variable, by at most two. The finite coordinate pairs remain independent as well, since \(\mathcal E_n\) is an intersection of their separate truncation events.

For every integer \(m\ge2\), summing (8) on \(m\mathbb Z\) gives

\[
\mathbb P_\infty(V_i\in m\mathbb Z)
=\tanh(\beta)\coth(m\beta)
\le\tanh(\beta)\coth(2\beta)
=\frac{1+\tanh^2\beta}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]

The inequality uses that \(\coth t\) decreases for \(t>0\); the equality on the right follows from the double-angle identity for \(\tanh\). Under the finite law, \(|V_i|\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\). A nonzero nonprimitive vector therefore has a common divisor between two and \(N\), and the zero vector belongs to \(2\mathbb Z^n\). Independence under \(\mathbb P_\infty\), the union bound over \(2\le m\le N\), and the conditioning estimate imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_n\{V=0\ \text{or }V\text{ is nonprimitive}\}
&\le2N\left(\frac{1+\tanh^2\beta}{2}\right)^n\\
&\le2n^3\,2^{-n}e^{n\beta^2}
\le4n^3\,2^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

This includes every nonprimitive direction and the entire diagonal.

We prove the required uniform small-ball upper bound. Put \(Z:=\sum_{i=1}^nV_i\). Summing the geometric series in (8), with \(s=e^{-2\beta}\), gives the one-coordinate characteristic function
\(\phi(\theta)=[(1-s)/(1+s)](1+2\sum_{k\ge1}s^k\cos(k\theta))
=(1-s)^2/(1-2s\cos\theta+s^2)
=\sinh^2\beta/(\sinh^2\beta+\sin^2(\theta/2))\).
It is nonnegative. Fourier inversion here follows directly by integrating the absolutely convergent probability series against \(e^{-ik\theta}\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\), whose integral annihilates each nonconstant integer exponential. Independence thus gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb P_\infty(Z=k)
&\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi(\theta)^n\,d\theta\\
&\le\frac{\sinh\beta}{2}
\int_{\mathbb R}(1+u^2)^{-n}\,du
\le\frac{\pi\beta}{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

For the second inequality, \(\sin(|\theta|/2)\ge|\theta|/\pi\) follows from concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/2]\); substitute \(u=\theta/(\pi\sinh\beta)\) and extend the resulting integral to \(\mathbb R\). For the last inequality, \(\sinh\beta\le2\beta\), since \(0\le\beta\le1\) and \(\sinh\beta=\int_0^\beta\cosh t\,dt\le\beta\cosh1<2\beta\). The elementary bound \(e<3\), obtained by comparison of the exponential series with a geometric series, implies \(\cosh1<2\).

For completeness, the remaining integral estimate is exact up to its final inequality. Substitution \(u=\tan t\) followed by integration by parts gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(1+u^2)^{-n}\,du
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^{2n-2}t\,dt
=\pi\frac{\binom{2n-2}{n-1}}{4^{n-1}}
\le\frac{\pi}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{12}
\]

Indeed, if \(c_m:=\binom{2m}{m}/4^m\), then \(c_0=1\) and \(c_{m+1}/c_m=(2m+1)/(2m+2)\). The inequality
\(((2m+1)/(2m+2))^2\le(m+1)/(m+2)\)
holds because clearing its positive denominators leaves \(3m+2\ge0\). Induction gives \(c_m\le1/\sqrt{m+1}\), proving (12).

Under the finite law, (11) gives the atom bound \(2\pi\beta/\sqrt n\). The identity \(|\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot V|=\beta|Z|\) holds exactly, and at most \(2L/\beta+1\) integer values satisfy \(|Z|\le L/\beta\). Therefore
\(\mathbb P_n\{|\boldsymbol\lambda_n\cdot V|\le L\}\le2\pi(2L+\beta)/\sqrt n\).
A retained pair either satisfies this balance condition or has zero or nonprimitive displacement. Its finite probability is exactly \(\widetilde G_n(L)/\widetilde T_n\). Combining this observation with (10) and \(\widetilde T_n/\widetilde Q_n\le2\) proves the last bound in (1), for example with \(C_*=8\pi+8\). Both terms there tend to zero when \(L_n=o(\sqrt n)\).

We now prove matching lower bounds, including the finite-law symmetry and variance facts needed for them. For an integer-valued random variable \(Y\) with a symmetric unimodal law, write \(p(k)=\mathbb P(Y=k)\); this means \(p(-k)=p(k)\) and \(p(k)\) is nonincreasing for \(k\ge0\). If \(Y\) has finite variance \(\sigma^2\), symmetry gives mean zero. For every integer \(u\ge0\),

\[
\mathbb P(|Y|\le u)\ge
\frac34\min\left\{1,\frac{2u+1}{4\sigma+3}\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]

If \(\sigma=0\), the law is concentrated at zero and the assertion holds. Otherwise let \(m=\lceil2\sigma\rceil\). Chebyshev's inequality gives \(\mathbb P(|Y|\le m)\ge3/4\), and \(2m+1\le4\sigma+3\). The average of the probabilities on a centered integer interval cannot increase when the interval grows: each newly added endpoint probability is at most every previously included probability. For \(u\le m\), this gives \(\mathbb P(|Y|\le u)\ge(2u+1)\mathbb P(|Y|\le m)/(2m+1)\); for \(u\ge m\), monotonicity gives probability at least \(3/4\). These two cases prove (13).

The finite one-coordinate law in \(\mathbb P_n\) has, at \(|k|\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), unnormalized mass
\((q_{\mathrm{geom}}^{2|k|}-q_{\mathrm{geom}}^{2(N-|k|+1)})/(1-q_{\mathrm{geom}}^2)\).
This is obtained by summing the midpoint from \(|k|\) to \(N-|k|\). For nonnegative \(k\) it decreases, since its first numerator term decreases and its subtracted term increases. It is zero beyond the displayed support. Thus it is symmetric unimodal.

Convolution preserves the finite symmetric unimodal property, as can be checked without a limit theorem. A finite symmetric unimodal probability sequence \(p\) is the mixture of uniform probabilities on \(\{-m,\ldots,m\}\) with coefficients \((2m+1)(p(m)-p(m+1))\), \(m\ge0\), where \(p\) is zero past its support. These coefficients are nonnegative, sum to one by telescoping, and recover \(p(k)\) by summing over \(m\ge|k|\). If \(p\) is any symmetric unimodal sequence, its convolution \(f\) with a uniform law on \(\{-m,\ldots,m\}\) is symmetric and satisfies
\(f(k)-f(k+1)=[p(k-m)-p(k+m+1)]/(2m+1)\ge0\) for \(k\ge0\), because \(|k-m|\le k+m+1\). Mixtures give the claim. The finite coordinate independence proved above therefore makes the law of \(Z\) symmetric unimodal.

Differentiating the geometric series twice gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}k^2s^k=s(1+s)/(1-s)^3\), \(0<s<1\). Applying this identity to (8), with \(s=e^{-2\beta}\), gives
\(\operatorname{Var}_\infty(V_i)=2s/(1-s)^2=1/(2\sinh^2\beta)\).
The mean is zero by symmetry, so independence yields \(\mathbb E_\infty Z^2=n/(2\sinh^2\beta)\). Conditioning on \(\mathcal E_n\) and using \(\sinh\beta\ge\beta\) now gives
\(\mathbb E_nZ^2\le n/\beta^2=nr^2\).
Applying (13) with \(u=r\) proves

\[
\mathbb P_n\{\beta|Z|\le1\}\ge\frac1{4\sqrt n}.
\tag{14}
\]

To check the stated constant, use \(\sigma\le r\sqrt n\) and
\((2r+1)/(4r\sqrt n+3)\ge1/(3\sqrt n)\), which follows from
\(3\sqrt n(2r+1)\ge4r\sqrt n+3\) for \(r\ge1\), \(n\ge1\). The other entry in the minimum in (13) is also at least \(1/(3\sqrt n)\).

Every pair in (14) is retained at \(L=1\), so
\(\widetilde G_n(1)/\widetilde Q_n\ge1/(4\sqrt n)\).
This proves the lower bound for the first quantity in (2), with constant \(1/[4(2+e)]\). The upper bound follows from (1) and \(1+L\le e^L\). The additional term \(n^3\,2^{-n}\) is \(o(n^{-1/2})\) and is also \(O(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly for \(n\ge4\): the sequence \(n^{7/2}2^{-n}\) is decreasing for \(n\ge8\), since its successive ratio is at most \((9/8)^4/2<1\), and its finitely many earlier values are bounded.

For the weighted reflected mass, log-affinity gives the exact identity
\(\widetilde w_{j,n}\min\{\widetilde w_{j+v,n},\widetilde w_{j-v,n}\}
=\widetilde w_{j+v,n}\widetilde w_{j-v,n}e^{-\beta|\sum_i v_i|}\).
After summation,

\[
\frac{\widetilde R_n}{\widetilde T_n}
=\mathbb E_n e^{-\beta|Z|}.
\tag{15}
\]

The lower bound (14) makes this at least \(1/(4e\sqrt n)\). The finite atom bound following (11) gives
\(\mathbb E_n e^{-\beta|Z|}
\le(2\pi\beta/\sqrt n)\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\beta|k|}
=(2\pi\beta/\sqrt n)\coth(\beta/2)\).
Since \(\coth(\beta/2)=1+2/(e^\beta-1)\le1+2/\beta\) and \(\beta\le1\), this is at most \(6\pi/\sqrt n\). Together with \(1\le\widetilde T_n/\widetilde Q_n\le2\), this proves the fifth estimate in (2).

It remains to verify all certificate corrections in (2), rather than replacing the exact resource by its baseline without accounting for the difference. The older numerator has the identity
\(\widetilde Q_n-\widetilde T_{\mathrm{bad},n}(L)+\widetilde D_n
=\widetilde G_n(L)-(\widetilde T_n-\widetilde Q_n)+\widetilde D_n\).
Its positive part is at most \(\widetilde G_n(L)+\widetilde D_n\). At \(L=1\), its ratio to \(\widetilde Q_n\) is at least
\(1/(4\sqrt n)-2/n^3\ge1/(8\sqrt n)\) for \(n\ge4\); the last inequality follows from \(n^{5/2}\ge16\). The diagonal upper bound in (1) is \(o(n^{-1/2})\). These observations prove both sides of the second estimate in (2).

For the newer coefficients,
\(2+e^L\le\gamma(L)\le(4/3)(2+e^L)\).

We prove the actual maximum comparison independently of those averaging certificates. Define the auxiliary Hellinger reflection sum

\tag{16}
\]

The one-coordinate factor is maximized at \(j_i=r\). To see this for every integer coordinate, if \(j_i>N/2\), replacing it by \(N-j_i\) preserves its count and increases its weight. \(\log((2r+1)/(2r-1))-1/r>0\).

\(\max_j\widetilde H_{j,n}=[(2r+1)e^{-1}]^n\), with zero value outside \(A_n\).
