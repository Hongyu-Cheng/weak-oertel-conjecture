## Statement

Suppose $1/2<b\le1$, $0\le a\le2b-1$, and $0\le c\le1$. Set
$$
h=[\min(b,c)-4(1-\max(b,c))/3]_+.
$$
Then
$$
\max\{(4b^2+7a^2)/9,\ (4+7h^2)/9\}\ge 2(a^2+b^2+1+c^2)/9.
$$

## Proof

When $c\ge b$, apply e9da2489a6c65517 at $d=2$. Its clipped term is $[(3/2)b+2c-2]_+=(3/2)h$. It gives $1+(9/4)h^2\ge (2b-1)^2+b^2+c^2$, so
$$
4/9+h^2/2\ge 2[(2b-1)^2+b^2+1+c^2]/9.
$$
Since $7/9\ge1/2$ and $a\le2b-1$, the second branch suffices.

Suppose $c\le b$ and the first branch misses. Multiplying by nine gives $a^2<(2/5)(1+c^2-b^2)$. Along with $a^2\le(2b-1)^2$ this bounds the target by the minimum of
$$
\frac29[(2b-1)^2+b^2+1+c^2],\qquad \frac{14(1+c^2)+6b^2}{45}.
$$
Comparison of the second branch against either expression is equivalent, respectively, to
$$
7h^2\ge R_1:=10b^2-8b+2c^2,\qquad 35h^2\ge R_2:=14c^2+6b^2-6.
$$
Set $e=1-b$ and note $c\le h+4e/3$, including $h=0$. Substituting this upper bound on $c$ yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
9(7h^2-R_1)&\ge45(h-8e/15)^2+108e-(674/5)e^2-18,\\
9(35h^2-R_2)&\ge189(h-8e/9)^2+e(108-(1282/3)e).
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0\le e\le1/4$, the bracket on the second line is at least $7/6$, giving the second comparison. For $1/4\le e\le3/7$, the final quadratic on the first line is concave, with endpoint values $23/40$ and $864/245$, giving the first. If $3/7\le e<1/2$, then $c\le b\le4/7$ and $R_1\le12b^2-8b\le0$. These intervals cover the domain. The comparisons also hold at $a=0$ or $c=0$ because the proof uses nonnegative variables throughout.
