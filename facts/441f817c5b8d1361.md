## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
\]
and assume \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\neq\varnothing\). For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), set
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\},
\]
and define
\[
h_{\max}(C):=
\sup_{y\in S}\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
For \(z\in Z\), let
\[
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
\]
and, for \(0<t\leq a_z\), use the canonical metronoid
\[
\mathsf M_t(C_z):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_{C_z}x f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_{C_z}f(x)\,dx=t
\right\}.
\]
Set
\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z,
\]
and let \(\|\cdot\|_{D_z}\) denote its Minkowski functional. With \(r_+:=\max\{r,0\}\), define
\[
\Delta_{\rm fib}(C):=
\min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{affine}}
\sum_{z\in Z}a_z
\bigl(\|s(z)-c_z\|_{D_z}-1\bigr)_+.
\tag{1}
\]

For completeness, for nonempty \(E\subseteq Z\), positive masses \(0<t_z\leq a_z\), and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), write
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z z=m\right\},
\]
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]
The metronoid-core capacity is
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C):=
\sup\left\{
\sum_{z\in E}t_z:
\begin{array}{l}
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq Z,\quad 0<t_z\leq a_z,\\
\text{there is }m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E
\text{ with }\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\neq\varnothing,\\
\displaystyle
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z\geq
2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
\text{ for every proper closed halfspace }J\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ containing }m
\end{array}
\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
where the supremum of the empty set is zero.

The minimum in (1) is attained. For every \(0<p<1/2\), put
\[
\delta_p:=
\frac{-(1-p)\log(1-p)/p}{\log2}-1.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(\delta_p>0\) and
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\geq
p\left(W-\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)}{\delta_p}\right)_+,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\gamma_d p\,2^{-n}
\left(W-\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)}{\delta_p}\right)_+.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\geq
\frac14\bigl(W-5\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)\bigr)_+.
\tag{6}
\]

If there is an affine map \(s_0:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) such that
\[
c_z=s_0(z)\qquad(z\in Z),
\]
then
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)=W.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \((C_j)\) be any sequence of such bodies, allowing \(n_j\) and \(d_j\) to vary, and assume \(W_j>0\). If
\[
H_j:=\frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{8}
\]
then
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}
\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C_j)}{W_j}
\geq\frac1{\log2}-1.
\tag{9}
\]

There is also the following exact calibration. For \(k\geq3\), put
\[
\rho:=\frac{19}{20},\qquad R_k:=2^{k^3},
\qquad
f_k(z):=1-\frac{15}{R_k}\bigl(\|z\|_2-\rho R_k\bigr)_+
\quad(z\in R_kB_2^k),
\]
and define the scalar mixed convex body
\[
C_k^{\rm ng}:=
\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R:
\|z\|_2\leq R_k,\quad |x|\leq\frac12f_k(z)\right\}.
\]
For \(z\in R_kB_2^k\cap\mathbb Z^k\), set
\[
a_{k,z}:=f_k(z),\qquad
W_k^{\rm ng}:=\sum_z a_{k,z},\qquad
Q_k(t):=\sum_z\left(a_{k,z}-\frac t2\right)_+.
\]
Also put
\[
\tau:=\frac{2953}{2997},\qquad
\varepsilon_k:=8\left(\frac{\sqrt k}{R_k-\sqrt k/2}\right)^k,
\qquad
c_k^{\rm ng}:=\max\{\varepsilon_k,4\tau^k\}.
\]
Then \(c_k^{\rm ng}\to0\), and
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_k^{\rm ng})=W_k^{\rm ng},
\qquad
\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\min\{2^{-k}Q_k(t),t\}}
{2^{-k}\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_k^{\rm ng})}
\leq\frac{c_k^{\rm ng}}2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

If \(W=0\), then \(Z=\varnothing\), the objective in (1) is the empty sum and is attained by every affine map, the right sides of (4)-(6) vanish, and (7) is \(0=0\). Thus suppose \(W>0\). Accepted fact 107b9182522633f9 gives that \(Z\) is finite. Accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b gives that every \(D_z\) is an origin-symmetric convex body, so \(\|\cdot\|_{D_z}\) is a norm.

We first prove attainment without requiring \(Z\) to affinely span \(\mathbb R^n\). In the finite-dimensional space
\[
Y:=(\mathbb R^d)^Z
\]
define
\[
V:=\{(s(z))_{z\in Z}:s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\text{ is affine}\},
\qquad
L:=\prod_{z\in Z}(c_z+D_z),
\]
and equip \(Y\) with the norm
\[
N((u_z)_{z\in Z}):=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z\|u_z\|_{D_z}.
\]
The evaluation set \(V\) is a linear subspace of \(Y\), hence is closed, even when the affine span of \(Z\) has dimension less than \(n\). The set \(L\) is compact. For every \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), the triangle inequality and radial projection onto \(c_z+D_z\) give
\[
\min_{\ell\in c_z+D_z}\|y-\ell\|_{D_z}
=\bigl(\|y-c_z\|_{D_z}-1\bigr)_+.
\tag{11}
\]
Coordinatewise minimization therefore gives
\[
\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)=
\inf_{v\in V,\ \ell\in L}N(v-\ell).
\tag{12}
\]
For fixed \(\ell\), the function \(v\mapsto N(v-\ell)\) is coercive on \(V\), since it is at least \(N(v)-N(\ell)\), and hence attains its minimum. The distance \(\ell\mapsto\operatorname{dist}_N(\ell,V)\) is continuous, indeed \(1\)-Lipschitz, and thus attains its minimum on compact \(L\). Consequently the infimum in (12) is attained by some \(v_*\in V\) and \(\ell_*\in L\). Choosing an affine map \(s_*\) whose evaluation vector is \(v_*\) proves attainment in (1). This argument also covers a singleton or any other affinely deficient support.

Set
\[
\psi(r):=-\frac{1-r}{r}\log(1-r)\qquad(0<r<1).
\]
One has
\[
\psi'(r)=\frac{r+\log(1-r)}{r^2}<0,
\tag{13}
\]
because \(\log(1-r)<-r\). Since \(\psi(1/2)=\log2\), equations (3) and (13) show that
\[
1+\delta_p=\frac{\psi(p)}{\log2}>1
\qquad(0<p<1/2),
\tag{14}
\]
so \(\delta_p>0\).

For this fixed \(p\), put
\[
e_z:=\bigl(\|s_*(z)-c_z\|_{D_z}-1\bigr)_+,
\qquad
E:=\{z\in Z:e_z\leq\delta_p\},
\qquad
W_E:=\sum_{z\in E}a_z.
\]
Every bad index has \(e_z>\delta_p\), so
\[
\delta_p\sum_{z\in Z\setminus E}a_z
\leq\sum_{z\in Z\setminus E}a_ze_z
\leq\Delta_{\rm fib}(C).
\]
Hence
\[
W_E\geq W-\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)}{\delta_p}.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(z\in E\), the definition of \(e_z\) gives
\[
s_*(z)\in c_z+(1+\delta_p)D_z.
\tag{16}
\]
Apply the nested-metronoid inclusion of accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b in \(C_z\) with its parameters \(q=1/2\) and the present \(p\). Since \(D_z=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z\), equations (14) and (16) give
\[
s_*(z)\in
c_z+\frac{\psi(p)}{\psi(1/2)}
\bigl(\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z\bigr)
\subseteq\mathsf M_{pa_z}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in E).
\tag{17}
\]

If the right side of (15) is positive, then \(E\neq\varnothing\). Accepted fact 107b9182522633f9, applied to the retained weights \((a_z)_{z\in E}\), supplies a point
\[
m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\geq2^{-n}W_E.
\tag{18}
\]
Set \(t_z:=pa_z\) for \(z\in E\). These masses are strictly positive and at most \(a_z\). For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), affineness of \(s_*\) and (17) give
\[
s_*(m)=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zs_*(z)
\in\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]
Thus
\[
s_*(m)\in\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t),
\tag{19}
\]
and multiplying (18) by \(p\) gives the balance condition in (2). Hence this is an admissible configuration in the metronoid-core capacity of accepted fact ae0161dd5d4ea672, with total mass \(pW_E\). By (15),
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\geq pW_E
\geq p\left(W-\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)}{\delta_p}\right).
\]
If the right side of (15) is nonpositive, (4) follows instead from \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq0\). This also handles the case \(E=\varnothing\), and proves the positive-part form (4). The master bound of accepted fact ae0161dd5d4ea672,
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\]
then proves (5).

For \(p=1/4\), the explicit coefficient in accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b gives
\[
1+\delta_{1/4}
=\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}>\frac65,
\]
so \(\delta_{1/4}>1/5\). Therefore
\[
\left(W-\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)}{\delta_{1/4}}\right)_+
\geq\bigl(W-5\Delta_{\rm fib}(C)\bigr)_+,
\]
and (6) follows from (4).

We next prove the saturation statement. Every admissible configuration in (2) has
\[
\sum_{z\in E}t_z\leq\sum_{z\in E}a_z\leq W,
\]
so \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\leq W\). Suppose \(c_z=s_0(z)\) on \(Z\). For each \(z\in Z\),
\[
\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)=\{c_z\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, an admissible density of mass \(a_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\) must equal \(1\) almost everywhere, and conversely this density realizes \(c_z\). Accepted fact 107b9182522633f9, now applied to \(E=Z\) and weights \(a_z\), gives an integral point \(m\in\operatorname{conv}Z\) satisfying the required \(2^{-n}\) balance. For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(Z)\), affineness and (20) give
\[
\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)
=\left\{\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_zc_z\right\}
=\left\{\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_zs_0(z)\right\}
=\{s_0(m)\}.
\]
Thus the full-mass core is the nonempty singleton \(\{s_0(m)\}\), so \(E=Z\) and \(t_z=a_z\) are admissible in (2) and give \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq W\). This proves (7), including affinely deficient \(Z\).

It remains to prove (9). For the \(j\)-th body, abbreviate
\[
r_j:=\frac{\Delta_{\rm fib}(C_j)}{W_j},
\qquad
\gamma_j:=\left(\frac{d_j}{d_j+1}\right)^{d_j}.
\]
Fix any \(p\in(0,1/2)\). Dividing (5) by \(2^{-n_j}W_j\) gives
\[
H_j\geq\gamma_jp
\left(1-\frac{r_j}{\delta_p}\right)_+.
\tag{21}
\]
For every positive integer \(d\),
\[
d\log(1+1/d)<1,
\]
and consequently
\[
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d>e^{-1}.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(r_j\geq\delta_p\), then directly, and if \(r_j<\delta_p\), then by rearranging (21), equations (21)-(22) give in both cases
\[
r_j\geq\delta_p\left(1-\frac{eH_j}{p}\right).
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(H_j\to0\), it follows that
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}r_j\geq\delta_p
\qquad\text{for every }p\in(0,1/2).
\tag{24}
\]
Finally,
\[
\lim_{p\downarrow0}\psi(p)=1,
\qquad
\lim_{p\downarrow0}\delta_p=\frac1{\log2}-1.
\]
Letting \(p\downarrow0\) in (24) proves (9).

Finally, \(C_k^{\rm ng}\) is exactly the scalar no-go family denoted \(C_k\) in accepted fact 00b950e061f1b2ef. Its positive lattice fibers are the centered intervals
\[
\left[-\frac12a_{k,z},\frac12a_{k,z}\right],
\]
so every positive-fiber centroid is zero. Applying (7) with \(s_0\equiv0\) gives
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_k^{\rm ng})=W_k^{\rm ng}.
\]
The same accepted fact 00b950e061f1b2ef proves \(c_k^{\rm ng}\to0\), the identity \(|\eta_k|=W_k^{\rm ng}/2\), and
\[
\sup_{t>0}\min\{2^{-k}Q_k(t),t\}
\leq c_k^{\rm ng}\,2^{-k}|\eta_k|.
\]
Substituting \(|\eta_k|=W_k^{\rm ng}/2=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_k^{\rm ng})/2\) proves (10).
