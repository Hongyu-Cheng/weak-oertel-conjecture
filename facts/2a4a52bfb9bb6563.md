## Statement

Let $K$ be a full-dimensional $d$-polytope, $d\ge2$. Let $n\ne0$ expose a face of dimension $k\le d-2$, and set $K(t)=K\cap\{n\cdot x\le h_K(n)-t\}$. For small $t\ge0$,
$$
h_{K(t)}(n)=h_K(n)-t,\qquad |K\setminus K(t)|=O(t^{d-k})=o(t).
$$
Uniformly for $m$ near $n$ and $t$ small, $h_K(m)-h_{K(t)}(m)\ge t(1-C\lVert m-n\rVert)$ for some $C$. Under nearby affine body motions and matched nearby directions this bound is $t-O(t|p|)$, where $p$ denotes the nearby parameters. Finitely many simultaneous cuts of this kind, with nonnegative depths $\sigma$, change volume and every halfspace cap volume by $o(|\sigma|)$, uniformly for nearby affine body motions and all caps.

## Proof

Triangulate $K$ into full-dimensional simplices using its vertices. If a simplex has $f+1$ vertices on the exposed face, with $f\le k$, the deficit $h_K(n)-n\cdot x$ is a barycentric sum of strictly positive constants times the $d-f$ coordinates of its off-face vertices. Each such coordinate is $O(t)$ in the discarded part, whose volume is therefore $O(t^{d-f})$. Simplices without an on-face vertex have a positive gap and contribute nothing for small $t$. Summing proves the volume bound. The new threshold is attained for small $t$, by a segment between a point of the exposed face and an interior point.

Near $n$, every maximizing old vertex is on the exposed face: other vertices have a strictly positive gap at $n$, and there are finitely many. Each new vertex of $K(t)$ lies on the cut plane on an old edge with one endpoint $v$ on the face and one outside. It is $v+t a$ for some $a$ in a finite list with $n\cdot a=-1$. At such a vertex, $m\cdot(v+t a)\le h_K(m)-t(1-C\lVert m-n\rVert)$. Old off-face vertices retain a uniform positive gap for nearby $m$, so they also satisfy this bound for small $t$. This gives the uniform support estimate.

For an affine motion $x\mapsto L_p x+b_p$, the support difference in a moved direction is evaluated at its pullback $L_p^\top m$, which is $n+O(|p|)$ in a smooth nearby chart. The volume estimate is multiplied by a locally bounded determinant. With finitely many simultaneous cuts, the union of removed parts has volume at most the sum of the single-cut losses, hence $o(|\sigma|)$. In each cap the lost subset lies in the same removed union, giving the uniform cap estimate.
