## Statement

For every integer \(n\geq2\), use the lattice-ball base from accepted fact
`3316a7b28eca6a19`:
\[
 R_n:=2^{n^3},\qquad
 B_n:=R_nB_2^n,\qquad
 D_n:=B_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
 N_n:=|D_n|.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(d_n,k_n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), where
\[
 \frac{k_n}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
For each \(n\), let
\(C_n\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n}\) be a convex body whose
base projection is \(B_n\).  Suppose there are an open set
\(U_n\subseteq\mathbb R^{k_n}\), a point
\(\theta_n^0\in U_n\), a linear map
\(A_n:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^{k_n}\), and a family of
\(d_n\)-dimensional convex bodies
\[
 \{K_{n,\theta}:\theta\in U_n\}
\]
such that, writing \((C_n)_x:=\{y\in\mathbb R^{d_n}:(x,y)\in C_n\}\),
the actual fibers have the exact identity
\[
 (C_n)_x
 =K_{n,\theta_n(x)},\qquad
 \theta_n(x):=\theta_n^0+A_n\frac{x}{R_n}
 \quad(x\in B_n).
\tag{3}
\]

Assume the following uniform hypotheses.  There are constants
\[
 M,\rho,a_-,a_+,\sigma,L_1,L_2>0
\tag{4}
\]
independent of \(n\), \(d_n\), and \(k_n\), such that
\[
 \|A_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq M,
 \qquad
 \theta_n^0+(M+\rho)B_2^{k_n}\subseteq U_n.
\tag{5}
\]
For every
\(\theta\in\theta_n^0+(M+\rho)B_2^{k_n}\), put
\[
 a_n(\theta):=\operatorname{vol}_{d_n}(K_{n,\theta}),
\qquad
 c_n(\theta):=\frac1{a_n(\theta)}\int_{K_{n,\theta}}y\,dy,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
 \Sigma_n(\theta)
 :=\frac1{a_n(\theta)}
 \int_{K_{n,\theta}}
 (y-c_n(\theta))(y-c_n(\theta))^{\mathsf T}\,dy.
\tag{7}
\]
The volume and covariance bounds are
\[
 a_-\leq a_n(\theta)\leq a_+,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_n(\theta)\succeq\sigma^2I_{d_n}.
\tag{8}
\]
The centroid map is \(C^2\) on \(U_n\), and, throughout the same parameter
neighborhood,
\[
 \|Dc_n(\theta)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_1,
 \qquad
 \|D^2c_n(\theta)[u,v]\|_2
 \leq L_2\|u\|_2\|v\|_2
\tag{9}
\]
for all \(u,v\in\mathbb R^{k_n}\).

For \(z\in D_n\), abbreviate
\[
 \theta_{n,z}:=\theta_n(z),\qquad
 a_{n,z}:=a_n(\theta_{n,z}),\qquad
 c_{n,z}:=c_n(\theta_{n,z}),\qquad
 \Sigma_{n,z}:=\Sigma_n(\theta_{n,z}),
\tag{10}
\]
and put
\[
 W_n:=\sum_{z\in D_n}a_{n,z}.
\tag{11}
\]
Define the affine Taylor selector
\[
 s_n(x)
 :=c_n(\theta_n^0)
 +Dc_n(\theta_n^0)A_n\frac{x}{R_n}
 \qquad(x\in\mathbb R^n).
\tag{12}
\]

Fix any constant \(\eta>0\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{L_2\eta^2}{2\sigma}\leq\frac1{128},
\tag{13}
\]
and set
\[
 E_n:=\{z\in D_n:\|\theta_{n,z}-\theta_n^0\|_2\leq\eta\},
 \qquad
 \beta_n:=\frac1{W_n}\sum_{z\in D_n\setminus E_n}a_{n,z}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 \frac1{N_n}\sum_{z\in D_n}
 \|\theta_{n,z}-\theta_n^0\|_2^2
 \leq\frac{k_nM^2}{n},
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 0\leq\beta_n
 \leq\frac{a_+}{a_-}\frac{k_nM^2}{n\eta^2}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover, for every \(z\in E_n\),
\[
 \sqrt{
 (s_n(z)-c_{n,z})^{\mathsf T}
 \Sigma_{n,z}^{-1}
 (s_n(z)-c_{n,z})}
 \leq\frac1{128}.
\tag{17}
\]
Let
\[
 F_n:=\left\{z\in D_n:
 (s_n(z)-c_{n,z})^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{n,z}^{-1}
 (s_n(z)-c_{n,z})\leq1/128^2\right\}.
\]
Then \(E_n\subseteq F_n\), and consequently
\[
 \sum_{z\in F_n}a_{n,z}
 \geq(1-\beta_n)W_n
 =W_n-o(W_n).
\tag{18}
\]

Let \(G(C_n)\) and \(R_{\rm sel}(C_n)\) have the exact meanings in accepted
fact `876a152563efe052`.  Then
\[
 \frac{G(C_n)}{W_n}\geq\frac{1-\beta_n}{2}
 =\frac12-o(1),
\tag{19}
\]
and, with
\(\gamma_{d_n}:=(d_n/(d_n+1))^{d_n}>e^{-1}\),
\[
 \frac{R_{\rm sel}(C_n)}{W_n}
 \geq\frac{\gamma_{d_n}}2(1-\beta_n)
 \geq\frac1{2e}-o(1).
\tag{20}
\]

Finally, let \(\mathcal Q_n\) be the set of all nonzero off-diagonal
fractional same-parity matchings \(q=(q_e)\) on \(D_n\) satisfying the free
endpoint bounds
\[
 d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\leq\frac{a_{n,z}}2
 \qquad(z\in D_n).
\tag{21}
\]
For \(q\in\mathcal Q_n\), let \(\Delta_{C_n}(q)\) be the weighted affine
defect from accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`.  Put
\[
 E_*:=\min\left\{2L_1M,\frac{L_2M^2}{2}\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Then the bound is uniform over every such matching:
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q_n}
 \frac{\Delta_{C_n}(q)}{W_n}
 \leq\frac{64E_*}{\sigma}\,\beta_n
 \longrightarrow0.}
\tag{23}
\]

## Proof

The exact fiber identity (3) is an assumption of the theorem.  In
particular, no first-order support profile is being integrated or promoted to
an actual fiber family in this proof.

The lattice ball \(D_n\) is invariant under every coordinate sign change and
every coordinate permutation.  Hence its unnormalized second-moment matrix
has the form
\[
 \sum_{z\in D_n}zz^{\mathsf T}=\lambda_nI_n
\tag{24}
\]
for some \(\lambda_n\geq0\).  Taking traces and using
\(\|z\|_2\leq R_n\) gives
\[
 n\lambda_n
 =\sum_{z\in D_n}\|z\|_2^2
 \leq N_nR_n^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1{N_n}\sum_{z\in D_n}
 \|\theta_{n,z}-\theta_n^0\|_2^2
 &=\frac1{N_nR_n^2}
 \sum_{z\in D_n}\|A_nz\|_2^2\\
 &=\frac{\lambda_n}{N_nR_n^2}\|A_n\|_{\mathrm F}^2\\
 &\leq\frac1n\|A_n\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \leq\frac{k_n}{n}\|A_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 \leq\frac{k_nM^2}{n}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
This proves (15).

Markov's inequality applied to (26) yields
\[
 \frac{|D_n\setminus E_n|}{N_n}
 \leq\frac{k_nM^2}{n\eta^2}.
\tag{27}
\]
The volume bounds in (8) give
\[
 \sum_{z\notin E_n}a_{n,z}
 \leq a_+|D_n\setminus E_n|,
 \qquad
 W_n\geq a_-N_n.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27)-(28) proves (16), including \(\beta_n\to0\) by (2).

Fix \(z\in D_n\) and write
\[
 h_{n,z}:=\theta_{n,z}-\theta_n^0=A_nz/R_n.
\]
Equations (5) and \(\|z\|_2\leq R_n\) give
\(\|h_{n,z}\|_2\leq M\).  Thus the whole segment from
\(\theta_n^0\) to \(\theta_{n,z}\) lies in the common neighborhood in
(5).  Taylor's formula with integral remainder and (9) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|c_{n,z}-s_n(z)\|_2
 &\leq\int_0^1(1-t)
 \|D^2c_n(\theta_n^0+th_{n,z})[h_{n,z},h_{n,z}]\|_2\,dt\\
 &\leq\frac{L_2}{2}\|h_{n,z}\|_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
For \(z\in E_n\), (8), (13), and (29) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &(s_n(z)-c_{n,z})^{\mathsf T}
 \Sigma_{n,z}^{-1}(s_n(z)-c_{n,z})\\
 &\qquad\leq\frac1{\sigma^2}\|s_n(z)-c_{n,z}\|_2^2
 \leq\left(\frac{L_2\eta^2}{2\sigma}\right)^2
 \leq\frac1{128^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (17).  Since \(E_n\subseteq F_n\), equations (14) and (16)
prove (18).

Apply accepted fact `876a152563efe052` to the affine map \(s_n\).  Its
covariance-near set is \(F_n\), which contains \(E_n\), so its complete-core
conclusion gives
\[
 G(C_n)\geq\frac12\sum_{z\in F_n}a_{n,z}
 \geq\frac12\sum_{z\in E_n}a_{n,z}
 =\frac{1-\beta_n}{2}W_n.
\tag{31}
\]
This proves (19).  Conclusion (20) follows immediately from nonnegativity if
\(F_n=\varnothing\),
because then \(E_n=\varnothing\) and \(\beta_n=1\).  Suppose now that
\(F_n\ne\varnothing\), and let \(\rho_n'\leq n\) be its affine dimension.
The residual-selector conclusion of that fact gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{\rm sel}(C_n)
 &\geq2^{n-\rho_n'}\frac{\gamma_{d_n}}2
 \sum_{z\in F_n}a_{n,z}\\
 &\geq\frac{\gamma_{d_n}}2(1-\beta_n)W_n.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Here no full-affine-rank assertion is needed, because
\(2^{n-\rho_n'}\geq1\).  Finally,
\(\gamma_d=(1+1/d)^{-d}>e^{-1}\) for every positive integer \(d\), so
(20) follows.

It remains to prove the uniform matching assertion.  For \(z\in D_n\), use
the notation
\[
 \mathcal K_{n,z}:=\mathsf M_{a_{n,z}/2}((C_n)_z),
 \qquad
 D_{n,z}:=\mathcal K_{n,z}-c_{n,z}.
\tag{33}
\]
For an edge \(e=\{z,z'\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{z+z'}2,\qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_{n,z}+c_{n,z'}}2,\qquad
 D_{n,e}:=\frac{D_{n,z}+D_{n,z'}}2,\qquad
 L_{n,e}:=\bar c_e+D_{n,e}.
\tag{34}
\]

If \(z\in E_n\), (17) and accepted fact `876a152563efe052` give
\[
 s_n(z)\in\mathcal K_{n,z}.
\tag{35}
\]
Consequently, if both endpoints of \(e\) lie in \(E_n\), affineness gives
\[
 s_n(m_e)=\frac{s_n(z)+s_n(z')}2
 \in\frac{\mathcal K_{n,z}+\mathcal K_{n,z'}}2
 =L_{n,e}.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus every good-good edge has zero primal defect at \(s_n\).

We next bound the gauge on every remaining edge.  The derivative bound in
(9), integrated along the same parameter segment, and the definition of
\(s_n\) give
\[
 \|s_n(z)-c_{n,z}\|_2
 \leq
 \|Dc_n(\theta_n^0)h_{n,z}\|_2
 +\|c_n(\theta_n^0+h_{n,z})-c_n(\theta_n^0)\|_2
 \leq2L_1M.
\tag{37}
\]
Equation (29) also gives
\[
 \|s_n(z)-c_{n,z}\|_2\leq\frac{L_2M^2}{2}.
\tag{38}
\]
Hence
\[
 \|s_n(z)-c_{n,z}\|_2\leq E_*
 \qquad(z\in D_n).
\tag{39}
\]

The covariance ellipsoid inclusion in accepted fact
`876a152563efe052`, together with (8), yields
\[
 D_{n,z}\supseteq\frac1{128}\Sigma_{n,z}^{1/2}B_2^{d_n}
 \supseteq\frac{\sigma}{128}B_2^{d_n}.
\tag{40}
\]
Therefore \(D_{n,e}\supseteq(\sigma/128)B_2^{d_n}\), and its Minkowski
norm satisfies
\[
 \|y\|_{D_{n,e}}\leq\frac{128}{\sigma}\|y\|_2.
\tag{41}
\]
Using affineness once more and then (39)-(41),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_{n,e}}(s_n(m_e),L_{n,e})
 &\leq\|s_n(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_{n,e}}\\
 &=\left\|\frac{(s_n(z)-c_{n,z})+(s_n(z')-c_{n,z'})}{2}
 \right\|_{D_{n,e}}\\
 &\leq\frac{128E_*}{\sigma}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
This is the required uniform bad-edge gauge bound; it is independent of the
matching, \(n\), \(d_n\), and \(k_n\).

Fix \(q\in\mathcal Q_n\).  Evaluate the primal minimization defining
\(\Delta_{C_n}(q)\) in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` at \(s_n\).
Equations (36) and (42) give
\[
 \Delta_{C_n}(q)
 \leq\frac{128E_*}{\sigma}
 \sum_{e:\,e\cap(D_n\setminus E_n)\ne\varnothing}q_e.
\tag{43}
\]
Every edge in the last sum is incident to at least one bad vertex.  Hence,
using the endpoint bounds (21),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{e:\,e\cap(D_n\setminus E_n)\ne\varnothing}q_e
 &\leq\sum_{z\in D_n\setminus E_n}d_z(q)\\
 &\leq\frac12\sum_{z\in D_n\setminus E_n}a_{n,z}
 =\frac{\beta_nW_n}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
Combining (43)-(44), dividing by \(W_n\), and taking the supremum over
\(q\in\mathcal Q_n\) proves (23).

The proof uses the exact affine parameterization (3) throughout.  It does not
assert that a first-order volume-neutral deformation integrates to (3), and
its conclusions do not imply a mode lower bound for families outside this
exact affine-parameter class.
