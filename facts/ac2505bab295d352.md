## Statement

Let $d\ge2$. On $d+1$ indices take positive $a_i,b_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$, with $\sum_i a_i=1$ and $\sum_{i\ne j}b_{ij}=a_j$. Set $f_i=d[\prod_{j\ne i}(a_i+b_{ij})]^{1/d}$. For every $i$,
$$
f_i+(d-1)\left(\frac{\sum_{j\ne i}f_j}{d}\right)^{d/(d-1)}\le d.
$$
Write $\bar f_j$ for the values after simultaneously averaging the rows and columns over permutations fixing $i$, and put $g=\sum_{j\ne i}f_j$, $\bar g=\sum_{j\ne i}\bar f_j$, $p=d/(d-1)$. The deficit is exactly
$$
d-f_i-(d-1)(g/d)^p
=(\bar f_i-f_i)+(d-1)\big[(\bar g/d)^p-(g/d)^p\big],
$$
with two nonnegative terms. Equality holds exactly when there are $A,B>0$ with $a_i=A$, $b_{ij}=B$ for $j\ne i$, and
$$
a_j+b_{ji}=1/d\quad(j\ne i),\qquad
a_j+b_{jk}=(1-A-B)/(d-1)>0\quad(k\ne i,j,\ j\ne i).
$$
For every prescribed $f_i\in(0,d)$ equality occurs for the normalized vertex evaluations of actual strict distinct-vertex simplex caps with interior common mark and nonzero spanning normals summing to zero.

## Proof

Simultaneous permutations preserve the positive matrix conditions. Fix $i$ and write $A=a_i$, $B=d^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}b_{ij}$, $t=(1-A)/d$ and $c=(t-B)/(d-1)$. The averaged matrix has selected diagonal $A$ and selected off-diagonal row entries $B$. The other diagonals are $t$, the other entries in column $i$ are $A/d$, and the remaining off-diagonal entries are $c$. In particular $c>0$ by strict positivity of the averaged matrix. Thus $\bar f_i=d(A+B)$, and each other averaged row has channel $t+A/d=1/d$ toward $i$, and $t+c=(1-A-B)/(d-1)$ toward every other column distinct from its own. Consequently
$$
\bar g=d\left(\frac{d-\bar f_i}{d-1}\right)^{(d-1)/d}.
$$
The quantity $d-\bar f_i$ is positive since $A<1$ and $B<t$.

The geometric mean $G(x)=(\prod_{\ell=1}^d x_\ell)^{1/d}$ for positive vectors has the representation
$$
G(x)=\inf\{d^{-1}\textstyle\sum_\ell r_\ell x_\ell:r_\ell>0,\ \prod_\ell r_\ell=1\}.
$$
AM–GM proves this formula, with the unique optimizer $r_\ell=G(x)/x_\ell$. It gives concavity, and equality under averaging holds exactly when all positive vectors averaged are proportional: the optimizer of the average must attain equality at every vector. Applying concavity to each row gives $\bar f_i\ge f_i$ and $\bar g\ge g$, since the permutations preserve the sum of nonselected row values. The formula for $\bar g$ proves both the deficit decomposition and the inequality.

If equality holds, the selected row is constant off its diagonal because all its permuted channel vectors are proportional and have the same sum. In each other row $j$, permutations fixing $i,j$ force all channels away from $i$ to have one value $Q_j$, also true when there is only one such channel. Comparison of vectors in the averaging of an other row, aligning the channel toward $i$, shows that the ratio $(a_j+b_{ji})/Q_j$ is independent of $j$. For column $j\ne i$, balance gives
$$
a_j=B+\sum_{\ell\ne i,j}(Q_\ell-a_\ell),
\qquad
Q_j=B+\sum_{\ell\ne i}Q_\ell-1+A.
$$
Thus $Q_j=Q$ is common, and so is $a_j+b_{ji}=P$. Column $i$ gives $A=dP-(1-A)$, or $P=1/d$. The displayed relation for $Q_j$ now gives $(d-1)Q=1-A-B$. Conversely, the stated channel values make the vectors within each averaging identical, and hence give equality.

To realize every frontier value $x\in(0,d)$, relabel the selected index as $0$ and take
$$
\max(0,(x-1)/(d-1))<A<x/d,\qquad
B=x/d-A,\quad t=(1-A)/d,\quad c=(t-B)/(d-1).
$$
The interval is nonempty because $x<d$ and these choices give $A,B,t,c>0$. In $\mathbb R^d$, let $v_j=e_j$ for $1\le j\le d$, $v_0=-\sum_{j=1}^d e_j$, and
$$
P_0=-(A/d)\mathbf1,\qquad P_j=(t+c)e_j-c\mathbf1\quad(1\le j\le d).
$$
Their signed evaluations have exactly the averaged matrix data above with total positive height $A+dt=1$. The edge matrix with columns $P_j-P_0$ is $(t+c)I+(A/d-c)\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$, with positive eigenvalues $t+c$ on $\mathbf1^\perp$ and $A+B$ on the span of $\mathbf1$. The vertices therefore form a nondegenerate simplex. The weights $B/(A+B)$ at $P_0$ and $A/[d(A+B)]$ at every other vertex sum to one and average the vertices to zero, so zero is interior. Positivity of the matrix entries verifies the strict incidence. The corner coarea lemma identifies the row data with the actual cap values, and the selected row value is $d(A+B)=x$.

For an ambient mixed cap family through a source in a simplex fiber, the same conclusion applies to that fiber when the continuous normals span and form a positive circuit, and their traces have the same strict incidence. Multiplying the entire $j$th ambient inequality by its positive circuit coefficient balances the continuous normals and multiplies the numerical base coefficients by the same positive number. The ambient halfspaces and all their traces are preserved.
