## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors, at bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use their disjoint summed $d$-volume, let $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$, fix $y\in K$, and let $Q$ be the raw ambient closed-halfspace depth at $(0,y)$. Set $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, let $B_d\in(1,2)$ solve $B_d^d-(2-B_d)^d=1$, put $y_d=(2-B_d)^d$, and write $L(h)=\max\{2-B_d,h^{1/d}\}$.

Suppose all three whole volumes are at most $M/2$. Suppose an actual closed cap $u\cdot(x-y)+\alpha z\ge0$ with $u\ne0$ attains $Q$ and has respective captures $U,q,V$, with $p=q/|K|\ge\Gamma$ and
$$
U^{1/d}+V^{1/d}\ge2(1-\eta)q^{1/d},\qquad 0\le\eta\le1.
$$
Set $T=(U+V)/q$ and $D=2(1-\eta)^d$. Then $|A|,|B|\ge Tp|K|$ and $0\le Tp\le1$, and
$$
\frac{Q}{M}\ge
\frac{(1+T)p}{1+L(Tp)^d+(2-L(Tp))^d}
\ge \frac{(1+D)\Gamma}{1+L(D\Gamma)^d+(2-L(D\Gamma))^d}.
$$
Also $Q/M\ge (1+T)\Gamma/[2(1+y_d)]$. In particular $Q/M\ge\Gamma/2$ if $\eta\le1-(2-B_d)/2^{1/d}$. The inequality is strict if $\eta\le1-[(\sqrt5-1)/4]^{1/d}$. If $\eta\le1/(d+1)$, then $Q/M>(20/39)\Gamma$.

For the same three bodies and the same $y$, suppose finitely many closed through-$(0,y)$ caps with nonzero continuous normals attain $Q$, their middle traces cover $K$, and every cap satisfies the displayed root inequality with the same $\eta\in[0,1]$. If a fiber has whole volume at least $M/2$, its centroid at its own base has normalized ambient depth at least $\Gamma/2$. Otherwise the point $(0,y)$ satisfies all the displayed uniform lower bounds and sufficient deficit conditions. In particular the bound $\Gamma/2$ is attained or exceeded at one of these specified actual points under the threshold $\eta\le1-(2-B_d)/2^{1/d}$. If no fiber has whole volume at least $M/2$ and $\eta\le1/(d+1)$, the point $(0,y)$ has depth ratio greater than $(20/39)\Gamma$.

## Proof

A through-point cap in this statement meets exactly the three full-dimensional volume layers being measured. Accepted fact 03c59c70402b66a2 applies to these three compatible bodies and the actual depth-attaining nonvertical cap. Its finite tilt comparisons give $|A|,|B|\ge U+V=Tp|K|$, including empty, full and supporting captures. The midpoint inclusion and Brunn–Minkowski then give $2(Tp)^{1/d}\le2$. Power convexity and the prescribed root inequality give $T\ge D$. The binomial inequality $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$ gives $\Gamma\le1/2$, so the product $D\Gamma$ is in $[0,1]$.

Apply the exact whole-volume frontier to the same bodies with floor $h=Tp$. The numerator $Q=(1+T)p|K|$ is the actual cap mass. The denominator $1+L(h)^d+(2-L(h))^d$ is nonincreasing in $h\in[0,1]$, because $L$ is nondecreasing and $x^d+(2-x)^d$ is nonincreasing for $x\in[0,1]$. Thus $p\ge\Gamma$, $T\ge D$ give the second ratio inequality. The same whole-volume theorem with $h=0$ gives $Q/M\ge (1+T)\Gamma/[2(1+y_d)]$. The bound is at least $\Gamma/2$ when $T\ge y_d$, which holds if $D\ge y_d$. This is the stated dimension-dependent sufficient condition. Since $y_d<(\sqrt5-1)/2$, the displayed dimension-independent comparison on $D$ gives strict inequality. Finally $\eta\le1/(d+1)$ gives $T\ge2\Gamma>2/3$ because $(1+1/d)^d<e<3$. Using the strict bound $M/|K|<1+\sqrt5<13/4$ yields $Q/M>(5/3)\Gamma/(13/4)=(20/39)\Gamma$.

For the cover, if a fiber has mass at least $M/2$, apply accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d to its ordinary centroid with the full volume as the selector mass. Every containing closed ambient cap either takes the entire fiber or restricts to a containing ordinary halfspace, so its raw mass is at least $\Gamma M/2$. If no such fiber exists, the same accepted centroid bound on $K$ shows that a cover trace containing its ordinary centroid has $q\ge\Gamma|K|$. Apply the one-cap conclusion at the original point $(0,y)$. Both applications count the mass on closed cap boundaries.
