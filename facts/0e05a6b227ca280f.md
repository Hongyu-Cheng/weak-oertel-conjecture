## Statement

Let $\Xi$ be uniform on the probability triangle, and let $0\le x\le y\le z$ be the sorted entries of a row. Set $G(c)=\Pr((x,y,z)\cdot\Xi\le c)$. Then
$$
\left(\inf_{c>0,G(c)>0}\frac{c}{\sqrt{G(c)}}\right)^2=z(x+y)-xy.
$$
In particular the result includes tied and zero entries.

## Proof

For $x<c<y$, when this interval is nonempty, the lower vertex cap gives $G(c)=(c-x)^2/[(y-x)(z-x)]$ by the area determinant. The ratio is decreasing, or constant when $x=0$. For $y\le c\le z$ and $y<z$, the complementary vertex cap gives $G(c)=1-(z-c)^2/[(z-x)(z-y)]$, allowing $x=y$. Where $G>0$, the sign of the ratio derivative is the sign of $G-cG'/2=1-z(z-c)/[(z-x)(z-y)]$. It changes from nonpositive to positive at $c_*=x+y-xy/z\in[y,z]$. When $c_*>0$, substitution gives $G(c_*)=c_*/z$, and the squared minimum is $zc_*$. If $x=y=0<z$, the formula instead gives $G(c)=2c/z-(c/z)^2$ and the ratio tends to zero as $c\downarrow0$. If $y=z>x$, the first interval extends to $z$ and its minimum is attained at $z$, giving squared value $z^2=z(x+y)-xy$. For a constant row the ratio infimum is that constant, including zero. Thresholds above the maximum entry have ratio $c$ and cannot lower any of these values.
