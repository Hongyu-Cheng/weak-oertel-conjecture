## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$, at respective integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and volumes $0<a=|A|\le b=|B|$ and $k=|K|$. Their lifted convex hull has these exact integer fibers. Write $g_A,g_B,g_K$ for their ordinary centroids and set $Y=(0,[k g_K+a(g_A+g_B)]/(k+2a))$. Raw mixed depth at $Y$ is the infimum of original mixed volume over all closed ambient halfspaces containing $Y$. In dimension $d=2$ this depth is at least $c_*(k+2a)$ with $c_*=6144/22745$. If additionally $b\le5a/3$, it exceeds the sharp three-fiber target $2(k+a+b)/9$ by at least $(2177/614115)a$. The continuous inequality underlying this bound is valid for every planar convex body $K$ and every $0\le\eta\le\mathbf1_K\,dx$: if $g$ is the centroid of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$, then $|K\cap G|+\eta(G)\ge c_*(|K|+2\eta(K))$ for every closed halfplane $G$ containing $g$.

## Proof

The lifted hull projects onto $[-1,1]$; its extreme sections are the lifted endpoints, and its middle section equals $\operatorname{conv}(K\cup (A+B)/2)=K$. Apply the triangular transport construction in accepted fact 486fe778f838d99d to $A,B$, with interpolation weight $1/2$ and containing body $(A+B)/2$. It gives a single coupling of the uniform probabilities whose midpoint law is supported in $(A+B)/2$ with density at most $1/v_*$, where $v_*=((a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2)^d$. Multiply the coupling and its midpoint law by $a$; call the latter $\eta$. The coupling marginals are the volume on $A$ and $a/b$ times the volume on $B$. The measure $\eta$ has mass $a$ and mean $(g_A+g_B)/2$ and satisfies $\eta\le(a/v_*)\mathbf1_K\,dx\le\mathbf1_K\,dx$. The continuous coordinate $y$ of $Y$ is therefore the centroid of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$ and belongs to $\operatorname{int} K$: a supporting linear functional is strictly below its support value throughout the positive-volume interior.

For each ambient closed halfspace $H$, let $H_z$ denote its trace at base $z$. If a coupled pair has midpoint in $H_0$, at least one of its lifted endpoints is in $H$ by the affine defining inequality. Integrate with the mass-$a$ coupling and use $a/b\le1$ to see that the original captured mixed volume is at least $|K\cap H_0|+\eta(H_0)$. It suffices to consider halfspaces through $Y$ because moving the boundary to $Y$ from a containing position only reduces the captured mass. A pure-base through-halfspace captures at least $k+a$.

For a nonvertical through-halfspace normalize the middle volume by $k$. Let $s=|K\cap H_0|/k$, $r=\eta(H_0)/k$ and $\alpha=a/k$. Here $0<s<1$, $0\le r\le s$ and $0\le\alpha-r\le1-s$. Apply the cap-root Jensen and leftmost-fill argument of accepted fact 607dca34de472b94, between its (1) and (2), to this very convex body and dominated measure, with $d=2$ and the centroid just verified. It gives, for $U=1/s$, $E=r/s$, $Q=(s+\alpha-r)/s$ and $\phi(x)=x-2x^{3/2}/3$,
$$
\phi(U)+2\phi(Q)+2\phi(E)\ge2/3 .
$$
For $x\ge0$ and any $j\in\mathbb R$, direct maximization gives $\phi(x)+jx\le(1+j)_+^3/3$. Use slopes $1/2,1/2,-7/16$ for $U,Q,E$ with multiplicities $1,2,2$ and subtract $2/3$. Doubling gives $U+2Q-7E/4\le17369/3072$. Since $(1+2\alpha)/s=U+2Q+2E-2$, this implies
$$
\frac{1+2\alpha}{s+r}\le
\frac{11225/3072+(15/4)E}{1+E}\le \frac{22745}{6144}.
$$
The last expression before the bound increases for $0\le E\le1$ because $15/4>11225/3072$. The scalar argument uses only a planar convex body and a measure dominated by its area, at their stated centroid. Multiplying by $k$ proves the continuous assertion for through-centroid halfplanes, and parallel tightening proves it for all containing halfplanes. The original cap comparison above now gives the stated mixed depth estimate. Pure-base capture $k+a$ is at least $(k+2a)/2$ and $c_*<1/2$.

Brunn–Minkowski and $b\ge a$ give $k\ge v_*=(a+b+2\sqrt{ab})/4\ge(b+3a)/4$. Since $c_*>2/9$, the excess over the original mixed target is at least
$$
(c_*/4-5/18)b+(11c_*/4-7/18)a.
$$
The coefficient of $b$ is negative, and substitution of $b=5a/3$ gives $(19c_*/6-23/27)a=(2177/614115)a>0$. The target uses $k+a+b$, while the selected centroid uses $k+2a$.
