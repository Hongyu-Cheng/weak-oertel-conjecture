## Statement

For every pair of integers \(L\ge1\), \(k\ge12\), and every real \(\eta>0\), there exist an integer \(D\), a full-dimensional lattice polytope \(\widetilde Q\subset\mathbb R^D\), a primitive integer linear functional \(H_M\), and a real \(\epsilon>0\) with the following properties. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde A&=\widetilde Q\cap\mathbb Z^D,
&\widetilde w_a&=e^{-\epsilon H_M(a)},
&\widetilde Z&=\sum_{a\in\widetilde A}\widetilde w_a,
&\widetilde p_a&=\widetilde w_a/\widetilde Z,\\
\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}&=2^D,
&\widetilde K_{\mathrm o}&=
\bigl|\lbrace a+2\mathbb Z^D:a\in\widetilde A\rbrace\bigr|.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each \(j\in\widetilde A\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde R_j&=\lbrace a\in\widetilde A:2j-a\in\widetilde A\rbrace,
&\widehat{\widetilde R}_j&=
\lbrace a\in\widetilde R_j:\widetilde p_a=\widetilde p_j\rbrace,\\
H_{\widetilde p}(j)&=
\sum_{a\in\widetilde R_j}\sqrt{\widetilde p_a\widetilde p_{2j-a}},
&\widehat H_{\widetilde p}(j)&=
\sum_{a\in\widehat{\widetilde R}_j}
\sqrt{\widetilde p_a\widetilde p_{2j-a}},\\
E_{\mathrm{amb}}&=\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{2}
\sum_{j\in\widetilde A}\widetilde p_j\widehat H_{\widetilde p}(j)^2,
&F_{\mathrm{amb}}&=\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{2}
\sum_{j\in\widetilde A}\widetilde p_jH_{\widetilde p}(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every ambient parity class contains at most two atoms, every selected row is \(\widehat{\widetilde R}_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\), and
$$
E_{\mathrm{amb}}<1<F_{\mathrm{amb}}<1+\eta,
\qquad
\left\lbrace
\frac12\left|\log\frac{\widetilde p_a}{\widetilde p_b}\right|:
 a,b\in\widetilde A,\ a\ne b,\ a\equiv b\pmod2
\right\rbrace
=
\lbrace k2^h\log2:0\le h\le L,\ h\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
In particular the minimum nonzero score is at least \(\log4096\).
There exist distinct two-atom occupied parity classes
\(\widetilde{\mathcal C}_0,\ldots,\widetilde{\mathcal C}_L\)
such that every class satisfies
\(\widetilde K_{\mathrm o}\sum_{a\in\widetilde{\mathcal C}_h}\widetilde p_a\in[1/2,2]\),
and the midpoint of \(\widetilde{\mathcal C}_h\) belongs to
\(\widetilde{\mathcal C}_{h+1}\) for \(0\le h<L\).

Define actual closed-halfspace depth by
\(\tau_{\widetilde p}(a)=\inf\lbrace
\sum_{b\in\widetilde A\cap G}\widetilde p_b:
G\subseteq\mathbb R^D\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }a\rbrace\).
For every real \(\theta>0\), the full original law satisfies
$$
\sum_{a\in\widetilde A}\widetilde p_a\tau_{\widetilde p}(a)^\theta
\ge\frac79\left(\frac23\right)^\theta
\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{-\theta}.
$$
All sums and normalizations include every actual lattice point. No independently specified selector masses are used.

## Proof

We first fix the original family and its notation. Apply accepted fact 1872f0ebbf46aa8a with the specified \(L,k\), and retain its parameters
$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=k2^L,&B&=q2^q,&t&=2^{-q}\log2,\\
m&=2^{2q+8}B^2,&n&=m+1,&r&=m/(8B),\\
\lambda&=e^{-t},&\beta&=e^{-tB}=2^{-q},&K&=2^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(K\) is an explicit alias for that fact's ambient capacity \(K_{\mathrm a}\); its occupied count continues to be denoted \(K_{\mathrm o}\).
For clarity, its construction is the following one. Let
\(\mathcal U=\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m:
|\sum_i u_i-m/2|\le r\rbrace\), and let \(N=|\mathcal U|\).
Let \(S_\ell\) be the first \(m/2^{\ell+1}\) coordinates,
\(s_\ell\) its indicator vector, \(c_0=0\), and
\(c_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}s_h\). Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{old}}
&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
\cup(\mathcal U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2B\rbrace)\bigr),\\
v_\ell&=(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell,0),
&j_0&=(0,B),&j_\ell&=(c_\ell,B/2^\ell),\\
Q&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(Q_{\mathrm{old}}\cup
\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L\rbrace\bigr),
&A&=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad H(x,z)=z,\\
w_a&=e^{-tH(a)},&Z&=\sum_{a\in A}w_a,&p_a&=w_a/Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
These are exactly the identically named objects in 1872f0ebbf46aa8a. In particular, \(Q\) is full-dimensional, every point of \(A\) has height in \([0,2B]\), and the complete support, including every optional profile atom, is retained in (2).

Write \(R_j=\lbrace a\in A:2j-a\in A\rbrace\), as in that predecessor, and define
\(W_3=\sum_{a\in A}w_a^3\) and
\(R_3=\sum_{j\in A}|R_j|^2w_j^3\).
Its selected rows are singletons. For every reflected pair, affine height gives
\(\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}}=p_j\), so the original ambient energies are exactly
$$
E_0=K^2W_3/Z^3<1,
\qquad
F_0=K^2R_3/Z^3>1.
\tag{3}
$$
The second strict inequality follows from the predecessor's full-energy lower bound and \(K\ge K_{\mathrm o}\).
Its other properties needed below are \(Z\le4N\), \(N\le2^m=K/2\), parity occupancy at most two, and the complete list of two-point classes
$$
\lbrace(u,0),(-u,2B)\rbrace\quad(u\in\mathcal U),
\qquad
\lbrace j_{\ell-1},v_\ell\rbrace\quad(1\le\ell\le L).
\tag{4}
$$
Their midpoints are respectively \(j_0\) and \(j_\ell\).
The exact nonzero score set of (2) is
\(\lbrace k2^h\log2:0\le h\le L\rbrace\).

We need a signed raw inequality stronger than the first inequality in (3).
Apply accepted fact 493ce33699090e90 at its parameter \(k=q\).
Its height parameter \(b\) is the present \(B\), its low height parameter \(a\) is 1, its \(j_\star\) is \(j_0\), and its \(Q,A,Z\) are the present \(Q_{\mathrm{old}},A_{\mathrm{old}},Z_{\mathrm{old}}\).
Its \(m,n,r,t,\lambda,\beta,\mathcal U,N\) are unchanged, and its ambient \(K\) is the present \(K\).
This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{old}}&=N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+\beta,\\
W_{3,\mathrm{old}}&=N(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6)+\beta^3,\\
2^m-N&\le\frac{\beta^2}{16}2^m,
&N&\ge\left(1-\frac{\beta^2}{16}\right)2^m,
&0<\beta&\le\frac1{64}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
These quantities use the fixed zero of the height in (2), with raw weight one at height zero; no raw rescaling is made.

The exponential series gives
\(e^{3/4}>1+3/4+(3/4)^2/2=65/32>2\), hence
\(\log2<3/4\). Therefore
\(1-\lambda<t=\beta\log2<3\beta/4\).
Using \(\lambda+2<3\) and \(\beta^4\le1/16\), we obtain
$$
(1-\lambda)^2(\lambda+2)+\beta^6-3\beta^2
<\left(\frac{27}{16}+\frac1{16}-3\right)\beta^2
=-\frac54\beta^2.
\tag{6}
$$
Also (5) implies \(N\ge(15/16)2^m\), and consequently
\(4(2^m-N)\le(4/15)N\beta^2\).
An exact expansion, followed by these bounds, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{3,\mathrm{old}}-3Z_{\mathrm{old}}+2K
&=4(2^m-N)
 +N\bigl((1-\lambda)^2(\lambda+2)+\beta^6-3\beta^2\bigr)
 +\beta^3-3\beta\\
&<-\frac{59}{60}N\beta^2+\beta^3-3\beta<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The last term is negative because \(0<\beta<1\).
The accepted containment \(A_{\mathrm{old}}\subseteq A\) and the height bound in (2) now give the exact identity
$$
W_3-3Z+2K
=W_{3,\mathrm{old}}-3Z_{\mathrm{old}}+2K
 +\sum_{a\in A\setminus A_{\mathrm{old}}}(w_a^3-3w_a)<0.
\tag{8}
$$
Indeed every summand in the additional sum is negative since \(0<w_a\le1\).
Thus the unknown presence of optional atoms cannot reverse this sign; none is removed from either raw sum.

Introduce the scalar ratios
\(\rho=Z/K\), \(a_3=W_3/K\), and \(f_3=R_3/K\).
These are respectively the seed's \(\rho,a,f\), with distinct subscripts to avoid using an atom index as a scalar name.
Equations (3) and (8) imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
0&<\rho\le2,\\
\delta_-&:=3\rho-2-a_3>0,\\
\delta_+&:=f_3-3\rho+2
>\rho^3-3\rho+2
=(\rho-1)^2(\rho+2)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
All these are finite quantities determined by the complete original support.

For the geometric construction, let \(J=2^\nu\ge8\) be a power of two and let \(M\ge1\) be an integer. Their finite choices will be specified below.
Here \(J\) counts selector vertices, as in the selector portion of a2798c1849e7c9ad; it does not denote the pair count \(J=N\) in 493ce33699090e90. The band radius \(r\) in (1) is unchanged, and \(\nu\) is the number of selector coordinates.
Put \(\epsilon=t/M\) and \(\lambda_M=e^{-\epsilon}\); the latter is the seed's weak-coordinate factor \(\mu\), not the predecessor's class-mass function.
In coordinates \((x,z,y)\in\mathbb R^{m+2}=\mathbb R^{n+1}\), define
$$
Q^+=Q\times[0,1],
\qquad
T_M(x,\xi,\zeta)=(x,\xi,\zeta-M\xi),
\qquad
Q_{\mathrm g}^{(M)}=T_M([0,1]^{m+2}).
\tag{10}
$$
The map \(T_M\) is an integer linear map with determinant one and integer inverse
\(T_M^{-1}(x,z,y)=(x,z,y+Mz)\).
Consequently the exact lattice support of \(Q_{\mathrm g}^{(M)}\) is
\(G_M=T_M(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{m+2})\).
This follows directly by applying the inverse to any integral point of the image cube: its preimage is integral and has every coordinate in \([0,1]\), hence is Boolean.
The exact support of \(Q^+\) is \(A\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace\).
Both polytopes are full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\).

Set \(V=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^\nu\), \(D=n+1+\nu\), and
$$
\widetilde Q=
\operatorname{conv}\left(
 (Q^+\times\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace)
 \cup\bigcup_{\omega\in V\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace}
 (Q_{\mathrm g}^{(M)}\times\lbrace\omega\rbrace)
\right).
\tag{11}
$$
Its selector projection is \([0,1]^\nu\). Any integral point therefore has a Boolean selector \(\omega\). In a convex combination of Boolean vectors equal to \(\omega\), every vector with positive coefficient agrees with \(\omega\) in every coordinate. Hence the entire fibre of (11) at \(\omega\) is exactly its displayed base polytope. We have proved the full census
$$
\widetilde A=
(A\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace\times\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace)
\cup
\bigcup_{\omega\in V\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace}
(G_M\times\lbrace\omega\rbrace).
\tag{12}
$$
This is an equality of complete lattice supports.
The polytope is a lattice polytope because all its generating base vertices and selectors are integral. It has dimension \(D\): the fibre at zero has affine difference space of dimension \(n+1\), and choosing one point at each selector unit vector adds \(\nu\) linearly independent directions modulo that base space. Thus it is bounded, full-dimensional, and has precisely the finite support (12).

Define one global integer height by
\(H_M(x,z,y,\omega)=Mz+y\).
Its coefficient of \(y\) is one, so it is primitive. On the zero-selector fibre,
\(e^{-\epsilon H_M(x,z,y,\mathbf0)}=e^{-tz}\lambda_M^y=w_{(x,z)}\lambda_M^y\).
On a good atom \(T_M(x,\xi,\zeta)\), one has \(H_M=\zeta\), so its raw weight is exactly \(\lambda_M^\zeta\).
There are \(K=2^{m+1}\) good atoms for each value \(\zeta=0,1\).
All selector coefficients in \(H_M\) are zero. Therefore every block weight below is forced by this one linear functional and the displayed geometry.

We next check parity and every reflected row. Distinct Boolean selectors have different parity, so two atoms of the same parity must lie in the same selector fibre. In the zero-selector fibre their Boolean \(y\)-coordinates also agree, and their base points have the same original parity. Each such copy consequently has exactly the original parity classes.
In a good fibre, unimodularity makes \(T_M\) invertible modulo two. The Boolean preimages have distinct parity, so all \(2K\) good atoms have distinct parity.
It follows that
$$
\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}=2KJ,
\qquad
\widetilde K_{\mathrm o}=2K_{\mathrm o}+2K(J-1),
\tag{13}
$$
and every parity class has at most two atoms.

If two supported endpoints reflect about a supported center, their Boolean selector coordinates satisfy \(\omega_1+\omega_2=2\omega\). Coordinatewise this forces \(\omega_1=\omega_2=\omega\).
In the zero-selector fibre the same argument forces both endpoint \(y\)-bits to equal the center's \(y\)-bit. Thus
\(\widetilde R_{(j,y,\mathbf0)}=R_j\times\lbrace y\rbrace\times\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\).
In a good fibre, reflected endpoints have the same parity and therefore coincide; hence every full row there is a singleton.
In a zero-selector row, equality of the global raw weight with the center's raw weight is equivalent to equality of the original height, since \(y\) is fixed and \(M>0\). The original selected row is a singleton. This proves \(\widehat{\widetilde R}_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\) everywhere.

The preceding parity classification also exhausts all pairs of distinct same-parity atoms. They are precisely the two \(y\)-copies of the original pairs (4), at selector zero. The common factors \(\lambda_M^y\) and \(\widetilde Z^{-1}\) cancel in their probability ratios. Their exact score set is therefore the original score set after (4), including every listed score and no others. Its minimum is \(k\log2\ge12\log2=\log4096\).

For every new reflected row, linearity of \(H_M\) gives
\(\sqrt{\widetilde p_a\widetilde p_{2j-a}}=\widetilde p_j\).
Define \(\widetilde W_3=\sum_{a\in\widetilde A}\widetilde w_a^3\) and
\(\widetilde R_3=\sum_{j\in\widetilde A}|\widetilde R_j|^2\widetilde w_j^3\).
The exact census and row identities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde Z&=(1+\lambda_M)\bigl(Z+(J-1)K\bigr),\\
\widetilde W_3&=(1+\lambda_M^3)\bigl(W_3+(J-1)K\bigr),\\
\widetilde R_3&=(1+\lambda_M^3)\bigl(R_3+(J-1)K\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
In particular all central atoms, optional original atoms, and good atoms contribute to these equalities. With
\(g_M=4(1+\lambda_M^3)/(1+\lambda_M)^3\), equations (13)--(14) yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{amb}}
&=\frac{\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{2}\widetilde W_3}{\widetilde Z^3}
=g_M\frac{J^2(J-1+a_3)}{(J-1+\rho)^3},\\
F_{\mathrm{amb}}
&=\frac{\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{2}\widetilde R_3}{\widetilde Z^3}
=g_M\frac{J^2(J-1+f_3)}{(J-1+\rho)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
These are ambient normalizations, not occupied normalizations.

Here are finite choices of \(J\) and \(M\) establishing all energy inequalities simultaneously. Put \(d_0=\rho-1\), so \(-1<d_0\le1\). Choose any power of two \(J\ge8\) satisfying
$$
J^2\delta_->|d_0|^3,
\qquad
J\delta_+>3d_0^2+|d_0|^3,
\qquad
J>\frac{16(\delta_++|d_0|^3)}{\eta}.
\tag{16}
$$
Such a finite power of two exists since \(\delta_-,\delta_+,\eta\) are positive and finite.
For any scalar \(c\), direct expansion gives
$$
J^2(J-1+c)-(J-1+\rho)^3
=J^2(c-3\rho+2)-3Jd_0^2-d_0^3.
\tag{17}
$$
Let
\(E_*=J^2(J-1+a_3)/(J-1+\rho)^3\) and
\(F_*=J^2(J-1+f_3)/(J-1+\rho)^3\).
For \(c=a_3\), the right side of (17) is at most
\(-J^2\delta_-+|d_0|^3<0\). Hence \(0<E_*<1\).
For \(c=f_3\), it is at least
\(J^2\delta_+-3Jd_0^2-|d_0|^3>0\) by the middle condition in (16) and \(J\ge1\).
Moreover \(J+d_0\ge J/2\), so
$$
0<F_*-1
\le\frac{J^2\delta_++|d_0|^3}{(J+d_0)^3}
\le\frac{8(\delta_++|d_0|^3)}J
<\frac\eta2.
\tag{18}
$$
Thus \(1<F_*<1+\eta/2\).

Set
\(\Delta=\min\lbrace E_*^{-1}-1,(1+\eta)/F_*-1\rbrace>0\),
and choose an integer
\(M>\max\lbrace1,4t,t\sqrt{3/\Delta}\rbrace\).
Then \(0<\epsilon=t/M<1/4\), and
\(\lambda_M=e^{-\epsilon}>1-\epsilon>3/4\).
The exact factorization
$$
g_M=1+3\frac{(1-\lambda_M)^2}{(1+\lambda_M)^2}
\tag{19}
$$
and \(1-e^{-\epsilon}<\epsilon\) imply
\(1<g_M<1+3\epsilon^2<1+\Delta\).
Consequently (15) gives
\(E_{\mathrm{amb}}=g_ME_*<(1+\Delta)E_*\le1\), and
\(1<F_*<F_{\mathrm{amb}}=g_MF_*<(1+\Delta)F_*\le1+\eta\).
All choices are sequential: the original complete support fixes (9), those numbers fix a finite \(J\), and that \(J\) fixes a finite integer \(M\). No limit support or interchange of two limiting operations is used.

It remains to verify occupied balance, rather than inheriting balance from a conditional law. Fix \(u\in\mathcal U\), and use the original classes
\(\mathcal C_0=\lbrace(u,0),(-u,2B)\rbrace\) and
\(\mathcal C_h=\lbrace j_{h-1},v_h\rbrace\), \(1\le h\le L\).
Their original raw masses \(c(\mathcal C)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal C}w_a\) satisfy
$$
c(\mathcal C_0)=1+\beta^2,
\qquad
c(\mathcal C_h)=1+e^{-tB/2^{h-1}},
\qquad
1\le c(\mathcal C_h)\le1+2^{-2k}\le1+\frac1{4096}.
\tag{20}
$$
For \(y\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), their lifts
\(\widetilde{\mathcal C}_{h,y}=
\mathcal C_h\times\lbrace y\rbrace\times\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\)
are entire occupied parity classes by the classification above.
Put \(\sigma_{\mathrm o}=K_{\mathrm o}/K\in(0,1]\).
Using the original global normalizer (14), the exact scaled class mass is
$$
\widetilde K_{\mathrm o}
\sum_{a\in\widetilde{\mathcal C}_{h,y}}\widetilde p_a
=
\frac{2\lambda_M^y}{1+\lambda_M}
\frac{J-1+\sigma_{\mathrm o}}{J-1+\rho}
 c(\mathcal C_h).
\tag{21}
$$
Since \(3/4\le\lambda_M\le1\), the first factor belongs to \([6/7,8/7]\) for either \(y\)-bit. Since \(0<\rho\le2\), \(0<\sigma_{\mathrm o}\le1\), and \(J\ge8\), the second factor satisfies
\((J-1)/(J+1)\le(J-1+\sigma_{\mathrm o})/(J-1+\rho)\le J/(J-1)\),
and thus belongs to \([7/9,8/7]\).
Equations (20)--(21) give the stronger interval
$$
\frac23
\le
\widetilde K_{\mathrm o}
\sum_{a\in\widetilde{\mathcal C}_{h,y}}\widetilde p_a
\le
\frac{64}{49}\frac{4097}{4096}
=\frac{4097}{3136}<2.
\tag{22}
$$
Every displayed class is therefore balanced in the required sense.
They are distinct two-atom classes, and their midpoint at index \(h\) is
\((j_h,y,\mathbf0)\), which belongs to the next class when \(h<L\).
Fixing \(y=0\) gives the required \(L\) links; (22) in fact proves the same result for both copies. No balance assertion about the class containing the last midpoint \((j_L,y,\mathbf0)\) is needed.

Finally let \(\mathcal G\subseteq\widetilde A\) be the union of all good-selector fibres. Their exact total probability is
$$
\sum_{a\in\mathcal G}\widetilde p_a
=\frac{J-1}{J-1+\rho}
\ge\frac{J-1}{J+1}\ge\frac79.
\tag{23}
$$
Every atom there has raw weight 1 or \(\lambda_M\), hence at least \(3/4\). Also
\(\widetilde Z=(1+\lambda_M)K(J-1+\rho)
\le2K(J+1)=\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}(1+1/J)\).
Therefore every good atom satisfies
$$
\widetilde p_a
\ge\frac{3}{4}\frac{J}{J+1}\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{-1}
\ge\frac23\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{-1}.
\tag{24}
$$
Every closed halfspace containing an atom contains that atom's full probability, so \(\tau_{\widetilde p}(a)\ge\widetilde p_a\).
For every \(\theta>0\), retaining only good atoms in the nonnegative original moment sum and using (23)--(24) proves
$$
\sum_{a\in\widetilde A}\widetilde p_a\tau_{\widetilde p}(a)^\theta
\ge\sum_{a\in\mathcal G}\widetilde p_a
\left(\frac{2}{3\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}}\right)^\theta
\ge\frac79\left(\frac23\right)^\theta
\widetilde K_{\mathrm a}^{-\theta}.
\tag{25}
$$
This is a bound on the full original law, with no conditional renormalization.

For comparison with accepted fact a2798c1849e7c9ad, its nested-template parameters are the original \(L,m,B,n,t,\lambda,\beta\) above, its \(U\) is \(\mathcal U\), and its template \(Q,A,H,Z,p,K_{\mathrm a},K_{\mathrm o}\) are exactly (2) with \(K_{\mathrm a}=K\). Its same-slope filler is \([0,1]^n\) with raw weight \(e^{-tz}\). The present enlarged bad base is instead \(Q^+=Q\times[0,1]\), and the new good base is the sheared cube (10), both in dimension \(n+1\). Their one common functional is \(Mz+y\) at slope \(t/M\), whose restriction to the good Boolean coordinates is \(\zeta\). Thus their conditional laws are not the pair of fixed conditional laws required by that predecessor's dilution theorem. In particular its same-slope cube obstruction is not contradicted.

Since \(L\) is arbitrary while \(\eta>0\) and \(k\ge12\) are freely prescribed, the construction defeats a uniform chain-length exclusion based on the stated low full-energy window, occupancy, balance, and minimum-score requirements. Equation (25) also shows exactly why this family is not a counterexample to dimension-uniform actual-moment lower bounds. No universal actual-moment theorem for other complete supports, and no proof or disproof of the locked mixed-integer Weak Oertel statement, follows from this construction.
