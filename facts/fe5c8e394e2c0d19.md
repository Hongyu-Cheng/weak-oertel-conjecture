## Statement

Let $Q=\{(u,v):-1\le u\le1,\ 0\le v\le4,\ 3u+v\ge0\}$ and $P=Q\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Assign weights $w_{(u,v)}=2^{-v}$ on $P$ and zero elsewhere. These are samples of the log-concave function $2^{-v}\mathbf 1_Q$. Put $B(j)=\sum_{p\in P}\min(w_p,w_{2j-p})$ for $j\in\mathbb Z^2$ and $M=\sum_{p\in P}w_p$. Then $M=65/16$, $\max_j B(j)=1$, and the maximum is attained precisely at $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,2)$. In particular the maximum retained fraction is $16/65<1/4$.

## Proof

The integer height rows are $\{0,1\}$ at heights $0,1,2$ and $\{-1,0,1\}$ at heights $3,4$, in the first coordinate. Consider decreasing positive row weights $t_0\ge\cdots\ge t_4$. A positive term in $B(j)$ requires $j$ to be the integer midpoint of two points of $Q$, so it requires $j\in P$. In each ordered pair the minimum weight is the weight at the larger height. Reflecting the five rows by $(u,v)\mapsto(2j_1-u,2j_2-v)$ gives the full table.

| $j$ | $B(j)$ |
| --- | --- |
| $(0,0),(1,0)$ | $t_0$ |
| $(0,1),(1,1)$ | $t_1+2t_2$ |
| $(0,2)$ | $t_2+4t_3+4t_4$ |
| $(1,2)$ | $t_2+2t_3+2t_4$ |
| $(-1,3)$ | $t_3$ |
| $(0,3)$ | $3t_3+4t_4$ |
| $(1,3)$ | $t_3+2t_4$ |
| $(-1,4),(1,4)$ | $t_4$ |
| $(0,4)$ | $3t_4$ |

Thus the maximum is the maximum of the first three rows. For $t_v=2^{-v}$ these three values are all one, and the others are strictly less. The row sizes give $M=2(t_0+t_1+t_2)+3(t_3+t_4)=65/16$. The restriction of an exponential affine function to a convex support, extended by zero, is log-concave. The gap to $1/4$ is $1/260$. The rectangular exchange from $(-1,3),(1,0)$ to $(-1,0),(1,3)$ preserves their sum but takes one point outside $Q$, so the resulting product of weights vanishes although the original product is positive.
