## Statement

Let L be a positive integer. For each i in \(\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), let \(n_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(w_{i,x}>0\) for every \(x\in A_i\). Retain the entire original laws
$$
Z_i=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},\qquad p_{i,x}=w_{i,x}/Z_i,\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^L n_i.
$$
For a finite probability law p on \(\mathbb R^n\), write
$$
\tau_p(j)=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\qquad
M_r(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^r\quad(r>0).
$$
These are the original ambient depths and moments used in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705.

For every \(i\) and \(j\in A_i\), choose an auxiliary subset \(R^{(3)}_{i,j}\subseteq A_i\) partitioned into the singleton \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) and any number, possibly zero, of pairwise disjoint sets of three distinct points. Require that every such triple have arithmetic mean j. Define \(T_{i,j}\) to fix j and to cycle the three points in each triple in either chosen orientation. Thus \(T_{i,j}^3\) is the identity; it may be the identity itself when the row is a singleton. No symmetry, support completeness, disjointness between different centers' rows, or exhaustive-support property is assumed.

The following are NEW cyclic quantities. They replace the roles, but do not assert equality with the reflected values, of \(H,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu\) in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. The explicit role map is
$$
(H,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu)
\longmapsto
(H_i^{(3)},b_i^{(3)},\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)},Y_{i,j}^{(3)},V_{i,j}^{(3)},q_i^{(3)},\nu_i^{(3)}).
$$
In particular \(R^{(3)}_{i,j}\) is not the canonical reflected row. On this new row put
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{i,j}(x)&=\bigl(p_{i,x}p_{i,T_{i,j}x}p_{i,T_{i,j}^2x}\bigr)^{1/3},\\
H_i^{(3)}(j)&=\sum_{x\in R^{(3)}_{i,j}}g_{i,j}(x),
&\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}(x)&=\frac{g_{i,j}(x)}{H_i^{(3)}(j)},\\
Y_{i,j}^{(3)}(x)&=\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{g_{i,j}(x)},
&V_{i,j}^{(3)}&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}}(Y_{i,j}^{(3)})^2,\\
b_i^{(3)}&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^{(3)}(j)^2,
&q_{i,j}^{(3)}&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^{(3)}(j)^2}{b_i^{(3)}},\\
\nu_i^{(3)}&=\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}^{(3)}V_{i,j}^{(3)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
All these quantities retain the original probabilities, including those outside the auxiliary rows. They satisfy
$$
\begin{gathered}
0<p_{i,j}\le H_i^{(3)}(j)\le\sum_{x\in R^{(3)}_{i,j}}p_{i,x}\le1,\qquad
b_i^{(3)}\ge\sum_{x\in A_i}p_{i,x}^3>0,\\
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}}X=j,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}}Y_{i,j}^{(3)}=0,\\
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}}\log\frac{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}(X)}{p_{i,X}}
=-\log H_i^{(3)}(j),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}}\!\left(\log\frac{\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}(X)}{p_{i,X}}\right)
=V_{i,j}^{(3)}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
$$
Their exact weight normalization is
$$
b_i^{(3)}=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R^{(3)}_{i,j}}
\bigl(w_{i,x}w_{i,T_{i,j}x}w_{i,T_{i,j}^2x}\bigr)^{1/3}\right)^2.
\tag{3}
$$

For every supported product target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), set
$$
h_J=\prod_iH_i^{(3)}(j_i),\qquad
\sigma_J^2=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^{(3)}.
$$
Every original ambient containing closed halfspace satisfies the pointwise depth estimate
$$
\tau_P(J)\ge\frac{h_J}{3}\exp(-\sqrt2\,\sigma_J)+\frac23P_J.
\tag{4}
$$
Consequently
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{2\sum_i\nu_i^{(3)}}\right),
\tag{5}
$$
and, for every real \(r\ge2\),
$$
M_r(P)\ge
3^{-r}\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)^{r/2}
\exp\left(-r\sqrt{2\sum_i\nu_i^{(3)}}\right).
\tag{6}
$$
All assertions include singleton factors and supports of arbitrary affine rank.

Here is the conditional price consequence, with the row choices part of the hypothesis. Fix \(C\ge0\). Define
$$
K_i=2^{n_i},\qquad A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_{x\in A_i}p_{i,x}^3.
$$
Call factor i active when \(A_{0,i}<1\). Suppose that every active factor is furnished with rows as above satisfying
$$
B_i:=K_i^2b_i^{(3)}\ge1,\qquad
\nu_i^{(3)}\le C\log B_i.
\tag{7}
$$
In each inactive factor choose only singleton rows. Then, without changing any original law,
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac19e^{-2C}4^{-N},\qquad
M_r(P)\ge\left(\frac{e^{-C}}3\right)^r2^{-rN}\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{8}
$$
Thus, if one common C and row certificates (7) were available for every active positive globally affine law on every nonempty complete convex lattice support, the original-law hypothesis \((H_r)\), namely
$$
\exists a>0\ \forall n\ge1\ \forall Q,\ell:\qquad
\sum_{j\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}p_j\tau_p(j)^r\ge a^r2^{-rn},
\quad
p_j=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot j}}{\sum_{x\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}e^{\ell\cdot x}},
\tag{9}
$$
for compact convex Q with nonempty lattice support, would hold for each fixed \(r\ge2\), with \(a=e^{-C}/3\). The same coefficient holds for their heterogeneous products. The existence of these certificates is an additional premise, not a conclusion of this theorem.

For global affine weights \(w_{i,x}=\exp(c_i+\ell_i\cdot x)\), every chosen triple obeys
$$
\begin{gathered}
g_{i,j}(x)=p_{i,j},\qquad
m_{i,j}:=|R^{(3)}_{i,j}|,\qquad H_i^{(3)}(j)=m_{i,j}p_{i,j},\\
\kappa_{i,j}^{(3)}(x)=m_{i,j}^{-1},\qquad
Y_{i,j}^{(3)}(x)=\ell_i\cdot(x-j),\qquad
V_{i,j}^{(3)}=\frac1{m_{i,j}}\sum_{x\in R^{(3)}_{i,j}}(\ell_i\cdot(x-j))^2,\\
b_i^{(3)}=\sum_jm_{i,j}^2p_{i,j}^3,\qquad
q_{i,j}^{(3)}=\frac{m_{i,j}^2p_{i,j}^3}{b_i^{(3)}}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{10}
$$
These formulas do not assume equal source probabilities.

The accepted tree triples are concrete instances of these rows. At a Boolean target they are the triples in (14) of accepted fact 3e23587245b6989f; at an arbitrary original prefix they are the triples in (5) of accepted fact abbc24783c13e0f6. More explicitly, retain the exact support \(\widehat A\) and entrance schedule of abbc24783c13e0f6, and map its tree dimension n, total integer dimension N, and total weight W to \(n_{\mathrm{tr}},d_{\mathrm{int}}=n_{\mathrm{tr}}+1,Z_{\mathrm{tr}}\), respectively. At a supported target \(\mathbf J=(j,t)\) of level s, take an integer \(r\ge1\) satisfying that fact's local two-predecessor and continuation hypotheses, \(s+r\le Q\), and \(a_{s+r}\le t\). Its \(2^{r-1}\) disjoint triples lifted to the same height t, together with \(\lbrace\mathbf J\rbrace\), define a row of size
$$
m_{\mathbf J}=1+3\cdot2^{r-1}.
\tag{11}
$$
With the original height-only weights \(w_{(z,h)}=\rho_h>0\) on this entire support, including the accepted choice \(\rho_h=2^{-h}\), this row has
$$
H^{(3)}(\mathbf J)=m_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf J},\qquad
Y_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}=0,\qquad V_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}=0,\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{tr}}\tau_p(\mathbf J)\ge(1+2^{r-1})\rho_t.
\tag{12}
$$
For a horizontal tilt \(w_{(z,h)}=e^{\ell\cdot z}\rho_h\), and in particular for a global affine weight when \(\rho_h=e^{c+\beta h}\), the same row still has geometric weight \(p_{\mathbf J}\) at every source, uniform cyclic kernel, and the exact variance given in the proof. Its source probabilities need not be equal. Neither this specialization nor (5) asserts (7) for arbitrary horizontal tilts.

## Proof

Fix one factor and center temporarily, suppressing the factor index in the cyclic notation. On a three-cycle the geometric weight g is constant. The sum of its three log-likelihood scores is
$$
\sum_{a=0}^2\log\frac{p_{T^ax}}{(p_xp_{Tx}p_{T^2x})^{1/3}}=0.
\tag{13}
$$
At j the geometric weight is \(p_j\) and its score is zero. Each cycle has geometric-weighted mean j, so summing the cycles and singleton proves the two expectations in (2). Arithmetic-geometric mean on every triple gives \(3(p_xp_{Tx}p_{T^2x})^{1/3}\le p_x+p_{Tx}+p_{T^2x}\); summing proves the H bounds. The lower bound on b follows from \(H(j)\ge p_j\), and proves that every q in (1) is a probability law. Direct substitution gives
$$
\log\frac{\kappa_j^{(3)}(x)}{p_x}
=-\log H^{(3)}(j)-Y_j^{(3)}(x).
\tag{14}
$$
Together with the zero score mean this proves the likelihood identities in (2), and in particular the variance is centered. Replacing every p by w/Z proves (3). These are direct proofs for the new cyclic rows, not an application of reflection invariance.

Now fix a product target J and write
$$
R_J=\prod_iR^{(3)}_{i,j_i},\qquad
\mathcal T_J(x_1,\ldots,x_L)=(T_{1,j_1}x_1,\ldots,T_{L,j_L}x_L),\qquad
\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^{(3)}.
$$
The only fixed point of \(\mathcal T_J\) is J. Every other orbit has length three: the order divides three, and an orbit of length one would require every coordinate to be fixed in its factor. Every orbit has arithmetic mean J, because in each coordinate its three entries are either the repeated center or one complete factor triple. Thus any closed halfspace containing J contains at least one point of every orbit. This uses only that if the three values of an affine defining functional were strictly negative, their average at J would be strictly negative as well. Points on the boundary are retained.

The law \(\kappa_J\) is invariant under \(\mathcal T_J\). For a=0,1,2 put
$$
S_a(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}^{(3)}(T_{i,j_i}^aX_i).
$$
Equation (13) and the singleton case imply
$$
S_0+S_1+S_2=0.
\tag{15}
$$
Each rotated score has mean zero under its factor kernel, and factor kernels are independent. Consequently
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}S_a^2=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^{(3)}=\sigma_J^2
\quad(a=0,1,2).
\tag{16}
$$
The exact original-law likelihood identity on the product row is
$$
P_{\mathcal T_J^aX}=h_J\kappa_J(X)e^{S_a(X)}.
\tag{17}
$$
In particular no conditional original row law replaces P.

Let \(\mathcal O\) run through the noncentral orbits in \(R_J\), and set \(m(X)=-\min_{a=0,1,2}S_a(X)\ge0\). It is constant on each orbit. Since every row source belongs to exactly one orbit, and J belongs to the halfspace, the original mass captured in any containing closed halfspace is at least
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_J+\sum_{\mathcal O}\min_{X\in\mathcal O}P_X
&=\frac{h_J}{3}\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-m(X)}+\frac23P_J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Indeed each noncentral minimum is counted three times in the expectation before division by three. The central term there is \(h_J\kappa_J(J)/3=P_J/3\); the correction supplies its remaining \(2P_J/3\). This proves the identity and the singleton accounting simultaneously.

For any three real numbers summing to zero, if their minimum is -m, the other two sum to m. Their squares sum to at least \(m^2/2\), so
$$
m^2\le\frac23(S_0^2+S_1^2+S_2^2).
\tag{19}
$$
Using (16), Cauchy-Schwarz, and Jensen for the convex function \(u\mapsto e^{-u}\) yields
$$
\mathbb E m\le\sqrt{\mathbb E m^2}\le\sqrt2\,\sigma_J,
\qquad
\mathbb E e^{-m}\ge e^{-\mathbb E m}\ge e^{-\sqrt2\,\sigma_J}.
\tag{20}
$$
This is the improvement over the estimate obtained from \(m^2\le S_0^2+S_1^2+S_2^2\). The coefficient \(2/3\) in (19) is attained by \((-2a,a,a)\) for \(a\ge0\). Combining (18) and (20) and taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves (4). If every factor row at J is a singleton, then \(h_J=P_J\), \(\sigma_J=0\), and (4) gives the exact available singleton mass \(P_J\).

Discard the nonnegative correction in (4), square, and average over the entire original P. The product auxiliary center law \(q^{(3)}=\bigotimes_iq_i^{(3)}\) satisfies
$$
P_Jh_J^2=\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)q_J^{(3)},\qquad
\mathbb E_{q^{(3)}}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\nu_i^{(3)}.
$$
Therefore Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)
\mathbb E_{q^{(3)}}e^{-2\sqrt2\,\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt2\,\mathbb E_{q^{(3)}}\sigma_J\right)\\
&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(3)}\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{2\sum_i\nu_i^{(3)}}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
This proves (5). For real \(r\ge2\), Jensen under the original P for \(u\mapsto u^{r/2}\) gives \(M_r(P)\ge M_2(P)^{r/2}\), proving (6). Distinct source usage was needed only within a fixed target's row. Rows belonging to different targets need not be disjoint, since their depth lower bounds are averaged separately rather than added within one cap.

For the price consequence, an inactive factor with singleton rows has
$$
H_i^{(3)}(j)=p_{i,j},\qquad b_i^{(3)}=\sum_jp_{i,j}^3,\qquad
B_i=A_{0,i}\ge1,\qquad \nu_i^{(3)}=0.
$$
Together with (7) on the active factors this gives, on writing \(G=\sum_i\log B_i\),
$$
G\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\nu_i^{(3)}\le CG,\qquad
4^N\prod_i b_i^{(3)}=e^G.
$$
Applying (5) gives
$$
4^NM_2(P)\ge\frac19\exp(G-2\sqrt{2CG})\ge\frac19e^{-2C},
$$
since \(G-2\sqrt{2CG}=(\sqrt G-\sqrt{2C})^2-2C\). Raising this M2 bound to the power r/2 proves (8). This argument includes C=0 and any number of inactive or singleton factors. Taking L=1 for every law in the conditional premise proves (9); applying the same calculation to arbitrary lists proves its heterogeneous-product version. No assertion of (7) was used without its explicit row certificate.

For affine weights, every triple has sum 3j, hence
$$
(w_xw_{Tx}w_{T^2x})^{1/3}
=\exp(c+\ell\cdot j)=w_j.
$$
Division by the one original Z proves \(g(x)=p_j\). The singleton has the same value. All formulas in (10) now follow directly from (1). The equality of geometric weights does not imply equality of the three original weights; their likelihood scores are precisely the three affine displacements.

Finally consider the exact tree specialization. At a Boolean target, accepted fact 3e23587245b6989f proves source membership, barycenter, and pairwise source disjointness for its displayed triples. Accepted fact abbc24783c13e0f6 proves these same properties for the arbitrary-prefix extension, including the preservation of all prefix twos and the same-height lifting condition \(a_{s+r}\le t\). Its first two source families have additional twos on different descendant paths; the third family retains the prefix and changes the coordinate \(U_1\) from one to zero. Thus none of these sources is the target. The family has exactly \(2^{r-1}\) triples, proving (11) and the partition required by our row definition. Cycling each displayed ordered triple defines the required \(T_{\mathbf J}\). One may choose such a row independently at each eligible target and choose singleton rows elsewhere.

For completeness, the exact score formula under a horizontal field can be read off from those accepted coordinates. Denote the two descendant paths in abbc24783c13e0f6 by \(U_1,\ldots,U_r\) and \(V_1,\ldots,V_r\), and write \(\ell_{U_a},\ell_{V_a}\) for the corresponding horizontal field coefficients. The three displacement scores for \(\varepsilon\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{r-1}\) are
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\varepsilon,1}
&=\ell_{U_1}-\ell_{V_1}
 +\sum_{a=2}^r(\ell_{U_a}-\varepsilon_a\ell_{V_a}),\\
L_{\varepsilon,2}
&=\ell_{V_1}
 +\sum_{a=2}^r(\ell_{V_a}-\varepsilon_a\ell_{U_a}),\\
L_{\varepsilon,3}
&=-\ell_{U_1}
 +\sum_{a=2}^r(\varepsilon_a-1)(\ell_{U_a}+\ell_{V_a}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
The sums are empty when r=1, and these three values sum to zero. The common actual height makes the height factor cancel, even for an arbitrary positive profile \(\rho_h\). The barycenter makes the horizontal geometric mean equal the target's horizontal weight. Thus under the entire original law with \(w_{(z,h)}=e^{\ell\cdot z}\rho_h\),
$$
\begin{gathered}
g_{\mathbf J}(X)=p_{\mathbf J},\qquad
H^{(3)}(\mathbf J)=m_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf J},\qquad
\kappa_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}(X)=m_{\mathbf J}^{-1},\\
V_{\mathbf J}^{(3)}=
\frac1{m_{\mathbf J}}
\sum_{\varepsilon\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{r-1}}
\sum_{a=1}^3L_{\varepsilon,a}^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{22}
$$
Every normalizer here sums over the full original lifted support. There is no new conditioning or omitted height-column atom. With \(\ell=0\), all the scores in (21) vanish. Equation (4), or the exact orbit count (18), then gives
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf J)\ge
\left(\frac{1+3\cdot2^{r-1}}3+\frac23\right)p_{\mathbf J}
=(1+2^{r-1})p_{\mathbf J},
$$
which proves (12), including the separate target singleton. For \(\rho_h=e^{c+\beta h}\), the weight is a single global affine exponential on the entire support and (22) is also exactly (10), with affine field \((\ell,\beta)\). Formula (22) defines the price under arbitrary horizontal tilts but provides no further inequality for it. The proof of the product comparison and the conditional price implication is complete.
