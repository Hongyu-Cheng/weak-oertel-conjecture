## Statement

Let \(R,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), with \(R\geq2\), and let
\[
C_{R,d}:=
\left\{
(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d:
0\leq x_j\leq R-\|z\|_\infty
\quad(1\leq j\leq d)
\right\}.
\]
Accepted fact `d9049ecf3fa5c7d5` establishes that this is a convex body and
that its fibers satisfy
\[
C_z=[0,R-\|z\|_\infty]^d
\quad\text{when }\|z\|_\infty\leq R.
\]
Put
\[
D_R:=[-R+1,R-1]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z.
\]
Thus
\[
w_z=(R-\|z\|_\infty)^d\quad(z\in D_R),
\qquad
w_z=0\quad(z\notin D_R).
\]
Let \(\mu_z\) be \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\(\{z\}\times C_z\).  For \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z,\qquad
\mu_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}\mu_z,
\qquad
T(p,q):=\frac{p+q}{2},
\]
and define
\[
\mathsf A\mu:=
\frac14\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}
\frac1{W_r}T_\#(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r),
\qquad
\kappa_c:=(\mathsf A\mu)(\{c\}\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
All four \(W_r\) are positive because \(R\geq2\).  Define
\[
P:=\operatorname{conv}\{c\in\mathbb Z^2:\kappa_c>0\}.
\]
For \(c\in\mathbb Z^2\) and \(x\in C_c\), define
\[
\delta_C(c,x):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_c\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\}.
\]

Set
\[
q:=\left\lfloor\frac{R-1}{d}\right\rfloor.
\]
If
\[
q(q+1)\bigl(3R-d(2q+1)\bigr)
>
3(2R-1)^2,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
P=[-R+1,R-1]^2
\tag{2}
\]
and there is no affine map
\[
s:\mathbb R^2\longrightarrow\mathbb R^d
\]
satisfying
\[
s(c)\in C_c,\qquad
\delta_C(c,s(c))\geq\kappa_c
\quad(c\in P\cap\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, (1) holds for the infinite family
\[
R=21d^2\qquad(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}).
\tag{4}
\]
Taking \(d=10\) in (4) gives \(R=2100\), so (2)--(3) prove the registered
subgoal exactly:
\[
P=[-2099,2099]^2
\]
and the required full-profile affine realization does not exist.

## Proof

The positive-volume base support is exactly \(D_R\).  For every
\(c\in D_R\), the diagonal ordered pair of fibers \(z=z'=c\) contributes
\[
\frac14\,\frac{w_c^2}{W_{c\bmod2}}>0
\]
to \(\kappa_c\), so \(\kappa_c>0\).  Conversely, every base point carrying
positive mass under \(T_\#(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r)\) is a midpoint of two points
of \(D_R\).  Since
\[
\operatorname{conv}(D_R)=[-R+1,R-1]^2,
\]
every such midpoint belongs to this square.  It follows that
\[
\{c\in\mathbb Z^2:\kappa_c>0\}=D_R,
\]
which proves (2), including both inclusions and all boundary lattice
points.

We next derive a lower bound for the target mass at the origin.  Symmetry
gives \(w_{-z}=w_z\), and \(-z\equiv z\pmod2\).  Hence the only ordered base
pairs with midpoint zero are \((z,-z)\), and therefore
\[
\kappa_0
=
\frac14
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{z\in D_R\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}w_z^2}
{W_r}.
\tag{5}
\]
For each parity \(r\), let
\[
N_r:=|\{z\in D_R:z\equiv r\pmod2\}|,
\qquad
N:=|D_R|=(2R-1)^2.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz within that parity class gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_R\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}w_z^2
\geq\frac{W_r^2}{N_r}.
\]
Since \(N_r\leq N\), equation (5) yields
\[
\kappa_0
\geq
\frac14\sum_r\frac{W_r}{N_r}
\geq
\frac{W}{4N}.
\tag{6}
\]

The shell \(\|z\|_\infty=m\) has \(8m\) lattice points for \(m\geq1\), so
\[
W=R^d+8\sum_{m=1}^{R-1}m(R-m)^d.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(1\leq m\leq q\), Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
(R-m)^d
=R^d\left(1-\frac mR\right)^d
\geq
R^d\left(1-\frac{dm}{R}\right).
\]
Using only these shells in (7) and evaluating the two elementary sums gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&\geq
8R^d\sum_{m=1}^q
m\left(1-\frac{dm}{R}\right)\\
&=
\frac43 R^{d-1}q(q+1)
\bigl(3R-d(2q+1)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Condition (1) and (8) imply
\[
W>4(2R-1)^2R^{d-1}=4NR^{d-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (6) and (9) gives
\[
\kappa_0>R^{d-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that an affine map \(s\) satisfies (3).
Put \(a:=R-1\) and consider the four corners
\[
c_\varepsilon:=(\varepsilon_1a,\varepsilon_2a),
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^2.
\]
Each corner fiber is exactly
\[
C_{c_\varepsilon}=[0,1]^d.
\]
Feasibility in (3) therefore gives
\[
s(c_\varepsilon)\in[0,1]^d
\quad\text{for all four }\varepsilon.
\]
Affineness and the fact that the average of these four corners is zero give
\[
s(0)=\frac14\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^2}s(c_\varepsilon)
\in[0,1]^d.
\tag{11}
\]
The central fiber is \(C_0=[0,R]^d\).  The proper closed halfspace
\[
J:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_1\leq s_1(0)\}
\]
contains \(s(0)\), and (11) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_0\cap J)
=s_1(0)R^{d-1}
\leq R^{d-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\delta_C(0,s(0))\leq R^{d-1},
\tag{12}
\]
where the closed boundary causes no loss, including when \(s_1(0)=0\).
Equations (10) and (12) contradict the required inequality
\(\delta_C(0,s(0))\geq\kappa_0\) at \(c=0\).  This rules out every affine
map in (3).

For \(R=21d^2\), one has
\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac{21d^2-1}{d}\right\rfloor=21d-1.
\]
The difference between the two sides of (1) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(21d-1)(21d)\bigl(63d^2-d(42d-1)\bigr)
-3(42d^2-1)^2\\
&=3969d^4+231d^2-3>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (1) holds for every \(d\geq1\), proving (4).  In the registered
specialization \(R=2100,d=10\), the exact comparison is
\[
92607900>52894803.
\]
Equivalently, using the exact \(W\) certified in accepted fact
`d9049ecf3fa5c7d5`,
\[
W-4(4199)^2\,2100^9
=389790568505683594457783268863960700000>0.
\]
All inequalities are exact integer inequalities.
