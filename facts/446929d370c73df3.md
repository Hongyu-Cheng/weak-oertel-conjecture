## Statement

Let $\phi_3$ be the row capacity $\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$ for $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$ with $U$ uniform on the barycentric tetrahedron. Fix strictly positive weights and choose a maximizer of $\sum_i\omega_i\phi_3(X_i)$ over nonnegative $4\times4$ matrices whose column sums equal one with the fewest nonzero rows. If all four rows are nonzero, each row has at least one zero entry.

## Proof

Suppose there is a full row, meaning all its entries are strictly positive. By least-active independence all four rows are linearly independent. The accepted full-cut exclusion applies at this positive-weight square stochastic maximum. A full row with a full-cut minimum saturates at least three columns. Every other row would then be supported in the at most one remaining column, inconsistent with the independence of all four rows. Therefore the full rows have proper minimizing cuts.

The common section-centroid lemma gives a common actual centroid $b$ and a positive weighted gradient for every full row. Every other nonzero row has exactly one zero. Indeed if it had at least two zeros, the accepted fixed-support capacity formula in the coordinate derivative jump lemma gives zero capacity there. A small transfer from one of its positive entries to a full row in the same column keeps its support and capacity fixed. The positive weighted derivative of the full row makes this a strict improvement. For a one-zero row with hole $h$, the boundary multiplier lemma gives $b_j\le1/3$ for every $j\ne h$.

For each row define $t_i=b\cdot X_i>0$. For full rows this is the actual minimizing threshold. The column sums and $\sum_j b_j=1$ imply for each $h$ that
$$
\sum_i(t_i-X_{ih})=0.
$$
Let $J=\{j:b_j>1/3\}$. If $J$ is empty, any full row has two strictly captured vertices with centroid coordinates at most $1/3$, because the accepted Schur law guarantees two strictly captured vertices at each proper minimizing full-row section.

If $J=\{h\}$, each one-zero row has its zero at $h$ and contributes $t_i>0$ to the balance in column $h$. If there is such a row, some full row contributes a strictly negative margin. If there is no such row, all rows are full and no full-row margin at $h$ can be zero, because the accepted same-side lemma makes a zero-margin centroid coordinate exactly $1/3$. The balance again forces some negative margin. In either case a full row strictly excludes the only noncentral vertex and has two strictly captured vertices with centroid coordinates at most $1/3$.

If $|J|\ge2$, the one-zero bound excludes all one-zero rows. Choose $h\in J$. The same-side lemma applies to each proper full-row minimizing section, gives nonzero margins at $h$, and puts all indices in $J$ on the same strict side of each cut. The balance forces some negative margin at $h$. That row therefore strictly excludes every vertex in $J$, so it again has two strictly captured vertices with centroid coordinates at most $1/3$.

In every case the corrected central-pair lemma produces a positive Hessian direction at the row normalized by its positive threshold. Homogeneity scales the Hessian to the actual row by a positive factor. Local $C^2$ regularity is supplied by the accepted Schur law. The joint positive-index bound would give $4+1\le4$, a contradiction.
