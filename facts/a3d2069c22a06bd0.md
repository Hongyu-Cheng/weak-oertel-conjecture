## Statement

Use the ordinary triangle corner coordinates of the exact elimination lemma: the mark is zero, the normals are $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$, and the vertices are $(a,-z),(-x,p),(-y,-(1-p-y))$ with $a>0$, $0<p<1$, $0<x<p$, $0<y<1-p$, $0<z<a$. Write $q_i$ for the three cap area fractions and $\lambda_1$ for the first normalized inverse chord measure. If all $q_i\le128/279$, every source barycentric coordinate is below $5/14$ and every $q_i>20/49$. If also $\lambda_1\le2/9$, then
$$
\frac15<a<\frac{448}{1395},\qquad
\frac3{10}<p<\frac7{10},\qquad
x+y<\frac{82603}{235904}<\frac9{25}.
$$
No sorted profile is assumed.

## Proof

Put $c=128/279$. The strict distinct-vertex incidence and cap ceiling give the barycentric bound and $q_i>20/49$ by accepted fact 2e657e976221a74d. The corner-elimination formula for the weight gives
$$
\frac a{q_1}\le\frac27\left(\frac p{q_2}+\frac{1-p}{q_3}\right)
<\frac7{10}.
$$
Therefore $a<(7/10)c=448/1395$.

By the cap fractions for rows two and three,
$$
1+a=(p+z)+(1-p+a-z)
\ge\frac{p^2}{c(1-y)}+\frac{(1-p)^2}{c(1-x)}
\ge\frac1{c(2-x-y)},
$$
where the last step is Cauchy–Schwarz. Row one gives $(a+x)(a+y)\ge a^2/c$, so $x+y\ge2a(1/\sqrt c-1)$ by AM–GM. Combining,
$$
(1+a)[2-2a(1/\sqrt c-1)]\ge1/c.
$$
Since $\sqrt c<19/28$, for $0<a\le1/5$ the left side is at most $(1+a)(2-18a/19)$, increasing on that interval. Its value at $1/5$ is $1032/475<279/128$ by a gap of $429/60800$, a contradiction. Thus $a>1/5$.

Because $p+z<p+a$ and $1-y<1$, the fraction formula gives $q_2>p^2/(p+a)$. For $p\ge7/10$, the polynomial $p^2-c(p+448/1395)$ is increasing and its value at $7/10$ is $167489/7784100>0$. Thus $q_2>c$ in that range, a contradiction. Interchanging rows two and three gives $1-p<7/10$, since $q_3>(1-p)^2/(1-p+a)$. Finally the first displayed Cauchy–Schwarz bound and the strict upper bound for $a$ give
$$
x+y\le2-\frac1{c(1+a)}
<2-\frac1{c(1+448/1395)}
=\frac{82603}{235904}<\frac9{25}.
$$
