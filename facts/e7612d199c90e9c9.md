## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty. Let $d\ge1$ and let $C$ be the convex hull of compact convex bodies $K_p$ of positive $d$-volume over $P$, with $C_p=K_p$ at each sampled base. The mixed measure sums $d$-volume on these fibers. Let $L>0$ on $C$ be the minimum of finitely many affine functions, and let $F\ge0$ on $C$ be concave and positive on every sampled own-fiber interior. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $0<s<1$, and put $f=L^sF^{1-s}$. Use buffered depth given by the infimum of closed caps with shifted threshold $N\cdot Y-\varepsilon f(Y)$ over covectors $N=(a,u)$ with $G(u)=1$ for a fixed norm $G$, together with the closed pure-base depth for the fiber masses. In relative base rank zero take that pure-base depth to be the full mass.

Suppose the largest sampled depth is $Q>0$. Let $J$ consist of the sampled maximizing bases whose closed pure-base depth exceeds $Q$. Assume that at every $p\in J$ the deepest mark $Y_p$ is unique, is in the own-fiber interior, and has an attaining nonvertical cap with proper own section. For each continuous covector $c$, let $J_c$ consist of those $p\in J$ at whose mark all affine functions active in the minimum defining $L$ have continuous slope $c$. Each nonempty $J_c$ equals $P\cap\operatorname{conv}J_c$, and distinct such classes occupy disjoint parities in the inherited lattice. Each such $c$ occurs as the own-fiber gradient of $L$ on a set of positive sampled volume.

## Proof

A real point of $C$ has buffered depth at least $Q$ precisely when its pure-base depth is at least $Q$ and for every nonvertical covector its shifted threshold is at most the right $Q$-quantile of the fixed projected measure. This follows because the upper tail of the nonvertical projected measure is continuous and nonincreasing, with compact support. The weighted geometric mean is concave and coordinatewise increasing on the nonnegative quadrant, so $f$ is concave. Thus all the nonvertical quantile inequalities define convex sets. The pure-base regions at levels at least $Q$ and strictly above $Q$ are convex as well. For any base halfspace containing a convex combination, at least one positive-weight base in the combination belongs to the halfspace. If every input base has depth strictly above $Q$, its finite minimum over those bases is strictly above $Q$, and this lower bound holds at the combination.

Take a strictly positive convex combination of marks from $J$ whose average base $r$ is integral. The averaged mark lies in $C$ and hence in the actual $r$-fiber. It has depth at least $Q$, while the pure-base depth at $r$ exceeds $Q$. It is therefore $Y_r$ for $r\in J$. At $Y_r$ use an attaining nonvertical cap of mass $Q$ with proper own section. Its threshold is the right $Q$-quantile because changing the threshold upward slightly removes a positive-volume strip of its own fiber. Every input mark satisfies the same quantile inequality. Averaging those inequalities, and using concavity of $f$ at the averaged mark, yields
$$
f(Y_r)=\sum_i t_i f(Y_{p_i}).
$$
Indeed the averaged shifted threshold is at most the quantile and the actual shifted threshold at $Y_r$ is at most that average by concavity. Concavity of $L$ and $F$ gives $L(Y_r)\ge\sum_i t_i L(Y_{p_i})$ and the corresponding inequality for $F$. The geometric mean of the two averages is at least the average of the geometric means. Both averages are positive. Strict monotonicity in each positive argument now forces equality in both component concavity inequalities, in particular $L(Y_r)=\sum_i t_i L(Y_{p_i})$.

An affine function active at $Y_r$ majorizes $L$ everywhere and has zero averaged gap at the input marks. It must be active at each input. For inputs from one class, this gives the same continuous slope for every active plane at $Y_r$, proving lattice closure. For equal-parity inputs from two classes use their integer midpoint, and an active plane there would have both slopes. This proves the parity claim. At an own-interior source all inactive planes have strict gaps. The finitely many active planes with slope $c$ agree on that own fiber, and remain the minimum on a small neighborhood of the source in it. This gives positive volume carrying gradient $c$.
