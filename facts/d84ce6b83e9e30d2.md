## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, $d\ge2$, with centroid $g$. Put $D=K-K$, let $\|\cdot\|_D$ be its gauge, and write $\gamma_j=(j/(j+1))^j$ for $j\ge1$. Suppose finitely many closed halfspaces $H_i=\{x:\ell_i(x-y)\ge0\}$ cover $\mathbb R^d$, where each $\ell_i$ has width one on $K$. Assume $|K\cap H_i|\le\tau|K|$ for every $i$, with $\gamma_d\le\tau<\gamma_{d-1}$. Then $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, and for every $v\in\partial D$,
$$
\max_i\ell_i(v)\ge\frac{\gamma_{d-1}-\tau}{d}.
$$
Equivalently, the convex hull of the $\ell_i$ contains $(\gamma_{d-1}-\tau)D^\circ/d$, where $D^\circ$ is the polar. Moreover,
$$
\|y-g\|_D
\le\min\left\{1,\frac{d^2(\tau-\gamma_d)}{\gamma_{d-1}-\tau}\right\}.
$$
In particular $\tau=\gamma_d$ forces $y=g$. If $0\le\epsilon=\tau-\gamma_d\le(\gamma_{d-1}-\gamma_d)/2$, the bound is at most $2d^2\epsilon/(\gamma_{d-1}-\gamma_d)$.

## Proof

The exterior-contact theorem 2eb9fc1dbf88b7c8 and the budget imply $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. Write $\Delta=\gamma_{d-1}-\tau>0$. For $v\in\partial D$ one has $y-v\notin\operatorname{int}K$, since otherwise $v$ would belong to the open set $y-\operatorname{int}K\subset D$. Translate all the halfspaces by $-v$. They still cover the entire space and now have the common boundary point $y-v$. The threshold shift in cap $i$ is $-\ell_i(v)$. The width-one section density bound gives a new mass fraction at most $\tau+d(\ell_i(v))_+$. The exterior-contact theorem supplies one translated cap with fraction at least $\gamma_{d-1}$. Thus $\max_i\ell_i(v)\ge\Delta/d$.

The support function of the convex hull of the normalized functionals is $v\mapsto\max_i\ell_i(v)$. The support function of $D^\circ$ is $\|\cdot\|_D$. Positive homogeneity extends the boundary estimate to all vectors, proving the polar inclusion.

If $y\ne g$, use $v=(g-y)/\|g-y\|_D$ and choose the corresponding cap with $\ell_i(v)\ge\Delta/d$. The centroid slab estimate for that cap gives
$$
\tau\ge\gamma_d+\ell_i(g-y)/d
\ge\gamma_d+\|g-y\|_D\Delta/d^2.
$$
Also $g-y\in D$ because $g,y\in K$. These two bounds give the result, including $y=g$ and the half-gap estimate.
