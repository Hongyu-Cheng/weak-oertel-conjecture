## Statement

\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put

\tag{1}

\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,\qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),\qquad

\tag{2}

\(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and

\tag{4}

Fix one arbitrary saturated-circuit datum

 \end{cases}

Choose once a primal optimizer and a gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer

695b44a4f0fdcd3a, and put

In the positive-load case retain the endpoint loads, normalized directions, and support contacts
\(\lambda_{e,z},u_{e,z},x_{e,z}\) belonging to this optimizer in the normalization of accepted fact dcccecd70b052857. Let

\tag{10}

\tag{11}

and assume the positive-overlap branch

\tag{12}

For \(e=\{z,a\}\), define its integral half-direction at \(z\) by

\tag{13}

Let \(\mathcal A_2(r)\) be the family of unordered pairs

 \quad(j,k\in\{0,1\}),

Thus (27) is a genuine nonseparable cap-versus-stress square.

## Proof

Accepted fact 179add32aa016da1 gives (4), or else (5) together with all of its synchronized conclusions. In the latter case it proves
\[
 \sum_{\xi\notin\mathcal G}A_\xi<4G
\tag{49}
\]
and, after this bad-parity loss has been removed, defines (8) with support entirely in \(\mathcal G\) and proves
\[
 S_i:=\sum_es_{i,e}>
 \frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{8}W.
\tag{50}
\]
Thus bad classes are removed before the overlap is formed: neither \(r\) nor any reservoir constructed from \(r\) has support in a bad class. Accepted fact 695b44a4f0fdcd3a gives the exact clipped identity
\[
 S_i
 =\sum_e\min\{s_{i,e},\omega_e\}
  +\sum_e(s_{i,e}-\omega_e)_+
 =R+\sum_e(s_{i,e}-\omega_e)_+.
\tag{51}
\]
Its affine-retention conclusion applies when the second term is macroscopic. Hypothesis (12) is the positive-overlap branch. From (8),
\[
 0\leq r_e\leq s_{i,e}\leq\frac12q_e^\times.
\tag{52}
\]

We rerun the transverse residual argument for these subweights. The feasible set in (14)-(15) is a nonempty compact polytope. For an optimizer put
\[
 \kappa_e
 :=\sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal A_2(r)}
       \vartheta_{\{e,f\}},\qquad
 \zeta_e:=r_e-\kappa_e\geq0,\qquad
 L:=\sum_e\zeta_e.
\tag{53}
\]
Each pairing variable is charged to both source edges, so
\[
 L=R-2P.
\tag{54}
\]
If two positive-residual edges share an endpoint and have independent half-directions there, their pairing variable can be increased. Thus all positive-residual incident directions at any vertex are pairwise dependent. Propagation along paths shows that each connected component of the positive-residual graph lies in one affine lattice line.

We now apply the affine-line mass estimate proved in accepted fact
cacf8017a747fd4c and redo its product calculation at the sharper scale (52). A residual component lies in one good parity class \(\xi\). Let \(V_{\xi,j}\) be its vertex set and put
\[
 B_{\xi,j}:=\sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z.
\]
The residual components in a fixed class have disjoint vertex sets. Since \(G<W/4\), the affine-line estimate in accepted fact cacf8017a747fd4c gives
\[
 B_{\xi,j}\leq2^{3-n}G.
\tag{55}
\]
Using (7), (52), and
\(\sum_jB_{\xi,j}\leq A_\xi\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{e\subseteq\xi}\zeta_e
 &\leq
 \sum_j\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V_{\xi,j}}
 \frac12q^\times_{\{u,v\}}\\
 &=\frac{\gamma_d}{4A_\xi}
 \sum_j\left(B_{\xi,j}^2
 -\sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z^2\right)\\
 &\leq\frac{\gamma_d}{4}\max_jB_{\xi,j}
 \leq\gamma_d2^{1-n}G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{56}
\]
There are at most \(2^n\) parity classes and \(\gamma_d\leq1/2\). Hence
\[
 \boxed{L\leq2\gamma_dG\leq G.}
\tag{57}
\]
Equations (54) and (57) give the first inequality in (16). Accepted fact 695b44a4f0fdcd3a records \(\gamma_d\geq e^{-1}\). Equations (5) and (12) give
\[
 \frac GR<\frac{4}{25\gamma_d}\leq\frac{4e}{25},
\tag{58}
\]
which gives the second inequality in (16). They also give directly
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P
 &\geq\frac{R-G}{2}\\
 &>\frac12\left(
 \frac{\gamma_d\eta^2}{16}
 -\frac{\eta^2}{100}\right)W
 =\frac{25\gamma_d-4}{800}\eta^2W,
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]
which proves the third inequality. Equations (20)-(21) follow by partitioning the optimizer according to the contact equality at its unique common endpoint.

Because \(R>0\), some \(\omega_e>0\). Equation (10) and accepted fact
dcccecd70b052857 imply that at every loaded endpoint all stress contacts and normalized stress directions collapse to \(x_z,u_z\). The same accepted fact proves that every nonzero direction of \(\mathcal K_z\) has a unique support contact and that every such contact has a unique nonzero outward-normal ray. Since
\[
 h_{D_z}(u_z)=h_{D_z}(\widehat w_{i,z})=1,
\]
this proves (18).

The cap-versus-stress gap is
\[
 g_{i,z}^{\mathrm{cap}}
 :=\langle u_z-\widehat w_{i,z},
             x_z-y_{i,z}\rangle
\tag{60}
\]
\[
 =\bigl(1-\langle u_z,y_{i,z}-c_z\rangle\bigr)
 +\bigl(1-\langle\widehat w_{i,z},x_z-c_z\rangle\bigr)
 \geq0.
\tag{61}
\]
Both summands are support gaps. Equality forces each cross point to be a support contact in the other direction, and contact uniqueness then gives
\(x_z=y_{i,z}\). Thus (60) is positive exactly in the type-\({\rm neq}\) branch. No quantitative lower bound on this gap is used.

Fix a positive contribution and orient it as in (22). Feasibility and (52) give

Choose exactly the nonzero reflected pairs in this slice in the reflected-cloud score. The set of their centroid points is nonempty, their available cloud energy is at least \(K_{B,m_0}\), and their affine rank is (47). This proves (48).

Every affine dimension used above is therefore taken only after the relevant point set or measure has been proved nonempty. The proof constructs each rank-two certificate separately and never adds energies across different charts.
