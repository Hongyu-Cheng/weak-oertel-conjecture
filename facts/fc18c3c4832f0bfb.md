## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)
 \quad(a_z>0).
\tag{1}
\]
For a parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}a_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Use the half-strength canonical product reservoir \(r\) of accepted fact `c81f02314cb34f8b`. Thus, on every good parity class,
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \qquad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2),
\tag{3}
\]
and \(r_e=0\) otherwise. For every reservoir edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 L_e:=\frac12(\mathcal K_u+\mathcal K_v).
\tag{4}
\]

Put \(Q:=|r|=\sum_e r_e\) and let \(R:=R_C(r)\) have the exact
affine-retention normalization of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d.

Let \((\alpha_e)_e\) be arbitrary numbers satisfying
\[
 0\leq\alpha_e\leq r_e,
\tag{5}
\]
and, whenever \(\alpha_e>0\), choose an arbitrary point
\(\ell_e\in L_e\). Define the positive target root-mass
\[
 \vartheta_e
 :=2^{-(d+1)}
 \bigl(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}\bigr)^d
 \qquad(e=\{u,v\}).
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \ell_e\in\mathsf M_{\vartheta_e}(C_{m_e}),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_e\geq\frac12\sqrt{a_ua_v}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix a parity class \(\xi\) and a physical target \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), and put
\[
 \mathcal E_{\xi,m}
 :=\{e=\{u,v\}:\alpha_e>0,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2,\ m_e=m\},
\tag{8}
\]
\[
 \alpha_{\xi,m}:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\xi,m}}\alpha_e,
 \qquad
 \kappa_{\xi,m}:=
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\xi,m}}
 \frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}.
\tag{9}
\]
For every nonempty \(\mathcal E_{\xi,m}\), define
\[
 x_{\xi,m}:=
 \frac1{\alpha_{\xi,m}}
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\xi,m}}\alpha_e\ell_e.
\tag{10}
\]
There is a measurable density \(g_{\xi,m}\) on \(C_m\) such that
\[
 0\leq g_{\xi,m}\leq\kappa_{\xi,m}\leq\frac14
 \quad\text{a.e.},
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}g_{\xi,m}=\alpha_{\xi,m},
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}xg_{\xi,m}(x)\,dx
 =\alpha_{\xi,m}x_{\xi,m}.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{x_{\xi,m}\in\mathsf M_{\alpha_{\xi,m}}(C_m).}
\tag{12}
\]

The parity colors can be combined with an exact congestion loss. For every target with
\[
 \alpha_m:=\sum_\xi\alpha_{\xi,m}>0,
\tag{13}
\]
put
\[
 \kappa_m:=\sum_\xi\kappa_{\xi,m},
 \qquad
 \chi_m:=\frac1{\max\{1,\kappa_m\}},
 \qquad
 \widehat\alpha_m:=\chi_m\alpha_m,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
 x_m:=\frac1{\alpha_m}
 \sum_\xi\alpha_{\xi,m}x_{\xi,m}.
\tag{15}
\]
Empty color-target cells contribute zero to these sums. Then
\[
 \boxed{x_m\in\mathsf M_{\widehat\alpha_m}(C_m).}
\tag{16}
\]
If \(k_m\) is the number of colors with \(\alpha_{\xi,m}>0\), then
\[
 \kappa_m\leq\frac{k_m}{4},
 \qquad
 \chi_m\geq\min\left\{1,\frac4{k_m}\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(k_m\leq4\) gives lossless pooling at that target.

Let
\[
 \widehat E:=\{m:\alpha_m>0\},
 \qquad
 \widehat\alpha:=\sum_{m\in\widehat E}\widehat\alpha_m.
\tag{18}
\]
Assume \(\widehat E\ne\varnothing\), put
\[
 \rho_J:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\widehat E),
\tag{19}
\]
and define
\[
 r_J:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(\widehat E)\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{x_m-s(m):m\in\widehat E\}.
\tag{20}
\]
With
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 \sigma_J:=\gamma_d
 \sum_{m\in\widehat E}
 \widehat\alpha_m\delta_{(m,x_m)},
\tag{21}
\]
let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed-volume measure of \(C\), so that for an
ambient halfspace \(H\) with fiber section \(H_z\),
\[
 \mu_C(H)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
Then, for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\),
\[
 \sigma_J(H)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{22}
\]
Moreover, in the normalizations of accepted facts `be359f8a6c628483` and `6b09aae189415c70`,
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\rm sel}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_J}
 \frac{\widehat\alpha}{r_J+1},
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_J}
 \frac{\widehat\alpha}{r_J+1}.}
\tag{23}
\]
Every individual pooled target also gives
\[
 \boxed{D\geq\gamma_d2^n\max_{m\in\widehat E}\widehat\alpha_m.}
\tag{24}
\]

Finally apply the construction to the load-minimal primal-dual optimizer of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` for the canonical reservoir: take
\[
 \alpha_e:=\omega_e,
\tag{25}
\]
and take its exposed contact \(\ell_e\in L_e\) on every positive-load edge. Write
\[
 \Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
 \qquad
 \Omega_m:=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\omega_e,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega:=\sum_m\chi_m\Omega_m.
\tag{26}
\]
In the canonical hard branch
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}
\tag{27}
\]
of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, one has
\[
 R<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 \Omega\geq Q-R>\frac{99Q}{100},
\tag{28}
\]
while the pooled system satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_J}
 \frac{\widehat\Omega}{r_J+1}
 <\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 \chi_m\Omega_m
 <\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n}
 \quad(m\in\widehat E).}
\tag{29}
\]
Thus affine failure exports macroscopic load into an exact genuine-fiber graph. If that graph is not terminal, the first unsupported implication is precisely a bound converting the cross-color congestion \(\kappa_m\) and the residual rank \(r_J\) into one of the existing capacities. Within one parity color, and across at most four colors, no such compatibility gap remains.

## Proof

The positive support of the reservoir is finite because the base projection of the compact body \(C\) meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in finitely many points. Every endpoint of a positive reservoir edge has positive fiber volume, so every metronoid and root in (1)--(7) is defined.

Fix an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(\alpha_e>0\). By (4), choose
\[
 p_{e,u}\in\mathcal K_u,
 \qquad
 p_{e,v}\in\mathcal K_v,
 \qquad
 \ell_e=\frac{p_{e,u}+p_{e,v}}2.
\tag{30}
\]
The two positive-root triples
\[
 \left(u,p_{e,u},(a_u/2)^{1/d}\right),
 \qquad
 \left(v,p_{e,v},(a_v/2)^{1/d}\right)
\tag{31}
\]
belong to the positive-root metronoid set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. That accepted fact proves that this set itself is convex before closure. Taking their midpoint gives
\[
 \left(m_e,\ell_e,
 2^{-1-1/d}(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d})\right)
\tag{32}
\]
in the same set. Raising the last coordinate to the \(d\)-th power proves the first part of (7). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2\right)^d
 \geq\sqrt{a_ua_v},
\tag{33}
\]
and (6) is one half of the left side in (33), proving the second part of (7).

Fix \((\xi,m)\) with \(\mathcal E_{\xi,m}\ne\varnothing\). For each edge in this cell, (7) supplies a measurable density \(f_e\) on \(C_m\) such that
\[
 0\leq f_e\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}f_e=\vartheta_e,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}xf_e(x)\,dx=\vartheta_e\ell_e.
\tag{34}
\]
Set
\[
 g_{\xi,m}:=
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\xi,m}}
 \frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}f_e.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (34)--(35) immediately give the mass and first-moment identities in (11), and pointwise
\[
 0\leq g_{\xi,m}\leq\kappa_{\xi,m}.
\tag{36}
\]

It remains to prove the dimension-free quarter bound. Every edge in this cell belongs to the good class \(\xi\), so (3), (5), and (7) give
\[
 \frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}
 \leq
 \frac{r_e}{\vartheta_e}
 \leq
 \frac{\sqrt{a_ua_v}}{2A_\xi}.
\tag{37}
\]
The reflection \(u\mapsto2m-u\) preserves the parity class and is an involution. Every unordered distinct reflected pair is counted twice in the corresponding ordered sum. Hence Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\xi,m}}\sqrt{a_ua_v}
 &\leq
 \sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}\sqrt{a_za_{2m-z}}\\
 &\leq
 \left(\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_{2m-z}\right)^{1/2}
 =A_\xi.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
Summing (37) and using (38) proves
\[
 \kappa_{\xi,m}\leq\frac14.
\tag{39}
\]
Thus (35) is itself admissible for the metronoid of mass \(\alpha_{\xi,m}\), proving (11)--(12).

Fix a target \(m\) with \(\alpha_m>0\), and define
\[
 g_m:=\chi_m\sum_\xi g_{\xi,m}.
\tag{40}
\]
Equations (11) and (14) give
\[
 0\leq g_m\leq\chi_m\kappa_m\leq1.
\tag{41}
\]
The mass of (40) is \(\chi_m\alpha_m=\widehat\alpha_m\), and its normalized first moment is (15). This proves (16). Summing (39) over the \(k_m\) nonempty colors gives \(\kappa_m\leq k_m/4\), and taking reciprocals through (14) proves (17).

Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied to (16), gives
\[
 x_m\in C_m,
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m,x_m)\geq\gamma_d\widehat\alpha_m.
\tag{42}
\]
For a closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing the atom \((m,x_m)\), the fiber section \(H_m\) is either a proper closed halfspace containing \(x_m\), or the whole continuous space. In both cases its \(C_m\)-volume is at least the right side of (42). Distinct \(m\)'s are distinct physical fibers. Summing (42) over precisely the atoms of \(\sigma_J\) that lie in \(H\), and retaining all other nonnegative fiber sections, proves (22). Taking one target at a time gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\widehat\alpha_m,
\tag{43}
\]
and hence (24).

Use the points \(x_m\), the weights \(\gamma_d\widehat\alpha_m\), and an affine map attaining (20) in the definition of the residual-selector capacity from accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Equation (42) verifies every fiber-depth constraint, and its normalization gives the first inequality in (23). Alternatively, apply the dominated-measure residual theorem in the same accepted fact to (21)--(22): the base rank is \(\rho_J\), the residual rank is \(r_J\), and the measure mass is \(\gamma_d\widehat\alpha\). Multiplication of its depth conclusion by \(2^n\) gives the second inequality in (23).

For the canonical load-minimal optimizer, accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` proves
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq r_e,
 \qquad
 \ell_e\in L_e,
 \qquad
 Q-R\leq\Omega.
\tag{44}
\]
Thus (25) satisfies every hypothesis above. In the hard branch (27), the definition of \(\mathcal A(C)\) in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives
\[
 R<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 R_{\rm sel}(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{45}
\]
Equations (44)--(45) prove (28). The first inequality in (23), followed by (45), proves the first inequality in (29), and (24), followed by (45), proves the second. This proves every assertion.
