## Statement

Let $C$ be the canonical convex hull of positive $d$-dimensional convex fibers $K_z$ at the sites of a nonempty finite $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, with $d\ge1$. Assume that the fibers of $C$ at the sites of $P$ are exactly the prescribed $K_z$. Write $S$ for their union at their sites, $m$ for the sum of their Lebesgue volumes, and $M=m(S)$. Let $L=\min_i A_i$ be a finite minimum of affine functions, nonnegative on $C$ and positive in each own-fiber interior. Assume $\int_S\log L\,dm=0$ and $\int_S L^{-1}\,dm<\infty$. For any affine $h$ on the relative physical space put $C_t=C\cap\{L+th\ge0\}$ and $S_t=S\cap C_t$, for $t>0$. For small $t$ every sampled fiber remains positive, their family is compatible and has exactly the old sites, and each fiber has inward radial displacement $O(t)$ with zero first radial velocity at $\mathcal H^{d-1}$-almost every old boundary point. The mass and logarithmic estimates are
$$
m(S\setminus S_t)=o(t),\qquad
\int_{S\setminus S_t}|\log L|\,dm=o(t),\qquad
\int_{S_t}\log(L+th)\,dm=t\int_S h/L\,dm+o(t).
$$
The positive concave function $U_t=(L+th+t^2)\exp[-m(S_t)^{-1}\int_{S_t}\log(L+th+t^2)\,dm]$ on $C_t$ has
$U_t=L+t(h-LH)+o(t)$ uniformly on compact sets of own-fiber interior marks, where $H=M^{-1}\int_S h/L\,dm$. Every fixed closed-cap mass, the mixed moments and covariance, and the marked projective energies of the fibers change by $o(t)$, with the energy estimate uniform on such compact mark sets. The energy at a mark $y$ in a body $K$ is the minimum of $\int_K(1+a\cdot(x-y))^{-d-1}\,dx$ over denominators positive on $K$.

## Proof

Fix a sampled fiber $K$. Every $A_i$ restricted to $K$ is nonnegative and positive in the interior. Its inverse is integrable because $A_i\ge L$. A plane with positive minimum contributes no sufficiently small sublevel set. In dimension one an affine function vanishing at an interval endpoint has divergent inverse integral, so all minima are positive in that dimension. Otherwise, for a restriction $A$ with zero minimum, let $s=A(x)$ and let $g(s)$ be the section area divided by the norm of the continuous gradient of $A$. The gradient is nonzero, and Fubini in affine coordinates gives $g(s)\,ds$ as the pushforward of volume. By convexity and Brunn–Minkowski, $g^{1/(d-1)}$ is positive and concave on the interior height interval. Its one-sided limit at zero is finite by boundedness of the sections. The integrability of $g(s)/s$ forces that limit to be zero. Concavity then makes $g$ nondecreasing near zero: choose two interior heights with the later root larger, and use the nonincreasing order of secant slopes. For small $t$, $\int_t^{\sqrt t}g(s)\,ds/s\ge\frac12g(t)|\log t|$, so $g(t)|\log t|\to0$. For any fixed $c>0$, integration on $[0,ct]$ gives both $|\{x\in K:A(x)\le ct\}|=o(t)$ and $\int_{\{A\le ct\}}|\log A|\,dx=o(t)$. Partition each fiber measurably by the first affine term attaining $L$. Summation gives both estimates for $L$.

For the retained tilted logarithm on $\{A\le ct\}$, use affine coordinates $(q,s)$ with $s=A(x)$, placing a point of the zero face at $(0,0)$ and an interior point at $(0,b)$, $b>0$, by a shear. The projection of the old collar onto the $q$-space lies in twice the section at height $2ct$: interpolate from $(q,s)$ toward $(0,b)$ to reach height $2ct$. The resulting $q$ is multiplied by $(b-2ct)/(b-s)\in[1/2,1]$, and zero belongs to the section. Thus the projection has area $O(g(2ct))$ in these coordinates. At fixed $q$ the new height $r=A+th$ has derivative $1+O(t)$ in $s$, and on the retained collar $0<r\le C_1t$ for a fixed $C_1$. The integral of the absolute new logarithm there is bounded by a constant times $g(2ct)\int_0^{C_1t}|\log r|\,dr=o(t)$. Zero levels can be discarded in the integrals because the new height gradient stays nonzero. The same minimizing affine index remains active after the common tilt $th$, so the finite partition gives this bound on the retained collar of $L$.

Choose an own-interior point $o_z$ in each old fiber. The homothety about $o_z$ with ratio $1-C_2 t$ lies in the corresponding cut fiber if the common constant $C_2$ is large enough: on that homothety concavity gives $L\ge C_2tL(z,o_z)$, whereas $h$ is bounded on $C$. The cut fibers are the exact sections of the cut set because the old fibers were exact. The cut set is convex and stays over $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, proving compatibility and site stability. This homothety bounds the radial and Hausdorff changes by $O(t)$ with the fixed centers $o_z$. An old boundary point with $L>0$ remains unchanged along its ray for small $t$. Each nonempty zero face of an $A_i$ is surface-null. Indeed a positive-area zero face, interpolated toward any interior point, would give a positive lower bound on section areas at all sufficiently small heights, contradicting inverse integrability. This proves the radial velocity assertion.

Take $c>2\sup_C|h|+1$. All removed points are in the old collar $\{L\le ct\}$, and outside it the logarithmic difference divided by $t$ is bounded by $2\sup_C|h|/L$ and tends to $h/L$. Dominated convergence on this bulk and the two collar estimates give the displayed logarithmic expansion. On the cut set every denominator $A_i+th$ is nonnegative. For a positive floor $\eta$, the increase $\log(1+\eta/(L+th))$ is at most the sum of the corresponding increases for the affine denominators. For each nonconstant old restriction, the perturbed gradient stays bounded away from zero and all sections of the cut body have bounded area. Its logarithmic increase integrates to $O(\eta(1+|\log\eta|))$ by bounding it by a constant times $\int_0^B\log(1+\eta/r)\,dr$, for a fixed upper bound $B$. A constant old restriction has positive value and remains uniformly positive, giving $O(\eta)$. The choice $\eta=t^2$ therefore changes the logarithmic integral by $o(t)$. The stated normalization follows by expansion of the exponential and $m(S_t)=M-o(t)$.

Cap masses and moments differ by at most a fixed bound on their integrands times the lost mass, and division by positive total or fiber masses gives the same order for the covariance. For the energy, compact sets of old own-interior marks retain centered balls of one fixed radius in the cut fibers. Positivity on these balls bounds the norms of the optimizing parameters uniformly. Accepted fact a56f8711406a3870 gives a denominator floor $1/(d+1)$ for each minimizer on its own body, since the mark is interior to a full-dimensional compact convex body on both sides. The Hausdorff displacement and the parameter norm bound give the new minimizer a floor $1/[2(d+1)]$ on the old body for small $t$, uniformly in the marks. The old minimizer already has this floor on the new body. Testing each minimizer on the other body bounds the energy difference by a fixed constant times the lost fiber volume, which is $o(t)$.
