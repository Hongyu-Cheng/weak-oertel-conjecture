## Statement

Fix any integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the log-affine specialization \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) in accepted fact 604889294a17d7df and the tree construction of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, let \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), let \(b_\ell=\lfloor2^\ell/(8\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), and put \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r\), \(Q=Q_k\), \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), \(n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\), and \(N=n+1\). The entrance heights retain their canonical meaning: \(a_0=0\), and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). No activity parameter is denoted by \(a\).

Choose any allowed symmetric saturated chain partition, and retain its exact objects \(D_m,F_m,\mathcal T_{m,Q},A_{m,Q},P_{m,Q}\). The support and all its columns are unchanged:
$$
\widehat A
=\lbrace (z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
 a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
For every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\) with \(|\gamma|\le1/(mQ)\), define \(\sigma(z)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}z_u\), put \(w_{(z,t)}=e^{\gamma\sigma(z)}2^{-t}\) on \(\widehat A\), and put all other integer weights equal to zero. Let \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}\) and \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). For \(\mathbf I\in\widehat A\), use its actual original halfspace depth
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)
=\inf\left\lbrace
 \sum_{\mathbf J\in H\cap\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}:
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I
\right\rbrace.
$$
Then
$$
\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3
\ge2^{-30}8^{-N}.
$$
The parameters, original law, and ambient halfspaces are exactly those in this statement. In particular, this is a perturbation of the original actual-depth conclusion of accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba, rather than a moment of a replacement measure.

## Proof

Write \(\varepsilon=|\gamma|\), \(\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}\), and \(H_j=\sum_{r=1}^{j}1/r\). The assumptions give \(b_8=4\), hence \(Q\ge4\) and \(m\ge1250\). Accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df give \(|D_m|=2^m\eta\), the injective successor \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), the exact original support, and \(\eta^Q>3/4\). We use their forward paths and inverse reconstruction with precisely those meanings.

We first prove the tilted censuses and the normalizer bound. For any real \(\lambda\), define \(Z(\lambda)=1+e^\lambda\) and \(D(\lambda)=\sum_{c\in D_m}e^{\lambda\sum_i c_i}\). At tree level \(s\), the Boolean sources with forward path length at least \(s\) are in bijection with their ordered list of \(s\) successive source blocks in \(D_m\) and their remaining free Boolean coordinates. The successive blocks are disjoint, their choices determine their locations, and exactly \(ms\) coordinates are assigned. The level-\(s\) point changes exactly \(s\) selected source zeros to twos. Consequently the bijection proves the weighted identity
$$
\sum_{\substack{z\in A_{m,Q}\\\operatorname{level}(z)=s}}
 e^{\lambda\sigma(z)}
=e^{2\lambda s}D(\lambda)^sZ(\lambda)^{n-ms}.
$$
No location-dependent weight occurs because the same field \(\lambda\) is used at every coordinate.

As in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba, let \(\mathcal C_s\) be the level-\(s\) base points whose inverse reconstruction succeeds through level \(Q\). Their first \(s\) path blocks are forward source blocks in \(D_m\) with the selected zero changed to two; the next \(Q-s\) blocks are in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\). These choices and all remaining Boolean coordinates determine the point uniquely. Conversely its twos recover the forward prefix, and injectivity of \(F_m\) recovers the inverse suffix. Thus this is a bijection using exactly \(Q\) disjoint blocks. Since \(F_m\) is injective and increases the coordinate sum by one,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c\in\operatorname{im}F_m}e^{\lambda\sum_i c_i}
&=e^\lambda D(\lambda),\\
\sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s}e^{\lambda\sigma(j)}
&=e^{2\lambda s}D(\lambda)^s
 \bigl(e^\lambda D(\lambda)\bigr)^{Q-s}Z(\lambda)^{n-mQ}\\
&=e^{\lambda(Q+s)}D(\lambda)^QZ(\lambda)^{n-mQ}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular the unrestricted Boolean coordinates have been summed exactly, not discarded.

Because \(D_m\) is a subset of the Boolean cube, \(D(\lambda)\le Z(\lambda)^m\). Also each term of \(D(\lambda)\) is at least \(e^{m\min(\lambda,0)}\), whereas \(Z(\lambda)^m\le2^m e^{m\max(\lambda,0)}\). It follows that
$$
\eta e^{-m|\lambda|}
\le\frac{D(\lambda)}{Z(\lambda)^m}\le1,
\qquad
\sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s}e^{\lambda\sigma(j)}
\ge\eta^Qe^{-(m+2)Q|\lambda|}Z(\lambda)^n.
$$
Here \(Q+s\le2Q\) bounds the possibly negative factor \(e^{\lambda(Q+s)}\).

The level census at \(\lambda=\gamma\) gives an upper bound \(e^{2Q\varepsilon}Z(\gamma)^n\) for each level's total spatial weight. Put \(R_k=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}\). Summing every original column and then using the geometric tails and \(b_\ell2^{-\ell}\le1/(8\ell)\) proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
W&\le e^{2Q\varepsilon}Z(\gamma)^nR_k,\\
R_k&\le2+2\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell2^{-\ell}
\le2+\frac{H_k-H_7}{4}
\le\frac{9+\log k}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality uses \(H_k\le1+\log k\). These bounds involve the full original normalizer.

For later use, twice applying \((x+y)^2\le2(x^2+y^2)\) yields \((1+x)^4\le8(1+x^4)\) for every \(x>0\). Hence
$$
\frac{Z(4\gamma)^n}{Z(\gamma)^{4n}}\ge8^{-n}.
$$

We next transfer the actual-halfspace argument of accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba. Let \(\nu=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I}\delta_{\mathbf I}\), and let \(h_\nu(\mathbf I)=W\tau_p(\mathbf I)\). Suppose first that \(k\ge2^{16}\), and set \(u=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\), \(v=2u\). Then \(u\ge256\), \(8\le u<v\le k\), and \(\log k\ge16\log2>32/3>9\). The inequality \(\log2>2/3\) follows by integrating the tangent line of the strictly convex function \(1/x\) at \(3/2\) over \([1,2]\).

We use the actual sampling subset
$$
\mathcal G
=\lbrace (j,a_s+1):j\in\mathcal C_s,\quad8\le a_s\le u\rbrace.
$$
Every displayed height belongs to the original column. The unchanged scalar estimates used in d9c4b095a13caaba can be checked directly as follows. Define \(U=\sum_{\ell=8}^{u}b_\ell2^{-\ell}\) and \(T=\sum_{\ell=v}^{k}b_\ell2^{-\ell}\). The floor inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
U&\ge\frac{H_u-H_7}{8}-\frac1{128}
\ge\frac{\log k}{16}-\frac{H_7}{8}-\frac1{128}
\ge\frac{\log k}{32},\\
T&\ge\frac{H_k-H_{v-1}}8-2^{1-v}
\ge\frac18\log\frac{k+1}{v}-2^{1-v}\\
&\ge\frac{\log k}{16}-\frac{\log2}{8}-2^{1-v}
\ge\frac{\log k}{32}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first last inequality, \(H_7=363/140<13/5\) implies \(H_7/8+1/128<1/3<(\log k)/32\). For the second, \(v\ge512\), \(\log2<1\), and \((\log k)/32>1/3\) suffice. We used \(u+1>\sqrt{k}\), \(v\le2\sqrt{k}\), and the elementary integral bounds for harmonic sums. Also \(2^u\ge256k\): since \(k<(u+1)^2\), it suffices that \(2^u/(u+1)^2\) is increasing for integers \(u\ge2\) and that \(2^{256}>256\cdot257^2\).

Fix \(\mathbf I=(j,h)\in\mathcal G\), let \(s=\operatorname{level}(j)\), and write \(h=a_s+1\). Its complete inverse continuation supplies coordinates \(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_Q\) with \(j_{u_r}=1\). Put \(V_r=\sum_{\ell=s+1}^{r}e_{u_\ell}\). The reconstruction in 032423921efad6c8 gives actual base points \(j-V_r\) of level \(s\) and \(j+V_r\) of level \(r\). It also gives \(j-e_{u_r}\in A_{m,Q}\) of level \(s\): changing that one coordinate from one to zero leaves all first \(s\) forward blocks unchanged. Therefore the original indices \((j\pm V_r,a_r)\) and \((j-e_{u_r},a_s)\) all exist whenever used below. Their spatial coordinate sums differ from \(\sigma(j)\) by at most \(Q\). Each of their new weights is thus at least \(e^{\gamma\sigma(j)-Q\varepsilon}\) times its untilted weight \(2^{-t}\).

For completeness, the geometry selects enough such indices in every halfspace. Consider a proper closed halfspace through \(\mathbf I\), written as
$$
H=\lbrace(z,t):c^{\mathsf T}(z-j)+b(t-h)\ge0\rbrace,
\qquad(c,b)\ne(0,0).
$$
If \(b\ge0\), for each \(r\) with \(a_r\ge v\) at least one of \((j-V_r,a_r)\) and \((j+V_r,a_r)\) is in \(H\), since their halfspace values are \(\pm c^{\mathsf T}V_r+b(a_r-h)\) and \(a_r>h\). The chosen indices are distinct across \(r\): lower endpoints have distinct \(V_r\), upper endpoints have distinct tree levels, and their levels distinguish the two families. Their untilted weight is at least \(T\).

If \(b<0\), put \(\beta=-b\) and call \(r>s\) bad when \(c_{u_r}\le\beta\). Let \(L\) be the number of bad indices. Each corresponding distinct neighbor \((j-e_{u_r},a_s)\) is in \(H\), because its halfspace value is \(\beta-c_{u_r}\ge0\). If \(L\ge2^{a_s}(\log k)/128\), these neighbors have untilted weight at least \((\log k)/128\).

Otherwise define \(S_r=\beta^{-1}c^{\mathsf T}V_r\). In any maximal run of nonbad indices, its successive increments exceed one, so at most \(2k+1\) of its values lie in \([-k,k]\). There are at most \(L+1\) runs and at most \(L\) bad indices. Since \(a_s\le u\) and \(L<2^{a_s}(\log k)/128\),
$$
\bigl|\lbrace r:s<r\le Q,\ |S_r|\le k\rbrace\bigr|
\le(2k+2)(L+1)\le4k\,2^u\log k.
$$
For every remaining \(r\) with \(a_r\ge v\), one of the same two endpoints is in \(H\), since its halfspace value is \(\beta(|S_r|-(a_r-h))>0\). Discarding the exceptional indices loses at most \(4k2^u(\log k)2^{-v}\) in untilted weight. The selected distinct endpoints therefore have untilted weight at least
$$
T-4k2^{-u}\log k
\ge\frac{\log k}{32}-\frac{\log k}{64}
=\frac{\log k}{64}.
$$
Thus in all cases a family of actual original indices inside \(H\), each spatially within coordinate-sum distance \(Q\) from \(j\), has untilted weight at least \((\log k)/128\). Every containing proper halfspace contains a parallel one through \(\mathbf I\); the whole-space case also contains the selected indices. Comparing just these selected weights proves the tilted actual-depth bound
$$
h_\nu(\mathbf I)
\ge e^{\gamma\sigma(j)-Q\varepsilon}\frac{\log k}{128}
\qquad(\mathbf I=(j,a_s+1)\in\mathcal G).
$$
This comparison does not assert a bounded density ratio for the full measures.

We now sum under the original tilted sampling law, retaining its fourth normalizer. The complete-point census at \(\lambda=4\gamma\), the inequality \(\eta^Q>3/4\), and \(U\ge(\log k)/32\) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3
&=\frac1{W^4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I}h_\nu(\mathbf I)^3\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-3Q\varepsilon}(\log k)^3}{128^3W^4}
 \sum_{\substack{s:8\le a_s\le u}}2^{-(a_s+1)}
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s}e^{4\gamma\sigma(j)}\\
&\ge\frac{\eta^Q e^{-(4m+11)Q\varepsilon}
 Z(4\gamma)^n U(\log k)^3}{2\cdot128^3W^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since \(\log k>9\), the original normalizer bound proved above gives \(W\le e^{2Q\varepsilon}Z(\gamma)^n(\log k)/2\). Substitution, including its fourth power, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3
&\ge3\cdot2^{-25}e^{-(4m+19)Q\varepsilon}
 \frac{Z(4\gamma)^n}{Z(\gamma)^{4n}}\\
&\ge3\cdot2^{-25}e^{-5}8^{-n}
=3\cdot2^{-22}e^{-5}8^{-N}
>2^{-30}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed \((4m+19)Q\varepsilon\le4+19/m<5\). Finally \(e<3\) implies \(e^5<243<256\), which proves the last inequality. This establishes the result for \(k\ge2^{16}\).

For \(8\le k<2^{16}\), all original Boolean base points at height zero belong to \(\widehat A\). At any original atom, every containing halfspace includes its own weight, so \(\tau_p(\mathbf I)\ge p_{\mathbf I}\). The Boolean atoms at height zero have fourth-power weight sum \(Z(4\gamma)^n\). Since \(\log k<16\), the proved normalizer bound has \(R_k<8\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3
&\ge\frac{Z(4\gamma)^n}{W^4}\\
&\ge e^{-8Q\varepsilon}8^{-4}
 \frac{Z(4\gamma)^n}{Z(\gamma)^{4n}}\\
&\ge e^{-8/m}2^{-9}8^{-N}
>2^{-10}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-30}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(8/m\le8/1250<2/3<\log2\), so \(e^{-8/m}>1/2\). Both ranges use the full original support, every original column height, and its actual original normalizer and halfspace depth. The conclusion is uniform over all allowed chain partitions and all \(mQ|\gamma|\le1\).
