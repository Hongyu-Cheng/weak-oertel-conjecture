## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad

Assume
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(D_+)=\mathbb R^n.
\tag{1}
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), write
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in H\}.
\tag{2}
\]

\[
 \gamma_k:=\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^k.
\tag{3}
\]

An affine subspace \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is called primitive if
\(A=b+V\) for some \(b\in\mathbb Z^n\) and
\(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is a saturated sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^n\) of
rank \(\dim V\).  For such \(A\), put
\[
 W_A:=\sum_{z\in D_+\cap A}a_z.
\]
Assume the strict no-rank-drop condition
\[
 W_A<2^{\dim A-n}W
 \quad\text{for every proper primitive affine }A\subsetneq\mathbb R^n.
\tag{NR}
\]

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) and \(0<t\le|K|_d\), define
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\le f\le1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf=t
 \right\}.
\]
For finite nonempty \(E\subseteq D_+\) and
\(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), put
\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m\right\},
\]
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]
Let \(G=\mathfrak C_{\rm core}(C)\) be the supremum of
\(\sum_{z\in E}t_z\) over all \(E,m\) as above and all
\(0<t_z\le a_z\) such that \(\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing\) and

\(m\).  This is the capacity of accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`.

For \(z\in D_+\), let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\), and put

\tag{6}

## Proof

We first establish (16).  The affine hull of integer points is a primitive
affine lattice, so \(B\cap\mathbb Z^n\) admits an intrinsic lattice chart
by \(\mathbb Z^q\).  The slice over \(B\), in this chart, is a
\((q+d)\)-dimensional convex body: \(q+1\) affinely spanning positive-volume
fibers supply a nonempty relative interior.  If \(q\le2\), accepted fact
`8aac56c49321466c` gives an intrinsic relative complete core of mass at
least \(\kappa_qW_A\).  If \(B\ne\mathbb R^n\), the exact hard-branch
normalization in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` gives
\(G\ge2^{n-q}\kappa_qW_A\); if \(B=\mathbb R^n\), the global clause of
the same two accepted facts gives the same formula.

Suppose \(q\ge3\).  Apply the accepted construction in
`70fe7ecc186e568c` intrinsically to the slice over \(B\).  Inspection of
its two congruence-simplex branches gives a certificate of some intrinsic
rank \(p\in\{0,\ldots,q\}\) whose intrinsic normalized score is at least
\[
 \frac{\gamma_d}{B_d}
 \max\left\{
 \frac1{q2^{q-1}(q+1)^q},
 \frac1{2^q(q+2)^q}
 \right\}W_A,
\tag{52}
\]
where
\[
 B_d=d2^d\int_0^1\frac{x^{d-1}}{(1+x)^d}\,dx<2.
\]
Indeed, `70fe7ecc186e568c` first produces a level \(t\) with
\(t^dN\ge\gamma_dW_A/(2^qB_d)\), then a rank-\(p\) lattice simplex (or a
singleton when \(p=0\)); multiplication of its constructed depth by
\(2^q\) gives (52).  When \(p\ge1\), this is exactly the normalized
one-factor packet score in `3d1f63abf09b2abb`.  When \(p=0\), accepted
fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` puts the selected depth point in the metronoid of
the same mass, so it is the corresponding rank-zero relative-core score.
Because \(\gamma_d>1/e\) and \(B_d<2\), (52) is strictly larger than
\(\kappa_qW_A\).  Applying the hard-branch normalization of
`3d1f63abf09b2abb` to the actual rank \(p\) yields
\[
 G\ge2^{n-q}\kappa_qW_A,
\]
proving (16).

Taking \(A=\mathbb R^n\) proves \(G\ge\kappa_nW\).  For \(n=1,2\),
\(\kappa_n\ge1/64\), contradicting the strict inequality in (14), so
\(n\ge3\).  Since every fixed \(n\) has \(\kappa_n>0\), the sequential
claim follows.  If the positive support in \(A\) has rank \(q=1\) or
\(2\), (16) gives respectively
\[
 W_A<8\varepsilon2^{-n}W,
 \qquad
 W_A<256\varepsilon2^{-n}W.
\tag{53}
\]
Every internal edge contributes twice to the matched degree in \(A\),
whereas \(d_z\le b_z=\gamma_da_z\).  Thus
\[
 2L_A\le\sum_{z\in A}d_z\le\gamma_dW_A,
\]
and (53) proves (17).

Accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 U=\sum_\xi U_\xi\le R_{\rm sel}(C),
 \qquad
 L=\frac{\gamma_dW-U}{2}.
\tag{54}
\]
This proves the first two inequalities in (19), and summing (8) proves
\(D_*=2L\).  Since \(\varepsilon\le1/64<1/(2e)\) and
\(\gamma_d>1/e\), the last inequality for \(L\) follows.

For fixed \(z\), use in (5) the singleton \(E=\{z\}\), the centroid
\(q_z=c_z\), weight \(w_z=b_z\), and the unique affine selector on that
singleton.  The centroid-depth input is part of the centroid specialization
in `be359f8a6c628483`.  It gives
\(R_{\rm sel}(C)\ge2^nb_z\), proving the first two bounds in (20).
Equation (8) gives \(\lambda_e\le\min\{b_z,b_{z'}\}\), proving the third.
Summing the fiber bound over \(D_+\) gives the support bound in (21), and
dividing the lower bound for \(L\) by the edge upper bound gives its edge
count.

For an atom \(p\), the measure
\(\nu_{\rm off}(\{p\})\delta_p\) is a submeasure allowed in (11), with
\(\rho_\sigma=r_\sigma=0\).  Hence
\[
 2^n\nu_{\rm off}(\{p\})\le M_{\rm off}(C)<\varepsilon W,
\]
which is (22).  Dividing (19) by (22) proves (23).

Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` says that
\((z,x)\mapsto\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}\) is jointly concave.  Together with
the centroid depths used in `be359f8a6c628483`, it gives
\[
 \delta_C(m_e,q_e)^{1/d}
 \ge\frac12\left(b_z^{1/d}+b_{z'}^{1/d}\right)
 \ge\min\{b_z,b_{z'}\}^{1/d}.
\]
Equation (8) then proves (25).

Let \(A\) be proper primitive of codimension \(r\).  By (NR),
\[
 \sum_{z\in A}d_z\le\gamma_dW_A<\gamma_d2^{-r}W.
\tag{55}
\]
Dividing by \(D_*>(\gamma_d-\varepsilon)W\) proves (27).  Also
\(\varepsilon<\gamma_d/4\), hence \(c_\varepsilon<4/3\).  Conditioned on
\(Z_0,\ldots,Z_{j-1}\) being affinely independent, their affine hull is a
primitive affine lattice subspace of codimension \(n-j+1\).  Therefore
the conditional failure probability is less than
\(c_\varepsilon2^{-(n-j+1)}\).  Thus
\[
 \mathbb P(\text{affine independence})>
 \prod_{\ell=1}^n(1-c_\varepsilon2^{-\ell})
 >\prod_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac43\,2^{-\ell}\right).
\]
Using \(\prod_{i}(1-x_i)\ge1-\sum_i x_i\) on the tail,
\[
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac43\,2^{-\ell}\right)
 \ge
 \frac13\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac56\cdot\frac{11}{12}
 \left(1-\sum_{\ell=5}^{\infty}\frac43\,2^{-\ell}\right)
 =\frac{605}{3888}>\frac3{20}.
\tag{56}
\]
This proves (28), and multiplying by \(D_*^{n+1}\) proves (29).

For (30), let \(L(V):=\sum_{v_e\in V}\lambda_e\).  Every endpoint of
such an edge lies in the primitive affine subspace \(M_0+V\), because
\(z=m_e+v_e\) and \(z'=m_e-v_e\).  Hence (NR) and the degree count give
\[
 2L(V)\le\sum_{z\in M_0+V}d_z
 <\gamma_d2^{\dim(L_0+V)-n}W.
\]
Divide by \(2L>(\gamma_d-\varepsilon)W\) to get (30).  Sample directions
successively modulo \(L_0\).  After \(j<s_0\) independent directions,
the current rational subspace has dimension \(\rho_0+j\), so (30) bounds
the next failure probability by
\(c_\varepsilon2^{-(s_0-j)}\).  The product estimate (56) proves (31).

For a parity class, apply (16) with \(A=\operatorname{aff}(D_\xi)\).
This gives (33).  Each term in (33) is at most
\(2^{-n}C_RG\) when \(q_\xi\le R\), and there are at most \(2^n\)
parity classes, proving (35).  The total matching mass in the low-rank
classes is at most
\[
 \frac12\sum_{q_\xi\le R}B_\xi
 =\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\sum_{q_\xi\le R}W_\xi
 \le\frac{\gamma_dC_RG}{2}.
\]
Subtract this from the exact lower bound
\(L\ge(\gamma_dW-R_{\rm sel}(C))/2\) to prove (36).  Under (37), (38)
follows.  The restricted edge measure is a submeasure allowed in (11), so
\[
 2^{n-\rho_{>R}}\frac{L_{>R}}{r_{>R}+1}
 \le M_{\rm off}(C)<\varepsilon W.
\]
Together with (38), this proves (39).

Apply the same inequality to the full off-diagonal measure and use (19):
\[
 2^{n-\rho_{\rm off}}\frac{L}{r_{\rm off}+1}
 <\varepsilon W.
\]
This proves the first part of (40), and the second follows from
\(r_{\rm off}\le d\).  For a fixed midpoint base, \(\sigma_m\) is an
allowed submeasure with base rank zero and residual rank \(r_m\).  Thus
\(2^nL_m/(r_m+1)<\varepsilon W\), proving (42).  Summing the \(L_m\)'s
gives \(L\), so (19) and (42) imply (43).

Since \(\rho_{\rm off}\le n\) and \(r_{\rm off}\le d\), the definition
of \(M_{\rm off}\) also gives
\[
 \varepsilon W>M_{\rm off}(C)\ge\frac{L}{d+1}
 >\frac{\gamma_d-\varepsilon}{2(d+1)}W.
\]
Rearrangement proves (44) and its consequences.

For fixed \(m\), take the full reflected cloud in (13).  Its affine rank
is at most \(d\), and \(\gamma_d\ge\gamma_{2d}\), so
\[
 R_{\rm cloud}(C)
 \ge2^n\frac{\gamma_{2d}P_m}{d+1}.
\]
This and (14) prove (46).  For a collection \(\mathcal J\) of matching
edges with midpoint \(m\), (25) first shows that \(a_m>0\), so the
reflected cloud at \(m\) is admissible.  Take the corresponding pair
components in (13).  For each \(e=\{m+v,m-v\}\),
\[
 \lambda_e\le\gamma_d\min\{a_{m+v},a_{m-v}\}.
\]
The resulting subcloud has rank \(s_{\mathcal J}\), so (13)-(14) prove
the first inequality in (47).  The second uses
\(\gamma_d\le1/2\) and \(\gamma_{2d}>1/e\).

Under (48), one representative from every reflected pair gives
\[
 P_m=a_m+\frac{W-a_m}{2}\ge\frac W2.
\]
Insert this into the preceding full-cloud estimate to get (49).  Combining
(49) with (14) gives (50), and setting \(d=2n\) gives (51).

All conclusions above stop before Cartesian completion: affine independence
of endpoints and complementarity of sampled edge directions do not imply
that the cross-corners of those directions occur as positive lattice fibers.
