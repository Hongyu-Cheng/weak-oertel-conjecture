## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the
objects from accepted fact `4bd2e6d22487dfef`:
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C,
\qquad
C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
\]
\[
\delta_C(z,y):=
\inf_{\substack{L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
y\in L}}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L),
\qquad
g(z):=\max_{y\in C_z}\delta_C(z,y)^{1/d},
\]
\[
B:=\{(z,u):z\in P,\ 0\leq u\leq g(z)\},
\]
and the measure
\[
\eta(E):=
\sum_{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}
\int_0^{g(z)}\mathbf 1_E(z,u)\,d\,u^{d-1}\,du.
\]
For a closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
H_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in H\},
\]
\[
\mathcal A_H:=
\left\{(z,u)\in B:
\text{there is }y\in C_z\cap H_z
\text{ with }\delta_C(z,y)\geq u^d
\right\},
\]
and
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
The whole space is allowed as an improper closed halfspace.  For every
closed ambient halfspace, including every vertical halfspace and the whole
space, one has the two-sided sandwich
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_d\,\mu_C(H)
\leq
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\leq
\mu_C(H).
}
\tag{1}
\]
This remains literal when some or all lattice fibers or caps have zero
\(d\)-volume.

For
\[
q\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R),
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q):=
\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed ambient halfspace}\\q\in\mathcal A_H}}
\eta(\mathcal A_H),
\tag{2}
\]
the following complete-core transfer holds.  Let
\(I\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty.  For every \(z\in I\),
let \(D_z\subseteq C_z\) be a nonempty compact convex set, and let
\(w_z\geq0\) satisfy
\[
\delta_C(z,y)\geq w_z
\qquad(y\in D_z).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}I\), define
\[
\Lambda_m(I):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^I:
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z=1,
\quad
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z z=m
\right\},
\]
\[
\mathcal K_m(D):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)}
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zD_z,
\qquad
b_m(w):=
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in I\cap J}w_z.
\tag{4}
\]
Assume \(\mathcal K_m(D)\neq\varnothing\).  Every
\(x\in\mathcal K_m(D)\) belongs to \(C_m\), and every closed ambient
halfspace \(H\) containing \((m,x)\) satisfies the anchored hit bound
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)\geq b_m(w).
\tag{5}
\]

For any
\[
0\leq t\leq\max_{y\in C_m}\delta_C(m,y),
\]
put
\[
L_m(t):=\{y\in C_m:\delta_C(m,y)\geq t\},
\qquad
q_t:=(m,t^{1/d}).
\tag{6}
\]
Then \(L_m(t)\neq\varnothing\),
\(q_t\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), and every single point
\(x\in\mathcal K_m(D)\) gives
\[
\boxed{
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)
\geq
\min\{t,b_m(w)\}.
}
\tag{7}
\]
No condition \(\delta_C(m,x)\geq t\) is required in (7).  If the stronger
whole-section compatibility
\[
L_m(t)\subseteq\mathcal K_m(D)
\tag{8}
\]
holds, then
\[
\boxed{
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)\geq b_m(w).
}
\tag{9}
\]

Here is the metronoid specialization.  Let
\[
I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0\},
\]
take a nonempty \(E\subseteq I_+\), choose
\[
0<s_z\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\qquad(z\in E),
\]
and let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\).  Define
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_s):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z),
\]
\[
b_m(s):=
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}s_z.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_s)\neq\varnothing\), then every point in
this core and every \(t\) in the range in (6) give
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)
\geq
\min\{t,\gamma_d b_m(s)\}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(L_m(t)\subseteq\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_s)\), then
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)\geq\gamma_d b_m(s).
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, if
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}s_z
\geq
2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}s_z
\tag{13}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), then (11) and (12)
become, respectively,
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)
\geq
\min\left\{t,
\gamma_d2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}s_z\right\},
\tag{14}
\]
and
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_t)
\geq
\gamma_d2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}s_z.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus one core point already gives (15) whenever the admissible threshold
also satisfies
\(t\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}s_z\); whole-section containment is
needed only to remove that threshold requirement.

Finally, the product cube is exactly calibrated.  If
\[
C=[0,1]^n\times K,
\]
where \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body, and
\[
M_K:=\max_{y\in K}
\inf_{\substack{L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
y\in L}}
\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap L),
\tag{16}
\]
then
\[
B=[0,1]^n\times[0,M_K^{1/d}],
\qquad
|\eta|=2^nM_K,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{q\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)}
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q)
=M_K
=2^{-n}|\eta|.
}
\tag{17}
\]

## Proof

We first record the exact mass in one lifted integer fiber.  For
\(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
T_z(H):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\max_{y\in C_z\cap H_z}\delta_C(z,y),
&C_z\cap H_z\neq\varnothing,\\[2mm]
0,&C_z\cap H_z=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
\]
When the intersection is nonempty, it is compact and the maximum is
attained by the upper semicontinuity established in accepted fact
`4bd2e6d22487dfef`.  Directly from the definition of \(\mathcal A_H\),
the hit levels over \(z\) are exactly
\[
[0,T_z(H)^{1/d}].
\]
If \(T_z(H)=0\), this interval is only the zero level and has zero
\(\eta\)-mass.  If the cap is empty, there are no hit levels and the mass
is again zero.  Therefore, in all cases,
\[
\eta\left(
\mathcal A_H\cap(\{z\}\times\mathbb R)
\right)
=
\int_0^{T_z(H)^{1/d}}d\,u^{d-1}\,du
=T_z(H).
\tag{19}
\]

Put \(A_z:=C_z\cap H_z\).  If \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace and
\(y\in A_z\), then \(H_z\) is an admissible competitor in the definition
of \(\delta_C(z,y)\), so
\[
\delta_C(z,y)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z).
\tag{20}
\]
If \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from
\(\delta_C(z,y)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\).  If \(H_z\) or its
intersection with \(C_z\) is empty, both sides of the desired fiberwise
upper bound are zero.  Hence
\[
T_z(H)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z)
\tag{21}
\]
in every case.

For the reverse comparison, suppose first that
\(a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z)>0\).  Then \(A_z\) is a
\(d\)-dimensional convex body.  Let \(c_z\) be its centroid.  The
constant density \(1\) is admissible in the full-mass metronoid of
\(A_z\) and realizes \(c_z\).  The full-mass case of accepted fact
`6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied to \(A_z\), therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z\cap L)\geq\gamma_da_z
\tag{22}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing
\(c_z\).  Since \(A_z\subseteq C_z\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L)
\geq
\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z\cap L)
\geq
\gamma_da_z.
\]
Taking the infimum over such \(L\) gives
\[
\delta_C(z,c_z)\geq\gamma_da_z.
\]
Because \(c_z\in A_z\), equation (18) now yields
\[
T_z(H)\geq\gamma_d\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z).
\tag{23}
\]
If \(a_z=0\), inequality (23) is automatic because \(T_z(H)\geq0\).
Thus (21) and (23) hold for every integer fiber, including nonempty
lower-dimensional fibers.

Only finitely many integer fibers meet \(C\).  Summing (19), (21), and
(23) proves (1).  If \(H\) is vertical, every \(H_z\) is either
\(\mathbb R^d\) or empty, precisely the cases already handled.  If
\(H=\mathbb R^{n+d}\), then \(\mathcal A_H=B\), \(A_z=C_z\), and the
same calculation proves
\(\gamma_d\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})\leq|\eta|
\leq\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})\).  If the empty improper halfspace is also
admitted by convention, both sides of (1) are zero; it cannot occur in
the infimum (2) for a hit point.  Closed boundaries cause no loss because
the cap centroids, all maximizers, and the zero level are retained.

We next prove the anchored complete-core hit bound (5).  Fix
\(x\in\mathcal K_m(D)\).  Choose any
\(\lambda^0\in\Lambda_m(I)\).  Membership in the corresponding Minkowski
sum gives points \(y_z^0\in D_z\) such that
\[
x=\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z^0y_z^0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
(m,x)=
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z^0(z,y_z^0)\in C,
\tag{24}
\]
which proves \(x\in C_m\), even when \(m\notin I\).

First let \(H=\mathbb R^{n+d}\).  For each \(z\in I\), any point of
\(D_z\) shows
\[
\max_{y\in C_z}\delta_C(z,y)\geq w_z.
\]
Equations (18)-(19) therefore give
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)=|\eta|
\geq\sum_{z\in I}w_z
\geq b_m(w),
\tag{25}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(w_z\geq0\).

Now let \(H\) be proper.  Move its boundary parallel toward \((m,x)\)
until the boundary contains \((m,x)\).  This produces
\(H^0\subseteq H\) of the form
\[
H^0=
\left\{(z,y):
a\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+b\mathbin{\cdot}(y-x)\geq0
\right\},
\tag{26}
\]
where \((a,b)\neq(0,0)\).  Witness monotonicity gives
\(\mathcal A_{H^0}\subseteq\mathcal A_H\).

For \(z\in I\), put
\[
r_z:=h_{D_z}(b)=\max_{y\in D_z}b\mathbin{\cdot}y.
\tag{27}
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)\), core membership gives
\[
b\mathbin{\cdot}x
\leq
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zr_z.
\tag{28}
\]
Let \(\theta\) be the minimum of the right side over
\(\Lambda_m(I)\).  The finite linear program has the dual
\[
\max\left\{\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}m:
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq r_z\ (z\in I)\right\}.
\]
Strong linear-programming duality gives an optimizer \((\alpha,g)\)
with
\[
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}m=\theta.
\]
The possible slack in (28) must not be discarded.  Set
\[
\sigma:=\theta-b\mathbin{\cdot}x\geq0,
\qquad
\ell(z):=\alpha-\sigma+g\mathbin{\cdot}z.
\tag{29}
\]
Then
\[
\ell(z)\leq r_z\quad(z\in I),
\qquad
\ell(m)=b\mathbin{\cdot}x.
\tag{30}
\]

Define
\[
J_0:=
\{z\in\mathbb R^n:(a+g)\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)\geq0\}.
\tag{31}
\]
If \(a+g\neq0\), this is a proper closed base halfspace containing
\(m\).  If \(a+g=0\), it is the whole base space.  For each
\(z\in I\cap J_0\), choose \(y_z\in D_z\) attaining (27).  Equations
(30)-(31) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+b\mathbin{\cdot}(y_z-x)
&=a\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+r_z-b\mathbin{\cdot}x\\
&\geq a\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+\ell(z)-\ell(m)\\
&=(a+g)\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Thus \(y_z\in C_z\cap H_z^0\).  The exact fiber identity (19) and (3)
give
\[
\eta\left(
\mathcal A_{H^0}\cap(\{z\}\times\mathbb R)
\right)
=T_z(H^0)
\geq\delta_C(z,y_z)
\geq w_z.
\tag{33}
\]
It follows that
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\geq\eta(\mathcal A_{H^0})
\geq\sum_{z\in I\cap J_0}w_z
\geq b_m(w).
\tag{34}
\]
For the last inequality, use the definition of \(b_m(w)\) when \(J_0\)
is proper, and use
\(\sum_{z\in I}w_z\geq b_m(w)\) when \(J_0=\mathbb R^n\).
This proves (5).

The argument includes vertical \(H\): then \(b=0\), one may take the
zero dual slope \(g=0\), and (31) is the original proper base halfspace
through \(m\).  It also includes every boundary incidence because all
halfspaces and inequalities are closed.  Finally, (3) forces \(w_z=0\)
whenever \(C_z\) has zero \(d\)-volume, so such fibers contribute only
the already valid zero inequality in (33).  Thus no positive mass is
silently assigned to a lower-dimensional fiber.

We now prove (7).  Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\) with
\(q_t\in\mathcal A_H\).  By definition, there is
\[
y\in L_m(t)\cap H_m.
\tag{35}
\]
If \(x\in H_m\), then \(H\) contains \((m,x)\), and (5) gives
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)\geq b_m(w).
\tag{36}
\]
If \(x\notin H_m\), the witness in (35) and the exact identity (19) give
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\geq T_m(H)
\geq\delta_C(m,y)
\geq t.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus every admissible \(H\) has hit mass at least
\(\min\{t,b_m(w)\}\).  Taking the infimum proves (7).  The whole-space
case always falls under (36), and a vertical halfspace hitting the
\(m\)-fiber has \(H_m=\mathbb R^d\), so it also falls under (36).  At
\(t=0\), equation (37) is the correct zero lower bound; no positivity is
assumed.

If (8) holds, the witness \(y\) in (35) is itself a complete-core point.
Apply (5) with \(x=y\).  This gives
\(\eta(\mathcal A_H)\geq b_m(w)\) for every \(H\) counted in (2), and
proves (9).  This argument explains exactly why one core point gives the
minimum in (7), while containment of the entire depth section gives the
untruncated base bound.

For the metronoid specialization, accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`
states that \(\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)\) is nonempty, compact, and convex and
that
\[
\delta_C(z,y)\geq\gamma_ds_z
\qquad
\left(y\in\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)\right).
\tag{38}
\]
Apply (7) and (9) with
\[
D_z=\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z),
\qquad
w_z=\gamma_ds_z.
\]
Since the common factor \(\gamma_d\) passes through the infimum in (4),
\[
b_m(w)=\gamma_db_m(s).
\]
This proves (11)-(12).  Condition (13) gives
\(b_m(s)\geq2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}s_z\), proving (14)-(15) and the stated
threshold consequence.  Every \(s_z\) is strictly positive and every
chosen fiber has positive \(d\)-volume, so no undefined zero-mass
metronoid is used.

It remains to prove the product calibration.  For
\(C=[0,1]^n\times K\), every base fiber is \(K\), so
\[
\delta_C(z,y)=\delta_K(y),
\qquad
g(z)=M_K^{1/d}.
\]
The integer points of \([0,1]^n\) are its \(2^n\) vertices.  Integrating
the lifted density over each of these identical fibers gives
\[
B=[0,1]^n\times[0,M_K^{1/d}],
\qquad
|\eta|=2^nM_K.
\tag{39}
\]

Fix a cube vertex \(m\) and its top lifted point
\[
q_m:=(m,M_K^{1/d}).
\]
If \(q_m\in\mathcal A_H\), there is
\(y\in K\cap H_m\) with \(\delta_K(y)\geq M_K\).  By the definition of
\(M_K\), equality holds.  The same point \(y\) witnesses every level
\((m,u)\) with \(0\leq u\leq M_K^{1/d}\).  Hence the entire lifted
integer fiber over \(m\) lies in \(\mathcal A_H\), and
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)\geq M_K.
\]
Therefore
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_m)\geq M_K.
\tag{40}
\]

Conversely, for any cube vertex \(m\), put
\(\varepsilon_i:=2m_i-1\) and take the proper vertical ambient halfspace
\[
H_m:=
\left\{(z,y):
\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i(z_i-m_i)\geq-\frac12
\right\}.
\tag{41}
\]
At an integer cube vertex \(z\), the left side is
\(-\operatorname{Ham}(z,m)\).  Thus \((H_m)_m=\mathbb R^d\), whereas
\((H_m)_z=\varnothing\) at every other integer base vertex.  It follows
that \(\mathcal A_{H_m}\) has exactly the one complete lifted integer
fiber over \(m\), of mass \(M_K\).  Every lifted point over \(m\) lies in
this range.  Consequently,
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(m,u)\leq M_K
\qquad(0\leq u\leq M_K^{1/d}).
\tag{42}
\]
Equations (40)-(42) prove (17), including the exact constant and the
closed vertical boundary convention.
