## Statement

There is a probability law $X$ on $\Delta_2=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^3:\sum_i x_i=1\}$ with exactly $678$ rational atoms and rational probabilities such that
$$
\phi_i=\inf_{t>0:\Pr(X_i\le t)>0}\frac{t}{\sqrt{\Pr(X_i\le t)}},
\qquad
(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)=\frac{(200,100,100)}{273}.
$$
It has $\sum_i\phi_i^2+2\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3-1=33583/20346417>0$.

## Proof

Use the same [rational table](../assets/pk-nov6/computations/beta_coupling_exact_certificate_root.json) as in 9bb8ad0f2aef88b0. Its $399$ positive rational entries $m(i,j,k)$ sum to one, the first marginal is $(2i+1)/40000$, the sum of the second and third marginals at index $l$ is $2(2l+1)/40000$, and every supported triple satisfies $2(i+1)+(j+1)+(k+1)\le546$. All indices lie in $\{0,\ldots,199\}$. Draw a triple and independently swap its last two indices with probability $1/2$. Set $U=((i+1)/200,(j+1)/200,(k+1)/200)$ using the resulting indices. Each $U_i$ has CDF $(l/200)^2$ at $l/200$, $0\le l\le200$, by summation of the first $l$ odd integers. Its CDF stays constant between grid points and thus is at most $t^2$ for every $t\ge0$.

Set $p=(200,100,100)/273$, $V_i=p_iU_i$, and $X=(1-V_2-V_3,V_2,V_3)$. The table support bound gives $\sum_iV_i\le1$. Thus $X\in\Delta_2$ and $X_i\ge V_i$, which gives $\Pr(X_i\le t)\le t^2/p_i^2$ and $\phi_i\ge p_i$. For coordinates $2$ and $3$, $t=100/273$ attains the lower bound since the CDF there is one.

For coordinate $1$, its value on the original or swapped triple is $(544-j-k)/546$. The mass at or below $6/13$ is therefore the sum of the table weights with $j+k\ge292$. The exact finite checks are reproduced by the following pseudocode, using rational accumulation and including repeated projected pairs with their added masses.

```text
masses := empty map with default rational zero
cdf := rational zero
for (i,j,k), m in the table:
    masses[j,k] += m/2
    masses[k,j] += m/2
    if j+k >= 292:
        cdf += m
assert number of keys in masses == 678
assert cdf == 3969/10000
```

The mass-map keys index distinct atoms since $(X_2,X_3)=((j+1)/546,(k+1)/546)$. At the displayed threshold $(6/13)^2/(3969/10000)=40000/74529=p_1^2$. This gives $\phi=p$ exactly. Substituting $p$ gives the stated cubic excess.
