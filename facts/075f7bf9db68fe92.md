## Statement

Take $K_j(a)=(1+aj)T$, $j=-1,0,1$, with $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$. Let $u_1=(1,0),u_2=(0,1),u_3=(-1,-1)$ in cyclic order and consider the three through-origin caps with margins $(u_i+\theta_i u_{i+1})\cdot x+\alpha_i j$. In their common open one-vertex chamber around $a=\theta_i=\alpha_i=0$, impose equal-weight continuous balance and require the selected caps to cover both entire endpoint planes. These conditions are exactly $\theta_1=\theta_2=\theta_3$ and $\sum_i\alpha_i=0$. The optimal largest selected cap mass is $6+4a^2=(4/9)(27/2+9a^2)$ for sufficiently small $|a|$, attained at all $\theta_i=\alpha_i=0$. The independently optimizing caps miss an actual neighborhood of the endpoint centroid with $aj>0$ whenever $a\ne0$ is sufficiently small.

At the origin, along $a(\varepsilon)=A\varepsilon$, allow smooth positive continuous balancing weights, each initially one, and retain whole-plane coverage at both endpoints. The extra minimum second-derivative cost for the sum of selected cap masses is $24A^2$ relative to independent normal optimization, or $18A^2$ after multiplying the cap sum by the fixed stress $3/4$.

## Proof

Equal-weight continuous balance is $\sum_i\theta_i u_{i+1}=0$, which holds exactly when all $\theta_i$ are equal since the sole linear dependence among the three $u_i$ has equal coefficients. Near zero the three normals have rank two, so their positive dependence is minimal. With equal weights, d192c5d880bad772 applies and gives whole-plane closed-cap coverage over $j$ exactly when $j\sum_i\alpha_i\ge0$. Equivalently, the three margins fill the affine plane in $\mathbb R^3$ with coordinate sum $j\sum_i\alpha_i$; a negative sum allows all three margins to be negative. Both endpoint requirements are therefore equivalent to the stated zero-sum condition.

The cyclic permutation of the three vertex coordinates of $T$ preserves its area and cycles $u_i$. Apply the affine-radius formula from the moving-triangle chart to each cap. With the common $\theta$, its mass is $6+3\theta^2/[2(1-\theta)]+(2a+\alpha_i-a\theta)^2/(1-\theta)$. Average the squares using $\sum_i\alpha_i=0$; their mean is at least $a^2(2-\theta)^2$. Hence the largest cap mass is at least
$$
(3/2+a^2)\frac{(2-\theta)^2}{1-\theta}
=6+4a^2+(3/2+a^2)\frac{\theta^2}{1-\theta}.
$$
The lower bound is achieved at the stated zero tilts and angles, which lie in the chamber for small $|a|$. Independent branch optimization instead gives all $\alpha_i=-2a,\theta_i=0$. At the centroid $0$ of the endpoint with $aj>0$ each margin is $-2aj<0$, and the centroid is interior, so an actual positive-area neighborhood is missed.

For variable positive balancing weights $\lambda_i$ near one, the same minimal-circuit criterion gives $\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_i=0$ if both endpoint planes are covered. Differentiating at the original zero tilts gives $\sum_i\alpha_i'=0$. The exact chart expansion gives the second derivative of the cap sum as $2\sum_i(2A+\alpha_i')^2+3\sum_i(\theta_i')^2$ at zero. The unconstrained minimum is zero. The tilt constraint makes the first sum at least $24A^2$ after its factor $2$, and the angular term is nonnegative. Constant weights and identically zero tilt and angle paths attain equality, so further continuous-balance conditions cannot change this minimum. The fixed factor $3/4$ yields $18A^2$, the second derivative of $27/2+9a(\varepsilon)^2$.
