## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers, and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Retain the locked mixed set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and its closed-halfspace depth \(h_S\). For each integer index \(\mathbf I\), put \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}}\), \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). On \(D\), define \(U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}/w_{\mathbf I}\). All logarithms are natural. Boundary fibers retain their actual weights, and no uniform law is assigned to a zero-volume fiber.

Use the integral and fractional root-cost conventions of accepted facts `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` and `793210f8a4be3647`. Explicitly, for \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) consist of the nonempty affinely independent sets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\). Their unique positive barycentric coefficients are \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\), and their costs are
\[
L_E=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}{
\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}.
\]
The singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\) has cost zero. For \(L\ge0\), \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) is the minimum original weight \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) of a set \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) with \(L_E\le L\). The value \(P_d(\mathbf J,L)\) is the maximum of \(\sum_E x_E\) over those supports, subject to \(x_E\ge0\) and \(\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E\le w_{\mathbf I}\) at every source. Set
\[
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\,L\ge0}\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L},
\qquad
\mathcal B_d(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\,L\ge0}\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L}.
\tag{1}
\]
These maxima are attained by the cited facts. The latter fact also identifies \(P_d\) with the optimum over arbitrary finite barycentric supports with the same full source charges.

For the entropy assertion use the actual feasible schemes of `793210f8a4be3647` and `839747fd50cbcae6`. At one \(\mathbf J\in D\), a component \(k\) consists of a nonempty set \(E_k\subseteq D\) of distinct indices, positive coefficients \(\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\) summing to one with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\), a mass \(x_k\ge0\), and a joint probability law \(\Pi_k\) with each source marginal exactly \(U_{\mathbf I}\). Require
\[
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad
M=\sum_kx_k>0,
\qquad
P=M^{-1}\sum_kx_kP_k,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(P_k\) is the law of the barycentric continuous output under \(\Pi_k\). Thus every occurrence of a source is charged the full \(x_k\), regardless of its positive coefficient. Central mass is a singleton component. For schemes whose final law \(P\) has a bounded measurable Lebesgue density, write
\[
\mathcal E(C)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\
\sup_{\text{finite feasible schemes and couplings at }\mathbf J}
\frac{M}{1+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})}.
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U)=\int f\log f\,dU\) for \(f=dP/dU\), with \(0\log0=0\). Convexity puts every component output in \(C_{\mathbf J}\); a bounded density for \(P\) therefore gives a bounded density ratio against its positive uniform reference and finite relative entropy. The score \(\mathcal E(C)\) denotes actual final-mixture entropy and is unrelated to the exponential capacity denoted \(E(p)\) elsewhere. The symbol \(M\) in (2) always denotes scheme mass. A common fiber-volume scale below is denoted \(\omega>0\), replacing the scale called \(M\) in the supplied staircase source.

The following local statements hold for every actual table containing the specified indices and weights; other fibers may be present.

(i) For real \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\), an integer \(q\ge0\), and \(h\ge0\), let \(k=|\{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}:\sum_{i\in A}b_i\le h\}|\). Then \(k\ge1\), and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^s b_i>(s-k+1)h
\quad\text{for every integer }k+1\le s\le q.
\tag{4}
\]

(ii) Let \(q\ge0\) be an integer and \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\). Suppose linearly independent integer vectors \(\mathbf v_1,\ldots,\mathbf v_q\in\mathbb Z^n\) give a cube \(\mathbf J-\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf v_i\), for all \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}\), of weight \(\omega\) at each vertex. Suppose the staircase indices \(\mathbf J+\sum_{i=1}^s\mathbf v_i\), \(1\le s\le q\), have weights \(\omega r_s\), where \(0<r_s\le1\). With \(R=1+\sum_{s=1}^qr_s\), one has
\[
m_d(\mathbf J,\log2)\ge\frac{4\omega}{9}R.
\tag{5}
\]
The case \(q=0\) means a single cube vertex and an empty staircase.

For the two-height version, write the integer dimension as \(n=n_0+1\), and let \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^{n_0}\) and linearly independent \(\mathbf v_i\in\mathbb Z^{n_0}\). Let \(T\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(\rho_0=1\) and \(0<\rho_{t+1}\le\rho_t/2\) for \(0\le t<T\). Suppose both cubes
\[
\left(\mathbf J-\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf v_i,0\right),
\qquad
\left(\mathbf J-\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf v_i,1\right)
\quad(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\})
\tag{6}
\]
are present with weights \(\omega\) and \(\omega\rho_1\), respectively. For arbitrary sets \(T_s\subseteq\{1,\ldots,T\}\), suppose \((\mathbf J+\sum_{i=1}^s\mathbf v_i,t)\) has weight \(\omega\rho_t\) whenever \(1\le s\le q\) and \(t\in T_s\). Put \(U=\sum_{s=1}^q\sum_{t\in T_s}\rho_t\). Then
\[
m_d((\mathbf J,1),\log2)\ge\frac{2\omega}{7}U.
\tag{7}
\]

For the tree consequences, use exactly the rooted-tree construction of accepted fact `032423921efad6c8`. Namely, for integers \(m,Q\ge1\), put \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^Q[m]^r\), \(n_{\mathrm{tree}}=\sum_{r=1}^Qm^r\), and \(\eta_m=1-2^{-m}\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\). Coordinates are indexed by the words in \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}\). In any symmetric saturated-chain partition of \(\{0,1\}^m\), let \(F_m\) be the injective partial successor map, adding one zero coordinate. For a Boolean tree vector \(\beta\), follow these successors down its child blocks, obtaining a path \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(\beta)}\). Define
\[
z_s(\beta)=\beta+2\sum_{i=1}^s\mathbf e_{u_i},
\qquad
A_{m,Q}=\{z_s(\beta):\beta\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}},\ 0\le s\le h(\beta)\},
\qquad
P_{m,Q}=\operatorname{conv}A_{m,Q}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(Q\) is tree depth, \(d\) is continuous dimension, and \(\eta_m\) is the tree census factor. The level \(\operatorname{lev}(z)\) is the number of its coordinates equal to two. The cited fact supplies \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}=A_{m,Q}\) and exactly \(2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\eta_m^s\) points at level \(s\).

(iii) If the integer dimension is \(n=n_{\mathrm{tree}}\), the actual table has positive support exactly \(A_{m,Q}\), and
\[
w_{\mathbf j}=\omega\quad(\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}),
\qquad
0<w_z\le\omega\quad(z\in A_{m,Q}),
\]
then
\[
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge
\frac4{9(1+\log2)}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{9}
\]
The non-Boolean weights may vary independently of level, subject to being actual volumes and the displayed bounds.

For lifted tables take integers \(T\ge0\) and \(0=a_0\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_Q\le T\), and set \(n=n_{\mathrm{tree}}+1\). Use the complete support
\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z\},\\
\widehat P&=\{(x,t):x\in P_{m,Q},\
\max(0,\max_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}a_{|u|}(x_u-1))\le t\le T\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
One has \(\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), including every boundary lattice point. This is the height lift of `604889294a17d7df` extended to the arbitrary entrance schedule in (10). Suppose an actual body has, for positive numbers \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_T\), exactly the table
\[
w_{(z,t)}=\omega\rho_t\quad((z,t)\in D),
\qquad w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D).
\tag{11}
\]
If in addition \(T\ge1\), \(a_1\ge1\), \(\rho_0=1\), and \(0<\rho_{t+1}\le\rho_t/2\) for \(0\le t<T\), then
\[
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge
\frac4{11+7\log2}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{12}
\]
This integral conclusion does not require affine volume roots or an affine logarithmic profile.

(iv) For every actual table (11) with \(m\ge2\), impose only the arbitrary entrances of (10) and positivity of \(\rho_t\). There is a target \(\mathbf J=(\mathbf1,t_\circ)\in D\) and a finite feasible scheme (2) for which every component has at most six sources and root cost at most one, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
M&\ge\frac{e-1}{3e(e+1)}\,2^{-n}W
>\frac1{20}\,2^{-n}W,\\
P&\le e\,U_{\mathbf J}\quad\text{as Borel measures},
&D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})&\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently
\[

\qquad
\mathcal E(C)\ge\frac1{40}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{14}
\]
The entropy in (13) is that of the final mixture itself. The theorem allows arbitrary continuous dimension and arbitrary fiber shapes realizing (11). It includes \(T=0\) and zero entrance heights. It extends the special-profile triple construction of accepted fact `f6828be957af3808` by constructing disjoint triples at every selected depth and then choosing a mass window in that depth's available height interval.

Every integral bound (9) or (12), divided by six, is a lower bound for \(h_S\) at one point in one positive fiber, by `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. The scheme in (13) supplies one point in its target fiber with \(h_S\ge M/12>(1/240)2^{-n}W\), by `793210f8a4be3647`. ## Proof

We first specify the accepted logarithmic input so that its orientation and normalization are fixed. For any common scale \(\omega>0\), put \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=-\log(w_{\mathbf I}/\omega)\). This equals the potential of `488f0a583886b5fc` plus the constant \(\log(\omega/w_{\max})\), where \(w_{\max}=\max_Dw\). Therefore every logarithmic barycentric gap and every difference \(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I}\) is unchanged. Denote that predecessor's nonnegative last normal coefficient by \(\tau\), rather than its \(t\), reserving \(t\) for an actual integer height. Its pointwise comparison and exact integral cap formula give
\[
m_d(\mathbf J,L)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
=\min_{\substack{(\mathbf b,\tau)\ne(\mathbf0,0)\\\tau\ge0}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+
\tau(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I})\ge0}}
w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{15}
\]
The predecessor applies because the table is an actual convex-body volume table; it includes its global log-concavity and every table zero in this application. Its logarithmic family uses exactly the barycentric supports in (1). In particular the root-cost family contains the logarithmic family, so the inequality is in the displayed direction for integral hitting. The cuts in (15) are closed lower caps in the convex-potential lift. The case \(\tau=0\), and all equality atoms, are part of the minimum. We will bound the mass of each such cap using only specified fibers, which also explains why additional fibers cannot weaken the local conclusions.

To prove (4), the empty subset is counted, so \(k\ge1\). Fix \(k+1\le s\le q\), put \(A_s=\{i\le s:b_i<0\}\), and let \(a=-\sum_{i\in A_s}b_i\ge0\). The subset \(A_s\) has sum \(-a\le h\). Its \(s\) distinct one-coordinate neighbors \(A_s\mathbin{\triangle}\{i\}\), \(i\le s\), have sums \(-a+|b_i|\). They are distinct from \(A_s\) and from each other. At most \(k-1\) neighbors can be among the \(k\) counted subsets of the full cube. Thus at least \(r=s-k+1\ge2\) satisfy \(|b_i|>a+h\), and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^s b_i
=\sum_{i=1}^s|b_i|-2a
>r(a+h)-2a
=(r-2)a+rh\ge rh.
\tag{16}
\]
This proves (4), including \(h=0\). The local symbols \(A_s,a,r\) in this counting argument do not denote tree entrance data or a volume profile.

For the one-height bound (5), write \(L=\log2\). In (15), divide weights by \(\omega\), and let \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\) be the normalized weight of the entire cap. Let \(u\) be the normalized weight of its included upper staircase points. These points and the cube vertices are all distinct: linear independence prevents equality between two different subset sums, two different prefixes, or a positive prefix and a negative subset sum.

First let \(\tau>0\), and divide the normal by \(\tau\). Put \(b_i=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf v_i\). Since the target and every cube vertex have potential zero, the included cube vertices are exactly the subsets satisfying \(\sum_{i\in A}b_i\le L\). Let \(k\ge1\) be their number. Then \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge k+u\). For a prefix \(s=k+r\le q\) with \(r\ge1\), (4) gives \(\sum_{i=1}^s b_i>(r+1)L\). If its upper point is excluded, its strictly negative cap score implies
\[
-\log r_s>L+\sum_{i=1}^s b_i>(r+2)L,
\qquad r_s<2^{-(r+2)}.
\tag{17}
\]
The first at most \(k\) prefixes have total normalized weight at most \(k\), and the remaining excluded weight is at most \(\sum_{r\ge1}2^{-(r+2)}=1/4\). Hence
\[
R\le1+u+k+\tfrac14\le\tfrac94(k+u)\le\tfrac94V_{\mathrm{cap}}.
\tag{18}
\]
The middle inequality follows from \(k\ge1\) and \(u\ge0\). If \(\tau=0\), use the cube threshold \(h=0\). Formula (4) makes every upper prefix \(s>k\) have strictly positive cap score, so only the first \(k\) prefixes can be excluded. Thus \(R\le1+u+k\le2(k+u)\le2V_{\mathrm{cap}}\). Both arguments also apply when no prefix exceeds \(k\), including \(q=0\). Taking the minimum in (15) proves (5).

For (7), let \(\phi_t=-\log\rho_t\), \(L=\log2\), and keep \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\) as the weight of the entire cap divided by \(\omega\). Let \(u\) be the included specified staircase weight in the same units, and set \(b_i=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf v_i\), \(p_s=\sum_{i=1}^sb_i\). The decay assumption gives
\[
\rho_1\le\tfrac12,
\qquad \rho_t\le\rho_1 2^{-(t-1)}\ (1\le t\le T),
\qquad \sum_{t=1}^T\rho_t\le2\rho_1.
\tag{19}
\]
At the target \((\mathbf J,1)\), the cap inequality on all the specified points is
\[
\mathbf b\cdot(z-\mathbf J)+c(t-1)
+\tau(\phi_1+L-\phi_t)\ge0,
\qquad (\mathbf b,c,\tau)\ne(\mathbf0,0,0),\quad\tau\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]
Here \(c\) is the coefficient of the actual integer height and \(\tau\) is the logarithmic lift coefficient. Linear independence makes each cube layer disjoint from the specified staircase points, as in the one-height argument. We prove \(U\le(7/2)V_{\mathrm{cap}}\) for every allowed normal. All infinite sums below bound finite sums of specified points by the positive geometric majorants in (19); no unsupported height is added to the actual table.

Suppose first that \(\tau>0\) and \(c\ge0\). Normalize \(\tau=1\). The height-one cube has threshold \(L\); if it includes \(k\) vertices, then \(k\ge1\) and \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge k\rho_1+u\). For \(s=k+r\le q\), \(r\ge1\), (4) gives \(p_s>(r+1)L\). Exclusion of a staircase point at that prefix and a height \(t\ge1\) gives
\[
\phi_t>\phi_1+L+p_s+c(t-1)>\phi_1+(r+2)L.
\]
Its weight is bounded both by \(\rho_1 2^{-(r+2)}\) and by \(\rho_1 2^{-(t-1)}\). Thus the excluded staircase weight beyond prefix \(k\) is at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_1\sum_{r\ge1}\sum_{t\ge1}
\min\{2^{-(r+2)},2^{-(t-1)}\}
&=\rho_1\sum_{r\ge1}(r+4)2^{-(r+2)}\\
&=\tfrac32\rho_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
For a fixed \(r\), the first \(r+3\) terms of the inner sum equal \(2^{-(r+2)}\), and the remaining geometric tail has sum \(2^{-(r+2)}\), proving the first equality. The second uses \(\sum_{r\ge1}2^{-r}=1\) and \(\sum_{r\ge1}r2^{-r}=\sum_{j\ge1}\sum_{r\ge j}2^{-r}=2\). The first \(k\) prefixes have total weight at most \(2k\rho_1\), by (19). Therefore
\[
U\le u+2k\rho_1+\tfrac32\rho_1
\le\tfrac72(u+k\rho_1)\le\tfrac72V_{\mathrm{cap}}.
\tag{22}
\]

Next suppose \(\tau>0\) and \(c<0\), again normalized to \(\tau=1\). The height-zero cube has threshold \(h=\phi_1+L+|c|>0\). If it includes \(k\) vertices, then \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge k+u\) and \(k\ge1\). For \(s=k+r\le q\), (4) gives \(p_s>(r+1)(\phi_1+L+|c|)\). An excluded staircase point satisfies
\[
\phi_t>(r+2)(\phi_1+L)+|c|(r+2-t).
\tag{23}
\]
For \(1\le t\le r+2\), this bounds its weight by \(\rho_1^{r+2}2^{-(r+2)}\le\rho_1 2^{-(r+2)}\). For \(t>r+2\), (19) bounds the entire height tail by \(\rho_1 2^{-(r+1)}\). Consequently the excluded weight beyond prefix \(k\) is at most
\[
\rho_1\sum_{r\ge1}\bigl((r+2)2^{-(r+2)}+2^{-(r+1)}\bigr)
=\tfrac32\rho_1.
\tag{24}
\]
The two geometric identities used in (21) give this equality as well. The first \(k\) prefixes weigh at most \(2k\rho_1\le k\). Since \(V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge1\) and \(\rho_1\le1/2\),
\[
U\le u+k+\tfrac32\rho_1
\le V_{\mathrm{cap}}+\tfrac34
\le\tfrac74V_{\mathrm{cap}}.
\tag{25}
\]

If \(\tau=0\) and \(c\ge0\), the height-one cube has threshold zero. With \(k\ge1\) included cube vertices, (4) gives \(p_s>0\) for every \(s>k\). All their staircase points have positive score in (20), because \(t\ge1\). Thus
\[
U\le u+2k\rho_1\le2V_{\mathrm{cap}},
\qquad V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge k\rho_1+u.
\tag{26}
\]
Finally, if \(\tau=0\) and \(c<0\), the height-zero cube has threshold \(|c|\). For \(s=k+r\le q\), (4) gives \(p_s>(r+1)|c|\). Every staircase point at such a prefix with \(t\le r+2\) has positive score \(p_s-|c|(t-1)\) and is included. The remaining excluded tails are at most \(\rho_1\sum_{r\ge1}2^{-(r+1)}=\rho_1/2\), while the first \(k\) prefixes weigh at most \(k\). It follows that
\[
U\le u+k+\tfrac12\rho_1
\le V_{\mathrm{cap}}+\tfrac14
\le\tfrac54V_{\mathrm{cap}},
\qquad V_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge k+u\ge1.
\tag{27}
\]
All normal types satisfy the bound in (22). Throughout, exclusion used a strictly negative score, so none of these estimates removes an equality atom. Taking the minimum in (15) proves the integral estimate (7).

We now prove the tree partition that converts these local bounds to global ones. For a Boolean vector \(\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\), start at the root and invert \(F_m\) on each visited child block of \(\mathbf j\), whenever that block is in its image. The inverse is unique and selects a coordinate of \(\mathbf j\) equal to one. Following that child defines distinct tree coordinates \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h_-(\mathbf j)}\), using exactly the inverse-reconstruction length \(h_-\) of `032423921efad6c8` at a level-zero center. Put
\[
z^+_{\mathbf j,s}=\mathbf j+\sum_{i=1}^s\mathbf e_{u_i}
\quad(0\le s\le h_-(\mathbf j)).
\]
The Boolean vector \(\beta=\mathbf j-\sum_{i=1}^s\mathbf e_{u_i}\) has, at each of the first \(s\) visited blocks, exactly the predecessor of that block of \(\mathbf j\). Indeed, its only change in that child block is to the selected one coordinate. Different visited child blocks are disjoint. Hence its forward successor path follows these \(s\) choices, and \(z_s(\beta)=z^+_{\mathbf j,s}\in A_{m,Q}\).

Conversely, take \(z=z_s(\beta)\in A_{m,Q}\), and replace its twos by ones to obtain a Boolean vector \(\mathbf j\). In each visited block, \(\mathbf j\) equals \(F_m\) of the source block of \(\beta\). Its inverse therefore recovers the same path through depth \(s\), and \(z=z^+_{\mathbf j,s}\). The vector \(\mathbf j\) is uniquely determined by \(z\), and its level determines \(s\). This proves the disjoint partition
\[
A_{m,Q}=\bigsqcup_{\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}}
\{z^+_{\mathbf j,s}:0\le s\le h_-(\mathbf j)\}.
\tag{28}
\]
Moreover, every vector \(\mathbf j-\sum_{i\in A}\mathbf e_{u_i}\), for \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,h_-(\mathbf j)\}\), is Boolean and hence belongs to \(A_{m,Q}\). These give precisely the independent cube directions needed in (5) and (7).

For the unlifted hypothesis, put \(R_{\mathbf j}=1+\sum_{s=1}^{h_-(\mathbf j)}w_{z^+_{\mathbf j,s}}/\omega\). Partition (28) gives \(\omega\sum_{\mathbf j}R_{\mathbf j}=W\). One of the \(2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\) roots therefore has \(\omega R_{\mathbf j}\ge2^{-n_{\mathrm{tree}}}W\). Apply (5) at that root and divide by \(1+\log2\) in (1), proving (9) without requiring equal weights on a tree level.

For (10), an integer point of \(\widehat P\) has its tree component in \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}=A_{m,Q}\). At a level-\(s\) point its twos occupy one path with depths \(1,\ldots,s\), and all other coordinates are at most one. Therefore the maximum defining its lower height in (10) is \(a_s\), by the nondecreasing entrance schedule. At level zero it is zero. This proves \(\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) in both directions, with equality at both height boundaries allowed. The defining inequalities are closed and convex. When \(T\ge1\), \([0,1]^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\times[0,T]\subseteq\widehat P\), so this polytope is full dimensional. When \(T=0\), the lattice identity still holds; the entropy assertion assumes an actual realization of its positive table and does not require this auxiliary polytope to be full dimensional. This verifies the arbitrary-schedule extension of the lift in `604889294a17d7df`.

For the geometric-profile assertion, let
\[
R_{\mathbf j}^{+}=\sum_{s=1}^{h_-(\mathbf j)}\sum_{t=a_s}^T\rho_t,
\qquad R_*^{+}=\max_{\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}}R_{\mathbf j}^{+}.
\]
The cube from (28) occurs at heights zero and one, because all its vertices are Boolean. Its staircase at prefix \(s\) has exactly the heights \(T_s=\{a_s,\ldots,T\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,T\}\), since \(a_1\ge1\). Estimate (7) at \((\mathbf j,1)\) gives \(m_d((\mathbf j,1),\log2)\ge(2\omega/7)R_{\mathbf j}^{+}\). Also every Boolean fiber at height zero has weight \(\omega\), and its cost-zero singleton must be hit, so
\[
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge\omega,
\qquad
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge
\frac{2\omega}{7(1+\log2)}R_*^{+}.
\tag{29}
\]
Partition (28) includes all lifted fibers when each partition point is supplied with its whole height column. Hence, using \(n=n_{\mathrm{tree}}+1\) and \(\sum_{t=0}^T\rho_t\le2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W}{2^n\omega}
&=\frac12\sum_{t=0}^T\rho_t
+\frac1{2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}+1}}\sum_{\mathbf j}R_{\mathbf j}^{+}
\le1+\frac{R_*^{+}}{2},\\
2^{-n}W
&\le\omega+\frac{\omega R_*^{+}}{2}
\le\left(1+\frac{7(1+\log2)}4\right)\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
