## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body with \(S=C\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\), where \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\). Retain the locked halfspace depth \(h_S\), with closed ambient halfspaces, and put \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)\). For every \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\), define

\[
C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I}),
\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

Let \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\). This is a finite set. When \(W>0\), let \(U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}/w_{\mathbf I}\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\), exactly as in accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647. All nonempty fibers remain in \(S\), including fibers on the boundary of the base projection. Fibers of zero \(d\)-volume retain their zero contribution to mixed volume and are not normalized. All logarithms are natural. Put \(a=\log2\) and \(\ell=-\log(1-e^{-1})\).

Fix \(\mathbf J\in D\). A finite feasible scheme has components indexed by \(k\), each with a nonempty support \(E_k\subseteq D\) of distinct source indices, coefficients \(\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}>0\), and mass \(x_k\ge0\), satisfying

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J,
\qquad
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le w_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D).
\tag{1}
\]

The source \(\mathbf I\) is charged the full mass \(x_k\) whenever it belongs to \(E_k\), independently of its positive barycentric coefficient. Supports of different components may coincide, and all their charges are shared in (1). There is no restriction to pairs, triples, affinely independent supports, or predetermined coefficients. Central mass \(x_\circ\) in accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 is included here as the component \(E_\circ=\{\mathbf J\}\), with coefficient one and cost zero. Thus (1) is exactly that predecessor's capacity system, with the central component included in the sums.

For each component, and then for the whole scheme, set

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_k&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}
\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}\ge0,\\
M&=\sum_k x_k,
&S_{\mathrm{cost}}&=\sum_k x_kL_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The notation \(S_{\mathrm{cost}}\) denotes a scalar cost mass; \(S\) continues to denote the mixed-integer set. For \(L\ge0\), let \(P_d(\mathbf J,L)\) be the maximum total mass of schemes (1) whose positive-mass components have \(L_k\le L\). This is exactly the unrestricted packing program \(P_d\) of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647; that fact supplies its finite support reduction and attainment. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L},\\
\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
&=\sup_{\substack{\text{finite feasible schemes at }\mathbf J\\
M>0}}
\frac{M^2}{aM+S_{\mathrm{cost}}},\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w),
\qquad
\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The cutoff score \(\mathcal B_d\) is exactly the unrestricted score of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647. The supplied source's average-cost scores \(\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}\) and \(\mathcal R_d\) are denoted \(\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}\) and \(\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}\) here. The superscript distinguishes these cost scores from the different integral score in the other supplied source. When \(W=0\), set \(\mathcal B_d(w)=\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)=0\), without defining positive-target schemes or uniform fiber laws.

For every finite feasible scheme with \(M>0\), set \(s=S_{\mathrm{cost}}/M\). There is a cutoff \(L\ge0\) for which discarding exactly the components with cost greater than \(L\) gives

\[
\frac{\sum_{k:L_k\le L}x_k}{1+L}
\ge
\frac{M}{1+s+\sqrt{s(s+2)}}.
\tag{4}
\]

The retained scheme uses the same target and satisfies every original source capacity. Consequently there is one \(\mathbf p\in\{\mathbf J\}\times C_{\mathbf J}\), selected before the ambient halfspace, with

\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge
\frac{M}{6\left(1+s+\sqrt{s(s+2)}\right)}.
\tag{5}
\]

At every positive target, and then after maximizing over targets,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)
&\le \mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
\le\frac{2}{a(2-a)}\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w),\\
\mathcal B_d(w)
&\le \mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
\le\frac{2}{a(2-a)}\mathcal B_d(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For \(W>0\), the two optimized depth guarantees supplied by accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 satisfy the strict comparison

\[
\frac{\ell}{8}\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
\le\frac{\ell}{4a(2-a)}\mathcal B_d(w)
<\frac{\mathcal B_d(w)}6.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(W=0\), all three quantities in (7) are zero and only the non-strict comparison is asserted.

There is nevertheless the following actual-mixture separation. For each integer \(m\ge1\), let \(N=4^m\), \(n=mN\), and index the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^n\) by \(\mathbf e_{r,i}\), where \(1\le r\le m\) and \(1\le i\le N\). Let \(I_i=[(i-1)/N,i/N]\), and define

\[
C_m=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(\{\mathbf0\}\times[0,1])
\;\cup\;
\bigcup_{r=1}^m\bigcup_{i=1}^N
(\{\mathbf e_{r,i},-\mathbf e_{r,i}\}\times I_i)
\right)\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R.
\tag{8}
\]

This is a full-dimensional rational convex polytope. Its complete nonempty lattice-fiber list consists of \((C_m)_{\mathbf0}=[0,1]\) and \((C_m)_{\pm\mathbf e_{r,i}}=I_i\). For its \(d=1\) volume table,

\[
w_{\mathbf0}=1,
\qquad
w_{\pm\mathbf e_{r,i}}=\frac1N,
\qquad
W_m=2m+1,
\qquad
\mathcal B_1(w)=1.
\tag{9}
\]

More precisely, all the target cutoff programs are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1(\mathbf0,L)
&=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le L<\log N,\\
m+1,&L\ge\log N,
\end{cases}\\
P_1(\pm\mathbf e_{r,i},L)&=\frac1N
\qquad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

At \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\), a feasible scheme saturating all original source capacities has total mass \(M=m+1\), central singleton mass one, and mass \(1/N\) on each antipodal pair with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\). It has couplings with the prescribed uniform source marginals such that its normalized output density \(q\) equals the uniform density \(u=\mathbf1_{[0,1]}\) almost everywhere. Thus

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|u)=0,
\qquad
\frac{M}{(1+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|u))\mathcal B_1(w)}=m+1,
\qquad
\frac{S_{\mathrm{cost}}}{M}
=\frac{m}{m+1}\log N
=\frac{m^2\log4}{m+1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|u)=\int q\log(q/u)\), with the usual zero-density convention; it is distinct from the positive lattice support \(D\). Each noncentral component of this scheme has actual relative entropy \(\log N\), equal to its root-volume cost. Thus its mass-weighted component cost diverges although the actual output entropy is zero. In view of (6), \(\mathcal R_1^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)\le2/[a(2-a)]\) throughout this family as well.

Writing \(S_m=C_m\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), the exact mixed depth is

\[
h_{S_m}((\mathbf0,1/2))=h_{\max}(C_m)=\frac{m+1}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]

In particular, there is no absolute \(A<\infty\) such that \(M/(1+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|u))\le A \mathcal B_d(w)\) for every actual convex body and every feasible coupled mixture, even when its normalized output is uniform on the target fiber.

The global sufficient estimate still unproved here is \(\mathcal B_d(w)\ge c\,2^{-n}W\), with one absolute \(c>0\) for all actual convex-body fiber-volume tables. By (6), replacing \(\mathcal B_d\) by \(\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}\) in this global requirement changes it only by an absolute factor. If the cutoff estimate held, accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 would give \(h_{\max}(C)\ge(c/6)2^{-n}W\), and hence the locked weak Oertel statement with, for example, \(c_0=\min\{c/6,1/(2e)\}\). The already accepted estimate from 03f071403cdfc69f is \(h_{\max}(C)\ge W/[288(n+1)2^n]\); the present comparison does not remove its factor \(n+1\). The family (8) is not a disproof of the conjecture: its weak Oertel scale is \(2^{-n}W_m=(2m+1)2^{-m4^m}\), which tends to zero and is smaller than its central fiber volume. No universal construction of sufficiently massive, geometrically usable actual output mixtures is proved.

## Proof

Accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 applies to exactly the convex body, positive fiber laws, barycentric coefficients, and full-occurrence capacities in (1). It implies that every cost in (2) is finite and nonnegative, gives the attained cutoff packing value and score in (3), and supplies the cutoff and mass-weighted depth bounds used below. Summing (1) over \(D\) gives \(\sum_k|E_k|x_k\le W\), so \(M\le W\). For \(W>0\), the central singleton shows \(P_d(\mathbf J,L)\ge w_{\mathbf J}>0\). In particular \(\mathcal B_d(w)>0\), and \(\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)\le W/a<\infty\). These observations check the normalizations and finiteness of all positive-target quantities.

Fix a finite scheme with \(M>0\), omitting zero-mass components if desired. Define \(F(t)=\sum_{k:L_k\le t}x_k\) for \(t\ge0\), and let \(b=\max_{t\ge0}F(t)/(1+t)\). This maximum is attained among zero and the finitely many component costs: between successive costs its numerator is constant and its denominator increases, and after the last cost its numerator is \(M\). Thus \(0<b\le M\) and \(F(t)\le b(1+t)\) for every \(t\ge0\). Finite summation gives the exact identity

\[
S_{\mathrm{cost}}
=\sum_k x_k\int_0^{L_k}dt
=\int_0^\infty(M-F(t))\,dt.
\tag{13}
\]

The values at individual cost endpoints do not affect this integral. Since \(M/b-1\ge0\), restricting the nonnegative integrand and then using its lower bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{cost}}
&\ge\int_0^{M/b-1}(M-b(1+t))\,dt\\
&=\frac{(M-b)^2}{2b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Consequently \(b^2-2(M+S_{\mathrm{cost}})b+M^2\le0\). Taking the smaller root as a lower bound for \(b\) and substituting \(s=S_{\mathrm{cost}}/M\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
b
&\ge M\left(1+s-\sqrt{s(s+2)}\right)\\
&=\frac{M}{1+s+\sqrt{s(s+2)}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

The second equality follows because the product of \(1+s-\sqrt{s(s+2)}\) and \(1+s+\sqrt{s(s+2)}\) is one. Choosing a cutoff attaining \(b\) proves (4). In the boundary case \(s=0\), all positive-mass costs are zero, so cutoff zero retains all mass and (4) is equality. No positive division by \(s\) has been used.

Deleting components weakly decreases every sum in (1), including the target-source charge, and leaves the barycenter of each retained component unchanged. The retained mass is \(F(L)>0\), with all costs at most \(L\). The cutoff conclusion of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 therefore supplies one point on the same target fiber with depth at least \(F(L)/[6(1+L)]\). Together with (15), this is (5). That predecessor supplies the point before every containing ambient halfspace and includes vertical halfspaces and whole boundary fibers. For \(M=0\), only the trivial zero guarantee is asserted, without defining \(s\) or evaluating (4)--(5).

To prove the lower comparison in (6), fix \(\mathbf J\in D\) and \(L\ge0\), and take a cutoff optimizer. Its mass \(M=P_d(\mathbf J,L)>0\) and cost satisfy \(S_{\mathrm{cost}}\le LM\). Hence

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)
\ge\frac{M^2}{aM+S_{\mathrm{cost}}}
\ge\frac{M}{a+L}
\ge\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L},
\tag{16}
\]

where \(a<1\). Maximizing over \(L\) gives the first inequality. For the upper comparison, take any finite positive-mass scheme at the same target and its value \(b\) from (13)--(15). Each retained scheme is admissible for its cutoff, so \(b\le \mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)\). The scaled square identity

\[
aM+\frac{(M-b)^2}{2b}
-\frac{a(2-a)}{2b}M^2
=\frac{((1-a)M-b)^2}{2b}\ge0
\tag{17}
\]

and (14) imply

\[
\frac{M^2}{aM+S_{\mathrm{cost}}}
\le\frac{2b}{a(2-a)}
\le\frac{2}{a(2-a)}\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w).
\tag{18}
\]

Taking the supremum over schemes proves the upper inequality. Taking maxima over the finite nonempty target set proves the global comparison.

Accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 gives the mass-weighted guarantee \(\ell M^2/[8(aM+S_{\mathrm{cost}})]\) and the optimized cutoff guarantee \(\mathcal B_d(w)/6\). The first optimizes to \((\ell/8)\mathcal R_d^{\mathrm{cost}}(w)\). The maximum defining \(h_{\max}(C)\) is attained by accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f, so optimizing such lower bounds does not require an additional attainment assumption for the schemes. Equation (18) gives the first inequality in (7).

For strictness, elementary exact estimates suffice. Substitution in the logarithm integral gives \(a=\int_0^{1/3}2/(1-t^2)\,dt>2/3\), while \(a=\int_1^2 t^{-1}\,dt<1\). Therefore \(a(2-a)>8/9\). Also \(e^{1/2}>1+1/2+1/8=13/8\), and \((13/8)^2-13/8-1=1/64>0\). Since \(t^2-t-1\) is strictly increasing for \(t>1/2\), it follows that \(e-e^{1/2}-1>0\), or \(1-e^{-1}>e^{-1/2}\). Thus \(\ell<1/2\), and

\[
3\ell<\frac32<\frac{16}{9}<2a(2-a).
\tag{19}
\]

This is precisely \(\ell/[4a(2-a)]<1/6\). Since \(\mathcal B_d(w)>0\) when \(W>0\), multiplication proves the strict inequality in (7). If \(W=0\), the score conventions give equality zero and any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\) satisfies the zero depth bound.

We now verify the actual bodies in (8) directly. The generating intervals have rational endpoints, so their convex hull is a rational polytope. Projection onto \(\mathbb R^n\) commutes with convex hull, and its image is \(\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf e_{r,i}\}=\{\mathbf x:\|\mathbf x\|_1\le1\}\). Indeed, the inclusion in this ball follows by convexity, and any point of the ball is a convex combination of its signed coordinate basis vectors and \(\mathbf0\), with weights given by the absolute coordinates and the remaining weight \(1-\|\mathbf x\|_1\). An integer vector in this ball has integer \(\ell^1\)-norm at most one, so it is exactly \(\mathbf0\) or one of the signed basis vectors.

Every generating point has its last coordinate in \([0,1]\), and the full central interval is included, proving \((C_m)_{\mathbf0}=[0,1]\). Each signed basis vector is an extreme point of the projection. More explicitly, the functional \(\mathbf x\mapsto \sigma x_{r,i}\), for \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\), has its unique maximum one at \(\sigma\mathbf e_{r,i}\) among the generating base points. A convex combination with that projected value must use only generators over that same base point. Hence its fiber is exactly \(I_i\). There are no other nonempty integer fibers. In particular every noncentral lattice fiber on the projection boundary is present with its full length \(1/N\).

To check full dimension, take the point \((\mathbf0,0)\), the point \((\mathbf0,1)\), and the \(n\) points \((\mathbf e_{r,i},(i-1)/N)\). They all belong to \(C_m\). Their \(n+1\) differences from \((\mathbf0,0)\) are linearly independent: projection onto the first \(n\) coordinates forces the coefficients of the latter \(n\) vectors to vanish, after which the coefficient of \((\mathbf0,1)\) vanishes. Thus \(C_m\) is a convex body of dimension \(n+1\). The complete fiber list gives \(W_m=1+2mN/N=2m+1\), proving the fiber and mass assertions in (9).

Consider now all finite schemes at target \(\mathbf0\), with arbitrary positive barycentric coefficients and arbitrary support sizes. If a component omits \(\mathbf0\), all its source lengths are \(1/N\). Since \(d=1\), its root-volume mean is exactly \(1/N\), and its cost is \(\log N\). Such a component has at least two sources, because no noncentral singleton has barycenter zero. If \(0\le L<\log N\), every allowed component therefore uses the central source. Its full-occurrence capacity limits their total mass to one, and the central singleton of mass one attains that value.

For any cutoff, the total mass of components containing \(\mathbf0\) is at most one. The remaining components satisfy

\[
2\sum_{k:\mathbf0\notin E_k}x_k
\le\sum_{k:\mathbf0\notin E_k}|E_k|x_k
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\ne\mathbf0}w_{\mathbf I}=2m.
\tag{20}
\]

The last inequality is the sum of their original noncentral capacities; any usage by components containing \(\mathbf0\) can only reduce the available amount. Thus every scheme has mass at most \(m+1\). At every \(L\ge\log N\), the central singleton of mass one and all antipodal pairs of mass \(1/N\), with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), are allowed. Each noncentral source occurs in exactly one of these pairs, so this scheme saturates every capacity and attains \(m+1\). This proves both branches of the first formula in (10), including equality at \(L=\log N\).

Consequently

\[
\mathcal B_{\mathbf0}(w)
=\max\left\{1,\frac{m+1}{1+\log N}\right\}=1,
\tag{21}
\]

because \(\log N=m\log4=2ma>m\). At target \(\sigma\mathbf e_{r,i}\), the same uniquely maximizing coordinate functional used above forces every source in a positive barycentric representation to equal that target. Thus the only possible support is its singleton. Its cost is zero and its capacity is \(1/N\), giving \(P_1(\sigma\mathbf e_{r,i},L)=1/N\) for every \(L\ge0\), and \(\mathcal B_{\sigma\mathbf e_{r,i}}(w)=1/N\). This proves all of (9)--(10) and the global value \(\mathcal B_1(w)=1\). The upper bounds were proved for the full original class of components, so this calculation does not rely on restricting to the attaining pair scheme.

Use that attaining scheme for the coupled mixture. In each pair indexed by \((r,i)\), sample \(T\) uniformly from \(I_i\), and use the joint source points \((\mathbf e_{r,i},T)\) and \((-\mathbf e_{r,i},T)\). Both source marginals are exactly their uniform fiber laws. Their midpoint is \((\mathbf0,T)\), so the output density is \(\rho_{r,i}(t)=N\mathbf1_{I_i}(t)\). Its mass multiplier is \(1/N\), and hence its marginal usage at each source is exactly \((1/N)U_{\pm\mathbf e_{r,i}}=\mu_{\pm\mathbf e_{r,i}}\). The central singleton of mass one uses exactly \(\mu_{\mathbf0}\). This verifies the measure capacities as well as their total-mass versions.

The output measure has total mass \(M=m+1\). Its normalized density is

\[
\begin{aligned}
q(t)
&=\frac1{m+1}\left(
\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(t)
+\sum_{r=1}^m\sum_{i=1}^N\frac1N\,N\mathbf1_{I_i}(t)
\right)\\
&=\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(t)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

The intervals form a partition except for their finitely many shared endpoints, so the almost-everywhere equality is exact. This proves \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|u)=0\). For every noncentral component, direct integration gives

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_{r,i}\|u)
=\int_{I_i}N\log N\,dt
=\log N=L_{r,i}.
\tag{23}
\]

The central component has both entropy and cost zero. There are \(mN\) pair components of mass \(1/N\), so \(S_{\mathrm{cost}}=m\log N\). This proves (11), including its divergent mass-weighted cost and the unbounded ratio to \(\mathcal B_1(w)\). Applying (6) to the same actual table also gives the stated bound on \(\mathcal R_1^{\mathrm{cost}}\).

For the exact depth, write \(\mu_m(A)=\mathcal H_1(S_m\cap A)\) for an ambient measurable set \(A\). Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace. For every \(r,i,t\), the point \((\mathbf0,t)\) is the midpoint of \((\mathbf e_{r,i},t)\) and \((-\mathbf e_{r,i},t)\). If its affine halfspace score is nonnegative, at least one of the two endpoint scores is nonnegative. Therefore

\[
\mathbf1_H(\mathbf0,t)
\le\mathbf1_H(\mathbf e_{r,i},t)
+\mathbf1_H(-\mathbf e_{r,i},t).
\tag{24}
\]

Integrating (24) over \(I_i\), summing, and including the central fiber gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_m(H)
&=\int_0^1\mathbf1_H(\mathbf0,t)\,dt
+\sum_{r=1}^m\sum_{i=1}^N
\int_{I_i}\left(
\mathbf1_H(\mathbf e_{r,i},t)
+\mathbf1_H(-\mathbf e_{r,i},t)\right)\,dt\\
&\ge(m+1)\int_0^1\mathbf1_H(\mathbf0,t)\,dt
=M\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_H(\mathbf0,t)q(t)\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

Now fix \(\mathbf p=(\mathbf0,1/2)\), before choosing \(H\). The section on the central line of any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p\) is a closed ray or the whole line and contains at least half of \([0,1]\). This includes a halfspace with zero continuous-coordinate normal: its central section is the whole line. Thus (25) gives \(h_{S_m}(\mathbf p)\ge(m+1)/2\). All the indicator implications use weak inequalities. In particular (25) keeps entire fibers lying on an ambient boundary; it makes no assertion that ambient hyperplanes are \(\mu_m\)-null.

For an upper bound at any point on a noncentral fiber \(\sigma\mathbf e_{r,i}\), take the halfspace \(\{(\mathbf x,t):\sigma x_{r,i}\ge1\}\). Among the complete lattice support it contains exactly that fiber, including the whole fiber on its boundary. Its mixed mass is exactly \(1/N\). Hence every such point has depth at most \(1/N\le(m+1)/2\).

It remains to bound all central points \((\mathbf0,t_0)\), where \(0\le t_0\le1\). The integer \(N=4^m\) is even. If \(t_0\le1/2\), choose the vector \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(v_{r,i}=2\) for \(i\le N/2\) and \(v_{r,i}=0\) for \(i>N/2\), and take

\[
H=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x-t\ge-t_0\}.
\tag{26}
\]

This proper closed halfspace contains the chosen point on its boundary. Its central contribution is the length \(t_0\) of \([0,t_0]\). For \(i\le N/2\), it contains the entire positive fiber and none of the negative fiber: the respective affine scores relative to the threshold are \(2-t+t_0>0\) and \(-2-t+t_0<0\). For \(i>N/2\), the condition is \(t\le t_0\); since these intervals start at or after \(1/2\), their captured lengths are zero. When \(t_0=1/2\), a shared endpoint can be included, but it has zero one-dimensional length. There are \(mN/2\) included whole noncentral fibers, giving exactly

\[
\mu_m(H)=t_0+\frac{mN/2}{N}
=t_0+\frac m2\le\frac{m+1}{2}.
\tag{27}
\]

If \(t_0\ge1/2\), instead take \(v_{r,i}=2\) for \(i>N/2\), zero otherwise, and use

\[
H=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x+t\ge t_0\}.
\tag{28}
\]

Its central contribution is \(1-t_0\). For \(i>N/2\), it includes the positive fiber and excludes the negative one, because \(2+t-t_0>0\) and \(-2+t-t_0<0\). For \(i\le N/2\), its condition is \(t\ge t_0\), which captures at most a zero-length shared endpoint. Therefore its exact mass is \(1-t_0+m/2\le(m+1)/2\). These bounds cover every point of \(S_m\), including all fiber endpoints. Together with the lower bound at \(\mathbf p\), they prove (12).

Finally, (11) grows without bound while \(\mathcal B_1(w)=1\), disproving the proposed universal comparison of actual-mixture entropy with a cutoff score. This is compatible with the general scale sought in the locked problem. Indeed \(n=m4^m\ge4m\), so \(2^n\ge n+1>2m+1\) and hence \(2^{-n}W_m<1\); also \((2m+1)2^{-m4^m}\le(2m+1)16^{-m}\) tends to zero.
