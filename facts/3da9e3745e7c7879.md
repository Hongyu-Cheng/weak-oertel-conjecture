## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and $H,J$ be integers with $1\le J\le H\le(2d+1)J+d$. Put $t=1/(d+1)$ and $\Gamma=(dt)^d$. Define the double knot
$$
a=\frac{t(H+J+1)}{H(2J+1)},\qquad
\beta=\frac{t(H-J)}{dH(2J+1)}.
$$
Write $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For $h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ put $r_h=(1-ah)_+$, $q_h=t-\beta h$, and $p_h=t+\beta(h+1)$. Define the finite diagonal-envelope deficit by
$$
D=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0}r_h^d
-d^d\sum_{\substack{h\ge0\\r_h>0}}\left[q_h^{d-1}(r_h-dq_h)_++p_h^{d-1}(r_h-dp_h)_+\right].
$$
The thresholds in the second sum are positive, and $D/\Gamma\le-1/1080$.

## Proof

**Normalization at the knot.** Put $\theta=dt$ and $v=\beta/(at)=(d+1)(H-J)/[d(H+J+1)]$. The bounds on $H$ give $0\le v\le1$. For $x=ah$ and $r_h>0$, the thresholds are $q_h=t(1-vx)\ge tr_h>0$ and $p_h=t(1+v(x+a))>0$. Define $b=dav$, $B_-=d+1-dv$, and $B=d+1+dv$. Substitution of the knot coordinates gives
$$
\frac{1}{B_-}=aH,\qquad
\frac{1-b}{B}=aJ,\qquad
b=\frac{H-J}{H(2J+1)}\le\frac{1}{2J+1}\le\frac13.
$$
In particular, $1/[a(d+1)]=J+\theta v(J+1)$ by the second endpoint identity. For $0\le s\le a$, set $A_s=1-dsv$, $C_s=1+sv$ and define, for $x\ge0$,
$$
L_v(x)=(1-vx)^{d-1}[1-B_-x]_+,\qquad
R_{s,v}(x)=(C_s+vx)^{d-1}[A_s-Bx]_+.
$$
Here $L_v$ is defined to be zero beyond $1/B_-$, and $1-vx\ge0$ on its support. The other kernel has support endpoint $A_s/B$, with $A_s\ge 1-b\ge2/3$. Both kernels vanish for $x\ge1$ since $1/B_-\le1$ and $A_s/B\le1/(d+1)$. For $x=ah<1$ the cap brackets are $r_h-dq_h=t(1-B_-x)$ and $r_h-dp_h=t(1-b-Bx)$. The factors of $t$ combine with $d^d$ to give $\Gamma$. Hence, with $W=\sum_{h\ge0}(1-ah)_+^d$,
$$
D/\Gamma=W-\sum_{h\ge0}L_v(ah)-\sum_{h\ge0}R_{a,v}(ah).
$$
At $v=1$, $aH=1$ and the kernel vanishes at that endpoint as required by the positive-radius convention.

**Mesh errors.** For a continuous piecewise smooth $g$ whose derivative has bounded variation, integration by parts on a cell $[u,u+a]$ gives the trapezoid error
$$
\frac a2[g(u)+g(u+a)]-\int_u^{u+a}g(x)\,dx
=\int_{(u,u+a)}\frac{(x-u)(u+a-x)}{2}\,dg'(x).
$$
The integrand vanishes at the cell endpoints, so derivative jumps there contribute zero. The kernel is nonnegative with maximum $a^2/8$. Summing the cells and dividing by $a$ shows that a compactly supported convex $g$ on $[0,\infty)$ has $\sum_{h\ge0}g(ah)-a^{-1}\int_0^\infty g-g(0)/2$ between $0$ and $(a/8)\operatorname{Var}(g')$. On a finite interval $[0,aJ]$ for a concave $g$ with $g(aJ)=0$, the same difference for the finite sum is between $-(a/8)[g'(0^+)-g'(aJ^-)]$ and zero.

The mass kernel $(1-x)_+^d$ is convex, with derivative variation $d$ and integral $1/(d+1)$. The two factors of $L_v$ are nonnegative, nonincreasing and convex on $[0,aH]$. On its interior the product rule gives a nonnegative second derivative. Its left derivative at $aH$ is nonpositive, so extending by zero preserves convexity, including when $v=1$ and $d=2$. Consequently,
$$
W\le\frac{1}{a(d+1)}+\frac12+\frac{ad}{8},
\qquad
\sum_{h\ge0}L_v(ah)\ge \frac1a\int_0^\infty L_v(x)\,dx+\frac12 .
$$

On $[0,aJ]$ the right kernel is $(C_a+vx)^{d-1}(A_a-Bx)$. Its second derivative in the interior is
$$
(d-1)v(C_a+vx)^{d-3}\big[(d-2)v(A_a-Bx)-2B(C_a+vx)\big]\le0,
$$
since $A_a-Bx\le 1\le C_a+vx$ and $(d-2)v\le B$. Use the concave estimate on precisely these $J$ complete mesh cells, with terminal value zero. For $Q=C_a+vaJ=1+av(J+1)$ the active derivative drop is
$$
\begin{aligned}
R'_{a,v}(0^+)-R'_{a,v}(aJ^-)
&=(d-1)v C_a^{d-2}A_a+B(Q^{d-1}-C_a^{d-1})\\
&\le2(d-1)vA_a Q^{d-2},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $Q-C_a=vA_a/B$ and the integer power has derivative at most $(d-1)Q^{d-2}$ on $[C_a,Q]$. Since $J\ge1$, $Q\le1+(J+1)/[d(2J+1)]\le1+2/(3d)$, so $Q^{d-2}\le\exp(2/3)<2$. The strict bound follows from $\log 2=2\int_0^{1/3}(1-y^2)^{-1}\,dy>2/3$. Thus the quadrature loss is at most $(a/8)\,4(d-1)vA_a\le b/2$, and
$$
\sum_{h\ge0}R_{a,v}(ah)\ge
\frac1a\int_0^\infty R_{a,v}(x)\,dx+\frac12R_{a,v}(0)-\frac b2.
$$

**Finite shift.** Set $I(s)=\int_0^\infty R_{s,v}(x)\,dx$. The active endpoint $A_s/B$ is positive and varies smoothly, and the kernel vanishes there. On the active interval, $\partial_sR_{s,v}=\partial_xR_{s,v}+(d+1)(C_s+vx)^{d-1}$ by differentiation. The moving endpoint has zero boundary term. Integrating the identity gives
$$
I'(s)=-C_s^{d-1}A_s+(d+1)\int_0^{A_s/B}(C_s+vx)^{d-1}\,dx.
$$
The integrand is at least $C_s^{d-1}$, so $I'(s)\ge -C_s^{d-1}A_s\,dv/B$. The nonnegative factor $C_s^{d-1}A_s$ has initial value $1$ and derivative $-v C_s^{d-2}(1+d^2sv)\le0$. Also $dv/B\le v/(1+v)$. Integration over $0\le s\le a$ yields $I(a)\ge I(0)-av/(1+v)$, including equality at $v=0$.

**The continuous gain and endpoint.** The predecessor theorem on the uniform quartic gain applies in integer dimension $d\ge2$ with $v\in[0,1]$. Its kernels are exactly $K_{-v}=L_v$ and $K_v=R_{0,v}$, so $\int_0^\infty L_v+I(0)\ge(1+v^2/4)/(d+1)$. At $v=0$ each kernel is $(1-(d+1)x)_+$ and their integral sum is $1/(d+1)$. Substituting the mesh and shift bounds gives
$$
\frac D\Gamma\le
-\frac{v^2}{4a(d+1)}+\frac{v}{1+v}-\frac12 R_{a,v}(0)+\frac{ad}{8}+\frac b2.
$$
Bernoulli's inequality gives $R_{a,v}(0)=(1+b/d)^{d-1}(1-b)\ge(1+b/2)(1-b)$ because $(d-1)/d\ge1/2$ and $1-b>0$. The combined endpoint and mesh correction is therefore at most $-1/2+3b/4+b^2/4+ad/8$. Since $\theta\in[2/3,1)$ and $J\ge1$, the knot identity gives $1/[a(d+1)]\ge1+(4/3)v$, and
$$
da=\frac{\theta}{J+\theta v(J+1)}\le\frac{\theta}{1+2\theta v}\le\frac1{1+2v},
\qquad b\le\frac{v}{1+2v}.
$$
Substituting the lower bound in the negative term and the upper bounds in the increasing positive terms gives $D/\Gamma\le U(v)$, where
$$
U(v)=-\frac{v^2}{4}-\frac{v^3}{3}+\frac{v}{1+v}-\frac12
 +\frac{3v}{4(1+2v)}+\frac{v^2}{4(1+2v)^2}+\frac1{8(1+2v)}.
$$

**Exact polynomial bound.** Multiplication by the positive denominator gives
$$
-24(1+v)(1+2v)^2 U(v)=P(v)=9+9v-60v^2-52v^3+88v^4+88v^5+32v^6.
$$
For $[l,r]\subseteq[0,1]$, write $P(l+(r-l)z)=\sum_{j=0}^6 c_j z^j$. Its degree-six Bernstein coefficient of index $k$ is $\sum_{j=0}^k c_j\binom{k}{j}/\binom{6}{j}$, by expanding $z^j(z+(1-z))^{6-j}$. Substitution gives the following rows in order of increasing index.
$$
\begin{aligned}
[0,1/2]\quad &9,\ 39/4,\ 19/2,\ 317/40,\ 76/15,\ 43/24,\ 3/4,\\
[1/2,3/5]\quad &3/4,\ 13/24,\ 317/750,\ 2071/5000,\ 3377/6250,\ 10353/12500,\ 20448/15625,\\
[3/5,1]\quad &20448/15625,\ 2019/625,\ 25726/3125,\ 457/25,\ 13588/375,\ 331/5,\ 114 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Each coefficient is at least $2/5$. The Bernstein basis functions $\binom6k z^k(1-z)^{6-k}$ are nonnegative and sum to one for $z\in[0,1]$, so $P(v)\ge2/5$ on all three closed intervals. Also $24(1+v)(1+2v)^2\le432$. Therefore $D/\Gamma\le U(v)\le-(2/5)/432=-1/1080$.
