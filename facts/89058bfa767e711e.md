## Statement

For every integer \(R\geq2\), put
\[
 P_R=[-R,R]^2,\qquad H_R=3R+1,\qquad p=\frac1{20},\qquad q=\frac{59}{20},
\]
and define, for \(y\in P_R\),
\[
 \ell_R(y)=-\frac{H_R}{2}+p\lVert y\rVert_\infty,
 \qquad
 u_R(y)=\frac{H_R}{2}-q\lVert y\rVert_\infty.
\]
In base coordinates \(y\in\mathbb R^2\) and continuous coordinates \((s,t)\in\mathbb R^2\), let
\[
 C_R=\{(y,s,t):y\in P_R,
 \ \ell_R(y)\leq s\leq u_R(y),\ 0\leq t\leq1\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(C_R\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its positive lattice fibers are indexed by
\[
 E_R=P_R\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\]
and, writing \(k=\lVert z\rVert_\infty\), their two-dimensional volumes are
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_2((C_R)_z)=H_R-3k>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Their total mixed volume is
\[
 W_R:=\sum_{z\in E_R}a_z
 =4R^3+4R^2+3R+1.
\tag{3}
\]
The lattice point \(m=0\) satisfies the stronger-than-required base balance
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_R\cap J}a_z\geq\frac{W_R}{2}
\tag{4}
\]
for every closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(0\).

For a two-dimensional convex body \(K\) and \(0<b\leq\operatorname{vol}_2(K)\), define
\[
 \mathsf M_b(K)=\left\{\frac1b\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=b\right\}.
\]
Let
\[
 \Lambda_0(E_R)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^{E_R}:
 \sum_z\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_z\lambda_zz=0\right\}
\]
and let the half-mass complete metronoid core be
\[
 K_R=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_0(E_R)}
 \sum_{z\in E_R}\lambda_z
 \mathsf M_{a_z/2}((C_R)_z).
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
 K_R=\varnothing.
\tag{6}
\]

Set the exact \(2^{-2}\)-target mass \(A_R=W_R/4\), and define its feasible coefficient polytope at base barycenter \(0\) by
\[
 \mathcal A_R=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^{E_R}:
 \sum_z\alpha_z=1,\quad \sum_z\alpha_zz=0,\quad
 A_R\alpha_z\leq a_z\ \text{for every }z\in E_R\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
This polytope is nonempty, and every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_R\) obeys
\[
 \left(\sum_{z\in E_R}\alpha_z^{3/2}\right)^2
 \leq\frac{4(3R+1)}{4R^3+4R^2+3R+1}.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_R}
 \left(\sum_{z\in E_R}\alpha_z^{3/2}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, there is no universal constant \(c>0\) such that emptiness of the half-mass complete core at a weighted lattice centerpoint in the case \(n=d=2\) implies the existence of a feasible exact target-mass coefficient vector with effective-support score at least \(c\). Assertions (1)-(9) do not give an upper bound for the depth of any point of \(C_R\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^2)\), and hence are not a counterexample to the weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

The function \(y\mapsto\lVert y\rVert_\infty\) is convex. Hence \(\ell_R\) is convex and \(u_R\) is concave. Moreover,
\[
 u_R(y)-\ell_R(y)
 =H_R-(p+q)\lVert y\rVert_\infty
 =H_R-3\lVert y\rVert_\infty\geq1
\tag{10}
\]
on \(P_R\). The set in (1) is the intersection of the base cylinder \(y\in P_R\), the epigraph constraint \(s\geq\ell_R(y)\), the hypograph constraint \(s\leq u_R(y)\), and the slab \(0\leq t\leq1\); all four sets are closed and convex. Because \(P_R\) is compact and \(\ell_R,u_R\) are continuous, these constraints also bound \(s\), so the intersection is compact. At \(y=0\), the point \((y,s,t)=(0,0,1/2)\) satisfies every non-base inequality strictly, and the base point is in the interior of \(P_R\). Continuity and (10) therefore give an open neighborhood contained in \(C_R\), proving full dimensionality in \(\mathbb R^4\).

For \(z\in E_R\), the fiber is the rectangle
\[
 (C_R)_z=[\ell_R(z),u_R(z)]\times[0,1],
\]
whose volume is its scalar length \(H_R-3\lVert z\rVert_\infty\). This is at least \(1\), proving (2), while there are no lattice fibers outside \(E_R\). There is one lattice point with max norm zero and exactly \(8k\) lattice points with max norm \(k\) for \(1\leq k\leq R\). Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_R
 &=H_R+8\sum_{k=1}^R k(H_R-3k)\\
 &=3R+1+4R(R+1)(3R+1)-4R(R+1)(2R+1)\\
 &=4R^3+4R^2+3R+1,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (3).

The weights are antipodally symmetric: \(a_z=a_{-z}\). Every closed halfspace containing \(0\) contains \(0\) and at least one point from each nonzero pair \(\{z,-z\}\). Indeed, writing the halfspace as \(\{y:\langle v,y\rangle\geq b\}\) with \(b\leq0\), exclusion of both \(z\) and \(-z\) would give simultaneously \(\langle v,z\rangle<b\) and \(-\langle v,z\rangle<b\), whose sum contradicts \(2b\leq0\). Therefore its weight is at least
\[
 a_0+\frac{W_R-a_0}{2}=\frac{W_R+a_0}{2}\geq\frac{W_R}{2},
\]
proving (4).

It remains to verify (6) using two exact barycentric representations of \(0\). The scalar center of the interval in the fiber at \(z\) is
\[
 c_z=\frac{\ell_R(z)+u_R(z)}2
 =-\frac{29}{20}\lVert z\rVert_\infty.
\tag{11}
\]
Apply the exact prismatic metronoid projection in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f with \(U=[0,1]\), \(V=1\), and scalar metronoid mass parameter \(\tau_z=a_z/2\). It gives
\[
 \operatorname{proj}_s\mathsf M_{a_z/2}((C_R)_z)
 =\left[c_z-\frac{a_z}{4},c_z+\frac{a_z}{4}\right].
\tag{12}
\]
The coefficient vector \(\lambda^0=\delta_0\) belongs to \(\Lambda_0(E_R)\), and the scalar projection of its Minkowski sum in (5) is
\[
 I^0=\left[-\frac{H_R}{4},\frac{H_R}{4}\right].
\tag{13}
\]
The second coefficient vector
\[
 \lambda^R=\frac12\delta_{(R,0)}+\frac12\delta_{(-R,0)}
\]
also belongs to \(\Lambda_0(E_R)\). Both endpoint fibers have \(a_z=1\) and \(c_z=-29R/20\), so the scalar projection of this second Minkowski sum is
\[
 I^R=\left[-\frac{29R}{20}-\frac14,
 -\frac{29R}{20}+\frac14\right].
\tag{14}
\]
For every \(R\geq2\),
\[
 -\frac{29R}{20}+\frac14
 <-\frac{3R+1}{4}=-\frac{H_R}{4},
\tag{15}
\]
because (15) is equivalent to \(R>5/7\). Thus \(I^0\cap I^R=\varnothing\). Projection commutes with Minkowski addition, so the two full Minkowski sums indexed by \(\lambda^0\) and \(\lambda^R\) are disjoint. Their common intersection contains \(K_R\), and hence (6) follows.

For completeness, \(\mathcal A_R\neq\varnothing\): the symmetric vector \(\alpha_z=a_z/W_R\) has total mass one, base barycenter zero, and
\[
 A_R\alpha_z=\frac{a_z}{4}\leq a_z.
\]
Now fix any \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_R\). The capacity inequalities in (7) and (2) imply
\[
 \max_z\alpha_z\leq\max_z\frac{a_z}{A_R}
 =\frac{4H_R}{W_R}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\sum_z\alpha_z=1\),
\[
 \sum_z\alpha_z^{3/2}
 \leq(\max_z\alpha_z)^{1/2}\sum_z\alpha_z
 =(\max_z\alpha_z)^{1/2}.
\]
Squaring and using (16) proves (8). Its right-hand side tends to zero because its numerator is linear and its denominator is cubic in \(R\). This proves (9) and the asserted failure of every universal positive effective-support lower bound under the empty-core hypothesis. Nothing in the construction estimates the infimum of mixed halfspace mass at any point, so no depth upper bound follows.
