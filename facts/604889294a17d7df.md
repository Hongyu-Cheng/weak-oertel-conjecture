## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge8\) and positive real numbers \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_k\) satisfying \(\rho_0=1\) and \(\rho_{t+1}\le\rho_t/2\) for \(0\le t<k\). Define \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), and \(b_\ell=\lfloor1/(8\ell\rho_\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\). Put \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r\), and \(Q=Q_k\). Assign entrance heights by \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). Thus \(a_1,\ldots,a_Q\) are nondecreasing integers between eight and \(k\).

Use the rooted-tree construction of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 with \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), its tree depth \(Q\), its dimension \(n_{m,Q}=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\), and its exact objects \(D_m,F_m,\mathcal T_{m,Q},A_{m,Q},P_{m,Q}\). The source transcript's tree-depth symbol \(q\) is exactly this \(Q\), not the parity-collision probability \(q\) in accepted fact 997f7c3a0c4804e4. In particular, \(F_m\) is the injective successor in a partition of the Boolean cube into symmetric saturated chains. For each Boolean vector \(b\) on the tree coordinates, its successive selected children form a path \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\). The points are \(z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}\), for \(0\le s\le h(b)\), and their union is \(A_{m,Q}\). Its level is its number of coordinates equal to two. Write \(n=n_{m,Q}\), \(N=n+1\), and \(\eta=\eta_m=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}\). The discrete profile \(\rho_t\) here is different from the interval-radius function \(\rho(x)\) in that predecessor.

For \(x\in P_{m,Q}\), define \(\phi(x)=\max\{0,\max_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}a_{|u|}(x_u-1)\}\), and put \(\widehat P=\{(x,t):x\in P_{m,Q},\ \phi(x)\le t\le k\}\). Then \(\widehat P\) is a full-dimensional rational compact convex polytope in \(\mathbb R^N\), with exact lattice support
\[
\widehat A=\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^N
=\{(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Set \(w_{(z,t)}=\rho_t\) on \(\widehat A\), and set all other integer weights to zero. Define \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^N}w_{\mathbf I}\), \(M(w)=\max_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}\), and \(S_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^N}\min\{w_{\mathbf J+v},w_{\mathbf J-v}\}\). Here \(M(w)\) is the maximum atom, not a kernel mass or a midpoint map. Displacement sums are oriented and include zero exactly once.

For every such profile, with \(H_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k1/\ell\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
M(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^N}S_{\mathbf J}&=1,\\
\frac W{2^N}
&=\frac12\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\eta^s\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}\rho_t,\\
\frac38\left(\frac{127}{128}+\frac{H_k-H_7}{8}\right)
&\le\frac W{2^N}\le1+\frac{H_k-H_7}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, \(W/2^N\ge(3/64)(H_k-H_7)\), uniformly over all allowed profiles.

For the following two specializations, use the canonical scalar quantities of accepted facts 280b299bc1e87c14 and 997f7c3a0c4804e4, with their integer dimension \(n\) replaced by \(N\) and their center \(j\) replaced by \(\mathbf J\). Thus, at a positive center, \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{\mathbf J+v},w_{\mathbf J-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}=\{v:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathbf J}(v)
&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}^2}{w_{\mathbf J+v}w_{\mathbf J-v}}
&& (v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}),\\
B_{\mathbf J}^{\Delta}(L)
&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}\\\Delta_{\mathbf J}(v)\le L}}\alpha(v),
&K(w)&=\max_{\substack{\mathbf J:w_{\mathbf J}>0\\L\ge0}}
\frac{B_{\mathbf J}^{\Delta}(L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All logarithms are natural.

First take \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\). These are the restrictions of a log-affine function to the rational polytope \(\widehat P\), with a globally log-concave zero extension. Every positive reflected pair has \(\Delta_{\mathbf J}(v)=0\), \(K(w)=1\), and \(W/2^N\sim H_k/8\) as \(k\to\infty\).

Second, for integers \(d\ge2k\), take \(\rho_t=(1-t/d)^d\), and rebuild \(b_\ell,Q,m,\widehat P,N\) from this exact profile. Define
\[
C_{k,d}
=\left\{(x,t,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d:
(x,t)\in\widehat P,\quad
|y_i|\le\frac{1-t/d}{2}\ (1\le i\le d)\right\}.
\]
This is a full-dimensional rational compact convex polytope with integer dimension \(N\) and continuous dimension \(d\). Its exact actual fiber volumes are \(w_{\mathbf I}=\operatorname{vol}_d((C_{k,d})_{\mathbf I})\), equal to the profile table just defined, including all zeros. This table is globally log-concave after extension by zero from \(\widehat P\), and \(M(w)=K(w)=\max_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}=1\). For \(\mathbf J=(j,h)\) and \(v=(v',u)\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}\),
\[
0\le\Delta_{\mathbf J}(v)
=-d\log\left(1-\frac{u^2}{(d-h)^2}\right)
\le\frac{4k^2}{3d}.

The following assertion concerns these actual bodies. For each integer index \(\mathbf I\in\widehat A\), let \(\mu_{\mathbf I}\) be Lebesgue measure restricted to its actual continuous fiber. Fix any integer target \(\mathbf J\). An admissible two-fiber scheme consists of a finite nonnegative central measure \(\sigma_{\mathbf J}\), supported in the target fiber and with mass at most \(w_{\mathbf J}\), and the following data for unordered pairs of distinct indices \(\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2\}\subseteq\widehat A\). After choosing an order for each pair, choose \(0<\theta<1\) satisfying \((1-\theta)\mathbf I_1+\theta\mathbf I_2=\mathbf J\), and a finite nonnegative coupling \(\pi_{\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2\}}\) on the two continuous fibers. Its first and second marginals must be dominated by \(\mu_{\mathbf I_1}\) and \(\mu_{\mathbf I_2}\), respectively. If a pair is used more than once, these constraints apply to its aggregate coupling after consistent ordering. The associated measure on the target fiber is
\[
\kappa_{\mathbf J}
=\sigma_{\mathbf J}
+\sum_{\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2\}}
\bigl((y_1,y_2)\mapsto(1-\theta)y_1+\theta y_2\bigr)_{\#}
\pi_{\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2\}}.
\]
Here \(\kappa_{\mathbf J}\) is a generalized measure, not an assertion that it equals the particular uniform midpoint kernel in accepted fact 280b299bc1e87c14. There is no uniformity or independence assumption on the selections or couplings. There is also no shared-capacity assumption across different unordered pairs. Consequently the following bounds also apply when such an assumption is imposed, including a requirement that all marginal usage at any one fiber, together with its central usage when relevant, be dominated by its actual Lebesgue measure.

Every admissible scheme satisfies \(|\kappa_{\mathbf J}|\le1\). If \(\mathbf J=(j,h)\in\widehat A\) and \(h\ge4\), it satisfies the stronger bound \(|\kappa_{\mathbf J}|<11/16\). The supremum over every integer target and every such scheme is exactly one. Every feasible pair with distinct tree base points necessarily has \(\theta=1/2\); its full lattice half-displacement is primitive. Pairs within one tree column may use any admissible interpolation parameter.

Finally, put \(\mathcal S=C_{k,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R^d)\), and give \(h_{\mathcal S}\) the locked problem's halfspace-depth definition. There is one point \(p\in\mathcal S\), fixed before the ambient halfspace is chosen, with \(h_{\mathcal S}(p)\ge2^{-N-1}W\). In particular the sequence \(C_{k,k^3}\), with its dimensions and entrance counts rebuilt at each \(k\), simultaneously satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
M(w)=K(w)=\max_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}
&=\sup_{\mathbf J,\ \text{admissible schemes}}|\kappa_{\mathbf J}|=1,\\
\max_{\substack{\mathbf J:w_{\mathbf J}>0\\v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}}}
\Delta_{\mathbf J}(v)&\le\frac4{3k}\longrightarrow0,\\
2^{-N}W&\longrightarrow\infty,
&h_{\mathcal S}(p)&\ge\tfrac12\,2^{-N}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, for any \(T,\varepsilon>0\), some member has \(2^{-N}W>T\) and all these curvatures at most \(\varepsilon\), while both maxima and the kernel-mass supremum remain exactly one.

## Proof

Accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 proves that its specified Boolean-chain successors exist, that \(P_{m,Q}\) contains \([0,1]^n\), and that \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^n=A_{m,Q}\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^n\). It proves that level \(s\) has exactly \(2^n\eta^s\) points. It also gives the complete reconstruction of reflected pairs: at a center \(j\) of level \(s\), there is precisely one unordered distinct base pair for each upper endpoint level \(r\in\{s+1,\ldots,s+h_-(j)\}\); its lower endpoint has level \(s\), and there are no other pairs. We use \(h_-(j)\) with exactly this inverse-reconstruction meaning. In particular, there is at most one pair for each \(r>s\). These are all properties of the accepted construction itself, with the parameter substitutions in the statement.

The decay assumption gives \(\rho_t\le2^{-t}\). In particular \(b_8=\lfloor1/(64\rho_8)\rfloor\ge4\), so \(Q\ge4\). Therefore all the chosen tree parameters are positive integers, and every entrance height is defined. A level-\(s\) lattice point has coordinates equal to two precisely along an initial path of depths \(1,\ldots,s\). Its other coordinates are zero or one. Since \(a_s\) is nondecreasing, substituting into \(\phi\) gives \(\phi(z)=a_s\), including \(\phi(z)=0=a_0\) when \(s=0\). This proves the asserted exact lattice columns: an integer point of \(\widehat P\) has its tree component in \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^n=A_{m,Q}\), and the remaining inequalities are exactly \(a_s\le t\le k\).

The conditions for \(\widehat P\) are \(x\in P_{m,Q}\), \(t\ge0\), \(t\ge a_{|u|}(x_u-1)\) for every tree coordinate, and \(t\le k\). These are finitely many rational linear inequalities, because \(P_{m,Q}\) is the convex hull of integer points. They define a compact convex polytope. For \(x\in[0,1]^n\), one has \(\phi(x)=0\); hence \([0,1]^n\times[0,k]\subseteq\widehat P\), proving full dimension. Every integer point of this polytope has positive weight. Thus an integer midpoint of two positive indices is also positive, and \(S_{\mathbf J}=0\) when \(\mathbf J\notin\widehat A\).

Fix a positive center \(\mathbf J=(j,h)\) and let \(s\) be the tree level of \(j\). Decompose \(S_{\mathbf J}\) as \(S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{col}}+S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cross}}\), according as its two tree base points agree or differ. These are nonnegative scalar contributions, and the column contribution includes zero. In the column case both tree points equal \(j\). A displacement has integer height component \(u\) with \(|u|\le h\), and its minimum endpoint weight is \(\rho_{h+|u|}\) whenever both endpoints are present. Dropping entrance and upper-height constraints and using \(\rho_t\le2^{-t}\) therefore gives
\[
S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{col}}
\le2^{-h}\sum_{u=-h}^{h}2^{-|u|}
=3\cdot2^{-h}-2^{1-2h}.
\]
Terms whose heights exceed \(k\) can simply be omitted before applying this upper bound. At \(h=0,1,2,3\), the displayed upper bounds are respectively \(1,1,5/8,11/32\).

For a cross pair, the accepted reconstruction gives endpoint tree levels \(s<r\), with at most one unordered tree pair for each \(r\). Orient it by putting its higher tree level at height \(h+u\). If \(a_r=\ell\), necessary height constraints are \(h+u\ge\ell\) and \(h-u\ge a_s\ge0\), so \(\ell-h\le u\le h\). There is no such pair if \(\ell>2h\). To express a bound without using nonexistent weights, put \(\bar\rho_t=\rho_t\) for integers \(0\le t\le k\), and \(\bar\rho_t=0\) otherwise, and define
\[
B_{h,\ell}:=\sum_{u=\ell-h}^{h}\bar\rho_{h+|u|}
\quad\text{when }\ell\le2h,
\qquad B_{h,\ell}:=0\quad\text{when }\ell>2h.
\]
This auxiliary \(B_{h,\ell}\) is a height sum, distinct from the curvature cutoff \(B_{\mathbf J}^{\Delta}(L)\). For \(\ell\le h\), the decay assumption bounds it by the two-sided geometric sum \(3\rho_h\). For \(h<\ell\le2h\), its indices have \(u>0\), so it is bounded by \(\sum_{t=\ell}^k\rho_t\le2\rho_\ell\). Thus
\[
B_{h,\ell}\le
\begin{cases}
3\rho_h,&\ell\le h,\\
2\rho_\ell,&h<\ell\le2h.
\end{cases}
\]
Each height assignment to an unordered tree pair gives one unordered pair of full fiber indices. In \(S_{\mathbf J}\), that pair contributes twice its minimum endpoint weight. Since there are exactly \(b_\ell\) tree levels with entrance height \(\ell\), the reconstruction bound yields
\[
S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cross}}
\le2\sum_{\ell=8}^{\min(k,2h)}b_\ell B_{h,\ell}.
\]
This is an upper bound at every center, including those for which inverse reconstruction stops early; no all-ones-center assumption is used.

For \(h<4\), this last sum is empty, so the four column bounds already give \(S_{\mathbf J}\le1\). For \(h\ge4\), the portion with \(\ell\le h\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\sum_{\ell=8}^{\min(h,k)}b_\ell B_{h,\ell}
&\le\frac34\sum_{\ell=8}^{h}\frac{\rho_h}{\ell\rho_\ell}
\le\frac34\sum_{\ell=8}^{h}\frac{2^{\ell-h}}{\ell}\\
&\le\frac3{32}\sum_{\ell=8}^{h}2^{\ell-h}
\le\frac3{16}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sums are empty when \(h<8\). The other portion satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\sum_{\ell=\max(8,h+1)}^{\min(k,2h)}b_\ell B_{h,\ell}
&\le4\sum_{\ell=\max(8,h+1)}^{\min(k,2h)}b_\ell\rho_\ell\\
&\le\frac12\sum_{\ell=h+1}^{2h}\frac1\ell
\le\frac{\log2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows by integrating the decreasing function \(1/x\) over \([h,2h]\). Also \(S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{col}}\le3\cdot2^{-h}\le3/16\). Finally \(\log2<3/4\), since the strictly convex function \(1/x\) lies strictly below its chord between one and two, whose integral is \(3/4\). Consequently \(S_{\mathbf J}\le3/8+(\log2)/2<3/4\) whenever \(h\ge4\). At any Boolean base \(j\) and height zero, the zero displacement has weight one. Therefore \(M(w)=\max_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}=1\), including all integer centers.

The exact level count gives the stated identity for \(W/2^N\). For the chosen \(m\), the central-binomial estimate proved in accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 gives \(0<1-\eta<1/(Q+1)^2\). Bernoulli's inequality yields \(\eta^s\ge1-s(1-\eta)>1-Q/(Q+1)^2>3/4\) for every \(0\le s\le Q\). Grouping the levels by their entrance heights, keeping the first weight in each height tail, and using the floor inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac W{2^N}
&\ge\frac38\left(1+\sum_{\ell=8}^k b_\ell\rho_\ell\right)\\
&\ge\frac38\left(1+\frac{H_k-H_7}{8}-\sum_{\ell=8}^k\rho_\ell\right)\\
&\ge\frac38\left(\frac{127}{128}+\frac{H_k-H_7}{8}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sum_{\ell=8}^k\rho_\ell\le\sum_{\ell=8}^{\infty}2^{-\ell}=1/128\). Conversely each tail beginning at height \(\ell\) is at most \(2\rho_\ell\), and \(\eta^s\le1\). Hence \(W/2^N\le1+\sum_{\ell=8}^k b_\ell\rho_\ell\le1+(H_k-H_7)/8\). These estimates prove the general mass assertions and the divergence uniformly in the permitted profile.

For the log-affine specialization, the function \(f(x,t)=2^{-t}\) on \(\widehat P\), extended by zero outside, is finite and globally log-concave: on the convex support its logarithm is affine, and a zero endpoint makes the log-concavity inequality automatic. For positive reflected endpoints the heights add to \(2h\), so \(w_{\mathbf J+v}w_{\mathbf J-v}=2^{-2h}=w_{\mathbf J}^2\). Thus every curvature is zero. In the canonical normalization of 280b299bc1e87c14 and 997f7c3a0c4804e4, \(B_{\mathbf J}^{\Delta}(L)=S_{\mathbf J}\) for every \(L\ge0\), and maximizing at \(L=0\) gives \(K(w)=1\).

To obtain the precise asymptotic, remove the factors \(\eta^s\) from the exact mass identity and denote the resulting sum by \(V_k\). Finite geometric summation gives
\[
V_k
=1+\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell2^{-\ell}-(Q+1)2^{-k-1}.
\]
The floor error in the sum is at most \(\sum_{\ell=8}^k2^{-\ell}<1/128\), so that sum equals \((H_k-H_7)/8+O(1)\). Also \(Q\le(1/64)\sum_{\ell=8}^k2^\ell<2^{k-5}\), making the final truncation term uniformly bounded. Therefore \(V_k=H_k/8+O(1)\). Since \(Q\ge b_k=\lfloor2^k/(8k)\rfloor\to\infty\), the factors \(\eta^s\), for all \(0\le s\le Q\), lie between \(1-Q/(Q+1)^2\) and one and tend uniformly to one. It follows that \(W/2^N\sim V_k\sim H_k/8\). This argument concerns the log-affine table; the actual bodies below use different exact weights and their own rebuilt supports.

For the rational specialization, \(d\ge2k\) ensures \(1-t/d\ge1/2\) on \([0,k]\). The weights are positive rational numbers and obey
\[
\frac{\rho_{t+1}}{\rho_t}
=\left(1-\frac1{d-t}\right)^d
\le\left(1-\frac1d\right)^d
<e^{-1}<\frac12.
\]
For example, the strict exponential inequality follows from \(\log(1-x)<-x\) for \(0<x<1\), and \(e>2\). The general construction and bounds therefore apply after computing each \(b_\ell\) from these weights. All defining inequalities of \(C_{k,d}\) are rational linear inequalities. Moreover,
\[
[0,1]^n\times[0,k]\times[-1/4,1/4]^d\subseteq C_{k,d}.
\]
This inclusion proves full dimension, while boundedness and the closed affine inequalities prove compactness and convexity. Its positive integer fibers are exactly the cubes specified in the statement, so their volumes equal \((1-t/d)^d\) without an approximation. On the convex support, \(d\log(1-t/d)\) is concave; extension by zero thus gives a global log-concave function agreeing with every integer weight. The canonical scalar hypotheses and curvature definitions consequently apply.

For \(v=(v',u)\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf J}\), direct multiplication of the two endpoint volumes gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_{\mathbf J+v}w_{\mathbf J-v}}{w_{\mathbf J}^2}
&=\left(
\frac{(1-(h+u)/d)(1-(h-u)/d)}{(1-h/d)^2}
\right)^d\\
&=\left(1-\frac{u^2}{(d-h)^2}\right)^d.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two endpoint heights belong to \([0,k]\), so \(|u|\le\min(h,k-h)\le k/2\). Since \(d-h\ge d/2\), one has \(0\le u^2/(d-h)^2\le k^2/d^2\le1/4\). The elementary integral bound \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\le4x/3\) for \(0\le x\le1/4\) proves the stated uniform curvature estimate. At each positive center and every \(L\ge0\), \(B_{\mathbf J}^{\Delta}(L)/(1+L)\le S_{\mathbf J}\le1\). A Boolean base at height zero has weight one and zero-displacement curvature zero, giving \(K(w)\ge1\). Thus the actual tables have \(K(w)=1\), with no need to identify another maximizing cutoff. Taking \(d=k^3\) proves the uniform curvature limit while preserving all the mass bounds.

We next establish the assertion for arbitrary two-fiber schemes. All the measures here are nonnegative; their total mass is additive, even if their supports overlap. For every unordered endpoint pair, marginal domination immediately gives \(|\pi_{\{\mathbf I_1,\mathbf I_2\}}|\le\min\{w_{\mathbf I_1},w_{\mathbf I_2}\}\), by evaluating each marginal on its whole fiber. This uses no assumption about marginal density being constant and no assumption about the coupling being independent. A fixed ordered pair of distinct indices determines at most one interpolation parameter with target \(\mathbf J\), because any coordinate where the indices differ determines it. Thus aggregating all uses of the same unordered pair, after correcting their order, loses no permitted choices. There are only finitely many possible pairs. Convexity of \(C_{k,d}\) puts every pushforward in its target fiber. If \(\mathbf J\notin\widehat A\), convexity of \(\widehat P\) also rules out every such endpoint pair, and its central mass is zero.

Suppose now that \(\mathbf J=(j,h)\in\widehat A\). Write the two endpoint indices as \((z_1,t_1)\) and \((z_2,t_2)\). If \(z_1\ne z_2\), choose a differing ternary coordinate. A strict convex combination of two consecutive integers cannot be an integer. Therefore a feasible differing coordinate must have values zero and two, and its only integer strict interior value is one. This forces \(\theta=1/2\). All other coordinates must then be consistent with that midpoint. Hence these are exactly some of the unordered cross reflected pairs already counted. Their total kernel mass is at most \(S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cross}}/2\), because that scalar counts both orientations and the scheme counts each unordered pair once. The same zero-to-two coordinate gives a coordinate equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) in the full lattice half-displacement, proving primitivity.

If \(z_1=z_2\), the target forces both to equal \(j\). Distinct indices then mean distinct integer heights \(t_-<t_+\), and strict interpolation implies \(t_-<h<t_+\). At each fixed upper height \(t_+\), there are at most \(h\) possible lower nonnegative integer heights. Since the profile decreases, each such pair has coupling mass at most \(\rho_{t_+}\). Therefore the central component plus all same-column pairs have combined mass at most
\[
\rho_h+h\sum_{t=h+1}^{k}\rho_t
\le(h+1)\rho_h
\le(h+1)2^{-h}.
\]
This estimate allows the same endpoint fiber to be reused separately in different pairs. Consequently imposing shared capacity cannot invalidate the upper bound. For \(h=0,1,2,3\), the final expression is respectively \(1,1,3/4,1/2\). There are no cross reflected pairs at these heights, as already proved. Thus the full kernel mass is at most one for those centers. For \(h\ge4\), the expression decreases with \(h\) and is at most \(5/16\). The previously proved bound \(S_{\mathbf J}<3/4\) gives \(S_{\mathbf J}^{\mathrm{cross}}/2<3/8\). It follows that \(|\kappa_{\mathbf J}|<5/16+3/8=11/16\). At a Boolean base and height zero, choose the central component to be the entire fiber Lebesgue measure and use no pairs. Its mass is exactly one and it also respects shared capacity. This proves the claimed exact supremum over all targets and all schemes.

Finally apply the purely integer specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to the finite measure \(\nu=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I}\delta_{\mathbf I}\) on \(\mathbb R^N\). Its mixed-integer parameters are \(n=N,d=0\); its total mass is our \(W\), not our maximum atom \(M(w)\). It supplies one \(\mathbf J_0\in\mathbb Z^N\) such that every closed base halfspace containing \(\mathbf J_0\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(2^{-N}W\). This point belongs to \(\widehat P\): otherwise strict separation from that compact convex polytope would produce a closed halfspace through \(\mathbf J_0\) with zero \(\nu\)-mass, contradicting the positive lower bound. Since the integer support is exact, \(\mathbf J_0\in\widehat A\).

Every actual fiber is centrally symmetric about the same continuous point \(0\). Fix \(p=(\mathbf J_0,0)\in\mathcal S\) now, before choosing any ambient halfspace. A proper closed ambient halfspace through \(p\) has an inequality \(a^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J_0)+b^{\mathsf T}y\ge0\), with \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\). If \(a\ne0\), the base halfspace \(G=\{\mathbf I:a^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J_0)\ge0\}\) has weight at least \(2^{-N}W\). If \(a=0\), take \(G=\mathbb R^N\), which has weight \(W\). For every \(\mathbf I\in G\), the ambient halfspace contains the part of its fiber with \(b^{\mathsf T}y\ge0\). Central symmetry gives this part at least half its fiber volume; when \(b=0\), the entire fiber is included. Summing over those indices proves mixed mass at least \(\nu(G)/2\ge2^{-N-1}W\). Any proper closed halfspace containing \(p\) contains a parallel one through \(p\), so the same lower bound follows by monotonicity. The whole-space case is immediate. The single point \(p\) thus works for every containing halfspace.

The harmonic lower bound tends to infinity, so the ratios \(2^N K(w)/W\), \(2^N\max_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}/W\), and \(2^N\sup_{\mathbf J,\text{ schemes}}|\kappa_{\mathbf J}|/W\) all tend to zero along \(C_{k,k^3}\). A finite measure's halfspace depth at any point cannot exceed its total mass. Therefore these fixed-target kernels cannot themselves have depth bounded below by an absolute positive multiple of \(2^{-N}W\). This conclusion includes the subclass with globally shared endpoint capacities. It supplies no upper bound on the actual mixed-integer depth, which the preceding argument shows is large at one fixed point. For any prescribed \(T,\varepsilon>0\), choose \(k\) sufficiently large for the harmonic lower bound to exceed \(T\) and for \(4/(3k)\le\varepsilon\), then use the exact body \(C_{k,k^3}\).
