## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $D$ be a centered $d$-simplex with vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_d$ and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_\ell$. Take $0<\delta<t=1/(d+1)$ and distinct $i,j$. Set
$$
K_A=(1-\delta)v_i+\delta D,\qquad K_B=(1-\delta)v_j+\delta D,
\qquad K_M=(K_A+K_B)/2.
$$
Use integer bases $A=(0,0)$, $M=(1,0)$, $B=(2,0)$, and let $C$ be the convex hull of $\{A\}\times K_A$, $\{B\}\times K_B$, and $([0,2]\times[1,3])\times D$. The three named bottom fibers are actual, all sampled fibers have positive $d$-volume and lie in $D$, and the plateau $([0,2]\times[1,3])\times D$ has relative-interior integer base $(1,2)$.

Write $\eta$ for the sum of the actual fiber surface measures, with physical coordinates $(z,x)$ and optional marks given by the outer unit normal at regular boundary points. Let $\mu_A,\mu_M,\mu_B$ be its three bottom-fiber restrictions, of common total mass $s$, and put $\gamma=\mu_A+\mu_B$. The normal-preserving translated swap self-coupling of $\gamma$ has midpoint law $\rho_0=2\mu_M$. For arbitrary weights $\omega_\ell>0$ with $\sum_{\ell=0}^d\omega_\ell=1$, put $W(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^d\omega_\ell\mathbf1_{\{\lambda_\ell(x)\ge t\}}$.

Every self-coupling of $\gamma$ whose physical midpoint law $\rho$ is dominated by the physical surface measure $\eta$ satisfies
$$
\int W\,d\rho\le\tfrac34\int W\,d\rho_0
=\tfrac32 s(\omega_i+\omega_j).
$$
Equality is attained by the equally weighted mixture of the diagonal and translated swap. This mixture preserves normals, has midpoint domination also with the normal marks, and satisfies $\rho_0\preceq_{\rm cx}\rho$ on the physical coordinates.

## Proof

For $x\in K_A$, the coordinate $\lambda_i(x)$ is at least $1-\delta$ and all other coordinates are at most $\delta$. Thus precisely cap $i$ contains $K_A$ pointwise. The corresponding statement for $K_B$ uses cap $j$. At every point of $K_M$ both selected coordinates are at least $(1-\delta)/2>t$, because $d\ge2$ and $\delta<t\le1/3$. All other coordinates are at most $\delta$, so precisely caps $i,j$ contain $K_M$ pointwise.

All fibers of $C$ lie in $D$ by convexity. Its base projection is $[0,2]\times[0,3]$. A combination at second base coordinate zero uses only the two bottom generators, so the actual bottom sections are their Minkowski interpolations and in particular are $K_A,K_M,K_B$. These are translates of $\delta D$. At each base in $[0,2]\times[1,3]$ the full $D$ is a generator and is the actual fiber. This accounts for every sampled base, proves positivity and shows that $C$ is full-dimensional. Translation of a scaled simplex preserves its surface measure and normal marks. Accordingly the pairings between the endpoint fibers with the same point of $\delta D$ preserve all facet normals and average to the translated regular boundary at $M$. Sending each endpoint to its translated partner in the other endpoint yields the swap, with law $2\mu_M$ and load $2s(\omega_i+\omega_j)$.

For any coupling with the prescribed marginals, the two off-diagonal flows between the bottom endpoint bases have the same mass $r$. Indeed the row and column sums for either endpoint both equal $s$, and the diagonal flow is the same term in its row and column sums. Thus the physical midpoint law has mass $s-r$ at each endpoint and $2r$ at $M$. Every such output lies in the corresponding convex fiber $K_A,K_B$ or $K_M$. Domination by the actual surface capacity at $M$ requires $2r\le s$. The load is therefore
$$
(s-r)\omega_i+(s-r)\omega_j+2r(\omega_i+\omega_j)
=(s+r)(\omega_i+\omega_j)\le\tfrac32s(\omega_i+\omega_j).
$$
This argument needs only physical domination, which follows also from any marked domination.

The equal mixture of the diagonal and swap has midpoint law $\rho=\tfrac12\mu_A+\mu_M+\tfrac12\mu_B\le\eta$, including the normal marks, and $r=s/2$. For each common scaled-simplex boundary point the physical midpoint of the two translated endpoints is the corresponding point at $M$, including its base coordinate. Pointwise Jensen and integration against the common surface measure give $2\mu_M\preceq_{\rm cx}\mu_A+\mu_B$. Averaging with $2\mu_M$ gives $\rho_0\preceq_{\rm cx}\rho$. The two laws have equal mass.

For the hinge calculation in §3 of the source, write $x_A=(1-\delta)v_i+\delta\xi$, $x_B=(1-\delta)v_j+\delta\xi$ for $\xi\in D$ and $\varphi_i=(\lambda_i-t)_+$. The diagonal-minus-cross hinge total is $t-\delta\lambda_i(\xi)>0$ by the strict cap incidences, and the same holds for $j$. For general convex tests every self-coupling midpoint law is below its common marginal by Jensen, and a strictly convex test is strictly improved by replacing any off-diagonal mass in physical space with the diagonal law.
