## Statement

Let $P$ be the pentagon with consecutive vertices $A=(0,0)$, $B=(3,0)$, $C=(4,2)$, $D=(3,3)$, $E=(0,3)$, and $S=P\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Give each vertex weight one and each other point of $S$ weight $\varepsilon=1/1200$. The total weight is $M=501/100$, every point weight is strictly below $M/4$, and the only same-parity pair of hull vertices is $A,C$. For the fractional packing value $f_w$ of the comparison lemma and $m=(A+C)/2=(2,1)$, one has
$$
 \max_{z\in\mathbb Z^2} f_w(z)=f_w(m)=2+4\varepsilon=\frac{601}{300}.
$$
For every $0<\delta\le1$, the mass-preserving merge $w'=w-\delta e_A-\delta e_C+2\delta e_m$ satisfies
$$
 \max_{z\in\mathbb Z^2} f_{w'}(z)=f_{w'}(m)=\frac{601}{300}+\delta .
$$
At $\delta=1$ the equality persists when the zero-capacity endpoints are deleted from the source set.

## Proof

The defining inequalities are $x\ge0$, $0\le y\le3$, $y\ge2x-6$, and $x+y\le6$. The last two imply $x\le4$, with $y=2$ if $x=4$; for integer $0\le x\le3$ they allow every integer $0\le y\le3$. Thus $S=\{0,1,2,3\}^2\cup\{C\}$ has exactly seventeen sites, of which twelve are light. The five listed vertices are precisely the extreme sites and their parities in order are $00,10,00,11,01$. Also $M=5+12\varepsilon=501/100>4$, so all weights are below $M/4$.

At $m$, the six disjoint groups
$$
 \{A,C\},\quad\{B,E\},\quad\{m\},\quad\{(1,0),(3,2)\},\quad
 \{(1,1),(3,1)\},\quad\{(2,0),(2,2)\}
$$
have convex hulls containing $m$; in the second group $m=(2B+E)/3$, and the four other two-point groups use the ordinary midpoint. Give the groups weights $1,1,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\varepsilon$. Every closed halfspace containing a target meets each group with that target in its convex hull, so its mass upper bounds the packing value. Outside $P$ the packing value is zero. For every $z\in S$ the table gives the counts of heavy and light sites in the cap $(a,b)\cdot((x,y)-z)\ge0$, computed from the lattice census:

| $z$ | $(a,b)$ | heavy | light |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| $(0,0)$ | $(-1,-1)$ | 1 | 0 |
| $(0,1)$ | $(-2,-1)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(0,2)$ | $(-2,1)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(0,3)$ | $(-1,1)$ | 1 | 0 |
| $(1,0)$ | $(-1,-2)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(1,1)$ | $(-3,-2)$ | 1 | 4 |
| $(1,2)$ | $(-3,2)$ | 1 | 4 |
| $(1,3)$ | $(-1,2)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(2,0)$ | $(1,-2)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(2,1)$ | $(1,-3)$ | 2 | 4 |
| $(2,2)$ | $(2,3)$ | 2 | 4 |
| $(2,3)$ | $(1,3)$ | 1 | 1 |
| $(3,0)$ | $(1,-1)$ | 1 | 0 |
| $(3,1)$ | $(2,-3)$ | 1 | 2 |
| $(3,2)$ | $(2,1)$ | 2 | 1 |
| $(3,3)$ | $(1,2)$ | 1 | 0 |
| $(4,2)$ | $(1,0)$ | 1 | 0 |

Each mass is at most $2+4\varepsilon$, proving the original maximum. After the merge, use weights $1-\delta$ on $\{A,C\}$ and $\varepsilon+2\delta$ on $\{m\}$, leaving all other weights unchanged. This gives $2+4\varepsilon+\delta$ at $m$. The comparison lemma gives the upper bound at every lattice target. At $\delta=1$ any feasible positive-weight group already avoids the zero-capacity endpoints, and $m$ remains a positive-capacity site. Thus removing the endpoints preserves the exact maximum.
