## Statement

Let $K_z$ be positive-volume compact convex $d$-bodies, $d\ge1$, at finitely many bases, and let $H=\{a\cdot z+u\cdot x\ge c\}$. Use surface measure $dS$ summed over their boundaries and write $n_z$ for the regular outer continuous normal. For $u\ne0$, use the usual cap indicator $s$ on strict old boundary traces. On an inside supporting facet allocate body density $s=\alpha$ and section density $1-\alpha$, and on an outside supporting facet allocate body and section densities both $\alpha$, with Borel $0\le\alpha\le1$. Count proper sections in full and divide section areas by $\|u\|$ to define total coarea $T$. Lower-dimensional faces have zero section area. For $u=0$ use whole-fiber bits and $T=0$.

For a unit $e$ let $B(e)=\sum_z|\operatorname{proj}_{e^\perp}K_z|_{d-1}$. Define the allocated cap shadow $B_H(e)$ as the sum over bases of the ordinary projection volume of a positive-volume cap body, zero for an empty or lower-area trace, and $|e\cdot u|\int_F\alpha\,dS/\|u\|$ at an outside supporting facet $F$. A pure-base cap uses the whole-fiber shadow for each selected base. All areas in dimension zero are counting volumes. Then $2B_H(e)=\int s\,|e\cdot n_z|\,dS+|e\cdot u|T$. The complementary allocation uses body density $1-s$ and the same section measure, and $B_H(e)+B_{H^{\rm comp}}(e)=B(e)+|e\cdot u|T$. Both allocated shadows are at most $B(e)$ and at least $|e\cdot u|T$.

## Proof

For a positive convex body $K$, the area formula for orthogonal projection on its rectifiable boundary integrates the Jacobian $|e\cdot n|$. Almost every point in its shadow has two boundary preimages, the endpoints of a nondegenerate line section. The boundary of the shadow has zero $(d-1)$-volume for $d\ge2$. For an interval the two preimages are its endpoints. This gives $\int_{\partial K}|e\cdot n|\,dS=2|\operatorname{proj}_{e^\perp}K|_{d-1}$.

A proper cap body has the captured old boundary and a new section of outer normal $-u/\|u\|$. The intersection of the old boundary with the cutting plane has zero surface area in the proper case. Apply the formula to the cap. An inside supporting trace is the whole body, and at its supporting facet the two densities $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$ restore the full contribution. An outside supporting trace has the same $\alpha$ in both terms and a facet normal parallel to $u$, so the two terms are exactly twice its selected projection. Strictly full and empty traces and lower-area touches give the asserted contributions directly. The pure-base bits use the same full-body formula.

Swapping the halfspaces exchanges the supporting statuses. Its densities are consistent with $1-s$, with the original section allocation retained, also using complementary bits at pure-base equality. Adding the two projection formulas proves the complementary identity. On a proper or inside trace the section shadow lies in the cap shadow, and its allocated projected area is at most the projected area of the section, by the hyperplane projection Jacobian $|e\cdot u|/\|u\|$. On an outside facet it equals the allocated shadow, which is at most the full-fiber shadow by $0\le\alpha\le1$ and inclusion of the facet in the fiber. The remaining traces have zero section contribution. Sum over the bases.
