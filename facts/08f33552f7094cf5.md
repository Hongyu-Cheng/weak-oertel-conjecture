## Statement

For each $d\ge2$ let $v_0=(-1,\ldots,-1)$ and $v_i=e_i$ for $1\le i\le d$, let $K$ be a $d$-simplex of volume $V$ and centroid $g$, and take $C=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}\times K$. Its mixed centroid $y=(0,g)$ is its unique deepest mixed point in the original measure. Put $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and $T_k(w)=y+2^{-k}(w-y)$. On the half lattice the full mixed measure of $T_1(C)$ is centered at $y$ and has through-$y$ cap fraction $(1+\Gamma_d)/(d+2)<\Gamma_d$. On the original lattice, for every sequence $a_k\ge0$ with $a_0>0$ and $A=\sum_ka_k2^{-kd}<\infty$, the measure $R=\sum_ka_k\mu_{T_k(C)}$ is centered at $y$ and has through-$y$ cap fraction
$$
\frac{\Gamma_d A+a_0}{A+(d+1)a_0}<\Gamma_d .
$$
The same caps witness both claims. For $d=2$ the half-lattice fraction is $13/36$.

## Proof

Apply 0143817849026887 with this exact product, continuous dimension $d$ and integer rank $d$. Its census and ordinary mixed-depth statement give the sampled bases $0,v_0,\ldots,v_d$, each of volume $V$, the claimed deepest point, and the caps
$$
H_j=\{\lambda_j(w)-1/(d+1)+\ell_j(z)\ge0\},\qquad
\ell_0(z)=-\sum_i z_i,\quad \ell_i(z)=(d+1)z_i-\sum_l z_l,
$$
where $\lambda_j$ are the simplex barycentrics. Each cap has mass $(1+\Gamma_d)V$, taking one outer fiber and a central simplex homothet of mass $\Gamma_d V$. The outer base vectors sum to zero and every fiber has centroid $g$, so the original measure is centered at $y$.

The inverse half-lattice base map at scale $1/2$ reindexes all original integer bases, and the continuous Jacobian is $2^{-d}$. Margins through $y$ scale by $1/2$, so the half-lattice image has the same fractions and centroid. For any $k\ge1$ none of the $v_i$ is divisible by $2^k$. The only actual integer fiber of $T_k(C)$ is then the homothet of $K$ at zero. It has volume $2^{-kd}V$, centroid $g$ and cap fraction $\Gamma_d$. Combining those fibers with the uncontracted term gives centered $R$ with total mass $V[A+(d+1)a_0]$ and cap mass $V[\Gamma_d A+a_0]$. Finiteness of the displayed total and the common compact support justify the centroid sum.

Bernoulli's inequality gives $(d/(d+1))^{d-1}\ge 2/(d+1)$, hence $\Gamma_d\ge 2d/(d+1)^2>1/(d+1)$ for $d\ge2$. This proves both strict inequalities and direct substitution gives the planar value.
