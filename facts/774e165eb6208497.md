## Statement

The universal assertion being disproved is the following.

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be a compact full-dimensional convex body,
and, for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\tag{1}
\]
All unqualified sums over \(z\) below are over \(\mathbb Z^2\).  Put
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z
\tag{2}
\]
and assume \(W>0\).  Assume the strict singleton inequalities
\[
w_z<\frac W4
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2)
\tag{3}
\]
and the strict primitive-line-coset inequalities
\[
\sum_{z\in(a+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\tag{4}
\]
for every coset \(a+L\) of every primitive rank-one sublattice
\(L\leq\mathbb Z^2\).

Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) satisfy
\[
w_{z_0}>0,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W4
\tag{6}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(z_0\), and
\[
z_0\in
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}
\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}.
\tag{7}
\]

There are four parity classes
\[
r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
For each such \(r\), define
\[
W_r:=
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}w_z.
\tag{8}
\]
Define the ordered off-diagonal same-parity mass by
\[
Q^\circ:=
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}W_r^2
-
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
Q^\circ
=
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}
\ \sum_{\substack{z,z'\in\mathbb Z^2\\
z\equiv z'\equiv r\pmod2\\z\neq z'}}
w_zw_{z'},
\tag{10}
\]
where both ordered pairs \((z,z')\) and \((z',z)\) occur.  Assume the exact
strict threshold
\[
Q^\circ>\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Define
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
R_0:=w_{z_0}^{1/d},
\qquad
S_0:=2(1-\gamma_d)^{1/d}R_0.
\tag{12}
\]
For every \(A,B\geq0\), define
\[
\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B)
:=
A^d+B^d
-
\max
\left\{
s^d+t^d:
0\leq s\leq A,\
0\leq t\leq B,\
s+t\leq S_0
\right\},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B)
:=
\min
\left\{
\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B),\
A^d,\
B^d
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The feasible set in (13) is nonempty and compact, so the displayed maximum
is attained.  Zero-volume fibers use the ordinary convention
\(0^{1/d}=0\).

To index every unordered nonzero symmetric pair exactly once, choose a set
\(\mathcal V\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\) satisfying
\[
\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}
=
\mathcal V\mathbin{\dot\cup}(-\mathcal V).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal V\) contains exactly one representative from each pair
\(\{v,-v\}\).  Define
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_d(w;z_0)
:=
\gamma_dw_{z_0}
+
\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}
\Psi_{d,S_0}
\left(
w_{z_0+v}^{1/d},
w_{z_0-v}^{1/d}
\right).
\tag{16}
\]
The summand is symmetric in its two arguments, so (16) is independent of
the choice of \(\mathcal V\).  Compactness of \(C\) implies that only
finitely many summands are nonzero.

The universal assertion claims that every \(d,C,z_0\) satisfying
(1) through (11) obeys
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_d(w;z_0)\geq\frac W{64}.
\tag{17}
\]

The following exact instance violates (17).  Take \(d=10\), write
\(z=(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(x\in\mathbb R^{10}\), and define
\[
C:=
\left\{
(z,x)\in\mathbb R^{12}:
\|z\|_\infty+\|x\|_2\leq21
\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Let
\[
\omega_{10}:=
\operatorname{vol}_{10}(B_2^{10})
=\frac{\pi^5}{120},
\qquad
z_0:=0.
\tag{19}
\]
Then the complete positive lattice-fiber profile is
\[
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}
=[-20,20]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\tag{20}
\]
\[
w_z
=
\omega_{10}(21-\|z\|_\infty)^{10}
\quad(z\in D),
\qquad
w_z=0
\quad(z\notin D).
\tag{21}
\]
Its total mass is
\[
W=\omega_{10}\overline W,
\qquad
\overline W
:=
21^{10}
+8\sum_{m=1}^{20}m(21-m)^{10}
=451480586453001.
\tag{22}
\]
This body satisfies every hypothesis (1) through (11), but
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)
=
\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}w_0
<
\frac W{64}.
\tag{23}
\]
Its actual mixed halfspace depth at \((0,0)\) is \(W/2\), so the
counterexample concerns only the universal profile inequality (17).

## Proof

The function
\[
(z,x)\longmapsto\|z\|_\infty+\|x\|_2
\]
is a norm on \(\mathbb R^{12}\).  Therefore (18) is compact, convex, and
has nonempty interior.  For every real base point \(z\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
C_z
=
\begin{cases}
(21-\|z\|_\infty)B_2^{10},
&\|z\|_\infty\leq21,\\
\varnothing,
&\|z\|_\infty>21.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
A radius-zero fiber has zero ten-dimensional volume.  Restricting (24) to
\(z\in\mathbb Z^2\) proves the complete profile (20) and (21), including
all zero-volume fibers.

For \(1\leq m\leq20\), the lattice shell
\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:\|z\|_\infty=m\}
\]
has exactly \(8m\) points.  The anchor shell has one point.  Summing (21)
over these complete shells proves (22).

We first verify the strict singleton hypothesis (3).  The largest fiber is
the anchor fiber,
\[
w_0=\omega_{10}21^{10},
\qquad
21^{10}=16679880978201.
\tag{25}
\]
The exact difference
\[
\overline W-4\cdot21^{10}
=384761062540197>0
\tag{26}
\]
gives
\[
w_z\leq w_0<\frac W4
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{27}
\]

We next verify (4) for every primitive rank-one coset.  If such a coset has
no lattice point, its mass is zero.  Otherwise, its lattice points can be
written
\[
a+kv,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,
\tag{28}
\]
where \(a\in\mathbb Z^2\) and
\(v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\) is a primitive generator.  Choose
\(i\in\{1,2\}\) with \(v_i\neq0\).  The coordinate values
\(a_i+kv_i\) are distinct integers.  For every positive-volume point in
(28), put \(s=a_i+kv_i\).  Then
\[
-20\leq s\leq20,
\qquad
\|a+kv\|_\infty\geq|s|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in(a+\mathbb Zv)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
&\leq
\omega_{10}
\sum_{s=-20}^{20}(21-|s|)^{10}\\
&=
\omega_{10}\overline L,
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
where
\[
\overline L
:=
21^{10}
+2\sum_{j=1}^{20}j^{10}
=65007024339901.
\tag{30}
\]
The exact difference
\[
\overline W-2\overline L
=321466537773199>0
\tag{31}
\]
proves
\[
\sum_{z\in(a+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\tag{32}
\]
for every coset of every primitive rank-one sublattice.

We now verify the three anchor requirements (5) through (7).  Equation (25)
gives \(w_0>0\).  The positive support has convex hull
\[
\operatorname{conv}D=[-20,20]^2,
\]
so
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}D.
\tag{33}
\]
Also \(w_z=w_{-z}\).  Let \(Q\subsetneq\mathbb R^2\) be any proper closed
halfspace containing \(0\).  Write
\[
Q=\{z:u^\top z\geq b\},
\qquad
u\neq0,\quad b\leq0,
\]
and put
\[
Q_0:=\{z:u^\top z\geq0\}\subseteq Q.
\]
Pairing each \(z\) with \(-z\) shows that \(Q_0\) contains at least one
member of every pair, and contains both members when they lie on its
boundary.  Hence
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
\geq
\sum_{z\in Q_0\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
\geq\frac W2>\frac W4.
\tag{34}
\]
This proves the closed-halfspace condition (6), with room beyond its
required value.

It remains to verify the strict off-diagonal threshold (11).  Write
\[
W_r=\omega_{10}\overline W_r
\qquad
\left(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\right).
\tag{35}
\]
On the shell \(\|z\|_\infty=m\), the four parity counts are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&00&01&10&11\\ \hline
m\ {\rm even}&4m&2m&2m&0\\
m\ {\rm odd}&0&2m&2m&4m.
\end{array}
\tag{36}
\]
The anchor has parity \(00\).  Therefore complete finite summation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline W_{00}
&=
21^{10}
+
\sum_{\substack{1\leq m\leq20\\m\ {\rm even}}}
4m(21-m)^{10}
=117456705062801,\\
\overline W_{01}
=\overline W_{10}
&=
\sum_{m=1}^{20}
2m(21-m)^{10}
=108700176368700,\\
\overline W_{11}
&=
\sum_{\substack{1\leq m\leq20\\m\ {\rm odd}}}
4m(21-m)^{10}
=116623528652800.
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
Their sum is \(\overline W\) from (22).  The complete diagonal square mass
is
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z^2
=\omega_{10}^2\overline D,
\]
\[
\overline D
:=
21^{20}
+8\sum_{m=1}^{20}m(21-m)^{20}
=2225091831194892164707306209.
\tag{38}
\]
Using the definition (9),
\[
Q^\circ=\omega_{10}^2\overline Q,
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\overline Q
&:=
\overline W_{00}^2
+2\overline W_{01}^2
+\overline W_{11}^2
-\overline D\\
&=
48803489853618364320273879392.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
The exact integer certificate
\[
256\overline Q-16\overline W^2
=9232337883423169904672002628336>0
\tag{40}
\]
implies
\[
Q^\circ>\frac1{16}W^2
>\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{41}
\]
This is precisely the strict hypothesis (11).

We finally evaluate every term in (16), using exactly the definitions
(12) through (16).  For \(d=10\), put
\[
\gamma:=\gamma_{10}
=\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10},
\qquad
q:=(1-\gamma)^{1/10}.
\tag{42}
\]
The exact integer inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
&11^{10}21^{10}
-10^{10}21^{10}
-20^{10}11^{10}\\
&\hspace{25mm}
=235117529492562122801>0
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
is equivalent to
\[
1-\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}
>
\left(\frac{20}{21}\right)^{10}.
\]
Therefore
\[
q>\frac{20}{21}.
\tag{44}
\]

At \(z_0=0\), definitions (12) give
\[
R_0=w_0^{1/10}
=21\omega_{10}^{1/10},
\qquad
S_0=2qR_0
=42q\omega_{10}^{1/10}.
\tag{45}
\]
Fix any \(v\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\), regardless of which
representative set \(\mathcal V\) contains \(v\) or \(-v\), and put
\[
m:=\|v\|_\infty.
\]
The two symmetric endpoint fibers have equal volumes.  If
\(1\leq m\leq20\), the two root volumes in (16) are
\[
A:=w_v^{1/10}
=\omega_{10}^{1/10}(21-m),
\qquad
B:=w_{-v}^{1/10}
=A,
\]
so
\[
A+B
\leq40\omega_{10}^{1/10}
<
42q\omega_{10}^{1/10}
=S_0
\tag{46}
\]
by (44).  Thus \((s,t)=(A,B)\) is feasible in the maximum in (13).
The coordinate bounds in (13) also give
\(s^{10}+t^{10}\leq A^{10}+B^{10}\) for every feasible \((s,t)\).
Hence the maximum in (13) is exactly \(A^{10}+B^{10}\), and
\[
\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=0,
\qquad
\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=0.
\tag{47}
\]
If \(m\geq21\), both endpoint volumes are zero, so \(A=B=0\) and (47)
again holds.  This proves (47) for every unordered nonzero symmetric pair,
including every zero-volume endpoint and independently of the transversal
\(\mathcal V\).

Every summand in (16) is therefore zero.  The complete score is
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)
=\gamma w_0
=
\omega_{10}
\frac{10^{10}21^{10}}{11^{10}}.
\tag{48}
\]
The final exact integer certificate is
\[
11^{10}\overline W
-64\cdot10^{10}21^{10}
=1035119843891335467677601>0.
\tag{49}
\]
Since \(W=\omega_{10}\overline W\) and \(\omega_{10}>0\), equation (49)
is exactly
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)<\frac W{64}.
\tag{50}
\]
This contradicts the universal conclusion (17) while every one of its
hypotheses has been checked.

For the promised calibration, put
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^{10})
\]
and, for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{12}\), define its
mixed mass by
\[
\mu(H)
:=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}
\operatorname{vol}_{10}
\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}.
\tag{51}
\]
The body and this mixed measure are centrally symmetric about \((0,0)\).
If a closed halfspace \(H\) contains \((0,0)\), moving its boundary toward
\((0,0)\) produces a boundary-through-origin halfspace
\(H_0\subseteq H\).  The union \(H_0\cup(-H_0)\) is all of
\(\mathbb R^{12}\), and symmetry gives
\[
\mu(H)\geq\mu(H_0)\geq\frac W2.
\tag{52}
\]
On the other hand, the closed halfspace
\[
\{(z,x):x_1\geq0\}
\]
bisects every positive-volume Euclidean-ball fiber.  Its boundary is
ten-dimensionally null in every such fiber, so its mixed mass is exactly
\(W/2\).  Therefore
\[
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{12}\ {\rm closed\ halfspace}\\
(0,0)\in H}}
\mu(H)
=\frac W2.
\tag{53}
\]
The counterexample refutes only the profile assertion (17), and its
`target_match` is `null`.
