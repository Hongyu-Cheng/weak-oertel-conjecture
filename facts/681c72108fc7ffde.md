## Statement

In the diverging setting of the fixed-projection representative estimate, suppose the summed fiber $d$-volume is positive at each stage. Extend $a_j$ affinely to the base ambient space. Along a subsequence with $(c_j-a_j)/R_j\to h$ and normalized fiber masses $p_{j,z}\to p_z$, the maps $(z,x)\mapsto(z,(x-a_j(z))/R_j)$ preserve normalized mixed depth at each finite stage. The transformed fibers converge in Hausdorff distance to $\{h(z)\}$, their hull converges to the hull of $\{(z,h(z)):z\in P\}$, and their pushed-forward normalized measures $\mu_j$ converge weakly to $\sum_zp_z\delta_{(z,h(z))}$. The hull of the graph has exactly those singleton integer fibers.

For closed halfspaces $H_j$ through $y_j\to(z_*,h(z_*))$ with inward unit normals $(u_j,v_j)\to(u,v)$, put $\ell(z)=u\cdot(z-z_*)+v\cdot(h(z)-h(z_*))$. Then
$$
\sum_{\ell(z)>0}p_z\ \le\ \liminf_j\mu_j(H_j)\ \le\ \limsup_j\mu_j(H_j)\ \le\ \sum_{\ell(z)\ge0}p_z.
$$
The zero set of $\ell$ in $P$ is lattice-complete in its own convex hull.

## Proof

The maps are invertible affine maps fixing the base and scaling every $d$-volume by $R_j^{-d}$, so normalized masses of corresponding closed halfspaces and depths of corresponding candidates are equal. The transformed fiber over $z$ has a representative $(c_j(z)-a_j(z))/R_j$ and diameter at most $D/R_j$. This gives simultaneous Hausdorff convergence, also for the convex hulls because taking a convex hull of finitely many unions is nonexpanding in Hausdorff distance. The representative estimate gives anchored affinity of each coordinate of $h$. An integer base in the graph hull belongs to $P$ by completeness, and every representation of that base then has graph coordinate $h(z)$. This identifies the exact integer fibers.

For a bounded continuous test function, on the transformed fiber over $z$ its deviation from its value at $(z,h(z))$ tends uniformly to zero by continuity there. Integrating and using $p_{j,z}\to p_z$ proves weak convergence. At a site with strict sign of $\ell(z)$ the cap margin on its transformed fiber eventually has that same strict sign throughout, by simultaneous convergence of points and normals. Sum the definite positive fibers for the lower bound, and exclude the definite negative fibers for the upper bound. At a site with zero margin the limit graph atom alone does not determine the fiber's selected fraction. Finally $\ell$ preserves anchored convex representations, since $h$ does and the remaining terms are affine. An integer point of the convex hull of the zero set lies in $P$ and is zero by such a representation.
