## Statement

For $0<e\le L$, put $A=[-L,L]\times[-e,e]$, $C=[-e,e]\times[-L,L]$, and $V=4Le$. In the endpoint-only source with these two unnormalized area layers at bases $0,1$, the maximum raw closed ambient halfspace depth over the entire real middle plane is $(1+e/L)V/2$, attained at $(1/2,0,0)$. The same maximum is attained at the middle origin when the bases are labeled $-1,1$ and only those layers are counted. For $L=1,e=1/4$, the maximum is $5/8<8/9=(4/9)(2V)$.

## Proof

The lifted hull at bases $0,1$ is full-dimensional and its integer fibers are exactly $A,C$, since an endpoint base cannot receive any positive hull weight from the other base. The common intersection area is $4e^2$. By pk-w35-reflected-symmetric-endpoint-intersection the depth at the middle origin is at least $(V+4e^2)/2$. The two closed caps
$$
x_2\ge e-2ez,\qquad x_2\le -e+2ez
$$
cover the entire real middle plane $z=1/2$. Each takes only an area-zero supporting edge from $A$. At base $1$ the vertical interval in the trace on $C$ has length $L+e$, even at $e=L$, and the transverse interval has length $2e$. Both caps therefore have mass $2e(L+e)=(1+e/L)V/2$. They give the matching upper bound at every middle-plane point. For $L=1,e=1/4$, the endpoint mass is $V=1$, giving the numerical comparison.

The invertible base change $z\mapsto2z-1$ carries the midpoint plane and closed cap incidences to those for endpoints $-1,1$, preserving each continuous area. For arbitrary centrally symmetric endpoints with separate centers, subtract from the continuous coordinates the affine interpolation of the two centers as a function of the base. This too preserves the two-layer measure and middle-plane depths, and puts both centers at zero. The predecessor then gives at least $V/2$ at the middle origin, and the crossed rectangles approach $V/2$ in ratio as $e/L$ decreases to zero, for any fixed $V$ by rescaling. At each actual endpoint of the consecutive-base hull, a finite tilt of any own-fiber cap isolates that cap by strict exposure and boundedness of the other rectangle. The reverse inequality comes from the own-fiber trace of every ambient cap. Thus the actual endpoint maximum is the ordinary symmetric depth maximum $V/2$.
