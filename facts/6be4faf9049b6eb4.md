## Statement

Let $\dim E>0$ and let $\sigma$ be the standard Gaussian for a Euclidean inner product on $E$. For nonempty sets $\Gamma_i\subseteq E^*$ put $T_i=\{v:g(v)\ge0\ \text{for every }g\in\Gamma_i\}$, $1\le i\le s$, and suppose they cover $E$. Then $\sum_i\sigma(T_i)=1+\int(\sum_i\mathbf1_{T_i}-1)\,d\sigma\ge1$, hence some $\sigma(T_i)\ge1/s$. If the labels are fixed deepest witnesses with $s\le k(I)\le2^n$, the bound is at least $1/k(I)\ge 2^{-n}$. Any menu containing a nonzero functional has $\sigma(T_i)\le1/2$. A one-cone cover forces its menu to be $\{0\}$. An inclusion-minimal two-cone cover consists of opposite closed halfspaces with Gaussian-null overlap, with the menus on opposite nonnegative rays. For every $s\ge2$ the abstract bound $1/s$ is sharp.

## Proof

Integrate the indicator sum, which is pointwise at least one. A nonzero $g\in\Gamma_i$ confines $T_i$ to $\{g\ge0\}$, a Gaussian halfspace of mass one half. This proves the one-cone claim. In an inclusion-minimal two-cone cover both cones are proper, so both menus have a nonzero covector. The union bound forces both masses to be one half and the overlap to have Gaussian measure zero. Each closed cone must then equal any halfspace from its nonzero menu: an omitted point in the interior of that halfspace would have an omitted open neighborhood of positive Gaussian measure, and closure gives the boundary. Two such halfspaces cover the space only if their inward normals are opposite positive multiples, since two non-opposite oriented linear inequalities admit a point strictly violating both. For each halfspace, a covector nonnegative there vanishes on its boundary subspace and is a nonnegative multiple of its inward covector. This proves the menu statement. In the plane, the $s$ consecutive closed sectors of angle $2\pi/s$ are each intersections of homogeneous halfspaces for $s\ge2$, cover the plane, and have Gaussian masses $1/s$ by rotational invariance.
