## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be nonempty and finite, and let $(K_z)_{z\in P}$ be positive compact convex $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$, satisfying every sampled anchored Minkowski inclusion. Let $C$ be the convex hull of their lifts and let $\eta$ be the sum of the original fiber boundary measures $\mathcal H^{d-1}$, marked by their almost everywhere unique outward unit normals. Write $h(z,u)=h_{C_z}(u)$ on the real projection of $C$. For any jointly continuous nonnegative field $\Psi(u,z,x)$, globally finite and convex in $(z,x)$ for every unit $u$, the roof cut
$$
C_\Psi(\varepsilon)=C\cap\{(z,x):u\cdot x+\varepsilon\Psi(u,z,x)\le h(z,u)\text{ for all unit }u\}
$$
is a nested compact convex path retaining the original projection and positive sampled fibers for small $\varepsilon\ge0$. One finite global-cut action has exactly the same sampled fibers as $C_\Psi(\varepsilon)$ for all $\varepsilon\ge0$, and its inward speed on the old measured boundary is $\Psi$.

The algebraic cone of finite global-cut speeds and the algebraic cone of roof speeds from finite sums $\sum_\ell\psi_\ell(u)\Phi_\ell(z,x)$, with $\psi_\ell$ continuous nonnegative and $\Phi_\ell$ globally finite continuous nonnegative convex, have the same $L^1(\eta)$ closure. Here the cut fields of the first cone are defined on a common open convex neighborhood of $C$ as in 0883fc7315c71f70. The images of both cones under any finite list of bounded Borel boundary responses have the same closure and the same polar inequalities. For each contact action, the approximating single-potential roofs give decreasing total and fixed closed-cap first losses tending to those of the action. A strictly positive uniform margin over any family of boundary weights with a common supremum bound persists at some finite penalty. For a fixed finite cap list, choose that penalty before taking the small deformation parameter.

## Proof

For real $z$ in the projection, the support $h(z,u)$ is the maximum of $\sum_p\lambda_p h_{K_p}(u)$ over $\lambda_p\ge0$, $\sum_p\lambda_p=1$, $\sum_p\lambda_pp=z$. This follows by taking support maximizers on the generating fibers for an optimal weight vector. The upper boundary of the finite convex hull of the lifted points $(p,h_{K_p}(u))$ gives this maximum, so for fixed $u$ it is continuous piecewise affine and concave in $z$. In the affine-graph case it is affine. Otherwise its graph is the minimum of the finitely many upper facet planes on the projection. All sections are contained in a common continuous ball, so their support functions are uniformly Lipschitz in $u$, yielding joint continuity. The roof inequalities are closed and convex because $h(\cdot,u)$ is concave and $\Psi(u,\cdot)$ is convex. Nonnegativity gives nesting.

At each sampled $p$, the anchored inclusion gives $C_p=K_p$. Apply 155691fc3768c06f at every $p$ to obtain a continuous affine support majorant $\ell_p(u,z)$ touching at $p$. In particular $\min_p\ell_p(u,z)=h_{K_z}(u)$ at every sampled $z$ and every unit $u$. The finite global-cut action with planes $\ell_p$ and field $\Psi$ on each plane consequently imposes exactly the same inequalities as the roof on each sampled fiber, for every $\varepsilon\ge0$. There is at least one active plane for each old sampled normal and all active fields there agree. The speed and small-parameter positivity now follow from 84cd8a85417b0d7a. Convexity and retention of all sampled bases give the original projection. This argument identifies sampled fibers only.

For a given contact action let $v$ be its physical speed and $\Phi_L$ its normal-independent approximants from 0883fc7315c71f70. That theorem supplies $0\le\Phi_L\downarrow v$ at regular old measured boundary points and convergence in $L^1(\eta)$. For each finite $L$, the actual roof just established has speed $\Phi_L$. Thus its first losses are $\int\Phi_L\,d\eta$ and $\int\kappa_H\Phi_L\,d\eta$ by 84cd8a85417b0d7a, with $\kappa_H$ the nonnegative inward selector for fixed closed caps, including inside and outside supporting traces and pure-base caps. Both decrease to the respective contact-action losses. For an ordinary continuous physical weight, the same radial thin-layer argument multiplies the first loss by its old boundary value, since its values in the layer converge uniformly to those on the old boundary. For arbitrary bounded Borel weights on the marked boundary, the first-response estimate is $|\int w(\Phi_L-v)\,d\eta|\le\|w\|_\infty\|\Phi_L-v\|_1$. This gives the stated uniform first-order margin transfer. Fix the finite $L$ before taking the small-parameter expansions of its actual roof. The difference $L(H)-\beta D$ can have either sign of approximation error and exact equality is not asserted.

The contact speeds form an algebraic cone by 82392d83cf301465, and sums of roof fields directly give the roof cone. Each contact speed lies in the closure of the roof cone because it is the limit of single-potential roof speeds. Conversely the identical-field construction realizes each roof speed by a contact action. Apply the continuous linear map of any fixed finite list of boundary responses to both inclusions to get equality of response closures and hence of polar inequalities.
