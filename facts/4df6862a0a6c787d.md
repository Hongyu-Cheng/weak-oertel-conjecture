## Statement

Let \(r\ge0\) be an integer and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope satisfying \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). With the complete lattice support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\), including every boundary lattice point, define the unnormalized capacity

\[
B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}.
\]

Then \(B(P)\le4^r\). In dimension zero, \(B(\{\mathbf0\})=1\).

For every \(r\ge1\), let \(\Delta_r=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\}\), where \(\mathbf e_i\) are the standard basis vectors, and put \(P_\Delta=(r+1)\Delta_r-\mathbf1\), where \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\). These polytopes satisfy the hypotheses and

\[
B(P_\Delta)=\binom{2r+1}{r},
\qquad
\lim_{r\to\infty}B(P_\Delta)^{1/r}=4.
\]

Consequently, no positive base smaller than \(4\) can replace \(4\) uniformly over all dimensions and all such polytopes.

## Proof

For \(r=0\), the only polytope and its complete support are \(\{\mathbf0\}\), and the defining sum has one term equal to \(1\). Hence assume \(r\ge1\).

We use \(r,P,D,B(P)\) with exactly the meanings in accepted fact a2e6106c48c8e525. That fact proves, with natural logarithms and \(0\log0=0\), the entropy identity

\[
\log B(P)=
\max_{\substack{\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\ \sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0}}
H(\lambda),
\qquad
H(\lambda)=-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log\lambda_{\mathbf I}.
\]

It also proves that an optimizer \(\lambda^*\) is positive at every point of \(D\), and that rational full-support probability vectors with exactly zero mean converge to \(\lambda^*\). Their entropies converge to \(H(\lambda^*)=\log B(P)\). Thus it suffices to prove \(H(\lambda)\le r\log4\) for each rational full-support zero-mean probability vector \(\lambda\).

Fix such a \(\lambda\), and choose a positive integer \(N\) with \(N\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) integral for every \(\mathbf I\in D\). Let \(k\ge2\) range through positive multiples of \(N\). As in a2e6106c48c8e525, let \(\mathcal T_k(\lambda)\) be the set of all distinct words in \(D^k\) with exactly \(k\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) copies of each label \(\mathbf I\). Every prescribed multiplicity is positive, and each word has block sum \(\mathbf0\). Use the same objects

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_k&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_k(\lambda),\\
V_k&=\left\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_k)\in(\mathbb R^r)^k:
\sum_{a=1}^k\mathbf x_a=\mathbf0\right\},\\
L_k&=V_k\cap(\mathbb Z^r)^k.
\end{aligned}
\]

The construction in a2e6106c48c8e525 proves \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_k)=V_k\), \(\dim V_k=r(k-1)\), and \(\operatorname{relint}(Q_k)\cap L_k=\{\mathbf0\}\). Its lattice chart is the linear isomorphism

\[
\Phi_k:(\mathbb R^r)^{k-1}\longrightarrow V_k,
\qquad
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{k-1})\longmapsto
\left(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{k-1},-\sum_{a=1}^{k-1}\mathbf y_a\right).
\]

It maps \(\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)}\) bijectively onto the entire lattice \(L_k\), which has rank \(r(k-1)\). Define the finite set of distinct integer vectors \(A_k=\Phi_k^{-1}(\mathcal T_k(\lambda))\) and its hull \(K_k=\operatorname{conv}(A_k)=\Phi_k^{-1}(Q_k)\). The cited properties give \(\operatorname{aff}(A_k)=\mathbb R^{r(k-1)}\) and \(\operatorname{int}(K_k)\cap\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)}=\{\mathbf0\}\). This chart introduces no lattice-index or multiplicity factor.

We now check the arithmetic mean of \(A_k\) itself. Every block permutation maps the complete set of distinct words \(\mathcal T_k(\lambda)\) bijectively to itself. Its uniform average is therefore fixed by every block permutation, so all its blocks equal one vector \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r\). The average lies in \(V_k\), since every word lies there, and hence \(k\mathbf u=\mathbf0\). Thus the average of the distinct words is exactly \(\mathbf0\). Linearity and injectivity of \(\Phi_k\) imply \(\sum_{a\in A_k}a=\mathbf0\).

Apply accepted fact ac927038e59fbb6d with its dimension \(d=r(k-1)\ge1\) and its finite set \(A=A_k\). We have checked distinctness, the full affine hull, the arithmetic-mean equation, and the unique interior lattice point in the standard lattice. That theorem counts this finite set directly; it requires neither \(\mathbf0\in A_k\) nor \(A_k=K_k\cap\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)}\). Permuting the positions and dividing by permutations among equal labels gives the exact multinomial count, so

\[
\frac{k!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(k\lambda_{\mathbf I})!}
=\lvert\mathcal T_k(\lambda)\rvert
=\lvert A_k\rvert
\le\binom{2r(k-1)+1}{r(k-1)}
<4^{r(k-1)}.
\]

For this fixed \(\lambda\), the elementary factorial estimate \(\log(m!)=m\log m-m+O(\log(m+1))\), proved in a2e6106c48c8e525 by comparison with the integral of \(\log x\), gives

\[
\frac1k\log\frac{k!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(k\lambda_{\mathbf I})!}
=H(\lambda)+O\!\left(\frac{|D|\log(k+1)}{k}\right)
<\frac{r(k-1)}k\log4.
\]

Letting \(k\to\infty\) along the multiples of \(N\) gives \(H(\lambda)\le r\log4\). The resulting bound is independent of \(\lambda\). Letting rational full-support zero-mean laws tend to the optimizer \(\lambda^*\), as supplied by a2e6106c48c8e525, now yields \(\log B(P)\le r\log4\), and hence \(B(P)\le4^r\).

For sharpness, apply the simplex calibration in ac927038e59fbb6d with its \(d=r\). Its \(P_\Delta\) is exactly the polytope in our statement, and its \(A_\Delta\) is our complete support \(D_\Delta=P_\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^r\). That fact proves that \(P_\Delta\) is full dimensional with sole interior lattice point \(\mathbf0\), that \(|D_\Delta|=\binom{2r+1}{r}\), and that \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_\Delta}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). The inequality \(e^x\ge1+x\) therefore gives, for every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r\),

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_\Delta}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_\Delta}(1+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I)
=|D_\Delta|.
\]

Equality holds at \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\), proving the stated capacity formula. The central binomial coefficient is the largest among the \(2r+2\) coefficients in the expansion of \((1+1)^{2r+1}\). Combining their sum with the strict upper comparison proved in ac927038e59fbb6d gives

\[
\frac{4^r}{r+1}
=\frac{2^{2r+1}}{2r+2}
\le\binom{2r+1}{r}
<4^r.
\]

Taking \(r\)-th roots and using \(\log(r+1)/r\to0\) proves \(B(P_\Delta)^{1/r}\to4\). For any fixed \(0<\beta<4\), these roots exceed \(\beta\) in all sufficiently large dimensions, so \(B(P_\Delta)>\beta^r\). This establishes the claimed sharp exponential base.
