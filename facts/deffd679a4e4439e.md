## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge2\), put \(k=m+1\), \(T=m\), \(h=1+m/2\), and \(B_j=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^j\) for each positive integer \(j\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D^{(1)}_m
&=\lbrace t\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^m:
\text{at most one coordinate of }t\text{ equals }2\rbrace,\\
D&=B_k\cup\bigl(\lbrace2\rbrace\times D^{(1)}_m\bigr),
\qquad A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subset\mathbb R^{k+1},\\
w_x&=T^{x_1}=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),
\qquad \beta=(-\log T,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^k,\\
Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
\qquad p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
The symbol \(D^{(1)}_m\) denotes the specified one-step support, not the chain-domain set denoted \(D_m\) elsewhere in accepted fact \(032423921efad6c8\). Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) as an alias. Every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two, and \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\).

For \(z\in A\), let
$$
\tau_p(z)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
$$
Define \(\alpha\) as the original probability mass on the points of \(D\) with at most one coordinate equal to two, define \(S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4\), and define the ordered-pair sum
$$
L_B=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2\\(x+y)/2\in B_k}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}.
$$
With \(F=1+T+T^2h\), their exact values satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=2^mF,
&\alpha&=\frac{1+T+T^2}{F},\\
2^{k/3}L_B&=\frac{2^{4/3}hT^{1/3}}{F^{4/3}},
&8^kS_4&=\frac{8(1+T^4+T^8h)}{F^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular,
$$
\alpha+2^{k/3}L_B+8^kS_4\longrightarrow0
\qquad(m\longrightarrow\infty).
$$
Therefore no absolute \(c>0\) gives \(\alpha+2^{k/3}L_B+8^kS_4\ge c\) uniformly over the stated two-step class.

On the same family, let \(\lambda=T^2h/F\) be the original mass of the face \(x_1=2\). Then \(\lambda>1/2\), and
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\lambda^4}{512}\,8^{-m}
=\frac{\lambda^4}{8}\,8^{-(k+1)}
\ge\frac1{128}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
The only predecessor inputs are the initial arbitrary one-step convex-realization lemma of accepted fact \(032423921efad6c8\) and the original moment theorem of accepted fact \(50c34c5bfb703ba9\).

## Proof

First, \(D^{(1)}_m\) consists of \(2^m\) Boolean points and \(m2^{m-1}\) points with exactly one coordinate equal to two. Consequently \(|D^{(1)}_m|=2^mh\). Applying the initial convex-realization lemma of accepted fact \(032423921efad6c8\), with all permitted one-step extensions included, gives \(\operatorname{conv}(D^{(1)}_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m=D^{(1)}_m\).

To verify the full support, let \(x=(s,t)\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\), where \(s\) is its first coordinate. Since \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq[0,2]^k\), the point \(x\) is ternary. If some tail coordinate of \(t\) equals two, every generator with positive coefficient in a convex representation of \(x\) must have that coordinate equal to two: all generating coordinates are at most two. Every such generator belongs to \(\lbrace2\rbrace\times D^{(1)}_m\), so all those generators have first coordinate two, and \(s=2\). It follows that if \(s\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), then \(t\) is Boolean and \(x\in B_k\). If \(s=2\), the same maximum-coordinate argument applied to the first coordinate forces every positively weighted generator into \(\lbrace2\rbrace\times D^{(1)}_m\). Thus \(t\in\operatorname{conv}(D^{(1)}_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m=D^{(1)}_m\), and again \(x\in D\). This proves the desired equality; its reverse inclusion is immediate. The hull contains \([0,1]^k\), so it is full-dimensional. Every generator is Boolean or has first coordinate two and at most one further coordinate two, proving the asserted maximum of two.

There are \(2^m\) support points in each of the first-coordinate layers zero and one, of weights one and \(T\), respectively. The layer two has \(2^mh\) points, each of weight \(T^2\). Summing these weights gives \(Z=2^mF\). The support points with at most one coordinate two are precisely all points in the layers zero and one, together with the \(2^m\) points of \(\lbrace2\rbrace\times B_m\). Their total weight is \(2^m(1+T+T^2)\), proving the formula for \(\alpha\). Similarly,
$$
S_4=\frac{2^m(1+T^4+T^8h)}{(2^mF)^4}
=2^{-3m}\frac{1+T^4+T^8h}{F^4}.
$$
Since \(k=m+1\), multiplication by \(8^k\) proves the stated fourth-power formula.

We now classify every ordered distinct same-parity pair with a Boolean midpoint. Write its endpoints as \(x=(s,t)\) and \(y=(s',t')\). If their first coordinates are odd, both first coordinates are one and both tails are Boolean. Equal parity then forces \(t=t'\), giving identical endpoints, which are excluded. Otherwise \(s,s'\in\lbrace0,2\rbrace\). If both are zero, the tails are again Boolean and equal parity again gives identical endpoints. If both are two, their midpoint has first coordinate two and is not Boolean. The only remaining possibility is one endpoint in layer zero and one endpoint in layer two.

For each \(t\in D^{(1)}_m\), let \(b(t)\in B_m\) be obtained by replacing its possible coordinate two by zero. The unique layer-zero point of the same parity as \((2,t)\) is \((0,b(t))\). Their midpoint is \((1,(t+b(t))/2)\), which is Boolean because \(t\) has at most one coordinate two and its other coordinates are Boolean. Hence every layer-two point contributes exactly this one unordered pair, or two ordered pairs, and the preceding cases prove that no other pairs enter \(L_B\).

In every such ordered pair the smaller and larger original probabilities are \(1/Z\) and \(T^2/Z\), since \(T\ge2\). Each summand is therefore \(T^{1/3}Z^{-4/3}\). Counting all \(2\cdot2^mh\) ordered pairs gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_B
&=2\cdot2^mh\,T^{1/3}Z^{-4/3}
=2^{1-m/3}\frac{hT^{1/3}}{F^{4/3}},\\
2^{k/3}L_B
&=2^{4/3}\frac{hT^{1/3}}{F^{4/3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This uses the original normalized probabilities throughout.

With \(T=m\) and \(h=1+m/2\), one has \(h/m\to1/2\) and \(F/m^3\to1/2\). The exact formulas above give the stronger limits
$$
\begin{aligned}
m\alpha&\longrightarrow2,\\
m^{8/3}\,2^{k/3}L_B&\longrightarrow2^{5/3},\\
m^3\,8^kS_4&\longrightarrow64.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first limit, divide the numerator of \(m\alpha\) by \(m^3\). For the second, its expression after dividing by the indicated powers of \(m\) is \(2^{4/3}(h/m)/(F/m^3)^{4/3}\). For the third, it is \(8(m^{-9}+m^{-5}+h/m)/(F/m^3)^4\). Thus each of the three nonnegative trichotomy terms tends to zero, as claimed.

It remains to verify the actual moment lower bound independently of that failed scalar inequality. The original mass of layer two is \(\lambda=T^2h/F\). Because \(m\ge2\) and \(h\ge2\), one has \(m^2h\ge2m^2>m+1\). Therefore \(T^2h>1+T\) and \(\lambda>1/2\).

Let \(A_1=D^{(1)}_m\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subset\mathbb R^{m+1}\), and define the probability distribution \(q\) on \(A_1\) by \(q_{(t,0)}=1/(2^mh)\). The map \(\iota:\mathbb R^{m+1}\to\mathbb R^{m+2}\) given by \(\iota(t,r)=(2,t,r)\) sends \(A_1\) bijectively onto the layer-two subset of \(A\). Directly from the original probabilities,
$$
p_{\iota(t,0)}=\frac{T^2}{2^mF}
=\lambda q_{(t,0)}.
$$
Thus the original distribution conditioned on this face is exactly the pushforward of \(q\); there is no additional tilt or change of its conditional normalization. Define \(\tau_q\) using all containing closed affine halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\), and put \(M_3(q)=\sum_{z\in A_1}q_z\tau_q(z)^3\).

For completeness, the conditional depths are preserved exactly by \(\iota\), including ambient halfspaces with zero pullback normal. Indeed, fix \(z=(t,0)\in A_1\), and let \(H\) be an ambient containing halfspace written as
$$
H=\lbrace(s,u,r)\in\mathbb R^{m+2}:
a_s s+a_u\cdot u+a_r r\ge b\rbrace,
\qquad (a_s,a_u,a_r)\ne0.
$$
Its pullback is \(\lbrace(u,r):a_u\cdot u+a_r r\ge b-2a_s\rbrace\). If \((a_u,a_r)\ne0\), this is a closed affine halfspace containing \(z\), so its \(q\)-mass is at least \(\tau_q(z)\). If \((a_u,a_r)=0\), the containment \(\iota(z)\in H\) implies \(0\ge b-2a_s\), and the pullback is all of \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\), with mass one, also at least \(\tau_q(z)\). An empty pullback is impossible for a containing halfspace. Conversely, every proper closed affine halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\) extends to an ambient halfspace by choosing \(a_s=0\) and the same other coefficients and threshold. Consequently the infimum conditional face mass over ambient halfspaces containing \(\iota(z)\) equals \(\tau_q(z)\). Allowing the whole ambient space as a halfspace, if that convention is used, leaves this equality unchanged.

For every ambient \(H\) containing \(\iota(z)\), its full original mass is at least its mass in the layer-two face. The pullback comparison therefore yields \(\tau_p(\iota(z))\ge\lambda\tau_q(z)\). Keeping the face points in the full moment sum, and using the original point probabilities as well as this depth inequality, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{z\in A_1}p_{\iota(z)}\tau_p(\iota(z))^3\\
&\ge\sum_{z\in A_1}\lambda q_z
\bigl(\lambda\tau_q(z)\bigr)^3
=\lambda^4M_3(q).
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular, the factor is the fourth power of the face mass: one factor comes from the averaging measure and three from depth.

Finally, \(D^{(1)}_m=B_m\cup E\), where \(E\) consists of all ternary tail vectors with exactly one coordinate two. The distribution \(q\) is precisely the original affine distribution of accepted fact \(50c34c5bfb703ba9\) with its support parameter equal to \(m\), ambient dimension equal to \(m+1\), and tilt vector zero. All hypotheses of that fact are therefore met, so \(M_3(q)\ge(1/512)8^{-m}\). Substituting this bound and using \(k=m+1\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac{\lambda^4}{512}\,8^{-m}
=\frac{\lambda^4}{8}\,8^{-(k+1)}\\
&\ge\frac1{128}\,8^{-(k+1)},
\end{aligned}
$$
where the last inequality uses \(\lambda>1/2\). Thus the original moment remains uniformly bounded below at its stated ambient scale on this counterfamily. The result refutes the proposed sufficient scalar trichotomy alone and supplies no disproof of the full original moment statement or of the locked mixed-integer halfspace-depth problem.
