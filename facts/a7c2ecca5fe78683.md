## Statement

Let \(C_{n,d}\), \(S_{n,d}=C_{n,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(p=(\mathbf 0,\mathbf 0)\) be the family and its mixed centroid from accepted fact `44d417ab69032c1f`, for integers \(n,d\geq1\).  Let \(\nu\) be unnormalized mixed-volume measure on \(S_{n,d}\), let \(W:=\nu(S_{n,d})\), and write one continuous coordinate as \(t\).  Set
\[
H:=\{(z,t,u):t\geq0\},\qquad \ell(z,t,u):=-t,
\]
where the coordinate \(u\) is absent when \(d=1\).  Then \(H=\{\ell\leq\ell(p)\}\), so \(H\) is a halfspace through \(p\) with nonzero continuous normal.

At selected mass \(a=W\) and integral target base coordinate \(z_0=\mathbf0\), consider the moment-constrained purification problem
\[
\min_g\int_{S_{n,d}}\ell\,g\,d\nu
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
0\leq g\leq1,\quad
\int g\,d\nu=W,\quad
\int z g\,d\nu=\mathbf0.
\tag{P}
\]
Its unique feasible density is \(g=1\) almost everywhere.  It is represented by the linked adjusted-halfspace KKT cap
\[
A=C_{n,d}\cap
\{\ell+\langle\lambda,z-z_0\rangle\leq\eta\}
=C_{n,d}
\]
with the finite parameters \(\lambda=\mathbf0\) and \(\eta=2\).  The mixed centroid of \(A\) is \(p\), the positive-fiber base support of \(A\) has intrinsic affine rank \(r=n\), and
\[
\frac{\nu(A\cap H)}{\nu(A)}
=\frac1{3^{n/2}+1}
=o\!\left(\frac1{r+1}\right).
\]
Consequently there is no universal \(c_{\mathrm G}>0\) for which every such linked adjusted-halfspace cap satisfies
\[
\nu(A\cap H)\geq\frac{c_{\mathrm G}}{r+1}\nu(A).
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `44d417ab69032c1f` gives the exact fibers of \(C_{n,d}\).  Put
\[
T:=3^{n/2},\qquad N:=3^n=T^2,\qquad V:=2^{d-1}.
\]
The positive-mass base fibers are precisely the points of \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\).  The central fiber is
\[
[-1,T]\times[-1,1]^{d-1},
\]
and each noncentral fiber is
\[
[-1,0]\times[-1,1]^{d-1}.
\]
In particular \(t\geq-1\) throughout \(C_{n,d}\), and hence \(\ell=-t\leq1\) throughout the body.

First consider feasibility and uniqueness in \((P)\).  Since \(0\leq g\leq1\) and \(\nu(S_{n,d})=W\), the full-mass constraint gives
\[
0=W-\int g\,d\nu=\int(1-g)\,d\nu.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative, so \(g=1\) \(\nu\)-almost everywhere.  Conversely, \(g=1\) has mass \(W\), and its base moment is zero because accepted fact `44d417ab69032c1f` gives the mixed centroid \(p=(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\).  Thus \(g=1\) is feasible and is the unique feasible density, hence in particular the unique optimizer of \((P)\).

Now take \(\lambda=\mathbf0\) and \(\eta=2\).  These parameters are finite, and \(\ell\leq1<2\) on the whole body.  Therefore
\[
C_{n,d}\cap
\{\ell+\langle\lambda,z-z_0\rangle\leq\eta\}
=C_{n,d}.
\]
Its indicator is exactly the optimizer \(g=1\).  Equivalently, for the usual KKT pointwise rule associated with the objective \(\ell\), the adjusted score \(\ell+\langle\lambda,z-z_0\rangle-\eta\) is strictly negative everywhere, so the upper-capacity choice \(g=1\) is selected with no boundary ambiguity.  The cap is linked to the asserted halfspace with the same objective orientation because
\[
H=\{t\geq0\}=\{-t\leq0\}=\{\ell\leq\ell(p)\},
\]
and the continuous component of the normal of \(\ell\) is \(-e_t\neq0\).  Since \(A=C_{n,d}\), its mixed centroid is the integral-base point \(p\).

The positive-fiber base support of \(A\) is \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\).  It contains \(\mathbf0,e_1,\ldots,e_n\), so its affine hull is all of \(\mathbb R^n\).  Its intrinsic affine rank is therefore exactly \(r=n\).

Finally, accepted fact `44d417ab69032c1f` computes
\[
\nu(A)=W=V(N+T)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\nu(A\cap H)=VT.
\]
Only the central fiber contributes positive \(d\)-volume to \(H\); the intersections at \(t=0\) in all noncentral fibers have \(d\)-volume zero.  Since \(N=T^2\),
\[
\frac{\nu(A\cap H)}{\nu(A)}
=\frac{VT}{V(T^2+T)}
=\frac1{T+1}
=\frac1{3^{n/2}+1}.
\]
With \(r=n\),
\[
(r+1)\frac{\nu(A\cap H)}{\nu(A)}
=\frac{n+1}{3^{n/2}+1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Given any proposed universal \(c_{\mathrm G}>0\), choose \(n\) so large that the last quantity is smaller than \(c_{\mathrm G}\).  Then this linked KKT cap violates the proposed lower bound.  This argument concerns only a bound depending on rank alone.  In particular, the same accepted fact shows that the displayed ratio divided by \(2^{-n}\) tends to infinity, so this construction does not disprove the locked weak Oertel target.
