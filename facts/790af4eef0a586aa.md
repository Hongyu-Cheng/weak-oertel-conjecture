## Statement

Let \(r,s\ge1\) be integers and put \(n=r+s\). Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^s\) be an origin-symmetric compact convex body. Let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb Z^s\) be a nonempty finite set, write its sites as \(\mathbf I=(x,y)\), and assume

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\operatorname{conv}D\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
(x,y)\in D&\ \Longrightarrow\quad
x\in\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r,\quad y\in Q,\\
\{y:(\mathbf0_r,y)\in D\}&=Q\cap\mathbb Z^s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) and \(R=\operatorname{conv}\{x:\text{some }(x,y)\in D\}\). Let \(V:R\to\mathbb R\) be finite and convex, with \(V(\mathbf0_r)=0\), let \(F:\mathbb R^s\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, even, and convex, with \(F(\mathbf0_s)=0\), and let \(B\in\mathbb R^{s\times r}\) be arbitrary. Define the original potential and complete scalar table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(x,y)&=V(x)+F(y-Bx)\quad((x,y)\in P),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\phi(\mathbf I)}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
&w_{\mathbf I}&=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),
&W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Assume only \(\sum_{(x,y)\in D}xw_{(x,y)}=\mathbf0_r\). There is no assumption on \(\sum_{(x,y)\in D}yw_{(x,y)}\). All logarithms are natural, and \(\mathbf0=(\mathbf0_r,\mathbf0_s)\) denotes the integer target.

At this target use exactly the family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) from accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc: its members are nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), with their unique positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). \[
\begin{aligned}

m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathcal T}w_{\mathbf I}:
\mathcal T\subseteq D,\quad \mathcal T\cap E\ne\varnothing\quad
\text{for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\text{ with }G_E\le L\right\}

\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

This is the original integral hitting convention of 488f0a583886b5fc; the same value results from hitting all arbitrary finite positive barycentric supports at the indicated gap cutoff.

## Proof

First, symmetry and convexity give \(\mathbf0_s\in Q\), so central completeness in (1) gives \(\mathbf0\in D\). The projections of \(P\) lie in \(R\) and \(Q\), because both sets are convex and contain the corresponding projections of every site of \(D\). Thus the potential in (2) is defined on all of \(P\). The map \((x,y)\mapsto y-Bx\) is real linear, and composing it with the convex function \(F\) gives a convex function. Adding the convex function \(V\) of the first projection proves that \(\phi\) is finite and convex on \(P\). No arithmetic assumption on \(B\) is used.

Define \(f=e^{-\phi}\) on \(P\) and \(f=0\) off \(P\). When both endpoints lie in \(P\), convexity of \(P\) and \(\phi\) gives the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). When an endpoint is outside \(P\), its right-hand side is zero and the inequality follows from nonnegativity. This is a finite-valued global extension. Since \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), it agrees with the complete table, including every zero, rather than only its positive part. Also \(V(\mathbf0_r)=F(\mathbf0_s)=0\) gives \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Convexity at every finite barycentric representation gives the convex-admissibility of \(\psi\) and \(G_E\ge0\). All these arguments apply in the actual affine span of \(P\) and require no full rank.

The finite set \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) contains zero, so \(M\ge1\). Between consecutive distinct central costs the numerator defining \(M\) is constant while \(1+L\) increases; after the largest cost the numerator remains constant. Each cost is included at its own cutoff. This proves the stated finite attainment of \(M\). Apply accepted fact f0ff12aa81c7461c with the exact map stated above. Its hypotheses hold for the present \(Q\) and \(F\), and its real-shift conclusion is

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-F(y-v)}
\le22\,2^sM
\quad\text{for every }v\in\mathbb R^s.
\tag{13}
\]

This accepted conclusion includes lower-rank occupied supports and all cost boundaries.

We derive the comparison with the original total weight directly. On \(\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\) set
\(\rho_x=2^{-r-1-\sum_{i=1}^r x_i}\).
At \(-\mathbf1_r\) its value is \(1/2\). The total over the nonnegative orthant is
\(2^{-r-1}(\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j})^r=2^{-r-1}2^r=1/2\).
These two parts are disjoint, so \(\rho\) is a probability law. Define positive comparison numbers on the original finite \(D\) by

\[
\pi_{(x,y)}
=\frac{\rho_x e^{-F(y-Bx)}}{22\,2^sM}.
\tag{14}
\]

For each occupied first projection its second-block sites form a subset of \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), and (13) applies at the real shift \(v=Bx\). Extending only the nonnegative auxiliary sum therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<\sum_{(x,y)\in D}\pi_{(x,y)}
&\le
\sum_{x\in\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}
\rho_x\,
\frac{\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-F(y-Bx)}}{22\,2^sM}\\
&\le\sum_x\rho_x=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular \(\pi\) is a subprobability on \(D\). This operation does not extend the original table or change any original source capacity.

Let \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\), the original probability law on the complete \(D\). All its entries and the entries of \(\pi\) on \(D\) are positive, so the following finite sums are well defined. Jensen's inequality for \(\log\) gives
\(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(\pi_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\le
\log(\sum_D\pi_{\mathbf I})\le0\).
Substituting (2) and (14) cancels the whole sheared \(F\)-term:

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\log\frac{p_{(x,y)}}{\pi_{(x,y)}}\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
+(r+1)\log2+\log(22\,2^sM)
+(\log2)\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W+\log(44\,2^nM).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The last equality uses exactly the assumed first-block moment. At the same original law, convexity of \(V\) gives
\(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)\ge
V(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x)=V(\mathbf0_r)=0\).
It follows from (16) that \(W\le44\,2^nM\). These two uses of the first-block mean are the only stationarity arguments; no second-block moment appears.

At first block zero, (1)-(2) give the complete central table
\(w_{(\mathbf0_r,y)}=e^{-F(y)}\) for all \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\). The singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) and has gap zero, so every hitting set in (3) pays its full weight one. For each nonzero central \(y\), the pair
\(\{(\mathbf0_r,y),(\mathbf0_r,-y)\}\) is affinely independent and has target \(\mathbf0\) with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\). Evenness gives equal weights at its endpoints and gap \(F(y)\). Grouping \(y\) and \(-y\) into one unordered pair, these pairs are disjoint from one another and from the singleton. Hence every set hitting the full eligible family has original weight at least

\[
1+\frac12
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s,\ y\ne\mathbf0_s\\F(y)\le L}}e^{-F(y)}
=\frac{Z_F(\mathbf0;L)+1}{2}.
\tag{17}
\]

The factor \(1/2\) counts one endpoint payment per disjoint equal-weight pair. Each selected endpoint still costs its full original weight, not its barycentric share. A hitting set may also be forced to pay other original sources, which preserves this lower bound.

Choose a central cutoff \(L_\star\) attaining \(M\). From (17) and (16),

\[

\ge\frac{Z_F(\mathbf0;L_\star)}{2(1+L_\star)}
=\frac M2\ge\frac{W}{88\,2^n}.
\tag{18}
\]

Accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc applies to the globally extendable table already checked and gives the stated equivalence with arbitrary positive barycentric supports. It also gives attainment of the fixed-target logarithmic score. Directly, \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) is finite, its singleton has cost zero, and the hitting minimum is over finitely many sets. Replacing any cutoff by the largest support gap below it leaves the eligible family unchanged and weakly decreases the denominator. Thus the maximum can be chosen among the finitely many \(G_E\). This proves (4), including zero-cost ties and potentials that vanish on some or all central sites.

Suppose now that the conditional actual body \(C\) in (5) is given. Its positive support is exactly \(D\), its target fiber has volume \(\alpha>0\), and its total mixed volume is \(W_C=\alpha W\). Common scaling cancels in the normalized logarithmic potential:

Here \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), the scale \(\alpha\) cancels exactly, and the inequality is the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. We next verify the full support and rank details of the actual construction. The normalized values \(\psi\) are nonnegative and convex-admissible, and their minimum on \(D\) is zero. \[

\tag{21}
\]

The decomposition \(\xi+T\tau\), with \(\xi\in\operatorname{span}D\), is unique because the two linear subspaces are complementary.
