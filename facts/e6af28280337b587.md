## Statement

Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}\) be a ten-dimensional simplex whose
centroid is the origin, let
\[
V:=\operatorname{vol}_{10}(K)>0,
\]
and let \(p_K\) be its Minkowski functional,
\[
p_K(x):=\inf\{\lambda\geq0:x\in\lambda K\}.
\]
Define
\[
C_K:=
\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{10}:
\|z\|_\infty+p_K(x)\leq21
\right\},
\qquad z_0:=0.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), put
\[
(C_K)_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C_K\},
\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{10}((C_K)_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(w_z>0\), define
\[
M(z):=
\max_{x\in(C_K)_z}
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_{10}((C_K)_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(C_K\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body, and its complete
positive lattice-fiber profile is
\[
D=[-20,20]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
w_z=V(21-\|z\|_\infty)^{10}\quad(z\in D),
\tag{4}
\]
with \(w_z=0\) outside \(D\).  In particular,
\[
W=V\overline W,
\qquad
\overline W=451480586453001.
\tag{5}
\]

This body and anchor satisfy all of the hypotheses in the registered
subgoal `n2-low-energy-uniform-simplex`: every singleton has mass
strictly below \(W/4\), every coset of every primitive rank-one sublattice
has mass strictly below \(W/2\), every proper closed base halfspace
containing \(0\) has mass at least \(W/4\), the anchor lies in the interior
of the positive support hull, and
\[
Q^\circ>\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Their support-safe energy satisfies
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)
=
\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}w_0
<
\frac W{64}.
\tag{7}
\]

Every positive fiber has the exact maximal absolute depth
\[
M(z)
=
\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}w_z.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently the rank-zero simplex fails the target threshold:
\[
M(0)<\frac W{64}.
\tag{9}
\]
Nevertheless, for
\[
T:=
\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\},
\tag{10}
\]
one has
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}T,
\qquad
\kappa(T,0)=2,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M(v)\,\kappa(T,0)
=
2\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}20^{10}V
>
\frac W{64}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus this exact low-energy, sharp-fiber-depth calibration satisfies the
registered conclusion in rank two even though its energy and rank-zero
branches both fail.

## Proof

Because \(K\) is a compact convex body containing \(0\) in its interior,
its Minkowski functional is finite, continuous, positively homogeneous,
subadditive, and coercive.  Hence the function
\[
(z,x)\longmapsto\|z\|_\infty+p_K(x)
\]
is convex and coercive.  Its sublevel set (1) is compact and convex.
It has nonempty interior because both summands are continuous and vanish
at the origin.  Therefore \(C_K\) is a convex body in
\(\mathbb R^{12}\).

For every real \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), positive homogeneity of \(p_K\)
gives
\[
(C_K)_z=
\begin{cases}
(21-\|z\|_\infty)K,&\|z\|_\infty\leq21,\\
\varnothing,&\|z\|_\infty>21.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
The radius-zero fiber has zero ten-dimensional volume.  Restricting (13)
to \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\) proves (4).  The shell
\(\{z:\|z\|_\infty=m\}\) has \(8m\) lattice points for \(m\geq1\);
therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=
V\left(
21^{10}+8\sum_{m=1}^{20}m(21-m)^{10}
\right)\\
&=V\cdot451480586453001,
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (5).

The normalized scalar profile in (4) is exactly the profile in accepted
fact `774e165eb6208497`, multiplied by the positive constant
\(V/\omega_{10}\).  That accepted fact verifies, by exact integer
calculations, all of the following assertions for that profile:
\[
\max_z w_z<\frac W4,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in(a+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\tag{15}
\]
for every coset of every primitive rank-one sublattice,
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}D,
\tag{16}
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W2
\tag{17}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(0\), and the strict parity estimate (6).  The inequalities
(14), (15), and (17) are homogeneous of degree one in the profile;
(6) is homogeneous of degree two.  Multiplication by
\(V/\omega_{10}>0\) therefore preserves every one of them.  This proves
all registered residual hypotheses, with (17) stronger than the required
\(W/4\).

We next evaluate the support-safe energy without using any fiber-shape
property.  Put
\[
\gamma:=\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10},
\qquad
q:=(1-\gamma)^{1/10}.
\]
The exact integer inequality
\[
11^{10}21^{10}
-10^{10}21^{10}
-20^{10}11^{10}
=235117529492562122801>0
\tag{18}
\]
is equivalent to \(q>20/21\).  At the anchor,
\[
R_0=w_0^{1/10}=21V^{1/10},
\qquad
S_0=2qR_0>40V^{1/10}.
\tag{19}
\]
For every nonzero \(v\in\mathbb Z^2\) with
\(m:=\|v\|_\infty\leq20\), the two symmetric endpoint root volumes are
\[
A=B=(21-m)V^{1/10}\leq20V^{1/10}.
\]
Thus \(A+B<S_0\), so the feasible choice \((s,t)=(A,B)\) in the
definition of \(\Phi_{10,S_0}\) gives
\[
\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=0.
\tag{20}
\]
When \(m\geq21\), both endpoint volumes are zero, and (20) again holds.
Every nonanchor summand in the energy therefore vanishes, proving the
equality in (7).

The strict comparison in (7) is the exact integer certificate
\[
\begin{aligned}
&11^{10}\overline W
-64\cdot10^{10}21^{10}\\
&\hspace{18mm}
=1035119843891335467677601>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, after multiplication by the positive factor \(V/11^{10}\),
(21) is precisely
\[
W-64\gamma w_0>0.
\]

It remains to determine \(M(z)\).  We first do so for \(K\).  Let
\(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_{10}\) be the barycentric coordinate
functions of \(K\).  For every \(x\in K\), some coordinate obeys
\[
\lambda_i(x)\geq\frac1{11}.
\]
The proper closed halfspace
\[
J_i(x):=\{y:\lambda_i(y)\geq\lambda_i(x)\}
\]
contains \(x\).  Its intersection with \(K\) is a homothetic copy of
\(K\) with linear ratio \(1-\lambda_i(x)\), and hence
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{10}(K\cap J_i(x))
=
(1-\lambda_i(x))^{10}V
\leq
\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}V.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus the absolute depth of every point of \(K\) is at most
\(\gamma V\).

The centroid of \(K\) is \(0\).  Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace inequality
states that every closed halfspace containing the centroid of a
ten-dimensional convex body captures at least the fraction
\((10/11)^{10}\) of its volume.  It follows that the absolute depth of
\(0\) in \(K\) is at least \(\gamma V\).  Combining this with (22) shows
\[
\max_{x\in K}\delta_K(x)=\delta_K(0)=\gamma V.
\tag{23}
\]
The upper cap in (22) with \(x=0\) has exactly this volume, so no equality
case is hidden.

Invertible homotheties map proper closed halfspaces bijectively and
multiply ten-dimensional volumes by the tenth power of their scale.
Equations (13) and (23) therefore give
\[
M(z)
=
\gamma(21-\|z\|_\infty)^{10}V
=\gamma w_z
\]
for every positive fiber, proving (8).  At \(z=0\), (21) is exactly
\(M(0)<W/64\), proving (9).

We finally verify the rank-two certificate completely.  The triangle in
(10) is contained in \([-1,1]^2\).  Its three edge inequalities are
\[
x+y\leq1,\qquad
2x-y\geq-1,\qquad
x-2y\leq1.
\tag{24}
\]
Checking the nine points of \([-1,1]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2\) against (24)
gives
\[
T\cap\mathbb Z^2
=
\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1),(0,0)\}.
\tag{25}
\]
The three vertices sum to zero, so \(0\) is in the interior of \(T\).
For every nonzero linear functional \(\ell\), the three vertex values
cannot all be negative because their sum is zero.  Every proper closed
halfspace through \(0\) consequently contains \(0\) and at least one
vertex.  Hence its count in (25) is at least two.  Conversely, the closed
halfspace
\[
\{(x,y):2x-y\geq0\}
\]
contains exactly \(0\) and \((1,0)\) from (25).  Moving the boundary of an
arbitrary containing halfspace toward \(0\) can only decrease its lattice
count.  This proves \(\kappa(T,0)=2\), including the closed-boundary
convention.

Every vertex of \(T\) has \(\ell_\infty\)-norm one.  Equations (8) and
(11) therefore give the equality in (12).  The strict final comparison
has the exact certificate
\[
\begin{aligned}
&128\cdot10^{10}20^{10}
-11^{10}\overline W\\
&\hspace{18mm}
=1396956330060024532322399>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
After multiplication by \(V/11^{10}>0\), equation (26) is precisely
\[
64\left[
2\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}20^{10}V
\right]-W>0.
\]
This proves (12) and completes the calibration.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by
Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), no. 4,
1257--1261, Theorem 2 and the centroid statement in its proof,
pp. 1259--1260, DOI https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary
source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf.  Exact form
used: if \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(c_L\),
then every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(c_L\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(L\cap H)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(L).
\]
Applicability: \(K\) is a ten-dimensional convex body with centroid \(0\);
the upper bound (22) independently proves that this lower bound is the
exact maximal absolute depth for a simplex.
