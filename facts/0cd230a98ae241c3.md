## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer and let \(a:\mathbb Z^k\to[0,\infty)\) be nonzero and finitely supported. Put \(D_a=\lbrace x:a_x>0\rbrace\), \(M_a=\sum_xa_x\), \(a_{\max}=\max_xa_x\), and, for \(\omega>0\),
$$
A^a_\omega=\lbrace x:a_x\ge\omega\rbrace,\qquad
r^a_\omega(q)=|A^a_\omega\cap(2q-A^a_\omega)|.
$$
Assume every positive superlevel is integer-midpoint-closed: \(x,y\in A^a_\omega\) and \((x+y)/2\in\mathbb Z^k\) imply \((x+y)/2\in A^a_\omega\). Define
$$
E(a)=\sum_{q\in D_a}\max_{0<\omega\le a_q}
          \omega^4r^a_\omega(q)^3,\qquad
\chi(a)=\frac1{M_a}\sum_{x\in D_a}a_x\log\frac{a_{\max}}{a_x}.
\tag{1}
$$
Then the maxima are attained at positive attained table values, including all ties, and
$$
27\,8^kE(a)M_a^2
\ge\left(\sum_{x,y\in D_a}\min(a_x,a_y)^2\right)^3,
\qquad
E(a)\ge\frac{512}{19683(1+\chi(a))^3}\,8^{-k}M_a^4.
\tag{2}
$$

Now let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers, let \(C\) be a convex body in \(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), and suppose \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) is nonempty. Retain exactly the original-fiber objects of accepted facts `488f0a583886b5fc` and `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`:
$$
C_z=\lbrace y:(z,y)\in C\rbrace,\quad
w_z=\mathcal L^d(C_z),\quad D=\lbrace z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\rbrace,\quad
W=\sum_zw_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
$$
Empty or lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. The original mixed-volume measure is
$$
\mu_C(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \mathcal L^d\bigl(\lbrace y\in C_z:(z,y)\in A\rbrace\bigr)
$$
for ambient Borel sets \(A\). Define \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H)\), over closed ambient halfspaces, and \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)\).

If \(W>0\), use (1) with the exact map \(k=n\), \(a=w\), \(D_a=D\), \(M_a=W\), and write \(A_\omega,r_\omega\) for its levels and reflected counts. Every positive level of \(w\) is integer-midpoint-closed. With the exact logarithmic integral hitting score \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\) of `488f0a583886b5fc`, one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(w)&\le128W\bigl(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\bigr)^3,\\
E(w)&\ge\frac{512}{19683(1+\chi(w))^3}\,8^{-n}W^4,\\
h_{\max}(C)
&\ge\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}6
\ge\frac16\left(\frac{E(w)}{128W}\right)^{1/3}
\ge\frac{4^{1/3}}{162(1+\chi(w))}\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The point maximum is attained. There is one \(p^\star\in S\), selected before the halfspace, such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies
$$
\mu_C(H)\ge h_{\max}(C)
\ge\frac16\left(\frac{E(w)}{128W}\right)^{1/3}
\ge\frac{4^{1/3}}{162(1+\chi(w))}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{4}
$$
This includes vertical halfspaces and all fibers on their boundaries. No full affine rank of \(D\) is assumed. If an absolute \(c_E>0\) satisfies \(E(w)\ge c_E8^{-n}W^4\) for all these actual bodies with \(W>0\), then (4) proves the locked target with
$$
c_0=\min\left\lbrace\frac16(c_E/128)^{1/3},\frac1{2e}\right\rbrace\in(0,1/e).
\tag{5}
$$
The uniform energy premise in this last implication is not asserted.

If \(W=0\), set \(E(w)=0\). Then \(\mu_C\) is the zero measure and every \(p\) in the assumed nonempty \(S\) has depth zero. No quotient by \(W\) or value of \(\chi(w)\) is evaluated in that case.

## Proof

We first prove the finite-table assertions without using any predecessor. If \(q\in D_a\) and \(0<\omega\le a_q\), let \(v\) be the smallest attained positive table value at least \(\omega\). It exists and satisfies \(v\le a_q\). There is no attained positive value between \(\omega\) and \(v\), so \(A^a_v=A^a_\omega\) and \(r^a_v(q)=r^a_\omega(q)\). Therefore \(v^4r^a_v(q)^3\ge\omega^4r^a_\omega(q)^3\). Thus every maximum in (1) is the maximum of finitely many values. The weak level inequalities include ties.

Write \(N_a(\omega)=|A^a_\omega|\) for \(0<\omega\le a_{\max}\). Partition \(A^a_\omega\) into its \(2^k\) coordinatewise parity classes, and denote their sizes by \(N_c(\omega)\). An ordered pair \((x,y)\) has an integer midpoint exactly when its endpoints have the same parity. By midpoint closure this midpoint is in \(A^a_\omega\). The bijection
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto\bigl(q=(x+y)/2,x\bigr),\qquad
(q,x)\longmapsto(x,2q-x)
$$
therefore gives, followed by Cauchy-Schwarz on parity classes,
$$
\sum_{q\in A^a_\omega}r^a_\omega(q)
=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}N_c(\omega)^2
\ge2^{-k}N_a(\omega)^2.
\tag{6}
$$
Diagonal pairs \((q,q)\) are included. This argument works in the ambient lattice regardless of the affine rank of the support.

Define the auxiliary level integral and per-root envelope by
$$
J(a)=\int_0^{a_{\max}}\omega N_a(\omega)^2\,d\omega,
\qquad
U_q=\max_{0<\omega\le a_q}\omega r^a_\omega(q)^{3/4}.
$$
Thus \(\sum_qU_q^4=E(a)\). For \(q\in A^a_\omega\), the envelope gives \(r^a_\omega(q)\le\omega^{-4/3}U_q^{4/3}\). Multiply (6) by \(\omega\), use this bound, and integrate. All sums are finite and the integral of \(\omega^{-1/3}\) near zero is finite. Hölder with exponents \(3\) and \(3/2\) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
J(a)
&\le2^k\sum_{q\in D_a}U_q^{4/3}
                    \int_0^{a_q}\omega^{-1/3}\,d\omega\\
&=\frac32\,2^k\sum_{q\in D_a}U_q^{4/3}a_q^{2/3}\\
&\le\frac32\,2^kE(a)^{1/3}M_a^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
It follows that
$$
E(a)\ge\frac8{27}\,8^{-k}\frac{J(a)^3}{M_a^2}.
\tag{8}
$$
Each ordered pair \((x,y)\) contributes \(\int_0^{\min(a_x,a_y)}\omega\,d\omega=\min(a_x,a_y)^2/2\) to \(J(a)\). Substitution in (8) proves the first inequality of (2).

Normalize only the auxiliary table by \(u_x=a_x/a_{\max}\), and put \(M_u=M_a/a_{\max}\). Its levels are \(A^u_\nu=A^a_{a_{\max}\nu}\), so
$$
E(u)=a_{\max}^{-4}E(a),\qquad
J(u)=a_{\max}^{-2}J(a),\qquad\chi(u)=\chi(a).
\tag{9}
$$
All its positive superlevels retain midpoint closure, so (8) applies to \(u\). For \(t\ge0\) set \(h(t)=e^{-t}N_u(e^{-t})\). A site with \(u_x>0\) contributes \(e^{-t}\) exactly when \(t\ge\log(1/u_x)\). Integrating these finite contributions gives
$$
\int_0^\infty h(t)\,dt=M_u,\qquad
\int_0^\infty th(t)\,dt=M_u(1+\chi(a)),\qquad
\int_0^\infty h(t)^2\,dt=J(u).
\tag{10}
$$
For the second identity use \(\int_s^\infty te^{-t}\,dt=(s+1)e^{-s}\); for the last use \(\nu=e^{-t}\). Beyond the largest \(\log(1/u_x)\), \(h(t)=|D_a|e^{-t}\), so all integrals are finite.

Put \(\mu=1+\chi(a)\ge1\) and \(R=3\mu\). The factor \(1-t/R\) is nonpositive on \((R,\infty)\), whereas \(h\ge0\). Hence
$$
\int_0^R(1-t/R)h(t)\,dt
\ge\int_0^\infty(1-t/R)h(t)\,dt
=M_u-M_u\mu/R=\frac23M_u.
$$
Since \(\int_0^R(1-t/R)^2\,dt=R/3=\mu\), Cauchy-Schwarz implies \(J(u)\ge4M_u^2/(9\mu)\). Equation (8) for \(u\) now gives
$$
E(u)\ge\frac8{27}\,8^{-k}
             \frac{(4M_u^2/(9\mu))^3}{M_u^2}
=\frac{512}{19683\mu^3}\,8^{-k}M_u^4.
$$
Multiply by \(a_{\max}^4\) and use (9) to obtain the second inequality of (2) for the original table, with its original fourth-power total \(M_a^4\). The normalization was confined to this auxiliary calculation.

We turn to the actual body. Compactness of its lattice-coordinate projection leaves finitely many nonempty integer fibers. Thus \(D\) is finite and \(W\) is finite. Work first with \(W>0\), so \(D\) is nonempty. Nonempty fibers of zero volume remain in \(S\) but contribute zero to \(\mu_C\).

We identify the accepted objects used below. For \(q\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_q\) consist of the nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(q\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}(E)\); its unique positive coefficients \(\lambda_{E,z}\) have sum one and barycenter \(q\). These are exactly the families of `488f0a583886b5fc` and `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. Write \(v_z=w_z^{1/d}\) for the volume roots; \(v_z\) is the quantity called \(a_z\) in `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, and is unrelated to the auxiliary finite table \(a\) in the first part of this proof. The canonical costs are
$$
L_E=\log\frac{w_q}{(\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_{E,z}v_z)^d},
\qquad
G_E=\log w_q-\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_{E,z}\log w_z.
\tag{11}
$$
Here \(G_E\) is exactly the logarithmic gap of `488f0a583886b5fc`: with its potential \(\phi_z=\log(w_{\max}/w_z)\), one has \(G_E=\sum_z\lambda_{E,z}\phi_z-\phi_q\). We use \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) for the optimized score, and do not identify it with the maximum potential \(B\) in that predecessor.

For \(L\ge0\) define the logarithmic hitting mass and score explicitly by
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(q,L;w)
&=\min\left\lbrace\sum_{z\in F}w_z:F\subseteq D,
 F\cap E\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }E\in\mathcal E_q
 \text{ with }G_E\le L\right\rbrace,\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{q\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(q,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
These have exactly the full original source charges of `488f0a583886b5fc`. A source in \(F\) costs its entire \(w_z\), with no barycentric multiplier. The corresponding fractional convention, when used, charges every source occurring in a component of mass \(x_E\) the full \(x_E\); no fractional convention is substituted for (12). The root hitting mass \(m_d(q,L)\) is defined by the same minimum with \(L_E\le L\) in place of \(G_E\le L\), as in `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`.

Accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` gives \(0\le L_E\). Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean gives
$$
d\log\left(\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_{E,z}w_z^{1/d}\right)
\ge\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_{E,z}\log w_z,
\qquad0\le L_E\le G_E.
\tag{13}
$$
This also proves the pointwise cutoff comparison in `488f0a583886b5fc`: every logarithmic eligible support is root eligible at the same cutoff, so
$$
m_d(q,L)\ge m_{\log}(q,L;w),\qquad
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w).
\tag{14}
$$
The singleton \(\lbrace q\rbrace\) has both costs zero. It forces every hitting set in (12) to contain \(q\), and \(D\) itself is a hitting set. Consequently \(w_q\le m_{\log}(q,L;w)\le W\). The family changes only at the finitely many \(G_E\). Replacing any cutoff by the largest \(G_E\) not exceeding it leaves its hitting mass unchanged and weakly decreases \(1+L\). The singleton ensures this largest value exists. Hence the maximum in (12) is attained over finitely many roots and cutoff values.

Before using (2), we verify midpoint closure of the actual positive superlevels. Let \(x,y\in A_\omega\) for \(\omega>0\) and let \(q=(x+y)/2\) be integral. If \(x=y\) there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose \(t\in C_y\). Convexity gives \((C_x+t)/2\subseteq C_q\), so \(w_q\ge2^{-d}w_x>0\). This establishes \(q\in D\) before a positive-target cost is used. The distinct pair \(\lbrace x,y\rbrace\) is then in \(\mathcal E_q\) with coefficients \(1/2\). Nonnegativity of its root cost, supplied by `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, gives
$$
w_q\ge\left(\frac{w_x^{1/d}+w_y^{1/d}}2\right)^d
\ge\min(w_x,w_y)\ge\omega.
\tag{15}
$$
Thus \(q\in A_\omega\). Empty levels satisfy closure vacuously, and no affine-rank hypothesis was needed. The first part of this proof applies to \(a=w\) and gives the second line of (3), with its full original \(W^4\).

It remains to prove the energy consumer. Fix \(q\in D\) and one allowed threshold \(0<\omega\le w_q\), and put \(\ell=\log(w_q/\omega)\ge0\). The reflected level set \(A_\omega\cap(2q-A_\omega)\) is invariant under \(z\mapsto2q-z\). Its only fixed point is \(q\), which is present. It consists of this singleton and \((r_\omega(q)-1)/2\) mutually disjoint two-element pairs. Each pair \(\lbrace z,2q-z\rbrace\) lies in \(\mathcal E_q\) with coefficients \(1/2\), and its logarithmic cost satisfies
$$
G_{\lbrace z,2q-z\rbrace}=\log w_q-\tfrac12\log w_z
                          -\tfrac12\log w_{2q-z}
\le\log(w_q/\omega)=\ell.
\tag{16}
$$
For every \(L\ge\ell\), a hitting set in (12) must therefore contain \(q\) and at least one endpoint of each of these pairs. Each pair is disjoint from \(q\) and from the other pairs. Every selected endpoint is charged its full original weight at least \(\omega\), regardless of its barycentric coefficient \(1/2\). The required singleton is charged \(w_q\ge\omega\). Thus, for the entire cutoff tail including \(L=\ell\),
$$
m_{\log}(q,L;w)
\ge w_q+\frac{r_\omega(q)-1}{2}\omega
\ge\frac{\omega(r_\omega(q)+1)}2
\ge\frac{\omega r_\omega(q)}2.
\tag{17}
$$
Disjointness is used only at this one root and threshold. No capacities or hitting requirements are added across different roots or across thresholds at a root.

Consider the auxiliary integral
$$
I_{\log}=\sum_{q\in D}w_q\int_0^\infty
                   e^{-L}m_{\log}(q,L;w)^3\,dL.
\tag{18}
$$
The hitting mass is a bounded step function with finitely many changes, so each integral is measurable and finite. Its bound \(m_{\log}(q,L;w)\le(1+L)\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\log}
&\le W\bigl(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\bigr)^3
            \int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)^3\,dL\\
&=16W\bigl(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\bigr)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
Indeed integration by parts gives \(\int_0^\infty e^{-L}L^j\,dL=j!\) for \(j=0,1,2,3\), and the polynomial integral is \(1+3+6+6=16\).

At the fixed root and level above, (17) instead gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_q\int_0^\infty e^{-L}m_{\log}(q,L;w)^3\,dL
&\ge\frac{w_q\omega^3r_\omega(q)^3}{8}
                         \int_\ell^\infty e^{-L}\,dL\\
&=\frac{\omega^4r_\omega(q)^3}{8}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
The exact cancellation is \(w_qe^{-\ell}=\omega\); it uses the original raw root weight \(w_q\) in (18). Select one attaining energy threshold for each root, using the attained-max argument already proved, apply (20) once at that root, and sum. This proves \(I_{\log}\ge E(w)/8\). Combining with (19) proves the first line of (3). In particular the proof has not replaced a root's maximum by a sum of independently charged levels.

Accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` gives the attained actual-point bound \(h_{\max}(C)\ge\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)/6\). Together with (14) and the energy inequality this yields
$$
h_{\max}(C)\ge\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}6
\ge\frac16\left(\frac{E(w)}{128W}\right)^{1/3}.
$$
Inserting the already proved table bound gives
$$
\frac16\left(\frac{E(w)}{128W}\right)^{1/3}
\ge\frac16\left(\frac4{19683(1+\chi(w))^3}
                         8^{-n}W^3\right)^{1/3}
=\frac{4^{1/3}}{162(1+\chi(w))}\,2^{-n}W,
$$
because \(19683=27^3\). This establishes every inequality in (3).

The point attainment and closed-halfspace quantifiers here are exactly those of `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, with the same \(C,S\), original \(\mu_C\), and \(W\). Choose an attaining point \(p^\star\) once. Every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing it has \(\mu_C(H)\ge h_S(p^\star)=h_{\max}(C)\), so (4) follows with one point before all halfspaces. In particular the accepted transfer includes vertical normals and all equality fibers. Our hitting comparisons used weak cost cutoffs throughout, and never removed any boundary atom or invoked zero boundary measure for a vertical halfspace. Substitution of the stated uniform energy premise gives (5), with a constant independent of \(n,d\), and \(C\).

For completeness, if the support is a singleton, its reflected count is one at every allowed level, so \(E(a)=M_a^4\) and \(\chi(a)=0\). In the body calculation the same singleton is compulsory in every hitting set; it was never replaced by an independent extra stock. Arbitrary lower-rank supports were already covered by the parity count, the independent pairs, and the accepted root hitting transfer. Positive-level and logarithm calculations exclude zero weights, while nonempty zero-volume fibers remain part of \(S\) with measure zero.

Finally, if \(W=0\), nonnegativity of all fiber volumes implies that every fiber volume is zero. Thus \(\mu_C\) vanishes on every Borel set, all points of \(S\) have depth zero, and the stated convention \(E(w)=0\) gives precisely the zero-mass conclusion without a division or logarithm at zero.
