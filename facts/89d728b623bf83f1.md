## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Retain exactly the definitions of entropy capacity and original-law capacity second moment from accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374`: for a finite positive law \(p\) on \(D\subset\mathbb R^r\),
\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r}
 \sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},
\qquad
\mathcal B_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2.
\]
For every integer \(m\ge1\), define the new trapezoid support and original law
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_m^{\mathrm{trap}}
&=\bigl(\{0,1\}\times\{0\}\bigr)
 \cup\bigl(\{0,1,\ldots,4m-1\}\times\{1\}\bigr),\\
\boldsymbol\ell_m^{\mathrm{trap}}&=(0,-\log(2m)),
\qquad Z_m^{\mathrm{trap}}=4,\\
p_{m,(x,t)}^{\mathrm{trap}}
&=\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\ell_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\cdot(x,t))}{4}
=\begin{cases}
1/4,&t=0,\\
1/(8m),&t=1.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]
These are new supports and sources; no predecessor support or root law is being renamed. The support is finite, complete, and of affine rank \(r=2\), with difference lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\). The source is strictly positive and globally log-affine, and it satisfies the exact certificate \(4^2\mathcal B_2(p_m^{\mathrm{trap}})\le505/256<2\).

For \(\mathbf X\sim p_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\), the original score \(\boldsymbol\ell_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\cdot\mathbf X\) takes the two values \(0\) and \(-\log(2m)\), each with probability \(1/2\). Consequently the shortest real interval carrying at least \(3/4\) of the original mass has width exactly \(\log(2m)\), which tends to infinity. In particular no finite absolute band width exists under the stated capacity hypothesis. Its original self-varentropy also equals \((\log(2m))^2/4\).

## Proof

First verify the support exactly. Its convex hull is
\[
K_m^{\mathrm{trap}}=
 \{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^2:0\le t\le1,\quad
                  0\le x\le1+(4m-2)t\}.
\]
Indeed this trapezoid has vertices \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(4m-1,1)\), all in the stated support, and contains both stated rows. Every integer point of this convex hull has height zero or one. At these heights its possible integer first coordinates are respectively \(0,1\) and \(0,\ldots,4m-1\). Therefore \(K_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\cap\mathbb Z^2=D_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\), proving completeness. The differences \((1,0)-(0,0)\) and \((0,1)-(0,0)\) generate \(\mathbb Z^2\), proving both full rank and the saturated difference-lattice claim.

The two lower-row unnormalized weights equal one, and the \(4m\) upper-row weights equal \(1/(2m)\). Their total is \(2+4m/(2m)=4\). This verifies the partition function, positivity, the one global affine logarithm, and the displayed original probabilities. Each row has original mass exactly \(1/2\).

We next prove a uniform interval capacity estimate using precisely the capacity normalization of `bf1ff94487296374`. For an integer \(N\ge2\), let \(\sigma_N\) denote the uniform probability law on \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\subset\mathbb R\), so \(\sigma_N(j)=1/N\). Write
\[
F_N(a,j):=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{a(k-j)},
\qquad
\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(j)=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R}F_N(a,j).
\]
Taking \(a=0\) gives \(\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(j)\le1\). For \(j\ge1\), put \(q=j/(j+1)\) and evaluate the exponential sum at \(a=\log q\). Extending its nonnegative terms to an infinite geometric series gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(j)
&\le\frac{q^{-j}}N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}q^k
\le\frac{q^{-j}}{N(1-q)}\\
&=\frac{j+1}{N}\left(1+\frac1j\right)^j
\le\frac{e(j+1)}N.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(\log(1+u)\le u\) for \(u\ge0\), obtained by integrating \(1/(1+u)\le1\). For \(j=0\), letting \(a\to-\infty\) gives \(\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(0)=1/N\), so the same upper estimate holds. Reflection \(k\mapsto N-1-k\), with \(a\mapsto-a\), gives \(\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(j)=\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(N-1-j)\). We have therefore proved, without approximating any capacity infimum,
\[
\mathsf C_{\sigma_N}(j)
\le\min\left\{1,\frac{e(j+1)}N,\frac{e(N-j)}N\right\}.
\]
The rational estimate \(e<11/4\) follows directly from its series: the sum through degree three is \(8/3\), and the tail starting at degree four is at most \((1/24)\sum_{h\ge0}5^{-h}=5/96\). Thus \(e\le87/32<11/4\).

Now take \(N=4m\). For every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\cup\{3m,\ldots,4m-1\}\), the preceding estimate gives \(\mathsf C_{\sigma_{4m}}(j)\le e/4<11/16\). Exactly half the support points lie in these two outer quarters. On the remaining half, capacity is at most one. Hence the exact rational bound is
\[
\mathcal B_2(\sigma_{4m})
=\frac1{4m}\sum_{j=0}^{4m-1}\mathsf C_{\sigma_{4m}}(j)^2
\le\frac12\left(\frac{11}{16}\right)^2+\frac12
=\frac{377}{512}.
\]
For the two-point uniform law, \(F_2(a,0)=(1+e^a)/2\) and \(F_2(a,1)=(1+e^{-a})/2\). Taking the respective limits shows that both capacities equal \(1/2\). Consequently \(\mathcal B_2(\sigma_2)=1/4\).

It remains to transfer these interval estimates to the actual original trapezoid law; this uses an exact face decoupling, not a surrogate reflected mass. For a lower-row target \((j,0)\), its exponential sum with vector \((a,b)\) equals
\[
\frac12F_2(a,j)+\frac12e^bF_{4m}(a,j).
\]
It is at least \(\tfrac12\mathsf C_{\sigma_2}(j)\) for all \(a,b\). Conversely, for each fixed \(a\), sending \(b\to-\infty\) removes the upper-row term; taking the infimum over \(a\) then proves \(\mathsf C_{p_m^{\mathrm{trap}}}(j,0)=\tfrac12\mathsf C_{\sigma_2}(j)\). For an upper-row target \((j,1)\), the exponential sum equals
\[
\frac12e^{-b}F_2(a,j)+\frac12F_{4m}(a,j).
\]
The corresponding lower bound, followed by \(b\to+\infty\), gives \(\mathsf C_{p_m^{\mathrm{trap}}}(j,1)=\tfrac12\mathsf C_{\sigma_{4m}}(j)\). These limiting arguments are valid for the infimum in the accepted definition, including exposed-face targets.

Each row has root mass \(1/2\), and its capacities have one additional factor \(1/2\). Thus the original-law second moment has exactly the cubic row-mass factors
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_2(p_m^{\mathrm{trap}})
&=\frac18\bigl(\mathcal B_2(\sigma_2)+\mathcal B_2(\sigma_{4m})\bigr)\\
&\le\frac18\left(\frac14+\frac{377}{512}\right)
=\frac{505}{4096}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying by \(4^r=16\) gives \(4^2\mathcal B_2(p_m^{\mathrm{trap}})\le505/256<2\). This is an analytic rational certificate for every integer \(m\), not a floating-point capacity calculation.

Finally, the original score is zero on the lower row and \(-\log(2m)\) on the upper row, and each row has probability \(1/2\). An interval of length less than \(\log(2m)\) can contain at most one of these two scores, so carries at most \(1/2\) of the original mass. The interval \([-\log(2m),0]\) contains both and carries all the mass. The shortest \(3/4\)-mass band width is therefore exactly \(\log(2m)\to\infty\). The variance of two equally likely values separated by this width is its square divided by four; subtracting the constant \(\log Z_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\) to obtain \(\log p_m^{\mathrm{trap}}\) does not change that variance. This completes the exact refutation of the registered score-band assertion.
