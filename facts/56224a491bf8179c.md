## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body invariant under each reflection \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\), with continuous coordinates unchanged. Write \(C_I=\{\mathbf y:(I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(w_I=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_I)\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(W=\sum_Iw_I>0\), always using the original total mass \(W\). Define the horizontal lattice-count marginal and its continuous halfspace depth by

\[
N_C(\mathbf y)=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathbf1_{C_I}(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)=
\inf_{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\int_{\{\mathbf z:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y)\le0\}}
N_C(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z .
\]

Then \(\int N_C=W\), the support of \(N_C\) is contained in \(C_{\mathbf0}\), and every \(\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf0}\) satisfies \(h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y))\ge2^{-n}\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)\).

Nevertheless, for every integer \(d\ge1\) there is such a body with \(n=4d(d+1)^2\), and an explicitly specified point \(\mathbf y_\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\), for which

\[
\sup_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf0}}
\frac{\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)}{W}
\le \frac{2}{d+1},
\qquad
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y_\star))\ge2^{-n}W .
\]

In particular, no absolute positive constant \(\alpha\) guarantees a point with \(\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)\ge\alpha W\) for all bodies in this reflection-invariant class. This is a counterexample to the stated whole-marginal certificate and is not a disproof of the fixed-corner depth assertion.

## Proof

First consider an arbitrary body in the statement. Its boundedness leaves only finitely many nonempty lattice fibers, so all sums and integrals below are finite sums of measurable terms. Integrating the defining indicators gives \(\int N_C=\sum_Iw_I=W\).

At a fixed continuous coordinate \(\mathbf y\), the integer section is invariant under all \(2^n\) coordinate reflections. The action is free because an integer coordinate cannot equal \(1/2\). Each orbit therefore contains \(2^n\) points and has, for suitable nonnegative integers \(k_1,\ldots,k_n\), the form \(\prod_{i=1}^n\{-k_i,1+k_i\}\). The convex hull of this orbit is \(\prod_i[-k_i,1+k_i]\), which contains \(\mathbf0\). Convexity of the section shows that \(N_C(\mathbf y)>0\) implies \(\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf0}\). Since \(C_{\mathbf0}\) is closed, the asserted support containment follows as well.

Fix \(\mathbf y_\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\). Every closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y_\star)\) contains a halfspace of the form

\[
H_0=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_\star)\le0\},
\qquad
(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0).
\]

In each integer orbit, select \(x_i=-k_i\) when \(a_i\ge0\), and select \(x_i=1+k_i\) when \(a_i<0\). Every term \(a_ix_i\) is nonpositive. Consequently, whenever \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_\star)\le0\), at least one point from each orbit is in the horizontal trace of \(H_0\). The number of points in that trace is at least \(2^{-n}N_C(\mathbf y)\). If \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), integration gives

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge 2^{-n}
\int_{\{\mathbf y:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_\star)\le0\}}
N_C(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y
\ge2^{-n}\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y_\star).
\]

If \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), the same count holds at every \(\mathbf y\), giving the stronger lower bound \(2^{-n}W\). Taking the infimum over containing halfspaces proves the claimed orbit reduction. It counts one actual lattice point per orbit and preserves closed inequalities.

We now construct the family. Put \(r=d+1\), \(s=4dr\), \(n=rs\), and \(\varepsilon=1/(2r)\). Partition the integer-coordinate indices into disjoint sets \(E_0,\ldots,E_d\), each of size \(s\). In the continuous space, let

\[
T=\left\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d:
y_i\ge0\ (1\le i\le d),\ \sum_{i=1}^dy_i\le1\right\}.
\]

Its vertices are \(\mathbf v_0=\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf v_j=\mathbf e_j\) for \(1\le j\le d\). Its barycentric coordinates are \(\lambda_0(\mathbf y)=1-\sum_i y_i\) and \(\lambda_j(\mathbf y)=y_j\) for \(1\le j\le d\). For \(0\le j\le d\), set

\[
L_j=\{\mathbf y\in T:\lambda_j(\mathbf y)\ge1-\varepsilon\}
=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf v_j+\varepsilon T .
\]

All these caps are full-dimensional. Write \(|K|=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) for continuous bodies. Iterated integration gives \(|T|=1/d!\), and the displayed homothety gives \(|L_j|=\varepsilon^d|T|\). The caps are pairwise disjoint because \(\varepsilon<1/2\).

Define the integer-coordinate boxes \(B_{\mathrm{in}}=[0,1]^n\) and

\[
B_j=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:
-1\le x_i\le2\ \text{for }i\in E_j,\quad
0\le x_i\le1\ \text{for }i\notin E_j\}.
\]

Finally, set

\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(B_{\mathrm{in}}\times T)\ \cup\
\bigcup_{j=0}^d(B_j\times L_j)\right).
\]

This is compact and convex. It contains the full-dimensional product \(B_{\mathrm{in}}\times T\), so it is a convex body. Both intervals \([0,1]\) and \([-1,2]\) are preserved by \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\); hence every defining product and their convex hull have every required coordinate reflection.

The complete actual fiber census is

\[
C_I=
\begin{cases}
T,&I\in B_{\mathrm{in}}\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
L_j,&I\in(B_j\setminus B_{\mathrm{in}})\cap\mathbb Z^n
       \text{ for some }0\le j\le d,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

To verify it, all continuous coordinates in \(C\) belong to \(T\), and the inner product supplies \(T\) at every inner integer site. Every base coordinate in \(C\) belongs to \([-1,2]\). If an integer site is not inner, some coordinate equals \(-1\) or \(2\); let its index belong to \(E_j\). In a convex representation using the defining products, let \(\theta_j\) be the total weight assigned to \(B_j\times L_j\). Grouping terms is legitimate because each defining product is convex. A coordinate in \(E_j\) is at most \(1+\theta_j\) and at least \(-\theta_j\). Attaining \(2\) or \(-1\) therefore forces \(\theta_j=1\). The entire point then belongs to \(B_j\times L_j\). In particular, outer coordinates cannot occur in two different blocks. Conversely, this defining product supplies all of \(L_j\) at every integer site of \(B_j\). These observations prove every case of the census, including all outer faces and their intersections. They also prove \(C_{\mathbf0}=T\).

There are \(2^n\) inner sites. For each \(j\), the number of outer sites supported by its block is
\((4^s-2^s)2^{n-s}=2^n(2^s-1)\). These sets of outer sites are disjoint for distinct \(j\). Consequently the marginal and original total mass are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_C(\mathbf y)
&=2^n\left(\mathbf1_T(\mathbf y)
+(2^s-1)\sum_{j=0}^d\mathbf1_{L_j}(\mathbf y)\right),\\
W
&=2^n|T|\left(1+r(2^s-1)\varepsilon^d\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

For the remainder of the proof set \(A=(2^s-1)\varepsilon^d\); this is a dimensionless auxiliary quantity, not a replacement for \(W\). It is at least one. Indeed, \(2^r\ge2r\) for every integer \(r\ge2\), and hence \(2^s\ge(2r)^{4d}\ge1+(2r)^d\).

Fix any \(\mathbf y\in T\). At least one barycentric coordinate, say \(\lambda_j(\mathbf y)=t\), is at least \(1/r\). The closed continuous halfspace
\(G=\{\mathbf z:\lambda_j(\mathbf z)\ge t\}\) contains \(\mathbf y\) on its boundary and is one of the halfspaces allowed in \(\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)\). For \(k\ne j\), every point of \(L_k\) has \(\lambda_j\le\varepsilon<t\). Thus \(G\) misses every cap except possibly \(L_j\).

More precisely, the same simplex homothety used above gives the exact mass formula

\[
\int_G N_C(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z
=2^n|T|
\left((1-t)^d+(2^s-1)\min\{\varepsilon,1-t\}^d\right)
\le2^n|T|(1+A).
\]

This formula remains valid at \(t=1\), when the intersection has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume. All boundaries involved in these continuous cuts are contained in finite unions of affine hyperplanes and have zero \(d\)-dimensional volume. The indicator formula nonetheless includes the complete closed fibers on those boundaries.

Since \(W=2^n|T|(1+rA)\), we obtain, for every \(\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf0}=T\),
\(\mathcal D_C(\mathbf y)/W\le(1+A)/(1+rA)\le2/r\), using \(A\ge1\). As \(d\) increases, this uniform upper bound tends to zero. This proves the failure of any absolute positive whole-marginal depth certificate, with the quantifier over all possible continuous point choices.

It remains to verify that the same family has the asserted actual depth. This also identifies an explicit source of mass omitted by the uniform orbit certificate. Here \(L_0=\varepsilon T\). Set
\(K=[0,\varepsilon/d]^d\subset L_0\) and
\(\mathbf y_\star=(\varepsilon/(2d),\ldots,\varepsilon/(2d))\).
Let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) consist of the integer vectors whose coordinates in \(E_0\) independently belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and whose other coordinates are zero. Then \(D\subset B_0\), and so
\(\mathcal U=D\times K\subset S\).

The set \(\mathcal U\) is centrally symmetric about \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y_\star)\), under \((I,\mathbf y)\mapsto(-I,2\mathbf y_\star-\mathbf y)\), and has mixed-integer mass \(3^s(\varepsilon/d)^d\). This reflection preserves the mixed-integer measure. Any closed halfspace through its center and its reflected halfspace cover \(\mathcal U\) and have equal mass on \(\mathcal U\). Each therefore has at least half its total mass. A containing halfspace contains one through the center, so \(h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y_\star))\ge\frac12\,3^s(\varepsilon/d)^d\).

This argument includes halfspaces with zero continuous normal: positive mass on a boundary only strengthens the lower bound.

On the other hand, \(A\ge1\) and \(|T|=1/d!\) give
\(2^{-n}W\le(r+1)2^s\varepsilon^d/d!\). It follows that

\[
\frac{h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y_\star))}{2^{-n}W}
\ge
\frac{(3/2)^s d!}{2(r+1)d^d}
\ge1.
\]

For completeness, the last numerical inequality holds with our exact integer choice \(s=4dr\). The binomial inequality \(2^d\ge d+1=r\) implies
\((3/2)^{4r}>4^r\ge4r^2\ge2(r+1)d\).
Raise this to the power \(d\), use \(s=4dr\), and then use
\([2(r+1)]^d\ge2(r+1)\) and \(d!\ge1\).
This proves the claimed bound at the explicitly chosen point using only an actual centrally symmetric subset of the original mixed-integer set. The original total mass \(W\) has been retained in both comparisons.
