## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, and let $m\in\mathbb Z$, $x_0\in\operatorname{int}K_m$, with $K_z=C_z$. For $u\ne0$ let $Q_z=K_z\cap\{a(z-m)+u\cdot(x-x_0)=0\}$. Put $s_z=\mathcal H_{d-1}(Q_z)$ on positive $d$-volume integer fibers and zero elsewhere. Assume $\mathcal H_{d-1}(Q_z\cap\partial K_z)=0$ on those positive fibers and that the cap with this margin is stationary in the integer tilt $a$. Then each closed side $z\le m$ and $z\ge m$ carries more than $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ of the total section mass, also for the derivative weights $s_z/|u|$. The constant is the infimum, even among regular sections stationary in the full ambient normal.

## Proof

The derivative formula in 1269c554ca9a41b8 §2 applies to the finite positive $d$-volume fibers. The stated regularity gives zero measure for nonproper traces, and varying $a$ and the absolute offset by $1$ and $m$, respectively, gives $\sum_z(z-m)s_z=0$. The proper central trace gives $s_m>0$.

Write $e=u/|u|$ and choose an isometry $Q:\mathbb R^{d-1}\to u^\perp$. The affine parametrization $(z,\xi)\mapsto(z,x_0-a(z-m)e/|u|+Q\xi)$ identifies the section with a convex set and is isometric on each fixed base. Its positive-volume section fibers are an interval of real bases by Brunn–Minkowski, and the positive original $d$-volume fibers also form an interval by Brunn–Minkowski. Intersecting the two intervals and sampling at integers leaves consecutive positive indices and preserves the concavity of the $(d-1)$st roots of $s_z$. Normalizing the masses, their mean is exactly $m$. Apply the scalar theorem 4d24a4fbd205befd with exponent $d-1$. Multiplication by $1/|u|$ leaves both tail fractions unchanged.

For sharpness take the scalar sharpness profiles $s_j=(L-j)_+^{d-1}$ for $j\ge0$ with integral means $m$ from 4d24a4fbd205befd, and choose a $(d-1)$-body $T$ of volume one centered at zero. Let $C=\{(z,\xi,t):0\le z\le L,\ \xi\in(L-z)T,\ |t|\le1\}$. This is convex and full dimensional. The cut $t=0$ has proper regular sections on every positive integer fiber; the lifted section centroid is $(m,0,0)$ by the weighted mean and the centers of $T$. The same derivative formula gives full-normal stationarity. The section tails are exactly the scalar ones. A shear $t\mapsto t-c(z-m)$ with $c\ne0$ makes the cut oblique and preserves the measured fibers and the full-normal stationarity, since it is an invertible base-preserving affine map with unit fiber determinant. Reflection reverses the two sides.
