## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq 1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the locked notation
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,
\qquad h_S.
\]
Assume \(S\ne\varnothing\).
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
where an empty or lower-dimensional fiber has volume zero.  Let
\(A:=\{z:w_z>0\}\) and \(W:=\sum_z w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)\).
The set \(A\) is finite.  Fix \(q\in A\), and define the oriented reflected-pair set
\[
\mathcal P_q:=\{z\in A:2q-z\in A\}.
\]
Thus every noncentral unordered pair occurs twice, while the central pair \(z=q\) occurs once; pairs having a zero-volume endpoint are omitted.  For \(z\in\mathcal P_q\), write \(z^*:=2q-z\) and put
\[
m_z:=\min\{w_z,w_{z^*}\},
\qquad
V_z:=\left(\frac{w_z^{1/d}+w_{z^*}^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(q\) is the target index denoted \(\mathbf J\) in accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647` and the center denoted \(j\) in accepted facts `373e992eb02b82d6` and `c382ad0f37a86dd8`; it is not the unrelated parity-collision scalar denoted \(q\) in `c382ad0f37a86dd8`.

For every compact convex set \(K\subseteq C\), possibly with zero \(d\)-volume in some or all fibers, define
\[
v_K:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_q),
\qquad
L_K:=\mathcal H_d\bigl((C\setminus K)\cap\Lambda\bigr).
\]
Then the reflected-pair removal inequality is
\[
2L_K\geq
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(1-\frac{v_K}{V_z}\right)_+.
\tag{2}
\]
The orientation convention in \(\mathcal P_q\), including its single central term, is part of (2).

The fixed-fiber maximum
\[
D_q:=\max_{x\in C_q}h_S((q,x))
\tag{3}
\]
is attained.  Set \(r:=1-e^{-1}\).  For every integer \(k\geq1\),
\[
D_q\geq
\frac1{2k}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(1-\frac{r^k w_q}{V_z}\right)_+.
\tag{4}
\]

For every real \(\theta>0\), define
\[
E_\theta(q):=
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(\frac{\sqrt{w_zw_{z^*}}}{w_q}\right)^\theta.
\tag{5}
\]
This \(\theta\) is the free power exponent in (5), not the common-core mass fraction denoted \(\theta\) in accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`.
Then
\[
D_q\geq
\frac{1-(1-e^{-1})^\theta}{2^{\theta+2}}E_\theta(q).
\tag{6}
\]
At \(\theta=1\), the quantity \(E_1(q)\) is exactly the oriented, zero-inclusive discounted score \(E_q(w)\) of accepted facts `373e992eb02b82d6` and `c382ad0f37a86dd8`; in particular,
\[
D_q\geq\frac1{8e}E_q(w).
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, the following is a sufficient scalar bridge, not an assertion made here.  If there are absolute constants \(\theta>0\) and \(a_0>0\) such that every actual fiber-volume profile with \(W>0\) satisfies
\[
\max_{q\in A}E_\theta(q)\geq a_0 2^{-n}W,
\tag{8}
\]
then the locked weak Oertel statement holds with the absolute constant
\[
c_0:=\min\left\{
a_0\frac{1-(1-e^{-1})^\theta}{2^{\theta+2}},
\frac1{2e}
\right\}\in(0,1/e).
\tag{9}
\]
When \(W=0\), the locked conclusion remains automatic at any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\), without using (1)--(8).

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes \(A\) finite.  We first record the two root-volume comparisons that will be used.  For \(z\in\mathcal P_q\), accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`, specialized to the two endpoints with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), gives
\[
w_q^{1/d}\geq\frac{w_z^{1/d}+w_{z^*}^{1/d}}2,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad V_z\leq w_q.
\tag{10}
\]
For distinct endpoints this specialization is applied to the compact convex segment restriction parametrized by \(s\in[-1,1]\), with base \(q+s(z-q)\); the endpoint indices are \(-1,1\) and their integer midpoint is \(0\).  Positive endpoint fibers make that restriction a convex body in \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\).  For \(z=z^*=q\), (10) is equality.  Arithmetic-geometric mean applied to \(w_z^{1/d}\) and \(w_{z^*}^{1/d}\) also gives
\[
V_z\geq\sqrt{w_zw_{z^*}}.
\tag{11}
\]

We prove (2), including all zero-volume cases.  Write
\[
k_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z),
\qquad
\ell_z:=1-\frac{k_z}{w_z}\in[0,1]
\quad(z\in A).
\]
Since \(K\subseteq C\), fiberwise subtraction and finiteness of the support give
\[
L_K=\sum_{z\in A}(w_z-k_z)=\sum_{z\in A}w_z\ell_z.
\tag{12}
\]
Fix \(z\in\mathcal P_q\).  If both \(k_z\) and \(k_{z^*}\) are positive and \(z\ne q\), apply the same two-endpoint specialization of `793210f8a4be3647` to the segment restriction of \(K\).  It is again a convex body because its two endpoint fibers have positive \(d\)-volume.  This gives
\[
v_K^{1/d}\geq\frac{k_z^{1/d}+k_{z^*}^{1/d}}2.
\tag{13}
\]
If either endpoint fiber of \(K\) has zero volume, the next displayed inequality has right side zero and is immediate.  If \(z=z^*=q\), it holds with equality.  Thus in every case,
\[
v_K
\geq
\min\{1-\ell_z,1-\ell_{z^*}\}V_z
=\bigl(1-\max\{\ell_z,\ell_{z^*}\}\bigr)V_z.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that
\[
\ell_z+\ell_{z^*}
\geq\max\{\ell_z,\ell_{z^*}\}
\geq\left(1-\frac{v_K}{V_z}\right)_+.
\tag{15}
\]
Multiplying by \(m_z\), summing over the oriented set, using invariance under \(z\leftrightarrow z^*\), and then using \(m_z\leq w_z\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(1-\frac{v_K}{V_z}\right)_+
&\leq
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z(\ell_z+\ell_{z^*})\\
&=2\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z\ell_z\\
&\leq2\sum_{z\in A}w_z\ell_z
=2L_K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This also checks the double counting: a noncentral pair is charged in both orientations, and each endpoint loss receives coefficient \(2m_z\leq2w_z\); the single central summand is \(2w_q\ell_q\), exactly its allowed coefficient in \(2L_K\).  If \(v_K=0\), (14)--(16) remain valid and the right side of (2) is \(\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z\).  In particular, no division by a remainder-fiber volume has occurred.

We next justify the maximum in (3).  Let \(\mu(B):=\mathcal H_d(S\cap B)\), a finite Borel measure.  Any proper closed halfspace containing a point can be translated inward, without changing its normal, until its boundary passes through the point; the translated halfspace is a subset of the original one.  Hence depth may be computed using through-point halfspaces.  If \(x_i\to x\) in \(C_q\), choose a through-\((q,x)\) halfspace
\(H_0=\{y:\langle u,y-(q,x)\rangle\geq0\}\) with
\(\mu(H_0)<h_S((q,x))+\varepsilon\).  For \(\delta>0\), the enlarged halfspaces
\[
H_\delta:=\{y:\langle u,y-(q,x)\rangle\geq-\delta\}
\]
decrease to \(H_0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\), so
\(\mu(H_\delta)\downarrow\mu(H_0)\).  For each fixed \(\delta>0\), \(H_\delta\) contains \((q,x_i)\) for all sufficiently large \(i\).  Therefore
\[
\limsup_i h_S((q,x_i))\leq\mu(H_\delta),
\]
and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) and \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) show upper semicontinuity.  Compactness of \(C_q\) proves attainment in (3).

We need one elementary consequence of the reflected orientation.  Put
\[
M_q:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z>0.
\tag{17}
\]
For each noncentral pair, its two oriented contributions total \(2m_z\leq w_z+w_{z^*}\), while the central contribution is \(w_q\); hence \(M_q\leq W\).
Every closed base halfspace \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(q\) has base-weight mass at least \(M_q/2\).  Indeed, translate its boundary inward to \(q\).  For every reflected pair, at least one of \(z,z^*\) lies in the resulting halfspace.  Thus, with \(\mathbf1_B\) denoting the base indicator,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in A\cap B}w_z
&\geq\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z\mathbf1_B(z)\\
&=\frac12\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}
m_z\bigl(\mathbf1_B(z)+\mathbf1_B(z^*)\bigr)
\geq\frac{M_q}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The central term only strengthens the last inequality because both indicators then equal one.

Fix \(k\geq1\) and abbreviate
\[
A_k:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(1-\frac{r^kw_q}{V_z}\right)_+.
\tag{19}
\]
The central term is \(w_q(1-r^k)>0\), so \(A_k>0\).  Every factor in (19) is strictly smaller than one, hence \(A_k<M_q\).  Suppose contrary to (4) that
\(D_q<\delta:=A_k/(2k)\).

We construct \(k\) centroid cuts while measuring every removal against the original mixed measure.  Start with \(K_0=C\), and after \(i\) cuts write
\[
v_i:=\operatorname{vol}_d((K_i)_q),
\qquad
L_i:=\mathcal H_d((C\setminus K_i)\cap\Lambda).
\]
Here \(K_0\) is compact and convex, \(v_0=w_q>0\), and \(L_0=0\).  After every positive number \(i\) of constructed cuts, \(K_i\) is compact and convex and \(L_i<i\delta\).  Before cut \(i<k\), its target fiber has positive volume: this was just noted for \(i=0\), while for \(1\leq i<k\), equality \(v_i=0\) and (2) would give
\[
M_q\leq2L_i<2i\delta=\frac{i}{k}A_k<M_q,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \((K_i)_q\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body and has a centroid \(c_i\).

Since \(h_S((q,c_i))\leq D_q<\delta\), there is a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \((q,c_i)\) whose original mixed mass is less than \(\delta\).  Translate its boundary inward until it passes through \((q,c_i)\), preserving the mass bound.  Its continuous normal is nonzero: otherwise it would be a base halfspace containing \(q\), and (18) would give mass at least \(M_q/2>\delta\).  Call this through-point halfspace \(H_i^+\), let \(H_i^-\) be the opposite closed halfspace, and set
\[
K_{i+1}:=K_i\cap H_i^-.
\]
The newly removed set is contained in \(H_i^+\), so
\(L_{i+1}<L_i+\delta<(i+1)\delta\).  Because the continuous normal is nonzero, the common boundary has zero \(d\)-volume in the target fiber.  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, at full metronoid mass, gives Grünbaum's centroid bound in \((K_i)_q\):
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((K_i)_q\cap H_i^+)
\geq\gamma_d v_i,
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\geq e^{-1}.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore
\[
v_{i+1}\leq(1-\gamma_d)v_i\leq r v_i.
\tag{21}
\]
This proves both continuation of every cut and, after \(k\) cuts,
\[
v_k\leq r^kw_q,
\qquad L_k<k\delta=A_k/2.
\tag{22}
\]
Applying (2) to \(K_k\), and using that each positive-part summand decreases with its volume argument, now gives
\[
2L_k
\geq\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\left(1-\frac{v_k}{V_z}\right)_+
\geq A_k,
\]
contradicting (22).  This proves (4), including the boundary and vertical-halfspace cases.  The estimate \(\gamma_d\geq e^{-1}\) used above follows from
\(d\log(1+1/d)\leq1\).

It remains to average (4) with the exact constant.  By (10)--(11), for every \(z\in\mathcal P_q\),
\[
0<u_z:=\frac{\sqrt{w_zw_{z^*}}}{w_q}
\leq t_z:=\frac{V_z}{w_q}\leq1.
\tag{23}
\]
Fix \(\theta>0\), put \(a:=r^\theta\in(0,1)\), and use the probability weights
\[
p_k:=(1-a)a^{k-1}\quad(k\geq1),
\qquad
\sum_{k\geq1}kp_k=\frac1{1-a}.
\tag{24}
\]
Since \((1-r^k/t)_+\) is nondecreasing in \(t>0\), multiply the equivalent inequality \(2kD_q\geq\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z(1-r^k/t_z)_+\) from (4) by \(p_k\), sum over \(k\), and use \(t_z\geq u_z\) to obtain
\[
\frac{2D_q}{1-a}
\geq
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_z
\sum_{k\geq1}p_k\left(1-\frac{r^k}{u_z}\right)_+.
\tag{25}
\]
Let \(\lambda:=-\log r>0\).  For any \(u\in(0,1]\), set
\[
k_0:=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2/u)}{\lambda}\right\rceil.
\]
Then \(r^k\leq u/2\) for every \(k\geq k_0\), while
\(a^{k_0-1}\geq(u/2)^\theta\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\geq1}p_k\left(1-\frac{r^k}{u}\right)_+
&\geq\frac12\sum_{k\geq k_0}p_k
=\frac12a^{k_0-1}
\geq\frac{u^\theta}{2^{\theta+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Substitution into (25) gives
\[
D_q\geq
\frac{1-a}{2^{\theta+2}}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal P_q}m_zu_z^\theta,
\]
which is (6).

For the exact power-one comparison, use the bijection
\(v\mapsto z=q+v\) between the feasible oriented displacements at center \(q\) and \(\mathcal P_q\).  Under the definitions in accepted facts `373e992eb02b82d6` and `c382ad0f37a86dd8`,
\[
\alpha_q(v)=m_{q+v},
\qquad
\Delta_q(v)=\log\frac{w_q^2}{w_{q+v}w_{q-v}},
\qquad
e^{-\Delta_q(v)/2}=
\frac{\sqrt{w_{q+v}w_{q-v}}}{w_q}.
\tag{27}
\]
The zero displacement is the unique central term on both sides.  Thus (5) at \(\theta=1\) is exactly their \(E_q(w)\), with no raw-minimum or parity-product substitution.  Since \(1-r=e^{-1}\), (6) becomes (7).

Finally, assume the additional scalar statement (8).  Choose an attaining \(q\) from the finite positive support and an attaining \(x\in C_q\) in (3).  Equations (6) and (8) give
\[
h_S((q,x))=D_q
\geq
a_0\frac{1-(1-e^{-1})^\theta}{2^{\theta+2}} 2^{-n}W
\geq c_0 2^{-n}W.
\]
The definition (9) makes \(c_0\) absolute, positive, and strictly smaller than \(1/e\).  This proves the stated implication to the locked target, while leaving (8) explicitly unproved.
