## Statement

A finite log-concave lattice law here means a probability mass function \(p=(p_x)_{x\in\mathbb Z}\) with finite contiguous positive support and \(p_x^2\ge p_{x-1}p_{x+1}\) for every integer \(x\), with zero masses outside its support. Thus the hypothesis includes the absence of internal zeros, as in the primary localization source.

Let \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\) be that support, and assume \(\mathbb E_pX=j\in\mathbb Z\). There exist \(1\le L\le N-M+1\), positive weights \(\pi_\ell\) summing to one, and component laws \(\nu^{(\ell)}\), such that
\[
 p_x=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\pi_\ell\nu^{(\ell)}_x
 \quad(x\in\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \mathbb E_{\nu^{(\ell)}}X=j
 \quad(1\le\ell\le L).
\]
Each component is either \(\delta_j\), or, for integers \(M\le a_\ell<j<b_\ell\le N\) and a finite real parameter \(s_\ell\), has the form
\[
 \nu^{(\ell)}_x=
 \frac{e^{s_\ell x}}{\sum_{y=a_\ell}^{b_\ell}e^{s_\ell y}}
 \quad(a_\ell\le x\le b_\ell),
 \qquad \nu^{(\ell)}_x=0\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
These labeled components are distinguished from the mean-indexed rows \(\nu_j\) in accepted fact 075a3f88fa24d326. The exact map to that fact is: translate a component interval by \(x'=x-a_\ell\), take its interval cardinality \(m=b_\ell-a_\ell+1\), its support \(D=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), and its target mean \(j'=j-a_\ell\). Choose the predecessor's source tilt \(\theta=0\); its source law is then a uniform reference law, not the original \(p\). The translated component is precisely its row \(\nu_{j'}\). For \(\delta_j\), use the singleton interval \(a_\ell=b_\ell=j\). Retain its constant \(K_{\mathrm{row}}=20288\). Every component satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu^{(\ell)}}(X-j)^4
 \le K_{\mathrm{row}}
       \bigl(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu^{(\ell)}}X\bigr)^2.
\]

Consequently, for every \(k\ge1\) and every independently specified finite log-concave lattice laws \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) with integral means \(j_1,\ldots,j_k\), let \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k p_i\) and \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)=\mathbb E_P\mathbf X\). For all \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^k\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\) and all \(t\ge0\),
\[
 P\{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\ge-t\}
 \ge\frac1{4K_{\mathrm{row}}}=\frac1{81152}.
\]
In particular \(\mathbf j\) is a support point of the original product, and its actual closed-halfspace depth satisfies \(\tau_P(\mathbf j)\ge1/81152\). Here, retaining the actual-depth convention of 075a3f88fa24d326, \(\tau_P(\mathbf j)\) denotes the infimum of \(P(H)\) over proper closed halfspaces \(H\) containing \(\mathbf j\). The bound is independent of the number of factors, their supports, and their parameters; no law is replaced in this depth assertion.

The reusable mixture principle is the following. Let \(\kappa\ge3\), let \(k,r_1,\ldots,r_k\) be positive integers, and let each probability law \(R_i\) on \(\mathbb R^{r_i}\) be a finite mixture \(R_i=\sum_\ell\pi_{i,\ell}R_{i,\ell}\). Suppose every component has the same mean \(\mathbf j_i\) as \(R_i\), has finite fourth moments, and satisfies, for every \(\mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^{r_i}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{R_{i,\ell}}
   \bigl[(\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i))^4\bigr]
 \le\kappa\bigl(
   \operatorname{Var}_{R_{i,\ell}}[\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf X_i]
   \bigr)^2.
\]
Then every proper closed halfspace containing the concatenated mean \((\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_k)\) has mass at least \(1/(4\kappa)\) under the original product \(\bigotimes_iR_i\). Conditional on independently sampled component labels, every centered linear projection of this product has the same fourth-moment bound with constant \(\kappa\). The conclusion requires conditional common means; it makes no unconditional standardized fourth-moment assertion.

## Proof

We first establish the exact finite decomposition, using only the extreme-point implication of the primary theorem stated in EXTERNAL SOURCES.

Fix the original \(p\), its support endpoints \(M,N\), and its mean \(j\). Define \(\mathcal S_j\) to be the class of all log-concave probability laws \(r\) supported inside \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\), allowing smaller contiguous supports, such that \(\sum_x(x-j)r_x\ge0\). This is exactly the primary source's \(\mathcal P_h^\gamma\) for counting measure \(\gamma(\{x\})=1\) and \(h(x)=x-j\). In particular \(p\in\mathcal S_j\). All laws are represented by their \(N-M+1\) mass coordinates.

The class \(\mathcal S_j\) is compact. To verify its only nontrivial closure condition, suppose \(r^{(q)}\in\mathcal S_j\) converges coordinatewise to \(r\). Nonnegativity, total mass one, the mean inequality, and every local log-concavity inequality pass to the limit. If \(r_a,r_b>0\) with \(a<b\), those endpoint masses are positive for all sufficiently large \(q\). Contiguity then gives positivity throughout \(\{a,\ldots,b\}\). On this interval the local inequalities say that the consecutive differences of \(\log r^{(q)}_x\) are nonincreasing. Comparing their averages on \(\{a,\ldots,y\}\) and \(\{y,\ldots,b\}\) gives, for \(a<y<b\),
\[
 r^{(q)}_y\ge
 (r^{(q)}_a)^{(b-y)/(b-a)}
 (r^{(q)}_b)^{(y-a)/(b-a)}.
\]
Taking limits shows \(r_y>0\). Hence the limiting support is contiguous, so \(r\in\mathcal S_j\). Thus \(\mathcal S_j\) is closed in the finite probability simplex and therefore compact. This also covers limits in which either support endpoint disappears.

Its convex hull \(\mathcal T_j=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal S_j)\) is compact, not merely relatively compact. Here are the finite-dimensional details. Any finite convex combination of more than \(N-M+1\) mass vectors admits an affine dependence \(\sum_q\beta_qr^{(q)}=0\), \(\sum_q\beta_q=0\), with some \(\beta_q>0\). After deleting zero weights, subtract from each weight \(w_q\) the quantity \(t\beta_q\), where \(t=\min_{\beta_q>0}w_q/\beta_q\). This preserves the represented law and the sum of the weights, leaves every weight nonnegative, and deletes at least one term. Repeating gives at most \(N-M+1\) terms. Every shorter combination can be padded with zero weights. Consequently \(\mathcal T_j\) is the continuous image of the compact product of the weight simplex and \(N-M+1\) copies of \(\mathcal S_j\), proving compactness.

Every point of a nonempty compact convex set in finite dimensions is a finite convex combination of its extreme points. For completeness, this follows by induction on its affine dimension, with no closure of the extreme-point set needed. A zero-dimensional set is a single extreme point. At a relative boundary point \(z\) of a compact convex set \(T\), take points \(y_q\notin T\) in its affine hull converging to \(z\), and nearest points \(z_q\in T\). Since \(\|y_q-z_q\|\le\|y_q-z\|\), we have \(z_q\to z\). Minimizing squared distance along segments from \(z_q\) to any \(x\in T\) shows
\[
 \frac{y_q-z_q}{\|y_q-z_q\|}\cdot(x-z_q)\le0.
\]
A convergent subsequence of these unit vectors gives a unit vector \(v\) in the direction space of the affine hull, with \(v\cdot(x-z)\le0\) for all \(x\in T\). The equality set is a compact exposed face of smaller affine dimension: otherwise \(v\) would be orthogonal to that entire direction space. Apply the induction hypothesis in this face. Every extreme point of the face is extreme in \(T\), because an equality point expressed as a convex combination of two points satisfying the displayed halfspace inequality forces equality for both points. For a relative interior point when the dimension is positive, a line in any nonzero affine-hull direction intersects \(T\) in a compact segment with that point strictly between its endpoints. The endpoints are relative boundary points, so the point is a convex combination of their finite extreme-point representations. This proves the assertion and the finiteness of every representation constructed.

Apply this assertion to \(\mathcal T_j\), and then apply the affine-dependence reduction above to its finite extreme-point representation of \(p\). We obtain at most \(N-M+1\) extreme laws \(\nu^{(\ell)}\), with positive weights after deleting zero terms. Every extreme law of \(\mathcal T_j\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_j\): its membership in the convex hull supplies a finite combination of members of \(\mathcal S_j\), and extremality forces each positive-weight member of that combination to equal it. Thus all these laws satisfy \(\mathbb E_{\nu^{(\ell)}}(X-j)\ge0\).

Theorem 2.10 of Marsiglietti and Melbourne applies to this precise convex hull and says that each extreme law is log-affine on a contiguous subinterval. Counting measure identifies its relative probability mass function with its ordinary masses. The theorem's positive, finite geometric ratio yields a finite real parameter \(s_\ell\), and normalization yields the displayed geometric formula. A singleton support yields a Dirac law. The one-sided expectations and the original equality now give
\[
 0=\mathbb E_p(X-j)
   =\sum_\ell\pi_\ell
      \mathbb E_{\nu^{(\ell)}}(X-j).
\]
A finite sum of nonnegative terms with positive weights is zero only if every term is zero. Therefore every component has mean exactly \(j\); no extreme law with a larger mean remains in the representation. A singleton component must accordingly be \(\delta_j\). For a nonsingleton component, positivity of both endpoint masses implies that its mean is strictly between its endpoints: \(\mathbb E(X-a_\ell)>0\) and \(\mathbb E(b_\ell-X)>0\). Thus \(a_\ell<j<b_\ell\). In particular an integral-mean nonsingleton component has at least three support points. There are no missing endpoint components or infinite-parameter laws. The representation is an equality of the original masses at every integer, including zeros outside \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\), rather than a localization replacement of \(p\).

We next check the predecessor map and the fourth-moment bound. On a nonsingleton component let \(x'=x-a_\ell\), \(m=b_\ell-a_\ell+1\), and \(j'=j-a_\ell\). Its translated masses are \(e^{s_\ell x'}/\sum_{y'=0}^{m-1}e^{s_\ell y'}\), with integral mean \(j'\in\{1,\ldots,m-2\}\). Fact 075a3f88fa24d326, instantiated with the uniform source law \(\theta=0\), supplies a unique finite geometric parameter for this mean, and its row is also the unique maximum-entropy law with that mean on \(D\). Since the translated component has that same geometric form and mean, its parameter equals the predecessor's \(\lambda_{j'}\) and its law equals \(\nu_{j'}\). Translation preserves variance and the centered fourth moment. That fact's row bound gives exactly the claimed inequality with \(K_{\mathrm{row}}=20288\). For a singleton component, the mapped row has \(m=1\), \(j'=0\), and both moments are zero. The original \(p\) has not been identified with the uniform source or with an entropy row on its full support.

We prove the reusable mixture principle before applying it. Delete any zero mixture weights. Sample the finite labels independently, with \(\Pr(L_i=\ell)=\pi_{i,\ell}\), and, conditional on the complete label tuple, sample the blocks independently with laws \(R_{i,L_i}\). This realizes exactly the original product: for every measurable rectangle \(\prod_i A_i\), its probability is
\[
 \sum_{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k}
   \prod_i\pi_{i,\ell_i}R_{i,\ell_i}(A_i)
 =\prod_i\left(\sum_{\ell_i}
   \pi_{i,\ell_i}R_{i,\ell_i}(A_i)\right)
 =\prod_iR_i(A_i).
\]
Equivalently, finite mixtures distribute over product measures, so this construction has law \(\bigotimes_iR_i\).

Fix any label tuple and any block directions \(\mathbf u_i\). Under its conditional product law put \(Y_i=\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)\), \(v_i=\mathbb E Y_i^2\), and \(T=\sum_iY_i\). The variables \(Y_i\) are independent and centered, because every component mean is exactly \(\mathbf j_i\). Their conditional fourth moments satisfy \(\mathbb E Y_i^4\le\kappa v_i^2\), also when \(v_i=0\). Expanding the fourth power, every term with an index appearing once has expectation zero, leaving
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E T^2&=\sum_iv_i,\\
 \mathbb E T^4
 &=\sum_i\mathbb E Y_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h\\
 &\le\kappa\sum_iv_i^2+2\kappa\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h
 =\kappa\left(\sum_iv_i\right)^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where \(\kappa\ge3\) justifies \(6\le2\kappa\). This proves the conditional directional assertion for every tuple, without any symmetry assumption.

If \(v=\mathbb E T^2>0\), Hölder with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), applied to \(|T|^{2/3}|T|^{4/3}\), gives
\[
 v\le(\mathbb E|T|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E T^4)^{1/3},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E|T|\ge\frac{v^{3/2}}{(\mathbb E T^4)^{1/2}}
 \ge\sqrt{v/\kappa}.
\]
Centering implies \(\mathbb E T_+=\mathbb E|T|/2\), where \(T_+=\max\{T,0\}\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 (\mathbb E T_+)^2
 \le\Pr(T\ge0)\,\mathbb E T_+^2
 \le\Pr(T\ge0)\,v,
 \qquad
 \Pr(T\ge0)\ge\frac1{4\kappa}.
\]
If \(v=0\), then the centered variable \(T\) is zero almost surely, and this closed-side probability is one. This handles deterministic components, zero coefficients, and directions constant on a conditional support.

Every proper closed halfspace containing the concatenated mean can be written as \(\{\sum_i\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf x_i-\mathbf j_i)\ge-t\}\), with a nonzero concatenated normal and \(t\ge0\). It contains the closed halfspace \(\{T\ge0\}\). For each label tuple it therefore has conditional mass at least \(1/(4\kappa)\). Averaging these conditional probabilities with the label probabilities, which sum to one, proves that very same lower bound for the original product. This proves the reusable principle, including all the claimed cap quantifiers.

Finally, apply the exact scalar decomposition separately to each original \(p_i\), take \(r_i=1\), \(R_i=p_i\), and \(\kappa=K_{\mathrm{row}}\). The component moment bounds have already been proved with the precise predecessor maps. In dimension one, multiplying a component inequality by the fourth power of any real coefficient verifies the directional hypothesis, including coefficient zero. Independent labels realize \(P=\bigotimes_i p_i\) exactly. The reusable principle yields the asserted bound for every \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\) and every \(t\ge0\), and taking the infimum yields the actual-depth assertion. Each \(j_i\) belongs to the positive support of \(p_i\): its mean is between the endpoints, and it is integral, while the support has no internal zeros. Thus \(\mathbf j\) belongs to the original product support.

The conditional argument must not be converted to an unconditional fourth-moment bound. Conditional centered variances may vary: averaging controls the average of their squares, not the square of their average. The unconditional variance is their average because all conditional means are zero. In fact, for \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), the law \((1-\varepsilon)\delta_0+\varepsilon\operatorname{Unif}\{-1,0,1\}\) is itself a finite log-concave lattice law of mean zero. Its masses are \(\varepsilon/3,1-2\varepsilon/3,\varepsilon/3\); the middle mass is at least each endpoint mass, proving log-concavity. Both its variance and its fourth moment equal \(2\varepsilon/3\), so their standardized fourth-moment ratio is \(3/(2\varepsilon)\), which is unbounded. This does not affect the cap proof: its two mixture components have the same mean, and their cap probabilities are averaged directly. No regular random-center decomposition or planar projection closure is used.

## External sources

Arnaud Marsiglietti and James Melbourne, Geometric and Functional Inequalities for Log-Concave Probability Sequences, arXiv:2004.12005v4, 15 June 2023. Primary HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12005v4 . The current abstract page https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12005 lists v4 as the latest version, submitted on 15 June 2023. Theorem 2.10, its proof, Remark 2.12, and Corollary 2.13 were inspected directly in that edition.

The mathematical statement used is this one-way extreme-point implication. Fix integers \(M\le N\), a reference measure \(\gamma\) on \(\mathbb Z\) with contiguous support, and an arbitrary real function \(h\) on \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\). Let \(\mathcal P_h^\gamma\) consist of probability laws supported inside that finite interval, whose mass functions relative to \(\gamma\) are log-concave with contiguous positive support, and which satisfy \(\mathbb E h(X)\ge0\). Theorem 2.10 says that if a law is extreme in \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal P_h^\gamma)\), its relative mass function equals \(c\rho^x\) on some \(\{a,\ldots,b\}\subseteq\{M,\ldots,N\}\), and equals zero elsewhere, for positive constants \(c,\rho\). A singleton interval is allowed. The theorem does not assert that every such log-affine law is extreme. Its definitions include contiguity; local adjacent inequalities alone without that support condition are not its hypothesis.

The applicability map is \(\gamma(\{x\})=1\), \(h(x)=x-j\), and \(\mathcal P_h^\gamma=\mathcal S_j\). Counting measure has contiguous support and strictly positive finite masses at every integer, so relative masses are ordinary masses. The interval is finite, all expectations are finite, and the original law satisfies the linear constraint with equality. The source imposes no extra sign or monotonicity condition on \(h\). Its proof splits a log-concave relative mass function against exponential functions and equalizes the two resulting \(h\)-expectations by continuity; this is consistent with arbitrary \(h\), including \(x-j\).

Remark 2.12 additionally records that a non-Dirac extreme law has \(\mathbb E h(X)=0\). Corollary 2.13 states the convex-functional reduction \(\sup_{\mathcal P_h^\gamma}\Phi\le\sup_{\mathcal A_h^\gamma}\Phi\), where \(\mathcal A_h^\gamma\) is the subfamily of relative log-affine interval laws satisfying the same constraint. Neither consequence substitutes for the exact decomposition here: compactness, finite extreme-point representation, and the equality of every retained component mean were proved above. The only imported localization assertion is Theorem 2.10's extreme-point implication.
