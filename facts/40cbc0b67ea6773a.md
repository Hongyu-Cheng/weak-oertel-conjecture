## Statement

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\), let \(M,H\) be finite real numbers, and suppose
\[
D=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z^2:|i|\le M,\ -H\le j\le\min(0,ai+b)\}
\]
is nonempty. For \(t>0\), put \(p_{(i,j)}=e^{tj}/Z\) on the entire set \(D\), where \(Z=\sum_D e^{tj}\), and extend \(p\) by zero. Let \(y_0=\max\{j:(i,j)\in D\}\), and let \(\eta=p\{j\le y_0-2\}\). Then
\[
\eta\le\frac{e^{-t}(2-e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-t})^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the primitive integer slab \(y_0-1\le j\le y_0\), of width one, has original root mass at least one minus the right side of (1). The bound is uniform in \(a,b,M,H\); it does not require diffuseness.

For the original-parity spreading alternative, use exactly the original root law and parity convention of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`: \(p\) has tilt \((0,t)\), \(p_{\max}=\max_Dp\), \(\pi_{\mathbf b}=p\{\mathbf X\bmod2=\mathbf b\}\), and \(\mathcal T(p)=q^{\mathrm{can}}\) selects the parity class with its original mass \(\pi_{\mathbf b}\). Let \(\kappa\in(0,1]\) be its one-dimensional separation constant. Fix \(\omega=1\), \(\gamma=1/2\), \(B_\omega=1+4\omega^2=5\), and the alternative's threshold
\[
\eta_0=\min\{\gamma/32,\kappa/(64B_\omega)\}=\kappa/320.
\tag{2}
\]
Every such law with \(t\ge\log(8/\eta_0)\) satisfies the slab clause with original mass at least \(1-\eta_0\). Consequently every sequence of these laws with \(t\to+\infty\) and \(p_{\max}\to0\) eventually satisfies the proposed alternative at each term. If an auxiliary interval-conditioned law \(v\) is used to state its other clause, its weights remain \(\pi^v_{\mathbf b}=v\{\mathbf X\bmod2=\mathbf b\}\); this proof does not need that clause or alter any of those weights.

For complete planar supports in this class, accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` further implies, with \(\delta=\operatorname{TV}(p,\mathcal T(p))\) normalized as one half of the \(\ell^1\) distance, that every sequence with \(t\to+\infty\) and \(p_{\max}\to0\) satisfies
\[
\liminf\delta\ge\frac{\kappa}{5},\qquad
\liminf D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal T(p)\|p)\ge\frac{2\kappa^2}{25}.
\tag{3}
\]
All root laws, atomic maxima, parity masses, and canonical operators in these conclusions belong to the original unconditioned laws on \(D\).

## Proof

The region cut out by the displayed inequalities is convex: \(j\le\min(0,ai+b)\) is the intersection of two affine halfspaces, and the other inequalities are affine. It is bounded whenever \(D\) is nonempty, since then \(M\ge0\) and \(-H\le j\le0\). The entire lattice support \(D\) is therefore finite and complete. Indeed, its convex hull lies in that region, so every lattice point of its convex hull lies in \(D\). This observation also verifies the completeness hypothesis when applying `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`; the assigned planar class additionally has rank two.

Set \(j_0=\lceil-H\rceil\) and \(K=y_0-j_0\ge0\). There are no points above \(y_0\) or below \(j_0\). For each integer \(0\le k\le K\), let \(n_k\) count the sites of \(D\) on the horizontal row \(j=y_0-k\). All these rows are nonempty: a column achieving \(y_0\) also contains every integer ordinate down to \(j_0\). If \(K\le1\), the stated width-one slab contains all of \(D\), and (1) is immediate. Assume henceforth that \(K\ge2\).

We prove the exact row-count inequality
\[
n_k\le k n_1\qquad(1\le k\le K).
\tag{4}
\]
Put \(N=2\lfloor M\rfloor+1\), the number of possible integer abscissae. If \(a=0\), each occupied row has all \(N\) possible abscissae, so (4) holds. If \(a>0\), reflect the first coordinate, replacing \(i\) by \(-i\) and \(a\) by \(-a\). This preserves the root law, all horizontal row counts, and the slab in question; on parity classes it is a bijection. We may therefore assume \(a=-c<0\).

Write \(i_{\min}=-\lfloor M\rfloor\), so the possible abscissae are \(i_{\min},i_{\min}+1,\ldots,i_{\min}+N-1\). The roof is nonincreasing. Nonemptiness of \(D\) implies that its leftmost column is occupied, and its highest integer ordinate is therefore \(y_0=\lfloor\min(0,b-ci_{\min})\rfloor\). In particular \(y_0\le0\) and \(b-ci_{\min}\ge y_0\). Define the row-count offset \(\xi=(b-y_0)/c-i_{\min}\ge0\). On the row \(j=y_0-k\), the zero facet imposes no further restriction because \(y_0-k\le0\). The sloping facet is exactly \(i\le(b-y_0+k)/c\). Hence the complete original row count is
\[
n_k=\min\{N,\lfloor\xi+k/c\rfloor+1\}\qquad(0\le k\le K).
\tag{5}
\]
If \(n_1=N\), then \(n_k\le N\le k n_1\). Otherwise \(n_1=\lfloor\xi+1/c\rfloor+1\). Since \(\xi\ge0\) and \(k\ge1\), one has \(\xi+k/c\le k(\xi+1/c)\). Also \(\lfloor k(\xi+1/c)\rfloor+1\le k(\lfloor\xi+1/c\rfloor+1)\), because \(0\le\{\xi+1/c\}<1\) gives \(\lfloor k\{\xi+1/c\}\rfloor\le k-1\). Apply these two elementary inequalities to (5) to obtain (4). This includes arbitrary rational denominators, intercepts, and a cap at the right endpoint.

Zero horizontal tilt makes every site in row \(y_0-k\) have the same unnormalized weight \(e^{ty_0}e^{-tk}\). This is an identity for the original law on all its sites, not a graph-conditioned or row-conditioned replacement. Its normalizer and its outside slab mass therefore satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-ty_0}Z&=\sum_{k=0}^{K}n_ke^{-tk}\ge n_1e^{-t},\\
\eta&=\frac{\sum_{k=2}^{K}n_ke^{-tk}}{\sum_{k=0}^{K}n_ke^{-tk}}
\le\frac{n_1\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}ke^{-tk}}{n_1e^{-t}}
=\frac{e^{-t}(2-e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-t})^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(n_1\ge1\), and the last equality follows from \(\sum_{k\ge1}ke^{-tk}=e^{-t}/(1-e^{-t})^2\). This proves (1), including the already handled supports with at most two occupied rows.

The slab has primitive normal \((0,1)\) and integer lower endpoint \(y_0-1\). For \(e^{-t}\le1/2\), (6) gives \(\eta\le8e^{-t}\). By (2), \(0<\eta_0\le1/320\). Thus \(t\ge\log(8/\eta_0)\) ensures \(e^{-t}\le\eta_0/8<1/2\) and \(\eta\le\eta_0\), establishing the exact slab clause of the proposed alternative. In particular, eventual validity follows uniformly along every prescribed sequence, without using its \(p_{\max}\to0\) assumption.

Finally apply the width-one original-root slab estimate of `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` to this very slab and its actual outside mass \(\eta\). It gives
\[
\delta\ge\frac{[\kappa-6p_{\max}/(1-\eta)]_+}{5}-4\eta.
\tag{7}
\]
By (1), \(\eta\to0\) along every sequence with \(t\to+\infty\). If also \(p_{\max}\to0\), the right side of (7) tends to \(\kappa/5\). The same accepted fact gives \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal T(p)\|p)\ge2\delta^2\), proving (3). Throughout, (7) is an estimate for the original root law; its accepted conditioning argument does not redefine the original \(p\), \(p_{\max}\), \(\pi\), or \(\mathcal T(p)\).
