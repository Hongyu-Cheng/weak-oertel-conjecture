## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\) with \(a\le b\), and let \(r:[a,b]\to(0,\infty)\) be finite and concave. Put \(W=\sum_{j=a}^b r(j)^d\). Suppose \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and, for every \(x,y\in[a,b]\),

\[
r\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^d
\le(1+\varepsilon)
\left(\frac{r(x)+r(y)}2\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]

There exists an affine function \(L\), positive on \([a,b]\), such that \(L(x)\ge r(x)\) for every \(x\in[a,b]\) and

\[
\sum_{j=a}^b\bigl(L(j)^d-r(j)^d\bigr)
\le\bigl((1+\varepsilon)^6-1\bigr)W.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, for \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/10\), the same affine majorant satisfies

\[
\sum_{j=a}^b\bigl(L(j)^d-r(j)^d\bigr)
\le8\varepsilon\sum_{j=a}^b r(j)^d.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(a<b\), it suffices to assume (1) only for \((x,y)=(a,b)\). All the summands in (2) and (3) retain their original unit integer-site multiplicity.

As a conditional mixed-integer-volume consequence, use precisely the actual-fiber notation of accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\) with \(n=1\): let \(C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, put \(C_x=\{y:(x,y)\in C\}\) and \(w_x=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)\), and assume its positive integer support is \(D_+=\{j\in\mathbb Z:w_j>0\}=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}\). Suppose its positive concave real root \(r(x)=w_x^{1/d}\) on \([a,b]\) obeys (1), with \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/10\). Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where the infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces. Thus the scalar \(W\) above is exactly the original mixed-integer volume \(\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{j\in D_+}w_j\). With \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), \(\theta_d=1-\gamma_d\), and \([t]_+=\max\{t,0\}\), there exists one actual fiber-centroid point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), chosen before every halfspace, satisfying

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac12\bigl[\gamma_d-8\theta_d\varepsilon\bigr]_+W.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/32\) gives \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge(5-e)W/(8e)\). This consequence has the displayed profile hypothesis and makes no assertion that the unconditional one-base depth question is open.

## Proof

If \(a=b\), take \(L\) to be the constant \(r(a)\). Its excess is zero, so (2) and (3) hold. Suppose henceforth that \(a<b\). Write \(m=(a+b)/2\), \(K=(1+\varepsilon)^{1/d}\), and \(R=\max_{a\le j\le b,\ j\in\mathbb Z}r(j)>0\).

Choose an affine supporting line to the concave function \(r\) at the interior point \(m\). For completeness, the existence of this line follows directly from secant slopes. Concavity implies that every slope \((r(y)-r(m))/(y-m)\), with \(y>m\), is at most every slope \((r(m)-r(x))/(m-x)\), with \(x<m\). The supremum of the former slopes and the infimum of the latter slopes are finite: a fixed left secant bounds every right secant above, a fixed right secant bounds every left secant below, and each of the two sets contains a finite fixed secant. Choose a real \(p\) between that supremum and infimum. Then \(L(x)=r(m)+p(x-m)\) satisfies \(L(m)=r(m)\) and \(L(x)\ge r(x)\) throughout \([a,b]\). In particular \(L\) is positive there.

The function \(L-r\) is nonnegative and convex. Since \(L(a)+L(b)=2r(m)\), the endpoint instance of (1) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(L(a)-r(a))+(L(b)-r(b))
&=2r(m)-r(a)-r(b)\\
&\le(K-1)(r(a)+r(b))\\
&\le2(K-1)R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

A convex function on an interval is bounded above by the chord joining its endpoint values. Consequently, if \(s=2(K-1)\), then

\[
0\le L(x)-r(x)\le sR\qquad(x\in[a,b]).
\tag{6}
\]

We next prove the discrete moment estimate needed to sum (6). Any positive concave sequence \(u_a,\ldots,u_b\), with \(N=b-a+1\) and maximum \(M\), satisfies \(\sum_{j=a}^b u_j\ge NM/3\). For \(N=1\) or \(N=2\), this follows from \(\sum u_j\ge M\). For \(N\ge3\), choose a site attaining \(M\). If it is an endpoint, interpolation to the other nonnegative endpoint gives \(\sum u_j\ge NM/2\). If it is an interior site \(k\), interpolation from \(a\) to \(k\) gives \(\sum_{j=a}^k u_j\ge(k-a+1)M/2\), and interpolation from \(k\) to \(b\) gives \(\sum_{j=k}^b u_j\ge(b-k+1)M/2\). Subtracting the twice-counted \(u_k=M\) yields \(\sum u_j\ge(N-1)M/2\ge NM/3\). These interpolations use the original integer sites, without any continuous replacement of the sums.

For every \(t\ge0\), apply this estimate to the positive concave sequence \(u_j=r(j)+tR\), whose maximum is \((1+t)R\). Also, since \(d\ge1\), the functions \(u\mapsto u^{d-1}\) and \(u\mapsto u\) are nondecreasing on \((0,\infty)\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
N\sum_j u_j^d
-\left(\sum_j u_j^{d-1}\right)\left(\sum_j u_j\right)
&=\frac12\sum_{i,j}
(u_i^{d-1}-u_j^{d-1})(u_i-u_j)\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Every sum in (7) is over the same integer interval \(a,\ldots,b\); the formula includes \(d=1\), where both sides before the inequality are zero. Combining (7) with \(\sum_j u_j\ge N(1+t)R/3\) gives

\[
\frac{\sum_j u_j^{d-1}}{\sum_j u_j^d}
\le\frac{N}{\sum_j u_j}
\le\frac{3}{(1+t)R}.
\tag{8}
\]

Set \(F(t)=\sum_{j=a}^b(r(j)+tR)^d\), so \(F(0)=W\). Differentiation and (8) show that \(F'(t)\le3dF(t)/(1+t)\). Equivalently, the derivative of \(F(t)/(1+t)^{3d}\) is nonpositive. Therefore \(F(s)\le(1+s)^{3d}W\). Since \(K\ge1\), we have \(1+s=2K-1\le K^2\). Together with (6), this proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=a}^b L(j)^d
&\le F(s)\\
&\le(1+s)^{3d}W\\
&\le K^{6d}W
=(1+\varepsilon)^6W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Subtracting \(W\) proves (2). For \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/10\), the binomial expansion yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
(1+\varepsilon)^6-1
&=\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^6\binom6k\varepsilon^{k-1}\\
&\le\varepsilon\left(6+\frac{15}{10}+\frac{20}{100}
+\frac{15}{1000}+\frac6{10000}+\frac1{100000}\right)\\
&=\frac{771561}{100000}\varepsilon
\le8\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

This proves (3), including \(\varepsilon=0\). No lower bound on the positive endpoint values, interval length, or individual integer weights was used.

For the mixed-integer-volume consequence, the affine \(L\) just constructed is an admissible positive convex majorant \(\varphi=L\) in accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\), with exactly \(D_+\), \(w_j=r(j)^d\), and \(W\) of its statement. Its fixed certificate weights are \(q_j=(w_j-\theta_dL(j)^d)_+\). Since the positive part of a real number is at least the number itself, (3) implies

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_L=\sum_{j\in D_+}q_j
&\ge\sum_{j\in D_+}\bigl(w_j-\theta_dL(j)^d\bigr)\\
&=\gamma_dW
-\theta_d\sum_{j\in D_+}\bigl(L(j)^d-w_j\bigr)\\
&\ge(\gamma_d-8\theta_d\varepsilon)W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Also \(Q_L\ge0\). The fixed-point assertion (2) of accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\), with \(n=1\), therefore supplies one actual fiber centroid of depth at least \(Q_L/2\), proving (4). For \(\varepsilon\le1/32\), (3) gives excess at most \(W/4\), and the stated \(W/4\) consequence of the same accepted fact gives \((5-e)W/(8e)\). This uses the original mixed-integer support and the original locked halfspace depth throughout.
