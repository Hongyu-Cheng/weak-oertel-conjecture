## Statement

For a compact full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), use the conventions
\[
h_D(y):=\max_{x\in D}\langle y,x\rangle,
\qquad
D^\circ:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:h_D(y)\leq1\},
\]
and
\[
\|x\|_D:=\inf\{s\geq0:x\in sD\}.
\]
For nonempty sets \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and scalars \(p_i\geq0\), write
\[
\sum_i p_iA_i
:=\left\{\sum_i p_ia_i:a_i\in A_i\right\}.
\]

Let \(I\) be a nonempty finite set, let \(D_i\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be compact full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex bodies, and let \(p_i>0\) satisfy \(\sum_{i\in I}p_i=1\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{i\in I}p_iD_i\right)^\circ
\subseteq
\sum_{i\in I}p_iD_i^\circ.}
\tag{1}
\]

For the application, retain the canonical zero-fresh target system and notation of accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b. In particular, \(\alpha_e\geq0\),
\[
t_m=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\alpha_e,
\qquad
E_m=\frac1{t_m}\sum_{e:m_e=m}\alpha_eD_e
\quad(t_m>0),
\tag{2}
\]
where every \(D_e\) and \(E_m\) is a compact full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body. Its target forces satisfy
\[
\lambda_m=h_{E_m}(Y_m),
\qquad
0\leq\lambda_m\leq t_m,
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{m:t_m>0}Y_m=0,
\qquad
\sum_{m:t_m>0}Y_mm^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The symbol \(b_e\) below is exactly the existing canonical capacity from equation (27) of accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b, with no change of normalization:
\[
b_e=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\lambda_{m_e}}{t_{m_e}}\alpha_e,&t_{m_e}>0,\\[2mm]
0,&t_{m_e}=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b earlier used \(v_e\) for its original reservoir dual optimizer. Denote that predecessor family here by \(v_e^{\mathrm{res}}\). The edge forces \(v_e\) asserted next are a new split family and are not identified with \(v_e^{\mathrm{res}}\).

There exist edge forces \(v_e\in\mathbb R^d\) such that, for every \(m\) with \(t_m>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{e:m_e=m}v_e=Y_m,
\qquad
h_{D_e}(v_e)\leq b_e\quad(e:m_e=m).}
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\lambda_m=0\), then necessarily \(Y_m=0\), all capacities \(b_e\) over \(m\) vanish, and the forces in (6) may and will all be chosen equal to zero. If \(t_m=0\), then every \(\alpha_e\) over \(m\) and every corresponding \(b_e\) vanish, and those edge forces are also chosen equal to zero. Globally,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_ev_e=0,
\qquad
\sum_ev_em_e^{\mathsf T}=0.}
\tag{7}
\]

This construction does not in general preserve translated support. In the notation \(L_e=\bar c_e+D_e\) and \(\mathcal Z_m=\bar z_m+E_m\) of accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b, it asserts no identity between \(\sum_{e:m_e=m}h_{L_e}(v_e)\) and \(h_{\mathcal Z_m}(Y_m)\), and hence no preservation of the strict target support objective \(\sum_mh_{\mathcal Z_m}(Y_m)<0\).

## Proof

We first record the gauge-polar identity, including its boundary case. Origin symmetry and full dimensionality imply \(0\in\operatorname{int}D\). Thus \(D\) contains a Euclidean ball about the origin, while compactness makes \(D\) bounded. These two facts make \(\|x\|_D\) finite for every \(x\) and positive for \(x\neq0\); they also make \(D^\circ\) closed and bounded, hence compact. If \(x\in sD\) and \(y\in D^\circ\), then \(\langle y,x\rangle\leq s\). Taking first the maximum over \(y\in D^\circ\) and then the infimum over admissible \(s\) gives
\[
h_{D^\circ}(x)\leq\|x\|_D.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(x\neq0\), put \(a:=\|x\|_D>0\). Closedness of \(D\) gives \(x/a\in D\), and minimality of \(a\) places \(x/a\) on \(\partial D\). A supporting hyperplane at \(x/a\) gives a nonzero \(y\) and a scalar \(c\) such that
\[
\langle y,z\rangle\leq c=\left\langle y,\frac xa\right\rangle
\qquad(z\in D).
\]
Because \(0\in\operatorname{int}D\), one has \(c>0\). Thus \(y/c\in D^\circ\) and
\[
\left\langle\frac yc,x\right\rangle=a,
\]
which reverses (8). For \(x=0\), both sides of (8) are zero. Therefore
\[
\boxed{h_{D^\circ}(x)=\|x\|_D\quad(x\in\mathbb R^d).}
\tag{9}
\]

Put
\[
E:=\sum_{i\in I}p_iD_i,
\qquad
F:=\sum_{i\in I}p_iD_i^\circ.
\]
Both are compact convex sets, and \(E\) is full-dimensional and origin-symmetric. Fix \(x\neq0\) and set
\[
a_i:=\|x\|_{D_i}>0,
\qquad
H:=\left(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{p_i}{a_i}\right)^{-1}>0.
\]
The minimum in each gauge is attained, so \(x/a_i\in D_i\). Consequently,
\[
\frac xH
=\sum_{i\in I}p_i\frac x{a_i}
\in E,
\]
and hence
\[
\|x\|_E\leq H.
\tag{10}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\left(\sum_{i\in I}p_ia_i\right)
\left(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{p_i}{a_i}\right)
\geq\left(\sum_{i\in I}p_i\right)^2=1,
\]
so
\[
H\leq\sum_{i\in I}p_ia_i.
\tag{11}
\]
Using (9), (10), (11), and Minkowski additivity of support functions, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{E^\circ}(x)
&=\|x\|_E
\leq H
\leq\sum_{i\in I}p_i\|x\|_{D_i}\\
&=\sum_{i\in I}p_ih_{D_i^\circ}(x)
=h_F(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
For \(x=0\), (12) holds because both support functions vanish. If a point \(y\in E^\circ\) were outside \(F\), strict separation of \(y\) from the compact convex set \(F\) would give an \(x\) with
\[
\langle x,y\rangle>h_F(x),
\]
whereas \(y\in E^\circ\) and (12) give
\[
\langle x,y\rangle\leq h_{E^\circ}(x)\leq h_F(x),
\]
a contradiction. This proves (1).

We now apply (1). Fix \(m\) with \(t_m>0\), and let
\[
I_m:=\{e:m_e=m,\ \alpha_e>0\},
\qquad
p_{m,e}:=\frac{\alpha_e}{t_m}\quad(e\in I_m).
\tag{13}
\]
The set \(I_m\) is nonempty, every \(p_{m,e}\) is positive, their sum is one, and (2) becomes
\[
E_m=\sum_{e\in I_m}p_{m,e}D_e.
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose first that \(\lambda_m>0\). By (3) and positive homogeneity,
\[
h_{E_m}\left(\frac{Y_m}{\lambda_m}\right)=1,
\]
so \(Y_m/\lambda_m\in E_m^\circ\). Applying (1) to (14), choose \(u_e\in D_e^\circ\) for \(e\in I_m\) such that
\[
\frac{Y_m}{\lambda_m}
=\sum_{e\in I_m}p_{m,e}u_e.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(e\in I_m\), the exact capacity alias (5) reads
\[
b_e=\lambda_mp_{m,e}.
\tag{16}
\]
Define \(v_e:=b_eu_e\) for \(e\in I_m\), and define \(v_e:=0\) for edges over \(m\) with \(\alpha_e=0\). Equations (15) and (16) give
\[
\sum_{e:m_e=m}v_e
=\lambda_m\sum_{e\in I_m}p_{m,e}u_e
=Y_m.
\]
Moreover, \(u_e\in D_e^\circ\) means \(h_{D_e}(u_e)\leq1\), and \(b_e\geq0\), so
\[
h_{D_e}(v_e)=b_eh_{D_e}(u_e)\leq b_e.
\]
For an edge with \(\alpha_e=0\), equation (5) gives \(b_e=0\), and the same inequality reads \(h_{D_e}(0)=0\leq0\). Thus (6) holds when \(\lambda_m>0\).

Suppose now that \(\lambda_m=0\). Since \(E_m\) is full-dimensional and contains the origin in its interior, there is a \(\rho_m>0\) such that \(\rho_mB_2^d\subseteq E_m\). If \(Y_m\neq0\), then
\[
h_{E_m}(Y_m)
\geq\left\langle Y_m,
\rho_m\frac{Y_m}{\|Y_m\|_2}\right\rangle
=\rho_m\|Y_m\|_2>0,
\]
contrary to (3). Hence \(Y_m=0\). Equation (5) gives \(b_e=0\) for every edge over \(m\), and choosing all those \(v_e=0\) proves (6) in the zero-load case.

Finally, if \(t_m=0\), nonnegativity and the definition of \(t_m\) in (2) imply \(\alpha_e=0\) for every edge over \(m\); equation (5) gives \(b_e=0\), and we choose \(v_e=0\). Summing (6) over the positive targets and using (4) gives
\[
\sum_ev_e
=\sum_{m:t_m>0}\sum_{e:m_e=m}v_e
=\sum_{m:t_m>0}Y_m=0,
\]
and, because \(m_e=m\) on each target class,
\[
\sum_ev_em_e^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_{m:t_m>0}
\left(\sum_{e:m_e=m}v_e\right)m^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_{m:t_m>0}Y_mm^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
This proves (7).

The proof used only the centered bodies \(D_e\) and their polars. Indeed,
\[
h_{L_e}(v_e)
=\langle v_e,\bar c_e\rangle+h_{D_e}(v_e),
\]
and the varying translations \(\bar c_e\) are not controlled by the identity \(\sum_{e:m_e=m}v_e=Y_m\). Therefore the construction does not in general preserve \(h_{\mathcal Z_m}(Y_m)\) or the summed translated support objective. No primal-density statement or translated-support preservation is used or claimed.
