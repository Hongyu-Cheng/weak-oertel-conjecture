## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $A,K,B$ be the only full-dimensional integer fibers at $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, masses $a,k,b$, $b\le a+k$, and $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. Suppose $d+1$ ambient caps attaining the raw mixed depth $Q$ at $(0,y)$ have continuous normals forming a full-rank minimal positive circuit, proper $A$ cuts and zero-volume $B$ captures. First send a maximum-volume inscribed simplex of $B$ to $\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$ by an affine change of coordinates, then translate its centroid to zero. In that common frame write $u_i$ for the transformed unit normals. Set $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, $r=1/((d+1)\sqrt d)$, and $C_d=3d2^d/(\omega_{d-1}r^{d-1})$, with $\omega_{d-1}$ the Euclidean unit $(d-1)$-ball volume. Define
$$
\varepsilon_d=\min\left\{\frac{r}{C_d2^d},\frac{1-2\Gamma}{2\omega_{d-1}C_d^d 2^{d(d-1)}}\right\}.
$$
If some pair satisfies $\lVert u_i+u_j\rVert\le\varepsilon_d$, then $Q>\Gamma(a+k+b)/2$ at the given mark. In particular, the supporting contact and volume estimates used below give $a+k\le5Q/(3\Gamma)$ and, under $Q\le\Gamma(a+k+b)/2$, give $Q\le3\Gamma b$ and $k\le3b$.

## Proof

The map is applied identically on all fibers, so it preserves the base, midpoint containment and ambient depth minimization, with one common positive volume scaling. The inscribed simplex exists by compactness. After sending it to the standard simplex, replacing a vertex by any point of $B$ proves that the absolute value of each barycentric coordinate of that point is at most one: the volume ratio is the absolute value of the replaced coordinate. Thus $1/d!\le b\le2^d$ because $B\subseteq[-1,1]^d$ before recentering. The distances from the simplex centroid to the coordinate facets are $1/(d+1)$ and to the sum facet $1/((d+1)\sqrt d)$, so $r\mathbb B_d\subseteq B$ after recentering.

For each cap the zero-volume $B$ threshold is at least $h_B(u_i)$; otherwise it captures interior volume. If strictly larger, lowering it slightly towards that support, at fixed middle threshold, raises the proper $A$ threshold and strictly decreases the mass while keeping the $B$ mass zero. This contradicts depth attainment. With positive circuit coefficients $\alpha_i$, the middle thresholds $u_i\cdot y$ have weighted sum zero, and the $A$ thresholds $2u_i\cdot y-h_B(u_i)$ have negative weighted sum, since $h_B(u_i)\ge r>0$. No point can strictly miss all the corresponding traces, so each family covers its entire fiber.

Order $A$ cap masses $U_1\le\cdots\le U_{d+1}=w$. The middle masses are $Q-U_i$. The actual three-fiber hypotheses of 03c59c70402b66a2 hold, and $B$ contributes zero, so $w\le2(Q-w)$ and $w\le2Q/3$. By the ordinary ordered cap-cover bound of ac7ba1e1e0ebc955 on the two actual convex bodies, with $\kappa=1/\Gamma-2\in(0,1)$, one has
$$
a\le U_{d+1}+U_d+\kappa U_{d-1},\qquad
k\le Q/\Gamma-U_1-U_2-\kappa U_3 .
$$
This gives $a+k\le(Q+w)/\Gamma\le5Q/(3\Gamma)$ and $k\le Q/\Gamma$. If $Q\le\Gamma(a+k+b)/2$, then $2Q/\Gamma\le b+5Q/(3\Gamma)$, which gives the two asserted bounds by $b$.

For the selected pair, use the actual antipodal-pair lemma in the same frame. Its radius $L$ is at most $C_db$; hence $\delta L\le\varepsilon_d C_d2^d\le r$ and
$$
\frac{\omega_{d-1}\delta L^d}{b}\le
\omega_{d-1}\varepsilon_d C_d^d2^{d(d-1)}
\le(1-2\Gamma)/2<1-2\Gamma .
$$
The lemma contradicts the assumed bound on $Q$ and proves the strict result.
