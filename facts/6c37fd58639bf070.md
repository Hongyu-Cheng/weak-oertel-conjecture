## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the exact canonical hypotheses and notation of accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 7ec63df101c30343. In particular, for every retained parity class \(\xi\),
\[
 L_\xi:=\sum_{y\equiv\xi}\beta_y,
 \qquad
 \max_{y\equiv\xi}\beta_y\le \frac{L_\xi}{4},
 \qquad
 s_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{\beta_u\beta_v}{L_\xi},
\tag{1}
\]
and, whenever \(\beta_y>0\),
\[
 p_y^\flat+\frac12D_*\subseteq \mathsf M_{\beta_y}(C_y).
\tag{2}
\]
For a positive edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), write
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For a retained label \((\xi,c)\), write
\[
 S_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
 \qquad
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ:=\frac1{S_{\xi,c}}
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_eF_e^\flat,
\tag{4}
\]
so that accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives
\[
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq \mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c).
\tag{5}
\]
Assume the \(I\)-branch of accepted fact 7ec63df101c30343. Thus the canonical triangle labels
\[
 \tau=(z;\{a,b\}),
 \qquad
 \theta_\tau=\frac{\beta_z\beta_a\beta_b}{2L_\xi^2},
\tag{6}
\]
whose opposite edges are \(I\)-bad have total mass
\[
 \Theta_I:=\sum_{\tau:\operatorname{opp}(\tau)\in\mathcal E_I}\theta_\tau
 >\frac{3Q}{100},
\tag{7}
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal E_I
 :=\left\{e:\left\|F_e^\flat-q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ\right\|_{D_*}>\frac14\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Fix one retained class \(\xi\), one anchor \(z\), and two nonempty disjoint blocks
\[
 A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_r\},
 \qquad
 B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_s\},
\tag{9}
\]
such that all \(1+r+s\) bases lie in \(\xi\), are distinct, have positive \(\beta\)-loads, every cross edge \(\{a_i,b_j\}\) lies in \(\mathcal E_I\), and the integer half-directions
\[
 h_i:=\frac{a_i-z}{2}\quad(1\le i\le r),
 \qquad
 k_j:=\frac{b_j-z}{2}\quad(1\le j\le s)
\tag{10}
\]
are jointly linearly independent. Put \(q:=r+s\le n\),
\[
 t:=\beta_z,
 \qquad
 \lambda_0=\mu_0:=t,
 \qquad
 \lambda_i:=\frac{\beta_z\beta_{a_i}}{L_\xi},
 \qquad
 \mu_j:=\frac{\beta_z\beta_{b_j}}{L_\xi}.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(V_1:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{h_1,\ldots,h_r\}\) and \(V_2:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{k_1,\ldots,k_s\}\). Choose integer matrices \(B_1\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r}\) and \(B_2\in\mathbb Z^{n\times s}\) whose columns are lattice bases of \(V_1\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and \(V_2\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and write
\[
 h_i=B_1v_i,
 \qquad
 k_j=B_2w_j,
 \qquad
 v_i\in\mathbb Z^r,
 \quad
 w_j\in\mathbb Z^s,
 \quad
 v_0=w_0=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Define
\[
 \Phi(u,v):=z+B_1u+B_2v,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
 X_{00}:=p_z^\flat,
 \qquad
 X_{i0}:=F_{\{z,a_i\}}^\flat,
 \qquad
 X_{0j}:=F_{\{z,b_j\}}^\flat,
 \qquad
 X_{ij}:=q_{\xi,(a_i+b_j)/2}^\circ.
\tag{14}
\]
Then \(\Phi\) is an injective integral affine rank-\(q\) chart and, for \(0\le i\le r\), \(0\le j\le s\),
\[
 \Phi(v_i,w_j)=
 \begin{cases}
 z,&i=j=0,\\
 (z+a_i)/2,&i>0,\ j=0,\\
 (z+b_j)/2,&i=0,\ j>0,\\
 (a_i+b_j)/2,&i>0,\ j>0,
 \end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 X_{ij}\in
 \mathsf M_{m_{ij}}\bigl(C_{\Phi(v_i,w_j)}\bigr),
 \qquad
 m_{ij}:=\frac{\lambda_i\mu_j}{2t}.
\tag{16}
\]
For every \(i,j>0\), one also has
\[
 m_{ij}=\theta_{z;\{a_i,b_j\}},
 \qquad
 \left\|X_{00}-X_{i0}-X_{0j}+X_{ij}\right\|_{D_*}>\frac14.
\tag{17}
\]

Put
\[
 \Delta_1:=\operatorname{conv}\{0,v_1,\ldots,v_r\},
 \qquad
 \Delta_2:=\operatorname{conv}\{0,w_1,\ldots,w_s\},
\tag{18}
\]
let \(\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_r\) and \(\psi_0,\ldots,\psi_s\) be their barycentric coordinates, and define
\[
 \mathcal B_1:=
 \sum_{u\in\Delta_1\cap\mathbb Z^r}
 \left(\sum_{i=0}^r\varphi_i(u)\lambda_i^{1/d}\right)^d,
\tag{19}
\]
\[
 \mathcal B_2:=
 \sum_{v\in\Delta_2\cap\mathbb Z^s}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^s\psi_j(v)\mu_j^{1/d}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B}:=
 \frac{\gamma_d}{2t}\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2,
\tag{20}
\]
where \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). Then
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge2^{-q}\mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B}
 \ge\gamma_d\sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{j=1}^s
 \theta_{z;\{a_i,b_j\}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 P_\star(C):=
 \sup_{(\xi,z,A,B)}2^{n-q}\mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B}
\tag{22}
\]
over all choices above, then every such star satisfies
\[
 P_\star(C)
 \ge
 \gamma_d2^{n-q}
 \sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{j=1}^s\theta_{z;\{a_i,b_j\}},
 \qquad
 P_\star(C)\le2^nh_{\max}(C).
\tag{23}
\]

## Proof

Joint independence in (10) implies that \(V_1\oplus V_2\) has dimension \(q=r+s\). Because each \(V_i\) is spanned by integer vectors, \(V_i\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is a rank-\(\dim V_i\) lattice, so the bases in (12) exist. The coordinate vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) and \(w_1,\ldots,w_s\) are respectively linearly independent. Hence the concatenated matrix \([B_1\ B_2]\) has rank \(q\), \(\Phi\) is injective, and (15) follows directly from (10)--(13). In particular, all product-vertex base fibers in one packet are distinct, even when \(q<n\).

We use the elementary monotonicity
\[
 x\in\mathsf M_m(K),\quad0<m'\le m
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 x\in\mathsf M_{m'}(K).
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed, multiplying a representing density by \(m'/m\) preserves its normalized first moment and keeps the density between zero and one.

At the anchor, (2) and (24) give
\[
 X_{00}=p_z^\flat\in\mathsf M_{t/2}(C_z)
 =\mathsf M_{m_{00}}(C_z).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(i>0\), retention gives
\[
 \frac{\lambda_i/2}{\beta_z}
 =\frac{\beta_{a_i}}{2L_\xi}\le\frac18,
 \qquad
 \frac{\lambda_i/2}{\beta_{a_i}}
 =\frac{\beta_z}{2L_\xi}\le\frac18.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus (2) and (24) put both \(p_z^\flat\) and \(p_{a_i}^\flat\) in their respective metronoids of the common mass \(\lambda_i/2\). The positive-root metronoid convexity in accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e, applied at equal root mass, therefore gives
\[
 X_{i0}=F_{\{z,a_i\}}^\flat
 \in\mathsf M_{\lambda_i/2}(C_{(z+a_i)/2})
 =\mathsf M_{m_{i0}}(C_{\Phi(v_i,0)}).
\tag{27}
\]
The same argument gives
\[
 X_{0j}\in\mathsf M_{\mu_j/2}(C_{(z+b_j)/2})
 =\mathsf M_{m_{0j}}(C_{\Phi(0,w_j)}).
\tag{28}
\]

For \(i,j>0\), put \(c_{ij}:=(a_i+b_j)/2\). By (1),
\[
 s_{\{a_i,b_j\}}=\frac{\beta_{a_i}\beta_{b_j}}{L_\xi},
 \qquad
 m_{ij}
 =\frac{\beta_z}{2L_\xi}s_{\{a_i,b_j\}}
 \le\frac18s_{\{a_i,b_j\}}
 \le\frac18S_{\xi,c_{ij}}.
\tag{29}
\]
Equation (5) and monotonicity (24) now give
\[
 X_{ij}=q_{\xi,c_{ij}}^\circ
 \in\mathsf M_{m_{ij}}(C_{c_{ij}}).
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (25), (27), (28), and (30) prove (16). The identity in (6) gives the first part of (17). Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_{00}-X_{i0}-X_{0j}+X_{ij}
 &=p_z^\flat-\frac{p_z^\flat+p_{a_i}^\flat}{2}
 -\frac{p_z^\flat+p_{b_j}^\flat}{2}
 +q_{\xi,c_{ij}}^\circ\\
 &=q_{\xi,c_{ij}}^\circ-F_{\{a_i,b_j\}}^\flat.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Since every cross edge lies in \(\mathcal E_I\), (8) proves the norm inequality in (17).

Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d applied to (16) gives, at every product vertex,
\[
 \delta_C\bigl(\Phi(v_i,w_j),X_{ij}\bigr)^{1/d}
 \ge
 \lambda_i^{1/d}
 \left(\frac{\gamma_d\mu_j}{2t}\right)^{1/d}.
\tag{32}
\]
The simplices in (18) are full-dimensional lattice simplices because their nonzero vertices are respectively linearly independent. Apply the factorized lattice-polysimplex theorem in accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 with two factors, chart matrices \(B_1,B_2\), first-factor vertex roots \(\lambda_i^{1/d}\), and second-factor vertex roots \((\gamma_d\mu_j/(2t))^{1/d}\). It yields
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \ge2^{-q}\frac{\gamma_d}{2t}\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2
 =2^{-q}\mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B},
\tag{33}
\]
which is the first inequality in (21).

Every vertex of each simplex is an integer point in its corresponding lattice-point set. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal B_1\ge t+\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_2\ge t+\sum_{j=1}^s\mu_j.
\tag{34}
\]
Using only the cross term in their nonnegative product gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_{\xi,z;A,B}
 &\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{2t}
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\right)
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^s\mu_j\right)\\
 &=\gamma_d\sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{j=1}^s
 \frac{\lambda_i\mu_j}{2t}
 =\gamma_d\sum_{i=1}^r\sum_{j=1}^s
 \theta_{z;\{a_i,b_j\}},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
which completes (21). Multiplying (33) by \(2^n\), then taking the supremum in (22), proves both inequalities in (23).

Injectivity of \(\Phi\) proves that no two corners of one packet use the same physical target fiber. The proof invokes metronoid monotonicity and convex interpolation separately for each corner and never adds representing densities or packet energies. Thus it uses neither source-density additivity nor target-fiber additivity across different stars. In particular, (23) controls each compatible star but supplies no inequality summing their masses into \(P_\star(C)\). It therefore does not imply
\[
 P_\star(C)+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)+R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)
 \ge\Theta_I/3,
\]
and by itself yields no contradiction with the hard assumption.
