## Statement

For $E_{w,\theta}$ and $J$ from the exact two-tent cap menu, normalize the endpoint widths to $1,R$ with $R\ge1$. For every $p,q\in[0,1]$ and every tilt,
$$
J(\alpha)\ge R/6+1/12+(1-1/R)^2/9+4/9.
$$
In particular $R\ge9/2$ gives $J\ge4/3+17/1458$. For every width ratio, $J(\alpha)\ge4/3$ if both thresholds are in the falling pieces or are limits of such configurations.

## Proof

Fix the peak of the width-one profile, with coordinate $b_A=(2p-1)/3$ and section length two. The midpoint roof is at least $1+f_B(2s-b_A)/2$ over $[(b_A+a_B)/2,(b_A+c_B)/2]$. Because $-b_A\in[a_B,c_B]$, its contribution above zero is $(c_B+b_A)/2+F_B(-b_A)/4$. From the upper end of this interval to the right midpoint support, fixing the upper support of $B$ and using the falling branch of $A$ supplies an additional triangle of area $(1-p)/4$. This interval lies above zero. Adding the two estimates and using $p\ge0,q\le1$ gives $Q\ge R/6+1/12+F_B(1/3)/4$.

In accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d take the density identically one on each centered area-one kite. It gives $F_B(0)\ge4/9$ and the analogous estimate for $A$. Contract the zero cap in $B$ toward an upper support point by factor $1-1/(3c_B)$, which is nonnegative since $c_B\ge R/3$. The contraction lies in the $1/3$ cap, and the factor is at least $1-1/R$. Thus $F_B(1/3)\ge (4/9)(1-1/R)^2$. One of the two endpoint thresholds is nonpositive, so the pair pays at least $4/9$. This proves the first estimate. Its right side increases for $R\ge1$ and at $9/2$ equals $4/3+17/1458$.

To handle two falling cuts at width ratios at most five, relabel so the last descending midpoint component is the first profile, and rescale axial and transverse coordinates inversely to make its width one. Now the second width $R$ lies in $[1/5,5]$. Let $a,b>0$ be their falling fractions, set $t=R^2b/a\ge1$, and $S=[1+R+a(1+t/R)]/3$. Since both thresholds are above their modes and sum to zero, the peak $H$ is nonpositive. Their distances from the upper support sum to $S$, so the sum of their squared distances divided respectively by $a$ and $at$ is at least $S^2/[a(1+t)]$. The middle formula from the cap menu, with the current first profile last in the roof, gives
$$
J\ge S/2-a/4+(S-a)^2/(4at)+S^2/[a(1+t)]=A a+B+C/a .
$$
For $R<1$ the same roof formula follows by interchanging the profiles and scaling to the menu's width order. In particular $S-a\ge[1+R+a(1/R-2)]/3>0$, using $a\le R^2$ when $1/2\le R<1$.
The coefficients are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\frac{-3R^2t^2+5R^2t+4R^2+6Rt^3+10Rt^2-4Rt+5t^3+t^2}{36R^2t(t+1)},\\
B&=\frac{(R+1)(3Rt^2+5Rt-2R+5t^2+t)}{18Rt(t+1)},\qquad
C=\frac{(R+1)^2(5t+1)}{36t(t+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With $x=R-1\in[-4/5,4]$ and $y=t-1\ge0$, the numerator of $A$ is
$(6x+11)y^3+(-3x^2+22x+41)y^2+(-x^2+32x+50)y+6(x+2)^2$.
All coefficients are positive, using concavity and the endpoint values of the two quadratic coefficients on this interval. Also
$$
108Rt(t+1)[4AC-(4/3-B)^2]
=(-3x^3+33x^2+8x+52)y^2+(-18x^3+86x^2-16x+56)y+96x^2.
$$
For $x\le0$ the coefficient of $y^2$ is at least $52-32/5$ and that of $y$ at least $56$. For $0\le x\le4$ the first is positive by $33-3x>0$, and the second is at least $14x^2-16x+56>0$. Hence $4AC\ge(4/3-B)^2$. If $B\ge4/3$ the claim follows from $A,C>0$, and otherwise $Aa+C/a\ge2\sqrt{AC}\ge4/3-B$. Limits follow by continuity of the kite cap and tail areas. The all-tilt estimate covers width ratios above five, completing the falling-branch result.
