## Statement

Use the finite full-dimensional fiber setting of the normalized actual-fiber hinge minimum, with $N$ closed caps $H_i=\{L_i\ge0\}$, $L_i=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$, $\lVert u_i\rVert=1$, pointwise cover of $S$, and budgets $\mu(H_i)\le\tau M$ for $0<\tau<1$. At an attained minimum choose any optimal minimax weights $\lambda_z\in\Delta_N$ on each fiber. Put $U_z=\sum_i\lambda_{zi}u_i$, $C_z=\min_{x\in K_z}\sum_i\lambda_{zi}L_i(z,x)\ge0$, $\Psi_i=\int_S (L_i)_+\,d\mu$, and let $c_i,P_i,m_i$ be the cap mass and raw $z,x$ moments.

There exist nonnegative multipliers $\alpha_0,r_i,\eta_z$, with at least one positive, satisfying $r_i(c_i-\tau M)=0$ and $\eta_zC_z=0$. For each cap choose effective section moments $(S_i,P_i^s,m_i^s)$ by integrating $(1,z,x)$ against densities $0\le\theta_{iz}\le1$ on $T_{iz}=K_z\cap\{x:L_i(z,x)=0\}$ with $\mathcal H_{d-1}$ and summing over $z$. The densities equal one almost everywhere on proper sections. The triple $(P_i^s,m_i^s,-S_i)$ is a single consistent generalized cap-mass gradient in $(a_i,u_i,b_i)$. For each $\eta_z>0$ there is one point $x_z\in K_z$ such that $L_i(z,x_z)\le0$, $\lambda_{zi}L_i(z,x_z)=0$ for every $i$, and $-U_z\in N_{K_z}(x_z)$. With zero-multiplier terms ignored, the balances are
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_i:=\alpha_0c_i+r_iS_i&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi},\\
\alpha_0P_i+r_iP_i^s&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}z,\\
\alpha_0m_i+r_im_i^s&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}x_z+\alpha_0\Psi_i u_i .
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular $\sum_i W_i=\sum_z\eta_z$ and $\sum_i W_i u_i+\sum_z(-\eta_z U_z)=0$, with $-\eta_z U_z\in N_{K_z}(x_z)$ at every positive $\eta_z$. The conditions allow $\alpha_0=0$.

## Proof

First consider the cap-mass function of $(a_i,u_i,b_i)$ near the given parameter. A perturbation of size $\varepsilon$ changes membership only in slabs of thickness $O(\varepsilon)$ in the bounded fibers, so the mass is locally Lipschitz when $u_i\ne0$. For fixed $(a_i,u_i)$ and each $z$ there are at most two values of $b_i$ at which the cap plane supports $K_z$. The exceptional graphs are measurable and null by Fubini, both in unrestricted parameters and in smooth unit-sphere charts with $b_i$ free.

Off these graphs all sections are proper or empty. To differentiate on one fiber, slice along the unit direction $u_i/\lVert u_i\rVert$. The perturbed plane is a graph over the perpendicular coordinates. At a point $x$ of the unperturbed section, its first-order threshold displacement in direction $(\dot a_i,\dot u_i,\dot b_i)$ is $-(\dot a_i\cdot z+\dot u_i\cdot x-\dot b_i)/\lVert u_i\rVert$. On almost every line through a proper section point, that point is interior to the fiber and the captured interval length changes to first order by the negative of the threshold displacement. The exceptional relative boundary of the section has zero section measure. On a line missing the section there is no first-order change by compactness, and a plane strictly disjoint from the fiber has zero gradient. The divided length changes are bounded uniformly on the bounded projection, so dominated convergence gives the directional derivative as the pairing with the integral of $(z,x,-1)/\lVert u_i\rVert$ on the section. Local Lipschitz continuity upgrades this to a full derivative: the directional difference quotients are uniformly Lipschitz in the unit direction, and their convergence to a linear expression is uniform by compactness of the direction sphere.

The ordinary gradients at these parameters are locally bounded. Take the convex hull of their cluster limits at the given cap. It is a nonempty compact convex set of generalized gradients, since the cluster set is closed and bounded and its elements are limits of ordinary gradients. To see the common-density representation, for one convergent sequence project each nearby cap section orthogonally onto the limiting plane at its base. The projection between planes is eventually one-to-one, its area Jacobian tends to one, and the projected section indicator is bounded by one. The sections stay in a fixed bounded set. Passing to a weak subsequence of the projected indicator measures on each of the finitely many planes gives measures dominated by $\mathcal H_{d-1}$ and supported on the limiting sections, since all accumulation points of the nearby sections belong to $T_{iz}$. The factor $1/\lVert u_i\rVert$ tends to one, and replacing a nearby section point by its projection changes its moment by $o(1)$. On the relative interior of a proper section, a point is in $\operatorname{int}K_z$ and its inverse projection lies inside $K_z$ for all sufficiently close parameters. Thus the limiting density is one almost everywhere there. In $d=1$ this projection is between two points and $\mathcal H_0$ is counting measure. Convex combinations preserve the joint density representation.

Now work in smooth local charts for the spheres, and on the affine spaces $\sum_i\lambda_{zi}=1$ for the weights, retaining the inequalities $-\lambda_{zi}\le0$. The exact minimax formula ensures the chosen weights are feasible for $C_z\ge0$. Conversely $C_z\ge0$ implies actual coverage of each fiber. Hence the attained minimum is a local minimum in the joint variables. Include the objective gradient from the fixed-offset stress theorem, the cap-mass gradient sets just constructed for active budgets, the active simplex gradients, and the following compact convex gradient set for each active constraint $-C_z\le0$. The function $-C_z$ is the maximum over $x\in K_z$ of $-\sum_i\lambda_{zi}L_i(z,x)$. Use the gradients of this expression over its maximizing face $x\in\operatorname{argmin}_{K_z}U_z\cdot x$. The $(a_i,u_i,b_i)$ gradient of the expression with positive sign is $\lambda_{zi}(z,x,-1)$, and its $\lambda_{zi}$ gradient is $L_i(z,x)$. These depend affinely on the same $x$, so the gradient set on the maximizing face is convex. All the gradient sets are pulled back into the chart and affine weight coordinates.

If zero were outside the convex hull of this finite union of compact sets, a strict separating direction would pair negatively with every member. For the objective and simplex constraints this gives strict decrease directly. For $-C_z$ it gives strict decrease by uniform first-order expansion over compact $K_z$: a sequence of maximizing points at nearby parameters has limit points in the maximizing face, so the upper directional derivative is bounded by the largest pairing on that face. For each active cap budget, the strict gradient bound persists for all ordinary gradients sufficiently near the chart point and outside its null exceptional graphs. Otherwise a sequence of violating gradients would have a cluster limit in the pulled-back gradient set, contradicting strictness. The cap mass in the chart is locally Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous on each short line segment parallel to the chosen direction. Almost every such line meets the exceptional set in a length-null set by Fubini. Integrating the ordinary derivative along these lines and taking limits to the line through the chart point gives strict decrease at a uniform linear rate. Inactive constraints persist by continuity. This would contradict local minimality.

Consequently a convex combination of the listed gradients is zero. Grouping terms yields the multipliers for the objective, active budgets, active cover constraints and active simplex coordinates. The combination cannot be supported solely on active simplex gradients. In each simplex tangent space a direction can increase all its zero coordinates and compensate at one positive coordinate, pairing strictly negatively with every active simplex normal. The objective, budget and cover multipliers are therefore jointly nontrivial. The common gradient choices within each cap are from the compact convex set constructed above. Within each active cover set the representative is obtained from a single exposed point $x_z$.

For $\eta_z>0$, simplex stationarity says that $\lambda_z$ maximizes the linear form with entries $L_i(z,x_z)$ on the simplex. Indeed for any $\lambda'\in\Delta_N$, the active simplex normals pair nonpositively with $\lambda'-\lambda_z$, so stationarity implies $\sum_i(\lambda'_{zi}-\lambda_{zi})L_i(z,x_z)\le0$. The weighted value equals $C_z=0$ because $x_z$ is exposed. This proves the cap contact conditions and the normal-cone inclusion at that same point. The free $b_i$ and $a_i$ stationarity equations are the first two displayed balances. The tangent $u_i$ equation is equivalent to
$$
\alpha_0m_i+r_i m_i^s-\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}x_z+\sigma_i u_i=0
$$
for a real $\sigma_i$. Pair it with $u_i$. The cap relation is $u_i\cdot m_i=\Psi_i+b_i c_i-a_i\cdot P_i$, and on the sections $u_i\cdot m_i^s=b_i S_i-a_i\cdot P_i^s$. At a positive contact coefficient, $u_i\cdot x_z=b_i-a_i\cdot z$. The first two balances cancel all terms involving $a_i,b_i$, giving $\sigma_i=-\alpha_0\Psi_i$. The remaining force identities follow by summing the first balance, either directly or with factors $u_i$.
