## Statement

For a nonempty finite support \(D\subset\mathbb R^n\) and any \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), use the canonical normalization and actual ambient halfspace depths
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in D}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad p_x=e^{\ell\cdot x}/Z,\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^n\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}p(H),
\qquad M_r(p)=\sum_{x\in D}p_x\tau_p(x)^r.
$$
Here \(p(H)=\sum_{y\in D\cap H}p_y\). Every halfspace in the infimum may have arbitrary nonzero normal, and boundary targets are included.

Let
$$
c_2=\frac{\bigl[\log(1+3\tanh^2(1/32))\bigr]^3}{3456},\qquad
\kappa=4^{-1/3},\qquad
C_*=\frac{1+\kappa}{(1-\kappa)^2},\qquad
c_{\mathrm{mask}}=\frac{c_2}{C_*^3}
=c_2\frac{(1-\kappa)^6}{(1+\kappa)^3}>0.
$$
The constant \(c_2\) is exactly that of accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf. The scalar \(\kappa\) is not a probability law.

For every integer \(n\ge1\), let
$$
D_n=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n:x_j=2,\ x_i=0\Longrightarrow j<i\}.
$$
Choose each mask \(A_i\) from
\(\{1\},\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,1,2\}\), and put
\(D=D_n\cap\prod_{i=1}^n A_i\).
This is a complete convex lattice census. For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), with the original law on this entire \(D\),
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{mask}}4^{-n},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{mask}}^{3/2}8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
$$
The bounds hold without restrictions on the number or arrangement of the masks, or the signs, magnitudes, or order of the fields.

There is also the following application. For every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(P\) be the strict partial order on labels \(u_i,v_i\), \(1\le i\le m\), whose relations are exactly
$$
u_i<_P u_j\ (i<j),\qquad
v_i<_P v_j\ (i<j),\qquad
u_i<_P v_j\ (i\le j),
$$
with no relation \(v_i<_P u_j\). Define its entire census by
$$
D_P=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{2m}:
     x_a=2,\ x_b=0\Longrightarrow a<_P b\}.
$$
Then \(D_P\) is a complete convex lattice census, and every original affine law on it satisfies, with \(c_{\mathrm{lad}}=c_{\mathrm{mask}}/8\),
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{lad}}4^{-2m},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{lad}}^{3/2}8^{-2m}.
\tag{2}
$$
The depths in (2) use all containing halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{2m}\).

## Proof

First, each mask is an integer interval \([a_i,b_i]\cap\mathbb Z\), with \(a_i\le1\le b_i\). Consider the compact convex polytope
$$
K=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:a_i\le x_i\le b_i\text{ for all }i,\quad
                       x_j-x_i\le1\text{ for all }i<j\}.
$$
An integral point satisfying the interval bounds has all coordinates in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Within these values, \(x_j-x_i>1\) is equivalent to \(x_i=0,x_j=2\). Consequently \(K\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), including all boundary points. In particular,
\(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), because
\(D\subset\operatorname{conv}(D)\subset K\).
If a full-dimensional convex body realizing the census is desired even when some masks are singletons, replace the bounds by
\(a_i-1/4\le x_i\le b_i+1/4\) and the chain inequalities by
\(x_j-x_i\le5/4\). Integrality gives exactly the same lattice census, and \(\mathbf1\) satisfies all these relaxed inequalities strictly. Thus this bounded polytope has nonempty interior and also realizes the complete census.

We record precisely what deleting fixed-one coordinates does. Let
\(F=\{i:A_i=\{1\}\}\), put \(N=n-|F|\), and write the remaining indices as
\(i_1<\cdots<i_N\). Let \(\iota:\mathbb R^N\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n\) insert value 1 on \(F\) and put \((\iota y)_{i_s}=y_s\). Define
$$
\widehat A_s=A_{i_s},\qquad \widehat\ell_s=\ell_{i_s},\qquad
\widehat D=D_N\cap\prod_{s=1}^N\widehat A_s.
$$
We use \(D_0=\{()\}\). Since a coordinate equal to 1 is neither a 2 nor a 0, \(\iota\) is a bijection from \(\widehat D\) to \(D\). The pushed-forward law \(\widehat p_y=p_{\iota y}\) has fields \(\widehat\ell\) and normalizer
$$
\widehat Z=\sum_{y\in\widehat D}e^{\widehat\ell\cdot y}
          =e^{-\sum_{i\in F}\ell_i}Z.
\tag{3}
$$
For \(N\ge1\), the trace on \(\iota(\mathbb R^N)\) of any original ambient halfspace containing \(\iota y\) is either a closed halfspace containing \(y\), or all of \(\mathbb R^N\). The latter occurs when its restricted normal is zero; its probability is 1. Conversely, every closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^N\) lifts to an ambient closed halfspace by assigning zero normal coefficients on \(F\). It follows in both directions that
\(\tau_p(\iota y)=\tau_{\widehat p}(y)\), and hence their depth moments agree exactly. If \(N=0\), the support is one point and every containing halfspace has mass 1; the depth and both moments are 1. This is also our singleton convention whenever a zero-dimensional factor occurs below. Notice that \(0<c_2<1\): the logarithm in its definition is positive and less than \(\log4<2\).

We construct the cover on \(\widehat D\), while retaining original ambient probabilities on its lifts. Its masks have no singleton. List the proper binary masks at positions
\(t_1<\cdots<t_k\), where \(k\ge0\), and label a binary mask \(H\) if it is \(\{1,2\}\), and \(L\) if it is \(\{0,1\}\). Put \(t_0=0\), \(t_{k+1}=N+1\). For \(0\le j\le k\), the unrestricted run
$$
R_j=\{t_j+1,\ldots,t_{j+1}-1\}
$$
has length \(r_j=t_{j+1}-t_j-1\), which may be zero. Define \(\widehat E_j\) by the following independent restrictions:
on \(R_j\), the coordinates, in their inherited order, range over \(D_{r_j}\);
on every position \(s\le t_j\), they range over \(\widehat A_s\cap\{1,2\}\);
on every position \(s\ge t_{j+1}\), they range over \(\widehat A_s\cap\{0,1\}\).
Let \(E_j=\iota(\widehat E_j)\).

Each outside factor is binary or \(\{1\}\). Exactly the \(L\) masks among \(t_1,\ldots,t_j\) and the \(H\) masks among \(t_{j+1},\ldots,t_k\) become fixed. Thus, among the \(N\) retained coordinates, the number of fixed coordinates is
$$
f_j=\#\{s\le j:t_s\text{ has label }L\}
       +\#\{s>j:t_s\text{ has label }H\},
\tag{4}
$$
and the number of outside binary coordinates is
\(b_j=N-r_j-f_j\).
All components are nonempty, since they contain \(\mathbf1\).

Every \(\widehat E_j\) is contained in \(\widehat D\). Indeed, its prefix has no 0, its suffix has no 2, and within its middle run every 2 precedes every 0. The masks are respected by construction.
Conversely take \(y\in\widehat D\). If it has no 2, it belongs to \(\widehat E_0\). Otherwise let \(a\) be its last position carrying 2. If \(a\in R_j\), every coordinate in the prefix \(s\le t_j\) has no 0, since it precedes this 2, and every coordinate in the suffix \(s\ge t_{j+1}\) has no 2, since it follows \(a\). The middle restriction is inherited from \(y\in D_N\), so \(y\in\widehat E_j\). If \(a=t_s\) is a binary-mask position, use the following run \(R_s\): the prefix through \(t_s\) has no 0, the suffix after that run has no 2, and the run belongs to \(D_{r_s}\). Thus \(y\in\widehat E_s\), even if that run is empty. These cases prove the exact ordinary union
$$
D=\bigcup_{j=0}^k E_j.
\tag{5}
$$
No disjointness is claimed or needed. When \(k=0\), this is the single component with run \(D_N\). When \(N=0\), it is the single singleton component.

We next prove the moment estimate for each component, using its original mass
$$
\alpha_j=p(E_j)=\frac{Z_j}{Z},\qquad
Z_j=\sum_{x\in E_j}e^{\ell\cdot x},
$$
and its conditional law \(q_j(x)=p_x/\alpha_j\) on \(E_j\). All these masses are positive. Let \(G_j\) be the set of the \(f_j\) fixed retained positions in (4), and let \(B_j\) be the \(b_j\) outside binary positions; write their binary supports as \(I_{j,s}\). Define the exact factor normalizers
$$
Z_{B,j}=\prod_{s\in B_j}\left(\sum_{a\in I_{j,s}}e^{\widehat\ell_s a}\right),
\qquad
Z_{R,j}=\sum_{z\in D_{r_j}}
              e^{\sum_{s\in R_j}\widehat\ell_s z_s}.
$$
Here the coordinates \(z_s\) are indexed in the inherited order of \(R_j\); empty products and zero-dimensional sums equal 1. With this indexing understood,
$$
Z_j=
 e^{\sum_{i\in F}\ell_i+\sum_{s\in G_j}\widehat\ell_s}
 Z_{B,j}Z_{R,j},\qquad
\alpha_j=
\frac{e^{\sum_{i\in F}\ell_i+\sum_{s\in G_j}\widehat\ell_s}
 Z_{B,j}Z_{R,j}}{Z}.
\tag{6}
$$
In particular, after suppressing its fixed-one coordinates, \(q_j\) is exactly
$$
q_j(v,z)=q_{B,j}(v)q_{R,j}(z),
\tag{7}
$$
where \(q_{B,j}\) is the product of the normalized binary affine laws with the original fields, and \(q_{R,j}\) is the original-field affine law on \(D_{r_j}\).

Fix a supported target with outside value \(v\) and run value \(z\), and take any ambient halfspace \(H\subset\mathbb R^n\) containing it. Restrict to the affine subspace on which every fixed coordinate is 1 and every outside binary coordinate is \(v\). Its trace in the run variables is a closed halfspace containing \(z\), or all run space when the restricted normal is zero. Therefore
$$
q_j(H)\ge q_{B,j}(v)\tau_{q_{R,j}}(z).
\tag{8}
$$
For an empty run the same assertion holds with \(\tau_{q_{R,j}}=1\), because \(H\) contains the target atom. Taking the infimum over all original ambient \(H\) proves (8) for the actual depth of \(q_j\). Summing its square against (7) gives
$$
M_2(q_j)\ge
\left(\sum_v q_{B,j}(v)^3\right)M_2(q_{R,j}).
\tag{9}
$$
This fixes an outside value in each containing halfspace; it does not multiply the depth moments of two independent factors.

A binary law with probabilities \(s,1-s\) has cubic atom sum
\(s^3+(1-s)^3=1/4+3(s-1/2)^2\ge1/4\).
Independence in \(q_{B,j}\) implies
\(\sum_v q_{B,j}(v)^3\ge4^{-b_j}\).
For \(r_j\ge1\), accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf, applied with dimension \(r_j\) and the restricted original field, gives
\(M_2(q_{R,j})\ge c_2 4^{-r_j}\).
For \(r_j=0\) this remains true because the singleton moment is \(1\ge c_2\). Consequently
$$
M_2(q_j)\ge c_2\,4^{-(b_j+r_j)}
           =c_2\,4^{-(N-f_j)}.
\tag{10}
$$

For completeness, the transfer to the original law uses no change of normalizer or halfspace class. For \(x\in E_j\) and every original ambient containing halfspace \(H\),
$$
p(H)\ge p(H\cap E_j)=\alpha_j q_j(H)
                 \ge\alpha_j\tau_{q_j}(x).
$$
Thus \(\tau_p(x)\ge\alpha_j\tau_{q_j}(x)\), and retaining just the targets in \(E_j\) gives
$$
M_2(p)\ge
\sum_{x\in E_j}p_x\tau_p(x)^2
\ge\alpha_j^3M_2(q_j)
\ge c_2\alpha_j^3\,4^{-(N-f_j)}
\quad(0\le j\le k).
\tag{11}
$$
Each inequality (11) is used separately, so overlapping targets are never counted twice in a lower bound for \(M_2(p)\). All \(\alpha_j\) in (11) are the masses in (6), with the original full \(Z\).

It remains to bound the weighted profile of the cover. If \(0\le j<l\le k\), every binary mask with index \(s\in\{j+1,\ldots,l\}\) contributes once to \(f_j+f_l\): an \(H\) contributes to \(f_j\), and an \(L\) contributes to \(f_l\). More explicitly,
$$
f_j+f_l=(l-j)
 +2\#\{s\le j:t_s\text{ has label }L\}
 +2\#\{s>l:t_s\text{ has label }H\}
\ge l-j.
\tag{12}
$$
Fix a nonnegative integer \(h\). If the set \(\{j:f_j=h\}\) has at least two elements, let \(j,l\) be its smallest and largest. Equation (12) gives \(l-j\le2h\), so the number of these integer indices is at most \(2h+1\). The same bound holds if it has zero or one element. Since \(0<\kappa<1\), it follows that
$$
\sum_{j=0}^k4^{-f_j/3}
 =\sum_{j=0}^k\kappa^{f_j}
 \le\sum_{h=0}^\infty(2h+1)\kappa^h
 =\frac{1+\kappa}{(1-\kappa)^2}=C_*.
\tag{13}
$$
The final identity follows from the geometric series and
\(\sum_{h\ge0}h\kappa^h=\kappa/(1-\kappa)^2\).

Put \(U=4^N M_2(p)\). Equation (11) implies, for every \(j\),
\(\alpha_j\le(U/c_2)^{1/3}4^{-f_j/3}\).
The ordinary cover (5) implies \(\sum_j\alpha_j\ge1\), including overlaps. Hence (13) yields
$$
1\le\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_j
\le (U/c_2)^{1/3}\sum_{j=0}^k4^{-f_j/3}
\le (U/c_2)^{1/3}C_*.
$$
Therefore
$$
M_2(p)\ge\frac{c_2}{C_*^3}4^{-N}
         \ge c_{\mathrm{mask}}4^{-n}.
\tag{14}
$$
Deleting fixed-one coordinates only improves the bound when it is displayed using the original \(n\). All-zero-dimensional factors, the case of no binary masks, the all-fixed support, and \(n=1\) were included. Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\), applied under the entire original law, gives
$$
M_3(p)=\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p^2)^{3/2}]
       \ge M_2(p)^{3/2}.
\tag{15}
$$
Combining (14) and (15) proves (1).

We now prove the ladder application, including its complete support description. Write \(\mathcal I=\{u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_m,v_m\}\). The stated relations form a strict partial order: relations within each row are increasing in the column index; a relation crossing from the \(u\) row to the \(v\) row has nondecreasing column index; and no relation crosses back. These observations verify transitivity and exclude cycles. Define
$$
K_P=\{x\in[0,2]^{\mathcal I}:
       x_a-x_b\le1\text{ for every distinct }a,b\in\mathcal I
                               \text{ with }a\not<_P b\}.
$$
At an integral point in the box, an inequality indexed by \((a,b)\) fails exactly when \(x_a=2,x_b=0\). Thus
\(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^{2m}=D_P\), and
\(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^{2m}=D_P\).
The point \(\mathbf1\) is strictly within the box and satisfies every displayed difference inequality strictly, so \(K_P\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

Let \(T\) be the total order
\(u_1,v_1,u_2,v_2,\ldots,u_m,v_m\), and let \(D_T\) be \(D_{2m}\) in that order. Every \(P\)-relation is a \(T\)-relation. Consequently \(D_P\subset D_T\). Also, a point of \(D_P\) cannot have both some \(v_i=2\) and some \(u_j=0\), because \(v_i\not<_P u_j\) for every \(i,j\). Define the two masked-chain supports
$$
A=D_T\cap\{x:x_{v_i}\in\{0,1\}\text{ for every }i\},\qquad
B=D_T\cap\{x:x_{u_i}\in\{1,2\}\text{ for every }i\}.
$$
The preceding observations prove \(D_P\subset A\cup B\).

For the reverse inclusion, take \(x\in A\) and any coordinates carrying 2 and 0. The 2 must occur at some \(u_i\). If the 0 occurs at \(u_j\), the \(D_T\) condition implies \(i<j\), hence \(u_i<_P u_j\). If the 0 occurs at \(v_j\), the \(D_T\) condition implies \(i\le j\), hence \(u_i<_P v_j\). This includes the allowed equal-column pair \(u_i<_P v_i\). Therefore \(A\subset D_P\).
For \(x\in B\), any 0 occurs at some \(v_j\). A 2 at \(u_i\) precedes \(v_j\) in \(T\) exactly when \(i\le j\), giving \(u_i<_P v_j\). A 2 at \(v_i\) precedes \(v_j\) exactly when \(i<j\), giving \(v_i<_P v_j\). Thus \(B\subset D_P\), and we have proved
$$
D_P=A\cup B.
\tag{16}
$$

Give this entire \(D_P\) its original law \(p_x=e^{\ell\cdot x}/Z\). By (16), one of \(A,B\), denoted \(E\), has original mass \(\alpha=p(E)\ge1/2\). Its conditional law is exactly \(q_x=e^{\ell\cdot x}/Z_E\) on that full masked chain, where
\(Z_E=\sum_{x\in E}e^{\ell\cdot x}\) and \(\alpha=Z_E/Z\).
The same containing-halfspace transfer used in (11), now in \(\mathbb R^{2m}\), gives
$$
M_2(p)\ge\alpha^3M_2(q)
\ge\frac18\,c_{\mathrm{mask}}4^{-2m}.
$$
Applying (15) under the original \(p\) proves (2).
There is no bound on \(m\) or any field. The poset is the union of its two row chains and thus has width at most two, with width exactly two when \(m\ge2\), since \(v_1\) and \(u_2\) are incomparable. Its height is unbounded, since its \(u\) row is a chain of length \(m\). For \(m\ge3\), it is not a total order of incomparable blocks: \(v_1\) is incomparable with both \(u_2\) and \(u_3\), although \(u_2<_P u_3\). This establishes the claimed ladder family beyond ordered blocks.
