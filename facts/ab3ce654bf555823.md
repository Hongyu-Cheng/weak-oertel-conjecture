## Statement

Let \(n\ge100\) be an integer, let \(k\) and \(\beta\) be rational numbers with \(1\le k\le n/100\) and \(0\le\beta\le2/5\), and put \(R=n/2+k\). Indices on \(n\)-vectors are cyclic, so \(t_0=t_n\). Set \(d=2n\), write the continuous coordinate as \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n\), and define

\[
C_\beta=\left\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t):
\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0,\quad
y_i\ge\left|z_i-\frac12-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})\right|\ (1\le i\le n),\quad
\sum_i y_i+\sum_i t_i\le R\right\}.
\]

Let \(S=C_\beta\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{2n})\), \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{(\mathbf y,\mathbf t):(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\in C_\beta\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_{2n}(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_{2n}(S)\), and \(T=2^{-n}W\). The depth \(h_S(\mathbf p)\) is the infimum of \(\mathcal H_{2n}(S\cap H)\) over closed ambient halfspaces \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p\). These objects and normalizations agree exactly with accepted fact \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\): its actual body \(C\) is our \(C_\beta\), its continuous variable \(x\) is our ordered pair \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\), and its \(d\) is \(2n\). All logarithms below are natural, and \((a)_+=\max\{a,0\}\).

The body \(C_\beta\) is a full-dimensional rational simplex whose ambient centroid has base projection \((1/2)\mathbf1_n\). For every integer base put \(\eta_{\mathbf z}=\sum_i(|z_i-1/2|-1/2)\). The complete nonempty support is exactly \(\eta_{\mathbf z}\le k\), and the positive-volume support is exactly \(\eta_{\mathbf z}<k\). Its actual volume table is

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)
&=\sum_i t_i+\sum_i\left|z_i-\frac12-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})\right|,\\
w_{\mathbf z}
&=\frac1{n!}\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}
\bigl(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\bigr)_+^n\,d\mathbf t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

If \(\eta_{\mathbf z}=k\), the fiber consists of the single point \(((|z_i-1/2|)_{i=1}^n,\mathbf0)\), and the corresponding ambient point \((\mathbf z,(|z_i-1/2|)_{i=1}^n,\mathbf0)\) has actual depth zero. All these boundary fibers remain in \(S\). Every Boolean fiber is positive, so \(W>0\).

There exists one point \(\mathbf p^\star\in S\), chosen before the halfspace, for which every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf p^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_{2n}(S\cap H)
\ge\frac{1}{25\cdot3^{16}}\,T.
\tag{2}
\]

The constant belongs to \((0,1/e)\) and is independent of \(n,k,\beta\). The conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and halfspaces whose boundary contains entire fibers. With \(\rho=n/k\) and \(M=ne^{-2\rho}\), the integer fiber may be chosen as follows. If \(\beta\ge2e^{-\rho}\), use a Boolean base whose signs have the maximum number of cyclic changes. If \(\beta<2e^{-\rho}\), use the zero base. In the latter case, \(M\le1\) is handled by cutoff zero; for \(M>1\), the proof uses the fixed cutoff \(L=8\rho\lfloor ne^{-\rho}/4\rfloor+4M+1\). The continuous point is supplied by the accepted fixed-center consumer at the chosen base and cutoff.

## Proof

The nonnegative simplex \(\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t)\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i(u_i+v_i+t_i)\le R\}\) maps onto \(C_\beta\) by the affine map and inverse

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_i&=\frac12+u_i-v_i+\beta(t_i-t_{i-1}),
&y_i&=u_i+v_i,\\
u_i&=\frac{y_i+z_i-1/2-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})}{2},
&v_i&=\frac{y_i-z_i+1/2+\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The displayed inverse makes the map bijective, and its linear determinant has absolute value \(2^n\). Thus \(C_\beta\) is a compact full-dimensional rational simplex in \(\mathbb R^{3n}\). Every latent coordinate at the simplex centroid equals \(R/(3n+1)\). The image centroid therefore has every base coordinate exactly \(1/2\).

At fixed \(\mathbf t\), subtract the absolute values in the body definition from the coordinates \(y_i\). The remaining \(\mathbf y\)-section is a nonnegative \(n\)-simplex of radius \(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\), or is empty when that radius is negative. Its \(n\)-volume is \((R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t))_+^n/n!\), including radius zero. Fubini proves (1). For a uniform sample from a positive fiber, the marginal density of \(\mathbf t\) is \((R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t))_+^n/(n!w_{\mathbf z})\); conditional on a positive remaining radius, the residual \(\mathbf y\)-slacks are uniform on the indicated simplex.

The triangle inequality and \(\sum_i|t_i-t_{i-1}|\le2\sum_i t_i\) give
\(F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\ge n/2+\eta_{\mathbf z}+(1-2\beta)\sum_i t_i\).
Since \(1-2\beta\ge1/5>0\), its minimum is \(n/2+\eta_{\mathbf z}\), attained at \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\). If \(\eta_{\mathbf z}>k\), the fiber is empty. If \(\eta_{\mathbf z}=k\), feasibility forces \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\) and then every \(y_i=|z_i-1/2|\), proving the asserted singleton description. If \(\eta_{\mathbf z}<k\), take all \(t_i\) equal to a sufficiently small positive number. Their cyclic differences vanish, and there is room for every residual \(y_i\)-slack to be strictly positive while the total-budget inequality remains strict. Thus this fiber has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{2n}\). This proves the complete support and positivity statements. Each \(\eta_{\mathbf z}\) is a nonnegative integer, so the support is finite; every Boolean base has height zero and positive volume.

For a boundary fiber with \(\eta_{\mathbf z}=k\), set \(s_i=\operatorname{sign}(z_i-1/2)\). The closed vertical halfspace \(\sum_i s_i(x_i-1/2)\ge R\) contains its point. Every nonempty fiber it meets has \(\sum_i|x_i-1/2|\le R\) by the just-proved support formula, while this halfspace forces the reverse inequality. Hence each such fiber has \(\eta_{\mathbf x}=k\) and zero volume. There are finitely many of them, so the halfspace has mixed volume zero. This proves actual depth zero at every nonempty zero-volume fiber without deleting any point from \(S\).

We use the fixed-center clause of accepted fact \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\). At a positive integer center \(\mathbf j\), retain its definitions \(\alpha(\mathbf v)=\min\{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(\mathbf v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)=\log(w_{\mathbf j}^2/(w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}))\). The cutoff sum is \(B_{\mathbf j}^\Delta(L)=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf j},\,\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)\le L}\alpha(\mathbf v)\), for \(L\ge0\). All sums are oriented and include zero. That accepted fact supplies the midpoint hypothesis for this actual volume table, so its curvatures are nonnegative. Its equation (3) supplies one point in \(\{\mathbf j\}\times C_{\mathbf j}\), fixed before all ambient halfspaces, with depth at least \(B_{\mathbf j}^\Delta(L)/[12(1+L)]\). Its hypotheses hold because \(C_\beta\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body and \(W>0\). We will fix the center and cutoff using the estimates below. In particular, cutoff zero always gives depth at least \(w_{\mathbf j}/12\), because \(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf0)=0\) and \(\alpha(\mathbf0)=w_{\mathbf j}\).

Put \(\rho=n/k\ge100\), and for \(0\le j<k\) put \(V_j=(k-j)^{2n}/(2n)!\). These \(V_j\) are auxiliary radial volumes, not actual coupled fibers or thinned source weights. At a positive integer fiber set \(s_i=\operatorname{sign}(z_i-1/2)\), and let \(r(\mathbf s)\) be the number of indices with \(s_i\ne s_{i+1}\). Put \(a_\beta=(1-4\beta^2)^{-1/2}\), and for \(\beta>0\) put \(\delta_\beta=\exp[-\rho(1-2\beta)/(4\beta)]\), with \(\delta_0=0\). We claim the uniform comparison

\[
\frac{24}{25}e^{-2n\delta_\beta}
a_\beta^{r(\mathbf s)}V_{\eta_{\mathbf z}}
\le w_{\mathbf z}
\le a_\beta^{r(\mathbf s)}V_{\eta_{\mathbf z}}.
\tag{4}
\]

To prove its upper bound, use the supporting sign of each absolute value. Telescoping gives

\[
F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)
\ge\frac n2+\eta_{\mathbf z}+\sum_i c_i t_i,
\qquad
c_i=1-\beta(s_i-s_{i+1})>0.
\tag{5}
\]

The numbers of changes from plus to minus and from minus to plus are equal on a cycle. Thus \(\prod_i c_i=(1-4\beta^2)^{r(\mathbf s)/2}\). Substituting (5) into (1) and scaling each \(t_i\) by \(c_i\) gives \(w_{\mathbf z}\le V_{\eta_{\mathbf z}}/\prod_i c_i\), the upper bound in (4).

For the lower bound, first suppose \(\beta>0\). If every \(t_i\le1/(2\beta)\), then \(|\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})|\le1/2\). Since \(|z_i-1/2|\ge1/2\), equality holds in every supporting-sign inequality in (5), including a possible zero residual. On this region the actual fiber is a restriction of the simplex in coordinates \(c_i t_i\) and the \(n\) nonnegative residual \(\mathbf y\)-slacks, with radius \(h=k-\eta_{\mathbf z}\). The shear in the \(\mathbf y\)-coordinates has determinant one, and the \(\mathbf t\)-scaling has factor \(1/\prod_i c_i\). It suffices to lower-bound the fraction of the uniform \(2n\)-simplex of radius \(h\) on which every \(c_i t_i\le c_i/(2\beta)\).

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{n+1}\) be independent exponential variables of mean one, and let \(E\) be their sum. The vector consisting of \(hX_i/E\) and the first \(n\) coordinates \(hY_i/E\) is uniform on that simplex. Indeed, changing variables from the exponential coordinates to their sum and their first \(2n\) normalized coordinates has Jacobian \(E^{2n}\). Integrating \(E^{2n}e^{-E}\) gives \((2n)!\) by integration by parts. After the radius scaling the density is \((2n)!/h^{2n}\), as required.

Put \(A_\beta=\rho(1-2\beta)/(4\beta)\). On the event \(X_i\le A_\beta\) for every \(i\) and \(\sum_iY_i\ge n/2\), one has
\(hX_i/E\le2hA_\beta/n\le(1-2\beta)/(2\beta)\le c_i/(2\beta)\), because \(h\le k\). The two events are independent. The sum of the \(Y_i\) has mean \(n+1\) and variance \(n+1\), so its squared deviation gives
\(\mathbb P(\sum_iY_i<n/2)\le4(n+1)/(n+2)^2\le4/n\le1/25\).
Consequently the desired volume fraction is at least \((24/25)(1-e^{-A_\beta})^n\).

Since \(\rho\ge100\) and \(1-2\beta\ge1/5\), one has \(A_\beta\ge5/\beta\ge25/2\). Thus \(\delta_\beta=e^{-A_\beta}<1/2\), and the integral formula for \(-\log(1-t)\) gives \((1-\delta_\beta)^n\ge e^{-2n\delta_\beta}\). This proves the lower bound in (4). At \(\beta=0\), equality \(w_{\mathbf z}=V_{\eta_{\mathbf z}}\) follows directly from (1), so (4) also holds. The bound keeps the accumulated sign-crossing tail loss for arbitrarily large \(n\); it never assumes that \(n\delta_\beta\) is small.

We need the following elementary inequalities throughout \(0\le\beta\le2/5\):

\[
\delta_\beta\le\frac{\beta^2}{100},
\qquad
1\le a_\beta\le\frac53,
\qquad
\log\frac{2a_\beta}{1+a_\beta}\ge\beta^2,
\qquad
\log\frac{1+a_\beta}{2}\le3\beta^2.
\tag{6}
\]

For the first inequality when \(\beta>0\), \(A_\beta\ge5/\beta\), and \(e^{-5/\beta}/\beta^2\) is increasing on \((0,2/5]\), as differentiation of its logarithm shows. At \(\beta=2/5\) its value is \((25/4)e^{-25/2}<1/100\), since \(e>2\) gives \(e^{25/2}>2^{12}>625\). The case \(\beta=0\) and the upper bound on \(a_\beta\) are immediate. For the third inequality, the function \(x\mapsto\log(2/(1+\sqrt{1-4x}))\) vanishes at zero and has derivative \(2/[\sqrt{1-4x}(1+\sqrt{1-4x})]\ge1\). Apply this for \(0\le x\le4/25\). Finally, \(\log((1+a)/2)\le(a-1)/2\), and
\((a_\beta-1)/2=2\beta^2/[\sqrt{1-4\beta^2}(1+\sqrt{1-4\beta^2})]\le(25/12)\beta^2<3\beta^2\).

We now bound the full actual normalization. At integer height \(j\ge0\), there are \(\binom{n+j-1}{n-1}\) nonnegative integer magnitude vectors with sum \(j\). Each sign pattern determines exactly one integer base from such a vector: its coordinate with magnitude index \(b_i\ge0\) is \(1+b_i\) for a plus sign and \(-b_i\) for a minus sign. The sum of \(a^{r(\mathbf s)}\) over all sign patterns on the cycle is \((1+a)^n+(1-a)^n\). This is the trace of the \(n\)-th power of the matrix with diagonal entries one and off-diagonal entries \(a\), whose eigenvalues are \(1+a\) and \(1-a\). With \(\varepsilon=e^{-\rho}\) and \(M=n\varepsilon^2\), (4) therefore yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T}{V_0}
&\le\frac{(1+a_\beta)^n+(1-a_\beta)^n}{2^n}
\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\\j<k}}
\binom{n+j-1}{n-1}(1-j/k)^{2n}\\
&\le2\left(\frac{1+a_\beta}{2}\right)^n(1-\varepsilon^2)^{-n}\\
&\le2\exp\!\left(n\log\frac{1+a_\beta}{2}+2M\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

To justify the second line, \((1-j/k)^{2n}\le e^{-2\rho j}\), and extending the nonnegative height series gives \(\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+j-1}{n-1}\varepsilon^{2j}=(1-\varepsilon^2)^{-n}\). This identity follows by multiplying \(n\) geometric series. Also \((1+a)^n+(1-a)^n\le2(1+a)^n\) for \(a\ge1\), including odd \(n\). The last line follows from \(-\log(1-t)\le2t\) for \(0\le t\le1/2\). Every positive height \(j<k\) is included. Heights \(j=k\), when present, have zero volume, and heights \(j>k\) have empty fibers. Thus (7) keeps the entire actual \(W\).

First suppose \(2\varepsilon\le\beta\le2/5\). Choose a Boolean base \(\mathbf j\) whose signs alternate on as many cyclic edges as possible. Its sign-change count is \(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\ge n-1\), and its height is zero. By (4), (6), and (7),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_{\mathbf j}}{T}
&\ge\frac{12}{25a_\beta}
\exp\!\left(n\left[\log\frac{2a_\beta}{1+a_\beta}
-2\delta_\beta-2\varepsilon^2\right]\right)\\
&\ge\frac{36}{125}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The exponent is nonnegative because its bracket is at least \(\beta^2-\beta^2/50-\beta^2/2\). The accepted cutoff-zero consumer at this fixed Boolean center supplies a point with depth at least \(w_{\mathbf j}/12\ge(3/125)T\). This is stronger than (2).

It remains to treat \(0\le\beta<2\varepsilon\). The last inequality of (6) gives \(\log((1+a_\beta)/2)\le12\varepsilon^2\). Also \(\delta_\beta\le\varepsilon^2\). For \(\beta>0\), this follows from
\(A_\beta\ge\rho/(20\beta)>\rho/(40\varepsilon)\ge2\rho\); the bound \(\varepsilon\le1/80\) follows from \(e^\rho\ge1+\rho\ge101\). It is immediate for \(\beta=0\). The zero base has no sign changes, so

\[
T\le2V_0e^{14M},
\qquad
\frac{24}{25}V_0e^{-2M}\le w_{\mathbf0}\le V_0.
\tag{9}
\]

If \(M\le1\), the cutoff-zero consumer at the zero base gives a point with
\(h_S(\mathbf p^\star)/T\ge w_{\mathbf0}/(12T)\ge e^{-16}/25>1/(25\cdot3^{16})\).
Here \(e<3\) follows by bounding its exponential series after the first two terms by a geometric series. This proves (2) in this regime.

Suppose now that \(M>1\). Put \(\ell=\lfloor n\varepsilon/4\rfloor\), and let \(\mathcal V\) consist of the integer displacement vectors with exactly \(\ell\) entries equal to one, exactly \(\ell\) entries equal to minus one, and every other entry zero. The following finite inequalities justify this shell:

\[
\frac{n\varepsilon}{8}\le\ell\le\frac{n\varepsilon}{4},
\qquad
\ell\ge\rho^2,
\qquad
\frac{\ell}{k}\le\frac{\rho\varepsilon}{4}<\frac12,
\qquad
\rho^2\varepsilon\le\frac1{100},
\qquad
128\varepsilon\le\frac1{100}.
\tag{10}
\]

Indeed, \(M>1\) gives \(n\varepsilon>1/\varepsilon=e^\rho\), so the quantity being floored is greater than two and its floor is at least half of it. Thus \(\ell\ge n\varepsilon/8>e^\rho/8\ge\rho^2\), where the cubic term of the exponential series gives \(e^\rho/8\ge\rho^3/48\ge\rho^2\). The quartic term gives \(\rho^2e^{-\rho}\le24/\rho^2\le1/100\) and \(128e^{-\rho}\le3072/\rho^4\le1/100\). The other assertions follow. In particular \(2\ell\le n\) and \(\ell<k\), so \(\mathcal V\) is nonempty and both endpoints of every selected displacement are positive fibers at center zero.

For every \(\mathbf v\in\mathcal V\), the two heights \(\eta_{\mathbf v}\) and \(\eta_{-\mathbf v}\) both equal \(\ell\). Drop the nonnegative sign-change improvement in (4), and write \(u=\ell/k\). The resulting bounds on actual endpoint weights and on the full actual curvature are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(\mathbf v)
&\ge\frac{24}{25}e^{-2M}V_0(1-u)^{2n},\\
\Delta_{\mathbf0}(\mathbf v)
&\le4n\log\frac1{1-u}+4M+2\log\frac{25}{24}
\le L:=8\rho\ell+4M+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The curvature numerator uses \(w_{\mathbf0}\le V_0\) from (9), and the denominator uses the two actual endpoint lower bounds. The last inequality uses \(-\log(1-u)\le2u\) for \(u\le1/2\) and \(2\log(25/24)<1\). All curvatures are nonnegative by the accepted actual-body clause, so the cutoff is admissible. No minimum-endpoint surrogate replaces the curvature.

The oriented shell has size \(|\mathcal V|=\binom n{2\ell}\binom{2\ell}\ell\ge(n/(2\ell))^{2\ell}\). For \(1\le r\le n\), the inequality \(\binom nr\ge(n/r)^r\) follows by comparing each factor \((n-r+i)/i\) with \(n/r\), for \(1\le i\le r\). The second binomial factor is at least one. Hence, using (9) and (11),

\[
\frac{B_{\mathbf0}^\Delta(L)}{(1+L)T}
\ge\frac{12}{25}
\frac{\exp\!\left(2\ell\log\frac{n}{2\ell}
+2n\log(1-u)-16M\right)}{1+L}.
\tag{12}
\]

No division by two occurs in this oriented cutoff sum. All omitted terms, including zero, are nonnegative. For \(0\le u\le1/2\), differentiation of \(\log(1-u)+u+u^2\) gives \(u(1-2u)/(1-u)\ge0\), so \(\log(1-u)\ge-u-u^2\). Also \(n/(2\ell)\ge2/\varepsilon=2e^\rho\). By (10),
\(2nu^2\le(\rho^2\varepsilon/2)\ell\le\ell/200\) and \(16M\le128\varepsilon\ell\le\ell/100\).
Consequently the exponent in (12) is at least \(\ell/2\), since \(2\log2>1\). The latter strict inequality follows from \(\log2=\int_1^2x^{-1}\,dx>1/2\).

Furthermore, \(1+L=8\rho\ell+4M+2\le10\rho\ell\), using \(M\le8\varepsilon\ell\) and (10). The quadratic term of the exponential series gives \(e^{\ell/2}\ge\ell^2/8\). Thus

\[
\frac{B_{\mathbf0}^\Delta(L)}{(1+L)T}
\ge\frac{12}{25}\frac{e^{\ell/2}}{10\rho\ell}
\ge\frac{12}{25}\frac{\ell}{80\rho}
\ge\frac35.
\tag{13}
\]

The accepted fixed-center consumer at the already chosen center zero and cutoff \(L\) now gives one point with depth at least \(B_{\mathbf0}^\Delta(L)/[12(1+L)]\ge T/20\). This completes (2) in the last parameter regime. The three regimes exhaust every allowed \(n,k,\beta\), including \(\beta=0\), \(\beta=2/5\), \(\beta=2e^{-\rho}\) whenever rational, and \(M=1\).

In every application, the center, cutoff, and continuous point are fixed before the ambient halfspace. The accepted conclusion quantifies over every closed containing halfspace and includes vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing entire fibers. Every positive actual fiber was included in (7), and the boundary fibers proved to have zero volume remain in \(S\). Thus the point and normalization in (2) have exactly the locked actual-depth meanings.

The proof is uniform when \(\beta\) varies with \(n,k\). Its crossover is \(\beta=2e^{-n/k}\), and its small-coupling normalization parameter is \(ne^{-2n/k}\), reflecting the actual radial exponent \(2n\). For example, at \(\beta=0\), \(n=m4^m\), \(k=4^m\), with integer \(m\ge100\), that parameter is \(m(4/e^2)^m\to0\), so this sequence falls in the heavy-zero-fiber regime. When the parameter exceeds one, (10)-(13) give the reflected-shell rescue. These are consequences of the uniform theorem and require no fixed-parameter assumption.
