## Statement

Let $Q\subset\mathbb R^k$ be a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone, $k\ge1$, and $v\in\operatorname{int} Q$. Write $Q^*=\{b:b\cdot q\ge0\ (q\in Q)\}$ and $J_Q(b)=\int_Q e^{-b\cdot q}\,dq$. The minimum $J_*(v)=\min_{b\in\operatorname{int}Q^*,\, b\cdot v=1}J_Q(b)$ is positive, finite, and uniquely attained at $n_v$. The probability with density $e^{-n_v\cdot q}/J_*(v)$ on $Q$ has mean $kv$.

## Proof

Zero does not belong to the compact convex hull of $Q\cap S^{k-1}$: a convex combination giving zero would imply that both a nonzero cone vector and its negative belong to $Q$. The nearest point of that hull to zero therefore gives a linear functional strictly positive on $Q\cap S^{k-1}$. For every $b\in\operatorname{int}Q^*$ this strict positivity gives $b\cdot q\ge c|q|$ on $Q$ for some $c>0$, hence finite positive $J_Q(b)$. Rescaling such a $b$ makes $b\cdot v=1$.

Choose $r>0$ with $v+rB_k\subset Q$. On the closed normalized dual slice $1=b\cdot v\ge r|b|$, so it is compact. At a dual boundary point there is a nonzero $w\in Q$ with $b\cdot w=0$. Disjoint translates of any fixed interior ball by spaced positive multiples of $w$ stay in $Q$ and give equal positive integrals. Thus $J_Q(b)=\infty$ there, and Fatou excludes such a point as a finite minimizing-sequence limit. Interior continuity and strict convexity follow by differentiating under a locally uniform exponential bound; the Hessian is $\int_Q qq^{\mathsf T}e^{-b\cdot q}\,dq$, positive definite by full dimension. This proves unique attainment. Homogeneity gives $J_Q(tb)=t^{-k}J_Q(b)$. At $n_v$, constrained stationarity makes $-\nabla\log J_Q(n_v)$ a multiple of $v$, and homogeneity gives its dot product with $n_v$ as $k$. This gradient is the claimed mean.
