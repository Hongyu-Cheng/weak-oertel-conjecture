## Statement

Let $q_i,f_i>0$ for $i=1,2,3$. Assume
$$
\frac{20}{49}\le q_i\le\frac{128}{279},\qquad
\sum_i q_i\le\frac{125}{93},\qquad
\sum_i f_i\sqrt{q_i}=2,\qquad
 f_i+\frac{(f_j+f_k)^2}{4}\le2\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).
$$
Then $(f_i/\sqrt{q_i})/\sum_\ell(f_\ell/\sqrt{q_\ell})>223/1000=2/9+7/9000$ for every $i$.

## Proof

Put $\theta=223/1000$, $c=128/279$, $q_0=119/279$, $s=\sqrt c$, $m=\sqrt{20/49}$, and $\rho=\sqrt{q_0}$. By symmetry suppose the first normalized quantity is at most $\theta$. Write $x=f_1$, $t=\sqrt{q_1}$, $g=f_2+f_3$, and $F=x+g$.

Summing the row inequalities yields $F+(F^2+\sum_i f_i^2)/4\le6$. Since $\sum_i f_i^2\ge F^2/3$, we have $F\le3$. The moment identity gives $F\ge2/s>59/20$, using $4/c-(59/20)^2=13/800$. The first normalized quantity is at least $xm/(sF)$ because its numerator is at least $x/s$ and its denominator is at most $F/m$. Hence $x\le3s\theta/m<71/100$, using
$$
(71/100)^2-9\theta^2 c/(20/49)=40107/38750000>0 .
$$

We claim that $f_j/s>x/\rho$ for both $j=2,3$. If $f_j\le(s/\rho)x$, then $T=x+f_j<3621/2500<29/20$, since $s/\rho=\sqrt{128/119}<26/25$. The row inequality for the remaining index gives $F\le T+2-T^2/4<4679/1600<59/20$, since $T+2-T^2/4$ is strictly increasing on $(0,2)$. This contradicts the lower bound on $F$.

Apply the square-root tangents at $q_0$ for $q_1$ and at $c$ for $q_2,q_3$. The latter two increments $q_j-c$ are nonpositive, so $f_j/s>x/\rho$ yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
2&\le x\rho+gs+\frac{x}{2\rho}(q_1-q_0)
             +\sum_{j=2,3}\frac{f_j}{2s}(q_j-c)\\
 &\le x\rho+gs+\frac{x}{2\rho}\left(\sum_i q_i-q_0-2c\right)
 \le x\rho+gs .
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step uses $q_0+2c=125/93$. The row inequality for index $1$ gives $g\le2\sqrt{2-x}$, so $2\le x\rho+2s\sqrt{2-x}$. The derivative of this last right-hand side is $\rho-s/\sqrt{2-x}>0$ on $(0,71/100]$, because $(129/100)q_0-c=2551/27900>0$. At $x=67/100$ the right-hand side is strictly below $2$. Indeed,
$$
\rho<\frac{65309}{100000},\qquad
s<\frac{33867}{50000},\qquad
\sqrt{133/100}<\frac{57663}{50000},
$$
as follows by squaring, and substituting these upper bounds leaves a gap of $10269/78125000$ below $2$. We obtain $67/100<x<71/100$.

For $m\le r\le s$, the reciprocal secant $1/r\le(m+s-r)/(ms)$ follows from $(r-m)(s-r)\ge0$. Apply it at $r=\sqrt{q_2},\sqrt{q_3}$ and use the moment identity, the row bound on $g$, and $t\le s$ to obtain
$$
Z:=\frac{f_2}{\sqrt{q_2}}+\frac{f_3}{\sqrt{q_3}}
\le\frac{(m+s)g-2+xt}{ms}
\le\frac{2(m+s)\sqrt{2-x}-2+xs}{ms}.
$$
Define $G(x)=4+x[(1554/223)m-2s]-4(m+s)\sqrt{2-x}$. The coefficient $(1554/223)m-2s$ is positive, as follows by squaring, and makes $G$ strictly increasing on $(0,2)$. At $x=67/100$, the coefficient of $m$ remains positive with $\sqrt{2-x}$ replaced by $57663/50000$, since $(1554/223)(67/100)-4(57663/50000)=155901/2787500>0$. Using $m>63887/100000$ by squaring, together with the previous upper bounds on $s$ and the radical, gives
$$
G(67/100)>\frac{975345521}{278750000000}>0 .
$$
Thus $G(x)>0$. Since $1554/223=2(1/\theta-1)$, the bound on $Z$ gives $Z<(1/\theta-1)x/s\le(1/\theta-1)x/t$. It follows that $(x/t)/(x/t+Z)>\theta$, a contradiction.
