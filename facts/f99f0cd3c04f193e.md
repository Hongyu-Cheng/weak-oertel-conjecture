## Statement

Let $\eta$ be a finite nonzero Borel measure on a compact physical set, let $W_1,\ldots,W_N$ be fixed nonnegative bounded Borel profiles, $N\ge1$, and let the test cone consist of nonnegative continuous convex functions and be closed under nonnegative linear combinations. The same conclusion holds for a cone of continuous normal-dependent potentials convex in the physical variables at each fixed normal on the marked space. If tests $\Phi_i$ have $\int\Phi_i\,d\eta=1$ and $\int\Phi_iW_i\,d\eta\ge\gamma_i>0$, then the single test $\Phi=\sum_i\Phi_i/\gamma_i$ satisfies
$$
\frac{\int\Phi W_i\,d\eta}{\int\Phi\,d\eta}\ge
\left(\sum_j\gamma_j^{-1}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{for all }i.
$$
If the individual suprema are at least a common $\Gamma>0$, then for every $0<\epsilon<\Gamma$ one normalized common test pays at least $(\Gamma-\epsilon)/N$ on every profile. For any fixed finite list of normalized tests $\Phi_j$, the exact best common minimum supplied by their convex hull is the finite linear program $\max_{a\ge0,\sum_j a_j=1}\min_i\sum_j a_jA_{ij}$ with $A_{ij}=\int\Phi_jW_i\,d\eta$.

## Proof

The sum has denominator $\sum_j\gamma_j^{-1}$. Each $i$th numerator receives at least one from its own summand. Every cross term is nonnegative. This proves the reciprocal-sum guarantee, with equality in a given row exactly when its diagonal is equal to the stated lower bound and all cross integrals in that row vanish. Under the supremum premise choose tests paying at least $\Gamma-\epsilon$, after normalizing each denominator to one, and use their average. For the finite menu, every convex combination retains denominator one and has numerator vector $Aa$. Maximizing the smallest row is the displayed program, or maximize a scalar bounded above by all the rows. It attains by compactness of the coefficient simplex.
