## Statement

For a one-sided family whose actual mixed candidate bases are drawn from one fixed finite set, suppose the total mass is $M(t)=M_0+t m+o(t)$, $M_0>0$, and the restricted raw maximum $Q_p(t)$ at each indexed base is defined and continuous at zero. Put $Q_0=\max_p Q_p(0)$, $D_0=Q_0/M_0$, and $I=\{p:Q_p(0)=Q_0\}$. If for each $p\in I$ one has $Q_p(t)\le Q_0+t a_p+o(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$, then for $D(t)=\max_p Q_p(t)/M(t)$,
$$
\limsup_{t\downarrow0}\frac{D(t)-D_0}{t}\le
\frac{\max_{p\in I}a_p-D_0m}{M_0}.
$$
In particular a strict negative numerator implies $D(t)<D_0$ for all sufficiently small positive $t$.

## Proof

If there are inactive bases, their maximum at zero is below $Q_0$ by a positive gap. Continuity of the finitely many functions, including the active ones, preserves the gap between inactive and active maxima for sufficiently small $t\ge0$. Hence
$$
\max_p Q_p(t)=\max_{p\in I}Q_p(t)\le Q_0+t\max_{p\in I}a_p+o(t).
$$
Indeed the maximum of the absolute errors in a finite family of $o(t)$ remainders is $o(t)$. Subtract $D_0 M(t)$, divide by $tM(t)$ for small $t>0$, and take the limit superior. A negative upper bound on that limit superior implies the stated strict comparison.
