## Statement

Let $C=[-1,2]\times[0,1]$ carry the sum of lengths on the four integer fibers. The actual winner cells of $L_0=3/5-z/5-x$ and $L_1=-L_0$ each have mass $2$ and have centroids $y_0=(0,3/10)$ and $y_1=(1,7/10)$. They attain minimum mass-weighted squared base centroid among actual partitions with integral base centroids. The centered own-score caps both have mass $9/10<\mu(C)/4$, lie within their own winner cell on $C$, and do not contain the opposite centroid. Their score gaps at the two centroids are $3/5$ and their cross-margin sum is $-6/5$.

Keeping the same continuous normals but changing the integer coefficients to $1/2,-1/2$, respectively, yields two caps of mass $21/10$ each whose union covers $C$. Both cross margins are $1/10$, and their sum equals the original $-6/5$ plus the integer correction $7/5$. Furthermore, with these same integer coefficients and two opposite unit continuous normals at each center, weighting the normals equally yields zero continuous-normal average and zero first moment of every margin on its own cell, yet the base circulation on the two-cycle is $1$. The four cap masses in label and sign order $(0,+),(0,-),(1,+),(1,-)$ are $29/10,21/10,21/10,29/10$.

## Proof

Write $s(z)=3/5-z/5$. Cell zero takes $0\le x\le s(z)$ and cell one takes $s(z)\le x\le 1$. The four values of $s$ are $4/5,3/5,2/5,1/5$. They sum to $2$, their $z$-weighted sum is zero, and their squared sum divided by two is $3/5$. Thus the mass and both moments of cell zero give $y_0$. The measure-preserving reflection $(z,x)\mapsto(1-z,1-x)$ exchanges the cells and gives $y_1$. The total base moment is $2$, so $j^2\ge j$ at every integer $j$ gives objective at least $2$ for any partition with integer base centroids. These cells attain it.

The own score values are $3/10$ and the cross score values are $-3/10$, so both gaps are $3/5$. Centering gives caps with margins $\ell_0=3/10-x-z/5$ and $\ell_1=x+z/5-9/10$. The fiber lengths in increasing base order are $(1/2,3/10,1/10,0)$ and $(0,1/10,3/10,1/2)$. In each cap the corresponding original score is at least $3/10>0$, which implies membership in its own winner cell. The two cross margins are $-3/5$ each.

With the new base coefficients the centered margins are $h_0=3/10-x+z/2$ and $h_1=x-7/10-(z-1)/2$. The lengths are $(0,3/10,4/5,1)$ and $(1,4/5,3/10,0)$. Their sum as margins is identically $1/10$, so they cover $C$; direct evaluation gives cross margin $1/10$ each. The changes of coefficients are $7/10,-7/10$, and the two base differences are $1,-1$, yielding the correction $7/5$.

Finally use $a_0=1/2,a_1=-1/2$ and the two margins $a_i(z-j_i)\pm(x-c_i)$ per center. The equal average is $a_i(z-j_i)$, so the two cross averages sum to $(1/2)\cdot 1+(-1/2)\cdot(-1)=1$. Each own-cell margin has integral zero by the computed centroids. The $(-)$ margin at zero and the $(+)$ margin at one are the preceding $h_0$ and $h_1$. Their opposite-sign partners have length vectors $(1/5,7/10,1,1)$ and $(1,1,7/10,1/5)$, giving $29/10$ each. All lengths use the same original mixed measure.
