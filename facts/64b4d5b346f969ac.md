## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), put \(m:=n+d\), and identify
\(\mathbb R^m=\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\). Write
\(\operatorname{vol}_j\) for Euclidean \(j\)-volume and put
\(\mathbb B_\infty^n=[-1,1]^n\).

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be a convex body, let \(A\subseteq D\) be
compact and convex, and suppose that
\[
\mathbf a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n
\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D)
\]
for some \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\rho>0\). For
\(G\in\{A,D\}\), \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n\), and \(s>0\), set
\[
G_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in G\},
\qquad
g_G(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(G_{\mathbf z})^{1/d},
\qquad
L_s^G:=\{\mathbf z:g_G(\mathbf z)\geq s\},
\]
where an empty fiber has volume zero, and put
\[
\mathcal S_A:=\{s>0:L_s^A\neq\varnothing\}.
\]

For a full-rank lattice \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) and an
\(n\)-dimensional convex body \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), define its covering
radius by
\[
\mu(\Lambda,L):=
\min\{r\geq0:\Lambda+rL=\mathbb R^n\}.
\]
For \(1\leq q\leq n\), let
\[
\mathscr R_q:=
\{E\leq\mathbb R^n:\dim E=q,\ P_E\mathbb Z^n
  \text{ is a rank-\(q\) lattice in }E\},
\]
where \(P_E\) is orthogonal projection. Equivalently,
\(E^\perp\cap\mathbb Z^n\) has rank \(n-q\); for \(\mathbb Z^n\), these
are exactly the rational \(q\)-subspaces. Define
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb Z^n,L):=
\max_{\substack{1\leq q\leq n\\E\in\mathscr R_q}}
\left(
\frac{\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)}
{\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL)}
\right)^{1/q}.
\]

Choose once and for all a universal constant \(C_{\mathrm{SF}}>0\) for which
the Reis--Rothvoss theorem gives
\[
\mu(\mathbb Z^n,L)
\leq C_{\mathrm{SF}}\log^3(2n)\,
\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb Z^n,L) \tag{1}
\]
for every \(n\geq1\) and every \(n\)-dimensional convex body
\(L\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), and put
\[
B:=C_{\mathrm{SF}}\log^3(2n). \tag{2}
\]

Define the positive-volume lattice-bad levels by
\[
\mathcal B_A:=
\{s\in\mathcal S_A:
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)>0,\
L_s^A+\mathbb Z^n\neq\mathbb R^n\}. \tag{3}
\]
For \(s\in\mathcal B_A\), define the minimal obstruction rank
\[
r_B(s):=
\min\left\{
q\geq1:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there is an }E\in\mathscr R_q\text{ such that}\\
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)
\leq B^q\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)
\end{array}
\right\}. \tag{4}
\]
Then (4) is well-defined, \(r_B:\mathcal B_A\to\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is
measurable, and it is nonincreasing: if \(s\leq t\) and
\(s,t\in\mathcal B_A\), then
\[
r_B(t)\leq r_B(s). \tag{5}
\]
Every eligible projected lattice also satisfies
\[
\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)
=\frac{1}{\det(\mathbb Z^n\cap E^\perp)}
\leq1. \tag{6}
\]

Let
\[
d\nu_A(s):=
d\,s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds, \tag{7}
\]
so that, for measurable \(S\subseteq(0,\infty)\),
\[
\nu_A(S):=
d\int_Ss^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds.
\]
For every integer
\[
Q\geq
\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac{4emB}{\rho}\right\rceil\right\}, \tag{8}
\]
the high-rank lattice-bad levels obey
\[
\nu_A\bigl(\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)\geq Q\}\bigr)
\leq
\frac83\,4^{-Q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\leq
\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D). \tag{9}
\]

Moreover, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) and every \(Q\) satisfying (8), at
least one of the following alternatives holds.

1. The total weighted mass of the positive-volume lattice-bad levels is small:
   \[
   \nu_A(\mathcal B_A)
   \leq\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D)+\varepsilon. \tag{10}
   \]

2. There are \(s_0\in\mathcal B_A\), an integer
   \(q=r_B(s_0)<Q\), and one \(E\in\mathscr R_q\) such that
   \[
   \operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)
   \leq B^q\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)
   \quad
   \text{for every }s\in\mathcal B_A\cap[s_0,\infty), \tag{11}
   \]
   while the weighted bad mass strictly below \(s_0\) satisfies
   \[
   \nu_A(\mathcal B_A\cap(0,s_0))
   \leq\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D)+\varepsilon. \tag{12}
   \]

In particular, taking
\[
Q_*:=
\max\left\{2,\left\lceil
\frac{4eC_{\mathrm{SF}}m}{\rho}\log^3(2n)
\right\rceil\right\}, \tag{13}
\]
the subspace in the second alternative has the safe bound
\[
q<Q_*
\leq
2+\frac{4eC_{\mathrm{SF}}m}{\rho}\log^3(2n)
=O\!\left(1+\frac m\rho\log^3(2n)\right). \tag{14}
\]
Nonempty lower-dimensional levels, meaning
\(\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)=0\), have zero \(\nu_A\)-mass and make no
claim in (10)--(12).

## Proof

We first extract the levelwise obstruction from Reis--Rothvoss. By accepted
fact `9b86278db81f0c28`, every positive-volume level \(L_s^A\) is a compact
convex set with nonempty interior, hence an \(n\)-dimensional convex body.
The source theorem applies to arbitrary convex bodies, with no symmetry or
centering assumption.

There is one minor point concerning a translated nonsymmetric body. For
any \(\mathbf x\in L_s^A\), covering by
\(\mathbb Z^n+rL_s^A\) is equivalent, after a global translation, to
covering by
\(\mathbb Z^n+r(L_s^A-\mathbf x)\). Since
\(\mathbf0\in L_s^A-\mathbf x\), the latter bodies are nested as \(r\)
increases. Thus
\[
L_s^A+\mathbb Z^n\neq\mathbb R^n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu(\mathbb Z^n,L_s^A)>1. \tag{15}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (15) gives
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb Z^n,L_s^A)>\frac1B.
\]
By the defining maximum, there are \(q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and
\(E\in\mathscr R_q\) such that
\[
\left(
\frac{\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)}
{\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)}
\right)^{1/q}>\frac1B.
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)
<B^q\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n), \tag{16}
\]
which in particular supplies a certificate with the weak inequality in
(4). Hence \(r_B(s)\) exists for every \(s\in\mathcal B_A\).

We next prove (6), including its normalization. Fix
\(E\in\mathscr R_q\), put \(F:=E^\perp\), and let
\[
\Gamma:=\mathbb Z^n\cap F.
\]
This is a primitive rank-\((n-q)\) sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^n\), and
\(P_E\mathbb Z^n\) is the quotient lattice \(\mathbb Z^n/\Gamma\).
Choose an integer basis matrix \(U\in\mathbb Z^{n\times(n-q)}\) for
\(\Gamma\). Since \(\Gamma\) is primitive, its columns extend to a
unimodular integer basis \([U\ V]\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\).
The columns of \(P_EV\) form a lattice basis for \(P_E\mathbb Z^n\).
Taking Euclidean parallelepiped volumes and using \(F\perp E\) gives
\[
1=|\det[U\ V]|
=\det(\Gamma)\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n),
\]
which proves the identity in (6).

If \(n-q>0\), integer Cauchy--Binet gives
\[
\det(\Gamma)^2
=\det(U^{\mathsf T}U)
=
\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|J|=n-q}}
(\det U_J)^2
\geq1, \tag{17}
\]
because at least one maximal minor is a nonzero integer. If \(q=n\),
\(\Gamma=\{\mathbf0\}\) and \(\det(\Gamma)=1\) by the standard
zero-dimensional convention. Thus \(\det(\Gamma)\geq1\) in every case,
and (6) follows.

We now verify measurability and monotonicity. The eligible subspaces form
a countable family: every such \(E\) is rational and therefore has a
basis in \(\mathbb Q^n\). For fixed \(E\in\mathscr R_q\), nesting of the
levels gives
\[
s\leq t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_EL_t^A\subseteq P_EL_s^A,
\]
so
\[
s\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A) \tag{18}
\]
is nonincreasing and hence Borel measurable. The function
\(s\mapsto\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\) is measurable for the same
reason. The noncovering condition in (3) is upward closed in \(s\):
once \(L_s^A+\mathbb Z^n\) fails to cover, every smaller higher level
\(L_t^A\), \(t\geq s\), also fails to cover. Therefore
\(\mathcal B_A\) is measurable.

For \(1\leq k\leq n\), countability and (18) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)\leq k\}
=\mathcal B_A\cap
\bigcup_{q=1}^k\ \bigcup_{E\in\mathscr R_q}
\left\{
s:
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)
\leq B^q\det(P_E\mathbb Z^n)
\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{19}
\]
Thus every rank sublevel, and hence every rank stratum, is measurable.
If \(s\leq t\) are both in \(\mathcal B_A\), every certificate at \(s\)
remains a certificate at \(t\) by nesting. Taking the minimum dimension
proves (5).

Apply the accepted generic weighted sectional budget. For \(1\leq q\leq n\),
set
\[
I_q:=\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)=q\},
\qquad
I_0:=\mathcal S_A\setminus\bigcup_{q=1}^n I_q. \tag{20}
\]
These sets measurably partition \(\mathcal S_A\). In particular, \(I_0\)
contains all positive-volume levels that cover and all nonempty levels of
zero \(n\)-volume. For every \(s\in I_q\), \(q\geq1\), choose a
certificate \(E_s\in\mathscr R_q\) from (4). Equations (4) and (6) give
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_{E_s}L_s^A)
\leq B^q\det(P_{E_s}\mathbb Z^n)
\leq B^q. \tag{21}
\]
No measurability of the choice \(s\mapsto E_s\) is needed. Thus accepted
fact `9b86278db81f0c28` applies with \(W=B\). Its rank masses are
\[
M_q
=d\int_{I_q}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds
=\nu_A(I_q).
\]
For every \(Q\) satisfying (8), its high-rank conclusion gives
\[
\sum_{q=Q}^nM_q
\leq\frac83\,4^{-Q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\leq\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D).
\]
The left side is exactly
\(\nu_A(\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)\geq Q\})\), proving (9).
The levels of zero \(n\)-volume placed in \(I_0\) contribute zero to
\(M_0\), as required.

It remains to prove the synchronization dichotomy. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\)
and a \(Q\) satisfying (8), and abbreviate
\[
\mathcal H_Q:=\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)\geq Q\},
\qquad
\mathcal L_Q:=\{s\in\mathcal B_A:r_B(s)<Q\}. \tag{22}
\]
If (10) holds, there is nothing to prove. Suppose instead that (10)
fails. By (9),
\[
\nu_A(\mathcal L_Q)
=\nu_A(\mathcal B_A)-\nu_A(\mathcal H_Q)
>\varepsilon, \tag{23}
\]
so \(\mathcal L_Q\neq\varnothing\).

The finite measure \(\nu_A\) is absolutely continuous with respect to
Lebesgue measure by (7), hence is nonatomic. Put
\(\alpha:=\inf\mathcal L_Q\). If \(\alpha\in\mathcal L_Q\), take
\(s_0=\alpha\). Otherwise choose \(s_j\in\mathcal L_Q\) with
\(s_j\downarrow\alpha\). Then
\[
\mathcal L_Q\cap(0,s_j)
\downarrow
\mathcal L_Q\cap(0,\alpha],
\]
and the limiting set is empty or the singleton \(\{\alpha\}\). It has
\(\nu_A\)-mass zero. Continuity from above therefore allows a choice
\(s_0\in\mathcal L_Q\) such that
\[
\nu_A(\mathcal L_Q\cap(0,s_0))\leq\varepsilon. \tag{24}
\]
This is the endpoint approximation; nonatomicity also shows that using
\((0,s_0]\) instead of \((0,s_0)\) would not change its mass.

Let \(q:=r_B(s_0)<Q\), and choose one certificate
\(E\in\mathscr R_q\) at \(s_0\). For every \(s\geq s_0\), nesting gives
\(P_EL_s^A\subseteq P_EL_{s_0}^A\), so the same \(E\) satisfies (11)
at every higher positive-volume bad level. Finally, (9) and (24) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_A(\mathcal B_A\cap(0,s_0))
&\leq
\nu_A(\mathcal H_Q)
+\nu_A(\mathcal L_Q\cap(0,s_0))\\
&\leq
\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D)+\varepsilon,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (12). Equations (13)--(14) follow from (2) and
\(\lceil x\rceil\leq x+1\).

## External sources

Victor Reis and Thomas Rothvoss, "The Subspace Flatness Conjecture and
Faster Integer Programming," arXiv:2303.14605v5, 27 March 2026,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14605.

The paper defines
\(\mu(\Lambda,K)=\min\{r\geq0:\Lambda+rK=\operatorname{span}(\Lambda)\}\).
For an eligible subspace \(W\), it defines
\[
R_W(\Lambda,K)=
\left(
\frac{\det(P_W\Lambda)}
{\operatorname{vol}_{\dim W}(P_WK)}
\right)^{1/\dim W},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\Lambda,K)=\max_W R_W(\Lambda,K).
\]
Its footnote after this definition says that \(P_W\Lambda\) must be a
lattice, equivalently \(W^\perp\) is a lattice subspace for \(\Lambda\);
otherwise one may set the projected determinant to zero. Theorem 3 states
exactly that, for every full-rank lattice
\(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) and every convex body
\(K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\Lambda,K)
\leq\mu(\Lambda,K)
\leq O(\log^3(2n))\,\mu_{\mathrm{KL}}(\Lambda,K).
\]
Choosing one universal constant hidden by this \(O(\cdot)\) gives
\(C_{\mathrm{SF}}\) in (1). The paper defines a convex body as compact,
convex, and full-dimensional, and distinguishes potentially asymmetric
bodies \(K\) from symmetric bodies \(Q\). Therefore Theorem 3 applies
without a symmetry, origin, or centering hypothesis to every
positive-volume \(L_s^A\). Lemma 17 gives the quotient determinant
identity
\(\det(\Lambda)=\det(\Lambda')\det(\Lambda/\Lambda')\) for a primitive
sublattice \(\Lambda'\); with
\(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\) and
\(\Lambda'=\mathbb Z^n\cap E^\perp\), it is exactly the identity in
(6).
