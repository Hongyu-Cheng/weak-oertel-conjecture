## Statement

Let $y$ be interior to a nondegenerate triangle with three closed caps bounded by lines through $y$, each strictly capturing a different vertex and strictly excluding the other two. Let $q_i$ be their planar area fractions. If each $q_i\le128/279$ and $\sum_iq_i\le125/93$, then for each cap both edge intercept fractions from the captured vertex are strictly greater than $1/2$. Equivalently, the two incident edge midpoints lie in the open capturing halfplane, though on the boundary of the triangle.

## Proof

Let $\alpha_i$ be the barycentric coordinates of $y$. Fact 2e657e976221a74d applies with exactly the strict vertex incidences and individual cap ceiling in the statement, giving $\alpha_i<5/14$. At the cap isolating vertex $i$, with intercept fractions $u,v$ toward vertices $j,k$, the through-source equation and determinant formula give $\alpha_j/u+\alpha_k/v=1$ and $q_i=uv$. Thus $q_i\ge4\alpha_j\alpha_k$ by AM–GM. If $m=\min_i\alpha_i$ and the other two coordinates are $x,z$, expanding $(5/14-x)(5/14-z)>0$ shows
$$
(5/14)(1-m-5/14)<xz\le32/279.
$$
Consequently $m\ge9/14-7(128/279)/10=9/28+11/39060>9/28$.

Suppose a cap has intercept $u\le1/2$. Write $b,e$ for the two barycentric coordinates in the directions of its two intercepts, with $b$ corresponding to $u$. The cap fraction is $e u^2/(u-b)$, with $u>b$. As a function of $u$ its derivative has the sign of $u-2b$, which is negative here because $b>9/28$. It is therefore at least $e/(2-4b)$. The two other caps together have fraction at least $4(b+e)(1-b-e)$ by the AM–GM bound. The sum is at least
$$
G(b,e)=\frac{e}{2-4b}+4(b+e)(1-b-e).
$$
On $[9/28,5/14]^2$ the derivative in $b$ is at least $63/25-12/7=141/175>0$, using $e\ge9/28$, $2-4b\le5/7$ and $b+e\le5/7$. At $b=9/28$ the function is concave in $e$, so its minimum is the smaller of the two endpoint values, $1341/980$ and $269/196$. The smaller value $1341/980$ exceeds $125/93$ by $2213/91140$. This contradicts the total budget. Interchanging the two directions of any cap gives the same conclusion for its other intercept.
