## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, let \(\Gamma:\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be coordinatewise nondecreasing with \(\Gamma(\mathbf0)=0\), let \(T\ge0\) be an integer, and let \(\beta,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\ge0\) be real numbers. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T&:=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&:=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}
       \exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i x_i-\beta t\right),\\
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}
&:=\frac{\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i x_i-\beta t\right)}{Z}
\qquad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

For each \(\mathbf z\in A_T\), put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&:=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}
                     \sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},\\
M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

where the infimum is over closed affine halfspaces
\(H=\lbrace\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\ge b\rbrace\)
with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Then

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$

The support \(A_T\) is exactly that of accepted fact 11d156df58653939, with the same \(k,\Gamma,T\). When all \(\alpha_i=0\), the present \(Z,p,\tau_p,M_3\) equal its objects with those names. For general \(\alpha_i\), they mean precisely the original tilted law (1)-(2); there is no change of sampling law in (3). The symbol \(Z\) remains an auxiliary table normalizer. The locked problem's \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S\), its mixed volume, and any locked notation \(N\) retain their meanings. No convexity or separability hypothesis on \(\Gamma\) is imposed.

## Proof

The table is finite and nonempty because every supported height is in \(\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace\) and \((\mathbf0,0)\in A_T\). All weights in (1) are strictly positive, so \(0<Z<\infty\).

We first prove the vector-weighted Boolean assertion needed below. For a finite ground set \(L\), give its coordinates activities \(0<\theta_i\le1\), and write \(\theta^I:=\prod_{i\in I}\theta_i\), with \(\theta^\varnothing=1\). For downsets \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq2^L\), including the empty family, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A)
&:=\sum_{I\in\mathcal A}\theta^I,\\
\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
&:=\sum_{\substack{P\in\mathcal A,\ Q\in\mathcal B\\P\cap Q=\varnothing}}
       \theta^P\theta^Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The second sum is over ordered pairs. We claim that

$$
\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
\ge
\bigl(\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A)
      \mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal B)\bigr)^{3/4}.
\tag{5}
$$

When every \(\theta_i=1\), these are the cardinality and ordered disjoint-pair count used in 11d156df58653939 and c13fd517ed177886. When every \(\theta_i=v\), the first quantity is exactly \(\mathfrak m_v\) of c13fd517ed177886, on the same ground set. The vector-weighted pair assertion (5) is established here, rather than inferred from that predecessor's scalar assertions.

For the numerical step, put \(\rho:=3/4\). The concavity-ratio calculation in the proof of c13fd517ed177886 says that
\(h_\rho(u):=((1+u)^\rho-1)/u^\rho\) is nondecreasing for \(0<u\le1\). Its exact exponent map is to choose that predecessor's scalar Boolean activity as \(2^{3/4}-1\), making its exponent, called \(\beta\) there, equal to the present \(\rho\). That predecessor exponent is unrelated to the height coefficient \(\beta\) in (1). Explicitly, the calculation is

$$
h_\rho'(u)
=\rho u^{-\rho-1}
  \bigl(1-(1+u)^{\rho-1}\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{6}
$$

For \(0<v\le1\) and \(0\le z\le1\), apply this monotonicity to \(vz\le v\), treating \(z=0\) directly. With \(c_v:=(1+v)^{3/4}-1\), it gives
\((1+vz)^{3/4}\le1+c_vz^{3/4}\). Also

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_v+\frac{c_v^2}{2}\le v
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(1+v)^{3/2}\le1+2v,\\
(1+2v)^2-(1+v)^3
&=v+v^2-v^3\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

The last inequality holds for \(0\le v\le1\), and both sides of the preceding unsquared comparison are positive. If \(x,y\in[0,1]\), then \(2x^{3/4}y^{3/4}\le x^{3/4}+y^{3/4}\). Multiplying the two ratio bounds and using (7) therefore yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl((1+vx)(1+vy)\bigr)^{3/4}
&\le(1+c_vx^{3/4})(1+c_vy^{3/4})\\
&\le1+\left(c_v+\frac{c_v^2}{2}\right)
             (x^{3/4}+y^{3/4})\\
&\le1+v(x^{3/4}+y^{3/4}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

We prove (5) by induction on \(|L|\). If either family is empty, both sides vanish. On the empty ground set, the only nonempty downset is \(\lbrace\varnothing\rbrace\), so the assertion is equality. In positive dimension choose \(i\in L\), and split each family into sections
\(\mathcal A_0:=\lbrace I\subseteq L\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace:I\in\mathcal A\rbrace\) and
\(\mathcal A_1:=\lbrace I\subseteq L\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace:I\cup\lbrace i\rbrace\in\mathcal A\rbrace\), and likewise \(\mathcal B_0,\mathcal B_1\). They are downsets and satisfy \(\mathcal A_1\subseteq\mathcal A_0\), \(\mathcal B_1\subseteq\mathcal B_0\). Let \(\boldsymbol\theta'\) denote the activities on \(L\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace\), and set

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&:=\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal A_0),
&b&:=\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal A_1),\\
c&:=\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal B_0),
&d&:=\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal B_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

Because the original families are nonempty, \(a,c>0\), and \(0\le b\le a\), \(0\le d\le c\). A disjoint pair may contain \(i\) in neither set or in exactly one set. Consequently the exact recurrence is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
={}&\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal A_0,\mathcal B_0)
 +\theta_i\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B_0)\\
&+\theta_i\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta'}(\mathcal A_0,\mathcal B_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

The induction hypothesis, including empty sections, bounds this below by
\((ac)^{3/4}+\theta_i(bc)^{3/4}+\theta_i(ad)^{3/4}\).
Apply (8) with \(v=\theta_i\), \(x=b/a\), and \(y=d/c\), and multiply by \((ac)^{3/4}\). The result is at least
\(((a+\theta_i b)(c+\theta_i d))^{3/4}\), which is the right side of (5). This completes the proof of (5), with all zero-section cases included. In particular,
\(\mathfrak D_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A,\mathcal A)\ge
\mathfrak m_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathcal A)^{3/2}\).

We next specify the accepted height estimate and every normalization used to apply it. Accepted fact cd9532b1d59c5977 proves the following assertion, also used in 11d156df58653939. If \(M\ge0\) is an integer, \(0<r\le1\), \(Z_{\rm aux}:=\sum_{j=0}^Mr^j\), and \(q_j:=r^j/Z_{\rm aux}\) on \(\lbrace0,\ldots,M\rbrace\), extended by zero to other integers, then

$$
F_q(j):=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min\lbrace q_{j+h},q_{j-h}\rbrace,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^M q_jF_q(j)^3\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{11}
$$

These are exactly that predecessor's auxiliary \(M,r,Z_{\rm aux},q,F_q\), and its estimate includes \(M=0\) and \(r=1\). For integers \(E\ge0\), put \(f_E:=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\), with \(f_E=0\) when \(E>T\). Thus \(f_E\) is exactly the quantity called \(A(E)\) in cd9532b1d59c5977 and 11d156df58653939, at the same \(E,T,\beta\). For \(0\le E\le T\), define \(q_t^{(E)}:=e^{-\beta t}/f_E\) on \(\lbrace E,\ldots,T\rbrace\), zero elsewhere. The exact map into (11) is

$$
j=t-E,\qquad
M=T-E,\qquad r=e^{-\beta},\qquad
Z_{\rm aux}=e^{\beta E}f_E,\qquad
q_t^{(E)}=q_{t-E}.
\tag{12}
$$

Writing \(F_E(t):=F_q(t-E)\), the same reflection sum gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_E(t)
&=\frac1{f_E}
  \sum_{|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)},\\
\sum_{t=E}^Tq_t^{(E)}F_E(t)^3&\ge\frac1{448}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

Indeed, both heights \(t+h,t-h\) lie in \([E,T]\) exactly under the displayed displacement restriction, and their smaller height weight is \(e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}\). The auxiliary probability \(q^{(E)}\) will only express sums of contributions sampled from the original \(p\).

Now set \([k]:=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), and use the spatial activities
\(\theta_i:=e^{-\alpha_i}\in(0,1]\) in (4)-(5). They are distinct from every scalar height probability \(q_j\) and \(q_t^{(E)}\). For \(L\subseteq[k]\), let \(\mathbf1_L\) be its indicator vector and put
\(\mathbf y_L:=\mathbf1_L\), \(e_L:=\Gamma(\mathbf y_L)\),
\(E_L(I):=\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I)\) for \(I\subseteq L\), and \(u_L:=\theta^L\).

We use the support partition and monotone endpoint construction of accepted fact 11d156df58653939. The exact maps of its objects are the present \(L,\mathbf y_L,e_L,E_L(I),\Gamma,A_T\), with no change. Its source weights and normalization are replaced only by (1), as specified below. Every spatial vector has the unique representation
\(\mathbf x=\mathbf1_L+\mathbf1_I\), where
\(L=\lbrace i:x_i>0\rbrace\) and \(I=\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\subseteq L\).
Its spatial weight is exactly \(u_L\theta^I\). Thus the original unnormalized block masses are

$$
Z_L:=u_L\sum_{I\subseteq L}\theta^I f_{E_L(I)},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}Z_L.
\tag{14}
$$

In particular the \(Z_L\) reduce to the \(Z_L\) of 11d156df58653939 when all \(\alpha_i=0\). The partition (14) includes every original atom and its complete height interval once.

For an integer \(E\ge e_L\), use the same sublevel and ordered pair families as in 11d156df58653939:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_L(E)&:=\lbrace I\subseteq L:E_L(I)\le E\rbrace,\\
\mathcal F_L(E)&:=\lbrace(P,Q)\in\mathcal D_L(E)^2:P\cap Q=\varnothing\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

Monotonicity makes \(\mathcal D_L(E)\) a downset. It contains the empty set because \(E_L(\varnothing)=e_L\). Define its weighted mass and pair stock by

$$
m_L(E):=\sum_{I\in\mathcal D_L(E)}\theta^I,
\qquad
R_L(E):=\sum_{(P,Q)\in\mathcal F_L(E)}\theta^P\theta^Q.
\tag{16}
$$

These are the weighted analogues of that predecessor's \(m_L(E),R_L(E)\); at zero spatial tilt they equal those cardinalities. Equation (5), on the ground set \(L\) with the restricted activities, proves \(R_L(E)\ge m_L(E)^{3/2}\).

The family \(\mathcal F_L(E)\) is invariant under exchanging its two sets. Its reflected spatial endpoints lie in the ternary cube, and the monotone containment from 11d156df58653939 is explicitly

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q&\le\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P,
&\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q)&\le E_L(P)\le E,\\
\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q&\le\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_Q,
&\Gamma(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q)&\le E_L(Q)\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

Only monotonicity is used, so arbitrary interactions in \(\Gamma\) are allowed.

Fix \(e_L\le E\le T\) and an integer \(t\in[E,T]\). For each \((P,Q)\in\mathcal F_L(E)\) and integer \(h\) satisfying
\(|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace\), define original atoms

$$
\mathbf z_+:=(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q,t+h),
\qquad
\mathbf z_-:=(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q,t-h).
\tag{18}
$$

Equation (17) and the height restriction prove their membership in \(A_T\). Their midpoint is the supported root \((\mathbf y_L,t)\). If
\(a_P:=\sum_{i\in P}\alpha_i\) and \(a_Q:=\sum_{i\in Q}\alpha_i\), their original minimum mass satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min\lbrace p_{\mathbf z_+},p_{\mathbf z_-}\rbrace
&=\frac{u_L e^{-\beta t}}{Z}
           e^{-|a_P-a_Q+\beta h|}\\
&\ge\frac{u_L}{Z}\theta^P\theta^Q
            e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$

Here \(|a_P-a_Q+\beta h|\le a_P+a_Q+\beta|h|\), since \(a_P,a_Q,\beta\ge0\). Thus the simultaneous spatial and height tilt is bounded directly for the original endpoint masses.

For this root, \((P,Q,h)\mapsto\mathbf z_+\) is injective. The coordinates equal to \(2\) identify \(P\), the zero coordinates within \(L\) identify \(Q\), and the height identifies \(h\). The involution
\((P,Q,h)\mapsto(Q,P,-h)\) exchanges endpoints and preserves the right side of (19). Its only fixed index is \((\varnothing,\varnothing,0)\), giving the root itself. Distinct two-element orbits therefore consist of disjoint pairs of original atoms and avoid the fixed root atom.

Every closed affine halfspace containing the root contains at least one endpoint of each pair in (18). Otherwise its defining affine functional would be strictly below its threshold at both endpoints and at their midpoint. Sum the lower bound (19) once over each two-element orbit, and charge half its value for the fixed root. This is at most the halfspace's mass and is one half of the sum over all oriented indices. The resulting uniform depth estimate, using (13) and (16), is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)
&\ge\frac{u_LR_L(E)}{2Z}
       \sum_{|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace}
             e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}\\
&=\frac{u_LR_L(E)f_E}{2Z}\,F_E(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

The original sampling probability is
\(p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}=(u_Lf_E/Z)q_t^{(E)}\).
Cube (20), multiply by this probability, sum over \(E\le t\le T\), and apply (13). This yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=E}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
&\ge
 \frac{u_L^4R_L(E)^3f_E^4}{8Z^4}
     \sum_{t=E}^Tq_t^{(E)}F_E(t)^3\\
&\ge\frac{u_L^4R_L(E)^3f_E^4}{3584Z^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$

There are four original factors \(Z\) and four spatial root factors \(u_L\), one from root sampling and three from the depth bound.

Let
\(\mathcal E_L:=\lbrace E_L(I):I\subseteq L,\ E_L(I)\le T\rbrace\).
If this set is nonempty, choose one maximizing threshold for
\(V_L:=\max_{E\in\mathcal E_L}R_L(E)^{3/4}f_E\).
If it is empty, put \(V_L:=0\). This is the weighted analogue of the stock \(V_L\) in 11d156df58653939, with the same finite threshold set. Equation (21), and nonnegativity at other root heights, give

$$
\sum_{t=e_L}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
\ge\frac{(u_LV_L)^4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{22}
$$

If \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty, then \(e_L>T\), because otherwise its empty subset would be feasible. Conversely \(e_L>T\) makes every subset infeasible. In that case the left side of (22) is an empty sum, and both sides vanish. Distinct \(L\) give distinct binary spatial roots. Summing (22) therefore uses each sampled root and height at most once and proves

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\mathcal V_4}{3584Z^4},
\qquad
\mathcal V_4:=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}(u_LV_L)^4.
\tag{23}
$$

Each root uses only its chosen threshold. Reusing an endpoint in the depth estimates of different roots causes no duplication in the sampling sum (23).

It remains to compare these stocks with the full original normalizer (14). Suppose \(\mathcal E_L\ne\varnothing\), and enumerate all feasible subsets as
\(I_1,\ldots,I_{b_L}\) in nondecreasing order of \(E_L(I_j)\), choosing \(I_1=\varnothing\) among the subsets with minimal energy. This is possible since the empty subset has energy \(e_L\), the minimum. Put
\(s_j:=\sum_{a=1}^j\theta^{I_a}\), so \(s_1=1\).
The number \(b_L\) is just this finite enumeration length and does not redefine any locked count. At threshold \(E_L(I_j)\), every first \(j\) subset belongs to the sublevel, even in an energy tie. Therefore (16) and (5) show

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_L
&\ge R_L(E_L(I_j))^{3/4}f_{E_L(I_j)}\\
&\ge m_L(E_L(I_j))^{9/8}f_{E_L(I_j)}
\ge s_j^{9/8}f_{E_L(I_j)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$

All \(\theta^{I_a}>0\). Since \(x^{-9/8}\) is decreasing, the increments
\(s_j-s_{j-1}=\theta^{I_j}\) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=1}^{b_L}\frac{\theta^{I_j}}{s_j^{9/8}}
&=1+\sum_{j=2}^{b_L}(s_j-s_{j-1})s_j^{-9/8}\\
&\le1+\int_1^{s_{b_L}}x^{-9/8}\,dx
\le1+\int_1^\infty x^{-9/8}\,dx=9.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$

For \(b_L=1\) the intermediate sum is empty and the finite integral is zero. Combining (24)-(25) with (14) proves \(Z_L\le9u_LV_L\). If no subset is feasible, both sides are zero, so this comparison holds for every \(L\).

Finally apply Hölder's inequality to the \(2^k\) nonnegative numbers \(u_LV_L\). Equations (14) and (23) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z^4
&\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}u_LV_L\right)^4\\
&\le9^4(2^k)^3\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}(u_LV_L)^4
=9^4\,8^k\mathcal V_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$

For \(L=\varnothing\), one has \(u_L=1\), \(e_L=E_L(\varnothing)=0\), \(R_L(0)=1\), and \(f_0>0\). Thus \(V_{\varnothing}>0\) and \(\mathcal V_4>0\). Combining (23) and (26) now proves

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{3584\cdot9^4\cdot8^k}
=\frac1{448\cdot9^4}\,8^{-(k+1)}
=\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{27}
$$

Every threshold, spatial sublevel, height interval, and halfspace uses weak inequalities, so all boundary atoms remain included. The numerical proof covers activities \(\theta_i=1\), hence zero spatial tilt coefficients. The height map (12) covers \(\beta=0\) through \(r=1\), and singleton height intervals through \(M=0\). Zero energy increments and arbitrary energy ties preserve the downsets, the endpoint containments, and the weighted rank estimate because the empty subset is placed first and has weight exactly one. If \(T=0\), the table may contain several zero-energy spatial points; all feasible thresholds then equal zero and the auxiliary height law is a singleton, so the same argument applies. Infeasible roots, empty labels, empty Boolean sections, and the empty ground set were handled above. This establishes (3) for all parameters in the statement.
