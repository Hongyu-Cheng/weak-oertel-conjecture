## Statement

For any integer $k\ge3$, there is a complete integer set with $k+1$ sites in $k$ occupied parity classes and exactly $s=k-1$ inclusion-maximal occupied minimal faces. At fixed witnesses of these faces there are compact convex full-dimensional abstract response menus on a Euclidean space $E$ of dimension $s-1$. They exclude zero and span $E$. For every independent selection $g_i$ from the menus, a unique balancing probability $\pi$ satisfies $\sum_i\pi_i g_i=0$ and $2/(3s)\le\pi_i\le4/(3s)$; it varies continuously with the selected vectors. No fixed probability balances every selection.

The protecting cones $T_i=\{x\in E:g\cdot x\ge0\text{ for every }g\in\Gamma_i\}$ are pointed and cover $E$, with $\max_i\min_{g\in\Gamma_i}g\cdot x\ge3\lVert x\rVert/(4s)$. No cone can be removed, and their intersection and the common annihilator of all menus are both $\{0\}$. The essential menu block has rank exactly $k-2$. These properties hold for ball menus and for rational polytopes generated by $2(s-1)$ explicitly given choices per witness.

## Proof

In $\mathbb Z^s$ take the lattice set of $\operatorname{conv}(0,2e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_s)$. Its defining inequalities $x_i\ge0$ and $x_1/2+\sum_{i\ge2}x_i\le1$ show that the lattice set is precisely $\{0,e_1,2e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_s\}$, with parities $0,e_1,\ldots,e_s$. The maximal occupied minimal faces are the edge $[0,2e_1]$ and singleton vertices $\{e_i\}$ for $i\ge2$. Choose $p_i=e_i$.

Let $E=\{x\in\mathbb R^s:\sum_i x_i=0\}$, $v_i=e_i-s^{-1}\mathbf1$, and $\varepsilon=1/(4s)$. Take $\Gamma_i=v_i+\varepsilon B_E$ for the closed Euclidean unit ball $B_E$. These menus are full-dimensional in $E$, and exclude zero because $\lVert v_i\rVert=\sqrt{(s-1)/s}>\varepsilon$. For any choices $g_i=v_i+d_i$, let $\Delta:\mathbb R^s\to E$ have columns $d_i$ and write $\pi=s^{-1}\mathbf1+\eta$ with $\eta\in E$. The balance equation is
$$
(I_E+\Delta|_E)\eta=-\Delta(s^{-1}\mathbf1).
$$
The operator norm of $\Delta$ is at most $\sqrt{s}\varepsilon\le1/4$. The inverse therefore exists with norm at most $4/3$, and the right side has norm at most $\varepsilon$. Hence $\lVert\eta\rVert\le1/(3s)$ and the unique normalized solution has the stated positive coordinate bounds. It is continuous by continuity of inversion. Varying the choice at any one label forces its coefficient in a putative common balancing vector to vanish, so no common probability exists.

For the balls, $\min_{g\in\Gamma_i}g\cdot x=x_i-\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert$, so $T_i=\{x:x_i\ge\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert\}$. It is pointed. For $x\ne0$ put $A=\max_i x_i>0$; express $x$ as a convex combination of the vectors $A\mathbf1-sAe_j$ with weights $(A-x_j)/(sA)$. These vectors all have norm $A\sqrt{s(s-1)}$, so $A\ge\lVert x\rVert/s$. Subtraction of $\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert$ proves the strict margin. If every cone contains $x$, summing the cone inequalities and using zero coordinate sum gives $x=0$.

For the finite rational variant put $\delta=1/(8s)$ and take $\Gamma_i$ to be the convex hull of $v_i\pm\delta(e_j-e_s)$ for $1\le j<s$. The error vectors have norm at most $\sqrt2\,\delta<\varepsilon$ and their differences span $E$, so the menus are full-dimensional subsets of the balls, exclude zero, and retain the balance bounds and continuity, strict margin, and the nonexistence of a common balancing probability by variation in each menu. Their exact cones are $T_i=\{x:x_i\ge\delta\max_{j<s}|x_j-x_s|\}$. If both $x$ and $-x$ lie in one cone, all coordinates coincide and their sum is zero, so $x=0$. Summing the inequalities also gives that their intersection is zero.

For either choice of menus, $v_i$ lies in $T_i$ because the strict margin ensures some cone contains it, while $v_j\cdot v_i=-1/s<0$ for $j\ne i$ and each $v_j$ belongs to its menu. Thus removal of any label leaves an uncovered direction and, by choosing $g_j=v_j$ for the remaining labels, fails the universal balance property. The centers span $E$, so the essential block has dimension $s-1=k-2$ and trivial common annihilator.
