## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(m\ge1\), put \(M=2m\) and
\[
D_M=\bigl(\{0\}\times\{0,\ldots,2m\}\bigr)
\cup\bigl(\{1\}\times\{0,\ldots,m\}\bigr)
\cup\{(2,0)\}.
\]
This is the complete lattice support of the triangle with vertices \((0,0),(0,M),(2,0)\). Write its coordinates as \((x,y)\); these are exactly the coordinates \((x_1,x_2)\) of the triangle in accepted fact `1ed6b6d9eb994c90`. In particular its \(D_M\) agrees with the present \(D_M\) whenever \(4\mid M\), but the present assertion includes every positive even \(M\).

For arbitrary source tilt coordinates \(\theta_x,\theta_y\in\mathbb R\), write \(\tau=\theta_x\), \(\theta=\theta_y\), and define the positive source law
\[
p(x,y)=\frac{e^{\tau x+\theta y}}{Z},\qquad
A_0(b)=\sum_{y=0}^{2m}e^{by},\qquad
A_1(b)=\sum_{y=0}^{m}e^{by},\qquad
Z=A_0(\theta)+e^\tau A_1(\theta)+e^{2\tau}.
\]
For each root \(\mathbf j\in D_M\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy law on \(D_M\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), and put \(H(\nu)=-\sum_{\nu(\mathbf x)>0}\nu(\mathbf x)\log\nu(\mathbf x)\). The exact aliases for accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374` are \(A=D_M\), \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\tau,\theta)\), and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{D_M,\mathbf j}\); its displayed ambient dimension is \(2\) here. Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathbf j}&=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=p(\mathbf j)e^{H(\nu_{\mathbf j})},
&B_2&=\mathcal B_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_M}p(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^2,\\
\omega_{\mathbf j}&=\frac{p(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^2}{B_2},
&V_{\mathbf j}&=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(X,Y)}{p(X,Y)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The capacity identity is that of `bf1ff94487296374`. These \(C_{\mathbf j},B_2,\omega_{\mathbf j},V_{\mathbf j}\) have exactly the same normalizations as in `81ad175d3c410508`, although its simplex support is not being identified with this triangle.

There is a finite absolute constant \(V_0\) such that, for all the preceding \(m,\tau,\theta\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_M}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}\le V_0.
\]
For example, define the numerical constants
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{self}}&=4+\frac{32}{e^2},\\
K_*&=\log(18e^2\sqrt{384})+\frac12\log(16e^2)+2,\\
K_{\mathrm{height}}&=\frac{110592e^2}{9}
\left(2K_*^2+\frac{288}{e^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(V_0=2K_{\mathrm{self}}+8K_{\mathrm{height}}+1120e^2\) is admissible. No optimality of these constants is claimed.

## Proof

First, log-affinity gives, for every law \(\rho\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), \(D(\rho\|p)=-H(\rho)-\log p(\mathbf j)\). Thus the relative-entropy minimizer in `bf1ff94487296374` is exactly the maximum-entropy law \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), including on proper faces. That fact supplies existence, uniqueness, positivity on the minimal face, its exponential representation there, and the displayed capacity identity. In particular, \(B_2>0\), and the unnormalized root weights of \(\omega\) are
\[
e^{3\tau j_x+3\theta j_y+2H(\nu_{\mathbf j})}.
\tag{1}
\]
The omitted common factor is \(Z^{-3}\).

The integer affine bijection \(T(x,y)=(x,2m-y-mx)\) preserves \(D_M\), and \(T^2\) is the identity. Pushing a source law through \(T\) changes its parameters to \(\tau'=\tau-m\theta\), \(\theta'=-\theta\), since
\[
\tau x+\theta y=2m\theta+(\tau-m\theta)x-\theta(2m-y-mx).
\]
It preserves mean constraints and entropy, so it pushes entropy rows to the corresponding entropy rows. The capacity identity and a change of variables preserve \(C,\omega,V\) at corresponding roots. We may consequently assume \(\theta\le0\), while \(\tau\) remains arbitrary. Below put \(a=-\theta\ge0\).

We describe all rows, including real means on the two vertical root segments used later. At mean \((0,t)\), \(0\le t\le2m\), the first coordinate is deterministically zero, and the entropy row is the geometric interval law on \(0,\ldots,2m\) with mean \(t\). Its endpoint rows are Dirac laws. The apex row is Dirac. At mean \((1,t)\), \(0<t<m\), the root is in the triangle interior. Write its finite exponential parameter as \((\lambda_x,\beta)\). These parameters are the exact aliases for the row parameter \(\boldsymbol\lambda\) in `1ed6b6d9eb994c90`; that fact's row law \(\nu\) is the present entropy row, not the present source \(p\).

The mean equation \(\mathbb EX=1\) says that the total weights of columns \(x=0\) and \(x=2\) are equal. Hence \(e^{2\lambda_x}=A_0(\beta)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_x&=\tfrac12\log A_0(\beta),
&\alpha&=\frac{\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}}{2\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}+A_1(\beta)},\\
\mathbb P(X=0)&=\mathbb P(X=2)=\alpha,
&\mathbb P(X=1)&=1-2\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\alpha\) always denotes a row's column-zero probability, not the fixed-family conversion constant in `bf1ff94487296374`. Given \(X=0\) or \(X=1\), \(Y\) has probabilities proportional to \(e^{\beta y}\) on \(0,\ldots,2m\) or \(0,\ldots,m\), respectively; given \(X=2\), \(Y=0\). Let \(\mu_0,\mu_1\) be the two conditional geometric means. The mean and entropy identities are
\[
t=\alpha\mu_0+(1-2\alpha)\mu_1,\qquad
H(\nu_{(1,t)})=\log\bigl(2\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}+A_1(\beta)\bigr)-\beta t.
\tag{3}
\]

Here is also a direct justification of the parameterization and its endpoints. Define \(f(\beta)=\log(2\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}+A_1(\beta))\). Differentiation gives \(f'(\beta)=t\) as in (3). Both \(\tfrac12\log A_0\) and \(\log A_1\) have strictly positive second derivatives, since \(m\ge1\) and their geometric interval laws are nondegenerate. The second derivative of the logarithm of their exponentials' sum is their positive weighted second derivative plus the variance of their first derivatives. Thus \(f''>0\). As \(\beta\to-\infty\), the row tends to the uniform law on \((0,0),(1,0),(2,0)\), and \(f'(\beta)\to0\). As \(\beta\to+\infty\), it tends to the uniform law on \((0,2m),(1,m),(2,0)\), and \(f'(\beta)\to m\). Every \(0<t<m\) therefore occurs once. An exponential law is the entropy maximizer at its own mean by the same relative-entropy identity used above, which identifies these laws with the rows already defined. The endpoint uniform laws maximize entropy on their three-point faces. In particular the integer endpoint rows \((1,0),(1,m)\) have \(\alpha=1/3\), and the row entropy is continuous on \(0\le t\le m\).

For later use write \(\alpha_j=\mathbb P_{\nu_{(1,j)}}(X=0)\), including these endpoint values. At every middle root,
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{(1,j)}}X=2\alpha_j.
\tag{4}
\]
For a finite-parameter row, \(T\) changes its parameters to \(\beta'=-\beta\), \(\lambda_x'=\lambda_x-m\beta\). Indeed \(A_0(-\beta)=e^{-2m\beta}A_0(\beta)\), so \(\lambda_x'=\tfrac12\log A_0(-\beta)\). It preserves \(\alpha\) and sends its mean \((1,t)\) to \((1,m-t)\). At \(\beta=0\) the mean is \((1,m/2)\); the strict increase of \(f'\) shows that \(\beta\le0\) is equivalent to \(t\le m/2\).

We first bound self-varentropy, without assuming any general rank-two extension of `1ed6b6d9eb994c90`. A middle row can be reflected by \(T\) for this estimate alone, so assume its row parameter \(\beta\le0\). Its source parameter need not be reflected in this auxiliary estimate. For \(q=e^\beta\le1\), the last \(m\) terms of \(A_0(\beta)\) have sum at most \(A_1(\beta)\). Therefore \(A_1(\beta)\ge A_0(\beta)/2\), and (2) gives \(\alpha\le2/\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}\). Consequently
\[
\lambda_x^2\operatorname{Var}X
=\frac{(\log A_0)^2}{4}\,2\alpha
\le\frac{(\log A_0)^2}{\sqrt{A_0}}
\le\frac{16}{e^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
The last inequality is the maximum of \(v^2e^{-v/2}\) for \(v\ge0\), attained at \(v=4\).

For \(\beta<0\), either conditional geometric interval law is an infinite geometric law of ratio \(q\) conditioned to lie in an initial interval. This conditioning decreases its second moment. The infinite law's second moment is \(q(1+q)/(1-q)^2\). The elementary bound used in the rank-one proof of `1ed6b6d9eb994c90` gives
\[
\beta^2\frac{q}{(1-q)^2}
=\left(\frac{|\beta|}{2\sinh(|\beta|/2)}\right)^2\le1.
\]
Thus \(\beta^2\mathbb EY^2\le2\), including the column mixture in (2), and \(\beta^2\operatorname{Var}Y\le2\). For \(\beta=0\), this product is zero. Since the row's log density is an affine function with score \(\lambda_xX+\beta Y\), the elementary variance inequality yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\log\nu_{(1,j)}(X,Y)
\le2\lambda_x^2\operatorname{Var}X+2\beta^2\operatorname{Var}Y
\le K_{\mathrm{self}}.
\tag{6}
\]
Reflection preserves the left side, so this covers every finite row parameter. The two endpoint middle rows are uniform and have self-varentropy zero. Each column-zero row has self-varentropy at most \(1\) by the rank-one statement of `1ed6b6d9eb994c90`, and the apex has zero. Hence (6), with the same bound, holds at every root.

We next prove a vertical second-moment bound in the fixed, source-normalized coordinates. No log-concavity of the three-column \(Y\)-marginal is asserted. The elementary moment calculation for a finite geometric interval law, also proved for finite log-concave laws in `81ad175d3c410508`, gives
\[
\mathbb EU^2\le2\mu^2+\mu\quad\text{when}\quad\mathbb EU=\mu.
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, its survival probabilities \(s_k=\mathbb P(U\ge k)\) satisfy \(s_{u+v}\le s_us_v\). To see this, if its masses are \(g_k\) on \(0,\ldots,N\), their consecutive ratio is constant. Thus \(s_k/g_k=\sum_{r=0}^{N-k}g_{k+r}/g_k\) is nonincreasing in \(k\), so \(s_{k+1}/s_k=1-g_k/s_k\) is nonincreasing. Comparing products of these survival ratios proves the claimed submultiplicativity, including zero tails. The tail identities now give \(\mathbb E[U(U-1)]/2=\sum_{u,v\ge1}s_{u+v}\le(\sum_{u\ge1}s_u)^2=\mu^2\), proving (7).

For the two geometric means in (3), \(\mu_0\ge\mu_1\), since the shorter law is the longer law conditioned on \(Y\le m\). Uniformly over all \(q=e^\beta>0\), we also have \(\mu_0\le5\mu_1\). If \(q\ge1\), pairing \(k\) with \(m-k\) in the shorter interval gives \(\mu_1\ge m/2\), while \(\mu_0\le2m\), proving the stronger factor \(4\). If \(q\le1\), let \(b\) be the longer law's probability of \(Y\ge m+1\). Its conditional mean on this event is at most \(m+1+\mu_1\), since subtracting \(m+1\) gives the ratio-\(q\) law on the shorter interval \(0,\ldots,m-1\). Also \(b\le q^{m+1}\). Since
\[
\mu_1=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^m kq^k}{\sum_{k=0}^m q^k}
\ge\frac{q^m m(m+1)/2}{m+1}=\frac m2q^m,
\]
we obtain \(\mu_0\le\mu_1+b(m+1)\le\mu_1+2(m+1)\mu_1/m\le5\mu_1\). On the other hand (3) implies \(j\ge(1-\alpha_j)\mu_1\ge\mu_1/2\). Thus \(\mu_1\le2j\) and \(\mu_0\le10j\). Applying (7) conditionally in the two columns, rather than to their mixture, proves, for integer \(j\ge1\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{(1,j)}}Y\le\mathbb EY^2
\le200j^2+10j\le210j^2.
\tag{8}
\]
The endpoint middle rows obey this as limits; the row \(j=0\) has \(Y=0\). A column-zero row with integer mean \(j\ge1\) obeys \(\mathbb EY^2\le2j^2+j\le3j^2\) by (7). The apex has \(Y=0\). Thus at every root \(\mathbf j\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Y\le210j_y^2.
\tag{9}
\]

We control the average of \(a^2j_y^2\) by a support-specific downward transfer. Let \(\mathcal H_0(t)=H(\nu_{(0,t)})\) on \([0,2m]\), and \(\mathcal H_1(t)=H(\nu_{(1,t)})\) on \([0,m]\). For a finite row parameter \(\beta\), the map \((x,y)\mapsto(x,y-k)\) is an injection from the row support with \(y\ge k\) into the same support. This is valid both for the full triangle and for its column-zero face. It preserves the \(x\)-coordinate, and a point's exponential weight is \(e^{\beta k}\) times its image's weight. If \(\beta<0\), it follows that \(\mathbb P(Y\ge k)\le e^{\beta k}\), and hence
\[
t=\mathbb EY\le\frac{e^\beta}{1-e^\beta},\qquad
e^\beta\ge\frac{t}{t+1}.
\tag{10}
\]
The entropy derivative on each open segment is \(\mathcal H_x'(t)=-\beta(t)\). For the middle segment this follows from (3), \(f'(\beta)=t\), and \(f''>0\); for column zero it follows by differentiating its geometric partition function, whose second derivative is its positive variance. Therefore, for all \(t\) in either open segment, (10) gives
\[
\mathcal H_x'(t)\le\log(1+1/t)=h_{\mathrm{geom}}'(t),\qquad
h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)=(t+1)\log(t+1)-t\log t.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(\beta\ge0\), the inequality follows directly from \(-\beta\le0\). Define \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(0)=0\). The geometric interval rows tend to Dirac laws at their two endpoints, and the middle rows have the endpoint limits already proved. Thus both entropy profiles are continuous on their closed segments, and (11) shows that \(\mathcal H_x-h_{\mathrm{geom}}\) is nonincreasing there. This is a proof on the specified triangle support, not an application of a simplex entropy-profile theorem to it.

By (1), conditional on a root's first coordinate \(x=0\) or \(x=1\), its second-coordinate weight is proportional to \(e^{-3ak+2\mathcal H_x(k)}\) on its finite segment. If \(a=0\), its vertical cost vanishes. For \(a>0\), let \(\pi_a(k)\) on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be proportional to \(e^{-3ak+2h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k)}\). The actual conditional root law is \(\pi_a\) multiplied by the nonnegative nonincreasing function \(e^{2(\mathcal H_x(k)-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k))}\) on the segment, extended by zero afterwards. Multiplication by a nonincreasing function decreases expectations of increasing functions: for independent \(K,K'\) of law \(\pi_a\),
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(\varphi(K),g(K))
=\mathbb E[(\varphi(K)-\varphi(K'))(g(K)-g(K'))]\le0.
\]
The normalization is positive, and all moments used here are finite. We can therefore use the geometric reference calculation of `81ad175d3c410508`, with the exact same \(a\) and reference law \(\pi_a\). Explicitly, \(\log(k+1)\le h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k)\le\log(k+1)+1\). For \(z=e^{-3a}\in(0,1)\), the exact power series give
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(z)&=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^2z^k=\frac{1+z}{(1-z)^3},\\
Q(z)&=\sum_{k\ge0}k^2(k+1)^2z^k
=\frac{4z(1+4z+z^2)}{(1-z)^5}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \((1+4z+z^2)/(1+z)\le3\) and \(z/(1-z)^2\le1/(9a^2)\), we get \(\mathbb E_{\pi_a}(aK)^2\le e^2a^2Q(z)/P(z)\le4e^2/3\). The last inequality for \(z/(1-z)^2\) again follows from \(\sinh(3a/2)\ge3a/2\). Applying the monotone comparison with \(\varphi(k)=(ak)^2\), and also including the apex's zero cost, proves
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}(a j_y)^2\le\frac43e^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}\theta^2
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Y\le280e^2.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to control the source-height price \(\tau^2\operatorname{Var}X\). For the middle entropy row put \(H_j=H(\nu_{(1,j)})\) and \(h_j=\min(j,m-j)\), for \(0\le j\le m\). Exactly \(2h_j+3\) support points occur in reflected pairs about \((1,j)\): there are \(2h_j+1\) points in column one and the additional pair \((0,2j),(2,0)\). The uniform law on these points has mean \((1,j)\). Entropy maximality therefore gives \(e^{H_j}\ge2h_j+3\).

For the upper bound, a nonnegative integer-valued law of mean \(t\) has entropy at most \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)\). For \(t>0\), this follows by its nonnegative relative entropy to the infinite geometric law of mean \(t\), whose log mass is affine in the integer argument; the case \(t=0\) is deterministic. Joint entropy is at most the sum of marginal entropies, so \(H_j\le\log3+h_{\mathrm{geom}}(j)\). Applying \(T\) to the row gives the same bound with \(m-j\). Hence
\[
2h_j+3\le e^{H_j}\le3e(h_j+1).
\tag{13}
\]
We also need the quantitative column mass bound
\[
\alpha_j\le\frac{2}{\sqrt{h_j+1}}.
\tag{14}
\]
To prove it, first suppose \(\beta\le0\), so \(j\le m/2\). The downward-transfer estimate applied within either conditional geometric interval gives its mean at most the sum of its nonzero geometric weights. Thus \(\mu_0\le A_0(\beta)-1\) and \(\mu_1\le A_1(\beta)-1\le A_0(\beta)-1\). Equation (3) implies \(j\le A_0(\beta)-1\). Together with \(\alpha\le2/\sqrt{A_0(\beta)}\), this proves (14) with \(h_j=j\). For positive \(\beta\), reflect the row: \(\alpha\) is unchanged and its new mean is \(m-j=h_j\). The endpoint values \(\alpha_0=\alpha_m=1/3\) also satisfy (14).

Write \(H_y^{(0)}=H(\nu_{(0,y)})\). The analogous reflected interval and geometric-entropy arguments give
\[
2\min(y,2m-y)+1\le e^{H_y^{(0)}}
\le e\bigl(\min(y,2m-y)+1\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(z=e^{3\theta}\), with \(0<z\le1\), define finite sums
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_0(\theta)&=\sum_{y=0}^{2m}z^y e^{2H_y^{(0)}},\\
F(\theta)&=\sum_{j=0}^{m}z^j e^{2H_j},\\
G(\theta)&=\sum_{j=0}^{m}z^j e^{2H_j}\alpha_j,\\
L_{\mathrm{eff}}&=\min\left(m+1,\frac1{1-z}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
At \(z=1\) the reciprocal in (16) means \(+\infty\), so \(L_{\mathrm{eff}}=m+1\). The quantity \(B_0\) is an unnormalized column-zero contribution to \(Z^3B_2\), not a differently normalized version of \(B_2\).

Here are finite-sum estimates with explicit uniform constants. For every \(N\ge0\), put \(L_N=\min(N+1,1/(1-z))\) with the same convention at \(z=1\). Then
\[
\frac{L_N^3}{384}\le\sum_{k=0}^N(k+1)^2z^k\le2L_N^3,
\qquad
\sum_{k=0}^N(k+1)^{3/2}z^k\le2L_N^{5/2}.
\tag{17}
\]
For the quadratic upper bound, the finite sum is at most \((N+1)^3\), while, for \(z<1\), the exact infinite sum \(P(z)\) is at most \(2/(1-z)^3\). Taking the smaller bound proves the assertion, including \(z=1\) by the finite bound. The other upper bound follows similarly: its finite bound is \((N+1)^{5/2}\), and Hölder's inequality bounds its infinite sum by \(P(z)^{3/4}(\sum_{k\ge0}z^k)^{1/4}\le2^{3/4}(1-z)^{-5/2}\le2(1-z)^{-5/2}\).

For the lower bound, if \(L_N<2\), the term \(k=0\) is already at least \(L_N^3/8\). Otherwise take \(r=\lfloor L_N/2\rfloor\ge L_N/4\). The indices \(0,\ldots,r-1\) lie in the finite sum. Moreover \(z\ge1-1/L_N\), and the integer Bernoulli inequality yields \(z^k\ge1-k/L_N\ge1/2\) on those indices. Thus their sum is at least \(\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^{r}i^2\ge r^3/6\ge L_N^3/384\). This proves (17) without replacing a finite sum by an unjustified asymptotic.

Apply (17) and (13) through (15). For \(B_0\)'s lower bound use \(0\le y\le m\), where \(2\min(y,2m-y)+1\ge y+1\). For its upper bound use \(e^{H_y^{(0)}}\le e(y+1)\) and \(L_{2m}\le2L_{\mathrm{eff}}\). For \(F\)'s lower bound use \(0\le j\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), where \(h_j=j\) and \(L_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\ge L_{\mathrm{eff}}/2\). For both remaining upper bounds use \(h_j\le j\). The resulting bounds are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{L_{\mathrm{eff}}^3}{384}
&\le B_0\le16e^2L_{\mathrm{eff}}^3,\\
\frac{L_{\mathrm{eff}}^3}{3072}
&\le F\le18e^2L_{\mathrm{eff}}^3,\\
G&\le36e^2L_{\mathrm{eff}}^{5/2},
\qquad \frac GF\le\frac{110592e^2}{\sqrt{L_{\mathrm{eff}}}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
In particular these inequalities include \(m=1\), \(\theta=0\), and every boundary middle row.

The total root-weight denominator in (1) is \(B_0+e^{3\tau}F+e^{6\tau}\), the last term coming from the apex's zero entropy. Let
\[
W=\frac{e^{3\tau}F}{B_0+e^{3\tau}F+e^{6\tau}},\qquad
u=3\tau-\tfrac12\log B_0.
\]
Thus \(W\) is the total \(\omega\)-mass of the middle roots. Since \(e^{3\tau}=\sqrt{B_0}e^u\), we have
\[
W\le\min\left(1,\frac{F}{\sqrt{B_0}}\frac{e^u}{1+e^{2u}}\right)
\le\min\left(1,18e^2\sqrt{384}\,
L_{\mathrm{eff}}^{3/2}e^{-|u|}\right).
\tag{19}
\]
This confines the middle mass to a logarithmic band but retains the exponential tails needed for an unbounded \(\tau\).

To bound these tails explicitly, put
\[
R=\log(18e^2\sqrt{384})+\tfrac32\log L_{\mathrm{eff}},\qquad
c=\tfrac12\log B_0.
\]
The term \(y=0\) shows \(B_0\ge1\), so \(c\ge0\), and (18) gives \(c\le\tfrac12\log(16e^2)+\tfrac32\log L_{\mathrm{eff}}\). Also \(R\ge0\). From \(|\tau|\le(|u|+c)/3\) and (19),
\[
\tau^2W\le\frac19(|u|+c)^2\min(1,e^{R-|u|})
\le\frac19(R+c+2)^2
\le\frac19(K_*+3\log L_{\mathrm{eff}})^2.
\tag{20}
\]
For the middle inequality, if \(|u|\le R\), simply use \(|u|+c\le R+c\). Otherwise set \(v=|u|-R>0\); then \((R+c+v)e^{-v/2}\le R+c+2\), because \(ve^{-v/2}\le2/e\le2\). Squaring proves the other case.

The required weighted column mass is, exactly,
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m}\omega_{(1,j)}\tau^2\alpha_j
=\tau^2W\frac GF.
\]
Combining (18) and (20), and using \(L_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge1\), bounds this by
\[
\frac{110592e^2}{9\sqrt{L_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
(K_*+3\log L_{\mathrm{eff}})^2
\le\frac{110592e^2}{9}
\left(2K_*^2+\frac{288}{e^2}\right)
=K_{\mathrm{height}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Here \((s+t)^2\le2s^2+2t^2\), and \((\log L)^2/\sqrt L\le16/e^2\) for \(L\ge1\). This last bound is again the maximum of \(v^2e^{-v/2}\). Since the other columns' rows have deterministic \(X\), (4) and (21) give
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}\tau^2
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}X\le2K_{\mathrm{height}}.
\tag{22}
\]

Finally, the log source density has score \(\tau X+\theta Y\). At every root the two elementary variance inequalities yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathbf j}
&\le2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}
 +2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\tau X+\theta Y)\\
&\le2K_{\mathrm{self}}
 +4\tau^2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}X
 +4\theta^2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Y.
\end{aligned}
\]
Average this inequality against \(\omega\), then use (12) and (22). The result is \(\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}\le2K_{\mathrm{self}}+8K_{\mathrm{height}}+1120e^2=V_0\). The initial affine reflection makes this conclusion valid for arbitrary source \(\theta\), not just \(\theta\le0\). All constants are numerical and independent of \(m,\tau,\theta\), and all face rows were included throughout.
