## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(K\) be an integer with \(0\le K\le2n\), and let \(\beta\in\mathbb R^n\). Define the original objects

\[
\begin{aligned}
F&=\left\{I\in\{0,1,2\}^n:\sum_i I_i=K\right\},
&w_I&=e^{-\beta\cdot I},
&W&=\sum_{I\in F}w_I,\\
p_I&=w_I/W,
&\tau_w(J)&=\inf_{H\ni J}\sum_{I\in F\cap H}w_I,
&A&=\sum_{J\in F}w_J\tau_w(J),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the infimum is over containing closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^n\). Let \(\nu_J\) be exactly the unique maximum-entropy law of barycenter \(J\) on the minimal-face support specified in accepted fact \(\texttt{021936625d57d0a7}\), specialized to \(m_i=2\). Put

\[
R_1(J)=\left(\sum_{I\in F}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
B_1=\sum_{J\in F}p_JR_1(J).
\]

Then, uniformly in \(n,K,\beta\),

\[
B_1\ge \frac{(11/20)^n}{(2n+1)^3}
\ge \frac{2^{-n}}{648000},
\qquad
A\ge \frac{\eta^2}{648000}\,2^{-n}W^2,
\quad
\eta=(4\cdot10^6)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, the requested fixed exponent and absolute constant can be taken as \(\epsilon=1\) and \(\alpha=1/648000\).

## Proof

We first record the elementary convolution property used twice below. A finite nonnegative sequence with interval support is log-concave when its consecutive terms satisfy \(f(j)^2\ge f(j-1)f(j+1)\). Its positive successive ratios are nonincreasing. Consequently, moving two indices inward while preserving their sum can only increase the product of their terms.

For such a sequence, every two-by-two minor of its Toeplitz matrix \((f(s-r))_{r,s\in\mathbb Z}\) is nonnegative. Indeed, for \(r<r'\) and \(s<s'\), the diagonal indices \(s-r,s'-r'\) lie between \(s-r'\) and \(s'-r\) and have the same sum, so the preceding inward-move inequality applies. Zeros cause no problem because the support is an interval. For two such Toeplitz matrices, their product is the Toeplitz matrix of the convolution. The two-by-two Cauchy-Binet identity expresses each minor of the product as a sum, over two intermediate indices, of products of their nonnegative minors. The sums needed for each fixed minor are finite because the sequences have finite support. Taking consecutive rows and columns proves log-concavity of the convolution; its support is again an interval. Thus finite convolutions preserve this property.

For \(k\ge0\), let \(T_k=\#\{u\in\{-1,0,1\}^k:\sum_i u_i=0\}\).

The coefficients of \((x^{-1}+1+x)^k\) are symmetric and log-concave by the convolution property, so their largest coefficient is the central one. Their sum is \(3^k\), and there are \(2k+1\) possible exponents. Therefore

\[
T_k\ge\frac{3^k}{2k+1},
\qquad T_0=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Use centered coordinates \(z=I-\mathbf1\), where \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), and set

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(\beta)&=\sum_{\substack{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\\\sum_i z_i=K-n}}
e^{-\beta\cdot z},\\
Z_B(\beta)&=\sum_{\substack{u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{B}\\\sum_{i\in B}u_i=0}}
e^{-\sum_{i\in B}\beta_i u_i}
\quad(B\subseteq[n]),
\qquad Z_{\varnothing}(\beta)=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(W=e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf1}Z(\beta)\), so the original normalized law is \(p_{\mathbf1+z}=e^{-\beta\cdot z}/Z(\beta)\).

For an original center \(J\), let \(B=\{i:J_i=1\}\), put \(k=|B|\), and let \(v=(J_i-1)_{i\notin B}\). Then \(v\in\{-1,1\}^{B^c}\) and \(\sum_{i\notin B}v_i=K-n\). Conversely, every such pair \(B,v\) specifies exactly one original center. Thus these pairs form a partition of the centers, with no multiplicities.

The minimal-face support from fact \(\texttt{021936625d57d0a7}\) fixes the coordinates outside \(B\) at \(J_i\). On \(B\), its centered coordinates range over the balanced layer in (3). Uniform measure on this set has mean zero in each active centered coordinate, because \(u\mapsto-u\) is a bijection. It therefore has barycenter \(J\). Uniform measure maximizes entropy over all laws on this \(T_k\)-point support, and hence it is exactly the prescribed \(\nu_J\). This also covers \(k=0\) and \(k=1\), when \(T_k=1\).

The map \(u\mapsto-u\) also gives \(Z_B(-\beta)=Z_B(\beta)\). Direct substitution of the uniform entropy law and the original \(p_I\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{I\in F}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}
&=\frac{Z(\beta)}{T_k^2}
e^{\sum_{i\notin B}\beta_i v_i}Z_B(\beta),\\
R_1(J)
&=\frac{e^{-\sum_{i\notin B}\beta_i v_i}}{Z(\beta)}
\frac{T_k^2}{Z_B(\beta)},\\
B_1
&=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)^2}
\sum_{B\subseteq[n]}\frac{T_{|B|}^2}{Z_B(\beta)}
\sum_{\substack{v\in\{-1,1\}^{B^c}\\\sum_{i\notin B}v_i=K-n}}
e^{-2\sum_{i\notin B}\beta_i v_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

An empty inner sum contributes zero. For \(K=n\), (4) is precisely the proposed balanced-layer formula.

For any real \(\lambda\), put \(a_i=\beta_i-\lambda\). On the centered layer, \(e^{-\sum_i a_i z_i}=e^{\lambda(K-n)}e^{-\beta\cdot z}\), so \(Z(a)=e^{\lambda(K-n)}Z(\beta)\) and the normalized law remains exactly the original \(p\). The active balanced partition sums satisfy \(Z_B(a)=Z_B(\beta)\), and the inner sums in the last line of (4) acquire the factor \(e^{2\lambda(K-n)}\). Thus (4) is unchanged when written with \(a\). This common shift is solely a freedom in the centered-coordinate calculation; all original \(w_I,W,\tau_w,A\) retain their definitions.

Write \(d(a)=1+2\cosh a\) and \(h(a)=9/d(a)\). Every term in a face partition sum is also a term of the unrestricted product sum, so \(Z_B(a)\le\prod_{i\in B}d(a_i)\).

Combining this exact finite-sum comparison with (2), for every \(B\subseteq[n]\) we obtain

\[
\frac{T_{|B|}^2}{Z_B(a)}
\ge\frac{1}{(2n+1)^2}\prod_{i\in B}h(a_i).
\tag{5}
\]

The loss in (5) depends only polynomially on \(n\); it will be paid explicitly by a strict exponential surplus. Inserting (5) into the exact original-center sum (4), and denoting a Laurent coefficient by \([x^j]\), gives

\[
B_1\ge
\frac{[x^{K-n}]\prod_{i=1}^n
\bigl(e^{2a_i}x^{-1}+h(a_i)+e^{-2a_i}x\bigr)}
{(2n+1)^2 Z(a)^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

We next construct a coefficientwise minorant of each factor in (6). Let \(q(a)=\min\{1,h(a)^2\}\). If \(a\ge0\), set
\(g_{-1}(a)=e^{2a}\), \(g_0(a)=h(a)\), and \(g_1(a)=q(a)e^{-2a}\).
If \(a<0\), set
\(g_{-1}(a)=q(a)e^{2a}\), \(g_0(a)=h(a)\), and \(g_1(a)=e^{-2a}\).
These three functions are positive and continuous, including at zero, because \(h(0)=3\) and \(q=1\) near zero. They do not exceed the respective coefficients of the original factor. Moreover, \(g_{-1}(a)g_1(a)=q(a)\le h(a)^2=g_0(a)^2\).

Thus they form a log-concave sequence on \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Put \(G(a)=g_{-1}(a)+g_0(a)+g_1(a)\). We claim the uniform surplus

\[
\frac{G(a)}{d(a)^2}\ge\frac{11}{20}
\quad(a\in\mathbb R).
\tag{7}
\]

First, before taking the minorant, let \(y=\cosh a\ge1\). An exact calculation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{e^{2a}+h(a)+e^{-2a}}{d(a)^2}-\frac{11}{20}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{72y^3-52y^2-146y+129}{20(1+2y)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

With \(u=y-1\ge0\), the numerator equals
\(72u^3+164u^2-34u+3
=72u^3+164(u-17/164)^2+203/164>0\).
This proves (7) whenever \(h(a)\ge1\), because then \(q(a)=1\) and the minorant equals the original factor.

If \(h(a)<1\), put \(r=e^{|a|}\ge1\). Then \(d(a)=r+1+r^{-1}>9\), while \(d(a)\le r+2\), so \(r>7\). The dominant endpoint is retained in the minorant, whence

\[
\frac{G(a)}{d(a)^2}
\ge\frac{r^2}{(r+2)^2}
>\frac{49}{81}>\frac{11}{20}.
\]

This proves (7) for all \(a\).

Suppose first that \(0<K<2n\). For a real \(\lambda\), let independent variables \(X_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\) have probabilities \(g_j(\beta_i-\lambda)/G(\beta_i-\lambda)\) at \(j\). Each mean is continuous in \(\lambda\). As \(\lambda\to-\infty\), the coefficient at \(-1\) dominates and each mean tends to \(-1\); as \(\lambda\to+\infty\), each mean tends to \(1\). The intermediate value theorem therefore supplies a finite \(\lambda\) for which

\[
\mathbb E\sum_i X_i=K-n.
\tag{8}
\]

Fix that \(\lambda\) and the corresponding \(a_i\). Let \(b_j=\mathbb P(\sum_iX_i=j)\) for \(-n\le j\le n\). Every \(b_j\) is positive, and the convolution property makes this sequence log-concave. Interpolate \(j\mapsto\log b_j\) linearly between consecutive integers; the resulting function is concave. Jensen's inequality at the integer mean in (8) therefore gives

\[
\log b_{K-n}\ge\sum_{j=-n}^n b_j\log b_j
\ge-\log(2n+1).
\tag{9}
\]

For the last inequality, convexity of \(t\log t\), applied to the \(2n+1\) numbers \(b_j\), gives
\(\sum_j b_j\log b_j\ge(2n+1)(1/(2n+1))\log(1/(2n+1))\).
In particular, \(b_{K-n}\ge1/(2n+1)\).

All Laurent coefficients are nonnegative, so coefficientwise minorization, followed by (9), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&[x^{K-n}]\prod_i
\bigl(e^{2a_i}x^{-1}+h(a_i)+e^{-2a_i}x\bigr)\\
&\qquad\ge[x^{K-n}]\prod_i
\bigl(g_{-1}(a_i)x^{-1}+g_0(a_i)+g_1(a_i)x\bigr)\\
&\qquad=b_{K-n}\prod_iG(a_i)
\ge\frac{1}{2n+1}\prod_iG(a_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Also \(Z(a)\le\prod_i d(a_i)\), because \(Z(a)\) is one coefficient sum of the unrestricted product partition sum. Equations (6), (7), and (10) now establish

\[
B_1\ge
\frac{1}{(2n+1)^3}\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{G(a_i)}{d(a_i)^2}
\ge\frac{(11/20)^n}{(2n+1)^3}.
\tag{11}
\]

When \(K=0\) or \(K=2n\), the layer has one point and \(B_1=1\), so (11) holds in these cases too.

For \(n\ge4\), the binomial theorem and \(n-1,n-2\ge n/2\) give

\[
\frac{(11/10)^n}{(2n+1)^3}
\ge\frac{\binom n3}{1000(3n)^3}
\ge\frac{1}{648000}.
\]

For \(1\le n\le3\), this ratio is at least \(1/343>1/648000\). This proves both Rényi inequalities in (1).

Finally, fact \(\texttt{021936625d57d0a7}\) gives \(\nu_J(H)\ge\eta\) for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(J\). Cauchy-Schwarz on the finite support, with the original positive \(p_I\), yields

\[
\eta\le\nu_J(H)
\le\left(\sum_{I\in F}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{I\in F\cap H}p_I\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Thus \(\tau_w(J)/W\ge\eta^2R_1(J)\). Multiplying by the original \(p_J\) and summing over every original center proves
\(A/W^2\ge\eta^2B_1\), and hence the last inequality in (1). Boundary atoms, singleton entropy laws, and all containing closed halfspaces are included throughout.
