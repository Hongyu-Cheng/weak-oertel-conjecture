## Statement

Let \(m\ge1\) and \(L\ge2\) be integers, put \(k=m+L\) and \(N=k+1\), and let
$$
\mathcal Q_0\subseteq
\left\lbrace(b,i):b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\ 1\le i\le m,\ b_i=0\right\rbrace
$$
be arbitrary, possibly empty. For each \(q\in\mathcal Q_0\), choose an arbitrary group \(G_q\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\) with \(n_q:=|G_q|\ge2\). No incidence or size-budget condition is imposed. Write the coordinates as \((y,t)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^L\). For \(q=(b,i)\), define
$$
E_q=\left\lbrace(b+2e_i,t):
\begin{array}{l}
\text{exactly one neutral coordinate is two, with its index in }G_q,\\
\text{and every other neutral coordinate is binary}
\end{array}\right\rbrace,
\qquad E=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}E_q.
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th active unit vector. Suppose that the actual finite support \(D\) satisfies
$$
\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
$$
every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two, and its exact set of double points is
$$
\left\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace j:x_j=2\rbrace=2\right\rbrace=E.
$$
The actual points with at most one two may otherwise be arbitrary.

Choose arbitrary active activities \(\rho_i>0\) and neutral activities \(a_h>0\). Initially assume
$$
a_h\in[1/2,2]\quad\text{for every }h\in\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}G_q;
\tag{1}
$$
activities outside this union are unrestricted. Use exactly the original affine weights and normalization
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,\\
w_{(y,t)}&=\prod_{i=1}^m\rho_i^{y_i}\prod_{h=1}^La_h^{t_h}
=\exp(-\gamma\cdot y-\eta\cdot t),\\
\gamma_i&=-\log\rho_i,\qquad \eta_h=-\log a_h,\\
Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) only as an abbreviation. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),\\
T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum counts ordered pairs, including the diagonal once. Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; allowing the whole space additionally does not change the depth. Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-64}8^{-N},\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-64}8^{-N}.
\tag{2}
$$
Moreover, (2) remains true if (1) is replaced by the weaker hypothesis
$$
\left|\left\lbrace h\in G_q:a_h\in[1/2,2]\right\rbrace\right|\ge2
\quad\text{for every }q\in\mathcal Q_0,
\tag{3}
$$
with all other neutral activities arbitrary positive numbers.

## Proof

We use accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 for its balanced private-leaf bound with private size at least two, and accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 for its final complete one-step support bound. Every application below identifies its support, field, normalization, and extra-zero embedding. The remaining scalar and geometric arguments are proved here.

We first record the exact restriction comparison. Let \(\pi\) be a normalized law on a finite set \(V\subseteq\mathbb R^h\), and let \(\Phi:V\to D\) be injective and extend affinely to the ambient spaces. Suppose
$$
p_{\Phi(u)}=\lambda\pi_u\quad(u\in V),\qquad \lambda=p(\Phi(V)).
$$
The map \((u,s)\mapsto(\Phi(u),s)\) pulls a containing closed halfspace in the original extra-zero embedding back to a containing closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{h+1}\), or to the whole space. In the latter case its conditional mass is one and is at least the conditional depth. Therefore
$$
\tau_p((\Phi(c),0))\ge\lambda\tau_\pi((c,0)).
$$
Every ordered reflected pair about \(c\) maps injectively to an ordered reflected pair about \(\Phi(c)\), and its minimum probability is multiplied by exactly \(\lambda\). Hence
$$
s_p(\Phi(c))\ge\lambda s_\pi(c).
$$
It follows, using the center probability as well, that
$$
M_3(p)\ge\lambda^4M_3(\pi),\qquad
T(p)\ge\lambda^4T(\pi).
\tag{4}
$$
For disjoint image sets their right-hand contributions can be summed. This argument uses the actual containing halfspaces and ordered reflection sums, without a depth factorization.

We next prove a finite scalar inequality. Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_s>0\) and let \(n_1,\ldots,n_s\) be positive integers. Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_0&=\sum_{i=1}^s n_ic_i,\\
\Delta_0&=\sum_{i=1}^s n_i^6c_i^4,\\
\mathcal K_0&=\sum_{\substack{1\le i,j\le s\\i\ne j}}
n_in_j\min(c_i,c_j)^{7/6}\max(c_i,c_j)^{1/6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For an empty list all three quantities are zero. We claim
$$
U_0\le(3/2)^{1/4}\mathcal K_0^{3/4}
+3^{3/4}\Delta_0^{1/4}.
\tag{5}
$$
For a nonempty list order the indices so that \(c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_s\), and let \(M_i=\sum_{j<i}n_j\). Call an index small if \(n_i\le M_i\), and large otherwise. The two ordered terms for \(j<i\) show
$$
\mathcal K_0
=2\sum_i\sum_{j<i}n_in_jc_i^{7/6}c_j^{1/6}
\ge2\sum_i n_iM_ic_i^{4/3}.
\tag{6}
$$
Expand each small index into the \(n_i\) distinct integer ranks \(r=M_i+1,\ldots,M_i+n_i\). These ranks are disjoint across all indices. Since \(n_i\le M_i\),
$$
\sum_{i\text{ small}}\sum_{r=M_i+1}^{M_i+n_i}r c_i^{4/3}
\le\sum_{i\text{ small}}n_i(M_i+n_i)c_i^{4/3}
\le2\sum_{i\text{ small}}n_iM_ic_i^{4/3}
\le\mathcal K_0.
$$
Hölder's inequality with exponents \(4/3\) and \(4\), together with
\(\sum_{r\ge1}r^{-3}\le1+\int_1^\infty x^{-3}\,dx=3/2\), gives
$$
\sum_{i\text{ small}}n_ic_i
=\sum_{i\text{ small}}\sum_{r=M_i+1}^{M_i+n_i}
(r c_i^{4/3})^{3/4}r^{-3/4}
\le(3/2)^{1/4}\mathcal K_0^{3/4}.
\tag{7}
$$
List the large indices in their original order as \(i_1,\ldots,i_v\). Their integer sizes satisfy
$$
n_{i_u}>M_{i_u}\ge\sum_{a<u}n_{i_a},
$$
so induction gives \(n_{i_u}\ge2^{u-1}\). Consequently
$$
\sum_{i\text{ large}}n_i^{-2/3}
\le\sum_{u\ge1}2^{-2(u-1)/3}
=\frac1{1-2^{-2/3}}<3.
$$
The strict inequality follows from \(2^{2/3}>3/2\), whose cube is \(4>27/8\). Hölder with exponents \(4\) and \(4/3\) now yields
$$
\sum_{i\text{ large}}n_ic_i
=\sum_{i\text{ large}}(n_i^6c_i^4)^{1/4}n_i^{-1/2}
\le\Delta_0^{1/4}
\left(\sum_{i\text{ large}}n_i^{-2/3}\right)^{3/4}
\le3^{3/4}\Delta_0^{1/4}.
\tag{8}
$$
Equations (7) and (8) prove (5), including lists with only one index.

For our labels put
$$
c_q=\prod_{j=1}^m\rho_j^{b_j+2\mathbf1_{j=i}}
\quad(q=(b,i)),
\qquad
U=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}n_qc_q,
\qquad
\Delta=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}n_q^6c_q^4.
$$
For each \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), apply (5) to its labels \((b,i)\in\mathcal Q_0\). Denote the corresponding quantities by \(U_b,\Delta_b,\mathcal K_b\), and put
$$
\mathcal K=\sum_b\mathcal K_b
=\sum_{b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}
\sum_{\substack{q=(b,i),\ q'=(b,j)\in\mathcal Q_0\\i\ne j}}
n_qn_{q'}\min(c_q,c_{q'})^{7/6}\max(c_q,c_{q'})^{1/6}.
\tag{9}
$$
The inner sum is ordered. Empty classes have zero quantities. Hölder over the \(2^m\) classes gives
$$
U\le(3/2)^{1/4}2^{m/4}\mathcal K^{3/4}
+3^{3/4}2^{3m/4}\Delta^{1/4}.
$$
Using \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
U^4&\le12\,2^m\mathcal K^3+216\,8^m\Delta\\
&\le216\,8^m\bigl(\Delta+4^{-m}\mathcal K^3\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
This scalar inequality allows arbitrary missing labels and imposes no restriction on their positive weights.

Assume now (1). Define the neutral products and spike factors
$$
B=\prod_{h=1}^L(1+a_h),\qquad
V=\prod_{h=1}^L(1+a_h^{4/3}),\qquad
\theta_h=\frac{a_h^2}{1+a_h}.
$$
For every group-used coordinate, monotonicity of \(a^2/(1+a)\) on \((0,\infty)\) gives
$$
1/6\le\theta_h\le4/3.
\tag{11}
$$
For each \(q=(b,i)\), let \(R_q\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^L\) have its coordinates on \(G_q\) in the set
$$
S_{n_q}=\left\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^{n_q}:\#\lbrace h:u_h=2\rbrace=1\right\rbrace
\ \cup\
\left\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n_q}:\sum_hu_h\ge2\right\rbrace,
$$
and its other coordinates in the full Boolean cube. An arbitrary fixed enumeration of \(G_q\) identifies its private coordinates with those of \(S_{n_q}\). Form the actual cell
$$
C_q=\left\lbrace(b+2e_i,t):t\in R_q\right\rbrace.
$$
Its points with a neutral two are exactly \(E_q\). Each of its other points also belongs to \(D\): select two private one-indices \(h\ne l\), replace their entries by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\) in two endpoints, and leave every other coordinate fixed. Both endpoints belong to \(E_q\), and their integral midpoint is the chosen point, so completeness puts it in \(D\). Thus \(C_q\subseteq D\). Different labels have different active vectors, so all these cells are disjoint, regardless of overlap between their groups.

Let
$$
Z_q=\sum_{t\in R_q}\prod_{h=1}^La_h^{t_h},\qquad
\pi_q(t)=\frac{\prod_ha_h^{t_h}}{Z_q},\qquad
\lambda_q=p(C_q)=\frac{c_qZ_q}{Z}.
$$
The map \(\Phi_q(t)=(b+2e_i,t)\) has \(p_{\Phi_q(t)}=\lambda_q\pi_q(t)\). Apply accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 after the coordinate permutation listing \(G_q\) first. Its private size is \(\ell=n_q\ge2\), its free size is \(f=L-n_q\), its ambient dimension is \(L+1\), its private activities are precisely \((a_h)_{h\in G_q}\in[1/2,2]^{G_q}\), and its free activities are the remaining original activities. Its support \(S_\ell\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^f\), normalization, and law are exactly \(R_q,Z_q,\pi_q\) in this coordinate order. It proves the common estimate
$$
M_3(\pi_q),\ T(\pi_q)\ge2^{-38}n_q^2 8^{-(L+1)}.
$$
The neutral spike sets at different indices are disjoint, and their total unnormalized weight is exactly \(B\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h\). Hence (11) gives
$$
Z_q\ge B\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h\ge Bn_q/6,
\qquad \lambda_q\ge\frac{c_qBn_q}{6Z}.
$$
Summing (4) over the disjoint cells yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p),\ T(p)
&\ge\frac{2^{-38}}{6^4}\frac{B^4}{Z^4}8^{-(L+1)}\Delta\\
&\ge2^{-49}\frac{B^4}{Z^4}8^{-(L+1)}\Delta,
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
where \(6^4=1296<2^{11}\). Free neutral activities were not bounded in this application.

For the cross-label estimate define the actual off-diagonal ordered reflection sum
$$
R(c)=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
\qquad
\mathcal L_B=\sum_{c\in B_k}p_c^{1/3}R(c),
\qquad B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k.
$$
For each fixed center, distinct unordered reflected pairs have disjoint endpoints, since an endpoint \(x\) has the unique partner \(2c-x\). Every containing closed halfspace contains at least one endpoint of every such pair. Its mass is therefore at least \(p_c+R(c)/2\). Since also \(s_p(c)=p_c+R(c)\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac18\sum_{c\in B_k}p_cR(c)^3,
\qquad
T(p)\ge\sum_{c\in B_k}p_cR(c)^3.
$$
Hölder over the \(2^k\) Boolean centers consequently gives the common bound
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)\ge\frac{\mathcal L_B^3}{8\,4^k}.
\tag{13}
$$

Fix an ordered pair of labels \(q=(b,i),q'=(b,j)\in\mathcal Q_0\) with the same \(b\) and \(i\ne j\). Necessarily \(b_i=b_j=0\). For every ordered choice
$$
h\in G_q,\qquad l\in G_{q'},\qquad h\ne l,
$$
and every neutral Boolean vector \(t\) with \(t_h=t_l=1\), put
$$
x=(b+2e_i,t+e_h-e_l),\qquad
y=(b+2e_j,t-e_h+e_l),\qquad
c=(b+e_i+e_j,t).
$$
Here the neutral \(e_h,e_l\) are neutral unit vectors. Then \(x\in E_q\), \(y\in E_{q'}\), \(x\ne y\), and \(x+y=2c\), with \(c\in B_k\subseteq D\). In particular these are actual reflected pairs. Write \(v_t=\prod_ha_h^{t_h}\). Their exact original probabilities satisfy
$$
p_x=\frac{c_qv_t}{Z}\frac{a_h}{a_l},\qquad
p_y=\frac{c_{q'}v_t}{Z}\frac{a_l}{a_h},\qquad
p_c=\frac{\sqrt{c_qc_{q'}}v_t}{Z}.
$$
Both chosen neutral coordinates are balanced under (1), so their activity ratio belongs to \([1/4,4]\). Consequently
$$
p_c^{1/3}\min(p_x,p_y)
\ge\frac{v_t^{4/3}}{4Z^{4/3}}
\min(c_q,c_{q'})^{7/6}\max(c_q,c_{q'})^{1/6}.
\tag{14}
$$
There is no overcounting of ordered endpoint pairs in this construction. The first endpoint determines its unique active label \(q\) and its unique neutral two-position \(h\); the second determines \(q'\) and \(l\); and the average of their neutral vectors determines \(t\). Thus all the data are recovered from the ordered pair \((x,y)\), even when groups overlap and even when different data have the same midpoint.

For a balanced coordinate,
$$
\frac{a_h^{4/3}}{1+a_h^{4/3}}\ge\frac1{1+2^{4/3}}>\frac14,
$$
because \(2^{4/3}<3\), whose cube is \(16<27\). Therefore, for distinct \(h,l\) as above,
$$
\sum_{\substack{t\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L\\t_h=t_l=1}}v_t^{4/3}
=V\frac{a_h^{4/3}}{1+a_h^{4/3}}
\frac{a_l^{4/3}}{1+a_l^{4/3}}
\ge V/16.
$$
Moreover the number of distinct-position choices is
$$
n_qn_{q'}-|G_q\cap G_{q'}|\ge n_qn_{q'}-\min(n_q,n_{q'})\ge\frac12n_qn_{q'},
$$
where the last inequality uses both \(n_q,n_{q'}\ge2\). Summing (14), using injectivity and then (9), gives
$$
\mathcal L_B\ge\frac{V}{128Z^{4/3}}\mathcal K.
\tag{15}
$$
Convexity of the power \(4/3\) gives, for every positive \(a\),
$$
\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\right)^{4/3}\le\frac{1+a^{4/3}}2,
\qquad (1+a^{4/3})^3\ge\frac{(1+a)^4}{2}.
$$
Thus \(V^3\ge2^{-L}B^4\), without any balance requirement on unused coordinates. Combining (13) and (15), and using \(k=m+L\), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p),\ T(p)
&\ge2^{-24}4^{-(m+L)}2^{-L}\frac{B^4}{Z^4}\mathcal K^3\\
&=2^{-21}\frac{B^4}{Z^4}8^{-(L+1)}4^{-m}\mathcal K^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
Each cubic is at least both (12) and (16), so it is at least half their sum, and hence
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)
\ge2^{-50}\frac{B^4}{Z^4}8^{-(L+1)}
\bigl(\Delta+4^{-m}\mathcal K^3\bigr).
\tag{17}
$$

It remains to capture actual probability mass. Let

D_1=\left\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace j:x_j=2\rbrace\le1\right\rbrace,
\qquad \alpha=p(D_1).
$$
This actual core is complete. Indeed an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) lies in \(D\) by completeness of \(D\). If it had two coordinates equal to two, the extremality of two in \([0,2]\) would force every point with positive coefficient in a convex representation by \(D_1\) to have both those coordinates equal to two, a contradiction.

M_3(p),\ T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\,8^k}
\ge2^{-14}8^{-N}.
\tag{18}

Otherwise \(\alpha<1/4\), so the exact double-point set has \(p(E)>3/4\). Different \(E_q\) have distinct active vectors and are disjoint. Their exact unnormalized weight is

\sum_{x\in E}w_x
=B\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_0}c_q\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h
\le\frac43BU,

by (11). Consequently

\frac{BU}{Z}\ge\frac34p(E)>\frac9{16}>\frac12.
\tag{19}

Apply (10) in (17) to obtain

\begin{aligned}
M_3(p),\ T(p)
&\ge\frac{2^{-50}}{216}\left(\frac{BU}{Z}\right)^4 8^{-(m+L+1)}\\
&>\frac{2^{-54}}{216}8^{-N}
>2^{-62}8^{-N},
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(216<256\). Together with (18), this proves (2) under (1). Empty parity classes and classes with just one label were already covered by (5); no cross-label term is asserted for such a class.

We finally prove the extension under (3). The conditional-section argument is given in full so that no full-label or size-budget assertion is imported. For each live label choose two indices in \(G_q\) whose activities are balanced, and retain them throughout. If \(\mathcal Q_0\) is empty the core argument already applies, so assume it is nonempty.

At an intermediate stage, fix a set \(J\) of neutral coordinates to binary values \(v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^J\), let \(I=\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\setminus J\), and let \(D'\) be the actual section with its fixed coordinates deleted. Put

\begin{aligned}

We delete only coordinates with activities outside \([1/2,2]\). In particular the two retained balanced coordinates in each group are never deleted, so every \(G'_q\) has size at least two, and \(d\ge2\). If \(a_j>2\), the same holds for the actual section \(u_j=1\). Fix this favored binary value and delete coordinate \(j\). This is an allowed next stage with every retained balanced coordinate still present; the new original section mass is exactly the old \(\mu\) times the chosen conditional mass, by (20).

Each continuation deletes a new neutral coordinate, so the process stops after finitely many steps, say \(r\). For example, once all initially biased group-used coordinates are deleted, every remaining group-used coordinate is balanced and the balanced stopping rule applies. At termination \(\mu\ge(5/8)^r\), and each stopping rule gives the common bound
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge2^{-64}8^{-N'}.
$$
Here (22) and (23) are stronger, and the balanced rule is the case of (2) already proved. Finally (21) and \(N'=N-r\) imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p),\ T(p)
&\ge2^{-64}(5/8)^{4r}8^{-(N-r)}\\
&=2^{-64}(625/512)^r8^{-N}
\ge2^{-64}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus every restriction carries its exact fourth-power mass factor, with a dimension-normalized gain greater than one at each binary deletion. This proves (2) under (3) as well. No singleton group or unrestricted max-two family is included in the assertion.
