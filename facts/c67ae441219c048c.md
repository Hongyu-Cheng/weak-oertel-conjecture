## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$, $g=(1/3,1/3)$, $A=T/5$, $u=(1,1)/\sqrt2$, $v=(1,-1)/\sqrt2$, and
$$
R=2g+[-3/10,3/10]u+[-109/270,109/270]v.
$$
The lifted hull of $A,T,R$ at bases $-1,0,1$ has exactly those integer fibers, all of positive area strictly below $M/2$ for the total mixed area $M=226/225$. Its global raw mixed depth maximum is $Q=109/450$, of normalized value $109/452$, attained at both $(0,g)$ and $(1,2g)$. The three caps with margins $x_1-1/3-10z$, $x_2-1/3-10z$, $-x_1-x_2+2/3-10z$ and the two caps with margins $\pm(x_1-2/3)+10(z-1)$ all have mass $Q$. Together they cover every integer plane. The two groups are active at their respective contacts and have positive continuous-normal balances of ranks two and one.

Shear by $x\mapsto x-(z+1)g$, then fold the fibers at $z=-1,0$ from the sheared right contact onto base zero, scaling the continuous coordinate at $z$ by $1/(1-z)$. Their area pushforward has density $f=\mathbf1_{T-g}+4\mathbf1_{T/10}$. Its Ball radial set about zero, defined by $\rho(e)^2=2\int_0^\infty t f(te)\,dt$ for unit $e$, is nonconvex. Its gauge is $1,5/\sqrt{34},1$ at $q_1=(2/3,-1/3)$, $q_3=(1/6,1/6)$, and their midpoint $(q_1+q_3)/2$, respectively.

## Proof

The rectangle has orthonormal axes, area $4(3/10)(109/270)=109/225$, and contains the radius-$3/10$ ball about $2g$. Each coordinate of $R$ is at least $2/3-19/(27\sqrt2)>0$, since $18\sqrt2>19$. The maximal coordinate sum on $A+R$ is $1/5+4/3+(3/10)\sqrt2<2$, since $\sqrt2<14/9$. Therefore $(A+R)/2\subset\operatorname{int}T$. At height zero any hull representation uses equal weights for the endpoint fibers, so the middle fiber remains $T$. The endpoint fibers are fixed by their extreme heights, and there are no other integer heights. The hull is full-dimensional because it contains a planar triangle and an off-plane point. The actual areas $1/50,1/2,109/225$ sum to $M=226/225$, and each is below $113/225$.

At $(0,g)$, the reflected source consisting of $A$ at height $-1$ and $2g-A$ at height $1$ is contained in the actual two endpoints. Indeed, $A$ lies in the radius-$1/5$ origin ball and $R$ contains the larger radius-$3/10$ ball about $2g$. The subsource is centrally symmetric about $(0,g)$, so every closed containing cap takes at least half its mass, which is $1/50$. In the middle fiber a through-mark cap with nonzero continuous normal has area at least $2/9$ by the full-mass case of accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, since $g$ is the centroid of $T$ and $\Gamma_2=4/9$. A cap with zero continuous normal through the mark contains all of $T$, and every containing cap contains a parallel through-mark cap. This yields raw depth at least $Q$ at $(0,g)$. At $(1,2g)$, symmetry of the own rectangle gives at least half its area in every closed containing cap, with the whole fiber included for a pure integer normal. This again gives $Q$.

All three stated middle caps include $A$ and miss $R$ strictly: on $A$ the coordinates lie in $[0,1/5]$ and their sum is at most $1/5$, and on $R$ both coordinates lie in $(0,2)$. Thus the tilt of size ten separates the two outer traces for each middle cap. At height zero each is a barycentric apex cap of $T$ at threshold $1/3$, of area $(2/3)^2/2=2/9$. The two stated right caps miss $A$ and $T$ strictly since $|x_1-2/3|<1$ there, and bisect $R$ at height one. The sums of the three middle margins and the two right margins are $-30z$ and $20(z-1)$, respectively. Thus the middle group covers all planes over $z\le0$, and the right group covers all planes over $z\ge1$. Every actual mixed point is in a closed cap of mass $Q$. This proves the global upper bound, so both contacts are deepest and the displayed caps are active. The continuous normals in each group sum to zero and have the stated ranks.

The shear is an area-preserving translation within each fiber. It takes both contacts to continuous zero and leaves the bottom fiber $A$ fixed. After the fold the middle contribution is uniform area on $T-g$. The bottom contribution has density four on $A/2=T/10$, by the inverse area Jacobian at height $-1$. This triangle lies in $T-g$, since its coordinates are nonnegative and their sum is at most $1/10<1/3$. The origin is interior to $T-g$, so the radial set contains an inball. Its gauge at any nonzero $q$ is $[2\int_0^\infty t f(tq)\,dt]^{-1/2}$, by the change from unit to nonunit ray parameter.

Take $q_1=(2/3,-1/3)$, $q_3=(1/6,1/6)$, and $q_2=(q_1+q_3)/2=(5/12,-1/12)$. Each is on the outer facet $x_1+x_2=1/3$ of $T-g$ and the ray exits that triangle at parameter one. For $q_1,q_2$ the negative second coordinate excludes $T/10$ at every positive parameter, so their gauges equal one. The ray $q_3$ is in $T/10$ until parameter $3/10$, giving radial integral $1+4(3/10)^2=34/25$ and gauge $5/\sqrt{34}$. The gauge at the midpoint $q_2$ is strictly larger than the mean of the two endpoint gauges. A convex set containing an inball about zero has convex gauge, so the radial set is nonconvex.

The normalized mixed maximum of the original source is $Q/M=109/452>2/9$. This witness uses the indicated sampled fold at two actual deepest contacts with a critical cover and strictly nonheavy fibers.
