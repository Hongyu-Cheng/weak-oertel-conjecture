## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body and $I=[\alpha,\beta]$ a closed interval of normal angles of length less than $\pi$. Take its closed exposed arc over $I$, including the entire exposed faces at the two endpoint normals, with the two exterior joins as its endpoints. Suppose those endpoints are distinct. There are arbitrarily Hausdorff-close planar convex bodies with the same support values outside $\operatorname{int} I$, whose exposed arc over $I$ is polygonal with the same exterior joins and signed area relative to the fixed endpoint chord. Endpoint facets are allowed.

## Proof

If the interval is a single direction, its exposed face is already a segment and no change is needed.

Write $u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. The two endpoints of the exposed face of a support function $h$ at $\theta$ are $h(\theta)u(\theta)+h'_\pm(\theta)u'(\theta)$. Indeed its two directional derivatives are the extreme values of $u'(\theta)\cdot x$ on that face, by testing points of the old face and taking convergent maximizing points at the nearby directions. The exterior joins use $h'_-(\alpha)$ and $h'_+(\beta)$, so supports agreeing outside $\operatorname{int}I$ have the same joins and the same complementary boundary.

For the inner body take the convex hull of the complementary closed boundary arc and a finite sample of the exposed arc, including both joins. Its support agrees with $K$ off $\operatorname{int}I$. In an interior direction of $I$, the maximum on the complementary arc is attained at a join. To see this, a larger value at a complementary interior point would allow a strictly intermediate linear level between that value and both joins. The upper part of the convex boundary above that level is a single arc containing the exposed face in that direction, so it cannot also contain that complementary point without meeting a join. This single-arc property follows by intersecting the convex body with the level line, which meets the boundary in exactly two points for a level strictly between its support extrema. Thus the inner support on $I$ is the maximum over the finite sample, and its exposed arc is polygonal. Dense samples give Hausdorff convergence to $K$.

For the outer body retain all the old supporting halfplanes with normals off $\operatorname{int}I$ and a finite sample of the old supporting halfplanes with normals in $\operatorname{int}I$. Their intersection contains $K$ and has its old supports off $\operatorname{int}I$. It is bounded because the retained exterior normals contain more than a semicircle and hence positively span the plane. At a point with supporting normal in $\operatorname{int}I$, at least one defining inequality is active, since the direction set of retained inequalities is compact. If an active normal lies outside $I$, the normal cone of the point contains the intermediate endpoint normal on the shorter angular arc joining it to the interior supporting normal. This uses full-dimensionality, which prevents opposite normals in a single normal cone. The corresponding retained endpoint plane is therefore active too. Hence the exposed arc over $I$ lies in the finitely many sampled support lines and the two endpoint lines, and is polygonal. With increasingly dense samples the intersections converge to $K$: any subsequential limit of points in the uniformly bounded intersections satisfies every old supporting inequality, by approximating its normal with retained normals.

Interpolate between the inner and outer bodies by Minkowski convex combination. All interpolants agree with the original support off $\operatorname{int}I$, so their joins and complementary boundary stay fixed. Between the finitely many combined edge normals, both supports on $I$ are evaluations of a fixed vertex against $u(\theta)$ and their linear combination has the same form. Thus the exposed interpolated arc is polygonal. Since the two endpoint bodies bracket $K$ by inclusion, their areas bracket its area. Continuity of area under Hausdorff convergence gives an interpolant with exactly the original area. The fixed complementary path and fixed endpoint chord identify this difference of total areas with the difference of signed arc areas. The support functions of both approximants converge uniformly to the original, so every interpolant is arbitrarily close when the samples are fine.
