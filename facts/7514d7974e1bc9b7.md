## Statement

Let $A$ be a planar convex body below a supporting line whose whole exposed segment is $F=[\alpha,\beta]\times\{0\}$. For $\alpha<t<\beta$, put $A_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(A\cup\{(t,\varepsilon)\})$. Define $T_t(s)=(s-\alpha)/(t-\alpha)$ for $s\le t$ and $T_t(s)=(\beta-s)/(\beta-t)$ for $s\ge t$ on $[\alpha,\beta]$. The added area is $\varepsilon(\beta-\alpha)/2+o(\varepsilon)$. In any halfplane $H$ properly cutting $A$, the added area is $\varepsilon\int_\alpha^\beta T_t(s)\mathbf1_H(s,0)\,ds+o(\varepsilon)$, uniformly for parallel threshold shifts bounded by a fixed multiple of $\varepsilon$. The gain outside the vertical projection of $F$ is $o(\varepsilon)$, and the whole added region lies in a horizontal band of height $\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)$. If $A$ is a polygon the gain is exactly the triangle over $F$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.

## Proof

Let $f$ be the upper boundary on the compact horizontal projection of $A$. It is upper semicontinuous, concave and has zero set $[\alpha,\beta]$. The upper left supporting segment from $(t,\varepsilon)$ has slope $m_\varepsilon$ given by minimizing $(\varepsilon-f(s))/(t-s)$ over projection coordinates $s\le\alpha$. The quotient is lower semicontinuous with positive denominator bounded away from zero, so it attains a minimum at $a_\varepsilon$. No point with $\alpha<s<t$ can lower it because there $f(s)=0$. The line through the apex of that slope majorizes $f$ to the left, and to the right it is at least $\varepsilon$. Thus the segment from its old contact to the apex is on the new upper boundary. If there are multiple contacts, choose the leftmost. The reflected construction gives the rightmost right contact $b_\varepsilon$. The function equal to the old boundary outside the two contacts and to the two joining segments inside is concave. At the left contact, all old secants from its left have slope at least the left supporting slope, by the supporting-line inequality. At the right contact the reflected comparison holds, and at the apex the slope drops. The function majorizes the old upper boundary and the apex, while every point on the two joining segments belongs to the new hull. Thus it is the new upper boundary. On the visible interval the old boundary is at least the lesser contact height by concavity, so the whole gain lies above that height. The lower boundary is unchanged because $A_\varepsilon\subseteq A+[0,\varepsilon e_2]$, where $e_2$ is the upward unit vector.

The left slope satisfies $0\le m_\varepsilon\le\varepsilon/(t-\alpha)$. Its contact equation gives $0\le-f(a_\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon(\alpha-a_\varepsilon)/(t-\alpha)$. Limit points of $(a_\varepsilon,f(a_\varepsilon))$ lie on $F$ by compactness, so $a_\varepsilon\to\alpha$ and $f(a_\varepsilon)=o(\varepsilon)$. Consequently $m_\varepsilon/\varepsilon\to1/(t-\alpha)$. The reflected proof gives $b_\varepsilon\to\beta$, its height $o(\varepsilon)$ and the right normalized slope. This gives the claimed band and uniform convergence of the rescaled new upper height to $T_t$ on $F$. The same vertical segment inclusion bounds the gain on each vertical line by $\varepsilon$, so the part off the projection of $F$ is $o(\varepsilon)$. Integrating on $F$ gives the area coefficient. For a cut transverse to the face, the trace indicator for heights in the band and shifts of size $O(\varepsilon)$ can differ from the old face indicator only within length $O(\varepsilon)$ of one crossing, yielding error $O(\varepsilon^2)$ there. A parallel proper cut is separated from the face line by a positive distance and is constant on the band for small $\varepsilon$. This gives the uniform integral for either kind of cut.

If $A$ is a polygon the apex violates only the named facet inequality for small $\varepsilon$. Every entering segment meets the old facet line at a point in $F$, since it still satisfies every other facet inequality. The gain is therefore exactly the triangle outside its old base.
