## Statement

Use the fixed-face ordinary quantile comparison on an open neighborhood of $0$ in a finite-dimensional real variation space $E$. Assume the actual family at $0$ is a local minimum of its maximum normalized ordinary mixed depth $\beta=Q/M$, and that $M$ and the eligible circuit roots have directional derivatives linear on $E$, for the full mixed measure including emerging fibers. For each of the fixed deepest witnesses $i$, let $\Gamma_i$ be the nonempty set of all its eligible circuit responses $g=\tau'_{i,C}-\beta M'$. Then every selection $g_i\in\Gamma_i$ satisfies $0\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_i\}$. Equivalently the closed convex cones $T_i=\{v\in E:g(v)\ge0\ \text{for all }g\in\Gamma_i\}$ cover $E$.

Some nonempty inclusion-minimal index set $J$ retains this universal zero-hull property and satisfies $\dim\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{i\in J}\Gamma_i\le |J|-1\le k(I)-1\le2^n-1$. On the joint kernel of the covectors in this span, every eligible circuit at every witness in $J$ has $\tau'_{i,C}=\beta M'$.

If only one-sided feasible directions are available, the directional sign condition and protecting-set coverage hold on those directions whenever the corresponding directional derivatives exist. The rank conclusion here requires the signed linear setting.

## Proof

Choose any one eligible circuit at each witness. The exact quantile comparison touches the old maximum. At a local minimum it gives $\max_i(\tau_{i,C_i}(tv)-\beta M(tv))\ge0$ along each feasible ray for small $t>0$, and all terms vanish at zero. Divide by $t$ and let $t$ decrease to zero. Finiteness gives $\max_i g_i(v)\ge0$. All derivatives used here include the actual mass of every sampled fiber.

If $v$ is outside every $T_i$, one can select a response negative on $v$ at each witness, contrary to the sign condition. If $v\in T_i$, every selection has a nonnegative response at that index. This proves the equivalence with covering directions, including its restricted form on a one-sided feasible set. In the open linear space, a finite selected convex hull omitting zero is strictly separated from zero by evaluation at some $v\in E$. After choosing its sign this makes every response negative, proving the zero-hull assertion. Conversely a convex combination equal to zero implies a nonnegative selected response on every $v$. Each $T_i$ is an intersection of homogeneous closed halfspaces.

Take an inclusion-minimal nonempty subset retaining the universal zero-hull property. Fact 5f38d364d9b7443c applies to its nonempty sets in $E^*$ and gives the span bound and the equality of its dimension with the codimension of the joint kernel. There are at most $k(I)$ fixed witnesses by the fixed-face comparison's construction. Evaluating each response on the joint kernel gives the claimed derivative identity.
