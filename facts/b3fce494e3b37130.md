## Statement

Let \(d,m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex body, and write \(\lambda_K:=\mathcal L^d|_K\).  Let
\[
 (t_j,p_j)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq m.
\]
Consider the following three conditions.

(i) There are measurable \(f_j:K\to[0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m f_j\leq1\quad\lambda_K\text{-a.e.},
 \qquad
 \int_K f_j\,d\lambda_K=t_j,
 \qquad
 \int_Kx f_j(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)=t_jp_j
\]
for every \(j\).

(ii) For every family of affine functions
\(\ell_j(x)=a_j+\langle v_j,x\rangle\),
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m t_j\ell_j(p_j)
 \leq
 \int_K\max\{0,\ell_1(x),\ldots,\ell_m(x)\}\,d\lambda_K(x).
\]

(iii) For every finite-valued nonnegative convex function
\(\Phi:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)\),
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m t_j\Phi(p_j)
 \leq
 \int_K\Phi(x)\,d\lambda_K(x).
\]

These conditions are equivalent.  If they hold, then there are measurable functions
\(g_j:\mathbb R^d\to[0,1]\), each supported on \(K\) and log-concave on
\(\mathbb R^d\), such that
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m g_j(x)\leq1\quad(x\in\mathbb R^d),
 \qquad
 \int_Kg_j\,d\lambda_K=t_j,
 \qquad
 \int_Kxg_j(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)=t_jp_j.
\]
Moreover, for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac1e\sum_{j=1}^m t_j\delta_{p_j}\right)(H)
 =\frac1e\sum_{j:p_j\in H}t_j
 \leq\lambda_K(H)
 =\mathcal L^d(K\cap H).}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact 2c093df4eb8158a9 proves that (i), (ii), and (iii) are equivalent and, under any one of them, supplies a jointly feasible family \((f_j)_{j=1}^m\) satisfying (i).  Apply accepted fact 4c1fd13733ff0675 to that entire family at once, with its one shared constraint \(\sum_jf_j\leq1\).  Since every \(t_j>0\), no color is discarded.  That fact gives representatives \(g_j\), extended by zero off \(K\), which are log-concave on \(\mathbb R^d\), have the stated masses and first moments, and satisfy the same single pointwise capacity
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m g_j(x)\leq1
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb R^d.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the log-concavification has not replaced the common capacity by separate bounds \(g_j\leq1\).

For each \(j\), define the probability measure
\[
 d\mu_j(x):=\frac{g_j(x)}{t_j}\,d\mathcal L^d(x).
\tag{2}
\]
It is a log-concave Radon probability measure, and its barycenter is
\[
 g_{\mu_j}
 =\int_{\mathbb R^d}x\,d\mu_j(x)
 =\frac1{t_j}\int_Kxg_j(x)\,d\lambda_K(x)
 =p_j.
\tag{3}
\]
The log-concave centroid-halfspace inequality stated and audited in EXTERNAL SOURCES therefore gives, for every closed halfspace \(Q\) whose boundary contains \(p_j\),
\[
 \mu_j(Q)\geq\frac1e,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 \int_Qg_j\,d\mathcal L^d\geq\frac{t_j}{e}.
\tag{4}
\]

Fix a proper closed halfspace \(H\).  Choose a unit vector \(u\) and \(a\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 H=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle u,x\rangle\leq a\},
\]
and put \(J_H:=\{j:p_j\in H\}\).  For each \(j\in J_H\), the closed halfspace
\[
 H_j:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:
 \langle u,x\rangle\leq\langle u,p_j\rangle\}
\tag{5}
\]
has boundary through the barycenter \(p_j\) and satisfies \(H_j\subseteq H\).  Hence (4), (5), and the common capacity (1) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1e\sum_{j:p_j\in H}t_j
 &\leq\sum_{j\in J_H}\int_{H_j}g_j\,d\mathcal L^d
 \leq\sum_{j\in J_H}\int_Hg_j\,d\mathcal L^d\\
 &=\int_{K\cap H}\sum_{j\in J_H}g_j(x)\,d\mathcal L^d(x)
 \leq\mathcal L^d(K\cap H)
 =\lambda_K(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This proves the asserted domination.

The boundary convention in (6) is exact.  Because \(H\) is closed, every atom with \(p_j\in\partial H\) belongs to \(J_H\); for such a label, \(H_j=H\), so its full weight \(t_j/e\) is included.  The source theorem itself uses a halfspace including the hyperplane through the barycenter.  Also \(\mu_j\ll\mathcal L^d\), so changing open to closed on that hyperplane would not change its \(\mu_j\)-mass.  If a convention admits \(H=\mathbb R^d\) as a degenerate closed halfspace, then integrating (1) gives \(\sum_jt_j\leq\lambda_K(K)\), which immediately implies the same assertion; the empty degenerate case is trivial.

## External sources

Primary source: Matthieu Fradelizi, Dylan Langharst, Jiaqian Liu, Francisco Marín Sola, and Shengyu Tang, “Grünbaum's inequality for Gaussian and convex probability measures,” arXiv:2507.06759, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 4.3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06759; published as Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées 213 (2026), Article 103938, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matpur.2026.103938.

Exact statement used: Theorem 1.4 takes an \(s\)-concave Radon probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), with \(s>-1\), and a unit vector \(u\).  If \(H=g_\mu+u^\perp\), then in the log-concave case \(s=0\), the designated closed side \(H^-\) through the barycenter satisfies
\[
 \mu(H^-)\geq e^{-1}.
\]
The paper specifies that \(H^-\) is one of the halfspaces whose boundary is \(H\); applying the theorem with \(u\) or \(-u\) gives either orientation.  Corollary 4.3 gives the density-to-measure bridge: a \(p\)-concave density \(\psi\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), for \(p\geq-1/n\), induces an \(s\)-concave Borel measure with
\[
 s=\frac{p}{1+np}.
\]
Thus a pointwise log-concave density is the case \(p=0\) and induces an \(s=0\) log-concave measure.

Applicability audit: for each \(j\), \(\psi_j=g_j/t_j\) is measurable, nonnegative, integrable with integral one, and pointwise log-concave because positive rescaling preserves log-concavity.  Corollary 4.3 therefore makes \(\mu_j=\psi_j\mathcal L^d\) a log-concave Radon probability measure.  Compact support in \(K\) gives a finite first moment, and (3) identifies its barycenter exactly as \(p_j\).  Theorem 1.4 consequently applies to every hyperplane through \(p_j\) and yields (4).  Only its inequality is used; no equality characterization is invoked.
