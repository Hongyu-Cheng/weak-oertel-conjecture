## Statement

For a convex d-body, d>=2, a centroid cap with positive-to-negative support ratio at most s<d has normalized mass at least Gamma_d+Gamma_d^(2+1/d)/d^2 * ((d-s)/(2d))^(d+1). If the entire centroid asymmetry is at most s, this holds for every containing halfspace at that centroid. Applied to the exact clipped partner of accepted645f91d128f54e5b, it yields the explicit endpoint centroid with raw depth at least Gamma_15[1+(11/32)^16/225], certifying OC for the whole previously specified 0<epsilon<=2^(-12) family. This is an ordinary sufficient surplus estimate, not a general mixed theorem.

## Proof

# An elementary quantitative centroid-cap surplus from support asymmetry

Research draft, 6 November 2026. This is an ordinary convex-body estimate
and an explicit endpoint certificate for the already constructed d=15
three-fiber family. It does not prove OC for arbitrary fibers. Every
nonstandard estimate is proved below; no maximal-depth rigidity or
stability theorem is assumed.

The same-base equal-volume cone comparison is classical: it appears in
Section 3, printed page 1259, of [Grünbaum, Partitions of mass-distributions
and of convex bodies by hyperplanes (1960)](https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf).
Quantitative ordinary stability is also established in the literature;
no claim of global novelty for an ordinary stability principle is made.
The explicit directional constant and its endpoint application below are
derived in full, without invoking an external stability estimate.

## 1. Statement

Let K be a convex d-body, d>=2, with ordinary centroid g. Set

    Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d.

For a nonzero linear functional u, let

    a=max_(x in K) [-u.(x-g)]>0,
    b=max_(x in K) [ u.(x-g)]>0,
    p=|K intersect {u.(x-g)>=0}|/|K|.

Suppose b/a<=s<d, where s>0. Then

    p >= Gamma + [Gamma^(2+1/d)/d^2]
                      *[(d-s)/(2d)]^(d+1).             (1)

In particular, if the centroid asymmetry satisfies

    -(K-g) subset s(K-g),      1<=s<d,                 (2)

then (1) holds for every closed halfspace containing g. The ratio bound
b/a follows from (2) by evaluating the supporting functional -u. Thus
the single actual point g has a quantitative ordinary depth surplus.
The statement is affine invariant. No integer coordinate is transformed.

## 2. The two cone-tail losses

Divide u by a, so the negative support length is one, and divide section
densities by |K|. Let f_+(t) and f_-(r) be the normalized coarea densities
at u.(x-g)=t and u.(x-g)=-r, respectively. Their supports are [0,b] and
[0,1], and their common value at zero is S>0. Here b has been renamed
after scaling, so b<=s. Ordinary Brunn--Minkowski says that the
(d-1)-st root of the common signed section density is concave. Also

    integral f_+=p,     integral f_-=1-p,
    integral t f_+(t)dt = integral r f_-(r)dr.          (3)

Put H=dp/S and define the positive comparison cone density

    c_+(t)=S(1-t/H)^(d-1),       0<=t<=H.

It has mass p. The same-mass cone argument gives b<=H: if b>H, root
concavity between zero and H makes f_+ at least c_+ on [0,H], and there
is positive additional mass beyond H. This contradicts equal mass.

On [0,b] subtract S^(1/(d-1))(1-t/H) from the section root. The result
h(t) is concave, h(0)=0, and h(t)/t is nonincreasing for t>0. Therefore
f_+-c_+ changes sign at most once, from positive to negative. Extending
f_+ by zero on (b,H] preserves that sign order. Choose a crossing point
z in [0,b]; if the two functions agree almost everywhere, then b=H
and the ensuing inequality is immediate. Equal mass gives

    integral t(c_+-f_+)dt
      =integral (t-z)(c_+-f_+)dt
      >=integral_b^H (t-b)c_+(t)dt
      = S H^2/[d(d+1)] * (1-b/H)^(d+1).              (4)

Every integrand before the first inequality is nonnegative by the
single-crossing sign order, and z<=b on the final interval.

Extend the cone's affine root in the negative direction, defining

    c_-(r)=S(1+r/H)^(d-1),       r>=0.

The actual density satisfies f_-(r)<=c_-(r) on [0,1]. To see this, the
same root difference h is concave on the whole signed interval. Equal
positive-side mass ensures there is some t>0 with h(t)>=0 (if h is
negative everywhere in (0,b], its mass is strictly too small). For
0<=r<=1, concavity and h(0)=0 give

    h(-r)<=-(r/t)h(t)<=0.

The same conclusion holds when h vanishes on the whole positive side.
This proves the asserted negative-side domination.

Choose A>=0 so that integral_0^A c_-(r)dr=1-p. Domination on [0,1]
implies A<=1. Direct integration gives

    A=H t,       t=p^(-1/d)-1.                         (5)

Because f_--c_- is nonpositive on [0,A] and has zero total integral
when c_- is truncated at A,

    integral r f_-(r)dr -integral_0^A r c_-(r)dr
      >= integral_A^1 (r-A) f_-(r)dr.                 (6)

Root concavity between the section at zero and the extreme negative
section gives f_-(r)>=S(1-r)^(d-1). Consequently the right side of (6)
is at least

    S/[d(d+1)] * (1-A)^(d+1).                        (7)

The comparison cone on the signed interval [-A,H] has total mass one.
Its centroid coordinate is (H-dA)/(d+1), by integrating its affine-root
density or the ordinary cone centroid formula. The actual centroid
coordinate is zero by (3). Adding (4) and (7) therefore yields

    H-dA >= (S/d)[H^2(1-b/H)^(d+1)+(1-A)^(d+1)].      (8)

All quantities on the right are nonnegative. In particular H-dA>=0,
so (5) itself recovers p>=Gamma without assuming Grünbaum's inequality.
Using S=dp/H and A=Ht, divide (8) by H to obtain

    1-dt >= p[(1-b/H)^(d+1)+(1-Ht)^(d+1)/H^2].       (9)

## 3. A uniform explicit surplus

Let r=(d-s)/(2d)>0 and h=(d+s)/2<d. Since p>=Gamma, (5) gives t<=1/d.
If H<=h, then

    1-Ht >=1-H/d >=r,       H^2<=d^2.

If H>h, then b<=s gives

    1-b/H >1-s/h=(d-s)/(d+s)>=r.

In either case (9) implies

    d+1-d p^(-1/d) >= (p/d^2) r^(d+1).               (10)

The function F(p)=d+1-d p^(-1/d) vanishes at Gamma and has derivative
p^(-1-1/d), which is at most Gamma^(-1-1/d) on [Gamma,1]. Thus

    Gamma^(-1-1/d)(p-Gamma)
       >= F(p) >= (Gamma/d^2)r^(d+1).

This is exactly (1). Every containing halfspace contains its parallel
through-centroid halfspace, establishing the stated depth conclusion.
There are no hyperplane atoms for ordinary full-dimensional volume.

## 4. Exact centroid asymmetry of the clipped d=15 partner

Let T be the unit-volume 15-simplex with barycentric coordinates
lambda_1,...,lambda_16, and let

    D={x in T : lambda_i(x)<=1/16 for i=1,...,13}.

Write u_i=lambda_i for i<=13. Once these coordinates are fixed, the
last three nonnegative barycentric coordinates sum to R=1-sum u_i.
The conditional two-dimensional simplex has area proportional to R^2.
Thus the marginal of u on [0,1/16]^13 has density proportional to R^2;
the fixed coordinate Jacobian cancels from normalized expectations.

For independent U_i uniform on [0,1/16], put

    r0=E R=19/32,
    E R^2=r0^2+13/(12*16^2)=137/384.

Writing U_i=1/32+Z_i, the Z_i are independent and symmetric. Hence

    E[Z_i R^2]=-2 r0 Var(Z_i)=-r0/(6*16^2),
    E_D lambda_i=1/32-r0/[6*16^2 E R^2]
                =529/17536       (i<=13).             (11)

Permutation symmetry of the last three coordinates and their sum give

    E_D lambda_i=3553/17536       (i=14,15,16).         (12)

For a polytope given by its facet inequalities, the least s satisfying
-(D-g_D) subset s(D-g_D) is the maximum support ratio in those facet
normals. The inequalities 0<=lambda_i<=1/16 for i<=13 and lambda_i>=0
for the last three are all facets. Their support ratios are

    567/529,      529/567,      13983/3553.

For the last ratio, max_D lambda_i=1, attained at the corresponding
untruncated vertex. Therefore

    s(D,g_D)=13983/3553<4.                            (13)

The ratio formula can also be checked directly by evaluating every
facet inequality on -(D-g_D); testing these defining inequalities is
both necessary and sufficient for the set inclusion.

Apply (1) with d=15 and the valid upper bound s=4. The point g_D has
ordinary normalized depth at least

    Gamma[1+q],
    Gamma=(15/16)^15,
    q=(11/32)^16/225 >2^(-40).                        (14)

The algebra is exact:
Gamma^(1+1/15)*(11/30)^16/225=(11/32)^16/225.
The final inequality uses 11/32>1/4 and 225<256.

## 5. An explicit endpoint center in the all-middle obstruction

Use exactly the bodies from `all-middle-fiber-sharp-target-obstruction.md`:

    B=T,
    A=g_T+epsilon(T-g_T),        0<epsilon<=2^(-12),
    D0=p0+rho(D-p0),
    C=2D0-g_T,

where p0 has its first thirteen barycentric coordinates 1/32 and its
last three 19/96, and rho^15=17179869184/29158754625. Those already
verified formulas give |A|=a=epsilon^15<=2^(-180), |B|=|C|=1 and
(A+C)/2 subset B. They also show that every point of the entire middle
fiber falls strictly below the sharp OC target Gamma(1+a/2).

The present result adds a completely explicit successful endpoint:

    y_C=(1, 2[p0+rho(g_D-p0)]-g_T).                   (15)

The continuous coordinate in (15) is the actual centroid g_C of C,
so y_C belongs to the endpoint fiber itself. Ordinary affine invariance
and (14) give absolute ordinary depth at least Gamma(1+q). Every ambient
closed halfspace containing y_C has a containing trace in C, possibly
the entire C, and all other fiber contributions are nonnegative. Thus
its full mixed cap mass is at least Gamma(1+q). Since

    a/2<=2^(-181)<2^(-40)<q,

this exceeds Gamma(1+a/2), the exact sharp target for total mass 2+a.
In particular no further unknown reduction of epsilon is required.

This family has an explicit middle-fiber obstruction and an explicit
safe endpoint centroid. The general estimate (1) supplies a sufficient
surplus criterion whenever a particular partner's centroid asymmetry
is bounded below d. It does not force that asymmetry condition or its
required surplus in an arbitrary hypothetical counterexample.
