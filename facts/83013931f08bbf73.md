## Statement

Let an actual integer fiber $K_p$ of a convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $n,d\ge1$, be centrally symmetric about $c$ and have volume $k>0$. For each unordered pair of distinct other positive-volume fibers at bases $z,2p-z$, write their volumes as $a_j\le b_j$ and $v_j=((a_j^{1/d}+b_j^{1/d})/2)^d$. Then the raw depth at $(p,c)$, for the sum of all integer fiber volumes, is at least
$$
k/2+\sum_j a_j\max(0,1-k/(2v_j)).
$$
In particular, for exactly three positive fibers $A,K,B$ at $-1,0,1$ with $|A|=|B|=v>0$ and symmetric middle fiber $K$, the depth at the middle symmetry center is at least $\max(k/2,v)\ge(k+2v)/4$.

## Proof

For each reflected pair use the uniform-marginal midpoint coupling of 793210f8a4be3647, equation (1), with two coefficients $1/2$ and actual target $K_p$. Scale its total mass to $a_j$. Its midpoint law $\eta_j$ has mass $a_j$ and density at most $a_j/v_j$ on $K_p$, and the original endpoint measures dominate the scaled marginals. If the midpoint falls in the middle trace of a closed ambient cap, at least one of the two lifted endpoints is captured. Thus the original endpoint volumes pay at least $\eta_j$ of the middle trace. Distinct reflected pairs use disjoint endpoints, since the partner of $z$ is uniquely $2p-z$.

For a through-$(p,c)$ cap with nonzero continuous normal the middle trace bisects $K_p$. The missed midpoint mass for pair $j$ is at most $a_j k/(2v_j)$ by the density bound, and the captured mass is nonnegative, giving the displayed sum. A pure-base cap through $p$ contains the whole middle fiber and at least one endpoint of each pair, so pays at least $k+\sum_j a_j$. Parallel tightening reduces any containing cap to these cases. For equal endpoint volumes the midpoint density ceiling equals one and the pair contribution is $\max(0,v-k/2)$, giving the asserted specialization.
