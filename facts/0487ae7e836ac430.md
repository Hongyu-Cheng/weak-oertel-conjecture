## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $p=d+1$, and let $P$ be a nonempty finite set of occupied bases with full-dimensional compact convex $d$-fibers $K_z$. Put $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$ and $C=\operatorname{conv}S$. Let $\mu$ be the sum of ordinary $d$-dimensional fiber Lebesgue measures on $S$. At $Y=(b,x)\in S$, define
$$
E(Y)=\inf_{L>0\text{ on }C}\int_S L(W)^{-p}\,d\mu(W),\qquad L(W)=1+a\cdot(W-Y),
$$
with the parameter in the actual relative linear span. Let $F$ be the minimal face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ containing $b$, $S_F$ the union of fibers over $P\cap F$, $C_F=\operatorname{conv}S_F$, and $\mu_F=\mu|_{S_F}$. The full infimum equals the analogous infimum on $C_F$ with $\mu_F$ and parameters in its relative linear span. If $F$ is proper, the full infimum is unattained.

When $Y\in\operatorname{relint} C_F$, the face infimum is attained at a unique strictly positive denominator $L$, and
$$
\int_{S_F}(W-Y)L^{-p-1}\,d\mu_F=0,\qquad
\int_{S_F} L^{-p-1}\,d\mu_F=\int_{S_F}L^{-p}\,d\mu_F=E(Y)>0.
$$

## Proof

Restriction of a full feasible denominator to the face preserves normalization and positivity and drops the off-face nonnegative terms. For the reverse inequality, extend any face-feasible denominator affinely to the full relative affine hull. If $F$ is proper, add $t$ times an exposing affine functional of the base, chosen to vanish on $F$ and be strictly positive at every $z\in P\setminus F$. The extended denominator remains unchanged on $S_F$, is uniformly positive on every other compact fiber for large $t$, and then is positive on $C$. The off-face integrals tend to zero by the uniform divergence of their denominators to $+\infty$. If $F$ is the full base hull, the domains already agree. For proper $F$, every full-feasible finite denominator gives strictly positive off-face integrals, so the full infimum is unattained.

Now let $Y\in\operatorname{relint} C_F$. A closed relative ball of radius $\varepsilon>0$ about $Y$ in $C_F$ bounds the norm of each feasible relative parameter by $1/\varepsilon$. At a boundary parameter the limiting affine denominator $L_0$ is nonnegative on $C_F$ and vanishes somewhere there, because strict positivity on the compact hull is open in the parameter. It has a zero on at least one of the actual fibers of $S_F$, since the minimum of an affine function on $C_F$ is its minimum on $S_F$. If $L_0$ vanishes identically on that fiber, its inverse-power integral is infinite. Otherwise its nonnegative restriction has a nonzero continuous coefficient and is positive in the interior. Choose a positive level $s_0$ through the interior and a small $(d-1)$-disk at that level inside the fiber. The cone from a zero point to that disk has level sections of $(d-1)$-volume a positive constant times $s^{d-1}$ for $0<s<s_0$. The affine level Jacobian is constant and positive, so the cone integral diverges as $\int_0^{s_0}s^{d-1-p}ds$ since $p=d+1$. In dimension one use a point disk of counting measure one. Fatou gives divergence along any feasible parameter sequence approaching this boundary point. Boundedness of the domain therefore makes the sublevel at the finite value of the zero parameter compact inside the domain.

On that domain differentiation is valid because the denominator is bounded away from zero locally in parameter on the fixed compact hull. The Hessian has quadratic form
$$
p(p+1)\int_{S_F} (v\cdot(W-Y))^2 L(W)^{-p-2}\,d\mu_F(W).
$$
For nonzero relative $v$ this is positive. Indeed, if it vanished, the affine form would vanish almost everywhere on every full-dimensional fiber, hence everywhere there by continuity, and therefore on the entire hull, contradicting nonzero relative $v$. This yields strict convexity and uniqueness. The first-order equation gives the vector identity, and its scalar product with $a$ gives $\int (L-1)L^{-p-1}\,d\mu_F=0$, proving the second identity.
