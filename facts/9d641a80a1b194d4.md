## Statement

Let $T$ be standard Borel and $X$ a nonempty compact metric space. Let $f_j:T\times X\to\mathbb R$, $j\ge1$, be jointly Borel and continuous in $x$ for each fixed $t$. Then the set $T_0$ of parameters for which $F(t)=\{x\in X:f_j(t,x)\le0\ \forall j\}$ is nonempty is Borel. There exists a Borel map $x:T\to X$ with $x(t)\in F(t)$ for all $t\in T_0$. Any fixed value may be used off $T_0$.

## Proof

Fix a countable dense sequence $(z_\ell)$ in $X$. The existence of $x\in F(t)$ implies that for each $k$ there is $\ell$ with $\max_{j\le k} f_j(t,z_\ell)<1/k$, by continuity. Conversely, if such points exist for every $k$, any cluster point as $k\to\infty$ lies in $F(t)$. This countable characterization makes $T_0$ Borel and the least-index approximants $x_k(t)$ Borel on $T_0$.

For each $m$ fix a finite closed cover of $X$ by sets of diameter at most $2^{-m}$. Inductively intersect the previously chosen compact set (initially $X$) with the first member of the $m$th cover for which the intersection contains infinitely many terms $x_k(t)$. Such a member exists by finiteness. The choices are Borel because infinitely many occurrences means that for every integer $N$ there is $k\ge N$ meeting the selected finite intersection. The resulting compact sets are nested with diameters tending to zero. For each $m$ choose the first index $k\ge m$ with $x_k(t)$ in the $m$th intersection. These points converge to the unique point in the nested intersection. Their Borel limit lies in $F(t)$ by the cluster-point property. Extend it by a fixed point on the Borel complement.
