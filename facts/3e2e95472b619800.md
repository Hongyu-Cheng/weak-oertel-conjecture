## Statement

For a simplex orientation $U=(u_0,\ldots,u_d)$ (the outward facet normals of a full-dimensional bounded simplex, up to individual positive rescaling), put $P_U(A)=\{x:u_j\cdot x\le h_A(u_j)\text{ for all }j\}$ for each nonempty compact convex $A\subset\mathbb R^d$. This is a singleton exactly when $A$ is a singleton; otherwise it is a full-dimensional simplex. All positive-size envelopes for one orientation are translates of positive homothets of a common simplex. Suppose $I\subset\mathbb R^n$ is finite and nonempty and $\sum_i\theta_i A_{z_i}\subset A_z$ whenever $z_i\in I$, $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\theta_i=1$ and $\sum_i\theta_i z_i=z\in I$. Then the same inclusions hold for $P_U(A_z)$, and the convex hull of the lifted envelopes has those exact fibers at every $z\in I$. If $I$ consists of all nonempty integer fibers of a bounded convex set, the hull of the lifted envelopes has no extra integer base.

## Proof

After a common invertible linear change of continuous coordinates the orientation is that of $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$. The envelope is $a+rT$ with $a_k=\min_A x_k$ and $r=\max_A\sum_k x_k-\sum_k a_k\ge0$. If $r=0$, every $x\in A$ satisfies $x_k=a_k$ for every $k$. This proves the shape claims. A nonnegative Minkowski sum of such envelopes has the summed facet offsets because the translates and simplex scales add separately.

Apply the support function in direction $u_j$ to any sampled inclusion: $\sum_i\theta_i h_{A_{z_i}}(u_j)\le h_{A_z}(u_j)$. The same inequalities hold for the envelopes because their support in each of the specified facet directions equals that of the original body. This proves the envelope inclusion. A point of the hull over $z\in I$ is a convex combination of lifted generator points, which may be grouped at repeated bases because every envelope is convex. Its continuous coordinate belongs to the stated fiber by the inclusion; conversely every lifted envelope point is a generator. If $I$ lists all nonempty integer fibers of the original set, any integer point of $\operatorname{conv}(I)$ lies in its convex projection and hence is in $I$. The new hull's projection is exactly $\operatorname{conv}(I)$.
