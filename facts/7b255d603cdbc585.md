## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-3,-1),(3,3),(1,3),(-1,2)\}$ and start with unit counting on $P\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Move the two unit masses at $(-3,-1)$ and $(1,3)$ to their preexisting midpoint $(-1,1)$. The total mass remains ten and the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth over the support of the new measure is exactly $4/10$, attained at the midpoint. The original maximum is $3/10$.

## Proof

Use the indices and original cut table of the ten-site baseline fact. The operation removes masses at $s_0,s_7$ and makes the mass at $s_2$ equal to three. A closed halfspace containing $s_2$ also captures at least one of $s_1,s_5$, because $s_2$ is their midpoint. The original cuts at the surviving indices $1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9$ have new masses $1,4,1,2,2,3,2,1$, respectively, by the listed traces. Thus no point has raw depth above four.
