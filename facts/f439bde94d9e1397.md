## Statement

Fix $0<x<y<1$. For a finite nonnegative concave density $f$ of integral one on $[0,1]$, set $A=\int_x^1 f$ and $B=\int_y^1 f$. Put $b_0=(1-y)^2$, $b_1=1-y^2$, $B_x=b_0/(1-x)$ and $B_y=1-y$. The feasible pairs are exactly $b_0\le B\le b_1$ and $L(B)\le A\le U(B)$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
L(B)&=(1-x)^2+\frac{x(1-x)}{y(1-y)}(B-b_0),\\
U(B)&=\begin{cases}
(1-x)^2B/b_0,&b_0\le B\le B_x,\\
1-x^2-x^2b_0/(B-b_0),&B_x\le B\le B_y,\\
1-(x^2/y^2)(1-B),&B_y\le B\le b_1.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Lower equality at each $B$ holds precisely for $2(1-\lambda)(1-t)+2\lambda t$ almost everywhere, where $\lambda=(B-b_0)/(2y(1-y))$. For upper equality with $B_x<B<B_y$ the density is the single normalized tent with peak $p\in(x,y)$ and far tail $B$. On $[b_0,B_x]$ and $[B_y,b_1]$ the upper equality densities are respectively the normalized tent mixtures supported on $[0,x]$ and $[y,1]$ with far tail $B$. The equalities of densities are pointwise for the continuous representative.

## Proof

Represent $f$ almost everywhere by the positive probability mixture of normalized tents. At any cut $q\in(0,1)$ the tail of $T_p$ is $(1-q)^2/(1-p)$ for $p\le q$ and $1-q^2/p$ for $p\ge q$, by integrating its affine pieces. Denote the two resulting tails by $A_p,B_p$. The map $p\mapsto B_p$ is continuous and strictly increasing from $b_0$ to $b_1$. Eliminating $p$ from $(A_p,B_p)$ in the ranges $p\le x$, $x\le p\le y$, and $y\le p$ gives $A_p=U(B_p)$.

The middle piece of $U$ has derivative $x^2b_0/(B-b_0)^2$ and strictly negative second derivative. At $B_x$ and $B_y$ its derivative equals the neighboring slopes $(1-x)^2/b_0$ and $x^2/y^2$. Thus $U$ is concave on the whole interval. Its endpoint chord is $L$ and $U(B)>L(B)$ at every interior $B$. Indeed equality at an interior point of the chord of a concave function would force the whole graph to be affine, whereas its middle piece has negative second derivative.

The actual $(A,B)$ is the average of the tent pairs under the mixing measure. Integrating the chord inequality gives $A\ge L(B)$, with equality exactly when the measure is supported on $\{0,1\}$. The unique weight at $1$ with the required far tail is $\lambda$. For the upper bound integrate the tangent inequality for $U$ at $B$, using one-sided tangents at the endpoints. The tangent contact set consists of one point for interior $B$ in the strict middle piece, the entire left linear piece for $B\le B_x$, and the entire right linear piece for $B\ge B_y$. Equality forces $B_p$ to belong to that contact set almost surely. The inverse of $p\mapsto B_p$ gives exactly the stated tent mixtures. At each feasible $B$ the endpoint mixture attains $L(B)$ and the unique tent with that far tail attains $U(B)$. Their convex combinations keep the same far tail and give every $A$ between the two bounds.

If only $B\ge\delta$ is prescribed, $0\le\delta\le1$, there is a feasible density exactly when $\delta\le b_1$. Since $L$ is strictly increasing, the exact minimum is $L(\max\{b_0,\delta\})=(1-x)^2+[x(1-x)/(y(1-y))](\delta-b_0)_+$. Equality requires the lower-bound affine density at $B=\max\{b_0,\delta\}$, hence the decreasing endpoint tent for $\delta\le b_0$ and the unique affine density with $B=\delta$ otherwise.
