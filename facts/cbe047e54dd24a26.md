## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), \(a_i>0\), \(R\ge0\), \(\theta_i\le0\), and \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) with at least one coordinate nonzero modulo 3. For \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), put \(D_r=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot z\le R,\ u\cdot z\equiv r\pmod3\}\). When \(D_r\ne\varnothing\), define
\[
Z_r=\sum_{z\in D_r}e^{\theta\cdot z},\qquad
p_r(z)=e^{\theta\cdot z}/Z_r.
\]
For \(j\in D_r\), define \(\tau_{p_r}(j)\) as the infimum of \(\sum_{z\in H\cap D_r}p_r(z)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j\), and put \(M_3(p_r)=\sum_{j\in D_r}p_r(j)\tau_{p_r}(j)^3\). Thus the normalizer is on the entire stated coset support.

Use exactly the quartic coordinate law and scalar constant of accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`:
\[
F_3(q)=\frac{1+23q+23q^2+q^3}{(1-q)^4},\qquad
Q_s(k)=\frac{(2k+1)^3e^{-4sk}}{F_3(e^{-4s})}\quad(k\ge0),\qquad
\alpha=(8/27)^{3/2}.
\]
Here \(k\) is the scalar index of the predecessor's \(Q_s\). For every product law whose coordinates are independent \(Q_{s_i}\), \(s_i>0\), or deterministic zeros, one has \(\mathbb P(u\cdot J\equiv0\pmod3)\ge1/384\).

For any \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), write
\[
\sigma_a(j)^2=\sum_i a_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3,\qquad
\sigma_\theta(j)^2=\sum_i\theta_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3,\qquad
\mathcal V(j)=\prod_i(2j_i+1).
\]
These are the same displacement variances and box cardinality used in `ac02a0ffd389f816`. If \(j\in D_r\) and \(a\cdot j+4\sigma_a(j)\le R\), then
\[
\tau_{p_r}(j)\ge\frac3{80}e^{-4\sigma_\theta(j)}p_r(j)\mathcal V(j).
\tag{1}
\]

The following explicit zero-coset constants are absolute:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_*&=65536,& b_*&=20+\log(48\gamma_*^4),\\
C_*&=576\sqrt{18}+24\sqrt{\log768},& B_*&=\gamma_*b_*+C_*,\\
c_*&=\frac{27e^{-(20+b_*)}}{393216000},& \delta&=\log(8\alpha)>0,\\
N_*&=\left\lceil\max\{1,(B_*/\delta)^2\}\right\rceil,&
\kappa_*&=\min\{c_*,1/(64\cdot8^{N_*})\}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
All logarithms are natural. The new inflation constant \(\gamma_*\) replaces the predecessor's inflation value for the construction below; the law \(Q_s\), its rate \(s\), and \(\alpha\) retain their meanings. One has, with the original \(Z_0\),
\[
M_3(p_0)\ge c_*e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n,
\qquad M_3(p_0)\ge\kappa_*8^{-n}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every nonempty \(D_r\), the intrinsic lattice \(\Gamma=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:u\cdot v\equiv0\pmod3\}\) has exactly \(2^n\) classes modulo \(2\Gamma\), and the original-law baseline is \(M_3(p_r)\ge64^{-n}/64\). There is no factor \(3^n\) in this parity count.

## Proof

First establish the two new residue estimates. Write \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\). If \(V_i\) are independent uniform integers on \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\), then for \(u_i\not\equiv0\pmod3\) their characteristic value \(\mathbb E\omega^{u_iV_i}\) is real and is respectively \(1/(2j_i+1),0,-1/(2j_i+1)\) when \(j_i\equiv0,1,2\pmod3\). For \(u_i\equiv0\), it is one. All factors have absolute value at most one, and every negative factor has absolute value at most 1/5. Their product is therefore at least \(-1/5\). The roots-of-unity filter gives
\[
\mathbb P(u\cdot V\equiv0\pmod3)
=\frac{1+2\prod_i\mathbb E\omega^{u_iV_i}}3\ge\frac15.
\tag{3}
\]
This constant is sharp for \(n=1,u=1,j=2\).

For a quartic coordinate put \(q=e^{-4s}\), \(P_3(q)=1+23q+23q^2+q^3\), and \(\chi(q)=F_3(q\omega)/F_3(q)\). A coefficient \(u_i\equiv2\) conjugates this value; a coefficient zero modulo 3 or a deterministic zero gives one. For \(q\ge1/32\), we claim
\[
|\chi(q)|\le127/256.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed
\[
|\chi(q)|=\frac{|P_3(q\omega)|}{P_3(q)}\frac{(1-q)^4}{(1+q+q^2)^2},\qquad
|P_3(q\omega)|^2=(q^2-q+1)(q^4-22q^3+483q^2-22q+1).
\]
If \(q\ge1/8\), the triangle inequality and the decreasing function \((1-q)^2/(1+q+q^2)\) bound this by \((49/73)^2<127/256\). For \(1/32\le q\le1/8\), expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
9P_3(q)^2-25|P_3(q\omega)|^2
&=q^5(989-16q)+q^3(22715-7475q)-7475q^2+989q-16\\
&\ge21780q^3-7475q^2+989q-16.
\end{aligned}
\]
The derivative of the last cubic is \(65340q^2-14950q+989\), which is positive on the real line: its leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is \(-34982540\). The cubic's value at \(1/32\) is \(67757/8192>0\). Hence \(|P_3(q\omega)|/P_3(q)\le3/5\) on this interval, and
\[
|\chi(q)|\le\frac35\left(\frac{961}{1057}\right)^2
=\frac{2770563}{5586245}<\frac{127}{256}.
\]
The last two rational comparisons are respectively \(614656<676783\) and \(709264128<709453115\) after multiplication by positive denominators. This proves (4).

It remains to control a product all of whose relevant rates have \(q<1/32\). The scalar zero-residue probability is at least its atom at zero, which is \(1/F_3(q)>1/2\), because \(F_3\) increases and \(F_3(1/32)=1825824/923521<2\). For any scalar residue probability vector \((\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)\) with \(\beta_0\ge1/2\), its characteristic value \(\chi=\beta_0+\beta_1\omega+\beta_2\omega^2=\rho e^{i\varphi}\) has \(|\varphi|\le\pi/3\) and satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\chi+\sqrt3|\operatorname{Im}\chi|\le1\). The first claim follows from \(\operatorname{Re}\chi\ge1/4\) and \(|\operatorname{Im}\chi|\le\sqrt3/4\); the second follows from \(|\beta_1-\beta_2|\le1-\beta_0\). Therefore
\[
\rho\le\frac1{\cos\varphi+\sqrt3\sin|\varphi|}
\le2^{-3|\varphi|/\pi}.
\tag{5}
\]
For the last inequality, the concave function \(\log(2\cos(t-\pi/3))\), \(0\le t\le\pi/3\), lies above the chord joining its endpoint values 0 and \(\log2\). Conjugation preserves (5). If \(\Phi=\sum_i\varphi_i\) and \(A=\sum_i|\varphi_i|\), the product modulus is at most \(2^{-3A/\pi}\). If its real part is negative, \(\cos\Phi<0\), so \(A\ge|\Phi|\ge\pi/2\). Consequently its real part is at least \(-2^{-3/2}>-127/256\). If its real part is nonnegative, the same lower bound is automatic. If instead any relevant coordinate has \(q\ge1/32\), (4) bounds the entire product modulus by \(127/256\), since other characteristic moduli are at most one. These cases exhaust every product, including deterministic coordinates. Applying the filter again proves
\[
Q(u\cdot J\equiv0\pmod3)\ge\frac{1-2(127/256)}3=\frac1{384}.
\tag{6}
\]

For (1), restrict the uniform displacement box to residue zero and to \(|a\cdot V|\le4\sigma_a(j)\), \(|\theta\cdot V|\le4\sigma_\theta(j)\). Chebyshev's inequality makes each failed slab have probability at most 1/16, so (3) leaves at least \(1/5-1/8=3/40\) of the box. A zero variance makes its slab automatic. This displacement set is symmetric under negation. Both endpoints \(j\pm v\) are nonnegative and satisfy the resource inequality and residue \(r\). Every closed halfspace containing \(j\) contains at least one of these two endpoints, so it contains at least \((3/80)\mathcal V(j)\) allowed endpoints. Each endpoint has its original probability at least \(p_r(j)e^{-4\sigma_\theta(j)}\). This proves (1), with \(Z_r\) throughout.

We now prove the resource survival and (2). When \(R=0\), \(D_0=\{0\}\) and (2) is immediate. Assume \(R>0\). Use the truncated one-resource dual construction proved in accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`, on the resource downset before restricting its residue. In the predecessor's notation, \(h=\log3\), \(\mu(s)=\mathbb E_{Q_s}J\), \(\psi(s)=\tfrac14\log F_3(e^{-4s})\), and \(\phi(s)=\max\{0,\psi(s)-\psi(h)\}\). Choose a minimizer of \(\lambda R+\sum_i\phi(\lambda a_i-\theta_i)\), and write \(t_i=\lambda a_i\), \(s_i=t_i-\theta_i>0\), \(\mu_i=\mu(s_i)\), \(\Lambda=\lambda R\). This \(\Lambda\) is the resource dual value from that accepted fact, rather than the mixed-integer lattice in the locked problem.

The accepted construction supplies \(x_i=\mu_i\) for \(s_i<h\), \(x_i=0\) for \(s_i>h\), and \(0\le x_i\le\mu_i\) at ties, with \(a\cdot x\le R\), \(\lambda(R-a\cdot x)=0\), and at most one partially filled coordinate. Thus, for \(I_0=\{i:x_i=\mu_i\}\), one has \(s_i\le h\) on \(I_0\), \(s_i\ge h\) off \(I_0\), \(\sum_{i\in I_0}a_i\mu_i\le R\), and \(\Lambda=\sum_i t_ix_i\). Its directly proved scalar estimates are
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2\le18/s^2,\quad \mu(s)\le5/s,\quad
\mu(s)\ge1/(20s)\ (0<s\le h),\quad \mu'(s)\le0,
\quad (1-e^{-s})^4F_3(e^{-4s})\ge\alpha.
\tag{7}
\]
We use those statements with the identical normalization; no depth theorem on a conditioned law is invoked.

If \(I_0\ne\varnothing\), select the \(m=\min\{|I_0|,\lceil\gamma_*\sqrt n\rceil\}\) largest \(a_i\mu_i\), call their set \(I\), and put \(U=\sum_{i\in I}a_i\mu_i>0\). Set \(s_i'=\gamma_*s_i\) on \(I\) and \(s_i'=s_i\) on \(I_0\setminus I\). Under \(Q\), take independent \(J_i\sim Q_{s_i'}\) on \(I_0\), and deterministic zeros elsewhere. If \(I_0\) is empty, take \(I=\varnothing,U=0,J=0\). In either case \(|I|\le\gamma_*\sqrt n+1\). When there are unselected active coordinates, each \(a_i\mu_i\le U/m\), so their squared sum is at most \(U^2/\gamma_*^2\).

On selected coordinates, (7) gives \(\mathbb E_QJ_i\le100\mu_i/\gamma_*\le\mu_i/4\); elsewhere monotonicity gives \(\mathbb E_QJ_i\le\mu_i\). Thus \(\mathbb E_Q(a\cdot J)\le R-3U/4\). Also both \(\operatorname{Var}_Q(a\cdot J)\) and \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_a(J)^2\) are at most
\[
7200\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{(a_i\mu_i)^2}{(s_i'/s_i)^2}
\le14400U^2/\gamma_*^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(1/s_i\le20\mu_i\), \(J_i(J_i+1)\le2J_i^2\), the selected squared sum is at most \(U^2\), and the unselected bound above gives the last inequality. Since \(|\theta_i|\le s_i\le s_i'\) on \(I_0\), (7) also gives \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_\theta(J)^2\le12n\le18n\).

Chebyshev for the resource sum and Markov for each squared displacement variance show that the three conditions
\[
|a\cdot J-\mathbb E_Q(a\cdot J)|\le48\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q(a\cdot J)},\qquad
\sigma_a(J)\le48\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q\sigma_a(J)^2},\qquad
\sigma_\theta(J)\le48\sqrt{18n}
\]
hold together with probability at least \(1-3/48^2=767/768\). On this event, (8) implies
\[
a\cdot J+4\sigma_a(J)
\le R-3U/4+28800U/\gamma_*\le R,
\]
because \(115200\le3\gamma_*=196608\). Empty or zero-variance cases hold identically. Hence the set
\[
G_* =\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot j+4\sigma_a(j)\le R,
\ \sigma_\theta(j)\le48\sqrt{18n}\}
\]
satisfies \(Q(G_*)\ge767/768\). Combining with (6), the event \(E=\{J\in G_*,u\cdot J\equiv0\pmod3\}\) has \(Q(E)\ge1/768\). This is the claimed residue survival. It is an intersection estimate, not conditioning followed by a presumed resource bound.

Put \(T=t\cdot J\). The same dual bookkeeping as in the accepted construction gives \(0\le\Lambda-\mathbb E_QT\le5(1+|I|)\): the one partially filled coordinate costs at most 5, and each selected coordinate costs at most \(t_i\mu_i\le5\). For \(v\ge0\), the log-Laplace function \(\ell(v)=\log\mathbb E_Qe^{-v(T-\mathbb E_QT)}\) has \(\ell''(v)\le18n\). Indeed the tilted coordinate rates are \(s_i'+vt_i/4\), and (7) bounds their contribution to \(\operatorname{Var}(T)\) by \(18t_i^2/(s_i'+vt_i/4)^2\le18\). Therefore \(\ell(v)\le9nv^2\). Restriction to \(E\), followed by conditional Jensen, yields
\[
\mathbb E_Q[T\mid E]\ge\mathbb E_QT-6\sqrt{n\log768}
\ge\Lambda-5(1+|I|)-6\sqrt{n\log768}.
\tag{9}
\]
This follows by minimizing \(\log768/v+9nv\); the case \(Q(E)=1\) also follows by letting \(v\) decrease to zero.

The actual zero-coset normalizer satisfies \(Z_0\le e^\Lambda\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}\), because \(\theta=t-s\) and \(t\cdot z\le\Lambda\) for every original \(z\in D_0\). Put \(Z_Q=\prod_{i\in I_0}F_3(e^{-4s_i'})\). For every vector in the support of \(Q\), its exact normalization gives
\[
\mathcal V(j)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}
=Z_QQ(J=j)e^{4\sum_{i\in I_0}(s_i'+\theta_i)j_i}
\ge Z_QQ(J=j)e^{4t\cdot j}.
\tag{10}
\]
Cube (1), multiply by the original \(p_0(j)\), and sum over the roots in \(E\), discarding only nonnegative other summands. Since \(\sigma_\theta(j)\le48\sqrt{18n}\), (9), (10), the original partition upper bound, and conditional Jensen give
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p_0)
&\ge\frac{27e^{-576\sqrt{18n}}}{512000Z_0^4}
\sum_{j\in G_*\cap D_0}\mathcal V(j)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}\\
&\ge\frac{27e^{-20}}{393216000}
e^{-20|I|-C_*\sqrt n}Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The first sum can include roots outside the support of \(Q\); only those inside are used for (10). The denominator remains \(Z_0^4\) at every step.

An uninflated active coordinate contributes at least \(\alpha\), by (7). A deterministic coordinate contributes at least \((1-e^{-h})^4=16/81\ge\alpha\). For an inflated coordinate,
\(F_3(e^{-4\gamma_*s})/F_3(e^{-4s})\ge1/(48\gamma_*^4)\): the numerator polynomial of \(F_3\) lies between 1 and 48, and \(1-q^{\gamma_*}\le\gamma_*(1-q)\). Consequently (11) is at least
\[
\frac{27e^{-20}}{393216000}
e^{-b_*|I|-C_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n
\ge c_*e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\]
This proves the first inequality in (2), including an empty active set and \(\lambda=0\).

For the intrinsic parity claim and the remaining finite dimensions, fix any nonempty \(D_r\) and choose \(b\in D_r\). Reduction modulo 2 maps \(\Gamma\) onto \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\): for any prescribed integer parity vector, add an even multiple of a coordinate whose coefficient in \(u\) is nonzero modulo 3 to correct its residue. The kernel is \(\Gamma\cap2\mathbb Z^n=2\Gamma\), since 2 is invertible modulo 3. This proves that \(\Gamma/2\Gamma\) has \(2^n\) classes. Partition the affine coset by \(z-b\) modulo \(2\Gamma\), with original masses \(\pi_c\).

If \(x,y\in D_r\) have the same intrinsic parity, their midpoint \(j=(x+y)/2\) is an integer point in the same coset, is nonnegative, and satisfies \(a\cdot j\le R\). Conversely the endpoints of every oriented reflected pair about a root in \(D_r\) have the same intrinsic parity. For \(k_{r,j}=\#(D_r\cap(2j-D_r))\), set \(\mathsf H_{r,j}=k_{r,j}p_r(j)\). Log-affinity gives the exact identity
\(\mathbb E_{p_r}\mathsf H_{r,j}=\sum_c\pi_c^2\ge2^{-n}\).
For any containing halfspace, reflection puts at least one endpoint of each pair in it; Cauchy--Schwarz on the reflected Hellinger sum gives \(\mathsf H_{r,j}\le2\sqrt{p_r(H\cap D_r)}\). Thus \(\tau_{p_r}(j)\ge\mathsf H_{r,j}^2/4\), and Jensen yields \(M_3(p_r)\ge\mathbb E\mathsf H_{r,j}^6/64\ge64^{-n}/64\). This argument uses the full original coset normalizer.

Finally \(\alpha>1/8\), as proved in the accepted fact, so \(\delta>0\). For \(n\ge N_*\), \(B_*\sqrt n\le\delta n\), and the first bound of (2) gives \(M_3(p_0)\ge c_*8^{-n}\). For \(1\le n<N_*\), the intrinsic baseline gives \(M_3(p_0)/8^{-n}\ge8^{-n}/64\ge1/(64\cdot8^{N_*})\). This proves the second bound of (2).

To delimit the nonzero-coset issue exactly, take \(n=1,a=u=1,R=2,\theta=-L\) with \(L>0\). Then \(D_1=\{1\}\) and its original law has \(M_3(p_1)=1\), while the mass of \(D_1\) under the unrestricted law on \(\{0,1,2\}\) is \(e^{-L}/(1+e^{-L}+e^{-2L})\), tending to zero. Also the unrestricted quartic coordinate law has \(Q_s(J\not\equiv0)\le1-Q_s(J=0)=1-1/F_3(e^{-4s})\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\). Thus neither (6) nor an unrestricted-law constant-probability conditioning argument supplies a nonzero-coset root bridge.
