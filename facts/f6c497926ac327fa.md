## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^D$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body with affine coordinates $(v,t,y)$, where $v\in\mathbb R^r$, $1\le r\le D-1$, $y\in\mathbb R^{D-r-1}$, and $t\ge0$ on $K$. Write $J>0$ for the constant volume Jacobian of these coordinates. The pushforward of $D$-volume on $K\cap\{t>0\}$ under $z=v/t$ has bounded integrable density
$$
F(z)=J\int_0^\infty\int_{\mathbb R^{D-r-1}}\mathbf1_K(tz,t,y)t^r\,dy\,dt
    =J\int_0^\infty\int_{\mathbb R^{D-r-1}}\mathbf1_\Omega(z,s,w)s^{-(D+1)}\,dw\,ds,
$$
where $\Omega=\{(z,s,w):s>0,\ (z/s,1/s,w/s)\in K\}$. The set $\{F>0\}$ is convex and full-dimensional, and $F^{-1/(r+1)}$ is convex on it. Its boundary may be discarded without changing the measure. The total mass is $|K|$, and any cap on $K$ with margin $a\cdot v+bt$ has exactly the mass of $\{z:a\cdot z+b\ge0\}$ under $F(z)\,dz$.

## Proof

The coordinate $t$ is nonconstant and nonnegative on $K$, hence positive on $\operatorname{int}K$. Its zero set is contained in an affine hyperplane of $D$-volume zero. At each positive $t$, $v=tz$ contributes the Jacobian $t^r$, giving the first formula. Compactness bounds $t$ and $y$ in fixed bounded sets, so the first formula is bounded uniformly in $z$. Integration in $z$ gives $|K|$. The projection $v/t$ maps $\operatorname{int}K$ to an open nonempty set: at an interior point one can vary $v$ while holding $t$ and $y$ fixed. For each such image point $z$, its $(t,y)$ section contains an open set with $t>0$, giving $F(z)>0$.

Now set $s=1/t$ and $w=y/t$. The change $(s,w)\mapsto(t,y)$ has absolute Jacobian $s^{-2}s^{-(D-r-1)}$; multiplying by $t^r$ gives $s^{-(D+1)}$. To check convexity of $\Omega$, the inverse image in $K$ of a convex combination of $(z_0,s_0,w_0)$ and $(z_1,s_1,w_1)$ with weights $1-\theta,\theta$ is the convex combination of their inverse images with weights proportional to $(1-\theta)s_0,\theta s_1$. These weights are positive for $0<\theta<1$. Thus $\Omega$ is convex, and the integrand $J\mathbf1_\Omega s^{-(D+1)}$ raised to the power $p=-1/(D+1)$ is the affine function $J^p s$ wherever the integrand is positive.

Extend the integrand by zero to $s\le0$. Apply functional Borell–Brascamp–Lieb to the $(s,w)$ sections at any two points of $\{F>0\}$ and at their interpolated point. In integration dimension $k=D-r$, [Bobkov, *Convex bodies and norms associated to convex measures*, Lemma 2.3](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~bobko001/papers/2010_PTRF_Norms.pdf) takes the input power mean $p>-1/k$ to the output power mean $p/(1+kp)$. Here the input inequality holds whenever both endpoint integrands are positive by convexity of $\Omega$ and affinity of their $p$-powers. All section integrals are finite by the first formula. The output exponent is
$$
\frac{p}{1+kp}=-\frac{1}{r+1}.
$$
In particular the interpolated density is positive, and taking its negative power gives the claimed convexity. A full-dimensional convex set has Lebesgue-null boundary. Finally, for $t>0$, $a\cdot v+bt=t(a\cdot z+b)$, giving exact mass preservation by the pushforward; the discarded set $t=0$ contributes zero.
