## Statement

Set $v_1=(1,0),v_2=(0,1),v_3=(-1,-1)$, $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1,\ i=1,2,3\}$, and $b=|T|=9/2$. Let $A,K$ be full-dimensional planar convex bodies with $(A+T)/2\subset K$, and place $A,K,T$ at bases $-1,0,1$, with no other positive-area fibers. Write $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $M=a+k+b$, $D_i=A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge-1\}$, $U_i=|D_i|$, $q_i=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge0\}|$, $h_i=\max_A v_i\cdot x$, and $F_i=T\cap\{v_i\cdot x=1\}$.

For each proper positive cut $0<U_i<a$, one has $|D_i+F_i|=U_i+3(h_i+1)$ and $q_i\ge[U_i+3(h_i+1)]/4$. In particular the raw mass $Q_i=U_i+q_i$ of the actual cap $v_i\cdot x\ge z$ satisfies $4Q_i\ge5U_i+3(h_i+1)$.

If all three $A$ cuts are proper and positive, the actual caps $v_i\cdot x\ge z$ have one common raw mixed-area mass $Q$, and $b\le M/2$, then $Q>2M/9$. In this nonheavy case, if $(0,0)$ is an actual mixed point and those caps attain its depth, its raw depth is therefore strictly greater than $2M/9$. When $b>M/2$ the actual centroid $(1,0)$ of the endpoint triangle already has raw depth strictly greater than $2M/9$.

## Proof

The triangle has vertices $(1,1),(1,-2),(-2,1)$; each facet $F_i$ has length $3\|v_i\|$. A proper positive $D_i$ ranges over the heights $-1\le v_i\cdot x\le h_i$, an orthogonal width of $(h_i+1)/\|v_i\|$. Translate $F_i$ to height zero. Each nonempty horizontal interval of $D_i$ is lengthened by exactly the length of $F_i$ in the Minkowski sum. Integrating the lengths over the height interval proves $|D_i+F_i|=U_i+3(h_i+1)$. The midpoint inclusion puts $(D_i+F_i)/2$ inside the middle cap, and halving scales area by $1/4$. The endpoint cap is the facet, which has zero area. This proves the single-cut assertions.

The three $A$ trace halfplanes cover the plane, and so do the three $K$ trace halfplanes, because $\sum_i v_i=0$. For a three-halfplane cover with areas $c_1\le c_2\le c_3$, accepted fact ac7ba1e1e0ebc955 in ordinary dimension two gives $|L|\le c_3+c_2+c_1/4$. Order $U_i$ as $u\le v\le w$, and put $F=u+v+w/4$. The $K$ cover has cap areas $Q-U_i$ in the reverse order. The bound on $K$ gives $k\le9Q/4-F$. If $Q\le2M/9$, then $k\le a+b-2F$, while $b\le M/2$ says $k\ge b-a$. Thus $F\le a$.

The strict complements of the two smaller $A$ caps intersect in area at least $\delta=a-u-v\ge w/4>0$. At a point of that intersection the two corresponding functionals are strictly below $-1$. Their sum with the third is zero, forcing the remaining support height $h_w>2$. The single-cut bound at that facet gives $Q>9/4+5w/4$. On the other hand the bound on the $A$ cover gives $a\le w+v+u/4$, so $\delta\le w$. The putative upper bound yields
$$
Q\le(4/9)(a+b-F)=(4/9)(b+\delta-w/4)\le2+w/3.
$$
