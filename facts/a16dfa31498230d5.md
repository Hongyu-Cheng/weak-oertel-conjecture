## Statement

Use the compact endpoint sets $X,Y$ and median segment map $T$ from the median-coupling setup, identifying its output with its continuous coordinate in the compact actual median fiber $K$. Write $\Pi(\alpha,\beta)$ for the probability couplings of the two marginals $\alpha=2\mu_-$ and $\beta=2\mu_+$. For $d\ge1$, put
$$
f_y(\sigma)=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}\sigma\{(p,q):u\cdot(T(p,q)-y)\ge0\},
\qquad V=\sup_{\sigma\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta),\,y\in K} f_y(\sigma).
$$
Then $V$ is attained at one pair $(\sigma_*,y_*)$ and is the maximum closed-halfspace depth of a realizable median target when optimized jointly over coupling and mark. For $d=0$ the analogous value is attained and is $1$.

## Proof

The set of couplings is nonempty by the product coupling and weakly compact. Probability measures on the compact metric product are weakly compact, and testing the marginals against all continuous functions gives a closed set. The median map is continuous on the compact product by the segment formula on each clopen base-pair stratum. For fixed $u$, if $\sigma_\ell\rightharpoonup\sigma$ and $y_\ell\to y$, then for each $\delta>0$ eventually
$$
\{u\cdot(T-y_\ell)\ge0\}\ \subseteq\ \{u\cdot(T-y)\ge-\delta\}.
$$
The right side is closed. Portmanteau and continuity from above as $\delta\downarrow0$ bound the limsup of the moving-cap masses by $\sigma\{u\cdot(T-y)\ge0\}$. An infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, so the maximum is attained on $\Pi(\alpha,\beta)\times K$. This includes charged cap boundaries. Restricting to through-$y$ closed halfspaces computes the depth because every containing halfspace contains its parallel through-$y$ cap. An ambient halfspace with constant restriction has full target mass if it contains $y$. For $d=0$ every target is the point mass at the sole continuous coordinate and every containing halfspace captures its full mass.
