## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $Z$ be uniform on the simplex $\Delta_d=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_j\ge0,\ \sum_jx_j=1\}$, and let $I$ be independent and uniform on its vertex indices. For $0\le b\le1$ set $Y_b=(1-b)Z+be_I$. Then $Y_b\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}} Z$ if and only if $b\le1/d$, where the order means $\mathbb E\phi(Y_b)\le\mathbb E\phi(Z)$ for every continuous convex $\phi$ on $\Delta_d$. Equivalently the inequality reverses for every continuous concave test.

At $b=1/d$, there is a coupling $(Y,X)$ of $Y_{1/d}$ and $Z$ with $\mathbb E[X\mid Y]=Y$ in which the minimum coordinate value and its unique index agree almost surely. The common minimum has survival function $(1-ds)_+^{d-1}$ for $s\ge0$. In fact for every $b\ge1/d$, $\mathbb E\min_j(Y_b)_j=(1-b)/(d(d-1))$, whereas $\mathbb E\min_j Z_j=1/d^2$.

At $b=0$ every test gives equality. For $0<b<1/d$ equality holds precisely for affine tests. At $b=1/d$ equality holds precisely when the test is affine on every slice $\{x\in\Delta_d:x_i=t/d,\ x_j\ge t/d\text{ for all }j\ne i\}$, for every $i$ and $0\le t\le1$. In particular the minimum is a nonaffine concave equality test at the critical step. When $d=2$ and $b=1/2$ the whole laws agree. For $d\ge3$ the critical comparison is strict for every strictly convex test.

## Proof

**Critical law and coupling.** Put $g=(1/d,\ldots,1/d)$ and $\rho=(d-1)/d$. The conditional map $z\mapsto\rho z+e_i/d$ has relative volume Jacobian $\rho^{d-1}$ and image $\{y:y_i\ge1/d\}$. Thus the density of $Y_{1/d}$ relative to uniform probability is
$$
f(y)=\frac{\#\{j:y_j\ge1/d\}}{d\rho^{d-1}}.
$$
Both this law and uniform probability give each unique-minimum cell $P_i=\{y:y_i<y_j\text{ for }j\ne i\}$ mass $1/d$ by symmetry and absolute continuity. Ties lie on finitely many null hyperplanes. The density of the conditional critical law relative to the conditional uniform law on $P_i$ is therefore still $f$.

The closure of $P_i$ is the simplex with vertices $g$ and $e_j$ for $j\ne i$. In this simplex use $t=dy_i$ and $y=tg+(1-t)w$, with $w_i=0$ and $w$ on the opposite facet. The barycentric coordinates of the cell are $t$ and $(1-t)w_j$ for $j\ne i$. Thus under conditional uniform probability the variables $t$ and $w$ are independent, with $t$ of density $(d-1)(1-t)^{d-2}$ on $(0,1)$ and $w$ uniform on the facet. The factor $(1-t)^{d-2}$ is the slice Jacobian. For $d=2$ the facet is a point.

For $t<1$, the coordinate $y_i=t/d$ lies below $1/d$, and $y_j\ge1/d$ exactly when $w_j\ge1/d$. Since $d\rho^{d-1}=(d-1)\rho^{d-2}$, the density $f$ in these coordinates equals $\#\{j\ne i:w_j\ge1/d\}/((d-1)\rho^{d-2})$. This is precisely the density of $\rho W+e_J/d$ relative to uniform probability on the facet, by the same affine change of variables in its $d-1$ coordinates. The formula also gives the constant density $1$ on the point facet when $d=2$. Hence under the critical law the radial coordinate $t$ keeps its uniform-cell density and is independent of the averaged facet point.

Draw $L$ uniformly from the cell labels and $T$ independently with the preceding density for $t$. Conditional on $L=i$, sample independently $W$ uniform on the opposite facet, $J$ uniform on its vertices and $R$ with density $(d-1)(1-r)^{d-2}$ on $(0,1)$, independently of $T$. Put $V=\rho W+e_J/d$ and $U=(1-R)W+Re_J$. The beta-step merging lemma in $d-1$ coordinates gives uniform $U$ on that facet and $\mathbb E[U\mid L,T,W,J]=V$. Set
$$
Y=Tg+(1-T)V,\qquad X=Tg+(1-T)U.
$$
The disintegrations show $Y\sim Y_{1/d}$ and $X\sim Z$. Conditional on $(L,T,W,J)$, the mean of $X$ is $Y$, which also gives $\mathbb E[X\mid Y]=Y$. The facet vectors $U,V$ have positive coordinates other than $L$ almost surely, including on the one-point facet, so $L$ and $T/d$ are the unique minimum index and value of both vectors. The density of $T$ gives the stated survival function. When $d=2$, $U=V$ and $X=Y$.

**Admissible parameters and sharpness.** For $b\le1/d$, on the original independent pair $(Z,I)$, write $\theta=db$. Then $Y_b=(1-\theta)Z+\theta Y_{1/d}$. Jensen's inequality on this pair and on the critical martingale gives
$$
\mathbb E\phi(Y_b)\le (1-\theta)\mathbb E\phi(Z)+\theta\mathbb E\phi(Y_{1/d})\le\mathbb E\phi(Z).
$$
For $b\ge1/d$, the raised coordinate is at least $b$, and the smallest of the other $d-1$ is at most $(1-b)/(d-1)\le b$. Thus $\min_j(Y_b)_j=(1-b)\min_{j\ne I}Z_j$, including when the minimum ties or $b=1$. For any fixed $k$ coordinates, translating them down by $s\ge0$ leaves a scaled simplex of ratio $1-ks$ when $s\le1/k$, giving survival $(1-ks)_+^{d-1}$ and mean $1/(dk)$. Take $k=d-1$ and $k=d$ to obtain the two means in the statement. The concave test $\min_jx_j$ violates the reversed comparison for every $b>1/d$.

**Equality.** We use the elementary observation that a function on a simplex that is affine on every segment from any vertex to any point is affine. Indeed, express a point as a convex combination of one vertex and a point on the opposite facet and apply induction to the restriction to that facet. This gives the barycentric interpolation of the vertex values, with a point simplex as the base case.

For $0<b<1/d$, equality forces the first Jensen gap on the original pair to vanish. The gap is nonnegative and continuous in $z$ for each $i$. Since $I$ gives every $i$ positive mass and $Z$ has full support, the gap vanishes for every $z,i$. Equality in the interior chord inequality makes the test affine on $[z,\rho z+e_i/d]$. On any ray from $e_i$ to the opposite facet, with radial coordinate $r$ measured as a fraction of the full ray, the resulting intervals are $[\rho r,r]$ for all $r\in[0,1]$. Every point of $(0,1)$ lies in the interior of one of these intervals. Thus the restriction is locally affine on $(0,1)$, affine there by connectedness, and affine up to the endpoints by continuity. The observation gives global affineness. Conversely, all affine tests give equality since both means are $g$.

At $b=1/d$, equality means the Jensen gap conditional on $(L,T,W,J)$ in the constructed martingale has zero expectation. For each $i,j$ it is continuous in $(t,w)$ by boundedness and continuity of the test, nonnegative, and must vanish on the full support $[0,1]\times\{w\in\Delta_d:w_i=0\}$. For fixed $i,t,w,j$, the conditional law of $X$ has full support on the segment from $tg+(1-t)w$ to $tg+(1-t)e_j$, because $R$ has positive density throughout $(0,1)$. Jensen equality implies that the test is affine on this segment. In the nondegenerate segment case, a supporting line to the one-dimensional restriction at its interior mean gives a nonnegative continuous error of zero integral, hence zero on the whole segment. The elementary observation now gives affineness on the entire slice. Conversely, slice affineness gives equality in every conditional Jensen comparison. The minimum is constant on each slice and nonaffine on $\Delta_d$. When $d\ge3$, the slices for $0<t<1$ have positive dimension, excluding equality for strictly convex tests. Negating the test gives the same equality classification for concave functions.
