## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), let \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\ge2\) be integers, let \(T\ge0\) be an integer, and let \(\beta\ge0\) be real. For \(i\in[k]:=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), define \(g_i(0)=0\), \(g_i(1)=1\), and \(g_i(2)=B_i\). Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i)\qquad(\mathbf x\in\{0,1,2\}^k),\\
A_T&=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\{0,1,2\}^k,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\},\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z\quad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(Z\) is exactly the source-table normalizer called \(W\) in the registered three-site subgoal. It is an auxiliary table mass, distinct from the locked mixed-integer volume \(W\); the locked \(n,d,C,W\) retain their original meanings.

Every halfspace below is a closed affine halfspace \(H=\{\mathbf z:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge b\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\), with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Define the original ambient depth and moment by
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\tag{2}
\]
Then, with the absolute constant \(\kappa=1/2939328\), one has \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(k+1)}\). This includes \(T=0\), \(\beta=0\), unequal or tied costs, and equality at every spatial-energy, height, and halfspace boundary. The only accepted input is the Boolean disjoint-pair inequality from `c13fd517ed177886`, stated precisely in the proof.

## Proof

The support is finite and nonempty: every supported height lies in \(\{0,\ldots,T\}\), and \((\mathbf0,0)\in A_T\). Thus \(0<Z<\infty\). For completeness, linearly interpolate \(g_i\) on \([0,2]\). Its slopes are \(1\) and \(B_i-1\ge1\), so the interpolant \(\overline g_i\) is convex. The compact convex set
\[
\mathcal P_T=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in[0,2]^k,\ \textstyle\sum_i\overline g_i(x_i)\le t\le T\}
\]
has lattice points exactly \(A_T\). In particular the table in (1) is a complete log-affine lattice table, including when its support has lower affine dimension. The following estimates use its given atoms and probabilities throughout.

We first prove the scalar height estimate without importing any scalar claim. Let \(M\ge0\) be an integer, \(0<r\le1\), \(Z_{\rm aux}=\sum_{i=0}^M r^i\), and \(q_j=r^j/Z_{\rm aux}\) on \(0\le j\le M\), with \(q_j=0\) otherwise. Thus \(Z_{\rm aux}\) is only the normalizer of this one-dimensional auxiliary table. Write \(C_j=\sum_{i=0}^j q_i\) and \(D_j=\sum_{i=j}^M q_i\). Its actual one-dimensional closed-halfspace depth is \(\tau_q(j)=\min\{C_j,D_j\}\), because the two closed rays ending at \(j\) are allowed and every containing closed ray includes one of these rays.

Partition \([0,1]\), in the order \(j=0,\ldots,M\), into intervals \([C_j-q_j,C_j]\), ignoring their measure-zero overlaps at endpoints. For \(v\) in the \(j\)-th interval, \(v\le C_j\) and \(1-v\le D_j\), so \(\min\{v,1-v\}\le\tau_q(j)\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{j=0}^M q_j\tau_q(j)^6
\ge\int_0^1\min\{v,1-v\}^6\,dv
=2\int_0^{1/2}v^6\,dv=\frac1{448}.
\tag{3}
\]
Define \(F_q(j)=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min\{q_{j+h},q_{j-h}\}\) and \(b_j=\min\{j,M-j\}\). Since \(r\le1\), its exact formula is \(F_q(j)=q_j(1+2\sum_{h=1}^{b_j}r^h)\). If \(j\le M/2\), then \(b_j=j\), and
\[
F_q(j)\ge q_j\sum_{h=0}^j r^h=r^j C_j,
\qquad D_j=r^j\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{M-j}r^i}{Z_{\rm aux}}\le r^j.
\]
Hence \(F_q(j)\ge C_jD_j\ge\tau_q(j)^2\). If \(j\ge M/2\), then \(b_j=M-j\), so \(F_q(j)\ge q_j\sum_{h=0}^{M-j}r^h=D_j\ge D_j^2\ge\tau_q(j)^2\). These inequalities also cover \(M=0\), \(r=1\), and equality at the midpoint. From (3) we therefore obtain
\[
\sum_{j=0}^M q_jF_q(j)^3\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{4}
\]

For each integer \(E\ge0\), put \(A(E)=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\), interpreted as zero if \(E>T\). If \(0\le E\le T\), define \(q^{(E)}_t=e^{-\beta t}/A(E)\) for \(E\le t\le T\), zero elsewhere. Its exact translation into (4) is
\[
j=t-E,\qquad M=T-E,\qquad r=e^{-\beta},\qquad
Z_{\rm aux}=e^{\beta E}A(E).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(q^{(E)}_t=q_{t-E}\). Moreover
\[
F_{q^{(E)}}(t)=\frac1{A(E)}
\sum_{|h|\le\min\{t-E,T-t\}}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, both sites \(t+h,t-h\) belong to \([E,T]\) exactly under the displayed displacement bound, and the smaller mass is at their larger height \(t+|h|\). The scalar auxiliary table is only used to prove a sum involving the original probabilities below.

We now record the precise accepted Boolean input. A downset of a finite ground set is a family of subsets closed under taking subsets; the empty family is allowed. Accepted fact `c13fd517ed177886` proves, for downsets \(\mathcal D,\mathcal E\subseteq2^L\),
\[
\mathfrak D(\mathcal D,\mathcal E)
:=|\{(P,Q)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal E:P\cap Q=\varnothing\}|
\ge(|\mathcal D|\,|\mathcal E|)^{3/4}.
\tag{7}
\]
The alias map is literal: its finite ground set is the present \(L\), its two downsets are the present \(\mathcal D,\mathcal E\), and its \(\mathfrak D\) counts ordered disjoint pairs, including empty sets. In particular \(\mathfrak D(\mathcal D,\mathcal D)\ge|\mathcal D|^{3/2}\). No actual-body consequence or cutoff conclusion of that predecessor is used.

Fix \(L\subseteq[k]\), put \(\ell=|L|\), and let \(\mathbf y_L=\mathbf1_L\). Its energy is \(\ell\). Suppose \(E\) is an integer with \(\ell\le E\le T\), and let \(\mathcal F\) be a family of ordered disjoint pairs \((P,Q)\) of subsets of \(L\), invariant under exchanging the two sets. Assume that the two spatial endpoints \(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q\) and \(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q\) have energy at most \(E\), and put \(R=|\mathcal F|\). For every integer \(t\) with \(E\le t\le T\) and every integer \(h\) with \(|h|\le\min\{t-E,T-t\}\), consider the original atoms
\[
\mathbf z_+=(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q,t+h),
\qquad
\mathbf z_-=(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q,t-h).
\tag{8}
\]
Their spatial coordinates lie in \(\{0,1,2\}^k\), their heights lie in \([E,T]\), and their energies are at most \(E\), so both belong to \(A_T\). Their midpoint is the original root \((\mathbf y_L,t)\).

For this fixed root, the map \((P,Q,h)\mapsto\mathbf z_+\) is injective: its coordinates equal to \(2\) recover \(P\), its zero coordinates within \(L\) recover \(Q\), and its height recovers \(h\). The involution \((P,Q,h)\mapsto(Q,P,-h)\) exchanges the endpoints. Its only possible fixed index has \(P=Q=\varnothing\) and \(h=0\), and then gives the root itself. Thus different nonfixed orbits give disjoint pairs of atoms and avoid any fixed root atom.

Every closed halfspace containing the root contains at least one endpoint of every pair in (8), since otherwise the affine functional defining that halfspace would be strictly below its threshold at both endpoints and hence at their midpoint. The minimum of the two original endpoint masses is \(e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}/Z\). Summing this minimum once per nonfixed orbit and charging half of the fixed root mass, if present, gives the valid uniform bound
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)
\ge\frac{R}{2Z}\sum_{|h|\le\min\{t-E,T-t\}}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}
=\frac{RA(E)}{2Z}F_{q^{(E)}}(t).
\tag{9}
\]
At this root the original sampling probability is \(p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}=(A(E)/Z)q^{(E)}_t\). Cubing (9), multiplying by this probability, and using (4)-(5) proves
\[
\sum_{t=E}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
\ge\frac{R^3A(E)^4}{8Z^4}
\sum_{t=E}^Tq^{(E)}_tF_{q^{(E)}}(t)^3
\ge\frac{R^3A(E)^4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{10}
\]
This is the original full normalizer \(Z^4\): one factor comes from sampling the root and three from the halfspace masses.

To supply these stocks, put \(a_i=B_i-1\ge1\). Every spatial vector has a unique representation \(\mathbf x=\mathbf1_L+\mathbf1_I\), where \(L=\{i:x_i>0\}\) and \(I=\{i:x_i=2\}\subseteq L\). Its energy is \(E_L(I)=\ell+\sum_{i\in I}a_i\). Set \(Z_L=\sum_{I\subseteq L}A(E_L(I))\). Each source vector appears once, and its entire height interval contributes exactly the corresponding \(A(E_L(I))\). Hence
\[
Z=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}Z_L.
\tag{11}
\]
These are unnormalized portions of the original table, with no conditioning or deletion of source mass.

For each integer \(E\ge\ell\), let \(\mathcal D_L(E)=\{I\subseteq L:\sum_{i\in I}a_i\le E-\ell\}\), let \(m_L(E)=|\mathcal D_L(E)|\), and let \(R_L(E)=\mathfrak D(\mathcal D_L(E),\mathcal D_L(E))\). Positivity of every \(a_i\) makes \(\mathcal D_L(E)\) a downset. The accepted inequality (7) yields \(R_L(E)\ge m_L(E)^{3/2}\). Its pair family is invariant under exchange and consists of disjoint sets. For every such pair, the exact endpoint energies are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in P}a_i-|Q|\le E,\\
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in Q}a_i-|P|\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
This uses the heterogeneous increments exactly: a \(1\to2\) change adds \(a_i\), and a \(1\to0\) change subtracts \(1\). In particular no reduction to independent mutually exclusive resource choices is assumed.

Let \(\mathcal E_L=\{E_L(I):I\subseteq L,\ E_L(I)\le T\}\), and define \(V_L=\max_{E\in\mathcal E_L}R_L(E)^{3/4}A(E)\), with \(V_L=0\) when \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty. For each nonempty finite \(\mathcal E_L\), select one maximizing \(E\). Equations (10) and (12), and nonnegativity of the root contributions at other heights, give
\[
\sum_{t=\ell}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
\ge\frac{V_L^4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty, the empty tail set shows \(\ell>T\), so the left side is an empty sum and both sides are zero. Distinct \(L\) give distinct binary spatial roots. Summing only these original sampled atoms gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\mathcal V_4}{3584Z^4},
\qquad \mathcal V_4:=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L^4.
\tag{14}
\]
Only one threshold was used at each root. Endpoint atoms can be reused in depth estimates for different roots, but (14) counts each sampled root and height once.

It remains to bound the full mass in (11). For a fixed \(L\), list the feasible tail sets as \(I_1,\ldots,I_{N_L}\) in nondecreasing order of \(E_L(I_j)\), breaking ties arbitrarily. All of the first \(j\) sets belong to \(\mathcal D_L(E_L(I_j))\). Thus, by (7) and the definition of \(V_L\),
\[
V_L\ge R_L(E_L(I_j))^{3/4}A(E_L(I_j))
\ge j^{9/8}A(E_L(I_j))\qquad(1\le j\le N_L).
\tag{15}
\]
Ties cause no loss because a sublevel includes all tail sets at its threshold. Since \(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-9/8}\le1+\int_1^{\infty}x^{-9/8}\,dx=9\), (15) gives \(Z_L\le9V_L\). If \(N_L=0\), both quantities vanish, so the same inequality holds. Combining (11) with Hölder's inequality on the \(2^k\) nonnegative stock values yields
\[
Z^4\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L\right)^4
\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L^4\right)(2^k)^3
=9^4\mathcal V_4\,8^k.
\tag{16}
\]
The empty label has threshold zero, one ordered pair, and \(A(0)>0\), so \(V_{\varnothing}>0\) and \(\mathcal V_4>0\). Equations (14) and (16) now prove
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{3584\cdot9^4\cdot8^k}
=\frac1{448\cdot9^4}\,8^{-(k+1)}
=\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{17}
\]
The denominator is the original \(Z^4\) throughout (10), (13), and (14); (16) compares stocks with that same full original normalizer.

All energies, thresholds, and height displacements used above are integral, and all inequalities defining intervals and stocks are weak. Every boundary atom is included. The case \(\beta=0\) gives \(r=1\) in the scalar proof; singleton height intervals give \(M=0\), which was also included. When \(T=0\), the original support is the singleton \(\{(\mathbf0,0)\}\) and the proof still applies, or directly \(M_3(p)=1\). No step requires distinct or uniformly bounded \(B_i\), and \(k=1\) is covered. This completes the proof.
