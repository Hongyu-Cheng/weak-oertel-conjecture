## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge7\), use precisely the standard recursive symmetric chain partition of accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c, equivalently its left-to-right stack matching in which a one is paired with the last previously unmatched zero whenever possible. Let \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), let \(D_m\) be the nonterminal words, and let \(i_m(c)\) be the first unmatched-zero position of \(c\in D_m\). With \(e_i\) the coordinate unit vector, retain exactly the block objects of accepted facts 6fee4107100a027d and a2c2d2b38e2fa409:
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_m(c)&=c+e_{i_m(c)},\\
E_m&=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\\
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\\
T_m&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
      =E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},\\
J_m&=F_m(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus \(J_m\) is the same reflected-center set as in accepted fact 2117de3255444894.

For every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^m\) and every spike penalty \(0<\epsilon_i\le1\), define a positive probability law on the entire set \(T_m\), extended by zero elsewhere, by
$$
p_x=\frac{e^{\gamma\cdot x}\epsilon_i^{\mathbf1_{\{x\in E_m\}}}}
{Z_i},\qquad
Z_i=Z_{C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}}(\gamma)+\epsilon_i Z_{E_m}(\gamma),
\qquad
Z_X(\gamma)=\sum_{x\in X}e^{\gamma\cdot x}.
$$
Here \(\epsilon_i\) denotes the spike penalty, not a height-deviation parameter. The normalizer \(Z_i\) does not replace the original serial normalizer \(W\). Use exactly the oriented reflected quantities of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc:
$$
H_p(x)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^m}\sqrt{p_{x+v}p_{x-v}},\qquad
b(p)=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_xH_p(x)^2,\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_xH_p(x).
$$
Then, simultaneously for all the stated dimensions, fields, and penalties,
$$
4^m b(p)\ge1+\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon_i}}{25920}.
\tag{1}
$$
This is a convex-potential family: the convex function \(\psi(x)=\max(0,\max_{1\le j\le m}(x_j-1))\) equals \(\mathbf1_{\{x\in E_m\}}\) on \(T_m\), so the weights are \(\exp(\gamma\cdot x-(-\log\epsilon_i)\psi(x))\).

For the exact boundary calibration, put \(t=\sqrt{\epsilon_i}\), \(e_m=|E_m|\), \(u_m=|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|\), and \(h_m=|J_m|\). The scalar count \(u_m\) is used instead of the point notation \(c_m\) in 6fee4107100a027d. At zero field,
$$
b(p_{0,\epsilon_i})=
\frac{e_m t^6+u_m-h_m+h_m(1+2t)^2}{(u_m+t^2e_m)^3}.
\tag{2}
$$
In particular, for each fixed \(0<t\le1\),
$$
\lim_{m\to\infty}4^m b(p_{0,t^2})
=\frac{t^6+(1+2t)^2}{(1+t^2)^3}
=1+\frac{4t+t^2-3t^4}{(1+t^2)^3}.
\tag{3}
$$
This limit is \(1+4t+O(t^2)\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Consequently no absolute \(\eta>0\) satisfies \(4^m b(p)\ge1+\eta\) throughout this family. The rate in (1) has the correct order in \(\epsilon_i\) uniformly in dimension.

## Proof

Fix \(m\ge7\), \(\gamma\), and \(0<\epsilon_i\le1\), and put \(N=2^m\), \(t=\sqrt{\epsilon_i}\), and \(\lambda=1+2t\). All matching and successor statements below concern the prescribed standard partition.

Accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d gives the complete support \(T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), injectivity of \(F_m\), and the parity description used in accepted fact 2117de3255444894: every spike \(a=c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) has parity \(c\), distinct spikes have distinct parities, and its only possible distinct same-parity companion in \(B_m\cup E_m\) is the Boolean word \(c\). Thus the distinct same-parity pairs in \(T_m\) are exactly \(\lbrace c,a\rbrace\) with \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\). Their distinct integer midpoints are \(j=F_m(c)\in J_m\), and these midpoints are Boolean by the successor rule and belong to the convex hull by convexity.

For each such pair, the original penalized probabilities satisfy
$$
p_cp_a=t^2p_j^2,\qquad
\sqrt{p_cp_a}=t p_j.
\tag{4}
$$
The zero displacement contributes \(p_x\), and the two orientations of this unique nonzero pair contribute \(2tp_x\) at a center \(x\in J_m\). There are no other pairs. Hence
$$
H_p(x)=
\begin{cases}
\lambda p_x,&x\in J_m,\\
p_x,&x\in T_m\setminus J_m.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
$$
In particular, every spike has reflected mass equal to its own probability.

For each parity \(v\in B_m\), let \(q_v=p(v+2\mathbb Z^m)\). The parity description and (4) give the exact defect identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{v\in B_m}q_v^2
&=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x^2+2t^2\sum_{j\in J_m}p_j^2,\\
\chi(p)
&=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x^2+2t\sum_{j\in J_m}p_j^2,\\
\chi(p)-\sum_{v\in B_m}q_v^2
&=2t(1-t)\sum_{j\in J_m}p_j^2\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Thus the log-affine parity equality in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc and 2117de3255444894 is used only at \(t=1\); for the present weights (6) is the required replacement.

Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=\frac1N\sum_{v\in B_m}(Nq_v-1)^2
            =N\sum_{v\in B_m}q_v^2-1,\\
\mathcal S&=N\sum_{j\in J_m}p_j^2,\\
\mathcal V&=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x(NH_p(x)-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are nonnegative. Here \(\mathcal D\) and \(\mathcal V\) retain the definitions from 2117de3255444894, evaluated at the present full probability law. Expanding \(\mathcal V\) and applying (6) yields
$$
N^2b(p)-1
=\mathcal V+2\mathcal D+4t(1-t)\mathcal S.
\tag{7}
$$

We next use only the geometric packing proved in 2117de3255444894, with no import of its log-affine probability equality. It supplies a set \(\mathcal G_m\subseteq J_m\) of exactly \(N/128\) Boolean targets and four injective maps \(z^{(a)}:\mathcal G_m\to E_m\), \(1\le a\le4\), such that every witness spike has its source in \(J_m\) and
$$
\frac14\sum_{a=1}^4z^{(a)}(y)=y
\qquad(y\in\mathcal G_m).
\tag{8}
$$
Explicitly, start with a length-\(m-2\) word beginning with five ones, insert two ones immediately before its last unmatched one, and obtain \(y\). Its last three unmatched ones are consecutive; denote their positions by \(j_1,j_2,j_3\). Keep all other entries fixed and use the four source patterns \(000,100,110,010\) at these positions. The four spike patterns are \(200,120,112,012\), whose average is \(111\). All four sources have at least four unmatched ones and hence lie in \(J_m\), as proved in 2117de3255444894 from the matching properties of 7538f1751c45475c. The first three maps are recovered by restoring respectively the first three, two, or one unmatched zeros of their source to ones. The fourth is recovered by replacing the spike position and its two immediate predecessors by \(111\). These are exactly the four injectivity certificates in 2117de3255444894, valid also when \(m=7\).

Every witness in (8) is a spike, while \(y\) is Boolean. Substitution of the original probabilities therefore gives the penalized identity
$$
\prod_{a=1}^4p_{z^{(a)}(y)}=t^8p_y^4,
\qquad
p_y=t^{-2}\left(\prod_{a=1}^4p_{z^{(a)}(y)}\right)^{1/4}.
\tag{9}
$$
In particular, if all four witness probabilities are at least \(\alpha/N\), then \(p_y\ge\alpha/(t^2N)\ge\alpha/N\). This is where the new penalty improves the four-spike probability comparison.

First we prove a packing bound that is independent of \(t\):
$$
\mathcal D+5\mathcal S\ge\frac1{2048}.
\tag{10}
$$
Let \(E_m^{(1)}\) be the spikes \(a\) whose source \(c\) lies in \(J_m\) and whose probability satisfies \(Np_a<1/4\). If \(Nq_c<1/2\), its parity contributes more than \(1/(4N)\) to \(\mathcal D\). Distinct spikes have distinct source parities, so at most \(4N\mathcal D\) such spikes occur. For each remaining spike in \(E_m^{(1)}\), its source lies in \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), and its parity mass is exactly \(q_c=p_c+p_a\). Therefore \(Np_c>1/4\). Each such distinct source contributes more than \(1/(16N)\) to \(\mathcal S\), giving
$$
\frac{|E_m^{(1)}|}{N}\le4\mathcal D+16\mathcal S.
\tag{11}
$$
The four witness maps are injective. Hence at least \(|\mathcal G_m|-4|E_m^{(1)}|\) targets have all four witness probabilities at least \(1/(4N)\). By (9), each such target contributes at least \(1/(16N)\) to \(\mathcal S\). If the count lower bound is negative, the same ensuing inequality holds because \(\mathcal S\ge0\). Thus (11) gives
$$
\mathcal S
\ge\frac1{16}\left(\frac1{128}-\frac{4|E_m^{(1)}|}{N}\right)
\ge\frac1{2048}-\mathcal D-4\mathcal S,
$$
which proves (10).

When \(0<t\le7/8\), one has \(4t(1-t)\ge t/2\). By (7) and (10),
$$
N^2b(p)-1
\ge2\mathcal D+\frac t2\mathcal S
\ge\frac t{10}(\mathcal D+5\mathcal S)
\ge\frac t{20480}.
\tag{12}
$$

It remains to handle \(7/8\le t\le1\), where \(\lambda\ge11/4\). Let \(E_m^{(2)}\) consist of the spikes whose source \(c\) lies in \(J_m\) and whose probability satisfies \(Np_a<4/9\). There are at most \(81N\mathcal D\) of these spikes with \(Nq_c<8/9\), because each such distinct parity contributes more than \(1/(81N)\) to \(\mathcal D\). Every remaining spike in \(E_m^{(2)}\) has \(Np_c>4/9\), and (5) gives \(NH_p(c)>11/9\). Its source contribution to \(\mathcal V\) is therefore at least
$$
p_c(NH_p(c)-1)^2
\ge\frac4{9N}\left(\frac29\right)^2
=\frac{16}{729N}.
$$
Distinct spikes have distinct sources, proving
$$
\frac{|E_m^{(2)}|}{N}
\le81\mathcal D+\frac{729}{16}\mathcal V.
\tag{13}
$$
For each target avoiding these low-probability witnesses, (9) gives \(Np_y\ge4/(9t^2)\ge4/9\). Moreover,
$$
NH_p(y)\ge\frac{4(1+2t)}{9t^2}\ge\frac43,
$$
because \(1+2t-3t^2=(1-t)(1+3t)\ge0\). Its contribution to \(\mathcal V\) is at least \(4/(81N)\). Injectivity and (13) now yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V
&\ge\frac4{81}\left(\frac1{128}-\frac{4|E_m^{(2)}|}{N}\right)\\
&\ge\frac1{2592}-16\mathcal D-9\mathcal V.
\end{aligned}
$$
As before, this is valid even if the initial count lower bound is negative. Therefore \(10\mathcal V+16\mathcal D\ge1/2592\). Equation (7) then implies
$$
10(N^2b(p)-1)
\ge10\mathcal V+20\mathcal D
\ge\frac1{2592},
\qquad
N^2b(p)-1\ge\frac1{25920}\ge\frac t{25920}.
\tag{14}
$$
Combining (12) and (14), and using \(N^2=4^m\), proves (1) for all fields and penalties.

We finish with the exact zero-field calibration. Accepted facts 7538f1751c45475c and a2c2d2b38e2fa409 identify the number of unmatched ones of a word with its prefix-walk maximum, and identify \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) exactly with the words whose maximum is at least three. In a nonterminal word with reduced unmatched-symbol word \(1^k0^\ell\), \(\ell\ge1\), the successor has reduced word \(1^{k+1}0^{\ell-1}\). Thus every point of \(J_m\) has at least four unmatched ones. Conversely, for a word with at least four unmatched ones, change its last unmatched one to zero. The internally matched intervening words remain matched by 7538f1751c45475c; the resulting reduced word is \(1^{k-1}0^{\ell+1}\), with at least three unmatched ones, and its first unmatched zero is the changed position. Its source belongs to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\), and its successor is the original word. Consequently
$$
J_m=\left\lbrace y\in B_m:
\max_{0\le k\le m}\left(2\sum_{j=1}^k y_j-k\right)\ge4\right\rbrace.
\tag{15}
$$
The exact spike count from 6fee4107100a027d is \(e_m=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\). The exact Boolean count from a2c2d2b38e2fa409 is
$$
\begin{aligned}
u_{2r}&=2^{2r}-\binom{2r}{r}-2\binom{2r}{r+1},\\
u_{2r+1}&=2^{2r+1}-2\binom{2r+1}{r+1}-\binom{2r+1}{r+2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
Reflection of increments after the first visit to height four counts (15) exactly. For even length, paths ending at height four are fixed at the endpoint under this reflection, and paths visiting four and ending at most two correspond to all paths ending at least six. For odd length, the paths visiting four and ending at most three correspond to all paths ending at least five. Hence, with out-of-range binomial coefficients equal to zero,
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{2r}
&=\binom{2r}{r+2}+2\sum_{k=r+3}^{2r}\binom{2r}{k}
 =2^{2r}-\binom{2r}{r}-2\binom{2r}{r+1}-\binom{2r}{r+2},\\
h_{2r+1}
&=2\sum_{k=r+3}^{2r+1}\binom{2r+1}{k}
 =2^{2r+1}-2\binom{2r+1}{r+1}-2\binom{2r+1}{r+2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
At \(\gamma=0\), each Boolean probability is \(1/(u_m+t^2e_m)\), and each spike probability is \(t^2/(u_m+t^2e_m)\). Formula (5) therefore gives (2) by summing over the three disjoint classes \(E_m\), \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\setminus J_m\), and \(J_m\), with every atom retained.

The central-binomial estimate proved in 7538f1751c45475c shows \(\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}/2^m\to0\). All subtracted terms in (16) and (17) are at most that central coefficient. Thus \(e_m/N\to1\), \(u_m/N\to1\), and \(h_m/N\to1\). Dividing (2) by the appropriate powers of \(N\) proves (3). Its final expression follows by expanding \((1+t^2)^3\), and it gives the expansion \(1+4t+O(t^2)\).

For any \(\eta>0\), first choose a positive \(t\) small enough that the limit in (3) is below \(1+\eta/2\), and then choose \(m\ge7\) sufficiently large that \(4^m b(p_{0,t^2})<1+\eta\). This disproves a penalty-independent strict gap. At any fixed dimension, the same exact formula instead gives \(\lim_{t\downarrow0}4^m b(p_{0,t^2})=4^m/u_m^2\), so the dimension limit is essential to that conclusion. Finally, a hypothetical uniform lower bound \(4^m b-1\ge c\epsilon_i^\alpha\) with \(c>0\) and \(\alpha<1/2\) would imply, by first taking \(m\to\infty\) and then \(t\downarrow0\), that \(4t+O(t^2)\ge c t^{2\alpha}\), which is impossible. This verifies the stated sharp order without asserting an actual-depth or Oertel counterexample.
