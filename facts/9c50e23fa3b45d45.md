## Statement

For a bounded set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) having a nonempty lattice intersection, define its unnormalized capacity by

\[
B(E):=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^d}
\sum_{a\in E\cap\mathbb Z^d}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}.
\]

Thus for a finite integer set \(A\), the sum defining \(B(A)\) runs over \(A\) itself.

Let \(d\ge1\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d\) be a finite set of distinct vectors, and put \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\). Assume that \(\operatorname{aff}(A)=\mathbb R^d\) and \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\). For every real \(0<\lambda\le1\),

\[
B(A)\le
\left(2+\frac4\lambda\right)^d B\bigl(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P)\bigr).
\]

In particular, if \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\), then the interior condition follows from the affine-rank assumption, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
|A|&\le
\left(2+\frac4\lambda\right)^d
\bigl|\lambda\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d\bigr|
\qquad(0<\lambda\le1),\\
|A|&\le 6^d k,
\qquad
k:=\bigl|\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d\bigr|\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\]

There is no requirement that \(0\in A\), that \(A=P\cap\mathbb Z^d\), or that \(P\) have continuous centroid zero.

For every full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) with \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), the same capacity convention gives

\[
B(P)\le
\left(2+\frac4\lambda\right)^d
B\bigl(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P)\bigr)
\le
\left(2+\frac4\lambda\right)^d B(\lambda P)
\qquad(0<\lambda\le1).
\]

Consequently \(B(P)\le10^d B(P/2)\).

## Proof

Write \(c_\lambda=2+4/\lambda\). We first prove a lattice-count inequality for a convex body with continuous centroid zero, in an auxiliary dimension \(r\ge1\).

Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^r\) be a convex body with \(\int_K x\,dx=0\), and put \(v=\operatorname{vol}_r(K)>0\). Its centroid belongs to \(K\) by convexity. It lies in the interior: otherwise a supporting nonzero linear functional at the centroid would be nonnegative on \(K\) and strictly positive on a nonempty open subset of \(K\), contradicting the zero integral of that functional. In particular, \(K\cap(-K)\) is a convex body.

For \(x\in 2K=K+K\), define
\(h(x)=\operatorname{vol}_r(K\cap(x-K))^{1/r}\).
For \(x,y\in2K\) and \(0\le t\le1\), convexity gives

\[
(1-t)\bigl(K\cap(x-K)\bigr)
+t\bigl(K\cap(y-K)\bigr)
\subseteq
K\cap\bigl((1-t)x+ty-K\bigr).
\]

The Brunn–Minkowski inequality therefore shows that \(h\) is concave on \(2K\). Here the form used is
\(\operatorname{vol}_r((1-t)E+tF)^{1/r}
\ge(1-t)\operatorname{vol}_r(E)^{1/r}
+t\operatorname{vol}_r(F)^{1/r}\)
for compact convex sets \(E,F\subseteq\mathbb R^r\); zero-volume intersections are permitted.

The convolution identity
\(h(x)^r=\int_{\mathbb R^r}\mathbf1_K(u)\mathbf1_K(x-u)\,du\)
and Fubini give

\[
\int_{2K}h(x)^r\,dx=v^2,
\qquad
\int_{2K}x\,h(x)^r\,dx
=2v\int_Ku\,du=0.
\]

Thus \(h(x)^r/v^2\) is a probability density on \(2K\) with mean zero. Jensen's inequality for the concave function \(h\), followed by Hölder's inequality on \(2K\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
h(0)
&\ge \frac1{v^2}\int_{2K}h(x)^{r+1}\,dx\\
&\ge
\frac{\left(\int_{2K}h(x)^r\,dx\right)^{(r+1)/r}}
{v^2\operatorname{vol}_r(2K)^{1/r}}
=\frac{v^{1/r}}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently
\(\operatorname{vol}_r(K\cap(-K))\ge2^{-r}v\).
All the displayed integrals are finite because the domain is compact and \(h\) is bounded.

Now fix \(0<\lambda\le1\) and take the open, symmetric, convex set
\(T_\lambda=(\lambda/2)\operatorname{int}(K\cap(-K))\).
Convex-body interiors have the same volume as their closures, for example by taking increasing interior dilations about an interior point. The overlap estimate gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_r(T_\lambda)\ge(\lambda/4)^r v\).
Symmetry and convexity also give

\[
T_\lambda-T_\lambda
=\lambda\operatorname{int}(K\cap(-K))
\subseteq\lambda\operatorname{int}(K).
\]

Let \(N=|K\cap\mathbb Z^r|\) and
\(k_\lambda=|\lambda\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\mathbb Z^r|\).
For any point \(x\) belonging to at least one translate
\(a+T_\lambda\), \(a\in K\cap\mathbb Z^r\), fix one contributing center \(a_0\).
Every contributing center \(a\), including \(a_0\) itself, determines the distinct lattice vector

\[
a-a_0=(x-a_0)-(x-a)
\in T_\lambda-T_\lambda
\subseteq\lambda\operatorname{int}(K).
\]

The number of translates containing \(x\) is therefore at most \(k_\lambda\). The openness of \(T_\lambda\) ensures that every difference lies in the required interior. Integrating this pointwise multiplicity bound, and using
\(K+T_\lambda\subseteq K+(\lambda/2)K=(1+\lambda/2)K\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N\operatorname{vol}_r(T_\lambda)
&\le k_\lambda\operatorname{vol}_r
\left(\bigcup_{a\in K\cap\mathbb Z^r}(a+T_\lambda)\right)\\
&\le k_\lambda(1+\lambda/2)^r v.
\end{aligned}
\]

Dividing by the lower bound on \(\operatorname{vol}_r(T_\lambda)\) proves

\[
|K\cap\mathbb Z^r|
\le c_\lambda^r
|\lambda\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\mathbb Z^r|.
\tag{1}
\]

We next construct convex bodies to which (1) applies. Return to \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d\) and \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\) in the statement, and put \(M=|A|\). Fix a rational probability vector \(p=(p_a)_{a\in A}\) satisfying \(p_a>0\) for every \(a\) and \(\sum_{a\in A}p_a a=0\). Such rational laws will be obtained below when proving the capacity inequality.

Let \(m\ge2\) tend to infinity through multiples of a common denominator of the \(p_a\). Let \(\mathcal T_m(p)\) be the set of all distinct words in \(A^m\) having exactly \(mp_a\) copies of each label \(a\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_m(p),\\
V_m&=\left\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_m)\in(\mathbb R^d)^m:
\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf x_j=0\right\},\\
L_m&=V_m\cap(\mathbb Z^d)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every word has block sum \(m\sum_a p_a a=0\), so
\(Q_m\subseteq V_m\cap P^m\).
We verify that \(Q_m\) has full affine hull \(V_m\). For distinct block positions \(i,j\) and distinct labels \(a,b\in A\), all multiplicities are positive, so some word has \(a,b\) at these positions. Subtracting its swap gives a difference of words having blocks \(a-b,b-a\) at \(i,j\) and zero elsewhere. Since \(A-A\) spans \(\mathbb R^d\), these differences span all vectors with arbitrary opposite blocks \(\mathbf u,-\mathbf u\) at \(i,j\). Taking \(j=m\) and all \(i<m\) spans \(V_m\). Thus \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_m)=V_m\) and \(\dim Q_m=d(m-1)\).

Every permutation of the \(m\) blocks preserves \(Q_m\), acts orthogonally on \(V_m\), and preserves relative volume there. Its continuous centroid is consequently fixed by every block permutation. Any fixed vector has all its blocks equal, and a vector of this form in \(V_m\) is zero. Thus the continuous centroid of \(Q_m\), with respect to Lebesgue measure on \(V_m\), is exactly zero. This conclusion concerns the type-word hull and does not require the continuous centroid of \(P\) to vanish.

The linear chart

\[
\Phi_m:(\mathbb R^d)^{m-1}\longrightarrow V_m,
\qquad
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1})
\longmapsto
\left(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1},
-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\mathbf y_j\right)
\]

is a real linear isomorphism whose inverse retains the first \(m-1\) blocks. It maps the whole lattice \(\mathbb Z^{d(m-1)}\) bijectively onto \(L_m\). Because a linear isomorphism changes volume by a constant factor, it takes continuous centroids to continuous centroids. Applying (1) to \(\Phi_m^{-1}(Q_m)\) therefore gives

\[
|\mathcal T_m(p)|
\le |Q_m\cap L_m|
\le c_\lambda^{d(m-1)}
|\lambda\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m|.
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{relint}\) is relative to \(V_m\), and linear isomorphisms commute with relative interior and with multiplication by \(\lambda\).

Put \(U_\lambda=\lambda\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d\). It is a finite set containing zero. Every point of \(\lambda\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\) has every block in \(U_\lambda\). To verify the interior assertion, take \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\). For each block \(i\), choose another block \(j\). Every sufficiently small perturbation \(\mathbf h\in\mathbb R^d\) of block \(i\) lifts to a perturbation in \(V_m\) with blocks \(\mathbf h,-\mathbf h\) at \(i,j\); its Euclidean norm is \(\sqrt2\|\mathbf h\|\). A relative ball about \(\mathbf y\) contained in \(Q_m\) therefore projects onto a neighborhood of \(\mathbf y_i\) contained in \(P\). Hence \(\mathbf y_i\in\operatorname{int}(P)\). Scaling by \(\lambda\) proves the assertion for every block of \(\lambda\mathbf y\).

The block-sum-zero constraint yields more than the bound \(|U_\lambda|^m\). For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\lambda\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m|
&\le
\sum_{\substack{(\mathbf u_1,\ldots,\mathbf u_m)\in U_\lambda^m\\
\sum_j\mathbf u_j=0}}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_j\mathbf u_j}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{\mathbf u\in U_\lambda}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}\right)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking the infimum over \(\boldsymbol\theta\) shows that this cardinality is at most
\(B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))^m\).
The infimum need not be attained: the inequality holds for every tilt, and taking the positive integer power commutes with the infimum of nonnegative real numbers. The capacity here is at least one because \(0\in U_\lambda\). In particular no condition on the arithmetic mean of \(U_\lambda\) has been used.

Combining this with (2), and counting words by permutations of their entries, gives

\[
\frac{m!}{\prod_{a\in A}(mp_a)!}
\le c_\lambda^{d(m-1)}
B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))^m.
\tag{3}
\]

For a positive integer \(h\), integral comparison of \(\log x\) gives
\(\log(h!)=h\log h-h+O(\log(h+1))\).
Indeed \(\int_1^h\log x\,dx\le\log(h!)\le\int_1^h\log x\,dx+\log h\).
Applying this estimate to the finitely many factorials in (3) yields
\(\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|=mH(p)+O_M(\log(m+1))\), where
\(H(p)=-\sum_a p_a\log p_a\).
Taking logarithms in (3), dividing by \(m\), and letting \(m\) tend to infinity along the permitted multiples proves

\[
H(p)\le d\log c_\lambda
+\log B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))
\tag{4}
\]

for every rational full-support mean-zero law on \(A\).

It remains to pass from these laws to \(B(A)\). Choose \(\rho>0\) with the closed radius-\(\rho\) ball about zero contained in \(P\). For
\(f(\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}\),
the support function of \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\) gives

\[
f(\boldsymbol\theta)
\ge e^{\max_{a\in A}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}
\ge e^{\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}.
\]

Thus \(f\) is continuous and coercive, and it attains its infimum at some \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\). Differentiation shows that
\(p_a^*=e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot a}/B(A)\)
is a strictly positive probability vector with
\(\sum_a p_a^*a=0\).
Its entropy is
\(H(p^*)=-\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\sum_a p_a^*a+\log B(A)=\log B(A)\).

The integer matrix with columns \((1,a)\), \(a\in A\), has rank \(d+1\). Choose \(d+1\) independent columns. Approximate the other coordinates of \(p^*\) by rational numbers, and solve the normalization and mean-zero equations for the selected coordinates. The selected square integer matrix has rational inverse, so every resulting coordinate is rational. The resulting vectors converge to \(p^*\); for all sufficiently close approximations, every coordinate remains positive. If there are no other coordinates, the same observation shows that \(p^*\) itself is rational. This gives rational full-support mean-zero laws converging to \(p^*\).

Entropy is continuous on this finite probability simplex. Passing to the limit in (4) therefore gives
\(\log B(A)\le d\log c_\lambda+\log B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))\).
Exponentiating proves the main capacity inequality.

Suppose now that \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\). The arithmetic average belongs to \(P\). If zero were on its boundary, a supporting nonzero linear functional would be nonnegative on all of \(A\). The sum of its values is zero, so every value would be zero, contradicting \(\operatorname{aff}(A)=\mathbb R^d\). Thus \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\). For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\), the inequality \(e^x\ge1+x\) gives
\(\sum_{a\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}\ge M+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_a a=M\).
Equality holds at \(\boldsymbol\theta=0\), so \(B(A)=M\). Also
\(B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))\le|U_\lambda|\) by choosing zero tilt. The scaled finite-set count follows, and \(\lambda=1\) gives \(M\le6^d k\) with the entire original-lattice interior counted.

Finally, if \(P\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope containing zero in its interior, take \(A=P\cap\mathbb Z^d\). Its vertices are in \(A\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(A)=P\), and \(B(A)=B(P)\). The main inequality applies without an arithmetic-mean hypothesis on this complete lattice set. Since
\(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P)\subseteq\lambda P\),
their exponential sums satisfy the same inclusion inequality for every tilt, and hence
\(B(\lambda\operatorname{int}(P))\le B(\lambda P)\).
This proves the stated lattice-polytope contraction, including \(c_{1/2}=10\).

The required continuous centroid condition is established above for the auxiliary bodies \(K\) and the type-word hulls \(Q_m\); it is never substituted for the arithmetic-mean condition on \(A\). The scaled multiplicity bound, capacity comparison, and lattice-chart arguments are proved above.
