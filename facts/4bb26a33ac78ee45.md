## Statement

Let \(m_0,m_1\) be nonnegative integers and let \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}\in\lbrace1,2\rbrace\). Require \(m_1\ge1\) when \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\); no additional restriction on \(m_1\) is needed when \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\). Thus both hubs below are nontrivial even when \(m_0=0\) or, in the four-point-spine case, \(m_1=0\).

Choose arbitrary positive real numbers \(\tau,u_1,\ldots,u_{m_0},v_1,\ldots,v_{m_1}\). The scalar \(\tau\) is the center-weight ratio called \(t\) in the proposed two-hub tables; it is distinct from the conditional scales \(t_0,t_1\) below. Empty sums have value zero.

Put \(n=m_0+m_1+1\). In \(\mathbb R^n\), let \(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{m_0},f_1,\ldots,f_{m_1}\) be the standard coordinate vectors. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
A={}&\lbrace-e_0,0,e_0\rbrace
\cup\begin{cases}
\varnothing,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1,\\
\lbrace2e_0\rbrace,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2
\end{cases}\\
&{}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{m_0}\lbrace e_i,-e_i\rbrace
\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_1}\lbrace e_0+f_j,e_0-f_j\rbrace,
\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}(A).
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(Q\) is full-dimensional, \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\), and the set of nontrivial integral midpoint centers of \(A\) is exactly \(\lbrace c_0,c_1\rbrace\), where \(c_0=0\) and \(c_1=e_0\).

The two full reflected cores \(R_a=A\cap(2c_a-A)\) are
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_0&=\lbrace-e_0,0,e_0\rbrace
 \cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{m_0}\lbrace e_i,-e_i\rbrace,\\
R_1&=\lbrace e_0\rbrace
 \cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_1}\lbrace e_0+f_j,e_0-f_j\rbrace
 \cup\begin{cases}
 \varnothing,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1,\\
 \lbrace0,2e_0\rbrace,&q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2.
 \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
They satisfy \(A=R_0\cup R_1\), \(|R_0\cap R_1|=q_{\mathrm{ov}}\), and, with the original pair-capacity convention \(M_a=(|R_a|+1)/2\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_0:=|R_0|&=2m_0+3,&M_0&=m_0+2,\\
h_1:=|R_1|&=2m_1+2q_{\mathrm{ov}}-1,&M_1&=m_1+q_{\mathrm{ov}},\\
K:=|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^n:x\in A\rbrace|
&=m_0+m_1+2=M_0+M_1-q_{\mathrm{ov}}=n+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The total atom count is \(|A|=2K+q_{\mathrm{ov}}-2\). In particular \(K\) is the occupied parity count, not the ambient parity capacity \(2^n\).

There is one global affine functional \(\ell\cdot x\) whose weights \(w_x=\exp(\ell\cdot x)\) are exactly
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{atom}&w_x\\ \hline
-e_0,\ 0,\ e_0&\tau^{-1},\ 1,\ \tau\\
2e_0\quad(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2)&\tau^2\\
e_i,\ -e_i&u_i,\ u_i^{-1}\\
e_0+f_j,\ e_0-f_j&\tau v_j,\ \tau v_j^{-1}.
\end{array}
$$
Every integral midpoint relation satisfies the exact equality \(w_{(x+y)/2}^2=w_xw_y\). The original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\), the original law \(p_x=w_x/Z\), and \(Z_3=\sum_{x\in A}w_x^3\) are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z={}&\tau^{-1}+1+\tau+(q_{\mathrm{ov}}-1)\tau^2
+\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}(u_i+u_i^{-1})
+\tau\sum_{j=1}^{m_1}(v_j+v_j^{-1}),\\
Z_3={}&\tau^{-3}+1+\tau^3+(q_{\mathrm{ov}}-1)\tau^6
+\sum_{i=1}^{m_0}(u_i^3+u_i^{-3})
+\tau^3\sum_{j=1}^{m_1}(v_j^3+v_j^{-3}).
\end{aligned}
$$

In each translated core \(R_a-c_a\), take zero and one representative from each noncentral reflection pair. Let \(\mu_a\) be the mean of \(\cosh(\ell\cdot b)\) over those \(M_a\) displacement representatives, including zero. Set \(t_a=\log(2\mu_a)\). Use the full oriented core variance
\(V_a=h_a^{-1}\sum_{x\in R_a}(\ell\cdot(x-c_a))^2\). These quantities are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0&=\frac{1+\cosh(\log\tau)+
 \sum_{i=1}^{m_0}\cosh(\log u_i)}{m_0+2},\\
\mu_1&=\frac{1+(q_{\mathrm{ov}}-1)\cosh(\log\tau)+
 \sum_{j=1}^{m_1}\cosh(\log v_j)}{m_1+q_{\mathrm{ov}}},\\
V_0&=\frac{2}{h_0}\left[(\log\tau)^2+
 \sum_{i=1}^{m_0}(\log u_i)^2\right],\\
V_1&=\frac{2}{h_1}\left[(q_{\mathrm{ov}}-1)(\log\tau)^2+
 \sum_{j=1}^{m_1}(\log v_j)^2\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
All formulas count each nonzero pair twice in the full row and the central atom once.

For each \(\mathcal S\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), use the full core row at \(c_a\) precisely when \(a\in\mathcal S\), and singleton rows at all remaining supported centers. Let \(\widehat b_{\mathcal S}=\sum_xp_x\widehat H_{\mathcal S}(x)^2\), where \(\widehat H_{\mathcal S}\) is the geometric-mean sum of the chosen row, and let \(\nu_{\mathcal S}\) be the selected-row score variance averaged with center weights \(p_x\widehat H_{\mathcal S}(x)^2/\widehat b_{\mathcal S}\). Then, without changing the original law or its normalizer,
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3=\frac{K^2Z_3}{Z^3},\\
\Delta_0&=\frac{K^2(h_0^2-1)}{Z^3},
&\Delta_1&=\frac{K^2(h_1^2-1)\tau^3}{Z^3},\\
A_{\mathcal S}&:=K^2\widehat b_{\mathcal S}
=A_0+\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}\Delta_a,\\
\nu_{\mathcal S}
&=\frac{K^2}{A_{\mathcal S}Z^3}
\left[\mathbf1_{\lbrace0\in\mathcal S\rbrace}h_0^2V_0
+\mathbf1_{\lbrace1\in\mathcal S\rbrace}h_1^2\tau^3V_1\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, for any fixed \(C>0\), the existence of nonnegative
\(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\delta_0,\delta_1\) satisfying, for every \(\mathcal S\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) and each \(a\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log A_{\mathcal S}
&\ge\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}\gamma_a-
 \sum_{a\notin\mathcal S}\delta_a,\\
\delta_a&\le Ce^{-t_a}\gamma_a,\qquad
\nu_{\mathcal S}\le C\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}t_a^2\gamma_a
\end{aligned}
$$
is identical for the heterogeneous scalar table and this complete globally affine realization. This remains identical upon restricting to \(A_0<1\), taking any parameter sequence, or requiring one \(C\) uniformly. Thus an unbounded minimum-\(C\) obstruction in this table class is already a complete globally affine occupied-capacity obstruction. No conclusion about an ambient-\(2^n\) normalization or about actual depth moments follows.

The following analytic consequence concerns the suggested outlier attack, rather than claiming feasibility of this budget. In the three-point-spine case \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\), let \(m_0=m\to\infty\), let \(m_1=s\ge1\) vary with \(m\), and take \(\tau=u_i=W>0\) for every \(i\). Assume
\(s/m\to0\) and \((m+1)W^{-2}=o(s)\). Permit completely arbitrary heterogeneous positive \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\), varying with \(m\). Along any infinite subsequence on which the original \(A_0<1\), one has
\(\limsup \exp(t_1)\le\rho\), where \(\rho\in(2,3)\) is the unique root in that interval of \(\rho^3-6\rho+2=0\). In particular, no outlier arrangement in this regime can make the conditional scale \(t_1\) diverge while preserving activity.

## Proof

Write coordinates as \((z,x,y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{m_0}\times\mathbb R^{m_1}\), with zero-dimensional coordinate blocks allowed. Every point of \(Q\) has \(\lVert x\rVert_1\le1\) and \(\lVert y\rVert_1\le1\).

First consider the lattice census. At height \(z=0\), let \(\lambda\) be the total coefficient assigned to vertices \(e_0\pm f_j\) in any convex representation. All those vertices have height one; all other vertices have height at least minus one. Hence \(0\ge\lambda-(1-\lambda)\), so \(\lambda\le1/2\). Only those vertices contribute to \(y\), and therefore \(\lVert y\rVert_1\le1/2\). An integral \(y\) is consequently zero. An integral \(x\) with \(\lVert x\rVert_1\le1\) is zero or one of the vectors \(\pm e_i\) in its coordinate block. These are all present at height zero.

When \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\), the only possible integral heights are minus one, zero, and one. At height minus one the only point is \(-e_0\). The height-one face is the convex hull of \(e_0\) and \(e_0\pm f_j\); its lattice points are exactly those points, by the \(\ell_1\) bound on \(y\). This proves the stated census in this case.

When \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\), the only possible integral heights are minus one, zero, one, and two. The extreme slices consist of the single points \(-e_0\) and \(2e_0\). At height one, let \(\lambda_0\) be the total coefficient assigned to height-zero vertices in a convex representation. Every other vertex has height at most two. Thus \(1\le2(1-\lambda_0)\), giving \(\lambda_0\le1/2\). Only height-zero vertices contribute to \(x\), so \(\lVert x\rVert_1\le1/2\), and an integral \(x\) is zero. The integral possibilities for \(y\), using \(\lVert y\rVert_1\le1\), are zero and its signed unit vectors. These are exactly \(e_0\) and \(e_0\pm f_j\). The already proved height-zero argument completes the four-point-spine census. Hence \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\) in both cases.

The origin belongs to \(A\), and the linear span of \(A\) contains \(e_0\), every \(e_i\), and every \(f_j=(e_0+f_j)-e_0\). Thus \(Q\) is full-dimensional, including the cases with empty coordinate blocks.

The occupied parity classes are precisely the following distinct classes:
the pair \(\lbrace-e_0,e_0\rbrace\);
the singleton \(\lbrace0\rbrace\) if \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\), or the pair \(\lbrace0,2e_0\rbrace\) if \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\);
the pairs \(\lbrace e_i,-e_i\rbrace\);
and the pairs \(\lbrace e_0+f_j,e_0-f_j\rbrace\).
Their residues are respectively \(e_0\), zero, \(e_i\), and \(e_0+f_j\), so they are distinct. There are exactly \(m_0+m_1+2\) classes. A pair of distinct lattice points has integral midpoint exactly when its members have the same coordinatewise parity. The listed nontrivial pair midpoints are zero and \(e_0\). Zero is always nontrivial because of \(\lbrace-e_0,e_0\rbrace\). When \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\), the hypothesis \(m_1\ge1\) makes \(e_0\) nontrivial. When \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\), the spine pair \(\lbrace0,2e_0\rbrace\) does so even for \(m_1=0\). Thus there are exactly two nontrivial centers.

The parity list also determines every reflected row. Apart from the two hubs, a supported row can only be a singleton, because any nontrivial reflected pair would make its supported center a further integral midpoint center. Reflecting the listed pairs about zero and \(e_0\) gives exactly the stated \(R_0,R_1\). Their intersection is \(\lbrace e_0\rbrace\) in the three-point-spine case and \(\lbrace0,e_0\rbrace\) in the four-point-spine case. The cardinalities, capacities, and occupied-count formula follow. The cores are complete convex lattice supports as well: each is \((Q\cap(2c_a-Q))\cap\mathbb Z^n\), using the established completeness of \(A\).

Define the single functional by
\(\ell\cdot e_0=\log\tau\),
\(\ell\cdot e_i=\log u_i\), and
\(\ell\cdot f_j=\log v_j\).
These assignments are consistent because the vectors are a basis. Exponentiation gives every stated weight. For all \(x,y\in A\) with integral midpoint \(j\), linearity gives \(\ell\cdot j=(\ell\cdot x+\ell\cdot y)/2\). Thus \(w_j^2=w_xw_y\), and dividing by the original \(Z^2\) proves the same equality for \(p\). In particular every required midpoint-log-concavity inequality holds with equality. Summing the weights and their cubes proves the displayed formulas for \(Z\) and \(Z_3\).

For any reflected pair about a supported center \(j\), affinity gives
\(\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=p_j\). Its score is
$$
\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}}=\ell\cdot(x-j).
$$
Thus the full row law at \(c_a\) is uniform on its \(h_a\) atoms. In \(R_0-c_0\), the noncentral representative scores are \(\log\tau\) and the \(\log u_i\). In \(R_1-c_1\), they are the \(\log v_j\), with the additional \(\log\tau\) precisely when \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\). Including the zero representative gives the two formulas for \(\mu_a\); taking both orientations and the zero score gives exactly the formulas for \(V_a\).

For the selected rows,
\(\widehat H_{\mathcal S}(c_a)=h_ap_{c_a}\) if \(a\in\mathcal S\), and otherwise \(\widehat H_{\mathcal S}(c_a)=p_{c_a}\). All other rows have geometric sum \(p_x\) and score zero. The original probabilities at the centers are \(p_{c_0}=1/Z\) and \(p_{c_1}=\tau/Z\). Hence
$$
\widehat b_{\mathcal S}
=\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3+
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal S}(h_a^2-1)p_{c_a}^3.
$$
Multiplication by \(K^2\) proves every selected-energy formula. The selected score average is exactly
$$
\nu_{\mathcal S}
=\frac{\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}h_a^2p_{c_a}^3V_a}
 {\widehat b_{\mathcal S}},
$$
which proves its displayed normalizer and coefficients. These calculations use the full original support; they neither duplicate a shared center in the cubic sum nor replace the original \(Z\) by a conditional normalizer. All data appearing in the four-subset budget are therefore identical to the scalar table data. The assertion about feasibility for every fixed \(C\), and consequently about uniform constants or unbounded minimum constants, is a direct identity of the same four inequalities on the same four unknowns.

It remains to prove the heterogeneous-outlier consequence. Work on an arbitrary infinite active subsequence. Define, only as algebraic aliases for sums computed from the same original weights,
$$
x=W^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal L=(m+1)x^2,\qquad
\mathcal U=\sum_{j=1}^s(v_j+v_j^{-1}),\qquad
\mathcal W=Z/W,\qquad \mathcal T=Z_3/W^3.
$$
The assumptions give \(x\to0\), \(\mathcal L=o(s)\), and \(s=o(m)\). Exact summation yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=m+s+2,\\
\mathcal W&=m+1+\mathcal L+x+\mathcal U,\\
\mathcal T&=m+1+(m+1)x^6+x^3+
 \sum_{j=1}^s(v_j^3+v_j^{-3}),\\
A_0&=K^2\mathcal T/\mathcal W^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The \(2s\) endpoints \(e_0\pm f_j\) have total original probability \(\mathcal U/\mathcal W\). Activity and Hölder's inequality give
$$
\frac{\mathcal U}{\mathcal W}
\le(2s)^{2/3}\left(\sum_{j=1}^s
 [p_{e_0+f_j}^3+p_{e_0-f_j}^3]\right)^{1/3}
<(2s/K)^{2/3}=o(1).
$$
Since \(\mathcal W=m+1+o(m)+\mathcal U\), this implies \(\mathcal U=o(m)\). Also \(\mathcal U\ge2s\).

Put \(\zeta=\mathcal W-K=\mathcal U-s-1+\mathcal L+x\), so \(\zeta=O(\mathcal U)\). Activity is the exact strict inequality
\(\mathcal T<\mathcal W^3/K^2\). After subtracting \(3\mathcal W-2K\), it becomes
$$
F:=\mathcal T-3\mathcal W+2K
<3\zeta^2/K+\zeta^3/K^2
=O(\mathcal U^2/m+\mathcal U^3/m^2).
$$
Let \(a_j=v_j+v_j^{-1}\ge2\). The identity
\(v_j^3+v_j^{-3}=a_j^3-3a_j\) gives the exact expansion
$$
F=2s+2+\sum_{j=1}^s a_j^3-6\mathcal U
 +(m+1)x^6-3\mathcal L+x^3-3x.
$$
Jensen's inequality for the cubic gives
$$
F\ge\frac{\mathcal U^3}{s^2}-6\mathcal U+2s+2
 -3\mathcal L-3x.
$$
The activity upper bound is \(o(\mathcal U^3/s^2)\), since
$$
\frac{\mathcal U^2/m+\mathcal U^3/m^2}
 {\mathcal U^3/s^2}
=\frac{s^2}{m\mathcal U}+\frac{s^2}{m^2}
\le\frac{s}{2m}+\frac{s^2}{m^2}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Writing \(r=\mathcal U/s\ge2\), combining the last bounds and using
\(\mathcal L=o(s)\) and \(x=o(1)\) gives
\(r^3-6r+2\le o(r^3)+o(1)\).
This first forces \(r\) to be bounded: an unbounded subsequence would, after division by \(r^3\), give \(1\le o(1)\). For bounded \(r\), it then gives
\(\limsup(r^3-6r+2)\le0\). The polynomial \(r^3-6r+2\) is strictly increasing for \(r\ge2\), is negative at two and positive at three, and therefore has precisely one root \(\rho\) there. It follows that \(\limsup r\le\rho\).

Finally the already proved conditional formula, including its zero representative, gives
$$
\exp(t_1)=2\mu_1=\frac{2+\mathcal U}{s+1}
=\frac{2+sr}{s+1}\le\max(2,r).
$$
Thus \(\limsup\exp(t_1)\le\rho\). This proves the stated barrier with arbitrary heterogeneous fields, including any number of outliers. It does not supply the missing four-subset budget inequality outside or inside this regime.
