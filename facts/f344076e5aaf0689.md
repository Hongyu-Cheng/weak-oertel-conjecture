## Statement

For a nondegenerate triangle $T$ of area $V$, put $L_\tau=\{x:f_T(x)\ge\tau V\}$, where $x\in T$ and $f_T$ is ordinary raw depth. For $0<\tau\le4/9$, set $a=(1-\sqrt{1-2\tau})/2$ and $b=\sqrt{\tau}/2$. Then $|L_\tau|/V=1-3a-3\tau\log(b/a)$, with endpoint values $|L_0|/V=1$ and $|L_{4/9}|/V=0$. For $0<\tau<4/9$ the level has positive area, is strictly convex with piecewise hyperbolic boundary and has the original triangle centroid. Also $\int_T f_T(x)\,dx=7V^2/54$.

## Proof

By 431423848c9bacf8 Part 1, the raw depth of a point with ordered barycentrics $A\ge B\ge C$ is $4BCV$. In a single ordered chamber use $(B,C)$ as coordinates, with $0\le C\le1/3$ and $C\le B\le(1-C)/2$. The uniform probability density in these barycentric coordinates is two and there are six chambers with null overlaps. The extra inequality $4BC\ge\tau$ changes the lower limit on $B$ to $\max(C,\tau/(4C))$. This interval first becomes nonempty at $C=a$ and its lower boundary switches at $C=b$. Consequently
$$
|L_\tau|/V=12\left[
\int_a^b\left(\frac{1-C}{2}-\frac{\tau}{4C}\right)dC+
\int_b^{1/3}\frac{1-3C}{2}\,dC\right]
=1-3a-3\tau\log(b/a),
$$
using $2a(1-a)=\tau=4b^2$. The zero level is all of $T$ and the peak chamber interval reduces to $a=b=1/3$. Direct polynomial integration yields $\int_T f_T(x)\,dx=12V^2\int_0^{1/3}\int_C^{(1-C)/2}4BC\,dB\,dC=7V^2/54$.

The depth condition is equivalently the three pair-product constraints $4\lambda_i\lambda_j\ge\tau$ for $i\ne j$. For positive $\tau$ all coordinates are positive, and each $\log\lambda_i+\log\lambda_j$ is strictly concave on the open triangle because that pair gives two independent affine coordinates. At a strict combination of two distinct points of the level, all three constraints are strict. The centroid has all inequalities strict when $\tau<4/9$, so these levels have interior, are strictly convex, and their boundary lies on the three hyperbolas. Every vertex permutation is a volume-preserving affine map of the triangle onto itself and preserves the level. Its centroid is therefore fixed by every such permutation, which forces all barycentric coordinates to be equal. The same symmetry gives the ordinary centroid of $L_0$.
