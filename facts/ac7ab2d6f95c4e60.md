## Statement

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}\{P=(0,1),R=(1,1),S=(-1,-2)\}$ and let the central planes have normals $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$. The exposed edge $PR$ through the upper endpoint $P$ of the first central chord misses both other planes, but filling with $P_\varepsilon=(0,1+\varepsilon)$ strictly increases both other normalized central section lengths, by $2\varepsilon/[3(3+\varepsilon)]$ and $3\varepsilon/[4(4+\varepsilon)]$, respectively.

## Proof

The origin is the centroid, hence interior. The edge $PR$ lies in $y>0,\ x+y>0$, and the edge $PS$ crosses both other lines, by the strict opposite signs at its endpoints. The fill is the triangle with vertices $P_\varepsilon,R,S$, since $P$ lies between the origin and $P_\varepsilon$ and the origin belongs to that triangle for small $\varepsilon$ (its barycentric coordinates remain positive).

On $y=0$, the new chord has $x$ endpoints $1/3$ on $RS$ and $-(1+\varepsilon)/(3+\varepsilon)$ on $P_\varepsilon S$, by linear interpolation of the endpoint heights. Its length was $2/3$, and the increase is $2\varepsilon/[3(3+\varepsilon)]$. On $x+y=0$, the scalar $x+y$ has values $2,-3,1+\varepsilon$ at $R,S,P_\varepsilon$. Thus the chord has $x$ endpoints $1/5$ and $-(1+\varepsilon)/(4+\varepsilon)$. Its Euclidean length is $\sqrt2$ times the interval length in $x$, and normalizing by $\lVert(-1,-1)\rVert=\sqrt2$ cancels that factor. Its old normalized length was $9/20$, and the increase is $3\varepsilon/[4(4+\varepsilon)]$. In particular, a subsequent shave avoiding both these planes cannot restore their old sections.
