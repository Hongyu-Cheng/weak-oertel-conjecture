## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Retain the complete table, potential, barycentric families, integral hitting masses, and score of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. The normalization is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\), and \(B=\max_D\phi\). In particular, \(B\) is the canonical potential range, not the hitting score.

For the array definitions of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc, use both indices in the complete positive support \(D\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(p)&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I},\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\qquad 0\log0=0,\\
S(q)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For any finite strictly convex function \(F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), use the arbitrary-cost objective and full-face entropy selection of accepted fact c300e9d112cefeb0:

\[
V_F(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}F(\mathbf J),\qquad
G_F=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{F,\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}).
\]

Here \(r_F\) maximizes \(H\) on the entire face minimizing \(V_F\), and \(q_F\) is its target law. The quadratic-specific row conclusions of 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc are not assumed for a general \(F\).

For every integer \(m\ge1\), set

\[
n=2m,\qquad N=2^m,\qquad
\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
Z=N^2+N-\frac12,
\]

and define the entire table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{0,1\}^n\ \cup\ \{-j\mathbf1:1\le j\le N\},\\
p_{\mathbf I}&=
\begin{cases}
1/Z,&\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{-N\mathbf1\},\\
1/(2Z),&\mathbf I=-N\mathbf1,\\
0,&\mathbf I\notin D.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

The new scalar \(Z\) is a probability normalization factor, distinct from the canonical total weight \(W=1\). This is a complete globally extendable table with

\[
\phi_{\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\log2,&\mathbf I=-N\mathbf1,\\
0,&\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{-N\mathbf1\},
\end{cases}
\qquad B=\log2.
\]

For every finite strictly convex \(F\), the full minimizing face is the singleton

\[
r_{F,\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
p_{\mathbf I},&\mathbf J=\mathbf0,\\
0,&\mathbf J\ne\mathbf0.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently

\[
S(q_F)=H(r_F)=0,\qquad G_F=\frac{\log2}{2Z}.
\]

With the full original capacities, every center and every cutoff satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;p)&\le
\begin{cases}
1/Z,&\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\},\\
(N+1/2)/Z,&\mathbf J=\mathbf0,\\
(N-j+1/2)/Z,&\mathbf J=-j\mathbf1,\quad 1\le j\le N,
\end{cases}
\qquad L\ge0,\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)&\le\frac{N+1/2}{Z}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular,

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)
\left(1+[n\log2-S(q_F)]+G_F\right)
\frac{e^{S(q_F)}}{1+H(r_F)}=0.
\]

Thus there is no absolute \(c>0\) for which

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)
\ge c\,e^{-S(q_F)}
\frac{1+H(r_F)}{1+[n\log2-S(q_F)]+G_F}

holds for all the stated tables and entropy-selected strictly convex costs. The same disproof applies when the cost may be chosen adaptively from the table: every such \(F\) has the displayed selected array. One explicit allowed choice is \(F(x)=\|x\|^2\). On these same tables,

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)\ge\frac2Z>2^{-n}.
\]

## Proof

First verify the entire lattice support. Let

\[
Q=[0,1]^n,\qquad
P=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(Q\cup\{-N\mathbf1\}\bigr).
\]

Every \(x\in P\) can be written as

\[
x=-\theta N\mathbf1+(1-\theta)y,
\qquad 0\le\theta\le1,\quad y\in Q.
\]

If \(\theta=0\) and \(x\) is integral, then \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\). If \(\theta>0\), the difference between the greatest and least coordinates of \(x\) is at most \(1-\theta<1\). For integral \(x\), all coordinates must therefore equal one integer \(a\). Every coordinate is at least \(-N\), and its upper bound \(1-(N+1)\theta\) is strictly below one, so \(-N\le a\le0\). These sites are \(\mathbf0,-\mathbf1,\ldots,-N\mathbf1\). Conversely, every negative chain site lies on the segment from \(\mathbf0\in Q\) to \(-N\mathbf1\). Therefore

\[
P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\]

This proves completeness, including all boundary sites, in the original integer dimension \(n\).

There are \(2^n=N^2\) cube sites. The sum of all unnormalized chain weights is \(N-1/2\), so the probabilities sum to one. The convex, finite function

\[
\Phi(x)=(\log2)\max\left\{
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i-(N-1),\ 0\right\}
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^n)
\]

is zero at every cube site and at \(-j\mathbf1\) for \(1\le j\le N-1\), and has value \(\log2\) at \(-N\mathbf1\). Define

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
Z^{-1}e^{-\Phi(x)},&x\in P,\\
0,&x\notin P.
\end{cases}
\]

If both endpoints lie in \(P\), convexity of \(P\) and of \(\Phi\) proves the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). If either endpoint lies outside \(P\), the right side is zero, so the inequality holds by nonnegativity. Thus \(f\) is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension. Completeness proves that it agrees with every lattice value and every lattice zero of \(p\), exactly in the convention of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Since \(p_{\max}=1/Z\), its normalized lattice potential is the stated \(\phi\), with \(B=\log2\).

The original mean is exactly zero. At any fixed coordinate, the sum of the unnormalized cube coordinates is \(2^{n-1}=N^2/2\), whereas the absolute value of the unnormalized negative-chain coordinate sum is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}j+\frac N2
=\frac{N(N-1)}2+\frac N2
=\frac{N^2}2.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0.
\]

We establish the entire minimizing face for arbitrary strictly convex costs directly. For every feasible array, summing its column equations gives \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}=1\), and summing its row moment equations gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0.
\]

Finite Jensen inequality yields

\[
V_F(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}F(\mathbf J)
\ge F(\mathbf0).
\]

For strictly convex \(F\), equality requires all positive-mass targets to be the same site. Indeed, if two distinct sites have positive coefficients, group those two terms, apply strict convexity to their normalized combination, and then apply finite Jensen inequality to the remaining terms. The resulting inequality is strict. Since the target mean is zero, equality therefore requires \(q_{\mathbf0}=1\).

The all-to-zero array in the statement is feasible because its column sums are \(p\) and its only row moment is the original mean zero. It attains \(F(\mathbf0)\). Conversely, \(q_{\mathbf0}=1\) forces all nonzero target rows to vanish by nonnegativity; the column equations then force \(r_{\mathbf0,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\) at every source. Thus the entire minimizing face, in the arbitrary-cost meaning of accepted fact c300e9d112cefeb0, is exactly this singleton. This holds for every finite strictly convex \(F\), including \(F(x)=\|x\|^2\), independently of how \(F\) is chosen.

Entropy maximization on the whole face has no remaining tie. The sole assignment share in every column is one, so \(H(r_F)=0\); the target law is unit mass at zero, so \(S(q_F)=0\). The only positive normalized row is the complete original table \(p\), whose globally log-concave extension and integer mean were proved above. Its assignment-share potential is identically zero on \(D\). All row properties needed for this arbitrary-cost example are therefore proved for its actual selected rows, without assuming an arbitrary-cost transfer of the quadratic theorem 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc. Since \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\),

\[
G_F=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
=\frac{\log2}{2Z}.
\]

We next bound the integral hitting mass at every center and cutoff with the full original capacities. For any nonzero base normal \(\mathbf b\), the closed set

\[
A_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf b)
=\{\mathbf I\in D:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0\}
\]

hits every barycentric support at \(\mathbf J\), because the positively weighted average of its atom scores is zero and not all scores can be strictly negative. It therefore hits every \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\), for every \(L\ge0\), with the original potential gaps \(G_E\) of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Equivalently, this is the vertical-normal case \(t=0\) of that fact's closed lower-halfspace characterization. All equality atoms are included, and

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;p)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in A_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf b)}p_{\mathbf I}.
\]

For a nonzero Boolean target \(\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^n\), choose

\[
b_i=
\begin{cases}
n+1,&\eta_i=1,\\
-1,&\eta_i=0.
\end{cases}
\]

The functional \(\mathbf b\cdot x\) has its unique maximum on the Boolean cube at \(\boldsymbol\eta\). If \(s\ge1\) is the number of one coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\eta\), then

\[
\sum_i b_i=(n+2)s-n\ge2,\qquad
\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\eta=(n+1)s>0.
\]

Every negative chain site has value \(-j\sum_i b_i<0\). Thus \(A_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf b)=\{\boldsymbol\eta\}\), including all its equality atoms, and its original weight is \(1/Z\).

For \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\), choose \(\mathbf b=-\mathbf1\). Its closed set consists precisely of zero and the entire negative chain. Its original weight is

\[
\frac{1+(N-1)+1/2}{Z}=\frac{N+1/2}{Z}.
\]

For \(\mathbf J=-j\mathbf1\), the same normal gives exactly the chain sites \(-\ell\mathbf1\) with \(j\le\ell\le N\), and no cube site. Its original weight, including the equality site \(-j\mathbf1\), is \((N-j+1/2)/Z\). These cases exhaust the complete support and prove the displayed bounds for every center and every cutoff. Division by \(1+L\ge1\) and maximization prove the score upper bound. These are actual integral hitting sets, rather than fractional full-occurrence packing certificates.

Since \(S(q_F)=H(r_F)=0\), the ratio of the proposed left side to its right side without the coefficient \(c\) is

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)
\left(1+n\log2+\frac{\log2}{2Z}\right).
\]

Using \(Z\ge N^2\), \(N=2^m\ge2\), and \(0<\log2<1\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)
\left(1+n\log2+G_F\right)
&\le\frac{N+1/2}{Z}(2m+2)\\
&\le\frac{(N+1)(2m+2)}{N^2}
\le\frac{4(m+1)}{2^m}\ \longrightarrow\ 0.
\end{aligned}
\]

For instance, \(2^m\ge\binom m3\) for \(m\ge3\) proves this convergence by comparison with a cubic polynomial. For every fixed \(c>0\), sufficiently large \(m\) therefore makes this ratio strictly less than \(c\). This proves the claimed violation, with actual selected strictly convex costs and upper bounds controlling all original centers and cutoffs. It also proves failure under adaptive choice because every such cost selects the same array.

Finally verify the dyadic-score calibration. At \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\) and cutoff zero, the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) is required. For each \(1\le j\le N-1\), the two-point support

\[
E_j=\{-j\mathbf1,\mathbf1\}
\]

is affinely independent, has positive barycentric coefficients \(1/(j+1)\) at \(-j\mathbf1\) and \(j/(j+1)\) at \(\mathbf1\), and has barycenter zero. Both potentials are zero, so \(G_{E_j}=0\). Any hitting set must contain zero and either the cube site \(\mathbf1\), or every one of the \(N-1\) indicated negative sites. Each such atom has original weight \(1/Z\); since \(N\ge2\), every hitting set has weight at least \(2/Z\). Therefore

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)
\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf0,0;p)
\ge\frac2Z
>\frac1{N^2}=2^{-n},
\]

where \(2N^2>Z=N^2+N-1/2\). The lower and upper bounds use one unchanged original table and the same canonical logarithmic costs. They refute the entropy-deficit consumer while leaving the locked original dyadic target unrefuted.
