## Statement

Let
$$
m=5,
\qquad L=100,
\qquad D=2,
\qquad a=(10000,1,2,1,1),
\qquad i=0.
$$
On the full original grid
$$
\mathcal K_{100}
=\left\lbrace k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{5}:
\sum_{\ell=0}^{4}k_\ell=100\right\rbrace,
$$
define
$$
Z=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{100}}(a\cdot k)^2,
\qquad
M_0=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{100}}k_0(a\cdot k)^2,
\qquad
F_0=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{100}}k_0(k_0-1)(a\cdot k)^2,
$$
and \(P_0=2M_0^2+ZM_0-ZF_0\). Then
$$
(a_1-a_2)(\partial_1P_0-\partial_2P_0)
=-818865398254701720686920000<0.
$$
Consequently \(P_0\), with \(a_0\) fixed, is not Schur-convex in its other four coefficients. At the same coefficient vector,
$$
P_0=164266639694770438409896878167850040000>0.
$$

## Proof

We first derive closed formulas for the original sums, including their multiplicities. For general integers \(m\ge2\), \(L\ge4\), and \(D=2\), use \(\mathcal K_L=\lbrace k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_\ell k_\ell=L\rbrace\) and write
$$
\sigma=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_\ell,
\qquad
\tau=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_\ell^2,
\qquad
\mathsf B_r=\binom{L+m-1}{L-r}
\quad(0\le r\le4).
$$
For nonnegative integers \(r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1}\) having total \(R\le L\), the exact full-grid factorial identity is
$$
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}
\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}(k_\ell)_{\underline{r_\ell}}
=\left(\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}r_\ell!\right)
\binom{L+m-1}{L-R}.
\tag{1}
$$
Here \((u)_{\underline r}=u(u-1)\cdots(u-r+1)\), with value one when \(r=0\). Indeed, the generating series for its left side over all \(L\) is
$$
\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
\left(\sum_{u\ge0}(u)_{\underline{r_\ell}}z^u\right)
=\left(\prod_\ell r_\ell!\right)
\frac{z^R}{(1-z)^{m+R}},
$$
and extracting the coefficient of \(z^L\) proves (1). Thus every original composition is counted once.

Expansion of the square using \(k_\ell^2=(k_\ell)_{\underline2}+k_\ell\) and (1) gives
$$
Z=\mathsf B_2(\sigma^2+\tau)+\mathsf B_1\tau.
\tag{2}
$$
The corresponding marked formulas are
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_0={}&\mathsf B_3
 (\sigma^2+2a_0\sigma+\tau+2a_0^2)\\
&+\mathsf B_2(\tau+2a_0\sigma+3a_0^2)
 +\mathsf B_1a_0^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_0={}&2\mathsf B_4
 (\sigma^2+4a_0\sigma+\tau+6a_0^2)\\
&+2\mathsf B_3(\tau+4a_0\sigma+10a_0^2)
 +8\mathsf B_2a_0^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
For an explicit derivation of the marking in (3) and (4), introduce a formal variable \(t\). The product
$$
\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}(1-ze^{ta_\ell})^{-1}
$$
has coefficient of \(z^L\) equal to
\(\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}e^{t(a\cdot k)}\).
Inserting \(k_0\) into this sum replaces the factor at coordinate zero by
\(ze^{ta_0}(1-ze^{ta_0})^{-2}\). Equivalently, the marked sum is over compositions of \(L-1\) in \(m+1\) coordinates, with the coefficient \(a_0\) duplicated and the score shifted by \(a_0\). Its coefficient sum and square sum are \(\sigma+a_0\) and \(\tau+a_0^2\). Its count, linear-score sum, and squared-score sum are respectively
$$
\mathsf B_1,
\qquad
\mathsf B_2(\sigma+a_0),
\qquad
\mathsf B_3\bigl((\sigma+a_0)^2+\tau+a_0^2\bigr)
+\mathsf B_2(\tau+a_0^2).
$$
Adding the score shift and expanding proves (3).

Inserting \(k_0(k_0-1)\) instead replaces that factor by
\(2z^2e^{2ta_0}(1-ze^{ta_0})^{-3}\). This is twice the sum over compositions of \(L-2\) in \(m+2\) coordinates, with two additional copies of \(a_0\) and score shift \(2a_0\). The analogous count, linear-score sum, and squared-score sum are
$$
\mathsf B_2,
\qquad
\mathsf B_3(\sigma+2a_0),
\qquad
\mathsf B_4\bigl((\sigma+2a_0)^2+\tau+2a_0^2\bigr)
+\mathsf B_3(\tau+2a_0^2).
$$
Multiplying by two and adding the shift \(2a_0\) proves (4). These auxiliary generating identities derive the original unit-multiplicity sums; they do not replace the original support or its measure.

Let a prime denote \(\partial_1-\partial_2\). Since
\(\sigma'=0\), \(\tau'=2(a_1-a_2)\), and \(a_0'=0\), equations (2)--(4) give the exact derivative identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z'&=2(a_1-a_2)(\mathsf B_1+\mathsf B_2),\\
M_0'&=2(a_1-a_2)(\mathsf B_2+\mathsf B_3),\\
F_0'&=4(a_1-a_2)(\mathsf B_3+\mathsf B_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
These are also precisely the original directional sums
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z'&=2\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}(k_1-k_2)(a\cdot k),\\
M_0'&=2\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}k_0(k_1-k_2)(a\cdot k),\\
F_0'&=2\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}k_0(k_0-1)(k_1-k_2)(a\cdot k).
\end{aligned}
$$

For the stated witness, all five coefficients are strictly positive, the full grid has
\(\mathsf B_0=\binom{104}{100}=4598126\) compositions, and
$$
\sigma=10005,
\qquad \tau=100000007,
$$
$$
(\mathsf B_1,\mathsf B_2,\mathsf B_3,\mathsf B_4)
=(91962520,1517381580,21243342120,257575523205).
$$
Substitution into (2)--(5), with \(a_1-a_2=-1\), gives

| Original sum | Exact value | Directional derivative | Exact value |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| \(Z\) | 312824355357948200 | \(Z'\) | -3218688200 |
| \(M_0\) | 13670031568990618900 | \(M_0'\) | -45521447400 |
| \(F_0\) | 683288246749011274800 | \(F_0'\) | -1115275461300 |

In particular,
$$
\frac{M_0'}{Z'}=\frac{99}{7},
\qquad
\frac{F_0'}{Z'}=\frac{693}{2}.
$$
Differentiating the original polynomial, with no omitted product term, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_0'
&=4M_0M_0'+Z'(M_0-F_0)+Z(M_0'-F_0')\\
&=\frac{Z'}{14}\bigl(806M_0-14F_0-4653Z\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
The smaller integer expression inside parentheses is
$$
806M_0-14F_0-4653Z=-3561735360251988400.
$$
Consequently
$$
P_0'
=\frac{(-3218688200)(-3561735360251988400)}{14}
=818865398254701720686920000>0,
$$
and multiplying by \(a_1-a_2=-1\) proves the claimed negative Schur product.

This sign also gives a direct smoothing violation: for
\(a(t)=(10000,1+t,2-t,1,1)\), the other four coefficients have fixed sum and become more equal for \(0<t<1/2\), while
\(\left.\frac{d}{dt}P_0(a(t))\right|_{t=0}=P_0'>0\).
By differentiability, sufficiently small positive \(t\) therefore increase \(P_0\), contrary to Schur-convexity.

Finally, direct substitution of the exact original sums gives
$$
2M_0^2+ZM_0-ZF_0
=164266639694770438409896878167850040000>0.
$$
Thus the finite witness refutes the proposed derivative inequality and the resulting global smoothing method, while leaving the numerical assertion \(P_i\ge0\) unresolved in general. No claim about the locked weak Oertel conjecture follows from this counterexample.
