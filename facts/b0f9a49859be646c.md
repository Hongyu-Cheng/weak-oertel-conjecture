## Statement

The symbols \(W,V,N,T\) below are real scalar random variables; they do not denote the locked problem's convex body or mixed-integer volume. Let \(K\ge1\). Let \(Y\) be a latent random element with law \(\mu\) on a measurable space \(\mathcal Y\). Suppose \(\iota:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal Y\) is a measurable measure-preserving involution, and \(W=w(Y)\), \(V=v(Y)\) are real-valued measurable functions satisfying, \(\mu\)-almost surely,
\[
w(\iota y)=-w(y),\qquad v(\iota y)=v(y),\qquad \mathbb EV=0.
\]
Let \(N\) be a real random variable with \(\mathbb E[N\mid\sigma(Y)]=0\), and put \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb EN^2<\infty\). Assume
\[
\mathbb EN^4\le K\sigma^4,\qquad \mathbb EV^4\le K\sigma^4.
\]
No integrability or fourth-moment hypothesis on \(W\) is imposed. Then, for \(T:=W+V+N\),
\[
\min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}\ge\frac1{65536K}.
\]
If \(\sigma=0\), the stronger lower bound \(1/2\) holds. If the supplied fourth-moment constant is any \(K\ge0\), the uniformly valid choice is \(c(K):=1/(65536\max\{1,K\})\), since a positive \(\sigma\) forces \(K\ge1\).

For the sum extension, let \(b\ge1\), and let the full experiments \((Y_i,N_i)\), \(1\le i\le b\), be independent. In row \(i\), let \(W_i=w_i(Y_i)\), \(V_i=v_i(Y_i)\), and suppose that the latent law admits a measure-preserving involution under which \(W_i\) is odd and \(V_i\) is even. Suppose also that \(\mathbb EV_i=0\), \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid\sigma(Y_i)]=0\), and, with \(\sigma_i^2:=\mathbb EN_i^2<\infty\),
\[
\mathbb EN_i^4\le K\sigma_i^4,\qquad \mathbb EV_i^4\le K\sigma_i^4.
\]
Then
\[
\min\left\{\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^b(W_i+V_i+N_i)\ge0\right),
\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^b(W_i+V_i+N_i)\le0\right)\right\}
\ge\frac1{196608K}.
\]
If \(\sum_i\sigma_i^2=0\), both probabilities are at least \(1/2\). For a supplied \(K\ge0\), replacing \(K\) in the displayed sum bound by \(\max\{1,K\}\) is valid.

## Proof

First suppose \(\sigma=0\). The second moment of \(N\) and the fourth-moment bound on \(V\) imply \(N=V=0\) almost surely. The measure-preserving involution makes the law of \(W\) symmetric. Consequently
\(\Pr(T\ge0)=\Pr(T\le0)=(1+\Pr(W=0))/2\ge1/2\).

Now suppose \(\sigma>0\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\sigma^4=(\mathbb EN^2)^2\le\mathbb EN^4\), so \(K\ge1\) is necessary. Put \(R:=V+N\), \(L:=16\sqrt K\,\sigma\), \(A:=\{|W|\le L\}\), \(q:=\Pr(A^c)\), and \(\delta:=1/(1024K)\). The pointwise inequality \((a+b)^4\le8(a^4+b^4)\) for real \(a,b\) gives \(\mathbb ER^4\le16K\sigma^4\). Hence
\[
\Pr(|R|>L)\le\frac{16K\sigma^4}{L^4}
=\frac1{4096K}=\frac\delta4.
\]

If \(q\ge\delta\), symmetry gives \(\Pr(W>L)=\Pr(W<-L)=q/2\). On \(\{W>L,|R|\le L\}\), one has \(T>0\), and on \(\{W<-L,|R|\le L\}\), one has \(T<0\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(T\ge0)&\ge\Pr(W>L)-\Pr(|R|>L)\ge\frac\delta4,\\
\Pr(T\le0)&\ge\Pr(W<-L)-\Pr(|R|>L)\ge\frac\delta4.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is stronger than the claimed bound.

It remains to consider \(q<\delta\). All subsequent moments are restricted to \(A\), where \(W\) is bounded, so no global moment of \(W\) is used. Define \(a_2:=\mathbb E[W^2\mathbf1_A]\), \(M_2:=\mathbb E[T^2\mathbf1_A]\), \(M_4:=\mathbb E[T^4\mathbf1_A]\), \(M_1:=\mathbb E[|T|\mathbf1_A]\), and \(m:=\mathbb E[T\mathbf1_A]\). These quantities are finite.

The event \(A\) is invariant under the latent involution. Its odd integrable functions \(W\mathbf1_A\) and \(WV\mathbf1_A\) therefore have expectation zero. Conditional centering gives
\(\mathbb E[N\mathbf1_A]=\mathbb E[WN\mathbf1_A]=\mathbb E[VN\mathbf1_A]=0\).
The last product is integrable by Cauchy--Schwarz, and its latent coefficient is measurable, so conditional centering applies. Thus
\[
M_2=a_2+\mathbb E[V^2\mathbf1_A]+\mathbb E[N^2\mathbf1_A].
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and \(q<\delta\) bound the lost noise variance by
\[
\mathbb E[N^2\mathbf1_{A^c}]
\le(\mathbb EN^4)^{1/2}q^{1/2}
\le\sqrt{K\delta}\,\sigma^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{32}.
\]
Consequently \(M_2\ge a_2+31\sigma^2/32\ge a_2+\sigma^2/2>0\).

Since \(\mathbb EV=0\), the restricted mean is \(m=\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_A]=-\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_{A^c}]\). Hölder's inequality gives
\[
|m|\le(\mathbb EV^4)^{1/4}q^{3/4}
\le K^{1/4}\sigma\delta^{3/4}
=\frac{\sigma}{128\sqrt{2K}}.
\]

The pointwise inequality \((a+b+c)^4\le27(a^4+b^4+c^4)\) and \(W^4\mathbf1_A\le L^2W^2\mathbf1_A\) give
\[
M_4\le27\bigl(256K\sigma^2a_2+2K\sigma^4\bigr).
\]
The lower bound on \(M_2\), together with
\((a_2+\sigma^2/2)^2-2\sigma^2a_2=(a_2-\sigma^2/2)^2\ge0\), implies \(\sigma^2a_2\le M_2^2/2\). It also implies \(\sigma^4\le4M_2^2\). Hence
\[
M_4\le27(128K+8K)M_2^2
=3672K M_2^2\le4096K M_2^2.
\]
In particular \(M_4>0\), since \(M_2>0\).

Hölder's inequality on the restricted measure \(\mathbf1_A\,d\Pr\), with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), gives \(M_2\le M_1^{2/3}M_4^{1/3}\). It follows that
\[
M_1\ge\frac{M_2^{3/2}}{\sqrt{M_4}}
\ge\frac{\sqrt{M_2}}{64\sqrt K}
\ge\frac{\sigma}{64\sqrt{2K}}
\ge2|m|.
\]
For \(T_+:=\max\{T,0\}\) and \(T_-:=\max\{-T,0\}\), the identities \(T_++T_-=|T|\) and \(T_+-T_-=T\) yield
\(\mathbb E[T_+\mathbf1_A]=(M_1+m)/2\ge M_1/4\) and
\(\mathbb E[T_-\mathbf1_A]=(M_1-m)/2\ge M_1/4\).
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives, for either sign,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(T\ge0,A)&\ge\frac{M_1^2}{16M_2}
\ge\frac{M_2^2}{16M_4}\ge\frac1{65536K},\\
\Pr(T\le0,A)&\ge\frac{M_1^2}{16M_2}
\ge\frac{M_2^2}{16M_4}\ge\frac1{65536K}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking larger unrestricted events proves the scalar claim. The cutoff uses the closed event \(A=\{|W|\le L\}\) and its strict complement throughout. Atoms at \(\pm L\) belong to \(A\), and symmetry pairs the strict tails exactly, so no atom-splitting or non-atomicity hypothesis is needed.

For the sum extension, define explicitly \(W_\Sigma:=\sum_iW_i\), \(V_\Sigma:=\sum_iV_i\), \(N_\Sigma:=\sum_iN_i\), and \(\sigma_\Sigma^2:=\sum_i\sigma_i^2\). On the product latent law, apply every row involution simultaneously. This is a measure-preserving involution, under which \(W_\Sigma\) is odd and \(V_\Sigma\) is even. Also \(\mathbb EV_\Sigma=0\).

Let \(\mathcal G:=\sigma(Y_1,\ldots,Y_b)\). Independence of the full row experiments and row conditional centering imply \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid\mathcal G]=0\). Indeed, for bounded measurable functions \(h_j\),
\[
\mathbb E\left[N_i\prod_jh_j(Y_j)\right]
=\mathbb E[N_ih_i(Y_i)]\prod_{j\ne i}\mathbb E[h_j(Y_j)]=0.
\]
Testing first against products of latent indicator functions and then using the monotone class theorem extends this identity to all bounded \(\mathcal G\)-measurable tests. Thus \(\mathbb E[N_\Sigma\mid\mathcal G]=0\). In particular each \(N_i\) is centered. Independence then gives \(\mathbb EN_\Sigma^2=\sigma_\Sigma^2\).

For independent centered real variables, the fourth-power expansion consists of the individual fourth moments and the terms with two squared factors. Every other mixed term has an independent centered factor to the first power. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EN_\Sigma^4
&=\sum_i\mathbb EN_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2\\
&\le K\sum_i\sigma_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2
\le3K\sigma_\Sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final inequality uses \(K\ge1\): the diagonal coefficient \(K\) is at most \(3K\), and the cross coefficient \(6\) is at most \(6K\). Put \(v_i:=\mathbb EV_i^2\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(v_i\le\sqrt K\,\sigma_i^2\). The centered, independent variables \(V_i\) therefore satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EV_\Sigma^4
&=\sum_i\mathbb EV_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j\\
&\le K\sum_i\sigma_i^4+6K\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2
\le3K\sigma_\Sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the sum representation satisfies the scalar hypotheses with parameter \(3K\), and one application of the scalar theorem gives \(1/(65536\cdot3K)=1/(196608K)\) for both caps. If \(\sigma_\Sigma=0\), every row noise and drift vanishes almost surely, and symmetry of \(W_\Sigma\) gives the stronger bound \(1/2\). Finally, if a supplied constant \(K<1\) were used and some \(\sigma_i>0\), that row would contradict \((\mathbb EN_i^2)^2\le\mathbb EN_i^4\le K\sigma_i^4\). Hence the stated replacements by \(\max\{1,K\}\) include all supplied nonnegative constants.
