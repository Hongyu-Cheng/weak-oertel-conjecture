## Statement

Let $\rho$ be a finite signed measure on $[0,1]$ with $\int d\rho=\int s\,d\rho(s)=0$. Put $G(s,t)=\min((1-t)s,t(1-s))$ and $K_\rho(t)=\int G(s,t)\,d\rho(s)$. The condition $\int v\,d\rho\ge0$ for every continuous concave $v$ is equivalent to $K_\rho(t)\ge0$ for every $t\in[0,1]$. If $\rho$ has piecewise constant density, $K_\rho$ has zero endpoints, is piecewise quadratic, and satisfies $-K_\rho''=\rho$ as distributions in the open interval. Then positivity can be checked at breakpoints and the interior stationary points of the quadratic pieces.

## Proof

Each $G(\cdot,t)$ is a continuous concave test, giving necessity. For a continuous concave $v$, its linear interpolants over partitions of vanishing mesh are concave and converge uniformly by uniform continuity. Each interpolant equals its endpoint affine interpolant plus a sum of $G(\cdot,t)$ over interior knots, each multiplied by the nonnegative drop in slope there. This identity follows from the zero endpoint values and the unit drop of the derivative of $G(\cdot,t)$ at $t$. The affine moments are zero and all Green costs are nonnegative, so each interpolant has nonnegative integral. Uniform convergence and finite total variation yield sufficiency.

The endpoint values of $G$ vanish. As a function of $t$, its negative second distributional derivative on the open interval is the unit atom at $s$ for interior $s$. Integrating this identity against the density gives $-K_\rho''=\rho$. A piecewise constant density gives a quadratic on each subinterval with continuous boundary values, so checking the stated points suffices.
