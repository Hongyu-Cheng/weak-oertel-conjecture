## Statement

Let $D\ge2$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^D$ be a convex body of volume $V$, and let $D+1$ closed halfspaces $H_i$ with nonzero normals cover $K$. Write $A_i=|K\cap H_i|$, $\gamma_D=(D/(D+1))^D$ and $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For $\alpha>0$,
$$
\sum_i(A_i-\alpha)_+ + \frac{\alpha}{\gamma_D}=V
$$
if and only if $K$ is a simplex, the $H_i$ are its $D+1$ distinct vertex halfspaces through its centroid and parallel to the opposite facets, and $\alpha=A_i=\gamma_D V$ for every $i$.

## Proof

For sufficiency, the halfspace at vertex $i$ is given by the barycentric inequality $\lambda_i\ge1/(D+1)$. The halfspaces cover because the barycentric coordinates sum to one. Each cap is a copy of the simplex scaled by $D/(D+1)$ about its vertex, so its volume is $\gamma_D V$.

Suppose there is equality. Write $H_i=\{x:u_i\cdot x\ge b_i\}$ and use the distal trimming construction of ac7ba1e1e0ebc955. For $A_i>\alpha$ choose $t_i>b_i$ with $|K\cap\{u_i\cdot x>t_i\}|=A_i-\alpha$. This follows from continuity of cap volumes, nullity of hyperplanes, and the vanishing mass above the top support value. Set
$$
L=K\cap\bigcap_{i:A_i>\alpha}\{x:u_i\cdot x\le t_i\}.
$$
The removed regions have total mass, counted with multiplicity, equal to $\sum_i(A_i-\alpha)_+$. Hence $|L|\ge\alpha/\gamma_D>0$. Every original halfspace has mass at most $\alpha$ in $L$. Since those halfspaces cover $L$, its maximal normalized depth is at most $\alpha/|L|\le\gamma_D$. The ordinary maximum-depth theorem 03bc771bddd39a8a forces equality and shows that $L$ is a simplex. In particular $|L|=\alpha/\gamma_D$ and the removed regions have pairwise intersections of volume zero.

Let $y$ be the centroid of $L$. If an original halfspace contains $y$, its parallel through-$y$ subcap has mass at least $\gamma_D|L|=\alpha$ by the ordinary centroid bound in 03bc771bddd39a8a. If the original halfspace contained $y$ in its interior, the slab between the two parallel boundaries would have positive volume in a small ball in $L$ about $y$, contradicting its mass bound. Thus such a halfspace passes through $y$ and attains centroid equality. The single-cap equality characterization in 03bc771bddd39a8a makes it a vertex halfspace of the simplex $L$, on the side containing that vertex.

The other original halfspaces miss a neighborhood of $y$. If the halfspaces containing $y$ omitted the one corresponding to a vertex $v_j$ of $L$, then $y+\varepsilon(v_j-y)$ for small $\varepsilon>0$ would lie in $L$ and fail every selected vertex inequality $\lambda_i\ge1/(D+1)$, since $\lambda_i(v_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$. It would also miss the halfspaces not containing $y$. Coverage therefore forces all $D+1$ distinct vertex halfspaces, which exhaust the original family.

Suppose some distal region for index $i$ had positive volume. Its pairwise null intersections with the other removed regions, together with the nullity of all cutting planes and $\partial K$, give a point $x\in\operatorname{int}K$ with $u_i\cdot x>t_i$ and all the other retained inequalities strict. At $y$ all retained inequalities are strict, since their thresholds exceed the corresponding original thresholds through $y$. The segment from $y$ to $x$ crosses $u_i\cdot x=t_i$ in $\operatorname{int}K$ and strictly inside all other retained inequalities. Consequently $L\cap\{u_i\cdot x=t_i\}$ contains a relatively open patch of that hyperplane. The plane is a support plane of $L$. Its outward normal is the canonical vertex-cap normal, which exposes only one vertex of $L$. This is impossible since $D-1\ge1$.

Thus $L=K$. All the original halfspaces are its canonical centroid vertex halfspaces, and equality gives $\alpha=\gamma_D V=A_i$.
