## Statement

Write \(C_{\mathrm{occ}}(M,U)\) for the finite nonnegative constant called \(C(M,T)\) in accepted fact 31c9b0b9219d18d9, with its score-bound parameter \(T\) set to \(U\). Define the absolute constants
$$
T_0=\log4096,\qquad C_{\mathrm{lab}}=1024,\qquad
C_0=C_{\mathrm{occ}}\left(2,\frac32T_0\right),\qquad
c=\frac14\exp\left(-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2-C_0\right)>0.
\tag{1}
$$
Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For each \(i\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, with positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) for every \(x\in A_i\). Assume every coordinatewise parity class contains at most two atoms of \(A_i\). For every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\), require
$$
j=\frac{x+y}{2}\in A_i,\qquad
w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{2}
$$
If a factor has any unequal-weight same-parity pair, assume there is a number \(T_i>0\) such that every such pair satisfies
$$
T_i\le\left|\frac12\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\le\frac32T_i.
\tag{3}
$$
Factors with no unequal-weight same-parity pairs are allowed. If all nonzero scores in a factor equal one magnitude, (3) holds by taking \(T_i\) to be that magnitude. No restriction is imposed on the number of weight values, the number of pairs sharing a midpoint, or the number of midpoint centers.

Retain the original normalizers and probability laws, and the occupied parity counts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&
p_{i,x}&=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&
N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i,&
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}&=\frac{\prod_iw_{i,x_i}}{\prod_iZ_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
All these laws are extended by zero off their supports. With the actual ambient closed-halfspace convention of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, for each supported \(\mathbf j\) and \(r\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\), put
$$
\tau_P(\mathbf j)=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,
\qquad
M_r(P)=\sum_{\mathbf j}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r.
\tag{5}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-3}.
\tag{6}
$$
The coefficient is paid once for the entire product and is independent of all dimensions, weights, band parameters, occupied counts, and the number of factors.

More precisely, the following scalar budgets underlie (6). Suppress the factor index and define the full reflected quantities and the same-weight selections by
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},&
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},&
\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j,\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace x\in R_j:w_x=w_j\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b_{\mathrm{equal}}&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2,\\
\widehat\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j),&
E&=K^2b_{\mathrm{equal}},&F&=K^2b(p),\qquad G=F-E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Here \(j\in A\), and \(\kappa_j(x)\) and \(Y_j(x)\) are defined for \(x\in R_j\). These full quantities are exactly those of 445a9e668ee1a705. The hats and \(E,F,G\) are exactly those of 98948b8e373afa7e, retaining its occupied count \(K\). Every row sum includes both orientations of each noncentral pair and the central atom once. One always has \(E\ge1/4\) and \(F\ge1\). If \(E<1\), there is an unequal-weight pair, so a band parameter \(T>0\) in (3) is available. If also \(T\ge T_0\), then \(F>1\), and, with \(d=-\log E>0\) and \(g=\log F>0\),
$$
d\le1024e^{-T}g,\qquad \nu\le3T^2g.
\tag{8}
$$
The same-weight selection has logarithmic scaled energy \(-d\) and score price zero; the full-row selection has logarithmic scaled energy \(g\) and score price \(\nu\). Thus (8) supplies one selectable label for the entire factor, including every center.

## Proof

The common-endpoint hypothesis of 98948b8e373afa7e is not assumed. We prove the scalar estimates for the varying scores allowed by (3), then reproduce the binning and selected-row conversion with explicit notation maps.

Fix one factor. If \(x\in R_j\), its reflected endpoint \(2j-x\) has the same coordinatewise parity as \(x\). Equation (2), including the trivial central case directly, gives \(p_xp_{2j-x}=p_j^2\). Every geometric row summand is therefore \(p_j\), and the full geometric row law is uniform. Moreover, \(w_x=w_j\) implies \(w_{2j-x}=w_j\), and equal endpoint weights imply that both equal the positive midpoint weight. Thus \(\widehat R_j\) consists exactly of the equal-weight reflection orbits, is symmetric about \(j\), contains \(j\), and has score zero throughout. In particular \(\widehat H_p(j)\ge p_j>0\).

Occupancy at most two gives \(|A|\le2K\). Hölder's inequality, in the form \((\sum_jp_j)^3\le |A|^2\sum_jp_j^3\), yields
$$
E\ge K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3
\ge\frac{K^2}{|A|^2}\ge\frac14.
\tag{9}
$$
For each occupied parity class \(\eta\), let \(\mu_\eta\) be its original probability mass and put \(R_\eta=K\mu_\eta\). The map \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) bijects full oriented row entries with ordered same-parity pairs. Its inverse midpoint lies in \(A\) by (2). The midpoint product makes each contribution \(p_j\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\) equal to \(p_xp_{2j-x}\). Consequently, with \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\),
$$
\chi(p)=\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2\ge\frac1K,
\qquad b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2,
\qquad F\ge1.
\tag{10}
$$
The diagonal pairs occur once. If no unequal-weight same-parity pair exists, every row equals its same-weight selection, so \(E=F\ge1\) and every score vanishes.

Suppose now \(E<1\), and fix a band parameter \(T\) from (3). Let \(\mathscr M\) be the set of occupied parity classes containing an unequal-weight pair. Each such class consists exactly of its two endpoints, because its occupancy is at most two. For \(\eta\in\mathscr M\), write these endpoints as \(x_\eta,y_\eta\), their midpoint as \(j_\eta\), and their score magnitude as
\(s_\eta=\frac12|\log(p_{x_\eta}/p_{y_\eta})|\in[T,3T/2]\).
Define \(\rho_j=Kp_j\) for every \(j\in A\), and \(\rho_\eta=\rho_{j_\eta}\) for \(\eta\in\mathscr M\). Unlike the single common-midpoint probability \(\rho\) in 98948b8e373afa7e, these scaled probabilities can vary. The variable \(\rho_j\) equals the variable \(t_j\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9. The midpoint product and score give the exact class-mass identity
$$
R_\eta=2\rho_\eta\cosh s_\eta
\qquad(\eta\in\mathscr M).
\tag{11}
$$
Use the unnormalized occupied parity defect and the deleted first-moment loss
$$
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}=\sum_{\eta\text{ occupied}}(R_\eta-1)^2
=K^2\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2-K,
\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}=2\sum_{\eta\in\mathscr M}\rho_\eta^2.
\tag{12}
$$
Thus \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) is precisely the unnormalized defect of 98948b8e373afa7e; the normalized \(\mathcal D\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9 is \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\). Removing the two orientations of each unequal pair from (10) gives the exact identity
$$
K\widehat\chi(p)
=1+\frac{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}}{K},
\qquad E\ge\bigl(K\widehat\chi(p)\bigr)^2.
\tag{13}
$$
Since \(E<1\) and \(\widehat\chi(p)>0\), one has \(0<K\widehat\chi(p)<1\), hence \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\).

For the remainder of the scalar argument assume \(T\ge T_0\). Call a class \(\eta\in\mathscr M\) balanced when \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). Let \(\mathscr B\) be the set of these balanced mixed source classes and define
\(\mathcal B_2=\sum_{\eta\in\mathscr B}\rho_\eta^2\).
For any unbalanced mixed class, \((R_\eta-1)^2\ge R_\eta^2/4\). Also (11) and \(s_\eta\ge T\) imply
$$
2\rho_\eta^2
=\frac{R_\eta^2}{2\cosh^2s_\eta}
\le2e^{-2T}R_\eta^2.
\tag{14}
$$
Separating balanced and unbalanced mixed classes therefore gives
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}
\le2\mathcal B_2+8e^{-2T}\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{15}
$$
Here the sum of defects over unbalanced mixed classes is bounded by the defect of all occupied classes, each counted once. Since \(T\ge T_0\ge\log4\), one has \(1-8e^{-2T}\ge1/2\). Combining (15) with \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\) yields
$$
\mathcal B_2>0,\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<4\mathcal B_2,\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\le2\mathcal B_2.
\tag{16}
$$
To verify the first assertion, if \(\mathcal B_2=0\), (15) and the strict inequality would force \((1-8e^{-2T})\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<0\), which is impossible.

Put \(a=K\widehat\chi(p)\) only in the following deficit calculation. Equations (9), (13), and (16), with \(0<a<1\), give
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-E&\le1-a^2\le2(1-a)
=\frac{2(\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})}K
\le\frac{4\mathcal B_2}{K},\\
d=-\log E&\le\frac{1-E}{E}\le4(1-E)
\le\frac{16\mathcal B_2}{K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
For a balanced source, (11) and \(s_\eta\in[T,3T/2]\) give
$$
\frac14e^{-3T/2}\le\rho_\eta\le2e^{-T}.
\tag{18}
$$
Indeed \(2\cosh s\le2e^s\) and \(2\cosh s\ge e^s\). There are at most \(K\) mixed classes, so \(\mathcal B_2\le4e^{-2T}K\). Thus (17) also gives \(d\le64e^{-2T}\).

We next count the target parity classes containing balanced-pair midpoints. Let
\(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}=\lbrace j_\eta:\eta\in\mathscr B\rbrace\), let \(N_{\mathrm{tar}}=|\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}|\), and let \(C_{\mathrm{tar}}\) be the number of distinct occupied parity classes meeting \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}\). For any \(j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}\), its scaled atom probability \(\rho_j\) satisfies (18). If its own parity class is a singleton or has two equal-weight atoms, its scaled class mass is at most \(2\rho_j\le4e^{-T}\). Otherwise its unique companion has scaled probability \(\rho_je^{2s'}\) or \(\rho_je^{-2s'}\), for some \(s'\in[T,3T/2]\) by (3). In the latter case the class mass is again at most \(2\rho_j\le4e^{-T}\); in the former it is at least
\(\rho_je^{2T}\ge\frac14e^{T/2}\).
At \(T\ge T_0=\log4096\), the first bound is at most \(1/1024<1/2\), and the second is at least \(16>2\). Hence every target class is unbalanced and contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\). It follows that
$$
C_{\mathrm{tar}}\le4\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<16\mathcal B_2,
\qquad
1\le N_{\mathrm{tar}}\le2C_{\mathrm{tar}}<32\mathcal B_2,
\qquad
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}}\rho_j
\le64e^{-T}\mathcal B_2.
\tag{19}
$$
The last sum is over distinct centers and uses their upper bound in (18). The first inequality in (19) counts the defect of each distinct target class once. A target class may also be an unbalanced mixed source class in (15); the two sub-sums of \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) have not been added, and no disjointness between those roles is assumed. In particular no midpoint multiplicity has been charged to a target-class defect.

For each \(j\in A\), let \(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}=|\widehat R_j|\), let \(r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\) count all mixed source classes with midpoint \(j\), and let \(r_{\mathrm{bal},j}\) count those in \(\mathscr B\). Then \(|R_j|=h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\), with \(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}\ge1\) and \(r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\ge r_{\mathrm{bal},j}\). The exact row summands already established give
$$
\begin{aligned}
G
&=\frac1K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
\left((h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j})^2-h_{\mathrm{eq},j}^2\right)\\
&\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}}
r_{\mathrm{bal},j}^{\,2}\rho_j^3,
\qquad
\mathcal B_2=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}}r_{\mathrm{bal},j}\rho_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to the vectors with entries \(r_{\mathrm{bal},j}\rho_j^{3/2}\) and \(\rho_j^{1/2}\), indexed by the distinct centers. It gives
$$
\mathcal B_2^2
\le\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}}r_{\mathrm{bal},j}^{\,2}\rho_j^3\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{bal}}}\rho_j\right).
\tag{21}
$$
Both \(\mathcal B_2\) and the second factor are positive. Equations (19)--(21) imply
$$
G\ge\frac{e^T\mathcal B_2}{16K}>0,
\qquad
d\le256e^{-T}G\le\frac{G}{16}.
\tag{22}
$$
The last inequality uses \(e^{-T}\le1/4096\). This is the scalar gain estimate without a common midpoint weight or a bound on the number of distinct centers.

If \(0<G\le1\), then \(1-E\le d\le G/16\), so \(F=E+G\ge1+G/2>1\). Integrating \(1/(1+u)\ge1/2\) for \(0\le u\le G/2\) gives \(g=\log F\ge G/4\). Thus \(d\le1024e^{-T}g\). If \(G\ge1\), then \(E\ge1/4\) gives \(F\ge5/4\), hence \(g\ge\log(5/4)\ge1/5\). The bound following (18) then gives
\(d\le64e^{-2T}\le320e^{-2T}g\le1024e^{-T}g\).
This proves \(F>1\) and the first inequality of (8) in every case.

Uniformity of the full row law and the scores \(\pm s_\eta\) of each mixed pair give the exact numerator
$$
F\nu=\frac2K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j})
\sum_{\substack{\eta\in\mathscr M\\j_\eta=j}}s_\eta^2.
\tag{23}
$$
Every score in this sum is at most \(3T/2\). For \(r\ge0\) and \(h\ge1\), the rowwise comparison
\(2r(h+2r)\le(h+2r)^2-h^2\)
therefore gives \(F\nu\le\frac94T^2G\) by (20). Since
\(G/F=1-E/F\le\log(F/E)=g+d\), and \(1024e^{-T}\le1/4\),
$$
\nu\le\frac94T^2(g+d)
\le\frac{45}{16}T^2g\le3T^2g.
\tag{24}
$$
This proves (8), and in particular both label bounds
\(d\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}e^{-T}g\) and \(\nu\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}T^2g\).

For the remaining factors, the bounded-score theorem 31c9b0b9219d18d9 applies whenever \(E<1\) and \(T<T_0\). Its occupancy parameter is \(M=2\), its fixed score bound is \(3T_0/2\), and every nonzero score is at most \(3T/2<3T_0/2\). Midpoint closure and midpoint equality are exactly (2); normalization by the original \(Z\) changes neither equality nor scores. Its occupied count is this \(K\), its full energy \(A_{\mathcal J}\) is this \(F\), and its full \(H_p,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu\) agree with (7). It yields
$$
F\ge1,\qquad \nu\le C_0\log F.
\tag{25}
$$
No conclusion of that fact is being applied to a selected row.

We now choose rows for the entire product. If \(E_i\ge1\), choose all the same-weight rows of factor \(i\). This includes all factors without unequal-weight pairs. If \(E_i<1\) and \(T_i<T_0\), choose all its full rows and use (25). The remaining factors have \(E_i<1\) and \(T_i\ge T_0\), and receive the labels \((d_i,g_i,T_i)\) proved in (8). Retaining a label chooses every full row of that factor; discarding it chooses every same-weight row.

This selection uses the binning argument of 98948b8e373afa7e with its proof capacities \(k_i\) all set to the present occupied counts \(K_i\), and its label constant replaced by \(C_{\mathrm{lab}}\). Here \(T_i\) is the lower endpoint of the score band, rather than the exact common score used there. The required label inequalities have been proved above for this new meaning; the common-endpoint scalar claim of that fact is not invoked. We give the binning calculation explicitly.

For every integer \(m\ge0\), let \(I_m\) contain the remaining labels with \(T_i\in[m,m+1)\), and set \(G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i\). Retain the entire bin exactly when \(G_m\ge4C_{\mathrm{lab}}(m+1)^2\). There are only finitely many nonempty bins. For a retained bin define \(W_m=C_{\mathrm{lab}}(m+1)^2G_m\). Its total price is at most \(W_m\), and its retention inequality implies \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\). For a discarded bin its total logarithmic deficit is at most
$$
\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i
\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}e^{-m}G_m
<4C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2.
\tag{26}
$$
An empty bin contributes zero. Differentiating the geometric series gives
\(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\) for \(0\le r<1\). Since \(e^{-1}<1/2\), the sum of (26) over all discarded bins is at most \(48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2\).

Use stars for the rows chosen by this procedure and retain hats for (7). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&
q_{i,j}^*&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2}{b_i^*},\\
V_{i,j}^*&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&
\nu_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}^*V_{i,j}^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The score \(Y_{i,j}\) is exactly the full-row score in (7), restricted to the chosen row. The stars have the same meaning as the selected-row stars in 98948b8e373afa7e. A full selection gives the full quantities; a same-weight selection gives \(b_i^*=b_{\mathrm{equal},i}\) and \(\nu_i^*=0\). Every row contains its positive-mass central atom, so all these auxiliary probabilities are defined, and every original center remains in every sum over \(A_i\).

Let \(B_{\log}\) be the sum of \(\log F_i\) over the factors using (25), with empty sum zero. These terms are nonnegative, and their total price is at most \(C_0B_{\log}\). The other initially selected same-weight factors have nonnegative logarithmic scaled energy and zero price. Hence, with \(S_g=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*)\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g&\ge B_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2,\\
\sum_i\nu_i^*&\le C_0B_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$
Subadditivity of the square root and \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}
&\ge B_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_0B_{\log}}-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2\\
&\ge-C_0-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
The last inequality is \((\sqrt{B_{\log}}-\sqrt{C_0})^2\ge0\). All displayed sums are finite except the explicitly bounded numerical series, so the calculation includes absent label bins or an empty bounded-score group.

We finally derive the original-product moments for the selected rows, using the reflection conventions of 445a9e668ee1a705 rather than applying a full-row theorem to a selection. Every \(R_{i,j}^*\) is symmetric about \(j\) and contains \(j\). Reflection preserves \(\kappa_{i,j}^*\) and negates \(Y_{i,j}\), so the latter has mean zero under that row law. Direct substitution gives, for every \(x\in R_{i,j}^*\),
$$
p_{i,x}=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}
=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},
\qquad
b_i^*=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{30}
$$
The normalizer in this expression is the full original \(Z_i\).

For a supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$
The geometric row mass on this selected product row factors as \(H_P^*\). Independence and the zero score means give
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}=0\) and
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\).
Multiplying (30) gives the exact likelihood identity
\(P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}\), with the original product probability \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,x_i}/\prod_iZ_i\).

Every closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflected pair \(X,2\mathbf j-X\): otherwise its defining affine inequality would strictly exclude both endpoints and therefore their midpoint. It also contains the central atom \(\mathbf j\). Simultaneous reflection preserves \(R_{\mathbf j}^*\) and its geometric law and negates \(Y_{\mathbf j}\). Summing the smaller original mass in each noncentral orbit and half the central mass, then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral pair twice before its factor \(1/2\), and counts the central atom once before that factor. This uses only half the available central mass and includes halfspaces whose boundaries contain supported atoms, singleton rows, and lower-dimensional supports.

Let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\). The exact identities
\(P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2=(\prod_i b_i^*)\prod_iq_{i,j_i}^*\) and
\(\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*\)
follow from (27) and (31). Squaring (32), summing under the original law \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under the auxiliary center law give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)
\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
$$
where the last step is (1) and (29). No original probability or center atom has been removed or renormalized. Finally, Jensen under the same original law gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\ge c^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}\).
