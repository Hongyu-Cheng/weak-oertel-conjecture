## Statement

Use finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$, full-dimensional compact convex $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, their source $S$ and summed $d$-volume $\mu$, and $M=\mu(S)$. Let $L_i=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$ with $\lVert u_i\rVert=1$ and $H_i=\{L_i\ge0\}$. The derivative of $\Psi=\sum_i\int_S (L_i)_+\,d\mu$ in these affine parameters is $\sum_i(P_i\cdot da_i+m_i\cdot du_i-c_i\,db_i)$, where $c_i=\mu(H_i)$ and $P_i,m_i$ are the raw $z,x$ first moments on $H_i$.

Fix $0<\tau<1$ and suppose a family minimizes $\Psi$ among all $N$-cap pointwise covers of $S$ with $\mu(H_i)\le\tau M$. If an offset exceeds $B_i=\max_S(a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x)$, it may be clipped to $B_i$. At the resulting minimizing family fix the normals and choose any optimal probability certificate $\lambda_z$ for each fiber's minimax margin. Put $U_z=\sum_i\lambda_{zi}u_i$ and $C_z=\min_{x\in K_z}\sum_i\lambda_{zi}L_i(z,x)\ge0$. Let $q_i$ be the smallest offset giving cap mass at most $\tau M$ with the same normals. There exist $\eta_z,\kappa_i\ge0$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_z C_z&=0,\qquad \kappa_i(b_i-q_i)=0,\qquad
c_i+\kappa_i=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi},\\
\sum_z\eta_z&=\sum_i(c_i+\kappa_i)\ge M,\qquad
\sum_i(c_i+\kappa_i)u_i=\sum_z\eta_z U_z.
\end{aligned}
$$
If $\kappa_i>0$ then $c_i=\tau M$.

## Proof

For any nonzero continuous normal, the cap plane has zero $d$-volume on every fiber. The hinge is differentiable away from this plane, with parameter derivative $\mathbf1_{H_i}(z,x)(z,x,-1)$. The pointwise remainder divided by the norm of the perturbation tends to zero off the plane and is bounded on the compact source by twice a common bound on $\lVert(z,x,-1)\rVert$. Dominated convergence proves the full derivative.

An offset beyond $B_i$ gives zero mass and zero hinge, and replacing it by $B_i$ changes neither because the plane still has zero mass. The replacement only enlarges the cover. Fix the normals after clipping. The cap mass is continuous and nonincreasing in its offset by the nullity of the planes. It equals $M$ below the minimum affine evaluation on $S$ and zero at $B_i$. Thus $q_i$ exists, $q_i<B_i$, the budget is equivalent to $b_i\ge q_i$, and the mass at $q_i$ is $\tau M$.

The sparse minimax certificate lemma gives the stated $\lambda_z$ and $C_z$. Keep these weights fixed as the offsets vary. The inequalities $C_z\ge0$ are then affine in the offsets and imply the original pointwise coverage because the maximum of the margins dominates their weighted sum. Therefore the clipped minimizer also minimizes the differentiable objective over the polyhedron cut out by the fixed certificates and $b_i\ge q_i$. At a minimum on a polyhedron the negative gradient is a nonnegative combination of the outward normals of the active inequalities. This follows by Farkas' alternative on feasible directions: if no such combination existed, a direction nonincreasing every active inequality would have strictly negative objective derivative and remain feasible for small positive steps. In the present case the gradient component is $-c_i$, the outward cover normals are $\lambda_z$, and the lower bounds have outward normals $-e_i$. This gives $c_i=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}-\kappa_i$ with the two complementary products. Summing components and summing with weights $u_i$ give the two aggregate identities. The actual cover gives $\sum_i c_i\ge M$, and the equality of the mass at $q_i$ gives the saturation statement.
