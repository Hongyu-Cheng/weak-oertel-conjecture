## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), and \(D_+(C)=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). Assume \(W>0\). Define \(\delta_C(z,q)\), for \(q\in C_z\), as the infimum of \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L)\) over all proper closed halfspaces \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(q\). For \(p\in S\), define \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces, and put \(h_{\max}(C)=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\).

Here \(w_z\) always denotes full fiber volume, which is the quantity called \(a_z\) in the centroid specialization of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78. When applying that fact's arbitrary-selector theorem, its symbol \(w_z\) will instead be instantiated by a selected weight \(\alpha_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\), as explicitly specified below. Its objects \(C_z,S,W,\delta_C,h_S,h_{\max}\) otherwise have exactly the definitions just given.

For each nonempty \(E\subseteq D_+(C)\) and points \(q_z\in C_z\), define \(\rho_E=\dim\operatorname{aff}E\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_E(q)&=\min_{s:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_z-s(z):z\in E\},\\
\mathfrak R(C)&=\sup_{\substack{\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D_+(C)\\q_z\in C_z\ (z\in E)}}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_E}}{r_E(q)+1}\sum_{z\in E}\delta_C(z,q_z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
No lower bound on the relative depths of the selected points is imposed. The residual-rank minimum is attained and satisfies
\[
r_E(q)=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{(z,q_z):z\in E\}-\rho_E
       =\operatorname{rank}[X\ Q]-\operatorname{rank}X,
\tag{2}
\]
where an affine coordinate isomorphism \(\vartheta:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R^{\rho_E}\) determines the rows \(X_z=(1,\vartheta(z)^{\mathsf T})\) and \(Q_z=q_z^{\mathsf T}\). The optimized value in (1) is unchanged by allowing arbitrary additional weights \(0\leq\alpha_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\), whether the ranks are computed on the initially chosen set or on the positive-weight support. Allowing nonempty fibers of zero volume also cannot increase it. Accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78 gives the valid consumer
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}\mathfrak R(C).
\tag{3}
\]

For every integer \(n\geq1\), set \(d=n\) and define
\[
C_n=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n:
             |x_i|/4\leq y_i\leq1\quad(1\leq i\leq n)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a compact full-dimensional rational convex polytope. In the remainder of the statement, \(C_z=(C_n)_z\), and \(w_z,W,S\) refer to \(C_n\). Its full nonempty integer support is \(\{z:C_z\ne\varnothing\}=\{-4,-3,\ldots,4\}^n\). For every point of that support, \(C_z=\prod_i[|z_i|/4,1]\). If at least one \(|z_i|=4\), the fiber is nonempty and has \(n\)-dimensional volume zero. All other integer fibers outside that support are empty. In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_n:=D_+(C_n)&=\{-3,-2,\ldots,3\}^n,\\
w_z&=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-|z_i|/4)\quad(z\in D_n),
& W&=4^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The fully optimized capacity and the single explicit point \(p_n=(0_n,(21/32)\mathbf1_n)\in S\) satisfy
\[
\frac{W}{2(n+1)}\leq\mathfrak R(C_n)\leq\frac{32768W}{n+1},
\qquad
h_S(p_n)\geq\frac{W}{72}.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, there is no absolute \(a>0\) such that \(\mathfrak R(C)\geq aW\) for every body with positive mixed-integer volume in the locked problem. The lower bound for \(h_S(p_n)\) is an actual mixed-depth bound, not an assertion that its depth equals \(W/72\).

## Proof

We first justify the residual-rank and weight conventions, including all zero-weight cases. The set \(D_+(C)\) is finite because \(C\) is bounded. Fix a nonempty \(E\), write \(N=|E|\) and \(\rho=\rho_E\), and form \(X,Q\) as in (2). The columns of \(X\) are independent, so \(\operatorname{rank}X=\rho+1\). Every affine map on \(\operatorname{aff}E\) is represented by a coefficient matrix \(B\), with residual matrix \(Q-XB\). Its row rank is the dimension of the residual span. Since \([X\ Q]\) and \([X\ Q-XB]\) have the same column span, every \(B\) satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}(Q-XB)\geq\operatorname{rank}[X\ Q]-\operatorname{rank}X\).

Choose \(B=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}Q\). Then every residual column is orthogonal to \(\operatorname{col}X\), and \(\operatorname{col}[X\ Q]\) is the direct sum of \(\operatorname{col}X\) and \(\operatorname{col}(Q-XB)\). Equality follows, proving attainment and the matrix identity in (2). Affine coordinates on \(\operatorname{aff}E\) preserve the affine dimension of the lifted graph, so \(\operatorname{rank}[X\ Q]=1+\dim\operatorname{aff}\{(z,q_z):z\in E\}\). This proves the other identity and also \(0\leq r_E(q)\leq\min\{d,N-\rho-1\}\). The argument includes \(\rho=0\), a singleton \(E\), and residual rank zero.

If \(F\subseteq E\) is nonempty, then \(\rho_F\leq\rho_E\) and \(r_F(q)\leq r_E(q)\): restrict a minimizing affine map for \(E\) to \(\operatorname{aff}F\). Thus removing indices of zero depth preserves the numerator in (1) and cannot decrease its score. If every selected depth is zero, that score is zero. For fixed \(E,q\), replacing depths by smaller nonnegative weights with the same denominator cannot increase the score. If instead the denominator is computed on \(J=\{z:\alpha_z>0\}\), a positive-total-weight score is at most the score in (1) for \(E=J\) and the same points, since \(\alpha_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\). Conversely, choosing the weights to equal the depths and discarding zero depths gives a score at least as large as any particular score in (1). These two comparisons prove equality of the optimized values for either weight convention. A nonempty zero-volume fiber has \(\delta_C(z,q)=0\) at every point, so its weight is zero and the same removal argument applies. A total weight of zero contributes only zero and does not require an affine hull of the empty positive support.

To obtain (3), fix \(E,q\) in (1) with a positive sum of depths and apply the arbitrary-selector conclusion of accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78 with its index set \(I=E\), its selected points equal to our \(q_z\), and its selected weights
\(w_z^{\mathrm{there}}=\alpha_z:=\delta_C(z,q_z)\).
Thus its \(T\) is \(\sum_{z\in E}\delta_C(z,q_z)\), its \(J\) is the subset of positive depths, its \(\rho\) is \(\rho_J\), and its \(r_q(J)\) is our \(r_J(q)\). The full fiber volumes remain our \(w_z\), which are its \(a_z\), rather than the selected weights. Its inequality (10) yields
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\sum_{z\in E}\delta_C(z,q_z)}{2^{\rho_J}(r_J(q)+1)}
 \geq
 \frac{\sum_{z\in E}\delta_C(z,q_z)}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E(q)+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]
The zero-sum case is automatic. Taking the supremum gives (3). Only this use of the accepted arbitrary-selector theorem is needed; all estimates for the family are proved below.

We now fix \(n\geq1\) and the body (4). Replace each absolute-value inequality by \(x_i\leq4y_i\) and \(-x_i\leq4y_i\). These and \(y_i\leq1\) are rational linear inequalities, imply \(0\leq y_i\leq1\) and \(|x_i|\leq4\), and are all strict at \((0_n,\tfrac12\mathbf1_n)\). Thus the body is compact, convex, and full-dimensional. At an integer vector \(z\), the fiber is nonempty precisely when every \(|z_i|\leq4\), with the intervals stated after (4). A coordinate with \(|z_i|=4\) fixes \(y_i=1\), so any such nonempty fiber has zero \(n\)-dimensional volume. All other nonempty integer fibers have positive interval lengths. The one-coordinate sum of positive lengths is exactly \(1+2(3/4+1/2+1/4)=4\), proving (5), including the contribution zero from every boundary fiber.

Normalized mixed volume on \(S\) is the law of independent coordinate blocks \((Z_i,Y_i)\). In one block,
\[
\mathbb P(Z=k)=\frac{4-|k|}{16}\quad(-3\leq k\leq3),
\qquad
Y\mid Z=k\sim\operatorname{Unif}[|k|/4,1].
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, before normalization the block measure is counting measure in the integer coordinate and Lebesgue measure on its interval, with total mass \(4\); the full body and measure are products of these blocks. Write \(\xi=Z/4\), \(R=|\xi|\), and \(g(t)=(1+|t|)/2\). The probability table for \(R\) is

| \(R\) | \(0\) | \(1/4\) | \(1/2\) | \(3/4\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| probability | \(1/4\) | \(3/8\) | \(1/4\) | \(1/8\) |

It gives the exact moments
\[
\mathbb ER=\frac5{16},\qquad
\mathbb ER^2=\frac5{32},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}R=\frac{15}{256}>\frac1{18}.
\tag{9}
\]
For example, the first two sums are \((3/8)(1/4)+(1/4)(1/2)+(1/8)(3/4)=5/16\) and \((3/8)(1/16)+(1/4)(1/4)+(1/8)(9/16)=5/32\). For \(t\in\mathbb R^n\), put \(\mathbf g(t)=(g(t_1),\ldots,g(t_n))\). The midpoint of \(C_z\), for \(z\in D_n\), is \(c_z=\mathbf g(z/4)\).

We prove a scalar Fourier estimate that is uniform in its affine coefficient. Let \(\phi(s,t)=\mathbb E\exp(i[s\xi-tg(\xi)])\). Then
\[
|\phi(s,t)|\leq e^{-t^2/288}
\quad\hbox{for every }s\in\mathbb R\hbox{ and }|t|\leq1.
\tag{10}
\]
To prove this, put \(\zeta(\xi)=\exp(i[s\xi-tg(\xi)])\) and \(m=\mathbb E\zeta=\phi(s,t)\). Since \(\xi\) and \(-\xi\) have the same law, \(\zeta(\xi)\zeta(-\xi)=e^{-it(1+R)}\). If \(m\ne0\), choose \(\omega=m/|m|\), so \(\mathbb E|\zeta-\omega|^2=2(1-|m|)\). The triangle inequality for unit complex factors, followed by the square inequality, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|\zeta(\xi)\zeta(-\xi)-\omega^2|^2
&\leq2\mathbb E|\zeta(\xi)-\omega|^2
     +2\mathbb E|\zeta(-\xi)-\omega|^2\\
&=8(1-|m|).
\end{aligned}
\]
For any unit-modulus random variable \(U\), the minimum of \(\mathbb E|U-v|^2\) over \(|v|=1\) is \(2(1-|\mathbb EU|)\). Applying this to \(e^{-it(1+R)}\) in the preceding inequality yields \(1-|m|\geq\tfrac14(1-|\chi(t)|)\), where \(\chi(t)=\mathbb Ee^{-itR}\). The same inequality holds when \(m=0\), since its right side is at most \(1/4\).

Let \(R'\) be an independent copy of \(R\). For \(|t|\leq1\), one has \(|t(R-R')|\leq1\). The elementary inequality \(1-\cos u\geq u^2/4\) on \([-1,1]\) and (9) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
1-|\chi(t)|^2
 &=\mathbb E[1-\cos(t(R-R'))]\\
 &\geq\frac{t^2}{4}\mathbb E(R-R')^2
 =\frac{t^2}{2}\operatorname{Var}R
 \geq\frac{t^2}{36}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For instance, the cosine inequality follows from \(\cos u\leq1-u^2/2+u^4/24\) for \(|u|\leq1\), by its alternating Taylor expansion. Since \(1+|\chi(t)|\leq2\), we obtain \(1-|\chi(t)|\geq t^2/72\), hence \(|m|\leq1-t^2/288\leq e^{-t^2/288}\). This proves (10) for every real \(s\).

Here is the Gaussian inequality used to pass from the scalar estimate to arbitrary projections. Let \(U\) be a Euclidean space of positive dimension \(k\), let \(u_i\in U\) be unit vectors, and let \(\ell_i>0\) satisfy \(\sum_i\ell_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}=I_U\). If \(G\) is standard Gaussian in \(U\), \(G_1\) is standard Gaussian on \(\mathbb R\), and the \(F_i:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) are bounded Borel functions, then
\[
\mathbb E\prod_i F_i(u_i^{\mathsf T}G)^{\ell_i}
 \leq\prod_i(\mathbb EF_i(G_1))^{\ell_i}.
\tag{11}
\]
We give a complete semigroup proof and the measurable limiting argument. Define
\(P_tF(v)=\int F(e^{-t}v+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}a)\,d\gamma_1(a)\) for \(t>0\), where \(\gamma_1\) denotes standard Gaussian measure. Gaussian addition shows that \(P_t\) preserves integration against \(\gamma_1\). Jensen's inequality then gives the contraction \(\|P_tF-P_tK\|_{L^1(\gamma_1)}\leq\|F-K\|_{L^1(\gamma_1)}\).

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and replace each \(F_i\) temporarily by \(F_i+\varepsilon\). Write \(f_i(t,v)=P_t(F_i+\varepsilon)(v)\) and \(g_i(t,v)=\log f_i(t,v)\). These functions are smooth for \(t>0\); differentiating the Gaussian kernel gives \(\partial_t f_i=\partial_v^2 f_i-v\partial_v f_i\). On every compact time interval contained in \((0,\infty)\), all spatial derivatives are bounded, since the input is bounded and derivatives of the kernel have finite Gaussian absolute moments. The positive lower bound \(f_i\geq\varepsilon\) gives the corresponding bounds for the spatial derivatives of \(g_i\). In particular,
\(\partial_tg_i=g_i''-vg_i'+(g_i')^2\), where primes denote derivatives in \(v\).

Let \(\mathcal L_U=\Delta-x\cdot\nabla\) and \(H_t(x)=\exp(\sum_i\ell_i g_i(t,u_i^{\mathsf T}x))\). Direct differentiation, using \(\|u_i\|=1\), gives
\[
(\partial_t-\mathcal L_U)H_t
 =H_t\left(\sum_i\ell_i(g_i')^2
       -\left\|\sum_i\ell_i g_i'u_i\right\|^2\right)\geq0.
\tag{12}
\]
All derivatives in this formula are evaluated at \((t,u_i^{\mathsf T}x)\). To check the final inequality, for any unit \(v\in U\), Cauchy-Schwarz and the frame identity give
\(\left|\sum_i\ell_i g_i'\langle u_i,v\rangle\right|^2
\leq\left(\sum_i\ell_i(g_i')^2\right)
\left(\sum_i\ell_i\langle u_i,v\rangle^2\right)
=\sum_i\ell_i(g_i')^2\).
Take the supremum over unit \(v\).

The function \(H_t\) and its first two spatial derivatives are bounded on the indicated compact time intervals. Its time derivative is bounded by a constant times \(1+\|x\|\). Thus differentiation under the Gaussian integral is valid, and Gaussian integration by parts gives \(\mathbb E\mathcal L_UH_t(G)=0\). It follows from (12) that \(\mathbb EH_t(G)\) is nondecreasing in \(t>0\).

As \(t\to\infty\), for every fixed \(v\), the Gaussian kernel with mean \(e^{-t}v\) and variance \(1-e^{-2t}\) converges in \(L^1\) to the standard Gaussian density. Indeed the densities converge pointwise and have equal integral one, and the identity \(\int|a-b|=2-2\int\min(a,b)\), followed by dominated convergence for the minimum, proves this \(L^1\) convergence. Consequently \(f_i(t,v)\to\mathbb E(F_i(G_1)+\varepsilon)\). Bounded convergence gives the limit of \(\mathbb EH_t\) as the product of these expectations raised to the powers \(\ell_i\).

As \(t\downarrow0\), one has \(P_tF\to F\) in \(L^1(\gamma_1)\) for every bounded Borel \(F\). Here are the needed approximation details. Continuous compactly supported functions are dense in this space: approximate a bounded Borel function by simple functions, approximate each involved Borel set from inside by a compact set and from outside by an open set under the finite regular Gaussian measure, and insert a continuous compactly supported cutoff between the compact set and a bounded open enlargement, first discarding a tail of arbitrarily small Gaussian measure. For a continuous compactly supported function, the explicit Gaussian formula gives pointwise convergence as \(t\downarrow0\), and bounded convergence gives \(L^1\) convergence. The contraction already proved transfers this convergence to the original bounded Borel function.

For fixed \(\varepsilon\), all values of \(f_i\) and \(F_i+\varepsilon\) lie in a compact positive interval. Each power function \(a\mapsto a^{\ell_i}\) is Lipschitz on that interval. A finite product difference is bounded by a constant times the sum of the individual differences. Since \(u_i^{\mathsf T}G\) has law \(\gamma_1\), the preceding \(L^1\) convergence therefore implies
\(\mathbb EH_t(G)\to\mathbb E\prod_i(F_i(u_i^{\mathsf T}G)+\varepsilon)^{\ell_i}\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Monotonicity between the two limits proves (11) for the functions with \(\varepsilon\) added. Finally let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); bounded convergence on both sides proves (11) for all bounded nonnegative Borel functions, including those with zeros or discontinuities. No external Gaussian inequality is assumed.

Set \(\eta=1-2^{-14}\). We next prove the following estimate simultaneously for every \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), \(b\in\mathbb R^n\), and orthogonal projection \(P\) on \(\mathbb R^n\) of rank \(k\):
\[
\mathbb E\exp\left(-2\left\|P\bigl(A\boldsymbol\xi+b-
                  \mathbf g(\boldsymbol\xi)\bigr)\right\|^2\right)
 \leq\eta^k.
\tag{13}
\]
Here the coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\xi\) are independent copies of \(\xi\) from (8). If \(k=0\), both sides are one. Suppose \(k>0\), let \(G\) be standard Gaussian in \(U=\operatorname{range}P\), and set \(T=2G\), independently of \(\boldsymbol\xi\). The Gaussian characteristic function gives \(\mathbb E_Te^{iT^{\mathsf T}v}=e^{-2\|Pv\|^2}\) for every \(v\in\mathbb R^n\). Fubini, the triangle inequality, and independence of the coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\xi\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\exp\left(-2\left\|P(A\boldsymbol\xi+b-
                                     \mathbf g(\boldsymbol\xi))\right\|^2\right)\\
&\quad=\mathbb E_T\left[e^{iT^{\mathsf T}b}
       \prod_{i=1}^n\phi((A^{\mathsf T}T)_i,T_i)\right]\\
&\quad\leq\mathbb E_T\prod_{i=1}^n
                    |\phi((A^{\mathsf T}T)_i,T_i)|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The first line is real and nonnegative, so the complex-valued equality is compatible with the displayed triangle inequality.

Define \(\psi(t)=e^{-t^2/288}\) for \(|t|\leq1\) and \(\psi(t)=1\) for \(|t|>1\). Equation (10), together with the trivial bound \(|\phi|\leq1\), bounds (14) by \(\mathbb E_T\prod_i\psi(T_i)\). For every \(i\) with \(\ell_i=P_{ii}>0\), put \(u_i=Pe_i/\sqrt{\ell_i}\). On \(U\) these satisfy
\[
\sum_i\ell_i u_i u_i^{\mathsf T}=I_U,
\qquad\sum_i\ell_i=k,
\qquad0<\ell_i\leq1,
\qquad T_i=2\sqrt{\ell_i}\,u_i^{\mathsf T}G.
\tag{15}
\]
These equalities follow from \(P=P^{\mathsf T}=P^2\): \(\|Pe_i\|^2=P_{ii}\), the sum of \(Pe_i(Pe_i)^{\mathsf T}\) is \(P\), and \(\operatorname{tr}P=k\). Coordinates with \(\ell_i=0\) have \(T_i=0\) identically and contribute the factor \(\psi(0)=1\).

Apply (11) to the bounded positive Borel functions \(F_i(v)=\psi(2\sqrt{\ell_i}v)^{1/\ell_i}\). For \(|v|\leq1/2\), one has \(|2\sqrt{\ell_i}v|\leq1\), so \(F_i(v)=e^{-v^2/72}\); everywhere \(F_i\leq1\). It follows that
\[
\mathbb EF_i(G_1)
 \leq1-\int_{1/4\leq|v|\leq1/2}(1-e^{-v^2/72})\,d\gamma_1(v)
 \leq\eta.
\tag{16}
\]
The constants in the last inequality can be checked without numerical integration. On this set, \(v^2/72\geq1/1152\) and \(v^2/72\leq1/288\), so \(1-e^{-v^2/72}\geq1/2304\), using \(1-e^{-a}\geq a/2\) for \(0\leq a\leq1/2\). The standard Gaussian density there is at least \(7/24\): \((2\pi)^{-1/2}>1/3\), since \(\pi<4\), and \(e^{-v^2/2}\geq e^{-1/8}\geq7/8\). The integration set has total length \(1/2\), hence its contribution is at least \(7/110592>1/16384=2^{-14}\). Equation (11) now bounds (14) by \(\prod_i(\mathbb EF_i(G_1))^{\ell_i}\leq\eta^{\sum_i\ell_i}=\eta^k\). This proves (13). The coefficient \((A^{\mathsf T}T)_i\) was arbitrary throughout, so no diagonal or coordinatewise restriction on \(A\) has been introduced.

We apply (13) to every subset and every collection of fiber points. Fix \(\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D_n\), choose arbitrary \(q_z\in C_z\), and abbreviate \(\rho=\rho_E\) and \(r=r_E(q)\). By attainment, choose an affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R^n\) whose residual span is a subspace \(V\) of dimension \(r\). Thus \(q_z-s(z)\in V\) for every \(z\in E\). Extend \(s\) to an affine map on \(\mathbb R^n\), by extending its linear part from the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}E\) to a linear complement. Write this extension as \(s(z)=A(z/4)+b\). This form is unrestricted, since division by four is an invertible change of base coordinates. Let \(P\) be orthogonal projection onto \(V^\perp\), so \(\operatorname{rank}P=n-r\).

For \(z\in E\), put \(u=P(s(z)-c_z)\). Because the residual \(q_z-s(z)\) is in \(V\), one has \(u=P(q_z-c_z)\), and therefore \(u\cdot(q_z-c_z)=\|u\|^2\). We claim
\[
\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)
 \leq w_z\exp(-2\|P(s(z)-c_z)\|^2).
\tag{17}
\]
When \(u=0\), this is the trivial bound \(\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)\leq w_z\). When \(u\ne0\), take the proper closed fiber halfspace \(L=\{y:u\cdot(y-c_z)\geq\|u\|^2\}\), whose boundary contains \(q_z\). For a uniform point \(Y\) of the positive-volume box \(C_z\), the centered coordinates are independent and their range lengths are at most one.

For completeness, a bounded centered random variable \(X\) of range length \(a\) satisfies \(\log\mathbb Ee^{\lambda X}\leq\lambda^2a^2/8\). Indeed, the second derivative of its log moment generating function is the variance under an exponential tilt, whose support has the same range length. Every variable on an interval of length \(a\) has variance at most \(a^2/4\), since its second moment around the interval midpoint is at most \(a^2/4\) and its variance is no larger. The log moment generating function and its first derivative are zero at \(\lambda=0\), so integration of this second-derivative bound proves the claim. Apply it to each \(u_i(Y_i-c_{z,i})\), whose range length is at most \(|u_i|\), and multiply the moment generating functions by independence. Markov's inequality with \(\lambda=4\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(Y\in L)
&\leq e^{-4\|u\|^2}\mathbb Ee^{4u\cdot(Y-c_z)}\\
&\leq e^{-4\|u\|^2+16\|u\|^2/8}
=e^{-2\|u\|^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplication by \(w_z\) proves (17), including boundary points of the selected boxes.

Summing (17), enlarging the nonnegative sum from \(E\) to \(D_n\), and using \(c_z=\mathbf g(z/4)\), the normalized base law (8), and (13) give
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)
 \leq\sum_{z\in D_n}w_z
       e^{-2\|P(A(z/4)+b-\mathbf g(z/4))\|^2}
 \leq W\eta^{n-r}.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, (18) holds for every affine map realizing the minimum and every possible resulting residual subspace; no special fiber points were chosen.

A separate bound deals with every lower-dimensional base subset. The maximal atom of the one-coordinate law of \(Z\) in (8) is exactly \(1/4\). Every affine subspace of \(\mathbb R^n\) of dimension \(\rho\) admits an injective projection onto some \(\rho\) coordinate axes: choose \(\rho\) independent rows from a basis matrix for its direction space. Given values of these \(\rho\) coordinates, membership in the subspace determines at most one value for each remaining coordinate. Independence of all coordinates of \(Z\) therefore bounds the conditional probability of membership by \((1/4)^{n-\rho}\), including the case of no possible completion. Averaging gives, for \(E\subseteq\operatorname{aff}E\),
\[
\frac1W\sum_{z\in E}\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)
 \leq\frac1W\sum_{z\in E}w_z
 =\mathbb P(Z\in E)
 \leq4^{-(n-\rho)}.
\tag{19}
\]
When \(\rho=0\), the chosen projection has no coordinates and there is at most one possible full vector; when \(\rho=n\), the bound is one. Thus the endpoint ranks are included.

The left side of (19) is also at most \(\eta^{n-r}\) by (18). Its geometric-mean bound consequently gives
\[
\frac{2^{n-\rho}}W\sum_{z\in E}\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)
 \leq\eta^{(n-r)/2}.
\tag{20}
\]
Put \(\kappa=\sqrt\eta\) and \(m=n-r\). Since \(0\leq r\leq n\), both \(r\) and \(m\) are nonnegative integers and \(n+1\leq(r+1)(m+1)\). Also \((m+1)\kappa^m\leq\sum_{j=0}^m\kappa^j\leq1/(1-\kappa)\). Therefore every score in (1) for \(C_n\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^{n-\rho}}{r+1}\sum_{z\in E}\delta_{C_n}(z,q_z)
&\leq\frac{W\kappa^{n-r}}{r+1}\\
&\leq\frac{W}{(1-\kappa)(n+1)}
 =\frac{(1+\sqrt\eta)W}{(1-\eta)(n+1)}
 <\frac{32768W}{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
The common bound with constant \((1-\kappa)^{-1}<32768\) holds for all choices, so it also bounds their supremum. This proves the upper capacity bound in (6), for all subsets, all points, and the equivalent arbitrary-weight versions already established.

For the lower capacity bound, choose \(E=D_n\) and \(q_z=c_z\). Each positive-volume box is centrally symmetric about \(c_z\). Reflection in that center exchanges the two sides of any hyperplane through the center, and such a hyperplane has volume zero in a full-dimensional box. Hence every proper closed halfspace through \(c_z\) has exactly half the box volume; a closed halfspace containing the center contains a parallel halfspace through it. Thus \(\delta_{C_n}(z,c_z)=w_z/2\).

The set \(D_n\) contains \(0_n\) and \(\pm e_i\) for every \(i\), so \(\rho_{D_n}=n\). For every affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\), the midpoint second differences give
\[
(c_{e_i}-s(e_i))+(c_{-e_i}-s(-e_i))
      -2(c_{0_n}-s(0_n))=\tfrac14e_i
\quad(1\leq i\leq n).
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, the affine terms cancel, and each of \(c_{e_i}\) and \(c_{-e_i}\) differs from \(c_{0_n}=\tfrac12\mathbf1_n\) by \(e_i/8\). Every residual span therefore contains all coordinate directions, so \(r_{D_n}(c)=n\). The corresponding value in (1) is \(W/[2(n+1)]\). This proves the lower capacity bound in (6) with its exact base and residual ranks.

It remains to verify the actual depth of the fixed point. Conditional on \(R\), the variable \(Y\) in (8) is uniform on \([R,1]\), so \(\mathbb E[Y\mid R]=(1+R)/2\) and \(\mathbb E[Y^2\mid R]=(1+R+R^2)/3\). Symmetry gives \(\mathbb E\xi=0\) and \(\mathbb E[\xi Y]=\mathbb E[\xi(1+R)/2]=0\). Substituting (9) yields the exact block moments
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\xi&=0,&\quad\mathbb EY&=\frac{21}{32},
&\quad\operatorname{Cov}(\xi,Y)&=0,\\
\operatorname{Var}\xi&=\frac5{32},
&\mathbb EY^2&=\frac{47}{96},
&\operatorname{Var}Y&=\frac{47}{96}-\frac{441}{1024}
                    =\frac{181}{3072}>\frac1{18}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Also, since \(|\xi|\leq3/4\) and \(0\leq Y\leq1\), almost surely
\[
\xi^2+(Y-21/32)^2
 \leq\frac9{16}+\frac{441}{1024}
 =\frac{1017}{1024}<1.
\tag{24}
\]
For any real coefficients \(a,b\), put \(V=a\xi+b(Y-21/32)\). Equations (23)-(24) give \(\mathbb EV=0\), \(\mathbb EV^2\geq(a^2+b^2)/18\), and the pointwise bound \(V^2\leq a^2+b^2\leq18\mathbb EV^2\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb EV^4\leq18(\mathbb EV^2)^2.
\tag{25}
\]
This includes \(a=b=0\); otherwise the variance is positive.

Every ambient linear functional centered at \(p_n\) is, under normalized mixed volume, a sum \(F=\sum_{i=1}^nV_i\) of independent centered block variables of the preceding form. Specifically, a normal \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n\) gives \(F=a\cdot Z+b\cdot(Y-(21/32)\mathbf1_n)\), with block coefficients \((4a_i,b_i)\) because \(Z_i=4\xi_i\). The variance is positive for every nonzero ambient normal by (23). Independence and centering eliminate all fourth-moment expansion terms with an index occurring only once. Using (25) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EF^4
 &=\sum_i\mathbb EV_i^4
       +6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb EV_i^2\,\mathbb EV_j^2\\
 &\leq18\left(\sum_i\mathbb EV_i^2\right)^2
 =18(\mathbb EF^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
No relation among the signs of the coefficients is required.

For any centered real random variable \(F\) of positive variance \(\sigma^2\) satisfying (26), Hölder's inequality applied to \(|F|^2=|F|^{2/3}|F|^{4/3}\) gives
\(\sigma^2\leq(\mathbb E|F|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|F|^4)^{1/3}\), hence \(\mathbb E|F|\geq\sigma/\sqrt{18}\). Since \(F\) is centered, its positive part satisfies \(\mathbb EF_+=\tfrac12\mathbb E|F|\). Cauchy-Schwarz then yields
\[
\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{18}}
 \leq\mathbb EF_+
 \leq\sqrt{\mathbb EF_+^2}\sqrt{\mathbb P(F>0)}
 \leq\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(F>0)},
\qquad
\mathbb P(F>0)\geq\frac1{72}.
\tag{27}
\]
The argument is valid even when \(F\) has an atom at zero.

The point \(p_n\) belongs to the fiber \(C_{0_n}=[0,1]^n\), so it lies in \(S\). Let \(H\) be any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p_n\). Choose its orientation so that its parallel halfspace through \(p_n\) is \(\{(x,y):a\cdot x+b\cdot(y-(21/32)\mathbf1_n)\geq0\}\), with \((a,b)\ne0\); this halfspace is contained in \(H\). Its normalized mixed volume is at least \(\mathbb P(F>0)\geq1/72\), by (23), (26), and (27). Thus \(\mathcal H_n(S\cap H)\geq W/72\) for every such \(H\). The whole ambient space, if admitted as an improper halfspace, has mass \(W\); the empty set cannot contain \(p_n\). This proves the actual-depth assertion in (6) with one point chosen before the halfspace.

Finally, (6) gives \(0\leq\mathfrak R(C_n)/W\leq32768/(n+1)\to0\), which disproves a uniform positive lower bound for the capacity. At the same time, \(2^nh_{\max}(C_n)/W\geq2^n/72\). Thus the capacity obstruction supplies no upper bound that could refute the locked depth target. Every geometric, probabilistic, Fourier, projection, and moment assertion used for this family has been proved here.
