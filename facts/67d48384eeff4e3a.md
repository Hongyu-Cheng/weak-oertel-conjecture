## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let positive $d$-volume convex bodies $A,K,B$ satisfy $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use the actual fibers at $-1,0,1$ of their layered convex hull, measured by the sum of their $d$-volumes. Put $b=|B|\ge|A|>0$, $a=|A|/b$, $k=|K|/b$, $M=b(1+a+k)$, $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, and $\rho=((d-1)/(d+1))^d$. For $h\ge0$ define $t(h)=(2h^{1/d}-1)_+^d$.

For $d=2$ and a fixed $0<a\le1$, put $v=(1+\sqrt a)^2/4$. Let $\sigma_*(a)$ be the least $\sigma\in[0,1/2]$ for which
$$
\max\{4/9,\ \sigma k+[a-(2\sqrt{(1-\sigma)k}-1)_+^2]_+\}\ \ge\ \frac29(1+a+k)
$$
for every scalar $k\ge v$. Then
$$
\sigma_*(a)=
\begin{cases}
4/[9(1-a)], &0<a\le1/9,\\
2(5+2\sqrt a+5a)/(17+18\sqrt a+17a), &1/9\le a\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
It lies in $[4/9,1/2]$, equals $1/2$ only at $a=1/9$, and equals $330/673$ at $a=4/25$. Consequently if an actual middle point $c$ has ordinary normalized depth at least $\sigma_*(a)$, one of $(0,c)$ and $(1,g_B)$ has raw mixed depth at least $2M/9$ for every compatible middle area. The algebraic necessity refers to the two certificates in the inequality.

## Proof

The layered hull has exactly the prescribed fibers, as convex combinations at base zero use equal endpoint weights. Ordinary midpoint Brunn–Minkowski and compatibility give $k\ge v$. Conversely every $k\ge v$ is an admissible area triple by using homothets of one centered area-one convex body in ratios $\sqrt a,\sqrt k,1$. The two lower bounds come from f0399a8f0bae48c6 §2 with the single reflected pair and ordinary middle depth at least $b\sigma k$, and from 6769574fc1bd9f8d at the full mass $b$ on the larger endpoint. The middle certificate is nondecreasing in $\sigma$, since increasing $\sigma$ increases own depth and decreases the missed-area budget.

If $0<a\le1/9$, put $\sigma=4/[9(1-a)]$. The endpoint certificate covers $k\le k_l=1-a$, and the own middle payment $\sigma k$ covers $k\ge k_l$: both pay exactly the target at $k_l$, with $\sigma>2/9$ for the latter. Also $4(5/9-a)-(1+\sqrt a)^2=(1-3\sqrt a)(15\sqrt a+11)/9\ge0$. Thus at $\sigma$ and $k_l$ the missed area is $5/9-a\ge v$. If $\sigma$ decreases, the residual credit is zero for $k$ just above $k_l$, the middle payment stays strictly below target there by continuity, and the endpoint payment also fails. These $k$ are admissible because $k_l>5/9-a\ge v$.

For $x=\sqrt a\in[1/3,1]$, write $D=17+18x+17x^2$ and take $\sigma=2(5+2x+5x^2)/D$. Direct subtraction gives $1-\sigma=7(1+x)^2/D$, $1/2-\sigma=(3x-1)(3-x)/(2D)$ and $\sigma-4/9=2(11x^2-18x+11)/(9D)>0$, the last quadratic having negative discriminant and positive leading coefficient. The endpoint pays for $k\le k_l=1-x^2$, and the own middle pays for $k\ge k_u=D/28$, as follows from solving $\sigma k=2(1+x^2+k)/9$. Their potential gap interval has length
$$
k_u-k_l=\frac{(3x-1)(15x+11)}{28}\ge0.
$$
At $k_u$ the missed area is exactly $v$, the residual credit is zero, and the middle payment is the target. Throughout $[k_l,k_u]$ we have $(1-\sigma)k\le v$. Thus the middle certificate there equals $L(k)=\sigma k+x^2-(2\sqrt{(1-\sigma)k}-1)_+^2$. The function subtracted is convex in $k$: the function $(2\sqrt h-1)_+^2$ is zero up to $1/4$, then has increasing derivative $4-2/\sqrt h$ starting at zero. Hence the gap between $L(k)$ and target is concave.

At the lower endpoint let $h_l=(1-\sigma)k_l$. The identity
$$
4D-63(1+x)^2(1-x^2)=(3x-1)(21x^3+49x^2+39x-5)\ge0
$$
gives $h_l\le4/9$, since the cubic is increasing and positive at $x=1/3$. The target at $k_l$ is $4/9$. If $h_l\le1/4$, $L(k_l)=1-h_l\ge3/4$. Otherwise $L(k_l)=4\sqrt{h_l}-5h_l$, and its excess above $4/9$ is $(2/3-\sqrt{h_l})(5\sqrt{h_l}-2/3)\ge0$. This gives both endpoint signs, so the inequality holds for all $k$ in the potential gap, even if part of the gap lies below $v$.

If $x>1/3$ and $\sigma$ is decreased from the displayed value, keep $k=k_u$. This is admissible since $(1-\sigma_*)k_u=v<k_u$. Now the residual credit is zero and the own middle payment is strictly below target, while the endpoint also fails because $k_u>k_l$. The case $x=1/3$ was already handled by the first branch. The formulas agree at $1/9$ and the range and sample value follow by the displayed differences and substitution.
