## Statement

Use the independent signed-exponential probability $P$ and $m,k,p,q,c,A,B,D,J$ of the all-index primitive lemma. Put $C=p+q$ and
$$
F=[z^q]\frac{1+2Bcz}{(1+Bcz)(1-2pBz)^2(1+2cCz)^{m+1}}.
$$
Then $F$ is a polynomial in $p$ of degree at most $2q$, with $F(1)=k2^{k-1}(m+k)^{k-1}$, and
$$
J(p)=(-1)^k\frac{2^kp^{m+k}B^{m+k}D-(1+p)^2A^{m+2}F}
                    {2^k m c^{k+1}(1+p)^2B^{m+k}}
\quad(0<p<1).
$$
Where $F>0$ in this interval put $R=2^kp^{m+k}B^{m+k}D/[(1+p)^2A^{m+2}F]$. Its logarithmic derivative is $L/[p(1+p)BADF]$, where
$$
\begin{aligned}
L={}&DF\bigl[(m+k)(1+p)BA+(m+k)p(1+p)A\\
&\hspace{20mm}-(m+2)(m+1)p(1+p)B-2pBA\bigr]+p(1+p)BA(D'F-DF').
\end{aligned}
$$
The degree of $L$ is at most $2k+3$ and $(p-1)^k$ divides $L$, so $L/(p-1)^k$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k+3$.

## Proof

The all-index primitive lemma applies with the same independent variables and the same positive parameter interval. Write $S(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^q S_\ell t^\ell$ for the polynomial there. Integrating $[A-(m+k)t]t^{m+\ell-1}$ gives
$$
Q(A/B)=\sum_{\ell=0}^q
 \frac{S_\ell A^{\ell+1}(A-\ell c)}
 {B^{\ell+1}(m+\ell)(m+\ell+1)},
$$
since $(m+\ell+1)B-(m+k)(m+\ell)=A-\ell c$. For $\ell=0$ the numerator has $A^2$ explicitly, and for $\ell\ge1$ this factor is in $A^{\ell+1}$.

Expand $(t-p)^j$ in $S$ and put $j=\ell+r$, $s=q-\ell-r$. Multiplying the last expression by $m(m+1)B^k/A^2$ yields
$$
\sum_{\ell+r+s=q}\binom{m+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{m+\ell+r+1}{r}
 (2A)^\ell(1-\ell c/A)(-2pB)^r(Bc)^s .
$$
This follows by canceling factorials in $\binom{m+\ell+r+1}{\ell+r}\binom{\ell+r}{\ell}$. Hold $A,B,c,p$ fixed and sum this expression against $z^q$ over $q\ge0$. The $s$ sum gives $(1-Bcz)^{-1}$. The $r$ sum gives $(1+2pBz)^{-m-\ell-2}$. With $y=2Az/(1+2pBz)$, the remaining $\ell$ sum is
$$
(1-y)^{-m}-(mc/A)y(1-y)^{-m-1}.
$$
The resulting rational series is
$$
\frac{1+2(pB-A-mc)z}{(1-Bcz)(1+2pBz)^2(1+2(pB-A)z)^{m+1}}.
$$
All coefficient sums at fixed degree are finite. Substituting $pB-A=-cC$ and $pB-A-mc=-cB$, then replacing $z$ by $-z$, proves $F=(-1)^q m(m+1)B^kQ(A/B)/A^2$. Every term of $[z^q]$ is a product of $q$ factors from $Bc,pB,cC$ counting multiplicities, so its degree is at most $2q$. At $p=1$ the series is $(1-2(m+k)z)^{-2}$, giving the claimed value. Substituting for $Q$ in the primitive proves the formula for $J$.

Logarithmic differentiation and multiplication by $p(1+p)BADF$ give the displayed $L$. The degree bound follows from $\deg D=2$, $\deg F\le2q$ and the displayed terms. To obtain the divisibility, use the integral for $J$ split at zero. Each piece is analytic near $p=1$: parametrizing its interval by a fixed interval leaves a rational integrand whose denominators stay away from zero uniformly in that interval for $p$ in a small complex neighborhood of $1$. Thus the numerator of the formula for $J$ has a zero of order at least $k+1$ at $p=1$. The denominator defining $R$ is nonzero there since $A(1),F(1)>0$. It follows that $R-1$ vanishes to order at least $k+1$ and $R'/R$ to order at least $k$. The factor clearing this logarithmic derivative is nonzero at $1$, proving the polynomial divisibility.
