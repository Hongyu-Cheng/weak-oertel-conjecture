## Statement

At bases $-1,0,1$, use the actual interval fibers $K_\pm=[-1,1]$ and $K_0=[-1,7]$. Denote summed lengths by $\mu$, their total by $M$, and the length-weighted within-fiber variance by $\Sigma$. At a sampled mark $(p,y)$ use raw buffered depth
$$
\inf_{(a,u)\ne(0,0)}
 \mu\{(z,X):a(z-p)+u(X-y)+|u|\varepsilon\sqrt{L(p,y)\Sigma E_{K_p}(y)/M}\ge0\},
$$
where $E$ is marked projective energy, with its zero boundary value. Let $\beta$ be the maximum raw buffered depth divided by $M$. At $L=1$, set $\delta=\varepsilon\sqrt{55/24}$ and assume $0<\delta\le1/10$. Then the unique maximizing mark is $Y=(0,2)$, with raw value $Q=5+\delta$ and $\beta=(5+\delta)/12$. On this fixed body $L=1$ minimizes the global buffered maximum over all positive concave physical weights on the full hull with $\int\log L\,d\mu/M=0$. Moreover $\delta_Y\preceq_{\rm cx}\mu/M$ in full physical coordinates, with constant-$L$ contact label zero.

The actual centroid-hull contraction has $K_\pm(t)=[-1+t,1-t]$, $K_0(t)=[-1+t,7-4t]$, and extends as a compatible family for small signed $t$. At fixed $\varepsilon,L=1$ its unique optimizer has height $y(t)=2-t/2$ at base zero, with
$$
\begin{gathered}
M(t)=12-9t,\quad
\Sigma(t)=\frac{2(2-2t)^3+(8-5t)^3}{12(12-9t)},\\
E_t=\frac{4(3-3t/2)(5-7t/2)}{8-5t},\quad
Q(t)=5-7t/2+\delta(t),\qquad
\delta(t)=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Sigma(t)E_t/M(t)}.
\end{gathered}
$$
At zero, $\delta'(0)=-433\delta/880$ and $\beta'(0)=1/48+227\delta/10560>0$. Thus the actual forward contraction increases optimized depth despite global concave-weight optimality and the full volume martingale. The locally feasible reverse path decreases it. In the regular two-cap physical density calibration, all three fiber forces vanish and the centroid-repair integral equals $1/4+227\delta/880=M\beta'(0)$.

## Proof

A combination at base zero uses equal weights from the two endpoint bases, whose average interval is contained in the middle one. This proves exact compatibility at the three sampled bases, and the same argument applies for small signed $t$ because the endpoint and middle lower ends agree while their upper ends retain strict slack. The old centroids are $0,3,0$, so the centroid-hull sections are $\{0\},[0,3],\{0\}$ in base order. This gives the displayed fibers for positive $t$, and the same interval formulas define the signed continuation. Variance of a uniform interval of length $V$ is $V^2/12$, giving $M(t)$ and $\Sigma(t)$ and in particular $M(0)=12$, $\Sigma(0)=11/3$. For interval $[l,r]$ the projective energy is $4(y-l)(r-y)/(r-l)$ by the interval energy formula. At $y(t)$ the endpoint distances in the middle fiber are $3-3t/2$ and $5-7t/2$, which gives $E_t$ and $E_0(2)=15/2$.

Fix small signed $t$. The two identical endpoint intervals have length $V=2-2t$ and are symmetric about zero. At the middle mark $y(t)$ all upper continuous-normal caps capture the same middle length $7-4t-y(t)+\delta(t)$. The cap with zero base tilt excludes both endpoints strictly and attains that value. For the lower continuous-normal caps the middle length is $y(t)-(-1+t)+\delta(t)$. Their two endpoint thresholds average to $y(t)+\delta(t)>0$. If $G(s)$ is the length in one symmetric endpoint interval below $s$, then $G(s)+G(-s)=V$. The two thresholds are $s$ and a value at least $-s$, so their summed length is at least $V$. The fixed direction $-X+4z$ makes one endpoint full and the other empty, both strictly, and attains this lower bound. At zero all these strict statuses and both proper middle traces hold since $0<\delta\le1/10$, and they persist for small signed $t$. The two resulting raw lengths are equal at $y(t)$ and give the asserted $Q(t)$. The smallest pure-base cap through the middle includes its full interval and at least one endpoint interval, so its mass is strictly greater than $Q(t)$ near zero. A pure-base cap through an endpoint mark can isolate its own interval, giving mass at most $V<Q(t)$. This proves the all-direction lower bound at the proposed mark and excludes both endpoint fibers from the global maximizers.

For all middle marks write $b_t(y)=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Sigma(t)E_{K_0(t)}(y)/M(t)}$. This is strictly concave on the middle interval, with zero boundary values. Its derivative at $y(t)$ has magnitude less than one for small $t$, since at zero it is $\delta/15$. The two caps in directions $X$ and $-X+4z$ at $y(t)$ have exact mass $Q(t)$ with proper middle cuts. In each fixed direction every threshold strictly above its displayed threshold gives strictly smaller mass, by strict decrease near that proper middle trace and monotonicity globally. Therefore at any middle mark of raw depth at least $Q(t)$,
$$
y-b_t(y)\le y(t)-b_t(y(t)),\qquad
-y-b_t(y)\le-y(t)-b_t(y(t)).
$$
Weight these inequalities by $(1+b_t'(y(t)))/2$ and $(1-b_t'(y(t)))/2$, respectively. The result is $b_t'(y(t))(y-y(t))\le b_t(y)-b_t(y(t))$, impossible by strict concavity unless $y=y(t)$. This proves the all-mark upper bound and uniqueness.

At zero the full original-volume mean is $Y$, since the middle length is eight with centroid three and the endpoints have centroid zero. For a normalized positive concave $L$, Jensen gives $L(Y)\ge\int L\,d\mu/M\ge\exp(\int\log L\,d\mu/M)=1$. Every cap buffer at $Y$ therefore weakly increases, giving the fixed-body global optimality of $L=1$. Full physical Jensen gives the one-row certificate from $\delta_Y$ to $\mu/M$, and $L=1$ is affine with zero gradient. Direct differentiation of the displayed formulas at zero gives $E'_0/E_0=-23/40$, $\Sigma'_0/\Sigma_0=-51/44$, and $M'_0/M_0=-3/4$. Thus $\delta'(0)/\delta=-433/880$, and the quotient $Q(t)/M(t)$ has the stated positive derivative.

For the response calibration take the two regular mass-$Q$ caps $X\ge2-\delta$ and $-X+4z\ge-2-\delta$, with probabilities $\rho_\pm=(1\pm\delta/15)/2$. Their coareas agree, their covariance-unit continuous covectors are $\pm1/\sqrt{\Sigma}$, and the energy parameter at $Y$ is $1/15$ from the interval denominator optimum. The regular density at $L=1$ specializes to
$$
\Xi_z(X)=W_z(X)-\beta+
\mathbf1_{z=0}\frac{\delta[1+(X-2)/15]^{-2}}{2(15/2)}
+\frac{\delta(X-g_z)^2}{2M\Sigma}-\frac{\delta}{12},
$$
where $W$ is the $\rho$-weighted cap indicator. The scale coefficient is $\delta$ since the common coarea with covariance-unit covectors is $\sqrt{\Sigma}$. This is an evaluation of the regular two-cap formula, not a joint-minimum assertion. Each endpoint interval has equal density at its two endpoints. At the middle the difference of the two endpoint cap loads is $\rho_+-\rho_-=\delta/15$ and the difference of the energy terms is $(\delta/15)[(3/4)^2-(5/4)^2]=-\delta/15$. Its quadratic terms agree, so all forces vanish. The lower middle endpoint has density $\rho_--\beta+5\delta/48+2\delta/11-\delta/12=1/12+227\delta/2640$. It is the only endpoint with nonzero centroid repair, of size three. The regular density's common affine trace vanishes, so $\int\Xi v=-3\Xi_0(-1)=-M\beta'(0)$. In particular at $\delta=1/10$ one has $\varepsilon^2=6/1375$, $Q=51/10$, $\beta=17/40$, $\beta'=809/35200$, and $\int\Xi v=-2427/8800$.
