## Statement

For the finite complete lattice set $I$ in the maximal-minimal-face lemma, join two of its maximal minimal faces when they intersect. For each connected component let $J_c$ be the union of the sites of $I$ in its faces. These $J_c$ partition $I$ and are exactly the components generated by the positive-coefficient convex representations in $I$ whose target belongs to $I$. Each $J_c$ is lattice-complete, and their occupied parity classes are pairwise disjoint. If $m_c$ is the number of maximal minimal faces in component $c$, $k_c=k(J_c)$, and $r$ is the number of nonsingleton components, then
$$
k(I)=\sum_c k_c,\qquad m_c\le k_c,\qquad
m_c=k_c\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |J_c|=1,\qquad m\le k(I)-r.
$$

## Proof

The dependence hypergraph of 3d5f56b513c21fc4 uses the edges $I\cap F_z$ for nonvertex $z$, equivalently all nontrivial integer-anchored convex representations. Adding edges for vertex $z$ only adds singletons and leaves components unchanged. Every such edge is contained in some edge $I\cap F_i$ of a maximal minimal face, and each maximal minimal face appears as one of these edges. Thus the latter edges generate exactly the same components. Two faces intersect if and only if their integer-site sets intersect: a nonempty intersection of faces contains a vertex of the lattice polytope $Q$. Hence the face graph gives the claimed $J_c$. The accepted dependence theorem now proves completeness, disjoint parity classes, and parity additivity.

For $z\in J_c$ its old minimal face $F_z$ is contained in a maximal face of component $c$. All vertices of that maximal face belong to $J_c$, so $F_z\subseteq\operatorname{conv}J_c$. The supporting equations of the old face expose it also as a face of $\operatorname{conv}J_c$, since this hull is contained in $Q$. The point $z$ is still in its relative interior. Consequently the minimal face of $z$ in $\operatorname{conv}J_c$ is the same $F_z$, and its maximal minimal faces are exactly the $m_c$ faces of the component. The saturation lemma applied to the complete $J_c$ proves $m_c\le k_c$. Equality makes all those faces singletons, so connectedness forces a single site. A single site has one face and one parity, proving the converse. Summing the integer deficits $k_c-m_c$, each at least one for a nonsingleton component, gives the final inequality.
