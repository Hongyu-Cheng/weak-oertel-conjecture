## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\Lambda$ be a finite nonempty complete base in an affine lattice, and let $K_z$ be positive compact convex $d$-fibers with $d\ge1$, satisfying the anchored Minkowski inclusions. Write $V_z=|K_z|$, $M=\sum_zV_z$, $g_z$ for the fiber centroids and
$$
G_K(u)^2=M^{-1}\sum_z\int_{K_z}[u\cdot(X-g_z)]^2\,dX.
$$
Take $\varepsilon>0$ and a positive sampled-concave array $\ell$, meaning $\ell_p\ge\sum_z\lambda_z\ell_z$ for every anchored convex representation of $p\in P$, with $\sum_z V_z\log\ell_z=0$. This includes positive affine multipliers. For a sampled mark $(p,y)$, take the infimum of the raw summed fiber masses of the caps with margin
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon(\ell_p E_{K_p}(y)/M)^{1/(d+1)}G_K(u)\ge0
$$
for $u\ne0$ and of the unchanged closed pure-base caps through $p$, including the whole-base trace in rank zero. Here $E$ is marked projective energy, extended by zero on the own boundary. Write $b_{\rm base}(p)$ for closed pure-base depth, $Q>0$ for the maximum buffered raw depth and $I$ for the bases carrying a maximizing mark. For $T\subseteq P$, let $k(T)$ count the classes modulo twice the direction lattice occupied by $T$.

If $b_{\rm base}(p)=Q$ for every $p\in I$ and $Q/M<1/k(I)$, there are arbitrarily close actual positive compatible families on $P$ and scalar renormalizations of the same array $\ell$ with respect to the new volumes, at the same $\varepsilon$, whose unpenalized normalized buffered maximum is strictly below $Q/M$. If the initial multiplier is affine, its renormalizations are affine.

## Proof

The least concave roof $\widehat\ell$ on $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ is the maximum of $\sum_z\lambda_z\ell_z$ over convex representations of the argument. It agrees with $\ell$ at sampled bases and is positive and continuous there and throughout the hull (the finite piecewise-affine roof construction in 7227bbfcd12ad3d2, applied to interval fibers with one fixed lower endpoint). On the canonical hull the jointly concave energy root $E^{1/d}$ is continuous with its zero own-boundary value, as used in 0b2fbdaf8d3134a0. The product in the form $M^{-1/(d+1)}\widehat\ell^{1/(d+1)}(E^{1/d})^{d/(d+1)}$ is continuous and concave by the monotone concave weighted geometric mean. Since $G_K$ is a positive definite norm from the positive fibers, an invertible continuous-coordinate change makes it Euclidean. Equivalently one divides by $\|u\|$ and uses the fixed positive direction-dependent coefficient $\varepsilon G_K(u/\|u\|)$ in each closed-quantile inequality of 0b2fbdaf8d3134a0. Each inequality has a convex left side and the pure-base quantile constraints are closed halfspaces. Thus the real superlevel at $Q$ is convex. The same formula with the whole-base condition applies if the base rank is zero.

The midpoint of sampled maximizing marks over same-parity sites $p,p'$ belongs to this superlevel, and its base lies in $P$ by completeness. Compatibility identifies its fiber with the actual fiber. By maximality the midpoint base is in $I$. More generally, the same argument for any convex combination of these lifts over a sampled base gives $I=P\cap\operatorname{conv}(I)$. The parity-compression-exact-atomic-caps lemma now applies to $I$ with the old volume weights and common pure-base depth $Q$: there are $1\le m\le k(I)$ fixed closed mass-$Q$ caps covering it.

The common erosion of atomic-closed-cap-hinge-budget applies. Since $\beta m\le\beta k(I)<1$ for $\beta=Q/M$, all retained normalized masses fall strictly below $\beta$ for sufficiently small positive $t$. Every point in every deformed fiber with base in $I$ is bounded by the raw mass of one of these same pure-base caps. Normalize $\ell$ by multiplying all its values by $\exp[-\sum_z V_z(t)\log\ell_z/M(t)]$. This factor tends to one and preserves positivity, sampled concavity and affineness when present. The values are constant within each fiber and converge at each sampled base. Thus the matched-mark uniform continuity of f2ead43a963c7026 applies to the energy, updated covariance and multiplier at fixed scale, in particular to the maximum on each fiber. For every base outside $I$ its old maximum is strictly below $Q$, so all such maxima stay below the largest retained cap mass, which tends to $Q$, for small $t$. If $I=P$ this comparison is unnecessary. The new global normalized maximum is bounded by the largest retained cap ratio and is therefore strictly smaller.
