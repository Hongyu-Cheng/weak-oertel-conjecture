## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \(w\) be a nonzero nonnegative finitely supported table on \(\mathbb Z^n\) with a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with all its lattice values, including zeros, in the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Set \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}\), \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\), \(D=\{\mathbf I:p_{\mathbf I}>0\}\), \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), \(p_{\max}=\max_Dp\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D\). Thus \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\); no affine-rank hypothesis is imposed. All logarithms are natural, and \(0\log0=0\).

Use exactly the complete-table arrays and information quantities of accepted fact `464815b935df9890`. For any finite probability law \(v\), write \(S(v)=-\sum_xv_x\log v_x\). Both indices of an array below range over \(D\). Explicitly, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(p)&=\left\{r\ge0:
  \sum_{\mathbf J}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\quad
  \sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
  \quad(\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       \log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\qquad
S(q)=-\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J},\\
I(r)&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\\r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0}}
       r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       \log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf I}}
       =S(q)-H(r),\\
G(r)&=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       \log\frac{p_{\mathbf J}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
     =\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       (\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}),\\
\mathcal A(r)&=I(r)+G(r),\qquad
\mathcal A_*(p)=\min_{r\in\mathcal F(p)}\mathcal A(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(r\) is the joint law of source \(\mathbf X\) and target \(\mathbf J\), \(H(r)=\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J\mid\mathbf X)\), and \(I(r)=I(\mathbf X;\mathbf J)\). The notation \(\operatorname{Ent}\) for entropies of random variables also follows `464815b935df9890`. In the first response of `continue-weak-oertel.txt`, \(\mathsf I\) is \(I\) here. Its objective \(\mathcal A=I+G\) is distinct from the other archived response's notation for conditional entropy, which is \(H\) here. The first-stage optimization in this statement is minimization of \(\mathcal A\).

There is a unique array \(r^\dagger(p)\) maximizing \(H\) among all minimizers of \(\mathcal A\). To state its bound, set \(\pi_{\mathbf b}=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\mathbf b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}p_{\mathbf I}\) for \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^n\), extend \(p\) by zero off \(D\), and put

\[
\bar r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
 =\frac{p_{\mathbf I}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}
        {\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}},\qquad
\bar q_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}\bar r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}.
\]

The denominator is positive for every indexed source \(\mathbf I\in D\). This array is feasible, and, with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\|v)=\sum_{u_a>0}u_a\log(u_a/v_a)\) for probability laws with the required support inclusion,

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le\mathcal A_*(p)
&\le\mathcal A(\bar r)
 =S(\pi)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar q\|p)
 \le S(\pi)\le n\log2,\\
G(r^\dagger)
&\le S(\pi)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar q\|p)
       -S(q^\dagger)+H(r^\dagger)\\
 &\le n\log2-S(q^\dagger)+H(r^\dagger).
\end{aligned}
\]

The coefficient one in the last inequality is sharp among strictly positive universal coefficients, by the all-array obstruction of `464815b935df9890`.

For every minimizer \(r\), and every active row \(q_{\mathbf J}>0\), let \(F_{\mathbf J}\) be the minimal face of \(P\) containing \(\mathbf J\). Its exact positive row support is \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). This set contains \(\mathbf J\), its convex hull is \(F_{\mathbf J}\), and its complete lattice intersection is itself. There are finite vectors \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\in\mathbb R^n\), one for each active row, for which

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(\mathbf I)
 &=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf K:q_{\mathbf K}>0\\\mathbf I\in F_{\mathbf K}}}
       \frac{q_{\mathbf K}}{p_{\mathbf K}}
       e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)}>0
       \qquad(\mathbf I\in D),\\
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
 &=p_{\mathbf I}\frac{q_{\mathbf J}}{p_{\mathbf J}}
       \frac{e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}}{Z(\mathbf I)}
       \qquad(\mathbf I\in D\cap F_{\mathbf J},\ q_{\mathbf J}>0).
\end{aligned}
\]

All other entries are zero. The function \(\log Z\) is convex-admissible on \(D\): its value at an integral barycenter is at most the corresponding average of its source values. On each active row support, the assignment-share potential \(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=-\log(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\) has a finite convex extension to \(F_{\mathbf J}\). Both the row and its normalization by \(q_{\mathbf J}\), extended by zero at all remaining lattice points, have finite-valued globally log-concave extensions. An inactive row is identically zero and has the zero extension; it is not normalized.

For every nonempty \(E\subseteq D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\) and positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) of sum one with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\), define the logarithmic gap of a positive table on these points by \(G_\lambda(u)=\log u_{\mathbf J}-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log u_{\mathbf I}\). Then

\[
0\le G_\lambda(p)
 \le G_\lambda(r_{\mathbf J,\cdot})
 =G_\lambda(r_{\mathbf J,\cdot}/q_{\mathbf J}).
\]

This is the original logarithmic-cost normalization of `488f0a583886b5fc`, with input table \(w=p\), total weight one, and the potential \(\phi\) defined above. It does not replace original incidence capacities by barycentric-weighted capacities.

For any second table \(p'\) satisfying the same hypotheses in \(\mathbb Z^{n'}\), use the complete product support \(D\times D'\). Both the value and the canonical selector tensorize exactly:

\[
\mathcal A_*(p\otimes p')=\mathcal A_*(p)+\mathcal A_*(p'),\qquad
r^\dagger(p\otimes p')=r^\dagger(p)\otimes r^\dagger(p').
\]

There is an explicit one-dimensional input for which the first-stage optimizer is already unique and has exactly three active targets. Let \(D=\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}\), \(N=113590\), and let \(p_i=w_i/N\) and \(a_i=A_i/N\) be the symmetric positive tables given by

| \(\lvert i\rvert\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(2\) | \(3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(w_i\) | 29920 | 21222 | 14952 | 5661 |
| \(A_i\) | 10880 | 6288 | 2688 | 544 |

The auxiliary \(A_i\) are integer numerator entries, not the objective \(\mathcal A\). Define probability rows

\[
\nu_0(i)=\frac{a_i}{p_0},\qquad
\nu_1(i)=\frac{a_i2^{i-1}}{p_1},\qquad
\nu_{-1}(i)=\frac{a_i2^{-i-1}}{p_1}.
\]

The unique optimizer is \(r_{j,i}=q_j\nu_j(i)\) for \(j\in\{-1,0,1\}\), zero for all other targets, where \(q_0=7480/N\) and \(q_{-1}=q_1=53055/N\). Its input is strictly log-concave. For every integer \(n\ge1\), the canonical selector for the \(n\)-fold product of this input therefore has exactly \(3^n\) positive rows.

Finally, the complete uniform-simplex support supplied by accepted fact `02a48d28569660b9`, namely \(D_n=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,-\mathbf1\}\) with \(p_{\mathbf I}=1/(n+2)\), satisfies \(\mathcal A_*(p)=0\) but \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)=2/(n+2)\), using the hitting score of `488f0a583886b5fc`. Consequently there is no absolute \(c>0\) such that \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(p)\ge c e^{-\mathcal A_*(p)}\) for every dimension and every admissible table. ## Proof

Accepted fact `464815b935df9890`, with precisely the same \(w,p,D,\phi,\mathcal F,H,I,G,S\), supplies complete support, convex-admissibility of \(\phi\), the nonempty feasible set, and \(G(r),I(r)\ge0\) for every feasible array, including zero rows. Its explicit parity array is denoted here by \(\bar r\), and its corresponding row law by \(\bar q\). The original logarithmic-cost and hitting conventions are those of `488f0a583886b5fc` with table \(w=p\) and total weight one.

Every entry of a feasible array lies in \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\), and the constraints are linear and closed. Thus \(\mathcal F(p)\) is compact and convex. It has total mass one, and each positive normalized row is a probability law of mean its target, directly from its constraints. We use the equality condition in relative-entropy nonnegativity as well: \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)=0\) for probability laws precisely when \(a=b\). In the elementary proof used in `464815b935df9890`, equality in \(-\log(b_a/a_a)\ge1-b_a/a_a\) requires equality at every positive numerator coordinate and no remaining denominator mass, which gives this condition also when some entries vanish.

Expand the objective using the fixed source marginal:

\[
\mathcal A(r)
=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\log r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
 -\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J}
 +\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}
 -2\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{1}
\]

For a nonnegative row \(v\) of mass \(s\), the first two contributions from that row are \(\sum_i v_i\log v_i-s\log s=s\sum_i(v_i/s)\log(v_i/s)\) when \(s>0\), and zero when \(s=0\). This is a convex function of the row. Indeed, the normalization of a mixture of two positive-mass rows is the mixture of their normalizations with weights proportional to their masses; convexity of \(\sum_i u_i\log u_i\) on the probability simplex gives the desired inequality after multiplication by the mixed mass. A zero-mass row causes no exception, because this row function is positively homogeneous and has value zero at the zero vector. Formula (1), including \(t\log t\to0\), proves continuity at zero entries and zero rows. The remaining terms are linear or constant, so \(\mathcal A\) is convex and continuous on \(\mathcal F(p)\).

Its minimizer set is consequently nonempty, compact, and convex. Each function \(-t\log(t/p_{\mathbf I})\) is strictly concave for \(t\ge0\), including a segment with a zero endpoint: the linear term \(t\log p_{\mathbf I}\) does not affect strictness, and \(-t\log t\) is strictly concave. The sum \(H(r)\) is therefore strictly concave in the whole array. It has a unique maximizer on the minimizer set, proving existence and uniqueness of \(r^\dagger\).

The parity construction in `464815b935df9890` gives \(\bar r\in\mathcal F(p)\), \(H(\bar r)=S(p)-S(\pi)\), and \(G(\bar r)+I(\bar r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar q\|p)=S(\pi)\le n\log2\), with positive denominators and all empty-class cases already covered there. Thus optimality gives \(\mathcal A_*(p)\le\mathcal A(\bar r)=S(\pi)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar q\|p)\). Nonnegativity of \(G,I\) gives the lower bound zero, and \(G(r^\dagger)=\mathcal A_*(p)-S(q^\dagger)+H(r^\dagger)\) proves the asserted entropy inequality. For every \(0<c<1\), the same accepted fact supplies an admissible one-dimensional input for which every feasible array fails \(G(r)\le c(\log2-S(q)+H(r))\). It applies in particular to \(r^\dagger\), proving the stated strictly positive coefficient sharpness.

Fix now any minimizer \(r\) and active row \(\mathbf J\). The defining property of a face implies that every positive source in a representation of \(\mathbf J\) as a convex combination lies in \(F_{\mathbf J}\). Hence the positive row support is contained in \(E=D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). The point \(\mathbf J\) lies in the relative interior of its minimal face. There exists a mean-\(\mathbf J\) probability law \(\nu\) positive at every point of \(E\): let \(\mathbf m\) be the average of all points of \(E\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the point \((\mathbf J-\varepsilon\mathbf m)/(1-\varepsilon)\) belongs to \(F_{\mathbf J}\), by relative interior. Represent that point as a convex combination of the vertices of the face, all of which belong to \(E\), and mix this representation with weight \(\varepsilon\) of the uniform law on \(E\). This constructs \(\nu\). If the face has dimension zero the same construction gives its singleton law.

Extend \(\nu\) by zero off \(E\), and choose \(c>0\) small enough that \(c\nu_{\mathbf I}\le p_{\mathbf I}\) at every source. The comparison array

\[
u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}
 =c\mathbf1_{\mathbf K=\mathbf J}\nu_{\mathbf I}
  +\mathbf1_{\mathbf K=\mathbf I}(p_{\mathbf I}-c\nu_{\mathbf I})
\]

is feasible, because its extra row has mean \(\mathbf J\) and its remaining masses are diagonal. Its \(\mathbf J\)-row is positive on all of \(E\). Suppose that \(r\) omits some point of \(E\) from that active row. Consider \(r(t)=(1-t)r+tu\) for \(0<t<1\).

In any previously positive row, previously positive coordinates and its positive row mass change their \(x\log x\) terms by \(O(t)\). A previously zero coordinate with \(u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}>0\) contributes \(u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}t\log t+O(t)\). In a previously inactive row the cancellation is exact: writing \(s_{\mathbf K}=\sum_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}\), its full first-two-term contribution to (1) is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I}tu_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}\log(tu_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I})
 -ts_{\mathbf K}\log(ts_{\mathbf K})
 =t\left(\sum_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}\log u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}
          -s_{\mathbf K}\log s_{\mathbf K}\right).
\]

This equality also holds when \(s_{\mathbf K}=0\). All linear terms change by \(O(t)\). Consequently

\[
\mathcal A(r(t))-\mathcal A(r)
 =t\log t
   \sum_{\substack{\mathbf K:q_{\mathbf K}>0\\\mathbf I:r_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}=0}}
      u_{\mathbf K,\mathbf I}+O(t).
\]

The coefficient is strictly positive because of the omitted point in row \(\mathbf J\). For sufficiently small \(t>0\), the displayed change is negative, contradicting optimality. Thus the exact support of every active minimizing row is \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). Since every vertex of the face belongs to \(D\), its convex hull is the whole face; its integer points are precisely \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). In particular it contains its target.

Finite stationarity multipliers can now be obtained without any assumption about inactive rows or independent constraint equations. Restrict the finite constraint matrix to the exact positive coordinates \(\mathcal T=\{(\mathbf J,\mathbf I):q_{\mathbf J}>0,\ \mathbf I\in D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\}\) of this minimizing array. Every vector in its kernel permits both signs of a sufficiently small feasible perturbation of \(r\). Differentiating (1) on this restricted positive set gives the gradient \(\log(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/q_{\mathbf J})+\log p_{\mathbf J}\). It annihilates the kernel, so the elementary finite-dimensional equality between the row space and the orthogonal complement of the kernel supplies finite column multipliers \(\alpha_{\mathbf I}\) and row moment multipliers \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\) such that

\[
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q_{\mathbf J}}+\log p_{\mathbf J}
 =\alpha_{\mathbf I}
  +\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
 \qquad((\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T).
\]

Every column has positive mass, so its set of active incident rows is nonempty. Exponentiating this identity and imposing its column sum gives \(p_{\mathbf I}=e^{\alpha_{\mathbf I}}Z(\mathbf I)\), with \(Z\) exactly as stated. It is finite and strictly positive. Substituting the resulting column factor proves the formula for \(r\). Redundant moment equations merely allow nonunique multipliers; all asserted formulas remain valid in the ambient coordinates even when \(P\) has lower rank.

To prove convex-admissibility across face boundaries, take any positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) on a nonempty set of sources with integral barycenter \(\mathbf z=\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\). Lattice convexity gives \(\mathbf z\in D\). If a face \(F_{\mathbf K}\) contains \(\mathbf z\), the face property forces it to contain every used source. Thus every summand in \(Z(\mathbf z)\) is present in each corresponding \(Z(\mathbf I)\). On this common summand set the exponential functions, including their positive constant factors, satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(\mathbf z)
 &=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf K:q_{\mathbf K}>0\\\mathbf z\in F_{\mathbf K}}}
      \prod_{\mathbf I}
      \left(\frac{q_{\mathbf K}}{p_{\mathbf K}}
       e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)}\right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\\
 &\le\prod_{\mathbf I}
       \left(\sum_{\substack{\mathbf K:q_{\mathbf K}>0\\\mathbf z\in F_{\mathbf K}}}
          \frac{q_{\mathbf K}}{p_{\mathbf K}}
          e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)}
       \right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}
 \le\prod_{\mathbf I}Z(\mathbf I)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For completeness, the middle inequality follows by dividing, for each \(\mathbf I\), the positive summands by their sum, applying weighted arithmetic-geometric mean to each resulting family indexed by \(\mathbf K\), and summing over \(\mathbf K\). The final inequality uses the proved inclusion of the active summand sets. Thus no constant active set near a boundary has been presumed. Taking logarithms proves convex-admissibility of \(\log Z\).

On an active row support the formula for the share potential is

\[
\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)
 =\log Z(\mathbf I)
  -\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
  +\log\frac{p_{\mathbf J}}{q_{\mathbf J}}.
\]

Subtracting an affine function preserves the just-proved inequality at barycenters, so this potential is convex-admissible. The values \(-\log r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=-\log p_{\mathbf I}+\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)\) are also convex-admissible, by log-concavity of \(p\).

Here is an explicit extension argument, including all lattice zeros. For either of these two finite sets of potential values on \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\), define its lower convex roof at \(x\in F_{\mathbf J}\) as the minimum of the coefficient-weighted data over all nonnegative coefficient vectors of sum one and barycenter \(x\). The coefficient set is nonempty and compact, so the roof is finite and attained. Combining two minimizing coefficient vectors proves convexity. At each lattice point, convex-admissibility and its singleton representation prove that the roof equals the prescribed value. The roof of \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\) gives its asserted convex extension. The negative exponential of the roof of \(-\log r_{\mathbf J,\cdot}\), set to zero outside \(F_{\mathbf J}\), is a finite globally log-concave function. A segment whose two endpoints have positive function value lies in the face, where convexity of the roof proves log-concavity; otherwise the required right side is zero. There are no missing lattice zeros inside this face, since its lattice intersection is exactly the positive row support. This proves the extension claim, including lower-dimensional and singleton faces. Dividing by \(q_{\mathbf J}>0\) preserves it. Inactive rows have the zero extension directly.

For a component of mean \(\mathbf J\) within this support, convex-admissibility of \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\) gives

\[
G_\lambda(r_{\mathbf J,\cdot})-G_\lambda(p)
 =\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)
  -\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf J)\ge0.
\]

The original gap is nonnegative by convex-admissibility of \(\phi\), and a constant scaling of the row cancels in its gap. These facts prove the full original-cost comparison without an affine-independence restriction on the component.

For tensorization, let \((\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2;\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2)\) have any feasible joint array for the product source \(p\otimes p'\). The complete product support has hull \(P\times P'\), and the product of the original global extensions is a global extension of the product input, including all lattice zeros. Marginalizing to \((\mathbf X_a,\mathbf J_a)\), \(a=1,2\), produces feasible arrays \(r_a\) for the separate problems: summing the full row moment equations over the other target and source retains the corresponding marginal moment equations. The product logarithm splits, so \(G(r)=G(r_1)+G(r_2)\).

The source blocks are independent. The usual finite conditional entropy is the average entropy of conditional laws over positive conditioning events. Its chain rules follow by expanding the finite sums, and each conditional mutual information is the corresponding average relative entropy, hence nonnegative. In particular,

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(r)-I(r_1)-I(r_2)
 &=\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X_1\mid\mathbf J_1)
   +\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X_2\mid\mathbf J_2)
   -\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2\mid\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2)\\
 &=I(\mathbf X_1;\mathbf J_2\mid\mathbf J_1)
   +I(\mathbf X_2;\mathbf X_1,\mathbf J_1\mid\mathbf J_2)
 \ge0.
\tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore every product-source feasible array has objective at least \(\mathcal A_*(p)+\mathcal A_*(p')\). A product of separate minimizing arrays is feasible and attains that value, because its energies and mutual informations add. This proves value tensorization. It also proves that for every product-source minimizer both marginals minimize and equality holds in (2). More explicitly, equality in (2) requires both displayed conditional mutual informations to vanish; on each positive target row this says that the conditional source law factors as the product of the two marginal conditional source laws. No such factorization is assumed for an arbitrary feasible array.

To identify the canonical selector, for every feasible product-source array the other entropy chain rule gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(r_1)+H(r_2)-H(r)
 &=\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J_1\mid\mathbf X_1)
   +\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J_2\mid\mathbf X_2)
   -\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2\mid\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2)\\
 &=I(\mathbf J_1;\mathbf X_2\mid\mathbf X_1)
   +I(\mathbf J_2;\mathbf J_1,\mathbf X_1\mid\mathbf X_2)
 \ge0.
\tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

On the product minimizer set, both marginals minimize, so the largest possible upper bound in (3) is \(H(r^\dagger(p))+H(r^\dagger(p'))\). The product of those two canonical arrays is a minimizer and attains this bound. Strict concavity of the tie-break, already proved, therefore identifies it as \(r^\dagger(p\otimes p')\).

The equality conditions also directly exclude a hidden correlated tie-break. Vanishing of both terms in (3) gives, on every positive conditioning event,
\(\Pr(\mathbf J_1\mid\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2)=\Pr(\mathbf J_1\mid\mathbf X_1)\) and \(\Pr(\mathbf J_2\mid\mathbf J_1,\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2)=\Pr(\mathbf J_2\mid\mathbf X_2)\). The conditional target law given the source pair therefore factors; since the source law itself is \(p\otimes p'\), the entire joint array is \(r_1\otimes r_2\). Conversely a product array gives equality. Zero target rows and zero conditional target probabilities cause no exception, because conditional statements are imposed only on events of positive probability.

We now verify the seven-site example by exact arithmetic and an inequality certificate valid for every feasible competitor. The input numerators sum to \(29920+2(21222+14952+5661)=113590=N\). The three distinct adjacent strict log-concavity gaps are

\[
w_0^2-w_1^2=444833116>0,\qquad
w_1^2-w_0w_2=3009444>0,\qquad
w_2^2-w_1w_3=103424562>0.
\]

Symmetry supplies all remaining adjacent gaps. Interpolate \(\log p_i\) linearly on each unit interval in \([-3,3]\). The displayed strict inequalities make its successive slopes decreasing. The exponential of this concave interpolation, set to zero outside \([-3,3]\), is a finite globally log-concave extension with exactly the asserted lattice values.

\]

The normalization and mean checks can all be read from this finite table:

These identities prove that \(\nu_0\) and \(\nu_1\) are probability laws of means zero and one. Reflection proves the assertions for \(\nu_{-1}\). To certify optimality, define \(t_0=1\), \(t_j=2\) for \(j>0\), and \(t_j=1/2\) for \(j<0\). Let \(C_j=\sum_i a_i t_j^{i-j}\). The preceding normalization checks and symmetry give

For example, for positive \(j\), \(NC_j=2^{1-j}\sum_i A_i2^{i-1}=2^{1-j}21222\), which proves every positive-target entry of this table; negative targets follow by reflection. Hence \(C_j\le p_j\), with equality exactly for \(j\in\{-1,0,1\}\).

For each target let \(\widetilde\nu_j(i)=a_i t_j^{i-j}/C_j\), a positive probability law on all seven sites. For any probability law \(\lambda\) on these sites with mean \(j\), including laws with zero entries and the forced singleton laws at boundary targets, direct expansion gives
