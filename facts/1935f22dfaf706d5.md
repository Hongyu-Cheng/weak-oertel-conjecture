## Statement

Let a compact convex source in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, have exactly the three positive-volume integer fibers $A,K,B$ at $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. At $(0,y)$ with $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, suppose $d+1$ closed halfspaces $u_i\cdot x+\beta_i z\ge u_i\cdot y$ each attain the original raw mixed depth, have proper $A$ cuts (threshold strictly between the two support levels), and have zero $d$-volume on $B$. Suppose the nonzero $u_i$ span $\mathbb R^d$ and form a minimal positive circuit. Then for any full-dimensional centroid-zero simplex $T_0$ there is an invertible affine map fixing $z$ that sends $(0,y)$ to $(0,0)$ and the caps to $v_i\cdot X\ge z$, with $\sum_i v_i=0$ and $B'\subseteq T_0=\{X:v_i\cdot X\le1\ \forall i\}$. It preserves actual midpoint compatibility and normalized mixed depth, scaling raw mixed depth by a common positive determinant factor.

## Proof

Write $h_i=h_B(u_i)$. Zero $B$ volume means $u_i\cdot y-\beta_i\ge h_i$: if the threshold is smaller, the corresponding open halfspace meets the interior of $B$ and has positive volume. If it is larger, increase $\beta_i$ slightly. The middle trace is fixed, the $B$ trace still has zero volume, and the $A$ threshold $u_i\cdot y+\beta_i$ increases inside its proper interval. Any open slab between two such levels meets $\operatorname{int}A$, so this strictly reduces the raw cap mass. Attainment forces $\beta_i=u_i\cdot y-h_i$.

Let $T=\{x:u_i\cdot x\le h_i\ \forall i\}$. It contains the full-dimensional $B$. The positive relation among the normals makes every recession direction orthogonal to all $u_i$, hence zero. A full-dimensional bounded $d$-polytope needs at least $d+1$ facets, so with these $d+1$ inequalities $T$ is a simplex. Let $g$ be its centroid, and let $a_i=h_i-u_i\cdot g>0$. At the vertex opposite the $i$-th facet the $i$-th support deficit is $(d+1)a_i$, by averaging that vertex and the other $d$ vertices on the facet. Thus $(h_i-u_i\cdot x)/((d+1)a_i)$ are the barycentric coordinates of $T$. Their sum is one, so $\sum_i u_i/a_i=0$.

Choose an invertible linear $L$ mapping $T-g$ to $T_0$, and send $(z,x)$ to $(z,X)$ with $X=L(x-y-z(g-y))$. Set $v_i=u_i L^{-1}/a_i$, treating normals as covectors. Substitution gives $v_i\cdot X\ge z$ and $\sum_i v_i=0$. The transformed fibers are $L(A+g-2y),L(K-y),L(B-g)$, which satisfy the same midpoint inclusion, and $L(B-g)\subseteq T_0=\{v_i\cdot X\le1\ \forall i\}$. This affine bijection preserves the mixed lattice and maps containing closed halfspaces bijectively. Each continuous slice volume scales by $|\det L|$, so both total mass and every raw cap mass scale by this factor, as does raw point depth. Thus normalized depths and criticality are preserved.
