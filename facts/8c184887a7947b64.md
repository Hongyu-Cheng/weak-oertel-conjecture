## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, where $n,d\ge0$ and $n+d\ge1$, with nonempty mixed trace $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. Let $K_z$ be its integer fibers, $q$ the maximum affine dimension of a nonempty fiber, $a=\max_z\mathcal H_q(K_z)>0$, and $P_{\max}=\{z:\mathcal H_q(K_z)=a\}$. Write $g_z$ for the intrinsic centroid at each $z\in P_{\max}$ and use counting measure when $q=0$. If $z_i\in P_{\max}$ and $w=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i\in\mathbb Z^n$ with all $\lambda_i>0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, then $w\in P_{\max}$, $K_w=\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}$, and $g_w=\sum_i\lambda_i g_{z_i}$. For $q>0$ all the participating fibers have the same affine direction and are translates of one another. In particular $P_{\max}$ is lattice-complete and the hull of $\{(z,g_z):z\in P_{\max}\}$ has exactly that set as its mixed trace.

## Proof

There are finitely many nonempty integer fibers by compactness. Each has positive finite intrinsic volume in its own affine hull, since it contains a simplex of its dimension and is bounded. Fibers of dimension less than $q$ have $\mathcal H_q$ measure zero. For $q=0$ every nonempty fiber is a point, and convexity of $C$ puts the displayed combination of points in the singleton $K_w$, giving all claims at the incidence.

Let $q>0$. Convexity gives $L:=\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}\subseteq K_w$. The direction of the affine hull of $L$ is the sum of the $q$-dimensional directions of the input fibers: holding all other summands fixed shows that it contains each direction, and all differences in $L$ belong to their sum. Since $\dim\operatorname{aff}K_w\le q$, these directions coincide with the direction of $K_w$. Translate the sets into this common $q$-space and apply Brunn–Minkowski. It gives $\mathcal H_q(L)^{1/q}\ge\sum_i\lambda_i a^{1/q}=a^{1/q}$, while $\mathcal H_q(L)\le\mathcal H_q(K_w)\le a$. Thus all volumes equal $a$. Proper containment of compact convex $q$-bodies in the same affine plane strictly increases volume: strictly separate an outside point of the larger body from the smaller and take small convex mixtures of that point with an interior ball of the larger. Hence $L=K_w$.

Equality in the two-body Brunn–Minkowski inequality for positive-volume bodies implies positive homothety; the equal volumes make the dilation factor one. For more than two inputs, group one input against the normalized average of the others. Both inequalities in the iterated Brunn–Minkowski bound are equalities, and the grouped average has volume $a$. Induction and the two-body equality case then make all inputs translates. For intervals this also follows from equality of lengths. The Minkowski average of translates of a single body has centroid equal to the corresponding average of their centroids, giving $g_w$.

Every point of the centroid hull over an integer $w$ has a representation with positive coefficients after deleting zeros, so is $(w,g_w)$ with $w\in P_{\max}$. Conversely each such centroid mark is in the hull. Projection gives $\operatorname{conv}(P_{\max})\cap\mathbb Z^n=P_{\max}$.

The Brunn–Minkowski input is the same ordinary equality case for positive-volume convex bodies used in the proof of e4253a0d6f78849c, now after the intrinsic affine direction is determined.
