## Statement

Assume the hypotheses and retain the notation of accepted fact
6e66cd69e25232b7, including \(E_\omega,r_z,p_z,m_e,D_z,D_e,b_e,
\omega_e,W,h_{\max}(C)\), and

\[
 H(C):=\frac{2^nh_{\max}(C)}{W}.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(0<\alpha<1/2\), put

\[
 \psi(\alpha):=-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\log(1-\alpha),
 \qquad
 \lambda_\alpha:=\frac{\psi(\alpha)}{\log2}-1>0.
\tag{2}
\]

For every affine map
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E_\omega)\to\mathbb R^d\), define

\[
 E_{s,\alpha}:=
 \left\{z\in E_\omega:
 \|p_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}\leq\lambda_\alpha\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \alpha 2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E_{s,\alpha}}r_z.}
\tag{4}
\]

For every affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), define

\[
 F_{s,\alpha}:=
 \left\{e:\omega_e>0,
 \ \|b_e-s(m_e)\|_{D_e}\leq\lambda_\alpha\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \alpha 2^{-n}\sum_{e\in F_{s,\alpha}}\omega_e.}
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, for every \(0<\alpha<1/2\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_\omega)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{z\in E_{s,\alpha}}r_z
 \leq\frac{H(C)W}{\alpha},}
\tag{7}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{e\in F_{s,\alpha}}\omega_e
 \leq\frac{H(C)W}{\alpha}.}
\tag{8}
\]

Since \(\lambda_{1/4}>1/5\), the common fixed-scale specialization is

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_s
 \sum_{\substack{z\in E_\omega:\\
 \|p_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}\leq1/5}}r_z
 \leq4H(C)W,}
\tag{9}
\]

where \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E_\omega)\to\mathbb R^d\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_s
 \sum_{\substack{e:\omega_e>0,\\
 \|b_e-s(m_e)\|_{D_e}\leq1/5}}\omega_e
 \leq4H(C)W,}
\tag{10}
\]

where \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\).

## Proof

Accepted fact 6e66cd69e25232b7 gives, for every \(z\in E_\omega\),

\[
 r_z\leq\gamma_da_z,
 \qquad
 p_z+\lambda_\alpha D_z
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\alpha a_z}(C_z),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{11}
\]

The second inclusion is the parameterized nesting furnished by accepted
fact 11e347c295e9430b and already incorporated into the saturated
selector.  The accepted setup also makes each \(D_z\), and hence each
\(D_e=(D_z+D_{z'})/2\), origin-symmetric.

Fix \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E_\omega)\to\mathbb R^d\).  If
\(E_{s,\alpha}=\varnothing\), (4) is immediate.  Otherwise, for every
\(z\in E_{s,\alpha}\), origin symmetry and (3) give

\[
 s(z)\in p_z+\lambda_\alpha D_z
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\alpha a_z}(C_z).
\tag{12}
\]

Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d applied with metronoid mass
\(\alpha a_z\) gives

\[
 \delta_C(z,s(z))
 \geq\gamma_d\alpha a_z
 \geq\alpha r_z.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the data

\[
 E=E_{s,\alpha},
 \qquad q_z=s(z),
 \qquad w_z=\alpha r_z,
 \qquad s|_{\operatorname{aff}(E)}
\tag{14}
\]

are admissible in the definition of \(R_{\rm sel}(C)\) from accepted fact
be359f8a6c628483.  Their residual vectors are all zero, so their residual
rank is zero.  With
\(\rho_{s,\alpha}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E_{s,\alpha})\leq n\), that
definition and the accepted inequality
\(h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}R_{\rm sel}(C)\) give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{\max}(C)
 &\geq
 2^{-n}2^{n-\rho_{s,\alpha}}
 \alpha\sum_{z\in E_{s,\alpha}}r_z\\
 &=\alpha2^{-\rho_{s,\alpha}}
 \sum_{z\in E_{s,\alpha}}r_z\\
 &\geq\alpha2^{-n}
 \sum_{z\in E_{s,\alpha}}r_z.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

This proves (4), including the residual-rank-zero application.

We next prove (6) by a separate midpoint measure.  Fix an affine
\(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and suppose first that
\(F_{s,\alpha}\ne\varnothing\).  For each
\(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_{s,\alpha}\), origin symmetry and (5) give

\[
 s(m_e)-b_e\in\lambda_\alpha D_e
 =\frac{\lambda_\alpha}{2}(D_z+D_{z'}).
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore one can choose \(u_{e,z}\in D_z\) and
\(u_{e,z'}\in D_{z'}\) such that

\[
 s(m_e)-b_e
 =\frac{\lambda_\alpha}{2}
 (u_{e,z}+u_{e,z'}).
\tag{17}
\]

Set

\[
 y_{e,z}:=p_z+\lambda_\alpha u_{e,z},
 \qquad
 y_{e,z'}:=p_{z'}+\lambda_\alpha u_{e,z'}.
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (11), (17), and the definition
\(b_e=(p_z+p_{z'})/2\) give

\[
 y_{e,z}\in\mathsf M_{\alpha a_z}(C_z),
 \qquad
 y_{e,z'}\in\mathsf M_{\alpha a_{z'}}(C_{z'}),
 \qquad
 s(m_e)=\frac{y_{e,z}+y_{e,z'}}2.
\tag{19}
\]

In particular, the full endpoint points

\[
 \widehat y_{e,z}:=(z,y_{e,z}),
 \qquad
 \widehat y_{e,z'}:=(z',y_{e,z'})
\tag{20}
\]

belong to \(C\).  The endpoints have the same parity, so
\(m_e=(z+z')/2\in\mathbb Z^n\).  Convexity of \(C\) and (19) therefore
show that

\[
 (m_e,s(m_e))
 =\frac{\widehat y_{e,z}+\widehat y_{e,z'}}2
 \in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{21}
\]

Define the positive finite measure

\[
 \sigma_{s,\alpha}
 :=\alpha\sum_{e\in F_{s,\alpha}}
 \omega_e\delta_{(m_e,s(m_e))}.
\tag{22}
\]

We verify halfspace domination literally.  Let \(H\) be a closed ambient
halfspace.  For every \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_{s,\alpha}\) whose midpoint atom
lies in \(H\), at least one of the two endpoint points in (20) lies in
\(H\), because their average is the midpoint atom.  Choose one such
endpoint and orient \(e\) toward it.  This remains valid when the midpoint
is on the boundary of \(H\).  Put

\[
 R_z(H):=
 \alpha\sum_{\substack{e\in F_{s,\alpha}:\\
 (m_e,s(m_e))\in H,\ e\ {\rm oriented\ toward}\ z}}
 \omega_e.
\tag{23}
\]

Each midpoint charge is assigned exactly once, so

\[
 \sigma_{s,\alpha}(H)=\sum_zR_z(H).
\tag{24}
\]

The charge entering one fiber is bounded by the available endpoint load:

\[
 R_z(H)
 \leq\alpha\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e
 =\alpha r_z
 \leq\gamma_d\alpha a_z.
\tag{25}
\]

If \(R_z(H)>0\), the fiber section
\(H_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\) contains every endpoint witness oriented toward
\(z\).  If \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace, choose any one of those
witnesses.  Equations (19) and accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d give

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \geq\gamma_d\alpha a_z
 \geq R_z(H).
\tag{26}
\]

This is one depth bound for the fiber section against its total charge;
no individual depth bounds are added.  If \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), then
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)=a_z\geq R_z(H)\) by (25).  The section
cannot be empty when \(R_z(H)>0\).  Summing once over the fibers and using
(24) yields

\[
 \mu_C(H)
 \geq\sum_zR_z(H)
 =\sigma_{s,\alpha}(H).
\tag{27}
\]

The same calculation covers the whole-space halfspace, if it is included
in the convention.  Thus \(\sigma_{s,\alpha}\) is halfspace-dominated by
\(\mu_C\).

By (21), this nonzero measure is carried by the mixed-integer set.  Its
support lies on the graph of \(s\), so the residual rank in the
dominated-measure theorem of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 is zero.
If

\[
 \rho^{\rm edge}_{s,\alpha}:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:e\in F_{s,\alpha}\}\leq n,
\tag{28}
\]

that theorem gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{\max}(C)
 &\geq
 \frac{|\sigma_{s,\alpha}|}
 {2^{\rho^{\rm edge}_{s,\alpha}}}\\
 &=\alpha2^{-\rho^{\rm edge}_{s,\alpha}}
 \sum_{e\in F_{s,\alpha}}\omega_e\\
 &\geq\alpha2^{-n}
 \sum_{e\in F_{s,\alpha}}\omega_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

If \(F_{s,\alpha}=\varnothing\), (6) is immediate.  Hence (6) holds in
all cases.

Multiplying (4) and (6) by \(2^n\), and using (1), gives (7) and (8).
Accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b gives

\[
 1+\lambda_{1/4}
 =\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}>\frac65,
\tag{30}
\]

so \(\lambda_{1/4}>1/5\).  The radius-\(1/5\) endpoint and edge tubes are
subsets of their radius-\(\lambda_{1/4}\) tubes.  Substituting
\(\alpha=1/4\) in (7) and (8) proves (9) and (10).
