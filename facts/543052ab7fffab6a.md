## Statement

Let $m\ge3$ be an integer. Let $X_3\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(3,1)$, $Y_m\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1)$, and let $E,E_0$ be mean-one exponentials, with all four variables independent. The second gamma parameter denotes scale. For $p\in[0,1]$, define
$$
P_m(p)=\Pr\!\left(X_3+pE\ge E_0+\frac{2+p}{m}Y_m\right).
$$
Then $P_m(p)\ge\min\{P_m(0),P_m(1)\}$, with strict inequality whenever $0<p<1$.

## Proof

**Density and derivative.** Set $b=(2+p)/m$, $T=E+Y_m$, $U=E/T$, $W=X_3-E_0$ and $Z=W/T$. The joint density after the change $(E,Y_m)=(tu,t(1-u))$ is $t^me^{-t}(1-u)^{m-1}/(m-1)!$ on $t>0$, $0<u<1$. It factors as the density of $\operatorname{Gamma}(m+1,1)$ times that of $\operatorname{Beta}(1,m)$, so $U$ is independent of $(T,W)$ and of $Z$. The comparison is $Z+(p+b)U\ge b$. The beta tail gives
$$
P_m(p)=\Pr(Z\ge b)+\int_{-p}^b \left(\frac{p+z}{p+b}\right)^m f_Z(z)\,dz .
$$
Direct convolution gives $f_W(w)=\frac{e^w}{2}\int_{\max\{w,0\}}^\infty x^2e^{-2x}\,dx$, which is $e^w/8$ for $w<0$ and $e^{-w}(1+2w+2w^2)/8$ for $w\ge0$. Using $f_Z(z)=\mathbb E[Tf_W(Tz)]$ and integrating gamma powers gives
$$
f_Z(z)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{m+1}{8(1-z)^{m+2}},&z<0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{m+1}{8}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^2
 \dfrac{2^j\binom{m+j+1}{j}z^j}{(1+z)^{m+j+2}},&z\ge0 .
\end{cases}
$$
In the second line the coefficients come from $\int_0^\infty t^{m+j+1}e^{-(1+z)t}\,dt=(m+j+1)!/(1+z)^{m+j+2}$ and the coefficients $1,2,2$ of the convolution polynomial. Differentiation on $0<p<1$ is justified by continuity of the density and the integrand and its parameter derivative on the finite moving range, locally in $p$. The upper endpoint term cancels the derivative of the tail and the lower endpoint term vanishes. Thus
$$
P_m'(p)=\frac{I(p)}{(p+b)^{m+1}},\qquad
I(p)=\int_{-p}^b (p+z)^{m-1}\,[2-(m+1)z] f_Z(z)\,dz .
$$

**Exact primitives.** For $-p\le z\le0$, use $t=(p+z)/(1-z)$, so $z=(t-p)/(1+t)$, $dz=(1+p)(1+t)^{-2}\,dt$ and $0\le t\le p$. The transformed integrand is $(m+1)t^{m-1}[2+(m+1)p-(m-1)t]/[8(1+p)^2]$. Its integral is
$$
\frac{p^m[2(m+1)+(3m+1)p]}{8m(1+p)^2}.
$$
For $0\le z\le b$, use $t=(p+z)/(1+z)$, so $z=(t-p)/(1-t)$, $dz=(1-p)(1-t)^{-2}\,dt$ and $p\le t\le T_*=N/(m+p+2)<1$, where $N=(m+1)p+2$. The inequality $T_*<1$ follows from $m+p+2-N=m(1-p)>0$. Substitution into the positive density gives the transformed integrand
$$
\frac{(m+1)t^{m-1}}{8(1-p)^4}
 [2+(m+1)p-(m+3)t]S(t),
\quad
S(t)=(1-p)^2+2(m+2)(1-p)(t-p)+2(m+2)(m+3)(t-p)^2 .
$$
Let $C_j$ be the coefficient of $t^j$ in $[2+(m+1)p-(m+3)t]S(t)$ and set $Q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^3 C_jt^j/(m+j)$. Then $t^mQ(t)$ is the primitive of $t^{m-1}[2+(m+1)p-(m+3)t]S(t)$. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
A(p)={}&(2m^2+12m+17)p^4+(10m^2+52m+62)p^3
       +(9m^2+36m+45)p^2\\
      &-(12m^2+36m+20)p+3m^2+8m+4,\\
D(p)={}&p^2+(2m+4)p+m+4 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Expanding the cubic in the primitive and evaluating at its endpoints gives the rational identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(T_*)&=\frac{N^2 A(p)}{m(m+1)(m+p+2)^3},\\
\frac{2(m+1)+(3m+1)p}{8m(1+p)^2}
-\frac{(m+1)Q(p)}{8(1-p)^4}
&=-\frac{2p^3D(p)}{m(1-p)^4(1+p)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Multiplying the first by $(m+1)T_*^m/[8(1-p)^4]$ and the second by $p^m$ yields
$$
I(p)=\frac{N^{m+2}A(p)}{8m(1-p)^4(m+p+2)^{m+3}}
 -\frac{2p^{m+3}D(p)}{m(1-p)^4(1+p)^2}.
$$

**Positivity and sign certificate.** Write $A=m^2F+mG+H$, where
$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=2p^4+10p^3+9p^2-12p+3,\\
G&=12p^4+52p^3+36p^2-36p+8,\\
H&=17p^4+62p^3+45p^2-20p+4.
\end{aligned}
$$
On $p\ge0$, the second derivatives are bounded below by $18,72,90$, respectively. For a second derivative bounded below by $2a>0$, the quadratic lower bound at $s$ has minimum $F(s)-F'(s)^2/(4a)$ when applied to $F$, and likewise for $G,H$. At $s=3/8$ for $F$ the bound is $681/2048-(-39/64)^2/36=5279/16384$. At $s=1/4$ for $G$ it is $135/64-(15/2)^2/144=55/32$. At $s=0$ for $H$ it is $4-20^2/180=16/9$. These are positive, so $A(p)>0$ on the nonnegative axis. There $D(p)>0$, $N>0$, $1+p>0$ and $m+p+2>0$. On $0<p<1$ the factors $p$, $p+b$ and $1-p$ are positive as well. In particular the first term in $I$ is positive, and $P_m'$ has the sign of $1-R$, where
$$
R(p)=\frac{16p^{m+3}(m+p+2)^{m+3}D(p)}
{(1+p)^2 N^{m+2} A(p)} .
$$
The logarithmic derivative is $(m+3)/p+(m+3)/(m+p+2)+D'/D-2/(1+p)-(m+2)(m+1)/N-A'/A$. Clearing its positive denominator on $0<p<1$ and factoring gives
$$
\frac{R'}R=\frac{(p-1)^3 K_m(p)}{p(1+p)(m+p+2)N D(p) A(p)},
$$
where the coefficients $[p^j]K_m$ are
$$
\begin{array}{c|l}
j &[p^j]K_m\\ \hline
0&-6m^5-70m^4-308m^3-632m^2-592m-192\\
1&-15m^5-173m^4-762m^3-1588m^2-1512m-480\\
2&3m^5-7m^4-260m^3-1006m^2-1308m-480\\
3&34m^5+249m^4+594m^3+369m^2-298m-240\\
4&22m^5+189m^4+558m^3+643m^2+180m-60\\
5&4m^5+44m^4+164m^3+241m^2+111m-6\\
6&2m^4+14m^3+29m^2+17m .
\end{array}
$$
For $m\ge1$ the degree-three coefficient is at least $(34+249+594+369-298-240)m^2=708m^2$, since powers $m^j$ with $j\ge2$ are at least $m^2$ and $m,1\le m^2$. The degree-four and degree-five coefficients are at least $(643-60)m^2$ and $(241-6)m^2$, respectively. The coefficient signs for $m\ge3$ are therefore negative, negative, unrestricted, positive, positive, positive, positive. Descartes' rule of signs gives at most one positive root, counting multiplicity. Since the constant is negative and the leading coefficient positive, there is a positive root $c$, which is simple, with $K_m<0$ on $(0,c)$ and $K_m>0$ on $(c,\infty)$. Summing the displayed coefficients gives $K_m(1)=6(m-3)(m+3)^2(7m^2+18m+9)$, so $c=1$ if $m=3$ and $0<c<1$ if $m>3$.

**Boundary and strictness.** The ratio extends continuously to $p=0,1$. At zero, $A(0)=(m+2)(3m+2)>0$ and $N(0)=2$, so $R(0)=0$. At one, $N(1)=m+3$, $D(1)=3(m+3)$ and $A(1)=12(m+3)^2=4(m+3)D(1)$, so $R(1)=1$. Since $(p-1)^3<0$ inside the interval, $R$ increases strictly up to $c$ and decreases strictly on the portion of $(0,1)$ after $c$. If $m=3$, then $R<1$ throughout $(0,1)$, giving $P_m'>0$. If $m>3$, strict decrease from $c$ to $1$ gives $R(c)>1$. Thus $R$ crosses $1$ exactly once on $(0,c)$, with $R<1$ before the crossing and $R>1$ afterward in $(0,1)$. This gives strict increase of $P_m$ before the crossing and strict decrease afterward.

The probability is continuous on the entire closed interval by coupling the same four variables and applying dominated convergence to the indicators. At every fixed $p$ the signed sum has no atom at zero, by conditioning on the variables other than $E_0$. This also covers the zero positive coefficient at $p=0$ and the case $b=1$ at $(m,p)=(3,1)$. Strict increase from $0$, or strict decrease toward $1$, makes every interior value strictly larger than at least one endpoint. At $p=0$ the probability is the saturated positive law with three positive units and $b=2/m$. At $p=1$ it has four positive units and $b=3/m$, since $X_3+E$ has shape four. When $m=3$, that endpoint is $1/2$ by symmetry of the two independent shape-four gamma variables.
