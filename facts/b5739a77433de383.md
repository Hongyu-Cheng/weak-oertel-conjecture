## Proof

The aliases (1)-(4), including the ordered-pair and diagonal conventions, are exactly the degree-tag definitions in accepted fact `d5d5de35c3f25af9`. Positivity of \(b_t\) implies \(M_{\xi(t)}>0\), so every denominator in (3), (4), and (12) is positive. Dividing (3) by \(b_t\) proves (17). Substitution of (4), \(r+s=2t\), \(x_t=q_t+h_t\), and (17) gives (18).

We next establish the source-capacity ledger. Accepted fact `35ca05c7683b7d12`, under the same degree-tag aliases, proves
\[
 c_r+D_0\subseteq\mathsf M_{w_r}(C_r)
 \qquad(r\in A).
\tag{41}
\]
Fix \(r\in A_\xi\). Equations (12), (15), and \(\omega_t\le b_t\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_r
 &\le
 \frac1{2M_\xi}
 \sum_{\substack{t\in F,\ \xi(t)=\xi\\(a,b)\in\mathcal P_t}}
 w_aw_b\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{a=r\}}+\mathbf1_{\{b=r\}}\bigr)\\
 &\le
 \frac1{2M_\xi}
 \sum_{s\in A_\xi}
 \bigl(w_rw_s+w_sw_r\bigr)
 =w_r.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
In the second line every ordered pair in \(A_\xi^2\) is used at its unique integral midpoint, and enlarging from the retained targets to all ordered pairs can only increase the sum. If \(r=s\), the two indicators in the first line equal two; this is exactly the two tagged occurrences of that diagonal pair. This proves the load bound in (19). Summing (15) over the two occurrences and then over each cell, while using (17), gives
\[
 \sum_rL_r
 =\sum_{t\in F}\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t}
 p_{t;r,s}\omega_t
 =\Omega,
\]
which completes (19).

For every occurrence \(o\) of positive load, (14) and (41) give
\(y_o\in\mathsf M_{w_{z_o}}(C_{z_o})\). Choose one representing density \(f_o\), so \(0\le f_o\le1\), its mass is \(w_{z_o}>0\), and its normalized first moment is \(y_o\). Put
\[
 g_o:=\frac{\ell_o}{w_{z_o}}f_o.
\tag{43}
\]
For a zero-load occurrence put \(g_o:=0\). Equations (19) and (43) give
\[
 \sum_{o:z_o=r}g_o
 \le\sum_{o:z_o=r}\frac{\ell_o}{w_r}
 =\frac{L_r}{w_r}
 \le1
\]
pointwise. Mass and first-moment scaling prove (20)-(22). Thus repeated sources, repeated target cells, and diagonal occurrences consume only their total scalar load.

We now transport the stress. Since \(D_0\) is compact and origin-symmetric and \(\widehat\eta_t=\eta_t\circ\pi\),
\[
 h_{D_0}(\widehat\eta_t)
 =h_{\pi(D_0)}(\eta_t)
 =\omega_t.
\tag{44}
\]
Positive homogeneity of the support function and (15) prove (23), including the case \(\omega_t=0\).

Accepted fact `913e5fd1657385d5` gives
\[
 \sum_{t\in F}\eta_t=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t\in F}t\otimes\eta_t=0.
\tag{45}
\]
Pulling back the covectors through \(\pi\), using (17), and using
\((r+s)/2=t\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\theta_o
 &=\sum_{t\in F}\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t}
 p_{t;r,s}\widehat\eta_t
 =\sum_{t\in F}\widehat\eta_t=0,\\
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal O}z_o\otimes\theta_o
 &=\sum_{t\in F}\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t}
 p_{t;r,s}\frac{r+s}{2}\otimes\widehat\eta_t\\
 &=\sum_{t\in F}t\otimes\widehat\eta_t=0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]
This proves (24), again with diagonal pairs contributing two equal half-occurrences.

The support-contact relation in `913e5fd1657385d5` says
\[
 \eta_t(\pi(x_t))
 =h_{\pi(q_t)+\pi(D_0)}(\eta_t),
 \qquad
 \sum_{t\in F}h_{\pi(q_t)+\pi(D_0)}(\eta_t)
 =-\Delta_H.
\tag{47}
\]
Applying \(\widehat\eta_t\) to the continuous coordinate of (18) and summing proves
\[
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\theta_o(y_o)
 =\sum_{t\in F}\widehat\eta_t(x_t)
 =-\Delta_H,
\]
which is (25). Since \(x_t=q_t+h_t\) and (47) is a support equality,
\[
 \widehat\eta_t(h_t)=h_{D_0}(\widehat\eta_t)=\omega_t.
\tag{48}
\]
The total shift contribution in (25) is therefore
\[
 \sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\theta_o(h_t)
 =\sum_{t\in F}\omega_t=\Omega.
\tag{49}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sum_{r\in A}\zeta_r(c_r)
 =-\Delta_H-\Omega.
\tag{50}
\]

Subadditivity of \(h_{D_0}\), followed by (23), gives
\[
 h_{D_0}(\zeta_r)
 \le\sum_{o:z_o=r}h_{D_0}(\theta_o)
 =L_r.
\tag{51}
\]
Summing proves (27). Equations (28) are the regrouped versions of (24). Using
\[
 h_{c_r+D_0}(\zeta_r)
 =\zeta_r(c_r)+h_{D_0}(\zeta_r),
\]
then substituting (50) and (26), proves the equality and inequalities in (29).

For completeness, fix any affine map \(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\). Equations (28) imply
\[
 \sum_r\zeta_r(a(r))=0.
\tag{52}
\]
For \(L_r>0\), the polar inequality for the \(D_0\)-norm gives
\[
 \zeta_r(a(r))-h_{c_r+D_0}(\zeta_r)
 \le
 h_{D_0}(\zeta_r)
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_0}(a(r),c_r+D_0)
 \le
 L_r\operatorname{dist}_{D_0}(a(r),c_r+D_0).
\tag{53}
\]
If \(L_r=0\), then (51) and full dimensionality of \(D_0\) force
\(\zeta_r=0\), so that index contributes zero. Sum (53), use (52), and take the infimum over \(a\). Equation (29) then gives (31).

To prove (32), fix \(r\) with \(L_r>0\) and any \(y\in c_r+D_0\). By (41), choose a representing density of mass \(w_r\) with barycenter \(y\). Scaling it by \(L_r/w_r\le1\) preserves the barycenter and gives an admissible density of mass \(L_r\). Hence \(y\in\mathsf M_{L_r}(C_r)\). Since \(y\) was arbitrary, (32) follows. No zero-mass metronoid is used.

It remains to audit the signs and caps. Every \(t\in F\) is a strict global miss in accepted fact `a4f0338595a3a00b`, and (10) lies in the retained metronoid tube. Thus
\[
 \Phi(t,x_t)<0.
\tag{54}
\]
Equation (18) shows that (54) is the \(p_{t;r,s}\)-weighted average of
\[
 \frac{\Phi(r,c_r+h_t)+\Phi(s,c_s+h_t)}2
\]
over the ordered pairs in \(\mathcal P_t\). This identity alone does not force either endpoint of an individual pair into \(H\).

For a physical source \(r\), put
\[
 L_{H,r}^{\mathrm{in}}
 :=\sum_{\substack{o:z_o=r\\\Phi(r,y_o)\ge0}}\ell_o,
\qquad
 G_r^{\mathrm{in}}
 :=\sum_{\substack{o:z_o=r\\\Phi(r,y_o)\ge0}}g_o.
\tag{55}
\]
If \(L_{H,r}^{\mathrm{in}}=0\), the desired cap inequality at \(r\) is automatic. Otherwise (20)-(22) show that
\[
 0\le G_r^{\mathrm{in}}\le1,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_r}G_r^{\mathrm{in}}=L_{H,r}^{\mathrm{in}}.
\tag{56}
\]
Its normalized first moment is the \(\ell_o\)-weighted average of the inside occurrence points \(y_o\). The fiber halfspace \(H_r\) is convex, so this barycenter lies in \(H_r\). Hence it belongs to
\[
 \mathsf M_{L_{H,r}^{\mathrm{in}}}(C_r)\cap H_r.
\tag{57}
\]
The continuous normal of \(H_r\) is the nonzero \(v\), so \(H_r\) is a proper closed halfspace. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applied to (57) gives
\[
 \gamma_dL_{H,r}^{\mathrm{in}}
 \le\operatorname{vol}_d(C_r\cap H_r).
\tag{58}
\]
Sum (58) over the distinct physical sources. Their cap volumes are disjoint fiber summands of the actual ambient halfspace, so (7) yields
\[
 \gamma_dL_H^{\mathrm{in}}
 \le\mu_C(H)
 <\frac{Q}{100\,2^n}.
\]
This proves (37), including all boundary occurrences because (33) uses the closed halfspace.

Each crossing pair has exactly one inside endpoint occurrence and therefore contributes half of its pair load to \(L_H^{\mathrm{in}}\). This proves (38). More generally, every pair that is not outside-outside has at least one inside endpoint and therefore has pair load at most twice its contribution to \(L_H^{\mathrm{in}}\). Since (17) and (35) give total pair load
\[
 \sum_{t\in F}\sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t}\lambda_{t;r,s}
 =\Omega,
\tag{59}
\]
we obtain
\[
 L_H^{\mathrm{out,out}}
 \ge\Omega-2L_H^{\mathrm{in}}
 >\frac{1077}{6400}Q-\frac{Q}{50\gamma_d2^n}.
\tag{60}
\]
The common-normal chain in `a4f0338595a3a00b` gives
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac4{11},
 \qquad
 2^n\ge2.
\tag{61}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac1{50\gamma_d2^n}<\frac{11}{400}
 =\frac{176}{6400}.
\]
Substitution in (60) proves (39). A diagonal pair is either inside-inside or outside-outside and is never crossing; its two half-occurrences make all preceding comparisons exact.

If \(n\ge3\), then \(2^n\ge8\). Equations (38) and (61) give
\[
 L_H^{\mathrm{cross}}
 <\frac{Q}{50(4/11)8}
 =\frac{11}{1600}Q
 <\frac{Q}{100},
\]
which is (40). The construction stops at the explicitly stated source defect and outside-outside load, so none of the disclaimed terminal conclusions has been used.
