## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-branch of accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70` and `7ec63df101c30343`. Thus, with the canonical normalizations,
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^n h_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D<\frac Q{100},
\]
and the actual transverse triangle construction has masses
\[
 \Theta_{\xi,c}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
 \qquad
 \Theta_I(E):=\sum_{c\in E}\sum_\xi\Theta_{\xi,c},
\]
with
\[
 \Theta_I(\mathbb Z^n)>\frac{3Q}{100}.
\]

Let \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be a nonempty set on which
\(\Theta_I(\{c\})>0\) for every \(c\in E\). At each \(c\in E\), choose one retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing \(\Theta_{\xi,c}\), and put
\[
 \widehat\theta_c:=\Theta_{\xi(c),c},
 \qquad q_c:=q^\circ_{\xi(c),c}.
\]
Define
\[
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)
\]
and
\[
 r_E:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-s(c):c\in E\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \ge
 \frac{4\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\,\Theta_I(E)}
 \tag{1}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge
 \frac{8\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\,\Theta_I(E).}
 \tag{2}
\]

Consequently, suppose all positive \(I\)-triangle target mass is supported on \(E\), so that \(\Theta_I(E)=\Theta_I(\mathbb Z^n)\). Then no canonical hard \(I\)-branch configuration can satisfy
\[
 2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)\le 24\gamma_d.
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular, such a configuration cannot have \(\rho_E\le2\) and \(r_E\le1\). This includes the case in which all positive target mass lies in one affine target plane and its heaviest-label selected centers have continuous residual rank at most one.

## Proof

For \(c\in E\), abbreviate \(\xi=\xi(c)\). There are at most \(2^n\) parity labels at the physical target \(c\), so maximality gives
\[
 \widehat\theta_c
 \ge 2^{-n}\sum_\xi\Theta_{\xi,c}
 =2^{-n}\Theta_I(\{c\}).
 \tag{4}
\]
Only one label and one point are selected in each physical target fiber; no target densities from different labels are added.

Accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` gives \(t_e\le s_e/2\). With the canonical quantity
\[
 S_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
\]
this implies
\[
 0<\widehat\theta_c\le\frac12S_{\xi,c}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives the actual single-label target-fiber inclusion
\[
 q_c+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq
 \mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c).
 \tag{6}
\]
Put \(m_c:=4\widehat\theta_c\). Equation (5) gives \(m_c\le2S_{\xi,c}\). In particular, \(q_c\in\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c)\). If \(f_c\) is a representing density for this membership, then
\[
 \widetilde f_c:=\frac{m_c}{2S_{\xi,c}}f_c
\]
still satisfies \(0\le\widetilde f_c\le1\), has mass \(m_c\), and has normalized barycenter \(q_c\). Hence
\[
 q_c\in\mathsf M_{m_c}(C_c).
 \tag{7}
\]
Accepted fact `07889c9b4686d8b4` gives
\(\widehat\theta_c\le a_c/16\), where
\(a_c=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_c)>0\). Thus
\[
 0<m_c\le\frac{a_c}{4}<a_c.
 \tag{8}
\]

Apply accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` to (7). Every closed fiber halfspace containing \(q_c\) has \(C_c\)-volume at least
\[
 w_c:=\gamma_dm_c=4\gamma_d\widehat\theta_c.
 \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, in the notation of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`,
\[
 0<w_c\le\delta_C(c,q_c).
 \tag{10}
\]
The finite family of distinct physical bases \(c\in E\), selected points \(q_c\in C_c\), and weights \(w_c\) is therefore admissible in the exact definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) from accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. The possible residual dimensions are integers in \(\{0,\ldots,d\}\), so the minimum defining \(r_E\) is attained; choose an attaining affine map. Equations (4) and (9) then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 &\ge
 2^{n-\rho_E}\frac{\sum_{c\in E}w_c}{r_E+1}\\
 &=
 2^{n-\rho_E}\frac{4\gamma_d\sum_{c\in E}\widehat\theta_c}{r_E+1}\\
 &\ge
 2^{n-\rho_E}\frac{4\gamma_d2^{-n}\Theta_I(E)}{r_E+1}\\
 &=
 \frac{4\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\Theta_I(E),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (1).

Accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge2^{-n}R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C),
\]
and therefore
\[
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)\ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).
 \tag{11}
\]
In the canonical definition from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) and \(D\) are two distinct nonnegative summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\). Equation (11) relates their sizes but does not identify the summands. Consequently it is legitimate to retain both terms:
\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+D
 \ge2R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).
 \tag{12}
\]
Combining (12) with (1) proves (2).

Now assume that \(E\) supports all positive \(I\)-triangle target mass and that (3) holds. By (2) and the strict hard-branch triangle-mass bound from accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`,
\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge
 \frac{8\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\Theta_I(E)
 \ge\frac13\Theta_I(E)
 >\frac Q{100},
\]
contradicting the hard hypothesis \(\mathcal A(C)<Q/100\).

Finally,
\[
 \gamma_d=\frac1{(1+1/d)^d}>\frac13.
 \tag{13}
\]
Indeed, the binomial theorem gives
\[
 (1+1/d)^d
 =\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk d^{-k}
 \le\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3,
\]
where the last inequality follows from
\(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\), with strict inequality for some term or from truncation. If \(\rho_E\le2\) and \(r_E\le1\), then
\[
 2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)\le8<24\gamma_d.
\]
Thus (3) holds, completing the quantified low-rank exclusion. The proof uses only the physical target bases and their selected target-fiber points; it never identifies the target plane with a source triangle chart, forms an integral packet from the residual coordinate, or sums incompatible packet energies.
