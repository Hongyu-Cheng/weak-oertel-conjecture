## Statement

Let $v_1,v_2,v_3$ span $\mathbb R^2$ and sum to zero, and put $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. Let $A,K,B$ be planar convex bodies with $B\subseteq T$, $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, and each line $v_i\cdot x=-1$ meeting $\operatorname{int}A$. Write $V=|T|$, $a=|A|$, $b=|B|$, $k=|K|$ and assume $h_K(-v_i)\le1+\eta$ for all $i$, $0\le\eta\le1/2$. Then
$
b/V<f(\eta)=1-(1-2\eta)^2/3.
$
If also $a<b/32$, $k\le a+b$ and $k\ge(1-\xi)V$, then $\xi>5/16-(11/8)\eta+(11/8)\eta^2$. In particular the sandwich $(1-\eta)(-T)\subseteq K\subseteq(1+\eta)(-T)$ cannot hold together with $a<b/32$ and $k\le a+b$ when $0\le\eta\le(27-\sqrt{539})/38$.

If the three bodies are the measured fibers over $-1,0,1$ and the three caps $v_i\cdot x\ge z$ have a common mixed mass $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$, then $k\le a+b$ is automatic.

## Proof

Properness gives $\alpha_i=h_A(-v_i)>1$, hence each upper barycentric bound from exact-actual-simplex-support-erosion is strictly below $c_*=(2+2\eta)/3$. The upper bounds $c_*$ in all three coordinates remove disjoint corner regions because $c_*\ge2/3$, giving relative volume $1-3(1-c_*)^2=f(\eta)$. The actual enclosure has strictly smaller area: take one coordinate strictly between $\max(0,c_i)$ and $c_*$, sufficiently near $c_*$, with the other two equal to half the remainder. This point and a relative open neighborhood lie in the reference enclosure and outside the actual one. This works at $c_*=1$ by choosing the first coordinate strictly below one. Nonpositive bounds already preclude a full-dimensional $B$. Thus $b<f(\eta)V$.

The area hypotheses give $k<(33/32)b$, so $1-\xi<(33/32)f(\eta)$. Expanding gives the asserted strict deficit. In the sandwich case $k\ge(1-\eta)^2V$ and the upper sandwich implies the support bounds. Combining the two area bounds would require $(32/33)(1-\eta)^2<f(\eta)$, equivalent to $5-54\eta+38\eta^2<0$. Its smaller root is $(27-\sqrt{539})/38\in(0,1/2)$, and the polynomial is nonnegative from zero through that root.

In the cap configuration the three closed traces at $z=0$ cover the plane by balance, so one contains the centroid of $K$. Fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d at the full middle mass gives that middle cap area at least $4k/9$. Its mixed cap includes that area and has mass $Q$, so $4k/9\le Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$ yields $k\le a+b$.
