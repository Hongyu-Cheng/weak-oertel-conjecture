## Statement

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite loopless oriented multigraph. Edges are distinct labeled objects, parallel edges are allowed, and every vertex has underlying undirected degree at most three, counting parallel edges separately. Write \(N:=|E|\), let \(\operatorname{comp}(G)\) count all connected components, including isolated vertices, and put \(r:=|V|-\operatorname{comp}(G)\) and \(n:=N-r\). Let \(\widehat A\) be the full incidence matrix, with \(-1\) at a tail and \(+1\) at a head. One may equivalently use the matrix \(A\) obtained by deleting one vertex row per component. Define
\[
D:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E}:\widehat A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},\qquad
Q:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Q}
\quad(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^{E}).
\]
These are the exact original \(D,Q,p_{\mathbf z},\boldsymbol\theta\) conventions of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2; our \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is exactly the tilt called \(\boldsymbol\ell\) in accepted fact 5a3fad9597efaf26. For \(N\ge1\), use the actual closed-halfspace quantities
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=
\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{E}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\]
with nonzero halfspace normals. These are exactly \(\tau_p,M_3\) of 7781e8f7b246c4d2, not depths of a conditional or surrogate law. The count \(n\) is the intrinsic real and integer kernel rank; in particular \(D\) has affine dimension \(n\).

An undirected cycle means the edge set of a nonempty connected 2-regular subgraph, with its incident vertices. Thus a pair of parallel edges is a cycle; rotations or reversal do not produce new cycles, while different edge sets do. Let \(\mathcal C(G)\) be this finite set and let \(\mathcal P(G)\) consist of the collections of pairwise vertex-disjoint members of \(\mathcal C(G)\), including the empty collection. For each \(C\in\mathcal C(G)\), choose either coherent traversal and let \(\mathbf h_C\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E}\) be its signed circulation relative to the fixed edge orientations, extended by zero outside \(C\). Put \(L_C:=\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf h_C\) and \(a_C:=2\cosh L_C\). These are cycle tilts and activities, not the reflected-cap lower count \(L_I\) of 5a3fad9597efaf26.

There is an exact bijection
\[
(\mathcal K,\boldsymbol\varepsilon)
\longmapsto
\sum_{C\in\mathcal K}\varepsilon_C\mathbf h_C,
\qquad
\mathcal K\in\mathcal P(G),\quad
\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{\mathcal K},
\]
onto \(D\). The empty collection gives \(\mathbf0\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\sum_{\mathcal K\in\mathcal P(G)}\prod_{C\in\mathcal K}a_C,\qquad
|D|=\sum_{\mathcal K\in\mathcal P(G)}2^{|\mathcal K|},\\
\mathbb P\{\text{support collection}=\mathcal K\}
&=\frac{\prod_{C\in\mathcal K}a_C}{Q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Conditional on this support collection, cycle orientations are independent, with probabilities \(\mathbb P\{\varepsilon_C=\pm1\mid\mathcal K\}=e^{\pm L_C}/(2\cosh L_C)\). This conditional orientation law does not assert independent cycle occupancy. Two distinct cycles conflict exactly when they share a vertex. In a subcubic graph this is equivalent to sharing an edge.

For an edge subgraph \(G_F:=(V,F)\), with inherited orientations and restricted tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta_F\), define its exact zero-flow partition by
\[
Q(G_F,\boldsymbol\theta_F):=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{F}\\\widehat A_F\mathbf u=\mathbf0}}
 e^{\boldsymbol\theta_F\cdot\mathbf u}
=\sum_{\mathcal L\in\mathcal P(G_F)}\prod_{C\in\mathcal L}a_C.
\]
Cycles in \(G_F\) inherit the previously chosen \(\mathbf h_C\) and \(L_C\). Thus \(Q=Q(G,\boldsymbol\theta)\). Empty products, empty-edge partitions, and empty-coordinate assignments have value one.

For every root \(\mathbf z\in D\), put \(I:=\{e\in E:z_e=0\}\) and use the canonical reflected objects
\[
R_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\},\qquad
k_{\mathbf z}:=|R_{\mathbf z}|,\qquad
B_{\mathbf z}:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}p_{\mathbf x}.
\]
Here \(k_{\mathbf z}\) is the count denoted \(k_{\mathbf z}\) in 5a3fad9597efaf26 and \(s_{\mathbf z}\) in 7781e8f7b246c4d2, not the latter fact's number \(k\) of zero coordinates. The residual section partition \(Q_I\) of 7781e8f7b246c4d2 has the exact alias \(Q_I=Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\eta_I)=Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\theta_I)\) for that fact's gauge \(\boldsymbol\eta=\boldsymbol\theta+A^T\boldsymbol\lambda\), because residual flows annihilate \(A_I\). No gauge change is needed here. Our \(Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\theta_I)\) is not the degree-product \(Q_I\) in 5a3fad9597efaf26, which is not used here.

Restriction to \(I\) is a bijection from \(R_{\mathbf z}\) onto the ternary zero flows of \(G_I\), and the exact reflected formulas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf z}
&=\{\mathbf x\in D:x_e=z_e\text{ for every }e\notin I\},\\
k_{\mathbf z}
&=\sum_{\mathcal L\in\mathcal P(G_I)}2^{|\mathcal L|}
=Q(G_I,\mathbf0),\\
B_{\mathbf z}
&=\frac{\exp(\sum_{e\notin I}\theta_ez_e)\,Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\theta_I)}{Q}
=p_{\mathbf z}Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\theta_I).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(\mathcal K\) is the support collection of \(\mathbf z\), then \(\mathcal P(G_I)\) consists exactly of the collections in \(\mathcal P(G)\) whose cycles are vertex-disjoint from every cycle in \(\mathcal K\). Thus the reflected partition is the packing partition over cycles compatible with the fixed root support, with the original tilts. In particular it is not replaced by one. If \(I=\varnothing\), then \(R_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf z\}\), \(k_{\mathbf z}=1\), and \(B_{\mathbf z}=p_{\mathbf z}\).

For \(N\ge1\), the count and weighted consequences, including every boundary atom, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}|R_{\mathbf z}\cap H|&=\frac{k_{\mathbf z}+1}{2},\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&\ge
\frac{(k_{\mathbf z}+1)^2p_{\mathbf z}^{2}}{4B_{\mathbf z}}
=\frac{(k_{\mathbf z}+1)^2e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}
{4Q\,Q(G_I,\boldsymbol\theta_I)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The halfspace attaining the minimum reflected count need not minimize weighted depth.

The support collections have the further exact count
\[
|\mathcal P(G)|=2^n,
\qquad
|\mathcal P(G_F)|=2^{|F|-|V|+\operatorname{comp}(G_F)}.
\]
In particular \(|\mathcal C(G)|\le2^n-1\). The sharply specified consumer test is the class of graphs satisfying \(V(C)\cap V(C')\ne\varnothing\) for every pair of distinct undirected cycles. In this class only empty and singleton collections are allowed, so
\[
|\mathcal C(G)|=2^n-1,\qquad
|D|=1+2|\mathcal C(G)|=2^{n+1}-1,
\qquad
Q=1+\sum_{C\in\mathcal C(G)}2\cosh L_C.
\]
For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^E\) and \(N\ge1\), there is therefore an actual root \(\mathbf z^\star\in D\) with
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)\ge p_{\mathbf z^\star}
\ge\frac{1}{2^{n+1}-1}
>\frac12\,2^{-n}.
\]
More generally, within this pairwise-intersection class the immediate atom criterion is \(1+2|\mathcal C(G)|\le C2^n\Longrightarrow\max_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge C^{-1}2^{-n}\), for any positive scalar \(C\) satisfying the premise. The intrinsic count proves that \(C=2\) always works in that class, without assuming a constant number of cycles.

There is no absolute \(C\) for which the atom-count premise \(|D|\le C2^n\) holds for all connected subcubic graphs. Specifically, a chain of \(n\ge1\) triangles joined by \(n-1\) bridges at distinct attachment vertices within each intermediate triangle is connected and subcubic, has intrinsic rank \(n\), and has \(|D|=3^n\). This is an obstruction to extending the atom-count consumer test, not to a deeper actual-depth or cubic-moment argument.

## Proof

We first establish the representation directly. For a zero flow \(\mathbf z\), at a vertex each supported incident edge contributes either \(+1\) or \(-1\) to the incidence sum. Reduction modulo two shows that the number of such edges is even. Since the underlying degree is at most three, the support degree is either zero or two. Hence every nonempty connected component of the support is 2-regular.

For completeness, a finite connected loopless 2-regular multigraph is one undirected cycle in the stated sense. Start at a vertex, choose an edge, and continue along the other incident edge at each subsequent vertex. Before any vertex repeats, all edges traversed are distinct. The first repeated vertex must be the starting one: entering an already visited noninitial vertex by a new edge would give it three incident traversed edges. The resulting closed traversal uses both incident edges at every vertex it visits. No edge of the connected component can lead out of its visited vertices, so it exhausts the component. Its length may be two, using two distinct parallel edges; looplessness excludes length one. Components of the support are vertex-disjoint.

Direct each supported edge according to the sign of its flow relative to the fixed graph orientation. At a used vertex, zero divergence and degree two force one directed edge in and one out. Thus the induced directions traverse the whole support cycle coherently. There are precisely two choices, namely \(\mathbf h_C\) and \(-\mathbf h_C\), regardless of the originally assigned edge orientations. This also verifies the two-edge case: when the two fixed orientations agree their circulation signs are opposite, and when they disagree their circulation signs agree.

Conversely, for a collection of vertex-disjoint cycles, the sum \(\sum_C\varepsilon_C\mathbf h_C\) is ternary, since the supports are disjoint, and has divergence zero, since each summand does. Its nonzero support is exactly the union of those cycles. The support's connected components recover the collection uniquely, and the signs recover each coherent orientation uniquely. This proves the bijection, including the empty collection and disconnected or empty graphs. If two cycles meet at a vertex, each uses two distinct incident edges there. Four incident uses among at most three edges force an edge to be shared. The converse is immediate. This proves the claimed conflict equivalence.

The original tilt of a flow represented by \((\mathcal K,\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\) is \(\sum_{C\in\mathcal K}\varepsilon_CL_C\). Summing its exponential over the independent sign choices gives \(\prod_{C\in\mathcal K}(e^{L_C}+e^{-L_C})\). Summing over collections proves \(Q\), and counting sign choices proves \(|D|\). Dividing these weights by \(Q\) gives the support probabilities. Dividing instead by the weight of a specified collection gives its conditional product orientation law. The same proof applies to every \(G_F\), which is still subcubic, proving its partition formula. Across connected components the choices of collections are independent combinatorially, so these partitions and oriented counts multiply across components; an isolated vertex contributes one. Thus disconnected graphs cause no additional identifications.

Next fix \(\mathbf z\in D\). If \(z_e=1\), both \(x_e\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and \(2-x_e\in\{-1,0,1\}\) force \(x_e=1\). If \(z_e=-1\), the same argument forces \(x_e=-1\). If \(z_e=0\), reflection is just \(x_e\mapsto-x_e\), with all three values allowed. The fixed nonzero coordinates have divergence zero by themselves, since \(\widehat A\mathbf z=0\) and \(\mathbf z_I=0\). Therefore a ternary assignment on \(I\) extends to an element of \(R_{\mathbf z}\) exactly when \(\widehat A_I\mathbf x_I=0\). The reflected assignment then also has zero divergence. This proves the reflected-support description and the restriction bijection, rather than merely a containment.

For any such extension its exponential weight is \(e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_I\cdot\mathbf x_I}\). Summing and dividing by the original \(Q\) gives the asserted \(B_{\mathbf z}\), with the exact original normalization. Counting gives \(k_{\mathbf z}\), and applying the cycle representation to the residual graph gives its displayed packing sum. In particular a nonempty residual collection contributes an even number of orientations, while the empty collection contributes one; hence \(k_{\mathbf z}\) is odd. The optional alias to the gauge used in 7781e8f7b246c4d2 is exact because \(\boldsymbol\eta_I\cdot\mathbf u-\boldsymbol\theta_I\cdot\mathbf u=\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot A_I\mathbf u=0\) on every residual zero flow.

At a vertex belonging to a root cycle, two incident edges are nonzero at the root and are removed from \(G_I\). The remaining degree there is at most one, so no cycle of \(G_I\) can contain that vertex. Conversely, every cycle of \(G\) vertex-disjoint from the root support uses only zero root edges and is a cycle of \(G_I\). Applying this to all cycles in a collection proves the exact compatibility description of \(\mathcal P(G_I)\). No extra vertex constraint or change of tilt has been omitted.

We also verify the cap consequences directly, consistently with the reflected objects of 5a3fad9597efaf26 and the probability normalization of 7781e8f7b246c4d2. Reflection partitions \(R_{\mathbf z}\) into its single fixed point \(\mathbf z\) and \((k_{\mathbf z}-1)/2\) distinct pairs. If a closed halfspace contains \(\mathbf z\), it must contain at least one member of every pair: otherwise the midpoint \(\mathbf z\) would also lie strictly outside the halfspace. Thus its reflected cap has at least \((k_{\mathbf z}+1)/2\) points.

A through-root halfspace attains this count when its normal is not orthogonal to any nonzero difference \(\mathbf x-\mathbf z\) from \(R_{\mathbf z}\). Such a nonzero normal exists for \(N\ge1\): after ordering the edges, normals \((1,t,\ldots,t^{N-1})\) are forbidden by a given nonzero difference only at the finitely many zeros of a nonzero polynomial. Avoid the finite union of those sets. Each reflected pair then has precisely one point in the cap, while the root belongs to its boundary. This proves the exact count minimum without imposing genericity on a weighted minimizing halfspace.

For every \(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\), the original exponential law gives \(p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}^{2}\). Put \(E_{\mathbf z,H}:=R_{\mathbf z}\cap H\). Cauchy-Schwarz and injectivity of reflection imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_{\mathbf z,H}|p_{\mathbf z}
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z,H}}
\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}}\\
&\le\left(
\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z,H}}p_{\mathbf x}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z,H}}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}\right)^{1/2}
\le\left(p(D\cap H)B_{\mathbf z}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Insert the count lower bound, square, and minimize over actual containing halfspaces. The exact \(B_{\mathbf z}\) substitution gives the stated partition form of the depth bound. All inequalities hold for every such halfspace, including nongeneric halfspaces with additional boundary atoms. This argument does not use a permanent bound or a degree-product estimate.

To count the unoriented support collections and identify intrinsic rank, choose a spanning forest \(T\subseteq E\). It has \(|V|-\operatorname{comp}(G)=r\) edges. Call the \(n\) remaining edges chords. Each chord has its endpoints in one tree component; that tree's unique path between its endpoints, together with the chord, forms a fundamental cycle. This is valid for parallel edges as well, when the path can be a single edge.

An edge subset is even if every vertex has even degree in it. Its intersection with the chords determines it uniquely: if two even subsets have the same chord intersection, their symmetric difference is an even subset contained in the forest. A nonempty edge subset of a forest has a vertex of degree one, as an endpoint of a longest path in one of its nontrivial tree components, so cannot be even. Conversely, for every prescribed subset of chords, take the symmetric difference of their fundamental cycles. Degrees remain even modulo two, and its chord intersection is exactly the prescribed subset, since each fundamental cycle contains just its own chord. This gives an elementary bijection between even edge subsets and subsets of the \(n\) chords, hence precisely \(2^n\) even subsets.

In a subcubic graph, an even edge subset has degrees zero or two, so the component argument at the start identifies it uniquely with an unoriented vertex-disjoint cycle collection. Conversely every such collection has even union. Thus \(|\mathcal P(G)|=2^n\). This proof applies unchanged to \(G_F\), including newly isolated vertices, yielding its count with \(|F|-|V|+\operatorname{comp}(G_F)\). In particular every individual cycle is one distinct nonempty even subset, proving \(|\mathcal C(G)|\le2^n-1\).

For the real and integer rank assertions, orient each fundamental cycle's signed circulation so its chord coordinate is \(1\). Their chord projections are the standard coordinate vectors. Any real circulation becomes supported on the forest after subtracting these fundamental circulations with coefficients equal to its chord coordinates. A circulation supported on a forest is zero, by removing a leaf edge whose coefficient is forced to vanish by the leaf equation and repeating. Thus the fundamental circulations are a real kernel basis. They also give an integer kernel basis: integral chord coordinates yield an integral combination, and every integral circulation has those integral coefficients. Projection onto chord coordinates is therefore a saturated lattice chart of rank \(n\), of the same normalization as the chart in 7781e8f7b246c4d2. Each basis vector and its negative belongs to \(D\), as does \(\mathbf0\), so \(D\) has affine dimension \(n\). The full and row-deleted equations agree because the incidence rows sum to zero on each component, and their rank is \(r\), in agreement with 5a3fad9597efaf26. The leaf and chord arguments cover the zero-rank and empty-graph cases.

We now perform the specified consumer test. If all cycles meet pairwise, a collection contains at most one cycle. Consequently the just-proved support count reads \(1+|\mathcal C(G)|=2^n\), and the oriented count reads \(|D|=1+2|\mathcal C(G)|\). This proves both exact formulas and the singleton partition formula, without bounding the number of cycles by a fixed constant. For any original tilt the probabilities sum to one, so a largest atom has probability at least \(1/|D|\). Every halfspace containing its point contains that atom. Hence its actual depth is at least its probability. This proves the all-tilt depth conclusion and the general immediate-atom criterion. In particular the intrinsic cycle-rank count, rather than a vertex-count or maximum-degree tuning, supplies the absolute factor \(C=2\) in this consumer class.

Finally, to locate the obstruction outside that class, take \(n\) vertex-disjoint triangles and connect successive triangles by one bridge each. Choose distinct attachment vertices for the two bridges of any intermediate triangle. All degrees are at most three. The graph has \(3n\) vertices, \(4n-1\) edges, and one component, so its intrinsic rank is \(n\). None of the joining edges lies in a cycle, because deleting it disconnects the graph, whereas deleting an edge of a cycle leaves an alternate path between its endpoints. Thus the only cycles are the \(n\) triangles, and all their subsets are allowable collections. The exact oriented count is therefore
\[
|D|=\sum_{\mathcal K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}2^{|\mathcal K|}
=(1+2)^n=3^n.
\]
Its partition is \(\prod_{j=1}^n(1+2\cosh L_{C_j})\), with bridge tilts irrelevant. At zero tilt every atom has mass \(3^{-n}\), and \(|D|/2^n=(3/2)^n\) is unbounded. Thus a proposed universal atom-count bound is false even for connected sparse subcubic graphs. This does not upper-bound their actual depth: the exact residual packing partitions remain available for a non-atom next step. The completed consumer test distinguishes the pairwise-conflicting case, where counting already suffices, from multiple-cycle packing, where counting alone does not supply the desired scale.
