## Statement

For $d=200$ let $B$ be the centered unit box and $A$ the centered box with one hundred sides $21/20$ followed by one hundred sides $20/21$. Put $K_0=(A+B)/2$, of volume $k=(1681/1680)^{100}$. Add to $K_0$ a pyramid of volume $\epsilon=1/1000$ over its positive first-coordinate facet, with apex on the first coordinate axis, and call the resulting convex body $K$. Use the actual lifted hull with $A,K,B$ at bases $-1,0,1$ and, for each $n\ge1$, take its product with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ in the integer coordinates. The centroid graph is nonaffine, the total mixed mass is $2^{n-1}(2+k+\epsilon)$, and the optimized shape-specific single-template shell bound, even including exact own-fiber depth, is strictly below $1633/3000$. The supremum here allows every full-dimensional centroid-zero convex template, every affine placement map, all contained radii, and every actual integer anchor. Its gap below the original raw target $\Gamma_{200}(2+k+\epsilon)/2$ is greater than $37/33000$. The actual mark at the center of $K_0$ in any binary block has raw depth at least $(k+1)/2$, strictly above that target.

## Proof

Both endpoint boxes have volume one. Put $a=21/20$ and $t=a^{-100}$. Their midpoint has side lengths $(a+1)/2$ and $(1/a+1)/2$, each repeated one hundred times, giving the stated $k$. The binomial series yields
$$
1<k<\sum_{j\ge0}(100/1680)^j=84/79<16/15,\qquad
a^{100}\ge\sum_{j=0}^6\binom{100}{j}/20^j=4209933/40000>100.
$$
Hence $t<1/100$. Also $(1+1/200)^{200}<e\le8/3+5/96=87/32<11/4$, by binomial comparison and bounding the exponential tail starting at the term $1/24$ by a geometric series of ratio $1/5$. Thus $\Gamma_{200}>4/11$.

If the positive first-coordinate facet of $K_0$ has area $F$ and first coordinate $b_1$, take the new apex $(b_1+200\epsilon/F,0,\ldots,0)$. It violates only the inequality of that facet, so the extra region is precisely the pyramid over it, of volume $\epsilon$. Its first coordinate is positive throughout its interior, whereas $K_0$ has zero first moment. The middle centroid is therefore different from the two zero endpoint centroids. The lifted hull has those exact integer fibers: the endpoint sites are exposed, the middle already contains the average of the two endpoint fibers, and there are no other integer sites in $[-1,1]$. All three fibers and the lifted body have interior.

For any pair of translates of $r_-J,r_+J$ contained in $A,B$, the smaller copy has each coordinate width at most the smaller of the corresponding box side lengths. This follows from translation invariance and linear scaling of widths. Its enclosing coordinate box therefore gives $V\min(w_-,w_+)\le t$, also when a radius is zero. In every direction the two closed halfspaces through any point divide the $K_0$ volume with a null boundary. One contributes at most $k/2$, so the ordinary depth of every point of $K$ is at most $k/2+\epsilon$. The same argument gives endpoint ordinary depth at most $1/2$.

In rank one the central anchor has $D_0(w)=w_0+\min(w_-,w_+)$ and $R_0(w)=\min(w_-,w_+)$. The exact-own expression in the shape-specific theorem is then
$$
\delta_0+2\kappa(J)V\min(w_-,w_+)
\le k/2+\epsilon+t<1633/3000.
$$
At an endpoint, $D_p(w)=w_p$ by base isolation and $R_p(w)=0$, so the expression is at most $1/2$. The bound is uniform over templates and placements. In the binary product any reflected pair remains in the anchor block, since two bits with an integer midpoint must coincide. To upper bound $D_p(w)$ use a base normal exposing the binary vertex strictly, with a coefficient large enough to exclude the other blocks while retaining either chosen within-block chain sign. The base depth is thus no greater than its within-block value. As $\beta(J)\ge0$, the same upper bound for the exact-own expression persists. The weaker expression is dominated by the exact-own expression. The original mass multiplies by $2^{n-1}$, so the raw target is unchanged and strictly exceeds $6/11$. The claimed uniform gap follows from $6/11-1633/3000=37/33000$.

For the separate actual depth claim take the middle mark with continuous coordinate zero. Every through-mark cap with nonzero continuous normal captures $k/2$ from $K_0$. The endpoint box thresholds relative to zero are opposite, so at least one endpoint contributes at least $1/2$ by central symmetry of that box. A pure-base cap in the restricted chain contains the entire middle and at least one endpoint. If its restriction to the chain is constant, it includes the whole chain. This proves the same bound for any containing cap, since it contains its parallel through-mark cap and additional binary blocks contribute nonnegative mass. Finally $\Gamma_{200}\le1/2$ and $(k+1)/2-(2+k+\epsilon)/4=(k-\epsilon)/4>0$.
