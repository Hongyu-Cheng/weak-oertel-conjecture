## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$, and $\gamma=(d/m)^d$. In the barycentric hyperplane $\sum_i \xi_i=1$ with a fixed Euclidean metric, the reference simplex has vertex matrix $I$ and volume $V>0$. Fix the $m$ cap halfspaces $\{\xi_i\ge1/m\}$. For a column-sum-one matrix $W$ near $I$, let $F_i(W)$ be its simplex volume in cap $i$. Then its total volume is $V\det W$, and
$$
F_i(W)=V\det W\frac{(W_{ii}-1/m)^d}{\prod_{j\ne i}(W_{ii}-W_{ij})}.
$$
The Lagrangian $L(W)=V\det W-(m\gamma)^{-1}\sum_iF_i(W)$ is stationary at $I$. For $W=I+tB$, where the column sums of $B$ are zero, set $A$ equal to the off-diagonal part of $B$, with column sums $c$ and row sums $s$. Then $F_i'(0)/(\gamma V)=\operatorname{tr}B+\sum_jB_{ij}$ and
$$
\frac{L''(0)}{V}=\frac{\lVert c\rVert^2}{d}-\frac{\lVert s\rVert^2+\lVert A\rVert_F^2}{m}.
$$
The Hessian of $L$ on the full physical vertex tangent space has inertia $(d,1,d^2-1)$ in the order positive, zero, negative.

## Proof

Since $W$ preserves the sum of coordinates, it acts as the identity on the one-dimensional quotient by the direction hyperplane. Thus its determinant on the direction hyperplane is $\det W>0$ near $I$. Near $I$ each fixed cap includes exactly its own vertex. Its intersection with the moving simplex is a simplex whose edge ratios from that vertex are $(W_{ii}-1/m)/(W_{ii}-W_{ij})$, giving the displayed formula and smoothness.

Put $T=\operatorname{tr}B$, $b_i=B_{ii}$, and $R_i=\sum_jB_{ij}=b_i+s_i$. Differentiating $\log F_i$ at zero gives $T+R_i$, since $1-1/m=d/m$. The second log derivative is
$$
-\operatorname{tr}(B^2)-\frac{m^2}{d}b_i^2+\sum_{j\ne i}(b_i-A_{ij})^2.
$$
Each $F_i(I)=\gamma V$. As $\sum_iR_i=0$, the first derivatives cancel in $L$. To obtain the second derivative of $F_i$ from its log derivatives, add $(T+R_i)^2$ to the second log derivative and multiply by $\gamma V$. Averaging the resulting expression and subtracting it from $(\det W)''(0)=T^2-\operatorname{tr}(B^2)$ gives
$$
\frac{L''(0)}V=-\frac{\lVert R\rVert^2}{m}+\frac{m}{d}\lVert b\rVert^2
-\frac1m\sum_{i\ne j}(b_i-A_{ij})^2.
$$
Expansion with $R=b+s$ cancels the cross terms and gives the claimed form, using $b=-c$.

For fixed $c$, minimize $J(A)=\lVert s\rVert^2+\lVert A\rVert_F^2$ on off-diagonal matrices with these column sums. The equations $A_{ij}+s_i=\eta_j$ for $i\ne j$ suffice: the $J$-inner product with every zero-column-sum perturbation vanishes, and $J$ is positive definite. Decompose $c=\bar c\,\mathbf1+c_0$, with $\sum_i(c_0)_i=0$. For the constant part, the lift $A_{ij}=\bar c/d$ for $i\ne j$ satisfies the equations and has $L''=0$. For the zero-sum part take
$$
A_{ij}=\frac{m(c_0)_j+(c_0)_i}{dm-1}\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
s_i=-\frac{(c_0)_i}{dm-1}.
$$
Its column sums are $c_0$ and its equations hold with $\eta_j=m(c_0)_j/(dm-1)$. In particular $J(A)=\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}(A_{ij}+s_i)=m\lVert c_0\rVert^2/(dm-1)$. The quadratic form on this lift is
$$
\frac{\lVert c_0\rVert^2}{d}-\frac{\lVert c_0\rVert^2}{dm-1}
=\frac{d^2-1}{d(dm-1)}\lVert c_0\rVert^2.
$$
The two lifts are orthogonal for $J$ since the zero-sum lift has total matrix sum and total row sum zero and the constant lift has constant off-diagonal entries and constant row sum. They are orthogonal for the column term as well. Both are $J$-orthogonal to the zero-column-sum subspace, on which the Hessian form is exactly $-J/m$. The constant and zero-sum lifts have dimensions one and $d$; their existence shows that the column map is onto $\mathbb R^m$. Its kernel therefore has dimension $dm-m=d^2-1$. This proves the complete inertia.
