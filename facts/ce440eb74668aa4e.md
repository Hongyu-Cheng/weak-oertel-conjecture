## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $A,K,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be full-dimensional compact convex bodies at the only integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use summed $d$-volume on those fibers. Fix $y_0\in\operatorname{int}K$ and $d+1$ nonzero spanning normals $v_i$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. Put $h_i=h_B(v_i)$, $b_i=2v_i\cdot y_0-h_i$. Suppose the closed caps $v_i\cdot(x-y_0)+(v_i\cdot y_0-h_i)z\ge0$ have a common raw mass $Q$ and proper cuts in $A,K$. Their $B$ traces have zero $d$-volume. Set $s_i=2\sigma_{Ai}+\sigma_{Ki}$, where $\sigma_{Ai}$ and $\sigma_{Ki}$ are the respective proper section measures divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, using counting measure for $d=1$.

Suppose there is a sequence of compact convex enlargements $A'\supseteq A$ with $(A'+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $\delta=|A'|-|A|>0$ tending to zero. Fix a nonempty index set $I$ and $\kappa>0$ such that almost every added point satisfies $v_i\cdot x\ge b_i+\kappa$ for $i\in I$ and $v_i\cdot x\le b_i-\kappa$ for $i\notin I$. One $C^1$ curve $(y(\delta),q(\delta))$ through $(y_0,Q)$ equalizes the actual enlarged caps, with the same normals and supporting $B$ thresholds, for all sufficiently small available $\delta$. They cover the whole middle plane, and $q'(0)=p_I$, where $p_I=(\sum_{i\in I}1/s_i)/(\sum_i1/s_i)>0$.

If $Q$ is the original global maximum raw depth and the ordinary maximum raw depth $m_A$ of $A$ satisfies $m_A<Q$, then the new global raw depth is at most $q(\delta)$ for small available $\delta$, allowing the unchanged endpoint to have $m_B=Q$. For $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$, the derivative of the normalized upper certificate $q(\delta)/(M+\delta)$ at zero is $(Mp_I-Q)/M^2$. In particular $p_I<Q/M$ gives strict normalized descent.

If $A$ is a polytope, put $E=\{x:x+B\subseteq2K\}$. A facet $F$ with the above strict signature on its entirety admits these enlargements whenever $\operatorname{relint}F\cap\operatorname{int}E\ne\varnothing$. For $p$ in this intersection and outward unit normal $n$, the enlargement $\operatorname{conv}(A\cup\{p+\varepsilon n\})$ adds precisely the facet pyramid, of volume $\varepsilon\mathcal H^{d-1}(F)/d$. Thus under the stated global-depth and strict-descent assumptions an attained local minimum has no such relative-interior feasible point on $F$.

## Proof

The hull of the three lifted bodies has precisely the stated integer fibers. The projection is $[-1,1]$. A convex combination at base zero puts equal total weight on the endpoints, so its point is a convex combination of an endpoint midpoint and a point of $K$. The midpoint inclusion puts both in $K$. At the outer bases only the fiber at that base can occur. This holds for each enlargement as well.

At a nearby mark $y$, the three thresholds in direction $v_i$ are $2v_i\cdot y-h_i$, $v_i\cdot y$ and $h_i$. Define
$$
F_i(y)=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge v_i\cdot y\}|
       +|A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge2v_i\cdot y-h_i\}|.
$$
The fixed margin makes the new cap mass exactly $F_i(y)+\delta\mathbf1_{i\in I}$ in one neighborhood of $y_0$ independent of the body $A'$. The supporting $B$ traces always have zero $d$-volume. Proper section measures are continuous as functions of the threshold in an open interval about each cut, by concavity of the section-volume roots for $d\ge2$ and the interval formula for $d=1$. Fubini then gives $F_i\in C^1$ and $\nabla F_i(y_0)=-s_iv_i$.

The derivative of $(F_i(y)+\delta\mathbf1_{i\in I}-q)_i$ in $(y,q)$ is invertible. In a kernel relation $-s_i v_i\cdot\dot y-\dot q=0$, multiply by $1/s_i$ and sum to obtain $\dot q=0$. Spanning gives $\dot y=0$. The implicit-function theorem gives the curve for a real parameter near zero. Differentiating and summing with the same weights gives $q'(0)=p_I$. Its evaluations at the available volumes therefore give actual equal budgets. The middle margins $v_i\cdot(x-y(\delta))$ sum to zero, so at least one is nonnegative at each middle point.

At either outer base, a prescribed ordinary halfspace through a mark can be made an ambient halfspace through that mark excluding the other two compact fibers by a finite tilt toward the exposed base. Conversely any containing ambient halfspace has an own trace containing the mark. This proves equality of mixed and ordinary depth at the endpoints. For every ambient continuous point $x$, even if $x\in A'\setminus A$, adding $\delta$ volume increases ordinary depth by at most $\delta$: each fixed halfspace gains at most this much, then take infima. The old ordinary depth is zero off $A$ by separation and is at most $m_A$ on $A$. The new endpoint maximum is at most $m_A+\delta$. The unchanged one is at most $Q$. Since $q'(0)>0$ and $q(0)=Q>m_A$, both are bounded by $q(\delta)$ for small positive $\delta$. Together with the middle cover this gives the global upper certificate. Differentiating its ratio by $M+\delta$ gives the asserted derivative.

The set $E$ is closed and convex as an intersection of translates of $2K$ and contains $A$. If $p\in\operatorname{relint}F\cap\operatorname{int}E$, a sufficiently small outward apex $p+\varepsilon n$ lies in $E$ and satisfies strictly every facet inequality other than that of $F$. Each segment from this apex into $A$ satisfies those other inequalities throughout and first enters $A$ through $F$. Thus the part added to $A$ is exactly the pyramid off its base. Its volume is the indicated height times base area divided by $d$, also valid with a singleton facet for $d=1$. A common positive signature margin on the facet persists at the nearby apex and hence on the pyramid by affine interpolation. The hull remains in $E$, so midpoint compatibility holds. The enlargement tends to $A$ in Hausdorff distance and contradicts a local minimum when the strict certificate holds.
