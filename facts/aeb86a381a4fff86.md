## Statement

Use exactly the notation and hypotheses of accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d`.  Thus \((C_j)\) is a strict no-rank-drop hard sequence, \(p_j\) is its endpoint-disjoint graph matching, \(\omega_{j,e}\) are the gauge-load-minimal stress weights, and
\[
 \gamma_j=\left(\frac{d_j}{d_j+1}\right)^{d_j},\qquad
 H_j=\frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j},\qquad
 T_j=\Omega_j=\sum_e\omega_{j,e}.
\]
Choose (j_0) so that all conclusions asserted there for sufficiently large indices hold.  For every (jgeq j_0), put
\[
 F_j:=\{e\in\operatorname{supp}p_j:\omega_{j,e}>0\},\qquad
 \widehat z:=(1,z)\in\mathbb R^{n_j+1},
\]
and, for \(e=\{z_e^-,z_e^+\}\in F_j\), put
\[
 \mathcal V_{j,e}:=\{\widehat z_e^-,\widehat z_e^+\}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_j\) be the affine Rado matroid on ground set \(F_j\): a set \(I\subseteq F_j\) is independent when one can choose one vector \(v_e\in\mathcal V_{j,e}\) for every \(e\in I\) so that the chosen vectors are linearly independent.  Equivalently, one can choose one endpoint of every edge in \(I\) so that the chosen endpoints are affinely independent.  For \(J\subseteq F_j\), write
\[
 \omega_j(J):=\sum_{e\in J}\omega_{j,e}.
\]

The following assertions hold.

1.  For every \(J\subseteq F_j\), the exact rank formulas are
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R_j}(J)
 =\min_{I\subseteq J}
 \left(|J\setminus I|+
 \dim\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{e\in I}\mathcal V_{j,e}\right)
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R_j}(J)
 =\min_{X\leq\mathbb R^{n_j+1}}
 \left(\dim X+
 |\{e\in J:\mathcal V_{j,e}\not\subseteq X\}|\right).
\tag{2}
\]

2.  Put \(\kappa:=e+3/2\) and \(\xi_j:=H_j/\gamma_j\).  If
\[
 \xi_j\leq\frac1{4\kappa+2},
\tag{3}
\]
then \(\mathcal R_j\) has full rank \(n_j+1\).  If \(\mathcal B_j\) denotes its set of full bases and
\[
 \nu_j:=\max\left\{
 \sum_{B\in\mathcal B_j}\lambda_B:
 \lambda_B\geq0,\quad
 \sum_{B\ni e}\lambda_B\leq\omega_{j,e}
 \text{ for every }e\in F_j
 \right\},
\tag{4}
\]
then the exact min-cut formula is
\[
 \nu_j=
 \min_{\substack{J\subseteq F_j:\
 \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R_j}(J)<n_j+1}}
 \frac{\omega_j(F_j\setminus J)}
 {n_j+1-\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R_j}(J)},
\tag{5}
\]
and it obeys
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{1-4\kappa\xi_j}{1-2\kappa\xi_j}\,
 \frac{T_j}{n_j+1}
 \leq\nu_j\leq\frac{T_j}{n_j+1}.}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, under (3),
\[
 \nu_j\geq\frac1{\kappa+1}\frac{T_j}{n_j+1},
\tag{7}
\]
so this is already a finite-instance dimension-free packing theorem.

3.  Along every hard sequence in accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d`,
\[
 \frac{(n_j+1)\nu_j}{T_j}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(c_R<1\), all sufficiently large indices admit capacities \((\lambda_B)_{B\in\mathcal B_j}\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_B\lambda_B\geq c_R\frac{T_j}{n_j+1},\qquad
 \sum_{B\ni e}\lambda_B\leq\omega_{j,e}.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular one may take the absolute constant \(c_R=1/2\) eventually.

## Proof

Fix an index \(j\) in the sufficiently-large range of accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d`, suppress the index \(j\), and put \(N:=n+1\).  This is only a local abbreviation for the target matroid rank and does not rename the residual matching \(r_j\) appearing in that accepted fact.

We first record the two hard capacity estimates.  Equations (12) and (29) of accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d` give
\[
 T\geq\left(\frac\gamma2-\kappa H\right)W
 =\frac{\gamma W}{2}(1-2\kappa\xi),
 \qquad \xi:=\frac H\gamma,
\tag{10}
\]
and, with the canonical endpoint load
\[
 \varrho_z:=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e,
\]
they give
\[
 \varrho_z\leq\gamma a_z.
\tag{11}
\]
There is also an atom estimate
\[
 \omega_e\leq h_{\max}(C)\qquad(e\in F).
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, (11) gives \(\omega_e\leq\gamma a_z\) at either endpoint \(z\) of \(e\).  For completeness, accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` with \(m=a_z\) says that the centroid \(c_z\) of the fiber \(C_z\) lies in \(\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)\) and every closed fiber halfspace containing \(c_z\) has volume at least \(\gamma a_z\).  The restriction to the fiber over \(z\) of any ambient closed halfspace containing \((z,c_z)\) is such a fiber halfspace, or the whole fiber.  Hence the mixed depth of \((z,c_z)\) is at least \(\gamma a_z\), and therefore \(h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma a_z\).  This proves (12).

If \(A\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\) is a primitive affine subspace of codimension \(c\), define
\[
 F_A:=\{e\in F:\text{both endpoints of }e\text{ lie in }A\}.
\]
Every edge in \(F_A\) contributes its weight at both endpoints in \(A\).  Using (11) and strict no-rank-drop from accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d`,
\[
 2\omega(F_A)
 \leq\sum_{z\in A}\varrho_z
 \leq\gamma\sum_{z\in A}a_z
 <\gamma 2^{-c}W.
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{\omega(F_A)<\gamma 2^{-c-1}W.}
\tag{13}
\]

We next prove the Rado rank formulas rather than invoking them.  We use the following finite-dimensional independent-transversal lemma.  For a finite family of nonempty vector sets \((V_i)_{i\in K}\), there is a choice \(v_i\in V_i\) whose chosen vectors are linearly independent if and only if
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{i\in L}V_i\geq|L|
 \qquad(L\subseteq K).
\tag{14}
\]
Necessity is immediate.  For sufficiency, induct on \(|K|\).  If a nonempty proper \(L\subsetneq K\) has equality in (14), apply induction to the family on \(L\), whose chosen vectors form a basis of
\(U:=\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{i\in L}V_i\).  For every \(Q\subseteq K\setminus L\), the images in the quotient by \(U\) span a space of dimension at least
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\left(
 U\cup\bigcup_{i\in Q}V_i\right)-\dim U
 \geq |L|+|Q|-|L|=|Q|.
\]
Induction in the quotient supplies independent representatives for the complementary family, and lifting them and adjoining the basis chosen on \(L\) gives the desired transversal.  If there is no such equality set, then every nonempty proper \(L\) has span dimension at least \(|L|+1\).  Choose any nonzero vector from one set, quotient by its span, and apply induction to the remaining sets: every subfamily loses at most one dimension and therefore still satisfies (14).  This completes the induction.

For a fixed \(J\subseteq F\), let
\[
 q:=\min_{I\subseteq J}
 \left(|J\setminus I|+
 \dim\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{e\in I}\mathcal V_e\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Every independent partial transversal has size at most the expression in (15), for each \(I\), by separating its chosen indices into \(I\) and \(J\setminus I\).  Thus \(\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)\leq q\).  Conversely, adjoin a dummy vector space of dimension \(|J|-q\), with a fixed basis, and add every dummy basis vector to every set \(\mathcal V_e\).  For every nonempty \(I\subseteq J\), the enlarged union spans a space of dimension
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{e\in I}\mathcal V_e+|J|-q
 \geq |I|,
\]
where the last inequality is exactly the definition of \(q\).  The independent-transversal lemma gives a full transversal of all \(|J|\) enlarged sets.  At most \(|J|-q\) of its representatives can be dummy vectors, so at least \(q\) representatives are original vectors.  Deleting the dummy representatives gives an independent partial transversal of size at least \(q\).  This proves (1).

For a linear subspace \(X\), let
\[
 I_X:=\{e\in J:\mathcal V_e\subseteq X\}.
\]
Using \(I_X\) in (1) shows that its minimum is at most
\(\dim X+|J\setminus I_X|\).  Conversely, for every \(I\subseteq J\), taking
\(X=\operatorname{span}\bigcup_{e\in I}\mathcal V_e\) makes
\(|\{e\in J:\mathcal V_e\not\subseteq X\}|\leq|J\setminus I|\).
Taking the two minima proves (2).

We now bound every deficient layer in (2).  Fix \(J\subseteq F\) with
\(\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)<N\), and choose a minimizing subspace \(X\) in (2).  Replace \(X\) by the span of all \(\mathcal V_e\) fully contained in it.  This preserves the set of contained edges and cannot increase the objective, so the replacement is also minimizing.

If \(X=\{0\}\), then every vector in every \(\mathcal V_e\) is nonzero and (2) gives
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)=|J|=:s<N.
\tag{16}
\]
By (12),
\[
 \omega(J)\leq s h_{\max}(C).
\tag{17}
\]

Suppose now that \(X\ne\{0\}\).  Because the replacement \(X\) is spanned by homogenized lattice points, its intersection with the affine hyperplane whose first coordinate is one is the homogenization of an affine hull \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) of lattice points, and
\[
 \dim X=\dim A+1.
\]
This \(A\) is primitive: after translating by one of its lattice points, its direction space \(V\) is spanned by lattice differences, while \(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is saturated because \(kx\in V\) with \(x\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(k\ne0\) implies \(x\in V\).  Thus \(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\) has rank \(\dim V\), exactly as required by the canonical definition in accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d`.
If \(c:=\operatorname{codim}A\), deficiency forces \(1\leq c\leq n\).  Put
\[
 s:=|\{e\in J:\mathcal V_e\not\subseteq X\}|.
\]
Formula (2) gives
\[
 \operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)=N-c+s,
 \qquad N-\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)=c-s,
 \qquad 0\leq s<c.
\tag{18}
\]
All but these \(s\) edges of \(J\) lie in \(F_A\).  Hence (12)-(13) give
\[
 \omega(J)<\gamma 2^{-c-1}W+s h_{\max}(C).
\tag{19}
\]
This proves directly that every Rado-deficient layer consists of a primitive-slice layer plus fewer exceptional edges than its codimension.

Assume now (3), and abbreviate
\[
 \theta:=2\kappa\xi,qquad
 L:=\frac1{1-\theta},qquad
 b:=\frac{2\xi}{1-\theta},qquad
 u:=\frac{h_{\max}(C)}T.
\tag{20}
\]
Equation (10), together with \(h_{\max}(C)=2^{-n}HW\), gives
\[
 \frac{\gamma W}{2T}\leq L,
 \qquad
 u\leq b2^{-n}.
\tag{21}
\]
Condition (3) implies \(\theta<1/2\) and
\[
 \frac b2=\frac{\xi}{1-\theta}
 \leq\frac{1/2-\theta}{1-\theta}
 =1-\frac L2.
\tag{22}
\]
In particular \(b\leq1\).

Consider first the nonzero-witness case (18).  Put \(G_c:=1-L2^{-c}\).  Dividing (19) by the lower bound (10) yields
\[
 \omega(F\setminus J)>T(G_c-su).
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(1\leq c\leq n\), (21)-(22) imply
\[
 cu\leq b\,c2^{-n}\leq\frac b2
 \leq1-\frac L2\leq G_c.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(0\leq s\leq c-2\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 \frac{G_c-(s+1)u}{c-s-1}
 -\frac{G_c-su}{c-s}
 =\frac{G_c-cu}{(c-s-1)(c-s)}\geq0.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the ratio is minimized at \(s=0\), and (18), (23) give
\[
 \frac{\omega(F\setminus J)}
 {N-\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)}
 \geq \frac{T}{c}(1-L2^{-c}).
\tag{26}
\]
For \(c\geq1\) and \(L\geq1\),
\[
 \frac{c+1}{c}(1-L2^{-c})\geq2-L.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, the difference between the left side and the right side at \(L=1\) is
\[
 \frac{1-(c+1)2^{-c}}c\geq0
\]
because \(2^c\geq c+1\), and its derivative in \(L\) is
\(1-(c+1)/(c2^c)\geq0\).  Since \(N=n+1\geq c+1\), (26)-(27) imply
\[
 \frac{\omega(F\setminus J)}
 {N-\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal R}(J)}
 \geq (2-L)\frac TN.
\tag{28}
\]

In the zero-witness case (16), equations (17) and (21) give
\[
 \omega(F\setminus J)\geq T(1-su).
\]
Moreover
\[
 Nu\leq b\frac{n+1}{2^n}\leq b\leq1.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{\omega(F\setminus J)}{N-s}
 \geq\frac TN
 \geq(2-L)\frac TN.
\tag{29}
\]
Here \(0<2-L\leq1\) by \(\theta<1/2\).  Equations (28)-(29) apply to every deficient \(J\).  They also prove full rank: otherwise \(J=F\) would be deficient, while their left side would have zero numerator and positive right side.

It remains to prove the exact packing formula (5).  We prove the following formula for any finite rank-\(N\) matroid \(M\) of full rank on \(F\), with nonnegative capacities \(\omega_e\):
\[
 \max\left\{\sum_B\lambda_B:
 \lambda_B\geq0,\ \sum_{B\ni e}\lambda_B\leq\omega_e\right\}
 =\min_{J:\operatorname{rk}_M(J)<N}
 \frac{\omega(F\setminus J)}{N-\operatorname{rk}_M(J)}.
\tag{30}
\]
The dual of the finite packing linear program is
\[
 \min\sum_{e\in F}\omega_e y_e
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 y_e\geq0,
 \qquad\sum_{e\in B}y_e\geq1
 \text{ for every base }B.
\tag{31}
\]
For a deficient \(J\), setting \(y_e=0\) on \(J\) and
\(y_e=(N-\operatorname{rk}_M(J))^{-1}\) off \(J\) is dual feasible, because every base uses at least \(N-\operatorname{rk}_M(J)\) elements outside \(J\).  Hence the dual optimum is at most the right side of (30).

Conversely, let \(y\) be any feasible dual vector, and choose a minimum-\(y\)-weight base \(B_*\) by the matroid greedy algorithm.  Put
\[
 E_t:=\{e\in F:y_e<t\}.
\]
For all \(t\) except the finitely many coordinate values of \(y\), greedy gives
\[
 |B_*\cap E_t|=\operatorname{rk}_M(E_t).
\]
If the right side of (30) is \(\beta\), layer-cake integration gives
\[
 \sum_e\omega_e y_e
 =\int_0^\infty\omega(F\setminus E_t)\,dt
 \geq\beta\int_0^\infty
 (N-\operatorname{rk}_M(E_t))\,dt
 =\beta\sum_{e\in B_*}y_e
 \geq\beta.
\tag{32}
\]
For full-rank \(E_t\), the integrand inequality is the trivial inequality with right side zero; for deficient \(E_t\), it is the definition of \(\beta\).  The last inequality uses dual feasibility.  Thus every dual solution has value at least \(\beta\).  Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality proves (30), and hence (5).

Since
\[
 2-L
 =2-\frac1{1-2\kappa\xi}
 =\frac{1-4\kappa\xi}{1-2\kappa\xi},
\tag{33}
\]
equations (28)-(30) prove the lower bound in (6).  Every full base has \(N=n+1\) edges, so for any feasible packing,
\[
 N\sum_B\lambda_B
 =\sum_e\sum_{B\ni e}\lambda_B
 \leq\sum_e\omega_e=T.
\]
This proves the upper bound in (6).  The coefficient in (33) is decreasing in \(\xi\), and at \(\xi=(4\kappa+2)^{-1}\) it equals \((\kappa+1)^{-1}\), proving (7).

Finally, accepted fact `3b33f556412af56d` gives \(H_j\to0\), while \(\gamma_j>1/e\), so \(\xi_j<eH_j\to0\).  Hence (3) eventually holds and the lower coefficient in (6) tends to one.  The matching upper bound proves (8).  Every fixed \(c_R<1\) is therefore valid eventually; in particular, \(c_R=1/2\) is valid once \(\xi_j\leq1/(6\kappa)\).  This proves (9) and all assertions.
