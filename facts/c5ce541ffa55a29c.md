## Statement

Use the finite mixed family, continuous concave buffer and closed buffered depth $b_\varepsilon$ from the closed quantile-region theorem. Suppose $\varepsilon>0$, $p\in P$ and $y$ is a local maximizer of $b_\varepsilon(p,\cdot)$ with $y\in\operatorname{int}K_p$ and $\varepsilon f(p,y)<\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial K_p)$. Put $q=b_\varepsilon(p,y)$. Then $0<q<b_{\rm base}(p)$. There are at most $d+1$ closed caps
$$
H_j=\{(Z,X):a_j\cdot(Z-p)+u_j\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon f(p,y)\ge0\},
\qquad \|u_j\|=1,
$$
each of actual mass $q$ and with threshold the right $q$-quantile for its normal. There are positive weights $\rho_j$ summing to one and an own-fiber supergradient $g$ of $f(p,\cdot)$ at $y$ with $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=\varepsilon g$. The caps cover $\{p\}\times K_p$.

## Proof

The shifted own thresholds at $y$ lie strictly between the support levels in every unit direction. The same holds in an own-fiber neighborhood of $y$ by continuity and the strict distance bound. A centered own inball gives positive unbuffered depth there. In positive base rank choose a pure-base minimizing cap through $p$, which exists because only finitely many subsets of $P$ can occur. For any unit $u$, scale its base normal by a sufficiently large positive factor in a nonvertical buffered cap. This keeps every strictly included base full and every strictly excluded base empty. On the zero-drift bases all original mass was included, and the new proper own cut omits positive mass. Thus $q<b_{\rm base}(p)$. In rank zero any proper own cut suffices.

Choose $H>0$ larger than all $|u\cdot(X-Y)+\varepsilon f(p,Y)|$ for $X$ in the sampled fibers, $\|u\|=1$ and $Y$ in a fixed neighborhood of $y$. To replace a tilt $a$, keep its values $a\cdot(z-p)$ with absolute value at most $H$ fixed. For every other row impose the corresponding inequality $\ge H$ or $\le-H$. The resulting nonempty closed polyhedron has a minimum-norm point. This point is orthogonal to the common kernel of its active rows, because sufficiently small changes of either sign along the kernel are feasible. Choosing an independent subset spanning those rows expresses the point as the minimum-norm solution to a fixed full-row-rank system with right-hand sides of absolute value at most $H$. There are finitely many independent row subsets, including the empty subset with solution zero, so the resulting norms are uniformly bounded. The replacement preserves every fiber trace for the thresholds at the chosen marks. Hence the cap infimum is attained on a compact parameter set by continuity of the nonvertical tails with respect to parameters. The strict pure-base gap ensures that any minimizing cap at $y$ has mass $q$. Its proper own trace makes the projected tail strictly decreasing near its threshold, so that threshold is the right $q$-quantile.

For nearby $Y$ let $G(Y)=\inf_{a,\|u\|=1}[c_q(a,u)-a\cdot p-u\cdot Y]$. The summands have unit slope in $Y$, their values at $y$ are at least $-\varepsilon f(p,y)$, and a minimizing cap attains this bound. Fix a small $\eta>0$ so all summands at $y$ within $\eta$ of this bound have their own quantile threshold strictly between the support levels. To clip their tilts use the same row construction, now taking $H$ also larger than $|u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon f(p,y)|+\eta$ for the finitely many sampled fibers and all unit $u$. It preserves their traces at $c_q(a,u)-a\cdot p$, so the mass there remains $q$ and the proper own trace preserves the right quantile. At $\|Y-y\|<\eta/3$, terms originally more than $\eta$ above the bound cannot beat the active affine term at $y$, by the unit slope bound. Therefore $G$ is locally a minimum over one fixed compact parameter set, with continuous parameter dependence by the quantile bound.

For nearby $Y$ the local maximum property gives $b_\varepsilon(p,Y)\le q$. The pure-base value is strictly greater than $q$, so an attained cap has mass at most $q$. If its mass is smaller, its threshold lies strictly above the right quantile. If its mass is $q$, properness makes it the right quantile. Consequently $G(Y)+\varepsilon f(p,Y)\le0$, with equality at $y$. The superdifferential of $G$ at $y$ is the convex hull of the negatives of the unit normals attaining $G(y)$. Indeed the directional derivative of the compact affine minimum is the minimum of the active slopes in that direction, by taking limits of minimizing parameters, and separation gives the claimed hull. The concave sum rule applies on the open own-fiber neighborhood, and the zero supergradient at its local maximum gives a convex combination of active $u_j$ equal to $\varepsilon g$ for an own supergradient $g$. Carathéodory's theorem gives at most $d+1$ normals. The local supergradient of the concave function supports it on the whole fiber, by restricting to segments from $y$. The weighted cap margin at $(p,X)$ is consequently $\varepsilon[f(p,y)+g\cdot(X-y)]\ge\varepsilon f(p,X)\ge0$, so at least one closed cap contains $(p,X)$.
