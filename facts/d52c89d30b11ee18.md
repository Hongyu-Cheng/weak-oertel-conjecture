## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb R^n\) be nonempty and finite, and give every \(\mathbf j\in D\) a weight \(w_{\mathbf j}>0\). Put \(W=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}\) and \(p_{\mathbf j}=w_{\mathbf j}/W\). For every target \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf y)&=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},
&T_w(\mathbf y)&=W\tau_p(\mathbf y),\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}p_{\mathbf j}\tau_p(\mathbf j)^2,
&F_2(w)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}T_w(\mathbf j)^2=W^3M_2(p).
\end{aligned}
\]
All halfspaces in this packet are closed ambient halfspaces; restricting to proper halfspaces gives the same infimum. For \(s>0\), put \(A_s=\{\mathbf j\in D:w_{\mathbf j}\ge s\}\) and define the unnormalized integer counting depth \(d_s(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}|A_s\cap H|\). This definition applies whether or not \(\mathbf y\in A_s\). In particular \(d_s\equiv0\) if \(A_s\) is empty; it is not defined to be zero merely because the target is unsupported. Then
\[
F_2(w)\ge3\int_0^\infty s^2\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf j)^2\,ds.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose additionally that \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) and every nonempty \(A_s\) is complete lattice-convex, meaning \(A_s=\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Write \(N(s)=|A_s|\) and \(r(s)=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_s)\) at a nonempty level. At empty levels set \(r(s)=0\) and interpret all summands below as zero. One has
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_2(w)
&\ge\frac34\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)\bigl(2^{-r(s)}N(s)+1\bigr)^2\,ds\\
&\ge\frac34\,4^{-n}\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)^3\,ds
+\frac32\,2^{-n}\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)^2\,ds
+\frac14\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The rank-zero convention is substantive: a nonempty finite set of affine dimension zero is a singleton, whose counting depth at its supported target is one. Equivalently its normalized depth is one. No minimization over an empty set of nonzero relative normals is intended.

For a finite lattice set \(A\), use the oriented, zero-inclusive reflection count
\(r_A(\mathbf j)=|\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n:\mathbf j+\mathbf v,\mathbf j-\mathbf v\in A\}|\).
Here \(r_A(\mathbf j)\) is a reflection count, whereas \(r(s)\) above is an affine dimension. Extend any lattice table's weights by zero to \(\mathbb Z^n\), and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathbf j}&=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\},\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)&=\frac34\int_0^\infty s^2\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_s}\bigl(r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)+1\bigr)^2\,ds,\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w)&=3\int_0^\infty s^2\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_s}r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)^2\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every finite positive lattice table, even without completeness,
\[
0\le\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)
\le\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w)
\le\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}S_{\mathbf j}^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover (1) always implies \(F_2(w)\ge\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\). If the superlevel sets are complete, the first lower bound in (2) is at most \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\).

There is no absolute \(c>0\) such that either \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\ge c4^{-n}W^3\) or \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w)\ge c4^{-n}W^3\) holds for every finite complete log-affine lattice table. Here this class consists of tables on \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), with \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) a compact convex polytope and \(\log w\) the restriction of an affine function on \(P\). The same two universal lower bounds fail on actual positive fiber-volume tables of full-dimensional compact convex bodies, allowing their integer and continuous dimensions to vary. Both failures follow from the exact families of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df.

## Proof

For any finite set, the intersections with halfspaces form a finite family of subsets. Consequently all infima in the definitions are attained among finitely many mass or cardinality values; in particular \(d_s(\mathbf y)\) is an integer. A containing proper halfspace exists for every target because \(n\ge1\), and adding the whole space cannot lower an infimum. Any containing proper halfspace contains a parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through the target, so the definitions also agree with the canonical through-target closed-halfspace convention.

The sets \(A_s\) are nested as \(s\) increases. Thus \(d_s(\mathbf y)\) is nonnegative and nonincreasing in \(s\), including when \(\mathbf y\notin A_s\). Each of these functions is a bounded step function, changing only at the finitely many positive weight values and vanishing above their maximum. All integrals below are therefore finite; values at isolated thresholds or at zero do not affect them.

Finite layer cake gives, for every \(H\ni\mathbf y\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}w_{\mathbf x}
=\int_0^\infty |A_s\cap H|\,ds
\ge\int_0^\infty d_s(\mathbf y)\,ds.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves \(T_w(\mathbf y)\ge\int_0^\infty d_s(\mathbf y)\,ds\). There is no interchange of an infimum and an integral in this argument.

Use this inequality, square nonnegative quantities, and express the factor \(w_{\mathbf j}\) by a third layer-cake variable. With \(u=\max\{a,s,t\}\), nesting gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_2(w)
&\ge\int_{[0,\infty)^3}\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}
\mathbf1_{\{a\le w_{\mathbf j}\}}d_s(\mathbf j)d_t(\mathbf j)\,da\,ds\,dt\\
&\ge\int_{[0,\infty)^3}\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_u}d_u(\mathbf j)^2\,da\,ds\,dt\\
&=3\int_0^\infty u^2\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_u}d_u(\mathbf j)^2\,du.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(\mathbf j\in A_u\) implies \(a\le w_{\mathbf j}\), and \(s,t\le u\) implies both depth factors are at least \(d_u(\mathbf j)\). For the last equality, the volume of \(\{(a,s,t)\ge0:\max(a,s,t)\le u\}\) is \(u^3\). Its derivative is \(3u^2\); alternatively the identity follows first for interval indicators by subtracting cube volumes and then for the step function here. This proves (1) and its exact normalization by \(W^3\).

We next prove the counting and parity estimate. Let \(A\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, let \(K=|A|\), and fix \(\mathbf j\in A\). The zero displacement occurs once in \(r_A(\mathbf j)\); its other displacements split into pairs \(\{\mathbf v,-\mathbf v\}\). A containing closed halfspace contains the center and at least one point from every antipodal pair, since the center is their midpoint. Distinct displacement pairs use distinct endpoints. Therefore
\[
d_A(\mathbf j)\ge\frac{r_A(\mathbf j)+1}{2},
\tag{5}
\]
where \(d_A\) means the same unnormalized counting depth as above with fixed support \(A\). This step requires no completeness. It proves \(F_2(w)\ge\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\) from (1).

Now assume \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and put \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\). For each ambient parity class \(\epsilon\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(K_\epsilon\) count the points of \(A\) in that class. Ordered same-parity endpoints \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in A^2\) correspond bijectively to the pairs counted by \(\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}r_A(\mathbf j)\), via
\(\mathbf j=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\), \(\mathbf v=(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)/2\).
Parity makes both vectors integral, and convexity and completeness put the midpoint in \(A\). The inverse is \((\mathbf j,\mathbf v)\mapsto(\mathbf j+\mathbf v,\mathbf j-\mathbf v)\). Diagonal endpoints are included exactly once.

At most \(2^r\) parity classes meet \(A\). To prove this without an assumption about a lattice basis, fix \(\mathbf a\in A\) and form the integer matrix whose columns are \(\mathbf x-\mathbf a\), for \(\mathbf x\in A\). Its real rank is \(r\). Its rank over \(\mathbb F_2\) is at most \(r\), because a nonzero minor over \(\mathbb F_2\) has an odd integer determinant and is also nonzero over \(\mathbb R\). The parity classes of \(A\) lie in a translate of that column span over \(\mathbb F_2\), which has at most \(2^r\) elements. Cauchy-Schwarz consequently gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}r_A(\mathbf j)
=\sum_\epsilon K_\epsilon^2
\ge2^{-r}K^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}d_A(\mathbf j)^2
\ge\frac14\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}(r_A(\mathbf j)+1)^2
\ge\frac K4(2^{-r}K+1)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality applies Cauchy-Schwarz to the \(K\) nonnegative numbers \(r_A(\mathbf j)+1\), then uses the first inequality. When \(r=0\), the set is a singleton, and every displayed inequality in (5)-(6) is equality. In the original ambient definition its depth at that point is one; at any different target its counting depth is zero by a separating halfspace. If one computes depths in the affine hull instead, for positive rank every relative proper halfspace extends to an ambient one, and every containing ambient halfspace restricts to a relative halfspace or the whole affine hull. Thus the supported depths agree. For rank zero the declared singleton convention gives that same agreement.

Apply (6) to every nonempty \(A_s\) and integrate in (1). This proves the first bound in (2), and proves that this bound is at most \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\). Since \(r(s)\le n\), replacing \(r(s)\) by \(n\) weakens the bound. Expanding the resulting square gives its two displayed integral terms and \((3/4)\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)\,ds\). Finite summation gives
\(3\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)\,ds=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}^3\),
which supplies the diagonal coefficient \(1/4\) in (2).

For the reflected obstruction, first consider any finite positive lattice table. The reflection count \(r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)\) is nonnegative and nonincreasing in \(s\). Counting its finitely many displacements and integrating gives the exact identity
\[
\int_0^\infty r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)\,ds
=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\}
=S_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{7}
\]
At every supported target in \(A_s\), one has \(r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)\ge1\), so \((r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)+1)/2\le r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)\). This proves \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)\le\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w)\). Apply precisely the cube-volume comparison in (4) to the nonincreasing functions \(r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)\), using (7) in place of the depth lower bound. It gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}S_{\mathbf j}^2
&=\int_{[0,\infty)^3}\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}
\mathbf1_{\{a\le w_{\mathbf j}\}}r_{A_s}(\mathbf j)r_{A_t}(\mathbf j)\,da\,ds\,dt\\
&\ge3\int_0^\infty u^2\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_u}r_{A_u}(\mathbf j)^2\,du
=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The restriction to \(\mathbf j\in A_u\) is justified by the indicator exactly as before, even if the table is not complete. This proves (3).

We now apply the accepted construction 604889294a17d7df. Its ambient integer dimension is denoted \(N\), which replaces the present general dimension \(n\), and its complete support \(\widehat A\) is the present \(D\). Its \(w_{\mathbf J}\), \(W\), and oriented zero-inclusive scalar \(S_{\mathbf J}\) are exactly the weights, total mass, and scalar in (7), without a normalization change. For each integer \(k\ge8\), take its exact profile \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\), with all entrance counts and dimensions prescribed by that fact. It supplies a compact convex polytope \(\widehat P\) and the complete table \(D=\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^N\), with affine logarithm on \(\widehat P\), such that
\[
\max_{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^N}S_{\mathbf J}=1,
\qquad
\frac W{2^N}\ge\frac3{64}(H_k-H_7)\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad H_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\frac1\ell.
\tag{9}
\]
Every superlevel set is complete: it is the integer intersection of \(\widehat P\) with an affine-logarithm superlevel halfspace, hence the integer intersection of a convex set. Equation (3) and (9) imply
\[
0\le\frac{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ref}}(w)}{4^{-N}W^3}
\le\frac{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{raw}}(w)}{4^{-N}W^3}
\le\frac{W}{4^{-N}W^3}
=\left(\frac{2^N}{W}\right)^2
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
This disproves either proposed positive absolute lower bound on the complete log-affine class.

The same accepted fact also supplies, for each \(k\ge8\), the exact body \(C_{k,k^3}\), with its support and all entrance counts rebuilt for the profile \(\rho_t=(1-t/k^3)^{k^3}\). Its integer dimension \(N\) may differ from that of the preceding log-affine specialization. Its weights are exactly its positive continuous fiber volumes, including all zeros outside its complete support, and it again satisfies (9). These are full-dimensional rational compact convex bodies. Their positive tables admit a log-concave extension on their convex support, so their superlevel sets are complete as well. Applying (3) to these exact tables gives (10) again. No approximation of their weights by the log-affine profile is used.

Neither (3) nor (10) bounds \(F_2(w)\) above: (1) and (5) provide lower bounds for it. Thus the reflection obstruction does not refute the actual-depth integral route, the second-moment hypothesis, or the locked weak Oertel statement.
