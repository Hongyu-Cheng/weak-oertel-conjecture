## Statement

For every integer \(L\ge1\) there are an integer \(n\ge1\), a finite nonempty support \(A\subset\mathbb Z^n\), an original positive probability law \(p\) on \(A\), and a threshold \(T\ge0\) with the following properties. Put \(K=2^n\). Every coordinatewise parity class contains at most two supported atoms, every distinct same-parity pair has its midpoint in \(A\), and the exact identity \(p_j^2=p_u p_v\) holds for every such pair with midpoint \(j\). There is no restriction on center multiplicity.

For each pair \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\), let \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\) and \(y_e=(1/2)\log(p_{u_e}/p_{v_e})\); its absolute score is independent of the endpoint ordering. Let \(d_T(j)\) count the pairs centered at \(j\) with \(|y_e|\le T\), and let \(d_{\rm full}(j)\) count all pairs centered at \(j\). Retain the central atom in every row and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_T(j)&=(1+2d_T(j))p_j,
&b_T&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_T(j)^2,\\
H_{\rm full}(j)&=(1+2d_{\rm full}(j))p_j,
&b_{\rm full}&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_{\rm full}(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
These full-row quantities also equal the genuine oriented reflected sums, because all supported reflected pairs obey exact equality. The constructed objects satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_T:=1-K^2b_T&>0,
&\mathcal E_{\rm full}:=K^2b_{\rm full}-1&>0,\\
\frac{\delta_T}{\mathcal E_{\rm full}}&\ge\frac{3^{-L}}{1818},
&e^{-T}&\le\frac32\left(\frac4{27}\right)^{2^L}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently, for every pair of constants \(a>0\) and \(C>0\), some member of this sequence satisfies
$$
(1-K^2b_T)_+>C e^{-aT}(K^2b_{\rm full}-1).
$$
The parameter \(K\) is the ambient parity capacity \(2^n\) throughout, and every original probability is retained in all these expressions.

## Proof

Fix \(L\ge1\) and set \(c=1/9\). For \(0<t\le c\) define
$$
\alpha(t)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,
\qquad d(t)=\left\lfloor\frac{1/t-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$
The defining equation gives \(\alpha(t)(1-\alpha(t))=t^2\). Rationalization, or this equation itself, gives
$$
t^2\le\alpha(t)\le\frac43t^2<t,
\qquad
\frac1{3t}\le d(t)\le\frac1{2t},
\qquad (2d(t)+1)t\le1.
\tag{1}
$$
For the upper bound on \(\alpha(t)\), first note that \(\alpha(t)<1/4\) for \(t\le1/9\), so \(\alpha(t)=t^2/(1-\alpha(t))<4t^2/3\). For the lower degree bound, the floor is greater than or equal to \((1/t-3)/2\), and this is at least \(1/(3t)\) when \(t\le1/9\). In particular every degree in (1) is a positive integer.

Define \(w_0=c\) and \(w_{\ell+1}=\alpha(w_\ell)\) for \(0\le\ell\le L\). These are raw weights, not the dimensionless probabilities \(Kp_x\). Let \(N_0=1\). For \(0\le\ell<L\) define
$$
d_\ell=d(w_\ell),\qquad N_{\ell+1}=N_\ell d_\ell,
\qquad M_\ell=N_\ell w_\ell.
$$
Also put \(M_L=N_Lw_L\), and assign the final degree
$$
d_L=\left\lceil\frac1{N_Lw_L^2}\right\rceil.
\tag{2}
$$
For the prior generations, (1) implies
$$
\frac13M_\ell\le M_{\ell+1}\le\frac23M_\ell.
$$
It follows that
$$
c3^{-L}\le M_L\le c(2/3)^L,
\qquad \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}M_\ell\le3c=\frac13.
\tag{3}
$$

We next give the exact finite lattice support. There is one tiny atom at level zero, and \(N_\ell\) tiny atoms at level \(\ell\). Every level-\(\ell\) tiny atom for \(0\le\ell\le L\) is the center of \(d_\ell\) pairs. Each new pair has one tiny endpoint of raw weight \(w_{\ell+1}\) and one large endpoint of raw weight \(1-w_{\ell+1}\). Let
$$
m=\sum_{\ell=0}^L N_\ell d_\ell,
\qquad n=\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil,
\qquad K=2^n.
\tag{4}
$$
Choose distinct nonzero Boolean vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\); (4) provides enough. Start with the origin as the level-zero tiny atom, of raw weight \(c\). Process centers by level. For a center \(j\), use the next \(d_\ell\) unused labels, and for each such label \(v_r\) create the pair with endpoints
$$
v_r\quad\hbox{of raw weight }w_{\ell+1},
\qquad
2j-v_r\quad\hbox{of raw weight }1-w_{\ell+1}.
\tag{5}
$$
The Boolean endpoint \(v_r\) is a new tiny atom at the next level. Level \(L+1\) tiny atoms are terminal and create no pairs. Include every remaining nonzero Boolean vector as a singleton atom of raw weight one. Call the resulting support \(A\) and its assigned raw weights \(w_x\).

All assignments in this construction are consistent. A processed center \(j\) is either zero or a Boolean label used earlier, so \(j\) differs from its new label \(v_r\). The partner \(2j-v_r\) has parity \(v_r\) but is not Boolean: the unique Boolean point of that parity is \(v_r\) itself, and equality to it would force \(j=v_r\). Partners from distinct pairs have distinct parities and therefore are distinct. Thus each used nonzero parity class consists exactly of the endpoints of its assigned pair, each unused nonzero class is its Boolean singleton, and the zero class is the singleton origin. In particular all \(K\) parity classes are occupied and every class has at most two atoms.

The midpoint of each assigned pair is its prescribed supported center \(j\). Conversely, two distinct supported atoms have an integer midpoint only if they have the same coordinatewise parity, and then they form exactly one of the assigned pairs. This proves the required midpoint closure and enumerates every pair; it also enumerates all nontrivial reflected rows. There are precisely \(N_\ell\) centers at level \(\ell\), each of degree \(d_\ell\), and every other supported atom has a singleton reflected row.

Every nonzero parity class has raw total weight one, whereas the zero class has raw total weight \(c\). Consequently the original normalizer and law are exactly
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x=K-1+c,
\qquad p_x=\frac{w_x}{Z}.
\tag{6}
$$
All raw weights are positive. In each level-\(\ell\) pair, its midpoint has weight \(w_\ell\) and its endpoint product is \(w_{\ell+1}(1-w_{\ell+1})=w_\ell^2\). Division by the same original \(Z\) proves \(p_j^2=p_u p_v\) for every pair, including the pairs that will be discarded. No change of law or normalizer occurs when selecting rows.

The absolute score of every level-\(\ell\) pair is
$$
y_\ell=\frac12\log\frac{1-w_{\ell+1}}{w_{\ell+1}}
=\log\frac{1-w_{\ell+1}}{w_\ell}.
\tag{7}
$$
The weights \(w_\ell\) strictly decrease, so these scores strictly increase with \(\ell\). Choose
$$
T=\log\frac1{2w_L}.
\tag{8}
$$
This is positive because \(w_L\le c\). For \(\ell<L\), (1) gives \(2w_L<w_{L-1}\), and hence
$$
y_\ell<\log\frac1{w_\ell}
\le\log\frac1{w_{L-1}}<T.
$$
For \(\ell=L\), the inequality \(w_{L+1}<1/2\) gives \(y_L>T\). Thus the threshold retains exactly all pairs from levels zero through \(L-1\) and discards exactly the level-\(L\) pairs. It retains every central atom as required.

We now bound the prescribed energies with their original normalization. Let \(J\) denote the raw atomic cubic sum, and let \(I_{\rm ret}\) and \(I_{\rm last}\) be the raw row increments from the retained and final generations. Because endpoints of distinct assigned pairs are disjoint, their endpoint cubes sum to \(1-3w_\ell^2\) per level-\(\ell\) pair. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
J&=\sum_{x\in A}w_x^3
=K-1+c^3-3\sum_{\ell=0}^L N_\ell d_\ell w_\ell^2,\\
I_{\rm ret}&=4\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}N_\ell d_\ell(d_\ell+1)w_\ell^3,\\
I_{\rm last}&=4N_Ld_L(d_L+1)w_L^3,\\
K^2b_T&=\frac{K^2}{Z^3}(J+I_{\rm ret}),
\qquad
K^2b_{\rm full}=\frac{K^2}{Z^3}(J+I_{\rm ret}+I_{\rm last}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The row increment formula uses \(((2d+1)^2-1)w^3=4d(d+1)w^3\). A tiny center that is also an earlier endpoint contributes to \(J\) only once, and (9) adds only its reflected-row increment.

For \(\ell<L\), the last inequality in (1) implies
$$
4d_\ell(d_\ell+1)w_\ell^2
=(2d_\ell+1)^2w_\ell^2-w_\ell^2\le1.
$$
Consequently \(I_{\rm ret}\le\sum_{\ell<L}M_\ell\le1/3\) by (3). The definition (2) implies \(N_Ld_Lw_L^2\ge1\). All other terms in the pair-square sum in (9) are nonnegative, so
$$
J+I_{\rm ret}\le K-\frac{11}3+c^3.
\tag{10}
$$
Since \(Z=K-8/9\), expansion of its cube gives
$$
\frac{Z^3}{K^2}\ge K-\frac83.
\tag{11}
$$
Indeed, the omitted part is \(3(8/9)^2/K-(8/9)^3/K^2\ge0\). Combining (9)-(11), and using \(Z<K\), proves
$$
\delta_T=\frac{K^2}{Z^3}
\left(\frac{Z^3}{K^2}-J-I_{\rm ret}\right)
\ge\frac{1-c^3}{K}>0.
\tag{12}
$$

For the full increment put \(\rho=N_Ld_Lw_L^2\). The ceiling in (2) gives
$$
1\le\rho<1+N_Lw_L^2=1+M_Lw_L<2,
$$
where \(M_Lw_L\le c^2<1\). A direct rearrangement yields
$$
I_{\rm last}=\frac{4\rho^2}{M_L}+4\rho w_L
<\frac{24}{M_L}.
\tag{13}
$$
For the last inequality use \(\rho<2\) and \(M_Lw_L<1\). From (10) one has \(J+I_{\rm ret}\le K\). Equations (9), (11), and (13), together with \(Z\ge K/2\), give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\rm full}
&\le\frac8K\left(\frac{24}{M_L}+\frac83\right)\\
&=\frac{192+(64/3)M_L}{KM_L}
\le\frac{200}{KM_L},
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
since \(M_L\le1/9\). Here \(K\ge2\), so \(Z=K-8/9\ge K/2\).

The full excess is strictly positive, which we verify rather than infer from the upper bound. Write \(r_\eta\) for the original parity masses. Exact equality on all pairs and the complete pair enumeration give
$$
\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_{\rm full}(j)
=\sum_{x\in A}p_x^2+2\sum_e p_{j_e}^2
=\sum_\eta r_\eta^2>\frac1K.
\tag{15}
$$
The strict inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz because the \(K\) masses are \(c/Z\) in the zero class and \(1/Z\) in every other class, and \(c\) differs from one. Jensen under the original probability law \(p\) then gives \(b_{\rm full}>1/K^2\). Thus \(\mathcal E_{\rm full}>0\). Dividing (12) by (14), using \(1-c^3>200/202\) and then (3), proves
$$
\frac{\delta_T}{\mathcal E_{\rm full}}
\ge\frac{M_L}{202}
\ge\frac{3^{-L}}{1818}.
\tag{16}
$$

Finally, the recurrence \(w_{\ell+1}\le(4/3)w_\ell^2\) implies by induction
$$
w_L\le\frac34\left(\frac{4c}3\right)^{2^L}
=\frac34\left(\frac4{27}\right)^{2^L}.
$$
By (8), \(e^{-T}=2w_L\), proving the stated doubly exponential bound. For every fixed \(a>0\), (16) therefore yields
$$
\frac{\delta_T}{e^{-aT}\mathcal E_{\rm full}}
\ge\frac1{1818}\left(\frac23\right)^a
3^{-L}\left(\frac{27}4\right)^{a2^L}
\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$
This exceeds any prescribed \(C\) for some finite \(L\). Every support, dimension, degree, raw weight, and original normalizer used for that \(L\) has been specified by exact finite formulas. The verification proves precisely parity midpoint closure and full midpoint equality; it does not assume or claim convex completeness, global affine weights, or a violation of the locked depth statement.
