## Statement

Let \(I=\lbrace1,2,3,4,5\rbrace\) and
$$
E=\lbrace(i,k):1\le i<k\le5\rbrace,\qquad
A_E=\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^5:x_i-x_k\le1\text{ for }i<k\rbrace.
$$
Thus the underlying graph is \(K_5\) and has degree four. Give this entire unmasked support the original affine law
$$
t_1=t_2=t_3=t_4=1,\qquad t_5=\frac15,\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in A_E}\prod_{i=1}^5t_i^{x_i},\qquad
p_x=\frac{5^{-x_5}}{Z}.
$$
Equivalently its field is \(\ell=(0,0,0,0,-\log5)\). Retain precisely the full rows and canonical quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
R_j=\lbrace x\in A_E:2j-x\in A_E\rbrace,\qquad
r_j=|R_j|,\qquad
H_p(j)=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},
$$
$$
b=\sum_{j\in A_E}p_jH_p(j)^2
 =\sum_{j\in A_E}p_j^3r_j^2,\qquad
q_j=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b}=\frac{p_j^3r_j^2}{b}.
$$
Use exactly the free-coordinate definition from accepted fact eace90aa0a930703:
$$
F(j)=\lbrace i:j_i=1,\quad
 j_k\ne0\text{ for all }k>i,\quad
 j_k\ne2\text{ for all }k<i\rbrace.
$$
Then
$$
Z=\frac{688}{25},\qquad
4^5b=\frac{253948}{79507},\qquad
\mathbb E_q|F(J)|=\frac{207737}{63487},
$$
and, for natural logarithms,
$$
\log(4^5b)-2\log(6/5)\mathbb E_q|F(J)|
 <-\frac{97189}{4190142}<0.
$$
For every positive integer \(m\), the disjoint union of \(m\) copies with the repeated factors has dimension \(n=5m\), underlying degree four, and balance strictly less than
$$
-\frac{97189}{20950710}\,n.
$$
Consequently no absolute \(\varepsilon>0\) can satisfy the proposed lower bound \(\log(4^nb)\ge2\log(6/5)\mathbb E_q|F(J)|+\varepsilon n\) throughout the degree-at-most-five class, or even throughout its degree-at-most-four subclass.

## Proof

All support points and rows below are unmasked. For this affine law, whenever \(x,2j-x\in A_E\),
$$
\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=\frac{5^{-j_5}}Z=p_j.
$$
Thus \(H_p(j)=p_jr_j\), and the expressions for \(b,q\) in the statement are exactly those of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, with its single-block specialization \(A_1=A_E\), \(w_{1,x}=5^{-x_5}\), \(Z_1=Z\), and \(p_1=p\). In particular no auxiliary law replaces the original \(p\), and the central row term is included once.

A ternary word belongs to \(A_E\) if and only if no coordinate equal to two precedes a coordinate equal to zero. For a supported word \(j\), let \(a\) be the position of its last zero, or zero if it has no zero; let \(c\) be the position of its first two, or six if it has no two. Then \(a<c\). The free-coordinate definition gives
$$
F(j)=\lbrace a+1,\ldots,c-1\rbrace,\qquad
f:=|F(j)|=c-a-1.
$$
Indeed every coordinate between these positions is one, has no later zero and no earlier two. A coordinate of value one at or before \(a\) has a later zero, and one at or after \(c\) has an earlier two.

The full row census in the proof of accepted fact eace90aa0a930703 applies, since this graph has degree four. Its displacement spins vanish outside \(F(j)\), and on the complete induced graph on these \(f\) coordinates they belong to \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\), with opposite nonzero spins forbidden at any two distinct coordinates. Hence an allowed row displacement either uses only zero and plus one, or uses only zero and minus one. There are \(2^f\) choices of each type, with their sole overlap the all-zero displacement, so
$$
r_j=2^{f+1}-1.
\tag{1}
$$
This includes \(f=0\), whose row has just its center.

The following table counts all centers by their last coordinate \(s=j_5\) and their free-coordinate count \(f\). Each column heading is a value of \(f\).

| \(s\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 16 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 16 |
| 1 | 20 | 16 | 7 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 48 |
| 2 | 28 | 12 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 48 |

Here is a direct counting rule for the table. For prescribed \(a,c\), the positions strictly before \(a\), when \(a>0\), are arbitrary zeroes and ones; position \(a\) is zero. Positions strictly between \(a,c\) are all one. Position \(c\), when \(c<6\), is two, and positions strictly after it are arbitrary ones and twos. Boundary indices zero and six contribute no coordinate. This rule describes every supported word uniquely. Sorting these choices by \(s\) and \(c-a-1\) gives the displayed entries; it also proves that there are \(112\) centers in the entire support.

For a variable factor \(t>0\) at coordinate five and factors one elsewhere, the table and (1) give the following three finite sums:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z(t)&=\sum_{j\in A_E}t^{j_5}=16+48t+48t^2,\\
B(t)&=\sum_{j\in A_E}t^{3j_5}r_j^2
      =16+6112t^3+1792t^6,\\
T(t)&=\sum_{j\in A_E}t^{3j_5}r_j^2|F(j)|
      =26544t^3+5792t^6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
The temporary sums \(B(t),T(t)\) are unnormalized numerators, not laws or replacements for \(b\). At \(t=1/5\) they equal
$$
Z=\frac{688}{25},\qquad
B(1/5)=\frac{1015792}{15625},\qquad
T(1/5)=\frac{3323792}{15625}.
$$
The original normalizer is retained cubically:
$$
b=\frac{B(1/5)}{Z^3},\qquad
\mathbb E_q|F(J)|=\frac{T(1/5)}{B(1/5)}.
$$
Exact simplification proves the two rational values in the statement.

For independent verification of the whole support, every full row, the free counts, and these rational sums, the following standalone Python certificate uses only integer arithmetic and `Fraction`. Its row calculation directly checks \(x,2j-x\in A_E\), independently of the row formula (1) and the preceding counting rule.

```python
from collections import Counter
from fractions import Fraction as Q
from itertools import product

n = 5
E = [(i, k) for i in range(n) for k in range(i + 1, n)]
A = [x for x in product(range(3), repeat=n)
     if all(x[i] - x[k] <= 1 for i, k in E)]
support = set(A)
rows = {j: sum(tuple(2*j[i] - x[i] for i in range(n)) in support
               for x in A) for j in A}
free = {j: sum(j[i] == 1
               and all(j[k] != 0 for u, k in E if u == i)
               and all(j[u] != 2 for u, k in E if k == i)
               for i in range(n)) for j in A}
counts = Counter((j[4], free[j]) for j in A)
expected = [[16, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0],
            [20, 16, 7, 3, 1, 1],
            [28, 12, 5, 2, 1, 0]]
assert len(A) == 112
assert [[counts[s, f] for f in range(6)] for s in range(3)] == expected
assert all(rows[j] == 2**(free[j] + 1) - 1 for j in A)
w = {j: Q(1, 5)**j[4] for j in A}
Z = sum(w.values())
B = sum(w[j]**3 * rows[j]**2 for j in A)
T = sum(w[j]**3 * rows[j]**2 * free[j] for j in A)
assert (Z, B, T) == (Q(688, 25), Q(1015792, 15625), Q(3323792, 15625))
assert 4**n * B / Z**3 == Q(253948, 79507)
assert T / B == Q(207737, 63487)
z = Q(11, 21)
upper = 2*(z + z**3/3 + z**5/(5*(1-z*z)))
assert upper == Q(25897553, 22226400)
assert Q(253948, 79507) < Q(16, 5)
assert upper < Q(7, 6)
assert Q(7, 6) - Q(4, 11)*T/B == -Q(97189, 4190142)
```

It remains to certify the logarithmic sign without floating-point arithmetic. For \(0<z<1\), integration of the geometric series, or its convergent power series, gives
$$
\log\frac{1+z}{1-z}
 =2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}
 \le2\left(z+\frac{z^3}{3}+\frac{z^5}{5(1-z^2)}\right).
\tag{3}
$$
Indeed every denominator in the tail beginning at \(z^5\) is at least five. At \(z=11/21\), this proves
$$
\log(16/5)
 \le\frac{25897553}{22226400}
 =\frac76-\frac{33247}{22226400}<\frac76.
$$
The exact rational comparison \(253948/79507<16/5\), whose difference is \(2372/397535\), therefore gives
\(\log(4^5b)<7/6\). At \(z=1/11\), the positive terms beyond the first term of the same series give \(\log(6/5)>2/11\). Combining these rigorous bounds,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(4^5b)-2\log(6/5)\mathbb E_q|F(J)|
&<\frac76-\frac4{11}\frac{207737}{63487}\\
&=-\frac{97189}{4190142}<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
No numerical minimization or numerical logarithm is needed for (4).

Finally take \(m\) disjoint copies of this directed graph and repeat the five factors on each component. There are no constraints between components, so its full support is \(A_E^m\), its original normalizer is \(Z^m\), and its original affine law is \(p^{\otimes m}\). Full reflected rows are Cartesian products, so their cardinalities multiply. The free-coordinate sets are disjoint unions, so their sizes add. It follows directly from the definitions that \(b\) becomes \(b^m\), that the full canonical center law becomes \(q^{\otimes m}\), and that the balance becomes \(m\) times (4). Since the dimension is \(5m\), this is the claimed negative linear bound. The underlying degree remains four. This completes the obstruction to the proposed balance only.
