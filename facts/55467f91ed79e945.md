## Statement

Let $r\ge1$ and $\beta\ge r+1$. Let $f$ be a Lebesgue density of mass $M\in(0,\infty)$, positive on a nonempty open convex set $U\subset\mathbb R^r$ and zero outside $U$. Assume $f^{-1/\beta}$ is finite, positive and convex on $U$. Let finitely many proper closed halfspaces $H_i$ satisfy $U\subset\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}H_i$, and write $A_i=\int_{H_i}f$ and $\gamma_r=(r/(r+1))^r$. For every $\alpha>0$,
$$
\sum_i(A_i-\alpha)_+ + \frac{\alpha}{\gamma_r}>M,
\qquad x_+=\max\{x,0\}.
$$

## Proof

The power $f^{-1/(r+1)}=(f^{-1/\beta})^{\beta/(r+1)}$ is convex by composition with an increasing convex function. The hereditary-depth argument at the beginning of 23a563a735c07c25 applies to this power. In particular, for any closed convex $L$ with positive $f$-mass it gives a point in $U\cap\operatorname{int}L$ of closed-halfspace depth at least $\gamma_r\int_L f$ for the restricted measure. The argument uses compact convex exhaustion of $U\cap\operatorname{int}L$, continuity of depth and vanishing tails, so it includes unbounded $U$ and $L$.

Use the distal trimming construction of ac7ba1e1e0ebc955. Write $H_i=\{u_i\cdot x\ge b_i\}$. For each $A_i>\alpha$, remove a parallel tail $\{u_i\cdot x>t_i\}$ with $t_i>b_i$ and $f$-mass $A_i-\alpha$. These thresholds exist by continuity and the vanishing of the tails at infinity. Put $L=\bigcap_{i:A_i>\alpha}\{u_i\cdot x\le t_i\}$, taking all space if no cap is trimmed. If $R=\sum_i(A_i-\alpha)_+$, then $\int_L f\ge M-R$, and every $H_i$ captures at most $\alpha$ of the restricted measure. The inequality is immediate when $M-R\le0$ because $\alpha>0$.

Otherwise take the hereditary depth point $y\in U\cap\operatorname{int}L$. It lies in $\operatorname{int}H_i$ for some $i$. Moving this cap's boundary parallel to itself through $y$ leaves a subcap $H_i^y$ and removes positive mass from $L\cap H_i$. In fact a small ball about $y$ lies in $U\cap\operatorname{int}L\cap\operatorname{int}H_i$, and $f$ is bounded below on the ball by a positive constant because its positive convex inverse power is continuous there. The half of the ball outside $H_i^y$ has positive volume. Hence
$$
\alpha\ge\int_{L\cap H_i}f
>\int_{L\cap H_i^y}f
\ge\gamma_r\int_L f
\ge\gamma_r(M-R).
$$
Rearranging proves the claim.
