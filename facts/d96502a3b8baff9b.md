## Statement

Let \(q:=1\), let \(r,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(n:=q+r\) and \(m:=r+d\), and write points of \(\mathbb R^{n+d}=\mathbb R^{m+1}\) as \((\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\). Here \(m\) denotes the partial-fiber dimension; the ambient dimension denoted by \(m\) in accepted fact 8444e75361af26b0 is the present \(m+1=n+d\). Set \(\Lambda_q:=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^{r+d}\) and \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d\).

For Borel \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\), define \(A_\eta:=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):(\eta,\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in A\}\), \(A_{j,\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x:(j,\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in A\}\), and
\[
\mu_q(A)=\mu_1(A):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{vol}_m(A_j),
\qquad
\mu_n(A):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^r}
                 \operatorname{vol}_d(A_{j,\mathbf z}).
\]
All volumes use the displayed Euclidean coordinates. Thus \(\mu_n(A)=\mathcal H_d(A\cap\Lambda)\) in the locked normalization, while \(\mu_1\) is the specified partial mixed measure. Let \(\pi_r\) denote projection onto the \(\mathbf t\) coordinates.

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\) be a convex body. Write \(F_\eta:=C_\eta\), \(g(\eta):=\operatorname{vol}_m(F_\eta)\), \(G:=\mu_1(C)=\sum_j g(j)\), and \(M:=\max_j g(j)\). Suppose \(G>0\), \(M\le G/12\), and for some \(\eta_0\in\mathbb R\), \(\mathbf a_0\in\mathbb R^r\), and \(R\ge3\), one has \(\mathbf a_0+R[-1,1]^r\subseteq\pi_r(F_{\eta_0})\). There is no integrality or positive \(m\)-volume assumption on this chosen section.

Put \(\widehat F_{\eta_0}:=C\cap(\{\eta_0\}\times\mathbb R^m)\), choose any \(\mathbf c_0\in\widehat F_{\eta_0}\), and define
\[
\alpha:=\frac3{2R},\qquad \beta:=\frac1{2R},\qquad
P:=\widehat F_{\eta_0}-\mathbf c_0,\qquad
E:=(1-\alpha)C+\alpha\mathbf c_0.
\]
Then \(P\subseteq\{0\}\times\mathbb R^m\) is compact and convex, contains \(\mathbf0\), and its \(\mathbf t\)-projection contains a translated cube of radius \(R\). The set \(E\) is a convex body, \(E+\alpha P\subseteq C\), \(\mu_1(E)>0\), and \(G\le\mu_1(C+\beta P)<\infty\). Moreover,
\[
\frac{\mu_1(E)}{\mu_1(C+\beta P)}
\ge \frac{21}{83}
       \left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m.
\tag{1}
\]

Set \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\), \(W:=\mu_n(C)\), and \(h_S(\mathbf p):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mu_n(C\cap H)\), with proper closed ambient halfspaces in the infimum. Let \(c_1:=e^{-1}-(1-e^{-1})\log(e/(e-1))\in(0,1/e)\), the constant supplied by accepted fact 758103b1282d6b91. There exists one \(\mathbf p\in S\), chosen before all halfspaces, such that
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge \frac{c_1}{2}\mu_1(E)
\ge \frac{21c_1}{166}
       \left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m W.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \(W>0\). If \(R\ge7m/(2r)=7(r+d)/(2r)\), then
\[
\frac{\mu_1(E)}{\mu_1(C+\beta P)}\ge\frac14\,2^{-r},
\qquad
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge\frac{c_1}{4}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{3}
\]
The hypotheses may be checked after an integer unimodular change of the \(n\) integer coordinates, acting identically on \(\mathbb R^d\). In that case \(\mu_1\) is defined in the chosen coordinate split, and the conclusion for full mixed measure and depth pulls back to the original coordinates.

The scalar estimates underlying (1) hold for every upper semicontinuous, compactly supported log-concave function \(f:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\). With \(I_f:=\int_{\mathbb R}f\), \(T_f:=\max_{\mathbb R}f\), \(G_f:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f(j)\), and \(M_f:=\max_{j\in\mathbb Z}f(j)\), they are
\[
\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f\!\left(\frac{j-b}{h}\right)-hI_f\right|
\le T_f\quad(h>0,\ b\in\mathbb R),
\qquad
M_f<G_f/2\ \Longrightarrow\
T_f\le\frac{M_f}{1-2M_f/G_f}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(G_f>0\) and \(M_f\le G_f/12\) imply \(T_f\le G_f/10\).

For an explicit family, take any \(r,d\ge1\) and \(R\ge7m/(2r)\), put \(B:=[-R,R]^r\times[-1,1]^d\) and \(\mathsf h:=8(m+1)\), and define
\[
C_{\mathrm{bi}}:=
\left\{(\eta,\mathbf u)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^m:
|\eta|\le\mathsf h,\quad
\mathbf u\in\left(1-\frac{|\eta|}{\mathsf h}\right)B\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
This convex body satisfies the hypotheses above with \(\eta_0=0\), so it has the buffer and depth conclusions (1)--(3). However, whenever \(C_{\mathrm{bi}}=K+P_0\) with nonempty compact convex \(K,P_0\) and \(P_0\subseteq\{0\}\times\mathbb R^m\), the set \(P_0\) is a singleton.

## Proof

First prove the scalar assertions, including the sampling endpoints. Upper semicontinuity and compact support make \(f\) bounded and ensure that \(T_f\) is attained. If \(T_f=0\), the sampling assertion is immediate. For \(v\in(0,T_f]\), the weak superlevel set \(J_v:=\{u:f(u)\ge v\}\) is a compact interval, possibly a singleton or empty, by upper semicontinuity and log-concavity. Every nonempty closed bounded interval \([a,b]\) has \(\max(0,\lfloor b\rfloor-\lceil a\rceil+1)\) integer points, and this count differs from \(b-a\) by at most one. The same bound holds for the empty interval, and it includes singleton intervals and intervals whose endpoints are integers.

For fixed \(h>0\) and \(b\in\mathbb R\), the interval \(hJ_v+b\) therefore satisfies \(\left|\#((hJ_v+b)\cap\mathbb Z)-h\,\operatorname{vol}_1(J_v)\right|\le1\). Layer cake with the weak positive levels, followed by Tonelli, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}f\!\left(\frac{j-b}{h}\right)
&=\int_{(0,T_f]}\#((hJ_v+b)\cap\mathbb Z)\,dv,\\
hI_f&=\int_{(0,T_f]}h\,\operatorname{vol}_1(J_v)\,dv.
\end{aligned}
\]
The integrands are measurable: the count is a sum of indicators of the conditions \(f((j-b)/h)\ge v\), and the interval length is a superlevel-volume function. All functions integrated are nonnegative, and compact support makes both displayed quantities finite. Integrating the count error proves the first assertion of (4). The zero level is excluded, so the unbounded set \(\{f\ge0\}\) never enters the argument.

Now suppose \(M_f<G_f/2\). This entails \(G_f>0\) and \(M_f>0\), so the denominator in the second assertion of (4) is positive. If \(T_f=M_f\), that assertion follows immediately. Otherwise take a maximizer \(u_0\). It is not an integer, since \(T_f>M_f\), so \(k<u_0<k+1\) for some \(k\in\mathbb Z\). Put \(\rho:=M_f/T_f\in(0,1)\).

If \(f(k)>0\), log-concavity applied to the three points \(j,k,u_0\), for \(j<k\) with \(f(j)>0\), gives
\[
f(j)\le f(k)
 \left(\frac{f(k)}{T_f}\right)^{(k-j)/(u_0-k)}
\le M_f\rho^{\,k-j}.
\]
The last step uses \(0<u_0-k<1\), \(f(k)\le M_f\), and \(0<\rho<1\). The same final bound holds for \(j=k\) and for zero samples. If \(f(k)=0\), any positive sample at \(j<k\) together with \(f(u_0)>0\) would force \(f(k)>0\) by log-concavity, a contradiction; hence this whole left tail is zero. Thus in all cases \(f(j)\le M_f\rho^{k-j}\) for \(j\le k\).

The identical argument on the right, using \(0<k+1-u_0<1\), gives \(f(j)\le M_f\rho^{j-k-1}\) for \(j\ge k+1\), with a zero whole right tail if \(f(k+1)=0\). Summing the two geometric bounds yields \(G_f\le2M_f/(1-\rho)\). Consequently \(\rho\ge1-2M_f/G_f>0\), and \(T_f=M_f/\rho\le M_f/(1-2M_f/G_f)\), proving (4). If \(M_f\le G_f/12\), this gives \(T_f\le(G_f/12)/(1-1/6)=G_f/10\).

We next check the hypotheses of (4) for the actual marginal \(g\). The initial section-regularity argument in accepted fact 4cfd86cd551df9e7, before any projection-thickness assumption is used, proves that section volume to the reciprocal fiber dimension is concave on the base projection of any convex body. Apply that result with its body \(D=C\), its base dimension \(n=1\), and its fiber dimension \(d=m\). Its function \(f\) is the present \(g\), and its root function \(g\) is the present \(g^{1/m}\). Thus \(g^{1/m}\) is concave on the compact projection interval of \(C\).

For two positive-volume sections and \(0<\lambda<1\), this concavity and the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality imply
\[
g((1-\lambda)\eta+\lambda\eta')
\ge\left((1-\lambda)g(\eta)^{1/m}
              +\lambda g(\eta')^{1/m}\right)^m
\ge g(\eta)^{1-\lambda}g(\eta')^\lambda.
\]
If either endpoint has zero volume, the log-concavity inequality is automatic. Hence the function \(g\), defined as zero for empty sections, is log-concave on all of \(\mathbb R\).

For completeness, upper semicontinuity holds on all of \(\mathbb R\), including projection endpoints and empty limit sections. Choose one compact box in \(\mathbb R^m\) containing every \(F_\eta\). If \(\eta_i\to\eta\), closedness of \(C\) gives \(\limsup_i\mathbf1_{F_{\eta_i}}(\mathbf u)\le\mathbf1_{F_\eta}(\mathbf u)\) for every \(\mathbf u\). Reverse Fatou under the integrable indicator of that fixed box yields \(\limsup_i g(\eta_i)\le g(\eta)\). The projection interval is compact, so \(g\) also has compact support. The application of (4) is therefore justified without assuming continuity at a positive-volume endpoint section.

Put \(I:=\int_{\mathbb R}g\) and \(T:=\max_{\mathbb R}g\). The diffuseness hypothesis and (4) imply \(T\le G/10\), and the sampling estimate with \(h=1,b=0\) gives \(G-T\le I\le G+T\).

Let \(s:=1-\alpha\) and \(t:=1+\beta\), so \(1/2\le s<1<t\le7/6\). Since \(\widehat F_{\eta_0}\) is nonempty compact and convex and \(\mathbf c_0\) belongs to it, \(P\) is nonempty compact and convex, has zero retained coordinate, and contains \(\mathbf0\). Its rounded-coordinate projection contains the translated cube with center \(\mathbf a_0-\pi_r(\mathbf c_0)\) and radius \(R\). The body \(E=sC+\alpha\mathbf c_0\) is full-dimensional because \(s>0\). Convexity gives the two containments
\[
\begin{aligned}
E+\alpha P
&=sC+\alpha\widehat F_{\eta_0}\subseteq C,\\
C+\beta P
&=C+\beta\widehat F_{\eta_0}-\beta\mathbf c_0
 \subseteq tC-\beta\mathbf c_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
In the first line \(s+\alpha=1\) and both sets being combined are contained in \(C\). In the second line \(C+\beta\widehat F_{\eta_0}\subseteq C+\beta C=(1+\beta)C\).

The first coordinate of \(\mathbf c_0\) is \(\eta_0\). Computing the actual integer sections of the homothety gives
\[
\mu_1(E)=s^m\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
                   g\!\left(\frac{j-\alpha\eta_0}{s}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The factor \(s^m\) scales only the \(m\)-dimensional continuous section. The shift of the retained integer index is kept inside the sum. Apply (4) with \(h=s\) and \(b=\alpha\eta_0\). It yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_1(E)
&\ge s^m(sI-T)\\
&\ge s^m\bigl(sG-(s+1)T\bigr)\\
&\ge s^m\left(s-\frac{s+1}{10}\right)G
 \ge \frac7{20}s^mG>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s\ge1/2\).

For the denominator, (6), the corresponding section formula, and (4) with \(h=t\) and \(b=-\beta\eta_0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_1(C+\beta P)
&\le t^m\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
                    g\!\left(\frac{j+\beta\eta_0}{t}\right)\\
&\le t^m(tI+T)\\
&\le t^m\bigl(tG+(t+1)T\bigr)\\
&\le t^m\left(t+\frac{t+1}{10}\right)G
 \le\frac{83}{60}t^mG.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(t\le7/6\) gives the last constant. Since \(\mathbf0\in P\), the actual set \(C+\beta P\) contains \(C\), so its partial mixed measure is at least \(G>0\). Compactness ensures finiteness. Division of (8) by (9) is therefore legitimate and gives
\[
\frac{\mu_1(E)}{\mu_1(C+\beta P)}
\ge\frac{7/20}{83/60}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^m
=\frac{21}{83}\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m,
\]
which proves (1).

For the radius consequence, set \(\theta:=r/m\in(0,1)\). Equation (29) in the proof of accepted fact 0790372855abe672 establishes the scalar inequality \((7-3\theta)/(7+\theta)\ge2^{-\theta}\) for every \(\theta\in[0,1]\). We use only this scalar statement, replacing that proof's specialization \(n/(n+d)\) by the present \(r/(r+d)\), and do not invoke its ambient-dimensional depth theorem. Since \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\) is increasing in \(R\), the assumption \(R\ge7/(2\theta)\) gives
\[
\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m
\ge\left(\frac{7-3\theta}{7+\theta}\right)^m
\ge2^{-\theta m}=2^{-r}.
\tag{10}
\]
This radius assumption also implies \(R>7/2>3\). Combining (1), (10), and \(21/83>1/4\) proves the ratio assertion in (3).

It remains to obtain actual full mixed depth. Apply accepted fact 758103b1282d6b91 to its body \(C=E\), its integer dimension \(1=q\), its continuous dimension \(d\) set to the present \(m=r+d\), its mixed mass \(T=\mu_1(E)>0\), and its universal constant \(c_0=c_1\). The full-dimensionality of \(E\) was established above. It supplies \(\mathbf y\in E\cap\Lambda_q\) satisfying \(h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)\ge(c_1/2)\mu_1(E)\), where \(h_{q,E}(\mathbf y):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mu_1(E\cap H)\).

Now apply the general-buffer clause of accepted fact 8444e75361af26b0. Its \(q,r,d,n,C,P,E,R,\alpha,\beta,\Lambda_q,\Lambda,\mu_q,\mu_n,S,W\) are precisely the objects here; its ambient \(m\) is the present \(m+1\). Its cube center is \(\mathbf a_0-\pi_r(\mathbf c_0)\). All its hypotheses hold by (6) and the verified projection property. For the already selected \(\mathbf y\), it supplies \(\mathbf w\in P\), chosen independently of every halfspace, such that \(D:=E+\alpha\mathbf w\) and \(\mathbf p:=\mathbf y+\alpha\mathbf w\) satisfy \(\mathbf p\in S\) and \(\mu_1(D\cap H)\le\mu_n(C\cap H)\) for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\).

Translation by \(\alpha\mathbf w\) preserves the retained integer coordinate and the \(m\)-dimensional volume in each retained fiber. If \(H\ni\mathbf p\), then \(H-\alpha\mathbf w\ni\mathbf y\), and hence
\[
\mu_n(C\cap H)\ge\mu_1(D\cap H)
=\mu_1(E\cap(H-\alpha\mathbf w))
\ge h_{q,E}(\mathbf y)
\ge\frac{c_1}{2}\mu_1(E).
\]
The point \(\mathbf p\) is the same for every \(H\). Taking the infimum proves the first inequality in (2). It is positive by (8), so \(W\ge h_S(\mathbf p)>0\). The same accepted general-buffer clause gives \(W\le\mu_1(C+\beta P)\). Consequently
\[
\frac{c_1}{2}\mu_1(E)
\ge\frac{c_1}{2}\,
 \frac{\mu_1(E)}{\mu_1(C+\beta P)}\,W,
\]
and (1) proves the second inequality in (2). The first assertion of (3) gives \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge(c_1/2)(1/4)2^{-r}W=(c_1/4)2^{-n}W\). Every measure used in (7)--(9) is the actual \(\mu_1\); \(G\) is never identified with \(W\), and no homothety of the retained integer coordinate is treated as measure preserving.

An integer unimodular change of the \(n\) base coordinates preserves the lattice of full integer fibers, each \(d\)-dimensional fiber volume, and the class of ambient halfspaces. The final coordinate-invariance clause of accepted fact 8444e75361af26b0 therefore pulls back the full mixed-depth conclusion. The partial measure and the sampling arguments are taken entirely within the transformed split, as asserted.

Finally verify the bicone. Its membership condition is equivalently
\[
\frac{|\eta|}{\mathsf h}
+\max\!\left(\frac{\|\mathbf t\|_\infty}{R},
                      \|\mathbf x\|_\infty\right)\le1.
\]
The left side is a norm, so \(C_{\mathrm{bi}}\) is convex, closed, bounded, and has nonempty interior. Let \(V:=\operatorname{vol}_m(B)>0\). Its partial section volume is \(g(\eta)=V(1-|\eta|/\mathsf h)_+^m\), where \(a_+:=\max(a,0)\), and its section at \(\eta=0\) is \(B\). The continuous and integer maxima both equal \(V\), since \(0\in\mathbb Z\). Direct integration gives \(\int_{\mathbb R}g=2\mathsf h V/(m+1)=16V\). Applying (4) with \(h=1,b=0\) gives \(G\ge15V\), and therefore \(M/G\le1/15<1/12\). The \(\mathbf t\)-projection of \(B\) is the radius-\(R\) cube. Thus every hypothesis of the buffer theorem holds, proving its applicability to (5).

Suppose \(C_{\mathrm{bi}}=K+P_0\) with the sets specified in the statement. Because every point of \(P_0\) has first coordinate zero, the section at the top coordinate satisfies
\[
\{(\mathsf h,\mathbf0)\}
=\bigl(K\cap(\{\mathsf h\}\times\mathbb R^m)\bigr)+P_0.
\]
The section of \(K\) on the right is nonempty: a representation of the top point of \(C_{\mathrm{bi}}\) as a sum already supplies one of its points. Fix such a point of \(K\). Adding any point of \(P_0\) must give the same displayed singleton, so all points of \(P_0\) coincide. Hence there is no nontrivial summand in these buffer directions, completing the proof.
