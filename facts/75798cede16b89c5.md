## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and define
\[
 C_z:=\{v\in\mathbb R^d:(z,v)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
 \quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
Let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be a finite set contained in one parity class, and suppose
\[
 \rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\geq1.
\]
Give every \(y\in A\) a weight \(w_y>0\), and put
\[
 W_A:=\sum_{y\in A}w_y.
\]
Assume that every subset of smaller affine rank has strictly less than half of this weight:
\[
 B\subseteq A,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(B)<\rho
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{y\in B}w_y<\frac{W_A}{2},
\tag{1}
\]
with \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(\varnothing):=-1\) and the empty subset understood to have weight zero, and assume
\[
 w_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in A).
\tag{2}
\]
Define the set of distinct off-diagonal midpoint bases
\[
 \mathcal M(A):=
 \left\{\frac{x+y}{2}:x,y\in A,\ x\ne y\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then every member of \(\mathcal M(A)\) is integral, and
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(A)}a_m>\rho W_A.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, for \(L:=\operatorname{aff}(A)\),
\[
 \sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z>\rho W_A.
\tag{5}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the required additive-combinatorial bound from first principles. Let \(B\) be a finite subset of a real affine space with
\[
 r:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(B)\geq1,
 \qquad
 N:=|B|.
\]
Write
\[
 B\mathbin{\dot+}B:=\{x+y:x,y\in B,\ x\ne y\}.
\]
We claim
\[
 |B+B|\geq(r+1)N-\frac{r(r+1)}2,
\tag{6}
\]
and, more specifically,
\[
 |B\mathbin{\dot+}B|
 \geq rN-\frac{r(r+1)}2
 \geq\frac r2N.
\tag{7}
\]

Identify the affine hull of \(B\) with \(\mathbb R^r\). Choose an affine basis \(b_0,\ldots,b_r\in B\), and start with the simplex on these \(r+1\) vertices. Insert the other points of \(B\) one at a time while maintaining a straight-line simplicial triangulation of the convex hull of the points inserted so far. This can be done directly as follows. If the new point \(p\) lies outside the current convex hull, cone \(p\) over the visible triangulated boundary facets; together with the old triangulation these cones triangulate the enlarged convex hull. If \(p\) lies in the current convex hull, it lies in the relative interior of a unique simplex \(F\) of the current triangulation, and the stellar subdivision of the star of \(F\) at \(p\) gives the new triangulation. Since \(p\) is new, \(\dim F\geq1\).

In the outside case, at least one visible boundary \((r-1)\)-simplex exists, so \(p\) is joined to its \(r\) vertices and no old edge is removed. In the inside case, choose an \(r\)-simplex containing \(F\). The stellar subdivision joins \(p\) to all \(r+1\) vertices of that simplex. If \(F\) is an edge, that one old edge is removed; if \(\dim F\geq2\), every old edge of \(F\) lies in its boundary and remains. Thus in either case the number of edges increases by at least \(r\). The initial simplex has \(r(r+1)/2\) edges, so the final triangulation \(T\), whose vertex set is exactly \(B\), satisfies
\[
 |E(T)|
 \geq\frac{r(r+1)}2+r(N-r-1)
 =rN-\frac{r(r+1)}2.
\tag{8}
\]

The map
\[
 \{x,y\}\in E(T)\longmapsto x+y
\tag{9}
\]
is injective. Indeed, equality of the sums for two edges makes their midpoints equal. That point lies in the relative interior of each edge, whereas two simplices in a geometric simplicial complex intersect in a common face. Hence the two edges must be the same. All sums in (9) are off-diagonal, so the first inequality in (7) follows from (8). Since an affinely spanning set has \(N\geq r+1\),
\[
 rN-\frac{r(r+1)}2-\frac r2N
 =\frac r2(N-r-1)\geq0,
\]
which proves the second inequality in (7).

For completeness, the same triangulation also proves (6), the usual Freiman dimension bound. The \(N\) diagonal sums \(2b\), \(b\in B\), are distinct. None equals an edge sum \(x+y\): otherwise the vertex \(b\) would be the relative-interior midpoint of the edge \([x,y]\), impossible in a geometric simplicial complex whose vertex set contains \(b\). Thus the diagonal sums and the edge sums in (9) give at least
\[
 N+|E(T)|
 \geq(r+1)N-\frac{r(r+1)}2
\]
distinct elements of \(B+B\). This completes the self-contained proof of (6)-(7).

We now apply (7) to the weighted set \(A\). For \(t>0\), put
\[
 A_t:=\{y\in A:w_y\geq t\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Among the finitely many affine bases \(D\subseteq A\), define
\[
 \tau:=\max_D\min_{y\in D}w_y.
\tag{11}
\]
The weights are positive, so \(\tau>0\). A basis attaining (11) is contained in \(A_t\) whenever \(0<t\leq\tau\), and hence
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(A_t)=\rho
 \qquad(0<t\leq\tau).
\tag{12}
\]
Set
\[
 H:=\{y\in A:w_y>\tau\}.
\]
If \(H\) had affine rank \(\rho\), it would contain an affine basis \(D\) with \(\min_{y\in D}w_y>\tau\), contradicting (11). Thus \(H\) has smaller affine rank when it is nonempty, and (1) gives
\[
 \sum_{y\in H}w_y<\frac{W_A}{2};
\tag{13}
\]
the same inequality is immediate if \(H=\varnothing\). The truncated layer-cake calculation is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^\tau|A_t|\,dt
 &=\sum_{y\in A}\min\{w_y,\tau\}\\
 &=W_A-\sum_{y\in H}(w_y-\tau)\\
 &\geq W_A-\sum_{y\in H}w_y\\
 &>\frac{W_A}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The first comparison in (14) is deliberately non-strict: it remains valid when \(H\) is empty. The final comparison is strict by (13).

For every \(m\in\mathcal M(A)\), define
\[
 u_m:=
 \max_{\substack{x,y\in A,\ x\ne y\\(x+y)/2=m}}
 \min\{w_x,w_y\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The maximum exists and is positive because \(A\) is finite and \(m\in\mathcal M(A)\). For every \(t>0\), the midpoint \(m\) belongs to \(\mathcal M(A_t)\) if and only if \(t\leq u_m\). Therefore the exact layer-cake identity, followed by (12), (7), and (14), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(A)}u_m
 &=\int_0^\infty|\mathcal M(A_t)|\,dt\\
 &\geq\int_0^\tau|\mathcal M(A_t)|\,dt\\
 &=\int_0^\tau|A_t\mathbin{\dot+}A_t|\,dt\\
 &\geq\frac\rho2\int_0^\tau|A_t|\,dt\\
 &>\frac{\rho W_A}{4}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Here multiplication by \(1/2\) is a bijection from the set of restricted sums to the set of midpoints, which proves the equality on the third line. All level sets are empty for \(t>\max_{y\in A}w_y\), so the first integral is finite. At the omitted level \(t=0\) nothing changes, and for \(t>\tau\) no rank assertion is used; only the nonnegativity that permits the second line is needed. On \(0<t\leq\tau\), (12) and \(\rho\geq1\) ensure that \(A_t\) contains at least two points, so there is no empty restricted-sumset exception in the application of (7).

We next verify integrality and the geometric volume comparison. Choose \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\) representing the parity class of \(A\). If \(x=\varepsilon+2p\) and \(y=\varepsilon+2q\) with \(p,q\in\mathbb Z^n\), then
\[
 \frac{x+y}{2}=\varepsilon+p+q\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus every \(m\in\mathcal M(A)\) is integral. It also belongs to \(L=\operatorname{aff}(A)\), being the midpoint of two points of \(A\).

Fix \(m\in\mathcal M(A)\), and choose a pair \(x_m,y_m\in A\) attaining the maximum in (15). Then
\[
 4u_m\leq4w_{x_m}\leq a_{x_m},
 \qquad
 4u_m\leq4w_{y_m}\leq a_{y_m}.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, both source fibers have positive \(d\)-volume. Choose any \(c_x\in C_{x_m}\) and \(c_y\in C_{y_m}\), and define
\[
 \lambda_x:=\left(\frac{4u_m}{a_{x_m}}\right)^{1/d},
 \qquad
 K_{m,x}:=c_x+\lambda_x(C_{x_m}-c_x),
\tag{19}
\]
\[
 \lambda_y:=\left(\frac{4u_m}{a_{y_m}}\right)^{1/d},
 \qquad
 K_{m,y}:=c_y+\lambda_y(C_{y_m}-c_y).
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (18) give \(0<\lambda_x,\lambda_y\leq1\). Convexity of each source fiber implies
\[
 K_{m,x}\subseteq C_{x_m},
 \qquad
 K_{m,y}\subseteq C_{y_m},
\]
and homogeneity of \(d\)-dimensional volume gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(K_{m,x})
 =\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{m,y})
 =4u_m.
\tag{21}
\]

For \(v\in K_{m,x}\) and \(v'\in K_{m,y}\), the points \((x_m,v)\) and \((y_m,v')\) lie in \(C\). Since \(C\) is convex and \(m=(x_m+y_m)/2\),
\[
 \frac{K_{m,x}+K_{m,y}}2\subseteq C_m.
\tag{22}
\]
Accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b supplies the following exact audited Brunn-Minkowski statement: if \(k\geq1\), \(P,Q\subseteq\mathbb R^k\) are nonempty measurable sets of finite positive volume, and \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), then
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_k((1-\lambda)P+\lambda Q)^{1/k}
 \geq
 (1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_k(P)^{1/k}
 +\lambda\operatorname{vol}_k(Q)^{1/k}.
\tag{23}
\]
Apply (23) with \(k=d\), \(P=K_{m,x}\), \(Q=K_{m,y}\), and \(\lambda=1/2\). The integer \(d\) is positive by hypothesis. Equations (19)-(21) show that \(P,Q\) are nonempty compact, hence measurable, and have the same finite positive volume \(4u_m\). Thus every hypothesis of the accepted theorem is satisfied, and it gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\left(\frac{K_{m,x}+K_{m,y}}2\right)^{1/d}
 &\geq
 \frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{m,x})^{1/d}
       +\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{m,y})^{1/d}}2\\
 &=(4u_m)^{1/d}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (22) and (24) gives the separate target-fiber bound
\[
 a_m\geq4u_m.
\tag{25}
\]

The set \(\mathcal M(A)\) contains each target base only once. Hence (25) may be summed over distinct target fibers, and (16) gives
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(A)}a_m
 \geq4\sum_{m\in\mathcal M(A)}u_m
 >\rho W_A,
\]
which is (4). Since \(\mathcal M(A)\subseteq L\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and every \(a_z\geq0\), (5) follows. Only finitely many of the latter terms are nonzero because the projection of the compact body \(C\) to \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded.

Finally, a source point may be selected for more than one distinct midpoint in (15), but this creates no capacity claim. For each fixed \(m\), the homothetic copies (19)-(20) are auxiliary witnesses only for the numerical inequality \(a_m\geq4u_m\). They are not required to coexist as source densities or as a disjoint allocation inside their source fibers. Conversely, for one target \(m\) we select only one maximizing pair and count \(a_m\) only once. Thus reuse is harmless precisely because the final sum combines separate lower bounds for distinct target-fiber volumes. The argument neither sums several pairwise images inside one target fiber nor supplies any simultaneous source-density realization. Its hypotheses are conditional data; it does not produce a stopped one-parity configuration, combine parity classes, or prove a final theorem for the locked problem.
