## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the actual-body notation of accepted facts 03f071403cdfc69f and d887d95075dfcc06: \(C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), \(T=2^{-n}W\), and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero.

Suppose \(b,c\in\mathbb R_{>0}^n\) and a finite-valued nonincreasing function \(g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with \(g(0)>0\), specify the entire integer table by
\[
w_z=\begin{cases}
g(\max\{b\cdot z,c\cdot z\}),&z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n,\\
0,&z\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Assume global log-concavity with the zero convention: for \(r,s\ge0\) and \(0<\theta<1\), \(g((1-\theta)r+\theta s)\ge g(r)^{1-\theta}g(s)^\theta\), where a positive power of zero is zero. No continuity or rationality assumption is imposed, and the two coefficient vectors may be proportional or nonproportional.

Put \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). This set is finite by compactness of \(C\). For \(j\in D\) and finite \(t\ge0\), define the original-weight threshold set \(F_j(t)=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ w_{j+u}\ge e^{-t}w_j\}\), with componentwise inequalities. This is exactly the set denoted \(F_t\) at fixed center \(j\) in the actual-body part of d887d95075dfcc06; it is distinct from the unparameterized all-pair scalar \(F_j\) discussed in 03f071403cdfc69f.

At each fixed positive center retain the actual quantities \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), with exactly the normalizations of those accepted facts. Also retain
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
&& (L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},&
K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The suppressed dependence of \(\alpha\) is on this fixed center. Every displacement sum is oriented and includes zero; each difference set counts every distinct integer difference once.

Then the following maximum is attained and satisfies
\[
\max_{\substack{j\in D\\t\ge0}}
\frac{w_j e^{-t}|F_j(t)-F_j(t)|}{1+2t}
\ge e^{-76}T,
\qquad K(w)\ge e^{-76}T.
\tag{3}
\]
There are one center \(j\in D\), one finite \(t\ge0\), and one \(x^\star\in C_j\), all selected before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
h_S((j,x^\star))
\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(2t)}{12(1+2t)}
\ge\frac{e^{-76}}{12}T
=\frac{e^{-76}}{12\,2^n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{4}
\]
This includes vertical halfspaces and closed halfspaces whose boundary contains whole fibers, and imposes no additional conditions on the actual fiber shapes.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. We first prove an auxiliary count for every pair \(b,c\in\mathbb R_{>0}^n\) and every finite \(R\ge0\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le R,\ c\cdot z\le R\},\\
A_j&=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ j+u\in\mathcal D\}
\qquad(j\in\mathcal D),\\
Q_n^{(2)}&=\frac1{2\cdot3^6}\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^n
\exp\left(-\frac83(\sqrt n+1)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The coefficient \(Q_n^{(2)}\) is the new two-constraint coefficient; it is different from the one-constraint coefficient \(Q_n\) in accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790. We claim that some \(j\in\mathcal D\) satisfies
\[
|A_j-A_j|\ge Q_n^{(2)}\,2^{-n}|\mathcal D|.
\tag{6}
\]
Positive coefficients make \(\mathcal D\) finite and nonempty. If \(R=0\), it equals \(\{0\}\), and (6) follows with \(j=0\): indeed \(Q_n^{(2)}\le(11/10)^n\le2^n\). Henceforth in proving the count we may assume \(R>0\).

Use precisely the one-coordinate function from the proof of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790, \(\mathcal M(s)=\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}(2k+1)e^{-sk}\) for \(s>0\). The elementary estimates (9) and (12) proved in that fact state
\[
2(1-e^{-3s/4})\mathcal M(s)>\frac{11}{10}
\quad(s>0),
\qquad
k\log\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}\le\log3<\frac43
\quad(k\ge1).
\tag{7}
\]
Only these one-coordinate estimates and the one-coordinate moment bounds stated below are taken from its auxiliary counting argument; its single-knapsack conclusion is not being applied to \(\mathcal D\).

Let \(\ell:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be the continuous piecewise-linear interpolation of \(\ell(k)=\log(2k+1)\) at the nonnegative integers. Its slope on \((k,k+1)\) is \(\log((2k+3)/(2k+1))\); these slopes are positive and strictly decrease with \(k\). Thus \(\ell\) is increasing and concave, and every slope is at most \(\log3\). The second estimate in (7) also gives \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\) wherever \(u>0\) and the derivative exists: on \((k-1,k)\), use \(u\le k\).

We need a finite-set consequence of the first estimate in (7). For any nonempty finite \(E\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), set \(\bar z=|E|^{-1}\sum_{z\in E}z\) and \(x=(3/4)\bar z\). For each coordinate, put \(k_i=\lfloor x_i\rfloor\) and choose \(s_i=\log((2k_i+3)/(2k_i+1))>0\). The decreasing-slope property shows that \(k_i\) and \(k_i+1\) maximize \((2k+1)e^{-s_i k}\) over all nonnegative integers. Since \(\ell\) is affine on \([k_i,k_i+1]\), it follows that \(e^{\ell(x_i)-s_i x_i}=\mathcal M(s_i)\), including when \(x_i\) is an integer or zero.

The formula \(\pi(z)=\prod_i[(1-e^{-3s_i/4})e^{-(3s_i/4)z_i}]\) defines a strictly positive probability mass function on the entire nonnegative integer orthant, by multiplying convergent geometric series. Its mass on \(E\) is at most one. The finite arithmetic-geometric mean inequality therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{|E|}
&\ge\frac1{|E|}\sum_{z\in E}\pi(z)\\
&\ge\left(\prod_{z\in E}\pi(z)\right)^{1/|E|}
=\prod_i[(1-e^{-3s_i/4})e^{-s_i x_i}].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Multiplying (8) by \(\exp(\sum_i\ell(x_i))\) and applying (7) in each coordinate proves
\[
\exp\left(\sum_i\ell(x_i)\right)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^n2^{-n}|E|.
\tag{9}
\]
This is an auxiliary counting estimate obtained from a product probability. No actual fiber weight has been changed.

We next record an elementary rounding observation. Given any \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n\), any integer \(m\ge1\), and \(m\) linear forms with nonnegative coefficients, there is an integer vector \(p\) in the coordinate rectangle \(\prod_i[\lfloor x_i\rfloor,\lceil x_i\rceil]\) such that every specified form at \(p\) is at most its value at \(x\), and
\[
\sum_i\ell(p_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-m\log3.
\tag{10}
\]
To prove this, maximize \(\sum_i\ell(y_i)\) on that compact rectangle subject to all the specified upper bounds. The feasible set contains \(x\). The objective is affine on this rectangle, because each coordinate interval has length zero or is one full interval of affinity of \(\ell\). At a maximizing point with more than \(m\) noninteger coordinates, there is a nonzero direction supported on those coordinates that annihilates all \(m\) specified forms. Small moves in both directions stay in the rectangle and preserve those forms. The objective's slope along this direction must be zero, since otherwise one of those moves improves the maximum. Move until at least one of the moving coordinates reaches an integer endpoint. Repeating this finite procedure gives a maximizing point with at most \(m\) noninteger coordinates. Round those coordinates down. Nonnegative coefficients ensure that no specified form increases, and each rounded coordinate loses at most \(\log3\) in the objective, because every slope of \(\ell\) is at most \(\log3\). This proves (10). The argument remains valid when some forms are redundant or have zero coefficients, and when some coordinate intervals have length zero.

Apply (9) with \(E=\mathcal D\) and \(x=(3/4)|\mathcal D|^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal D}z\). Then \(a\cdot x\le3R/4\) for either row \(a\in\{b,c\}\). Apply (10) to this \(x\) and the two forms \(b\cdot y,c\cdot y\). It gives an integer vector \(k\ge0\) with
\[
a\cdot k\le\frac{3R}{4}\quad(a\in\{b,c\}),
\qquad
\sum_i\ell(k_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-2\log3.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(k=0\), take \(j=0\); the product estimate (18) below follows directly from (9) and (11), with room to spare. We now construct \(j\) when \(k\ne0\).

For \(a\in\{b,c\}\) put \(\beta_a=a\cdot k>0\), and define
\[
\theta_i=\max\left\{\frac{b_i k_i}{\beta_b},\frac{c_i k_i}{\beta_c}\right\},
\qquad
\widetilde k_i=\frac{k_i}{1+(\sqrt n+1)\theta_i}.
\tag{12}
\]
These definitions give \(0\le\theta_i\le1\), \(\sum_i\theta_i\le2\), and \(0\le\widetilde k_i\le k_i\). In particular a zero coordinate of \(k\) stays zero. For each row define \(\delta_a\ge0\) and \(\zeta_a\ge0\) by
\[
\delta_a=\beta_a-a\cdot\widetilde k,
\qquad
\zeta_a^2=\sum_i a_i^2 k_i\widetilde k_i.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(\theta_i\ge a_i k_i/\beta_a\), equation (12) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_a
&=\sum_i\frac{a_i k_i(\sqrt n+1)\theta_i}
 {1+(\sqrt n+1)\theta_i}
\ge\frac{\sqrt n+1}{\beta_a}\,\zeta_a^2,\\
\beta_a-\delta_a
&=a\cdot\widetilde k
\le\sqrt n\left(\sum_i a_i^2\widetilde k_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt n\,\zeta_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that \(\delta_a\ge\zeta_a\). Indeed this is immediate if \(\zeta_a=0\); otherwise, \(\delta_a<\zeta_a\) and the second line of (14) imply \(\beta_a<(\sqrt n+1)\zeta_a\), after which the first line implies \(\delta_a>\zeta_a\), a contradiction.

Apply (10) to \(\widetilde k\) and the following four linear forms, with the original vector \(k\) held fixed: \(b\cdot y\), \(c\cdot y\), \(\sum_i b_i^2 k_i y_i\), and \(\sum_i c_i^2 k_i y_i\). All coefficients are nonnegative. The resulting integer vector \(j\) satisfies \(0\le j_i\le k_i\), since \(k_i\) is an integer and \(\widetilde k_i\le k_i\). It also satisfies, for either row,
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot j&\le a\cdot\widetilde k,\\
\sum_i a_i^2 j_i^2
&\le\sum_i a_i^2 k_i j_i
\le\sum_i a_i^2 k_i\widetilde k_i=\zeta_a^2,\\
\sum_i\ell(j_i)&\ge\sum_i\ell(\widetilde k_i)-4\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The first two lines, \(\delta_a\ge\zeta_a\), and (11) give the required variance reserve:
\[
R\ge\frac43\beta_a
\ge\frac43\left(a\cdot j+
\left(\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\right)^{1/2}\right)
\qquad(a\in\{b,c\}).
\tag{16}
\]
In particular \(j\in\mathcal D\). Equation (16) also holds for the choice \(j=0\) made when \(k=0\).

There is only a sublinear logarithmic loss in (12). For every coordinate with \(k_i>0\), its \(\widetilde k_i\) is positive, and integrating \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\bigl(\ell(k_i)-\ell(\widetilde k_i)\bigr)
&\le\log3\sum_{i:k_i>0}\log\frac{k_i}{\widetilde k_i}\\
&=\log3\sum_i\log\bigl(1+(\sqrt n+1)\theta_i\bigr)\\
&\le(\sqrt n+1)\log3\sum_i\theta_i
\le2(\sqrt n+1)\log3
<\frac83(\sqrt n+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Zero coordinates contribute zero to every line. Combining (9), (11), (15), and (17) yields
\[
\prod_i(2j_i+1)
\ge\frac1{3^6}\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^n
\exp\left(-\frac83(\sqrt n+1)\right)
2^{-n}|\mathcal D|.
\tag{18}
\]
When \(k=0\), (9) and (11) already give \(1\ge3^{-2}(11/10)^n2^{-n}|\mathcal D|\); hence (18) holds with \(j=0\) as asserted earlier.

For clarity, the exact difference-set description at any \(j\in\mathcal D\) is
\[
A_j-A_j=
\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:
|v_i|\le j_i\text{ for every }i,\quad
a\cdot v^+\le R-a\cdot j,\quad
a\cdot v^-\le R-a\cdot j
\text{ for }a\in\{b,c\}\right\},
\tag{19}
\]
where \(v_i^+=\max\{v_i,0\}\) and \(v_i^-=\max\{-v_i,0\}\). If \(v=u-u'\) with \(u,u'\in A_j\), then \(v^+\le u\) and \(v^-\le u'\), which proves the right-hand conditions. Conversely those conditions put both \(v^+\) and \(v^-\) in \(A_j\), and \(v=v^+-v^-\). Thus (19) describes distinct differences, irrespective of the number of other representing pairs.

Take independent random variables \(V_i\), uniform on the integers \(-j_i,\ldots,j_i\). The one-coordinate moment estimates (13)--(14) in the proof of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790 state, with precisely this uniform law,
\[
\mathbb E V_i^+=\frac{j_i(j_i+1)}{2(2j_i+1)}\le\frac{j_i}{3},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V_i^+)\le\frac29j_i^2.
\tag{20}
\]
They include \(j_i=0\). Reflection gives the same estimates for \(V_i^-\). For each row \(a\), the sums \(a\cdot V^+\) and \(a\cdot V^-\) have a common mean \(\mu_a\le a\cdot j/3\) and common variance \(\sigma_a^2\le(2/9)\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\), by independence across coordinates. The two signed sums need not be independent. Equation (16) implies
\[
R-a\cdot j
\ge\frac13a\cdot j+\frac43\left(\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\ge\mu_a+\sqrt8\,\sigma_a.
\tag{21}
\]
For any of the four sums with positive variance, the second-moment bound shows that its probability of exceeding \(\mu_a+\sqrt8\sigma_a\) is at most \(1/8\). If its variance is zero, it equals its mean almost surely and cannot violate the corresponding upper bound in (21). A union bound over the two rows and the two signs therefore gives probability at least \(1/2\) that all four inequalities in (19) hold. The vector \(V\) is uniform on a box of exactly \(\prod_i(2j_i+1)\) distinct integer vectors. Consequently
\[
|A_j-A_j|\ge\frac12\prod_i(2j_i+1).
\tag{22}
\]
This includes the zero box, and it remains valid when constraints or events coincide. Equations (18) and (22) prove (6). The argument used finite averages and finite rounding rectangles, so arbitrary positive real coefficients and simultaneous discrete ties require no limiting or genericity assumption.

Return to the actual table (1). It is coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative integer orthant, including its zero values, and its maximum is \(M:=g(0)=w_0>0\). For each positive site \(z\), both coefficient rows at \(\lfloor z/2\rfloor\) are at most half their values at \(z\). Monotonicity of \(g\) and its log-concavity between zero and \(\max\{b\cdot z,c\cdot z\}\) thus give
\[
w_{\lfloor z/2\rfloor}
\ge g\left(\tfrac12\max\{b\cdot z,c\cdot z\}\right)
\ge\sqrt{Mw_z}.
\tag{23}
\]
Each integer image under coordinatewise floor-halving has at most \(2^n\) preimages, of the form \(2u+\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\). Since the positive support is finite, summing (23) gives \(\sum_{z\in D}\sqrt{Mw_z}\le2^nW\).

Set \(J=\lceil2(n+1)\log2\rceil\), so \(1\le J\le2(n+1)\). At sites with \(0<w_z\le Me^{-J}\), the inequality \(w_z\le e^{-J/2}\sqrt{Mw_z}\) gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\w_z\le Me^{-J}}}w_z
\le e^{-J/2}2^nW\le\frac W2.
\tag{24}
\]
The \(J\) bands \(\{z\in D:Me^{-(k+1)}<w_z\le Me^{-k}\}\), for \(k=0,\ldots,J-1\), therefore contain a band of mass at least \(W/(2J)\). Strict lower and weak upper endpoints place every boundary weight in exactly one band. Fix such a \(k\), set \(t=k+1\), and define the original-weight prefix \(D_t=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z\ge Me^{-t}\}\). This is finite and nonempty. The chosen band lies in it and has every weight at most \(eMe^{-t}\). Hence
\[
Me^{-t}|D_t|\ge\frac{W}{2eJ},
\qquad 1\le t\le J.
\tag{25}
\]

Let \(R=\max_{z\in D_t}\max\{b\cdot z,c\cdot z\}\). It is a finite attained nonnegative real number, and
\[
D_t=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le R,\ c\cdot z\le R\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The forward inclusion follows from the definition of \(R\). For the reverse inclusion, choose \(z_0\in D_t\) attaining \(R\). Any nonnegative integer \(z\) with both row values at most \(R\) satisfies \(w_z\ge g(R)=w_{z_0}\ge Me^{-t}\). This proves (26), including discrete ties and zero radius, without assuming that a real superlevel endpoint is included or that \(g\) is continuous.

Apply (6) to exactly this prefix and these original coefficient rows. It supplies \(j\in D_t\) with \(|A_j-A_j|\ge Q_n^{(2)}2^{-n}|D_t|\), where \(A_j=\{u:0\le u\le j,\ j+u\in D_t\}\). Set \(\tau=\log(w_j/(Me^{-t}))\). Then \(0\le\tau\le t\le J\), the threshold identity \(w_je^{-\tau}=Me^{-t}\) is exact, and \(A_j=F_j(\tau)\), including all threshold boundary sites. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_je^{-\tau}|F_j(\tau)-F_j(\tau)|}{1+2\tau}
&\ge\frac{Q_n^{(2)}2^{-n}Me^{-t}|D_t|}{1+2J}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_n^{(2)}}{2eJ(1+2J)}T
\ge\frac{Q_n^{(2)}}{20e(n+1)^2}T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The last estimate uses \(J\le2(n+1)\) and \(1+2J\le5(n+1)\).

Here is an explicit absolute bound for the coefficient. The elementary inequalities \(\log(11/10)>1/11\), \(\log(n+1)\le\sqrt n\) for \(n\ge1\), \(\log3<4/3\), and \(\log40<4\) also appear in the constant estimates of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790. For the second inequality, the derivative of \(\sqrt x-\log(x+1)\) on \(x\ge1\) is \((\sqrt x-1)^2/[2\sqrt x(x+1)]\ge0\), and its value at one is positive. Substituting the coefficient in (5) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{Q_n^{(2)}}{20e(n+1)^2}
&=n\log\frac{11}{10}-\frac83\sqrt n-2\log(n+1)
-\frac{11}{3}-6\log3-\log40\\
&>\frac n{11}-\frac{14}{3}\sqrt n-\frac{47}{3}\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\frac n{11}}-\frac{7\sqrt{11}}3\right)^2
-\frac{680}{9}
>-76.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (27)--(28) prove the corner bound in (3).

The maximum there exists. At a fixed \(j\in D\), the box of integer \(u\) with \(0\le u\le j\) is finite. Its finite threshold values \(\log(w_j/w_{j+u})\), over positive forward weights, form a finite set of nonnegative numbers containing zero. For any \(s\ge0\), \(F_j(s)\) agrees with its value at the largest such threshold at most \(s\), while \(e^{-s}/(1+2s)\) decreases. The maximum at this center is therefore attained at a finite threshold. There are finitely many positive centers. Zero weights never enter a finite-threshold set, and zero coordinates of a center cause no exception.

It remains to apply the accepted actual-body consumers. The entire table in (1) is supported in the nonnegative orthant and is coordinatewise nonincreasing there, so all actual-body hypotheses of d887d95075dfcc06 hold for the original \(C\) and \(w\). The local set in its equation (14) at center \(j\) is exactly \(F_j(s)\), since that fact's envelope agrees with the entire original integer table. Consequently its equation (14) gives
\[
B_j^\Delta(2s)\ge w_je^{-s}|F_j(s)-F_j(s)|
\qquad(s\ge0).
\tag{29}
\]
For this implication, each difference has its positive and negative parts in the same threshold downset; its actual endpoint weights are at least the displayed original threshold. The curvature in (29) is the actual \(\Delta_j\), and every distinct oriented difference is counted once, including zero.

Use (29) at the center and \(s=\tau\) chosen in (27). It gives \(B_j^\Delta(2\tau)/(1+2\tau)\ge e^{-76}T\), proving the assertion about \(K(w)\) in (3). This center has \(w_j>0\), its cutoff \(2\tau\) is finite and nonnegative, and \(C\) has \(W>0\). The fixed-center, fixed-cutoff actual-body statement (3) of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f therefore supplies a single \(x^\star\in C_j\) such that
\[
h_S((j,x^\star))\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(2\tau)}{12(1+2\tau)}
\ge\frac{e^{-76}}{12}T.
\tag{30}
\]
The center and threshold were selected from the original table, and the continuous point is supplied before any ambient halfspace is chosen. That accepted statement includes every closed ambient halfspace containing this point, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing full fibers. This proves (4), with \(t=\tau\), and completes the theorem.
