## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer. Let \(P\subseteq[0,2]^k\) be convex and invariant under every permutation of its coordinates, and assume \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\ne\varnothing\). No full-dimensionality or closedness assumption on \(P\) is imposed. Set \(A:=D\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\). For arbitrary positive real numbers \(v_0,\ldots,v_{2k}\), define

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}v_{\sum_i x_i},
&p_{(\mathbf x,0)}&:=\frac{v_{\sum_i x_i}}{Z},\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&:=\inf_{\substack{H\ni\mathbf z\\H\text{ a closed affine halfspace of }\mathbb R^{k+1}}}
\sum_{\mathbf w\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf w},
&M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here every halfspace has the form \(H=\{\mathbf w:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w\ge b\}\) with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Thus the spatial dimension is \(k\), the height is zero, and the auxiliary ambient arithmetic dimension is \(N=k+1\). Then, with the absolute constant \(\kappa:=1/(8\cdot271^3)\),

$$
M_3(p)\ge \kappa\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$

All probabilities and depths in this conclusion refer to the original law on \(A\) and to the stated ambient space. The locked problem's objects are not redefined.

## Proof

We first prove a lower-rank estimate with variable orbit cutoffs. The only accepted predecessor used is fact \(803f398e39c669b9\). Its notation \(k,j,a,c_j,N_j,K_{j,a},K_j,d_{j,a}\) will retain the same combinatorial meanings. Its deficit-profile symbol \(v_j\) is written \(w_j\) below, to distinguish it from the statement's coordinate-sum profile \(v_s\). Its common cutoff \(\min\{r_{\max},\lfloor(k-j)/2\rfloor\}\) is replaced by a rank-dependent cutoff \(a_{\max}(j)\). The consequences of that replacement are proved here.

Let \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,k\}\) be nonempty. For each \(j\in J\), choose an integer \(a_{\max}(j)\) with \(0\le a_{\max}(j)\le\lfloor(k-j)/2\rfloor\). Let \(\mathcal A\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^k\times\{0\}\) consist, for each \(j\in J\), of every vector having \(a\) twos, \(a+j\) zeros, and \(k-2a-j\) ones, for every \(0\le a\le a_{\max}(j)\), and of no other vectors. Thus its nonempty ranks have coordinate sum \(k-j\). For arbitrary positive \(w_0,\ldots,w_k\), let \(q_{(\mathbf x,0)}:=w_j/Z_q\) on rank \(j\), where \(Z_q:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal A}w_{j(\mathbf z)}\). Define \(\tau_q\) and \(M_3(q)\) by the same closed ambient-halfspace formulas as in the statement, with support \(\mathcal A\).

For \(j\in J\), put \(c_j:=\binom{k}{j}\), and set

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{j,a}&:=\frac{(k-j)!}{a!^2(k-j-2a)!}
&&\left(0\le a\le\left\lfloor\frac{k-j}{2}\right\rfloor\right),\\
K_j&:=\sum_{a=0}^{a_{\max}(j)}K_{j,a},
&d_{j,a}&:=\binom{j+a}{a},\\
N_j&:=\sum_{a=0}^{a_{\max}(j)}
\frac{k!}{a!(a+j)!(k-2a-j)!},
&Z_q&=\sum_{j\in J}N_jw_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

Here \(N_j\) is the number of original atoms of rank \(j\), and \(K_j\ge K_{j,0}=1\). Factorial cancellation gives, separately at every present rank,

$$
N_j=c_j\sum_{a=0}^{a_{\max}(j)}\frac{K_{j,a}}{d_{j,a}}.
\tag{2}
$$

For \(j\in J\) and \(L\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\) with \(|L|=k-j\), the binary root \(\mathbf y_L:=(\mathbf1_L,0)\) belongs to \(\mathcal A\). For disjoint \(E,F\subseteq L\) with \(|E|=|F|=a\le a_{\max}(j)\), form

$$
\mathbf z_+:=(\mathbf1_L+\mathbf1_E-\mathbf1_F,0),
\qquad
\mathbf z_-:=(\mathbf1_L-\mathbf1_E+\mathbf1_F,0).
\tag{3}
$$

These index sets \(E,F\) are the pair-index sets denoted \(P,Q\) in fact \(803f398e39c669b9\); the new names avoid collision with the convex set \(P\) in the present statement. Each endpoint in (3) is an original atom of rank \(j\), of probability \(w_j/Z_q\), and their midpoint is \(\mathbf y_L\).

For fixed \(a\), there are exactly \(K_{j,a}\) ordered choices \((E,F)\). The map from these choices to \(\mathbf z_+\) is injective, since its twos recover \(E\) and its zeros within \(L\) recover \(F\). Exchanging \(E,F\) exchanges the two endpoints; the only fixed choice is \(E=F=\varnothing\), giving the root. Consequently these choices give the root and \((K_j-1)/2\) disjoint pairs of other original atoms. Every closed affine halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) containing their midpoint contains at least one endpoint of each pair. This follows by applying its affine inequality to the midpoint. The argument includes halfspaces with a nonzero height component. Hence

$$
\tau_q(\mathbf y_L)\ge\frac{K_j+1}{2}\frac{w_j}{Z_q}
\ge\frac{K_jw_j}{2Z_q}.
\tag{4}
$$

There are \(c_j\) such roots at rank \(j\), and roots from different ranks are distinct. Restricting the original nonnegative sampling sum to these roots gives

$$
M_3(q)\ge\frac{\mathcal V}{8Z_q^4},
\qquad
\mathcal V:=\sum_{j\in J}c_jK_j^3w_j^4.
\tag{5}
$$

Define \(\mathcal S:=\sum_{j\in J}N_j^{4/3}/(c_j^{1/3}K_j)\). Hölder's inequality with exponents \(4\) and \(4/3\), applied to

$$
Z_q=\sum_{j\in J}
(c_jK_j^3w_j^4)^{1/4}
\left(\frac{N_j^{4/3}}{c_j^{1/3}K_j}\right)^{3/4},
$$

gives \(Z_q^4\le\mathcal V\mathcal S^3\).

We next establish the bound on \(\mathcal S\) uniformly in \(J\) and \(a_{\max}(j)\). For any finite positive lists \(b_a,d_a\), Hölder with exponents \(4/3\) and \(4\) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sum_a\frac{b_a}{d_a}\right)^{4/3}
&\le
\left(\sum_a\frac{b_a^{1/3}}{d_a^{4/3}}\right)
\left(\sum_a b_a^3\right)^{1/3}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_a\frac{b_a^{1/3}}{d_a^{4/3}}\right)\sum_a b_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Indeed, the factors before Hölder are \((b_a^{1/3}/d_a^{4/3})^{3/4}\) and \(b_a^{3/4}\); their product is \(b_a/d_a\). The last step uses \(\sum_a b_a^3\le(\sum_a b_a)^3\). Apply (6) at each \(j\in J\), on its own range \(0\le a\le a_{\max}(j)\), with \(b_a=K_{j,a}\). By (1)-(2),

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S
&\le\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{a=0}^{a_{\max}(j)}
\frac{c_jK_{j,a}^{1/3}}{d_{j,a}^{4/3}}\\
&\le\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}T_a,
&T_a&:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2a}
\frac{\binom{k}{j}
\left((k-j)!/(a!^2(k-j-2a)!)\right)^{1/3}}
{\binom{j+a}{a}^{4/3}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

The second inequality merely adds nonnegative terms: every original pair \((j,a)\) satisfies \(0\le a\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) and \(0\le j\le k-2a\). It therefore handles both variable cutoffs and missing ranks.

The \(T_a\) in (7) is exactly \(T_a\) of equation (11) of fact \(803f398e39c669b9\), with the identical spatial dimension \(k\) and identical \(j,a\), and with that fact's parameter chosen as \(r_{\max}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\). In particular, all its indices \(0\le a\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) are available. Its \(c_j,K_{j,a},d_{j,a}\) have precisely the formulas in (1), without involving either its fixed cutoff or our variable cutoffs. That accepted fact proves \(T_0=2^k\) and, by its equations (12)-(19),

$$
T_a\le2^k\,3a(9/10)^a
\qquad\left(1\le a\le\left\lfloor\frac k2\right\rfloor\right).
\tag{8}
$$

Thus no theorem for variable cutoffs is being assumed. Only the stated universal combinatorial inequalities for the exactly matching full sums \(T_a\) are used. Summing (8) after (7) yields

$$
\mathcal S\le2^k\left(1+3\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}a(9/10)^a\right)
=271\,2^k.
\tag{9}
$$

Combining (5), \(Z_q^4\le\mathcal V\mathcal S^3\), and (9) proves the lower-rank estimate

$$
M_3(q)\ge\frac1{8\mathcal S^3}
\ge271^{-3}8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{10}
$$

We now show why convexity and coordinate-permutation invariance give precisely the orbit condition just used. Let \(Q\subseteq[0,2]^k\cap\{\mathbf x:\sum_i x_i\le k\}\) be convex and invariant under coordinate permutations, with \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^k\ne\varnothing\). At rank \(k-j\), every lattice point is ternary. If it has \(a\) twos, the equations for its number of coordinates and its coordinate sum force it to have \(a+j\) zeros and \(k-2a-j\) ones. Hence \(0\le a\le\lfloor(k-j)/2\rfloor\).

If a point of this rank has \(a>0\), it has both a two and a zero, since \(a+j\ge a>0\). Let \(\pi\) exchange one coordinate equal to two with one equal to zero and leave the other coordinates fixed. Coordinate-permutation invariance puts \(\pi\mathbf x\) in \(Q\). Convexity puts \((\mathbf x+\pi\mathbf x)/2\) in \(Q\). This midpoint is again integral: the selected two and zero both become ones. It has the same sum and exactly \(a-1\) twos. Invariance then includes every point of its coordinate-permutation orbit. Repeating proves that the presence of an orbit with \(a\) twos forces every orbit with \(0,\ldots,a\) twos at the same rank.

For each nonempty rank choose the largest number of twos and call it \(a_{\max}(j)\); this maximum exists because the lattice support is finite. The preceding argument and permutation invariance show that the rank consists exactly of all the orbits \(0\le a\le a_{\max}(j)\). Empty ranks are simply omitted from \(J\). Therefore every such \(Q\), with any positive deficit profile \(w_j\), satisfies (10), including when its lattice support lies in a proper affine subspace.

Return to the convex set \(P\) in the statement. Partition the original support disjointly as

$$
A_-:=\{(\mathbf x,0)\in A:\sum_i x_i\le k\},
\qquad
A_+:=\{(\mathbf x,0)\in A:\sum_i x_i>k\}.
\tag{11}
$$

Whenever \(A_-\) is nonempty, the convex coordinate-permutation-invariant set \(Q_-:=P\cap\{\sum_i x_i\le k\}\) has lattice support \(A_-\) at height zero. The normalized restriction \(q_-\) of the original law to \(A_-\) has deficit profile \(w_j=v_{k-j}\). Its normalizer is \(Z_-:=\sum_{(\mathbf x,0)\in A_-}v_{\sum_i x_i}\). These are exactly the \(w_j,Z_q\) in the lower-rank estimate for this support, so (10) applies to \(q_-\).

For the upper half use the invertible affine map
\(T:\mathbb R^{k+1}\to\mathbb R^{k+1}\),
\(T(\mathbf x,t):=(2\mathbf1-\mathbf x,t)\).
The spatial image
\(Q_+:=\{2\mathbf1-\mathbf x:\mathbf x\in P,\ \sum_i x_i>k\}\)
is convex, coordinate-permutation-invariant, and contained in
\([0,2]^k\cap\{\sum_i x_i<k\}\). Strictness of this rank inequality is harmless: the lower-rank argument imposed no closedness assumption. Moreover,
\(T(A_+)=(Q_+\cap\mathbb Z^k)\times\{0\}\), since \(T\) preserves integrality and is its own inverse.

When \(A_+\ne\varnothing\), let \(q_+\) be the normalized restriction of the original law to \(A_+\). Under \(T\), an image point with sum \(k-j\) came from a point with sum \(k+j\). Thus the image law \(\widetilde q_+:=T_*q_+\) has the lower-rank deficit profile \(w_j=v_{k+j}\), with the same normalizer \(Z_+:=\sum_{(\mathbf x,0)\in A_+}v_{\sum_i x_i}\). Missing ranks, including the absent image rank of sum \(k\), are allowed in (10).

For completeness, these are the actual ambient moments before and after reflection. If \(H=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x,t)\ge b\}\) has nonzero normal, then \(T^{-1}H\) is a closed affine halfspace with normal obtained by negating the first \(k\) components of \(\mathbf u\) and keeping its last component. Its normal is still nonzero. Inverse images give a bijection between all closed ambient halfspaces containing \(T\mathbf z\) and those containing \(\mathbf z\). The corresponding law masses agree. Therefore \(\tau_{\widetilde q_+}(T\mathbf z)=\tau_{q_+}(\mathbf z)\) and \(M_3(\widetilde q_+)=M_3(q_+)\). Applying (10) to \(Q_+\) gives

$$
M_3(q_-)\ge271^{-3}8^{-(k+1)}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
M_3(q_+)\ge271^{-3}8^{-(k+1)}
\tag{12}
$$

for each of the conditional laws whose support is nonempty.

Finally suppose first that both halves in (11) are nonempty, and put \(\alpha:=p(A_-)=Z_-/Z\), so \(1-\alpha=p(A_+)=Z_+/Z\). For every \(\mathbf z\in A_-\) and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf z\),
\(p(A\cap H)\ge\alpha q_-(A_-\cap H)\ge\alpha\tau_{q_-}(\mathbf z)\).
Taking the infimum gives \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\tau_{q_-}(\mathbf z)\). The corresponding upper-half inequality is
\(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge(1-\alpha)\tau_{q_+}(\mathbf z)\) for \(\mathbf z\in A_+\).
The sampling weights on the two disjoint halves are respectively \(\alpha q_-\) and \((1-\alpha)q_+\). Consequently (12) yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\alpha^4M_3(q_-)+(1-\alpha)^4M_3(q_+)\\
&\ge\bigl(\alpha^4+(1-\alpha)^4\bigr)271^{-3}8^{-(k+1)}
\ge\frac1{8\cdot271^3}8^{-(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

The last step follows from convexity of \(t\mapsto t^4\):
\((\alpha^4+(1-\alpha)^4)/2\ge(1/2)^4\).
If either half is empty, the original law equals the other conditional law, and (12) gives the stronger coefficient \(271^{-3}\), which also implies (13). Since \(D\ne\varnothing\), the two halves cannot both be empty. This proves the claimed bound in every case, with original probabilities, closed boundaries, height zero, and the original ambient halfspaces throughout.
