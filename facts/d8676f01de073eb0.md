## Statement

Use the exact two-hub scalar convention of accepted fact a9f4cb83f6711543, specialized to q_ov=1. Explicitly, let m_0,m_1 be positive integers, let h belong to {0,1}, and let X be the disjoint union of the singleton c_h and the endpoint sets of m_0 pairs labeled zero and m_1 pairs labeled one. The two distinguished atoms c_0,c_1 are distinct; c_{1-h} occurs as an endpoint in one pair of label h, and c_h is not an endpoint. In particular the pair of label h containing c_{1-h} has endpoint weights w_{c_{1-h}} and w_{c_h}^2/w_{c_{1-h}}. Require every pair of label a to have endpoint-weight product w_{c_a}^2. Give all atoms positive original weights w_x and use exactly their original normalizer Z=sum_x w_x and original law p_x=w_x/Z. Set a_a=p_{c_a}, let S_a be the total original probability of the endpoints of the pairs of label a, and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=m_0+m_1+1,
&\mu_a&=\frac{S_a/a_a+2}{2(m_a+1)},\\
A&=K^2\sum_{x\in X}p_x^3,
&\Delta_a&=4m_a(m_a+1)K^2a_a^3,\\
A_{\mathrm{full}}&=A+\Delta_0+\Delta_1,
&H&=\frac{\Delta_0}{\mu_0}+\frac{\Delta_1}{\mu_1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here K is the occupied parity count in an exact forced-spine realization, not 2^n. The total counts m_a in (1) are not the additional-axis counts of accepted fact 4bb26a33ac78ee45. Their exact aliases are (m_0-1,m_1) in that predecessor when h=0, and (m_1-1,m_0) after interchanging that predecessor's core labels when h=1.

The following atomic estimate holds without a bound on either count or either mean:
$$
1-A\le\frac34H.
\tag{2}
$$
If, in addition, A<1 and A_full<=9/8, define d=-log A and the exact Shapley increments
$$
r_a=\frac12\left[
\log\frac{A+\Delta_a}{A}
+\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{full}}}{A+\Delta_{1-a}}
\right].
\tag{3}
$$
Then
$$
d\le\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}\,r_a.
\tag{4}
$$
This includes the requested subregion mu_0>=2mu_1 and both possible h.

The assertions apply to arbitrary positive nonforced pair weights, so they apply to the exact forced-spine Jensen minima as well. For their globally affine realization from accepted fact 4bb26a33ac78ee45, let W=w_{c_h}. The predecessor's anchored raw weights are the minimizing original raw weights divided by W, its anchored normalizer is Z/W, and its anchored core zero is the present core h. Its center ratio is tau=w_{c_{1-h}}/W. Multiplying all its raw weights and its normalizer by W recovers the original raw scale and the unchanged original probabilities. No law is conditionally renormalized.

The signed condition R<=Delta_0/(4mu_0) of accepted fact a9f4cb83f6711543 is false on some exact q_ov=2 tables with A<1, mu_0>=2mu_1, and A_full<=9/8. A finite globally affine witness, and the exact remaining q_ov=2 capacity residual for the present proof, are given below. Neither observation asserts failure of (2) or (4) on q_ov=2.

## Proof

We first prove two scalar capacity inequalities. For every integer m>=1, every u>=2m, and mu=(u+2)/(2(m+1)), put
$$
E_m(u)=\frac{u^3}{m^2}-3u+
\frac{3m(m+1)}{\mu}
=\frac{u^3}{m^2}-3u+
\frac{6m(m+1)^2}{u+2}.
\tag{5}
$$
The inequalities needed below are
$$
E_m(u)\ge\frac{u^3}{(m+1/4)^2},
\qquad
1+E_m(u)\ge\frac{(u+1)^3}{(m+3/4)^2}.
\tag{6}
$$
The fractions 1/4 and 3/4 in these two capacities are complementary, so the eventual sum of capacities remains exactly K.

For the first inequality, set
$$
C_m=\frac1{m^2}-\frac1{(m+1/4)^2}
=\frac{8m+1}{m^2(4m+1)^2}.
\tag{7}
$$
Multiplying the first difference in (6) by u+2 gives
$$
P_m(u)=C_mu^4+2C_mu^3-3u^2-6u+6m(m+1)^2.
\tag{8}
$$
When m>=2, the inequality 16m^2-16m-3>0 implies C_m>=3/(8m^3). Since u>=2m, one has -6u>=-3u^2/m. Discarding the nonnegative cubic term and completing a square as a polynomial in u^2 therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_m(u)
&\ge C_mu^4-\frac{3(m+1)}m u^2+6m(m+1)^2\\
&\ge6m(m+1)^2-
\frac{9(m+1)^2}{4m^2C_m}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
For m=1, write u=2+v with v>=0. Direct expansion gives
25P_1(2+v)=9v^4+90v^3+249v^2+54v+288>0.
This proves the first inequality in (6) for every m.

We next prove the second inequality in (6). If u>=5m, the first one suffices. Indeed, for 0<=x<=1/5, squaring both nonnegative sides shows (1+x)^{3/2}<=1+2x, because (1+2x)^2-(1+x)^3=x(1+x-x^2)>=0. Consequently
$$
(m+1/4)(1+1/u)^{3/2}
\le(m+1/4)\left(1+\frac2{5m}\right)
\le m+3/4.
\tag{10}
$$
Thus E_m(u)>=u^3/(m+1/4)^2>=(u+1)^3/(m+3/4)^2 when u>=5m, even before adding the central contribution one.

For the remaining interval 2m<=u<=5m, multiply the second difference in (6) by the positive number m^2(4m+3)^2(u+2). The resulting polynomial is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_m(u)={}&(24m+9)u^4+(-48m^2+48m+18)u^3\\
&-(48m^4+72m^3+171m^2)u^2\\
&-(80m^4+120m^3+157m^2)u\\
&+96m^7+336m^6+438m^5+284m^4+102m^3-14m^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
For m=1 and u=2+v, this is
Q_1(2+v)=33v^4+282v^3+609v^2-249v+36.
The quadratic part is strictly positive: its leading coefficient is 609>0 and its discriminant is 249^2-4*609*36=-25695<0. Hence Q_1(u)>0 for every u>=2.

Here is a nonnegative-coefficient certificate for all the remaining integers m>=2 on the whole interval 2m<=u<=5m. Put w=m-2>=0 and z=(u/m-2)/3, so 0<=z<=1. In the Bernstein expansion
$$
\frac{Q_m(m(2+3z))}{m^2}
=\sum_{i=0}^4 b_i(m)\binom4i z^i(1-z)^{4-i},
\tag{12}
$$
the following table gives the coefficients of 4b_i(w+2), in descending powers w^5,w^4,w^3,w^2,w,1:

| i | w^5 | w^4 | w^3 | w^2 | w | 1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 384 | 4416 | 19928 | 43856 | 46608 | 18792 |
| 1 | 384 | 3840 | 14792 | 27044 | 22737 | 6426 |
| 2 | 384 | 2976 | 8648 | 13094 | 13842 | 9108 |
| 3 | 384 | 1824 | 5384 | 28574 | 80025 | 71874 |
| 4 | 384 | 384 | 16664 | 149624 | 386364 | 313632 |

For clarity, (12) and the table are polynomial identities obtained by expansion of (11), not numerical positivity tests. Every coefficient in the table is positive. Thus b_i(m)>0 for every real m>=2, and every basis term in (12) is nonnegative on 0<=z<=1. At least one basis term is positive, so Q_m(u)>0 on this whole interval. Together with (10) and the m=1 calculation, this proves the second inequality in (6).

We apply these two scalar inequalities to the original law. A pair of label a with total original probability s and endpoint product a_a^2 contributes exactly s^3-3a_a^2s to the atomic cubic sum. Jensen over the m_a pair totals therefore gives
$$
\sum_{x\in R_a\setminus\lbrace c_a\rbrace}p_x^3
\ge\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}-3a_a^2S_a.
\tag{13}
$$
Here R_a consists of c_a and all the endpoints of label a; the endpoint sets are disjoint, although the full cores intersect at c_{1-h}. Since each pair total is at least 2a_a, the algebraic variable u_a=S_a/a_a satisfies u_a>=2m_a. Definition (1) gives exactly mu_a=(u_a+2)/(2(m_a+1)), so the endpoint contribution plus (3/4)Delta_a/(K^2 mu_a) is at least a_a^3 E_{m_a}(u_a).

The singleton is c_h. Apply the second inequality of (6) to label h, including its singleton cubic contribution a_h^3, and apply the first inequality to label 1-h. The two masses are S_h+a_h and S_{1-h}; they sum to one because they partition the original support. The two capacities are m_h+3/4 and m_{1-h}+1/4; they sum to the occupied count K. Therefore weighted Holder gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
A+\frac34H
&\ge K^2\left[
\frac{(S_h+a_h)^3}{(m_h+3/4)^2}
+\frac{S_{1-h}^3}{(m_{1-h}+1/4)^2}
\right]\\
&\ge K^2\frac{(S_h+a_h+S_{1-h})^3}
{(m_h+3/4+m_{1-h}+1/4)^2}=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
This proves (2) without imposing the residual condition from accepted fact a9f4cb83f6711543, a bound on m_a/mu_a^2, or a mean comparison.

We give the stronger atomic-to-Shapley conversion explicitly. It also works for any two positive increments Delta_0,Delta_1 and any means mu_a>=1, provided A>=1/4, A<1, A_full<=9/8, and (2) holds. Formula (3) has the integral representation
$$
r_a=\frac12\int_0^{\Delta_a}
\left[\frac1{A+s}+\frac1{A+\Delta_{1-a}+s}\right]ds.
\tag{15}
$$
The mean of the denominator in this average of two uniform intervals is (A+A_full)/2. Convexity of the reciprocal therefore yields
$$
r_a\ge\frac{2\Delta_a}{A+A_{\mathrm{full}}}.
\tag{16}
$$
For mu_a>=1, the coefficient 256/(256+2mu_a)=128/(128+mu_a) is at least 128/(129mu_a). Using (2) gives, with delta=1-A>0,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_a\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}r_a
&\ge\frac{256}{129(A+A_{\mathrm{full}})}H\\
&\ge\frac{1024}{387(A+9/8)}\,\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
The original one-overlap law has 2K-1 atoms. Holder consequently gives A>=K^2/(2K-1)^2>1/4, with the same original probabilities and occupied K.

For completeness, the following elementary bound controls the logarithm uniformly on 1/4<=A<1:
$$
(A+9/8)\frac{-\log A}{1-A}
\le\frac{11}{6}\log4
<\frac{77}{30}<\frac{1024}{387}.
\tag{18}
$$
To prove the first inequality, for c>=1 put f_c(v)=(v+c)(-log v)/(1-v). Its derivative is nonpositive on 0<v<1 precisely when
J_c(v)=(v+c)(1-v)+(1+c)v log v>=0.
Here J_c(1)=0 and J_c'(v)=2(1-v)+(1+c)log v<= (1-c)(1-v)<=0, using log v<=v-1. Thus J_c(v)>=0 for v<1, so f_c is nonincreasing. Evaluate at c=9/8 and v=1/4 to obtain the first inequality in (18). For the second, log4<7/5: the exponential series through degree four at 7/10 already has sum greater than 2, so log2<7/10. The last comparison follows from 77*387=29799<30720=30*1024.

Equations (17)-(18) give the requested inequality (4), in fact strictly on the active branch.

Finally, (13) is valid for arbitrary positive nonforced pair weights. Equalizing the nonforced pair totals within a core preserves the original hub weights and endpoint sums and attains their Jensen minimum; it changes neither the original normalizer Z nor any mu_a or Delta_a. The theorem applies directly to that minimizing law. Accepted fact 4bb26a33ac78ee45 realizes it with the additional-axis count aliases stated above. Its anchored nonforced weights are the minimizing raw weights divided by W=w_{c_h}; its forced spine weights are the same raw weights divided by W because the forced products are exactly w_{c_h}^2 and w_{c_{1-h}}^2. Thus its anchored normalizer is Z/W and its normalized law is precisely the minimizing original law. This is also the raw-scale map in accepted fact a9f4cb83f6711543, now with the arbitrary orientation h retained explicitly. No atom was deleted, no shared atom was counted twice in (14), and no ambient parity capacity replaced occupied K.

We next record exactly what remains in the two-overlap case. In this paragraph only, take q_ov=2, so K=m_0+m_1, the endpoint sets partition the original support, and both forced pair totals
L_a=a_{1-a}+a_a^2/a_{1-a}
occur. Retain all the meanings in (1), now with this occupied K. For m_a>=2 define
$$
\mathcal J_a=L_a^3+
\frac{(S_a-L_a)^3}{(m_a-1)^2}-\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}.
\tag{19}
$$
When m_a=1, the exact domain requires S_a=L_a; define J_a=0 in that case. Convexity of the cubic makes J_a>=0. At the exact forced-spine minimum, its endpoint cubic contribution is exactly S_a^3/m_a^2-3a_a^2S_a+J_a. This follows by retaining the forced pair and equalizing the other m_a-1 pair totals; it also includes the stated empty case.

Define the scalar capacity slack and the two-block Holder defect by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_a&=a_a^3E_{m_a}(S_a/a_a)
-\frac{S_a^3}{(m_a+1/4)^2},\\
\mathcal G&=K^2\sum_{a=0}^1
\frac{S_a^3}{(m_a+1/4)^2}
-\left(\frac{K}{K+1/2}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
The first inequality of (6) gives E_a>=0; weighted Holder, using S_0+S_1=1 and total capacity K+1/2, gives G>=0. At the exact forced-spine minimum one has the exact identity
$$
A+\frac34H-1
=\mathcal G+K^2\sum_{a=0}^1(\mathcal E_a+\mathcal J_a)
-\frac{K+1/4}{(K+1/2)^2}.
\tag{21}
$$
For arbitrary nonforced pairs the left side is at least the right side. Thus the explicit remaining sufficient inequality for this capacity route is
$$
\mathcal G+K^2\sum_{a=0}^1(\mathcal E_a+\mathcal J_a)
\ge\frac{K+1/4}{(K+1/2)^2}.
\tag{22}
$$
It is equivalent to the 3/4 atomic estimate at the exact minima. Every term and the missing amount in (22) use the unchanged original law. The nonnegativity of the three displayed defects alone does not establish (22), and this packet does not assert it. The right side is the exact cost of the remaining one-half unit of capacity; no defect was silently discarded.
