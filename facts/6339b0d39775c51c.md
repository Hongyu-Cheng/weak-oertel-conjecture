## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^n h_{\max}(C).
\]
Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard instance of accepted facts 6bfe50fed9a5540b and 1d61837127904315. Thus \(Q>0\) is the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir, the hard ledger gives
\[
 D<\frac Q{100},
\tag{1}
\]
and the target loads \((\lambda_m)\) have total mass
\[
 \Lambda:=\sum_m\lambda_m>\frac{49}{50}Q.
\tag{2}
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), write
\[
 B_\xi:=\sum_{m\equiv\xi}\lambda_m,
 \qquad
 \beta_\xi:=\max_{m\equiv\xi}\lambda_m,
 \qquad
 U_\xi:=(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+,
 \qquad
 U:=\sum_\xi U_\xi,
\tag{3}
\]
with \(\beta_\xi=0\) in an empty class. Let \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\) be the exact minimum-unmatched off-diagonal same-parity target matching supplied by 1d61837127904315, and use the noncolliding notation
\[
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}:=|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|
 =\sum_f s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
 =\frac{\Lambda-U}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
For every physical target fiber \(C_m\), let \(a_m\) be its volume, \(c_m\) its centroid, and
\[
 \mathcal K_m:=\mathsf M_{a_m/2}(C_m),
 \qquad
 D_m:=\mathcal K_m-c_m.
\]
For \(f=\{m,m'\}\) in the positive support of \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\), put
\[
 \widehat m_f:=\frac{m+m'}2,
 \qquad
 \bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\frac{c_m+c_{m'}}2,
 \qquad
 D_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\frac12(D_m+D_{m'}),
\tag{5}
\]
and define, in the exact normalization of accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b,
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}:=\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
 :=\min_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_f s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
 \left(
 \|a(\widehat m_f)-\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\|_{D_f^{\mathrm{tar}}}-1
 \right)_+.
\tag{6}
\]
Finally, put
\[
 \delta_{1/8}:=\frac{-7\log(7/8)}{\log2}-1.
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 U<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}<\frac{11}{400}Q,
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}>\frac{381}{800}Q.}
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{4}
 \left(
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}
 \right)_+,}
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda\leq\frac{9D}{\gamma_d}
 +\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}.}
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}
 >\frac{\delta_{1/8}}2
 \left(\frac{49}{50}-\frac{9}{100\gamma_d}\right)Q
 >\frac{293}{2400}Q
 >\frac3{25}Q.}
\tag{11}
\]
This is a large-affine-defect normal form only. It does not give an upper bound for \(\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}\), absorb that defect into mixed depth, prove an iteration with comparable mass, identify the variance descendant as another canonical matching state, or prove the locked theorem.

## Proof

Accepted fact 1d61837127904315, on the common instance retained above, gives both
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}U
\tag{12}
\]
and the exact mass identity (4). Multiplying (12) by \(2^n\) gives
\[
 D\geq\gamma_d U.
\tag{13}
\]
Together with the strict hard bound (1), inherited from accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b, this yields
\[
 U\leq\frac D{\gamma_d}<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}.
\tag{14}
\]
Accepted fact 2fe46f47d19112ea proves the dimension-uniform strict estimate
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac4{11}.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}<\frac{11}{400}Q,
\tag{16}
\]
which proves the first part of (8). Equations (2), (4), and (16) give
\[
 2P_{\mathrm{tar}}
 =\Lambda-U
 >\left(\frac{49}{50}-\frac{11}{400}\right)Q
 =\frac{381}{400}Q.
\tag{17}
\]
This proves the mass bound in (8), in particular without assuming in advance that the target matching is nonzero.

We next apply the general nested-metronoid affine-defect estimate of accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b with \(p=1/8\). By (15), \(1/8<\gamma_d\), as that theorem requires. Define the scaled matching
\[
 q_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=2\gamma_d s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{18}
\]
Accepted fact 1d61837127904315 gives
\[
 d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\leq\frac{a_m}{2}
\]
for every physical target fiber. Hence
\[
 d_m(q^{\mathrm{tar}})
 =2\gamma_d d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
 \leq\gamma_d a_m,
\tag{19}
\]
so \(q^{\mathrm{tar}}\) satisfies exactly the matching-capacity hypothesis of 11e347c295e9430b. Under the alias map
\[
 (z,z',m_e,\bar c_e,D_e,q_e)
 \longleftrightarrow
 (m,m',\widehat m_f,\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}},D_f^{\mathrm{tar}},q_f^{\mathrm{tar}}),
\tag{20}
\]
all midpoint, centroid, and Minkowski-gauge objects in (5)-(6) are precisely those of that accepted theorem. The support is unchanged by the positive scaling in (18), and the defect objective is linear in the edge weights. Therefore
\[
 |q^{\mathrm{tar}}|=2\gamma_dP_{\mathrm{tar}},
 \qquad
 \Delta_C(q^{\mathrm{tar}})
 =2\gamma_d\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(p=1/8\), the predecessor's quantity
\[
 \delta_p=\frac{\psi(p)}{\log2}-1,
 \qquad
 \psi(p)=-\frac{1-p}{p}\log(1-p),
\]
is exactly the number \(\delta_{1/8}\) in (7). Its estimate applied to (18) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_{\max}(C)
 &\geq\frac18\,2^{-n}
 \left(
 |q^{\mathrm{tar}}|
 -\frac{\Delta_C(q^{\mathrm{tar}})}{\delta_{1/8}}
 \right)_+\\
 &=\frac{\gamma_d}{4}\,2^{-n}
 \left(
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}
 -\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}
 \right)_+,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
where the second line also uses \((tx)_+=t(x)_+\) for \(t=2\gamma_d>0\). Multiplication by \(2^n\) proves (9).

We record an exact lower bound on the denominator before using it. Formula (7) gives
\[
 \delta_{1/8}>\frac13
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 21\log\frac87>4\log2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left(\frac87\right)^{21}>16.
\tag{23}
\]
The last inequality follows by an integer comparison:
\[
 25\cdot8^7=52{,}428{,}800
 >51{,}883{,}209=63\cdot7^7,
\]
so
\[
 \left(\frac87\right)^{21}
 >\left(\frac{63}{25}\right)^3
 =\frac{250{,}047}{15{,}625}
 >16.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus \(\delta_{1/8}>1/3>0\), and all subsequent divisions by \(\delta_{1/8}\) are legitimate.

It remains to extract (10) from the positive part in (9). By (4),
\[
 \Lambda=2P_{\mathrm{tar}}+U,
\tag{25}
\]
and (13) gives \(U\leq D/\gamma_d\). If
\[
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}\leq
 \frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}},
\]
then directly from (25),
\[
 \Lambda
 \leq\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}
 +\frac D{\gamma_d}
 \leq\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}
 +\frac{9D}{\gamma_d}.
\tag{26}
\]
If instead
\[
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}>
 \frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}},
\]
then (9) gives
\[
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}
 \leq\frac{4D}{\gamma_d}
 +\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}.
\tag{27}
\]
Substituting (27) and \(U\leq D/\gamma_d\) into (25) proves
\[
 \Lambda
 \leq\frac{9D}{\gamma_d}
 +\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus (10) holds in both signs of the positive-part argument. No division by \(P_{\mathrm{tar}}\) was used.

Combining (1), (2), and (10), and using \(\delta_{1/8}>0\), gives
\[
 \frac{49}{50}Q
 <\Lambda
 \leq\frac{9D}{\gamma_d}
 +\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}
 <\frac{9}{100\gamma_d}Q
 +\frac{2\Delta_{\mathrm{tar}}}{\delta_{1/8}}.
\tag{29}
\]
Rearrangement proves the first strict lower bound in (11). By (15),
\[
 \frac{49}{50}-\frac{9}{100\gamma_d}
 >\frac{49}{50}-\frac{99}{400}
 =\frac{293}{400}.
\tag{30}
\]
Together with \(\delta_{1/8}>1/3\), this gives
\[
 \frac{\delta_{1/8}}2
 \left(\frac{49}{50}-\frac{9}{100\gamma_d}\right)Q
 >\frac16\cdot\frac{293}{400}Q
 =\frac{293}{2400}Q.
\tag{31}
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac{293}{2400}>\frac{288}{2400}=\frac3{25},
\tag{32}
\]
which completes (11). Every step used the same canonical zero-fresh instance. The argument eliminates the unmatched-load, zero-matching, and small-affine-defect exits, but it supplies no mechanism for absorbing the remaining large defect or closing the locked theorem.
