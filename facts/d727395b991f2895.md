## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Put \(U=96\), and define the following absolute constants:
$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=C_c(U)=4U^2+24(1+50000U^2e^{2U}),\\
C_0&=16C,\qquad C_1=2^{20}C+2U^2+32,\qquad
C_{\mathrm{dis}}=48C_1^2,\\
c&=\frac14\exp\bigl(-C_0-C_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{dis}}}\bigr)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(C_c(U)\) is exactly the coefficient of accepted fact 149d1105f63274b9.

For every integer \(L\ge1\), integers \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\), finite nonempty sets \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), and strictly positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) on all of \(A_i\), assume the following. Every coordinatewise parity class of \(A_i\) contains at most four atoms, and every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\) satisfies
$$
j=(x+y)/2\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{2}
$$
For each \(i\), there is a finite list of positive real numbers \(T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,\ell_i}\), where \(\ell_i\) is a nonnegative integer, with \(T_{i,b+1}\ge4T_{i,b}\) whenever both indices exist. Every nonzero score of a distinct same-parity pair satisfies
$$
\frac12\left|\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\in[0,1]\ \cup\ \bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},3T_{i,b}/2].
\tag{3}
$$
There is no positive lower bound on the first listed base, and no common bound on the number of bands, the maximum score, total band width, dimensions, or number of factors. Empty lists, bands containing no scores, and equal-weight pairs are allowed.

Keep the original normalizers, probabilities, occupied parity counts, and product:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&
p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i,&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&
N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Extend these laws by zero off their supports. At every supported product center \(\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i\), define its actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and the original-law moments by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,\\
M_r(P)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\qquad(r=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-2},
\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-3}.
\tag{6}
$$
These conclusions include singleton and lower-dimensional supports, arbitrary within-class weight ties, and arbitrary multiplicities of pairs sharing a midpoint. All centers, probabilities, and normalizers in (4)--(6) are the original ones.

## Proof

We use the selected-core notation of accepted facts 149d1105f63274b9 and 6d5cb397c4ad27d5. The largest-atom mechanism of accepted fact 0539ec4ffa895fed is applied below only after proving that the charged stars remain high. Its zero-score rows are replaced here by the specified bounded-score core. Accordingly, its zero-score energy \(E\) is not renamed or used: the present energy is \(E_c\). The quantity \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\) below equals \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) in 0539ec4ffa895fed. The present \(B\) will count balanced high star pairs, namely the restriction of that fact's \(B_*\) to high stars; it is not the all-balanced-pair sum called \(B\) in 6d5cb397c4ad27d5. We prove every estimate required by these changes.

Fix one factor and suppress its index. Let \(\mathcal P(A)\) be the occupied representatives in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), and let \(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\). Let \(\mathscr Q\) be all unordered pairs \(e=\lbrace x,y\rbrace\) of distinct atoms in one such class. Define \(j_e=(x+y)/2\) and \(s_e=\frac12|\log(p_x/p_y)|\). Normalization cancels in both (2) and this score. Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}}\quad(x\in R_j),\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j),\\
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,&
F&=K^2b(p),\\
\rho_x&=Kp_x,&
R_\eta&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\rho_x,\qquad
D_{\mathrm{un}}=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}(R_\eta-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Thus \(F\) always denotes the full-row energy. Every noncentral reflection orbit at \(j\) is a pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(j\). Distinct pairs at a fixed center have disjoint endpoints because the reflected mate of \(x\) is uniquely \(2j-x\). The central atom occurs once, and (2) makes every geometric-mean summand equal to \(p_j\). The bijection \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) from full oriented row entries to ordered same-parity pairs, including the diagonal, therefore gives
$$
\chi(p)=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}
\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x\right)^2
=\frac1K+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K^2},
\qquad F\ge K^2\chi(p)^2\ge1.
\tag{8}
$$
The energy inequality is Jensen under the original probability \(p\).

Select the core \(\mathscr Q_c\) by retaining every zero-score pair, every pair with score at most one, and every pair in a listed band whose base is strictly less than \(64\). Retain a pair only once if descriptions overlap. Every selected score is at most \(U=96\). The remaining pairs \(\mathscr Q_H=\mathscr Q\setminus\mathscr Q_c\) lie in unique high bands with bases at least \(64\), because the listed bands are disjoint. The cutoff is by band base, not by the inequality \(s_e\le U\): a band with base \(64\), including its score endpoint \(96\), is high. A band with base strictly less than \(64\) has upper endpoint strictly less than \(96\).

At every original center keep its central atom and the endpoints of all selected core pairs:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{c,j}&=\lbrace j\rbrace\ \cup
\bigcup_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}e,&
r_{c,j}&=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_c:j_e=j\rbrace|,&h_j&=1+2r_{c,j},\\
H_c(j)&=h_jp_j,&
\chi_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j),&
b_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j)^2,\qquad E_c=K^2b_c,\\
\kappa_{c,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_c(j)}
\quad(x\in R_{c,j}),&
q_{c,j}&=\frac{p_jH_c(j)^2}{b_c},\\
v_c(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{c,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_c&=\sum_jq_{c,j}v_c(j),&
V_c&=\sum_jp_j(KH_c(j)-1)^2,\\
\Lambda_H&=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_H}\rho_{j_e}^2.&&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
Every sum over centers in this proof is over all of \(A\). All denominators in (9) are positive because each row contains its original center. Every omitted unordered pair contributes separately to \(\Lambda_H\), even when midpoints coincide. These are exactly the selected quantities of 149d1105f63274b9, with the present \(\mathscr Q_c\), original \(p\), and \(U=96\). That fact yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_c\nu_c&\le C\left(V_c+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}\right),&
K\chi_c&=1+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_H}{K},\\
E_c&=1+V_c+\frac{2D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}-\frac{2\Lambda_H}{K},&
E_c&\ge\frac1{16},\\
E_c\nu_c&\le C\left(E_c-1+\frac{2\Lambda_H}{K}\right).&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
Also \(E_c\ge(K\chi_c)^2\) by Jensen, and \(\chi_c>0\).
If \(\mathscr Q_H\) is empty, the core is the full row system, so \(E_c=F\ge1\). Equations (10) and \((E_c-1)/E_c\le\log E_c\) give \(\nu_c\le C\log E_c\). Such a factor uses the core without any label in the product selection below. This treats an empty list and any list without actual high pairs.

Suppose now that \(\mathscr Q_H\) is nonempty. When indexing high-band sums, delete bands containing no high pair; this preserves separation and changes no selection. Let \(T_{\min}\ge64\) be the smallest remaining high base and put \(\epsilon_H=64e^{-2T_{\min}}\le1/2\). Every core score is strictly below \(T_{\min}\). Indeed, a core score exceeding one lies in a listed band with base less than \(64\), hence in a band preceding the band at \(T_{\min}\). Factor-four separation makes that band's upper endpoint at most \(3T_{\min}/8\). Scores at most one are also strictly below \(T_{\min}\).

Call an occupied class balanced if \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). In each balanced class fix a largest atom \(z_\eta\), breaking ties lexicographically. Let \(\mathscr S_H\) consist of the pairs \(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\in\mathscr Q_H\) in these classes, and set
$$
B=\sum_{e\in\mathscr S_H}\rho_{j_e}^2,\qquad
B_b=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr S_H\\s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]}}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\qquad B=\sum_bB_b,\qquad \beta=B/K.
\tag{11}
$$
Every high pair has unequal endpoint weights. Orient such a pair in a balanced class as \(\rho_x<\rho_y\). Since \(\rho_y\le\rho_{z_\eta}\), the pair \(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) has score at least \(s_e\ge T_{\min}\). It is a same-parity pair of the original support, so (3) applies, and the preceding strict core bound shows that it is high. Exact midpoint equality gives
\(\rho_{(x+y)/2}^2=\rho_x\rho_y\le\rho_x\rho_{z_\eta}=\rho_{(x+z_\eta)/2}^2\).
For a fixed smaller endpoint \(x\), at most three larger endpoints can occur. Thus the sum of squared midpoint masses of all balanced high pairs is at most \(3B\), hence at most \(4B\). This also counts pairs whose larger endpoint is tied with the chosen maximum: their smaller endpoint still determines the stated high star. Equal-weight pairs have score zero and require no charge.

In an unbalanced source class, any high pair has squared midpoint mass at most \(e^{-2T_{\min}}R_\eta^2\), by writing its larger endpoint mass as \(\alpha\) and its midpoint mass as \(\alpha e^{-s_e}\). There are at most six unordered pairs in that class. Outside \([1/2,2]\) one has \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\). Including the factor two in \(\Lambda_H\), the unbalanced contribution is therefore at most \(48e^{-2T_{\min}}D_{\mathrm{un}}\). Combining the two contributions proves the slightly weaker convenient bound
$$
\Lambda_H\le8B+\epsilon_HD_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{12}
$$
Here \(\epsilon_H\le1/2\) follows, for example, from \(e>2\) and \(T_{\min}\ge64\).

Every midpoint of a balanced high star has an unbalanced own parity class. To prove this, take such a star in band \(b\), and let its largest endpoint mass be \(\alpha=\rho_{z_\eta}\). Because its balanced source class has at most four atoms, \(\alpha\in[1/8,2]\). With score \(s\in[T_b,3T_b/2]\), its midpoint \(j\) has mass \(\rho_j=\alpha e^{-s}\le2e^{-T_b}<1/8\). If the own class of \(j\) were balanced, its largest atom would have mass \(\alpha'\in[1/8,2]\) and would be distinct from \(j\). Their positive same-parity score would be
$$
s'=\frac{s}{2}+\frac12\log\frac{\alpha'}{\alpha}
\in[T_b/2-\tfrac12\log16,\ 3T_b/4+\tfrac12\log16]
\subset(3T_b/8,T_b).
\tag{13}
$$
The inclusions are strict because \(\frac12\log16<2\) and \(T_b\ge64\). Every earlier listed band, whether used by the core or high, ends at or before \(3T_b/8\); the current and later bands start at or above \(T_b\). Also \(1<3T_b/8\). Thus (13) contradicts (3). This argument includes \(T_b=64\) and both closed endpoints of every permitted band.

For a high band \(b\), let \(J_b\) be the set of its distinct balanced-star midpoints, and let \(r_{b,j}\) count its balanced high star pairs at midpoint \(j\). Each class meeting \(J_b\) is unbalanced and contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\). There are at most \(4D_{\mathrm{un}}\) such classes and at most \(16D_{\mathrm{un}}\) distinct target atoms. Since every such atom has mass at most \(2e^{-T_b}\),
$$
\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\le32e^{-T_b}D_{\mathrm{un}},
\qquad B_b=\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}\rho_j^2.
\tag{14}
$$
Only distinct target classes and atoms are counted in the first inequality. The pair multiplicities remain in \(r_{b,j}\). A target class can also be a source class in (12); the two estimates are separate bounds using the same total defect, not disjoint charges added to it.

Define the alternative row \(R_{b,j}\) by adjoining to the entire core \(R_{c,j}\) all pairs of \(\mathscr Q_H\) in band \(b\) centered at \(j\), including those from unbalanced source classes. Let \(r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) count these added unordered pairs, and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{b,j}}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
F_b&=K^2\sum_jp_jH_b(j)^2,&G_b&=F_b-E_c,\\
\kappa_{b,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_b(j)}
\quad(x\in R_{b,j}),&
q_{b,j}&=\frac{K^2p_jH_b(j)^2}{F_b},\\
v_b(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{b,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_b&=\sum_jq_{b,j}v_b(j).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is precisely the core-plus-entire-band selection of 6d5cb397c4ad27d5, now on the present occupancy-four support. The auxiliary row and center laws use \(H_b\) and \(F_b/K^2\) in place of \(H_c\) and \(b_c\) in (9); the original law and centers and the full quantity \(F\) in (7) are unchanged. The core multiplier \(h_j\) is not redefined. Disjointness of reflection orbits gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=(h_j+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})p_j,\\
G_b&=\frac1K\sum_j\rho_j^3
\left((h_j+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})^2-h_j^2\right)
\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz on the distinct centers in \(J_b\), together with (14)--(15), yields
$$
B_b^2
\le\left(\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3\right)
   \left(\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\right)
\le8KG_be^{-T_b}D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{16}
$$
The square on the pair multiplicity in the gain is retained. We have not added gains from different bands, nor treated any row selection as a new midpoint-closed support.

If \(B>0\), there is a band with \(B_b\ge e^{-T_b/4}B>0\). Indeed, after the indexing above, separation implies \(T_b\ge T_{\min}+3T_{\min}(b-1)\). For \(q=e^{-T_{\min}/4}<1/2\),
\(\sum_b e^{-T_b/4}\le q/(1-q^3)<4/7<1\).
The strict reverse inequalities for every band would therefore contradict \(\sum_bB_b=B\). Choose one such band, and write \(\theta=T_b/2\ge32\) and \(G=G_b>0\). There are at most three stars per balanced class, hence at most \(4K\) stars in total, and every star in this band has squared midpoint mass at most \(4e^{-2T_b}\). The choice of band and (16) give
$$
\beta\le e^{T_b/4}B_b/K\le16e^{-7\theta/2},
\qquad
\beta^2\le8e^{-\theta}G\,D_{\mathrm{un}}/K.
\tag{17}
$$
The positivity of \(G\) follows directly from (15), since \(B_b>0\).

First consider \(E_c<1\). Positivity of \(\chi_c\), Jensen, and (10) imply \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_H\). With (12) and \(\epsilon_H\le1/2\), this gives
\(B>0\), \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<16B\), \(\Lambda_H\le16B\), and \(\Lambda_H-D_{\mathrm{un}}\le8B\).
For example, \(B=0\) would force \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<\epsilon_HD_{\mathrm{un}}\), impossible. Put \(d=-\log E_c>0\). Writing \(a_{\mathrm{core}}=K\chi_c\in(0,1)\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-E_c&\le1-a_{\mathrm{core}}^2
\le2(1-a_{\mathrm{core}})
=2(\Lambda_H-D_{\mathrm{un}})/K\le16\beta,\\
d&\le(1-E_c)/E_c\le256\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
The last step uses \(E_c\ge1/16\). In the band selected above, (17) and \(D_{\mathrm{un}}/K<16\beta\) imply \(G\ge e^\theta\beta/128\). Equations (10), (17), and (18) therefore yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
d&\le32768e^{-\theta}G,&
d&\le4096e^{-7\theta/2},\\
\nu_c&\le512C\beta\le65536Ce^{-\theta}G,&
\nu_c&\le8192Ce^{-7\theta/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
For the core-price estimate, explicitly,
\(E_c\nu_c\le C(E_c-1+2\Lambda_H/K)\le32C\beta\);
division by \(E_c\ge1/16\) gives the stated \(512C\beta\).

Since \(e>2\) and \(\theta\ge32\), \(32768e^{-\theta}<1/64\), so \(d\le G/64\). If \(0<G\le1\), then \(1-E_c\le d\) and \(F_b=E_c+G\ge1+G/2>1\). Thus \(g=\log F_b\ge G/4\), by integrating \(1/(1+t)\ge1/2\) on \(0\le t\le G/2\). If \(G\ge1\), then \(F_b\ge17/16\), so \(g\ge\log(17/16)\ge1/17\ge1/32\). In the first case use the gain bounds in (19); in the second use the absolute bounds in (19) and \(e^{-7\theta/2}\le e^{-\theta}\). Both cases, including \(G=1\), give
$$
F_b>1,\qquad
d\le2^{17}e^{-\theta}g\le g/4,\qquad
\nu_c\le2^{18}Ce^{-\theta}g.
\tag{20}
$$
The second inequality for \(d\) follows from \(2^{17}e^{-32}<1/4\). The lower logarithmic bound at \(17/16\) is the integral bound
\(\log(17/16)\ge(1/16)/(17/16)=1/17\).

Next consider \(E_c\ge1\), allowing \(E_c=1\). Put \(a_+=\log E_c\), \(u=\sqrt{E_c}-1\), \(\delta=D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\), and \(\lambda_H=\Lambda_H/K\). These quantities are nonnegative. Jensen and (10) give \(1+\delta-\lambda_H=K\chi_c\le1+u\), hence \(\delta\le\lambda_H+u\). Combining this with (12) yields
$$
\delta\le16\beta+2u,\qquad
\lambda_H\le16\beta+2\epsilon_Hu.
\tag{21}
$$
Indeed, \((1-\epsilon_H)\delta\le8\beta+u\), and substituting the resulting \(\delta\) estimate back into (12) proves the other inequality. Since \(4\epsilon_Hu\le2u\le E_c-1\), equation (10) then gives
$$
\nu_c\le2C\frac{E_c-1}{E_c}+32C\frac{\beta}{E_c}
\le2Ca_++32C\frac{\beta}{E_c}.
\tag{22}
$$
If \(B=0\), this is \(\nu_c\le2Ca_+\). Use only the core, without a label, even if unbalanced high pairs exist. At \(E_c=1\), this case forces \(\nu_c=0\).

If \(B>0\), choose the band giving (17). Equations (17) and (21) imply
\(\beta^2\le e^{-\theta}G(128\beta+16u)\le e^{-\theta}G(128\beta+32u)\).
For \(v_{\mathrm{gain}}=e^{-\theta}G>0\), the positive root of this quadratic gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta&\le64v_{\mathrm{gain}}
+\sqrt{4096v_{\mathrm{gain}}^2+32v_{\mathrm{gain}}u}\\
&\le128v_{\mathrm{gain}}+\sqrt{32v_{\mathrm{gain}}u}
\le144v_{\mathrm{gain}}+u/2
\le160v_{\mathrm{gain}}+u/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The penultimate inequality follows by squaring
\(\sqrt{32v_{\mathrm{gain}}u}\le16v_{\mathrm{gain}}+u/2\).
Moreover, \(u/E_c\le(E_c-1)/(2E_c)\le a_+/2\). Thus (22)--(23) imply
\(\nu_c\le10Ca_++5120Ce^{-\theta}G/E_c\).
Independently, (17) and (22) give
\(\nu_c\le2Ca_++512Ce^{-7\theta/2}\).
Consequently, using \(C_0=16C\),
$$
\nu_c\le C_0a_++5120Ce^{-\theta}G/E_c,
\qquad
\nu_c\le C_0a_++512Ce^{-7\theta/2}.
\tag{24}
$$
Set \(g=\log(F_b/E_c)=\log(1+G/E_c)>0\). If \(G/E_c\le1\), then \(G/E_c\le2g\), and the first estimate in (24) applies. If \(G/E_c\ge1\), then \(g\ge\log2\ge1/2\), and the second estimate applies. In both cases, including \(G/E_c=1\),
$$
\nu_c\le C_0a_++10240Ce^{-\theta}g.
\tag{25}
$$
The inequalities \((t-1)/t\le\log t\) for \(t\ge1\), \(\log(1+x)\ge x/2\) for \(0\le x\le1\), and \(\log2\ge1/2\) all follow by bounding the integrand \(1/t\) on the corresponding intervals. The nonnegative baseline \(a_+\) has been retained throughout this branch.

We must also price the alternative when the core has nonzero scores. At a fixed center put \(r=r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\), and let \(S_c(j)\) and \(S_b(j)\) be the sums of squared unordered pair scores in its core and added band, respectively. Then \(S_c(j)\le U^2r_{c,j}\), \(2r_{c,j}\le h_j\), and \(S_b(j)\le9\theta^2r\). The selected geometric row laws are uniform because each summand equals \(p_j\). Thus the exact numerator for the alternative is
\(F_b\nu_b=(2/K)\sum_j\rho_j^3(h_j+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})(S_c(j)+S_b(j))\),
whereas \(E_c\nu_c=(2/K)\sum_j\rho_j^3h_jS_c(j)\).
Its extra core part satisfies
$$
\frac4K\sum_j\rho_j^3r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}S_c(j)
\le\frac{2U^2}{K}\sum_j\rho_j^3r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}h_j
\le\frac{U^2}{2}G.
\tag{26}
$$
For the high-score part, the rowwise inequality
\(2r(h_j+2r)\le(h_j+2r)^2-h_j^2\)
and (15) give the upper bound \(9\theta^2G\). We conclude
$$
F_b\nu_b\le E_c\nu_c+(U^2/2+9\theta^2)G.
\tag{27}
$$
These exact expansions are the row-selection price calculation of 6d5cb397c4ad27d5, now proved with every multiplicity for the present rows.

In the deficient branch, \(G/F_b=1-E_c/F_b\le\log(F_b/E_c)=g+d\le5g/4\) by (20). In the nondeficient branch it is at most \(\log(F_b/E_c)=g\). Also \(E_c/F_b\le1\). Since \(\theta\ge32\), equations (20), (25), and (27) yield the following uniform choices. For every factor define \(a_+=(\log E_c)_+\) and \(d=(-\log E_c)_+\), where \(t_+=\max\lbrace t,0\rbrace\). Every labeled factor has two genuine selections:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{selection}&\text{logarithmic scaled energy}&\text{score price bound}\\ \hline
\text{core}&a_+-d&C_0a_++C_1e^{-\theta}g\\
\text{core and selected high band}&a_++g&C_0a_++C_1\theta^2g
\end{array}
\qquad d\le C_1e^{-\theta}g.
\tag{28}
$$
In the deficient branch \(g=\log F_b\); in the nondeficient branch \(g=\log(F_b/E_c)\). To check the constants explicitly, \(C\ge1\), so \(C_1\) dominates \(2^{17}\), \(2^{18}C\), and \(10240C\). The alternative price in the deficient branch is at most
\([2^{18}C+(5/8)U^2+45/4]\theta^2g\).
In the other branch it is at most
\(C_0a_++[10240C+U^2/2+9]\theta^2g\).
Both bracketed coefficients are at most \(C_1\) in (1), since \(e^{-\theta}\le1\le\theta^2\).
An unlabeled factor is one with no actual high pair or one with \(E_c\ge1\) and \(B=0\). It uses the core, has logarithmic scaled energy \(a_+\ge0\), and has price at most \(C_0a_+\), by (10) or (22). No deficient factor is unlabeled.

Return to the finite product. For every integer \(m\ge0\), let \(I_m\) be the set of labeled factors whose \(\theta_i\) belongs to \([m,m+1)\), and put \(G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i\). Retain a bin exactly when \(G_m\ge4C_1(m+1)^2\). In a retained bin select the core and its selected high band for every factor; in every other bin use the core. Unlabeled factors also use the core. For a retained bin put \(W_m=C_1(m+1)^2G_m\). Its excess score price is at most \(W_m\), and the cutoff implies \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\), including equality at the cutoff.

Define the discarded deficit and residual core-price budget by
$$
D_{\mathrm{dis}}=\sum_{m\text{ discarded}}\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{dis}}=C_1\sum_{m\text{ discarded}}\sum_{i\in I_m}e^{-\theta_i}g_i.
\tag{29}
$$
Equation (28) gives \(D_{\mathrm{dis}}\le V_{\mathrm{dis}}\). A discarded bin contributes at most \(C_1e^{-m}G_m<4C_1^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2\) to \(V_{\mathrm{dis}}\). Since \(e>2\) and
\(\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m}(m+1)^2=12\), we obtain
$$
D_{\mathrm{dis}}\le C_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad V_{\mathrm{dis}}\le C_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad C_{\mathrm{dis}}=48C_1^2.
\tag{30}
$$
For completeness, the numerical series identity follows from
\(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\) at \(r=1/2\), obtained by differentiating the geometric series. Only finitely many bins are nonempty. The bounds therefore charge both discarded quantities once for the entire product.

For the final chosen rows \(R_{i,j}^*\), define, using the original probabilities,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&
q_{i,j}^*&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2}{b_i^*},\\
v_i^*(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&
\nu_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}^*v_i^*(j),\\
S_g&=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*),&
A_{\log}&=\sum_i a_{+,i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$
The score \(Y_{i,j}\) is the original score from (7) restricted to the selected row. These quantities have exactly the final-row meanings in 6d5cb397c4ad27d5, with the present choices (28). In particular \(A_{\log}\ge0\) includes labeled and unlabeled factors. The exact logarithmic energies and price bounds imply
$$
S_g=A_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-D_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad
\sum_i\nu_i^*\le C_0A_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m+V_{\mathrm{dis}}.
\tag{32}
$$
Subadditivity of the square root, \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\), and
\(A_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_0A_{\log}}\ge-C_0\) now give
$$
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}
\ge-C_0-D_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{dis}}}
\ge-C_0-C_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{dis}}}.
\tag{33}
$$
The baseline inequality follows by completing the square in
\(\sqrt{A_{\log}}-\sqrt{C_0}\). Thus (33) also covers no labels, no retained bins, or a core containing only central atoms.

We finally prove the conversion to actual ambient halfspaces directly, retaining the original-law normalization as in 0539ec4ffa895fed and 6d5cb397c4ad27d5. Each selected row is symmetric about its original center, and reflection preserves its geometric probability law and negates \(Y_{i,j}\). Hence \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}=0\). For every selected endpoint, the exact identity and normalization are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{i,x}&=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}
=w_{i,x}/Z_i,\\
b_i^*&=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
$$
Thus the auxiliary geometric and center laws have not replaced or conditioned \(p_i\).

At an arbitrary original product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), form
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_i v_i^*(j_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
$$
Here \(\sigma_{\mathbf j}\) is the nonnegative square root. Finite product sums show that \(H_P^*(\mathbf j)\) is the geometric mass of this product row. Independence under \(\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*\) and the zero score means give
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\).
Multiplying (34) yields
\(P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}\),
where \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,X_i}/\prod_iZ_i\) is the original product mass.

Any ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2\mathbf j-X\rbrace\). Otherwise its affine defining inequality would strictly exclude both endpoints and hence their midpoint. It also contains the central atom. Simultaneous reflection preserves the selected product row and its geometric law, and negates \(Y_{\mathbf j}\). Summing the smaller original probability on every noncentral orbit and using half the available central mass gives a lower bound independent of the halfspace. Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz then imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}
\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
$$
The oriented sum counts a noncentral orbit twice before division by two, and the central atom once. Closed-halfspace boundary atoms, singleton rows, and zero variances cause no exception. The argument does not require full-dimensionality.

Let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\), an auxiliary law on all original centers. Equations (31) and (35) give
\(P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2=(\prod_i b_i^*)q_{\mathbf j}^*\)
and \(\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*\).
Squaring (36), summing under the original \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q^*\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)
\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
$$
by (1) and (33). Jensen under that same original law, for \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\), finally gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P[(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}]\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\),
which proves the second inequality in (6).

Only selected reflection rows and auxiliary laws factor in (34)--(37); no actual-depth or moment tensorization is used. All original centers remain in the final moments. The constants in (1) are finite and absolute, so their single payment is independent of the number of factors and bands. The core and high-band cutoff equalities, empty lists, empty high parts, \(B=0\), both energy branches, singleton supports, and lower-dimensional supports have all been included in the preceding estimates.
