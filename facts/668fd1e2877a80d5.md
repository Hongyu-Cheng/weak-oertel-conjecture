## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(I=\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\), and let \(E\) be any set of ordered pairs of distinct elements of \(I\). Let \(G\) be its underlying simple undirected graph: \(\lbrace u,v\rbrace\) is an edge of \(G\) exactly when at least one of \((u,v),(v,u)\) belongs to \(E\). Assume that every vertex of \(G\) has degree at most two. Both orientations of an edge may belong to \(E\), and the graph may be disconnected or have isolated vertices.

For arbitrary \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), define the full support and original law by
$$
A_E=\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^n:x_u-x_v\le1
       \text{ for every }(u,v)\in E\rbrace,\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in A_E}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z}\quad(x\in A_E),
$$
and extend \(p\) by zero outside \(A_E\). In the notation of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, this is exactly the specialization \(L=1\), \(n_1=n\), \(A_1=A_E\), \(w_{1,x}=e^{\ell\cdot x}\), \(Z_1=Z\), \(p_1=p\), with the block index suppressed. Thus, for every \(j\in A_E\), use exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A_E:2j-x\in A_E\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A_E}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)}
       \quad(x\in R_j),&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,&
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then
$$
\nu(p):=\sum_{j\in A_E}q_jV_j
\le Cn,\qquad
C=4(1+\sqrt2)(\log3)^2.
$$
All logarithms are natural. The estimate uses the full rows \(R_j\), the full supported center law \(q\), and the original normalizer \(Z\).

In particular this holds for
\(A_n=\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^n:x_i-x_{i+1}\le1
\text{ cyclically}\rbrace\) for every \(n\ge1\). When \(n=1\) its sole loop constraint is vacuous and is deleted; when \(n=2\) its two constraints are the two orientations of the same undirected edge.

## Proof

For a supported center \(j\), write
\(\mathcal E_j=\lbrace v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v\in R_j\rbrace\) for the full displacement row. This is only a translation of the canonical row, with \(x=j+v\). Affineness gives
$$
\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot j}}Z=p_j
\quad(v\in\mathcal E_j).
$$
Consequently the canonical quantities specialize exactly to
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(j)&=p_j|R_j|,&
\kappa_j(j+v)&=\frac1{|R_j|}\quad(v\in\mathcal E_j),&
Y_j(j+v)&=\ell\cdot v,\\
b(p)&=\frac1{Z^3}\sum_{k\in A_E}e^{3\ell\cdot k}|R_k|^2,&
q_j&=\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
           {\sum_{k\in A_E}e^{3\ell\cdot k}|R_k|^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
The vector \(0\) belongs to every \(\mathcal E_j\), so all these denominators are positive. In particular \(q\) is the canonical probability law of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, with no change of normalization.

We first determine every reflected row. Define the free set of a center by
$$
F(j)=\lbrace i\in I:j_i=1,\
       j_v\ne0\text{ for all }(i,v)\in E,\
       j_u\ne2\text{ for all }(u,i)\in E\rbrace.
\tag{2}
$$
The exact displacement census is
$$
\mathcal E_j=
\left\lbrace v\in\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace^n:
 v_i=0\ (i\notin F(j)),\quad
 |v_u-v_v|\le1
 \text{ for every edge }\lbrace u,v\rbrace
 \text{ of }G[F(j)]\right\rbrace.
\tag{3}
$$
Here \(G[F(j)]\) is the induced simple undirected graph. To prove (3), the coordinate restrictions \(j_i\pm v_i\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\) first force \(v_i=0\) at centers \(j_i=0,2\) and allow \(v_i\in\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\) at centers \(j_i=1\). For a directed edge \((u,v)\), its two reflected inequalities are equivalent to
$$
|v_u-v_v|\le1-(j_u-j_v).
\tag{4}
$$
Since \(j\in A_E\), the center difference is at most one. If it equals one, the center pair is \((1,0)\) or \((2,1)\). Its extreme coordinate already has zero displacement, and (4) forces the displacement at the coordinate with center value one to zero. These are precisely the exclusions in (2). If the center difference is zero and both centers are one, (4) is the displayed adjacency restriction in (3), unless one displacement has already been forced to zero, in which case it is automatic. Equal extreme centers have zero displacements. For a negative center difference, at least one center is extreme and has zero displacement, so the displacement difference has magnitude at most one, while the right side of (4) is at least two. Thus these edges impose nothing further. This proves both inclusions in (3), and also shows that using both edge orientations imposes the same single adjacency restriction on two free coordinates.

Every connected component of the finite graph \(G[F(j)]\) is an isolated vertex, a path, or a simple cycle, because its maximum degree is at most two. Treat an isolated vertex as a path with one vertex. Formula (3) and the uniform law in (1) show that the displacement coordinates on these components are independent, each uniformly distributed over its allowed configurations. No independence of coordinates or components under the original law \(p\) or under the center law \(q\) is asserted.

Here is an explicit count for each component, as well as a uniform covariance estimate. Use the transfer matrix and the displacement matrix
$$
\mathsf T=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&1&1\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathsf D=\operatorname{diag}(-1,0,1),
\qquad
\rho=1+\sqrt2,\qquad \rho_-=1-\sqrt2=-\rho^{-1}.
\tag{5}
$$
The alphabet order is \(-1,0,1\). A path with \(m\ge1\) vertices has
\(z_m=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathsf T^{m-1}\mathbf1\) allowed configurations, and a cycle with \(m\ge3\) vertices has
\(c_m=\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf T^m)\) allowed configurations. The orthonormal eigenvectors
$$
u_+=\frac12(1,\sqrt2,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
u_-=\frac12(1,-\sqrt2,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
u_o=\frac1{\sqrt2}(-1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}
$$
have respective eigenvalues \(\rho,\rho_-,1\). Therefore
$$
z_m=\frac{\rho^{m+1}+\rho_-^{m+1}}2,\qquad
c_m=\rho^m+\rho_-^m+1.
\tag{6}
$$
For the path formula, use
\((\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}u_+)^2=\rho^2/2\),
\((\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}u_-)^2=\rho_-^2/2\), and
\(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}u_o=0\). The cycle formula is the trace of the diagonalization. It follows that the exact full row cardinality is the product of \(z_m\) over its free path components and \(c_m\) over its free cycle components. An empty product is one. Thus (2), (3), and (6) give a complete census of all rows.

We prove that any one of these uniform component laws, with displacement coordinates \(W_1,\ldots,W_m\), satisfies
$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{a=1}^m t_aW_a\right)
\le \rho\sum_{a=1}^m t_a^2
\quad\text{for every }(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in\mathbb R^m.
\tag{7}
$$
All means are zero, because global sign reversal preserves the allowed configurations.

For a path, write
\(\mathsf T^k\mathbf1=(a_k,b_k,a_k)^{\mathsf T}\).
The nonnegative matrix \(\mathsf T\) preserves componentwise inequalities. With
\(h=(1,\sqrt2,1)^{\mathsf T}\), we have
\(\mathbf1\le h\), \(\mathbf1\ge h/\sqrt2\), and
\(\mathsf T h=\rho h\). Hence
\(a_k\le\rho^k\), and
$$
z_m=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathsf T^{m-1}\mathbf1
\ge \rho^{m-1}\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h}{\sqrt2}
=\rho^m.
\tag{8}
$$
The vector \((-1,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\) is an eigenvector of \(\mathsf T\) with eigenvalue one. For \(1\le a\le b\le m\), the transfer expression for the covariance therefore reduces to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E W_aW_b
&=\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathsf T^{a-1}
       \mathsf D\mathsf T^{b-a}\mathsf D
       \mathsf T^{m-b}\mathbf1}{z_m}\\
&=\frac{2a_{a-1}a_{m-b}}{z_m}
\le \frac2\rho\,\rho^{-(b-a)}
\le \rho^{-(b-a)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
This also proves the bound on the diagonal \(a=b\). In particular the sum of the absolute covariances in any row of the path covariance matrix is at most
\(1+2\sum_{d\ge1}\rho^{-d}=1+2/(\rho-1)=\rho\).

For a cycle, indices are taken modulo \(m\). At cyclic separation \(d\in\lbrace0,\ldots,m-1\rbrace\), the transfer expression is
$$
\mathbb E W_1W_{1+d}
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf D\mathsf T^d
                         \mathsf D\mathsf T^{m-d})}
       {\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf T^m)}.
\tag{10}
$$
The eigenvectors above satisfy
\(\mathsf D u_+=\mathsf D u_-=u_o/\sqrt2\) and
\(\mathsf D u_o=(u_++u_-)/\sqrt2\). Expanding the trace in this orthonormal basis gives
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf D\mathsf T^d
                  \mathsf D\mathsf T^{m-d})
=\frac{\rho^d+\rho_-^d+\rho^{m-d}+\rho_-^{m-d}}2.
\tag{11}
$$
Since \(|\rho_-|<1\), the denominator in (10) is
\(\rho^m+1+\rho_-^m\ge\rho^m\). Also
\(|\rho_-|^k\le\rho^k\) for every integer \(k\ge0\).
Thus (10), including \(d=0\), implies
$$
|\mathbb E W_1W_{1+d}|
\le \rho^{-d}+\rho^{-(m-d)}.
\tag{12}
$$
The sum of these bounds over one entire covariance row is
$$
\sum_{d=0}^{m-1}\left(\rho^{-d}+\rho^{-(m-d)}\right)
=\frac{\rho+1}{\rho-1}(1-\rho^{-m})
=\rho(1-\rho^{-m})\le\rho.
\tag{13}
$$
For either component type, let \(K_{ab}=\operatorname{Cov}(W_a,W_b)\). Symmetry and the absolute row-sum bounds just proved give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_a t_aW_a\right)
&\le\sum_{a,b}|K_{ab}|\,|t_at_b|\\
&\le\frac12\sum_{a,b}|K_{ab}|(t_a^2+t_b^2)
\le\rho\sum_a t_a^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
This proves (7). Applying it to each independent component of the full row, using zero displacements outside \(F(j)\), proves
$$
V_j=\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_j}(\ell\cdot(X-j))
\le\rho\sum_{i\in F(j)}\ell_i^2.
\tag{14}
$$
The equality uses (1) and the zero score mean furnished here by sign symmetry.

It remains to control the free-site probabilities in the full center law. Fix a center \(j\) and a free site \(i\in F(j)\). Let \(j^{(0)}\) and \(j^{(2)}\) be obtained by replacing \(j_i=1\) by zero and two, respectively, with all other coordinates fixed. Both replacements belong to \(A_E\). For replacement by zero, every incoming edge has preceding center different from two by (2), so no incoming constraint is violated; every outgoing constraint is automatic at a zero center. Replacement by two is valid because all outgoing neighbors are nonzero, again by (2), and every incoming constraint is automatic at a center of value two.

Write
\(N^-(i)=\lbrace u:(u,i)\in E\rbrace\) and
\(N^+(i)=\lbrace v:(i,v)\in E\rbrace\).
The free-site criterion (2) gives exactly
$$
F(j^{(0)})=F(j)\setminus(\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^-(i)),
\qquad
F(j^{(2)})=F(j)\setminus(\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^+(i)).
\tag{15}
$$
For example, changing the center at \(i\) from one to zero newly pins precisely its formerly free incoming neighbors; the incoming-neighbor test at an outgoing neighbor changes from a value different from two to another value different from two. If both orientations occur, the same neighbor is removed once. The corresponding statement for replacement by two follows by the outgoing-neighbor test.

Combining (15) with the exact census (3) yields the stronger section identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{j^{(0)}}
 &=\lbrace v\in\mathcal E_j:
       v_u=0\text{ for every }u\in\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^-(i)\rbrace,\\
\mathcal E_{j^{(2)}}
 &=\lbrace v\in\mathcal E_j:
       v_u=0\text{ for every }u\in\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^+(i)\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
Indeed, after these coordinates are fixed to zero, every original adjacency involving a removed free vertex is automatic, because its other displacement is in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\); the remaining free adjacency restrictions are exactly those of the replacement row. Setting any selected displacement coordinates to zero preserves the constraints in (3). Thus zeroing the coordinates in either line of (16) maps the whole old row onto the stated section. Each fiber has at most \(3^3=27\) elements, because each vertex has at most two distinct undirected neighbors. We conclude that
$$
|R_j|\le27|R_{j^{(0)}}|,
\qquad
|R_j|\le27|R_{j^{(2)}}|.
\tag{17}
$$

Now let \(J\) have the canonical center law \(q\). Condition on all coordinates \(J_k\) with \(k\ne i\). If no supported completion with \(J_i=1\) makes \(i\) free, the conditional free-site probability is zero. Otherwise let \(j\) be that completion. Both replacement completions are supported, as proved above. Using the exact weights in (1) and (17), their conditional unnormalized weights have ratios
$$
\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
     {e^{3\ell\cdot j^{(0)}}|R_{j^{(0)}}|^2}
\le729e^{3\ell_i},
\qquad
\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
     {e^{3\ell\cdot j^{(2)}}|R_{j^{(2)}}|^2}
\le729e^{-3\ell_i}.
\tag{18}
$$
For \(\ell_i\ge0\), bound the conditional probability by the ratio to the completion of value two. For \(\ell_i\le0\), use the completion of value zero. Including the trivial probability bound one and then averaging the conditional bounds proves, for every site and every field,
$$
\mathbb P_q(i\in F(J))
\le\min\lbrace1,729e^{-3|\ell_i|}\rbrace.
\tag{19}
$$
This conditioning argument uses all supported completions, and all normalizing factors cancel from the ratios; no alternative center normalization is used.

Set \(a=(\log729)/3=2\log3\), so \(a>2/3\). For \(0\le t\le a\),
\(t^2\min\lbrace1,729e^{-3t}\rbrace=t^2\le a^2\).
For \(t\ge a\), the same expression is \(729t^2e^{-3t}\), whose derivative is
\(729e^{-3t}t(2-3t)\le0\). Its value at \(a\) is \(a^2\). Consequently (19) gives
$$
\ell_i^2\mathbb P_q(i\in F(J))
\le a^2=4(\log3)^2.
\tag{20}
$$
Averaging the full-row bound (14) under \(q\), and then summing (20), proves
$$
\nu(p)=\mathbb E_qV_J
\le\rho\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_i^2\mathbb P_q(i\in F(J))
\le4(1+\sqrt2)(\log3)^2n.
\tag{21}
$$

For completeness the smallest graph cases can be read directly from the same census. When \(n=1\), there are no edges, the support is \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), and the full row has size three at center one and size one at centers zero and two; its free component is a one-vertex path. When \(n=2\) and there is no edge, the support is all nine points and \(|R_j|=3^{|\lbrace i:j_i=1\rbrace|}\). When there is exactly one directed edge \((u,v)\), the support excludes only the ordered center pair \((2,0)\); the row has size seven at \((1,1)\), size three at \((0,1)\) and \((1,2)\), and size one at its other five supported centers, with coordinates ordered as \((u,v)\). If both orientations occur, the support excludes \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\); the row has size seven at \((1,1)\) and size one at its other six centers. A two-vertex free path has \(z_2=7\), and a one-vertex free path has \(z_1=3\), in agreement with (6). These descriptions include either orientation and isolated vertices. Formula (3) also establishes their factorization with any other connected components.

Finally, the original cyclic support has underlying degree at most two for \(n\ge2\). Its free-site condition from (2) is exactly
\(j_i=1,\ j_{i-1}\ne2,\ j_{i+1}\ne0\), with cyclic indices. For \(n=2\) this is the bidirected-edge case just listed, with seven supported centers and a seven-point full row only at \((1,1)\). For \(n=1\), deleting the vacuous constraint \(x_1-x_1\le1\) gives the one-vertex case. Thus (21) applies to the full canonical reflection quantities for every original cyclic support, including both small conventions.
