## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, and let the positive-length intervals $K_z=[l_z,u_z]$ for $z\in P$ be the actual fibers of a convex set. Let $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu$ be the sum of interval lengths on $S$, $M=\mu(S)$, $W=\max_z(u_z-l_z)$, and $D=[-W,W]$. There is a cover of $S$ by at most $2^{n+1}$ nonvertical closed affine caps $H_i=\{L_i\ge0\}$ such that $(L_i)_+/h_D(v_i)\le1/2$ everywhere on $S$, where $v_i$ is the continuous coefficient of $L_i$ and $h_D$ is the support function of $D$. In particular
$$
\sum_i\int_S \frac{(L_i)_+}{h_D(v_i)}\,d\mu
\le \frac12\sum_i\mu(H_i)\le 2^n M .
$$

## Proof

For each $p\in P$, compatibility gives the anchored Minkowski inclusion for every convex combination of bases with mean $p$. Apply 155691fc3768c06f at $p$ in the directions $1$ and $-1$. The support heights are $u_z$ and $-l_z$, respectively. The resulting affine majorants give an affine $b_p\ge u_z$ and, after negating the second one, an affine $a_p\le l_z$, both with equality at $p$. They support the sampled endpoint hull; no assertion about a nonvertical support at a boundary fiber of the original unsampled body is needed.

The base halfspaces $G_p=\{z:b_p(z)-a_p(z)\le W\}$ cover $P$, since $p\in G_p$. To select the pairs, apply the finite-set witness lemma in bee3cc8e50a0dcea to the open convex complements of the $G_p$. An empty vertex subset of $P$ contains at most one point per parity: two points of one parity have a midpoint in $P$ by lattice completeness, which lies in their convex hull without being a vertex. Thus at most $2^n$ of the complements still have common intersection disjoint from $P$; the corresponding $G_p$ cover $P$. This argument also permits a constant supporting width and $n=0$.

For each selected $p$ use $L_p^+=x-b_p(z)+W/2$ and $L_p^-=a_p(z)-x+W/2$. Their sum is $W-(b_p(z)-a_p(z))$, nonnegative on $G_p$. Consequently their closed caps cover the whole continuous line at every selected base in $G_p$, and in particular all endpoints of its sampled interval. The support inequalities give $L_p^+\le W/2$ and $L_p^-\le W/2$ throughout $S$. Each continuous coefficient is $1$ or $-1$, so its support denominator is $W$. Each hinge vanishes off its cap and is bounded by $1/2$ on the cap. Integration and the cap count give the two inequalities.
