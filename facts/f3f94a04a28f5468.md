## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and set
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in E}a_z>0.
\]
Let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed-volume measure of \(C\), let
\[
G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\]
be the global complete metronoid-core capacity in accepted facts
\(\texttt{ae0161dd5d4ea672}\) and \(\texttt{3d1f63abf09b2abb}\), and put
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
\kappa_d:=\frac{\gamma_d}{2}.
\]

Fix \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\) and \(a\in(0,W]\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\ge a
\tag{1}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\). Let \(B\subseteq C\) be a nonempty compact convex set, let \(t\in(0,a)\), and assume that the descendant surplus \(\Delta_{m,B}(t)\) of accepted fact \(\texttt{2e2778e4564d7745}\) is positive. Identify the nonempty compact reachable slice
\[
Y:=\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\subseteq\{m\}\times\mathbb R^d
\]
with its continuous-coordinate image in \(\mathbb R^d\).

For every \(z\in E\), define
\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\qquad
h_z(v):=\max_{q\in D_z}\langle v,q\rangle,
\]
and define
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_z\lambda_z=1,
\ \sum_z\lambda_zz=m\right\},
\qquad
\phi_m(v):=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_z\lambda_zh_z(v).
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose that \(0<\delta<\kappa_da\) and that finitely many proper closed ambient halfspaces
\[
H_i:=\left\{(z,x):
\langle v_i,x\rangle+\langle u_i,z-m\rangle\ge c_i\right\},
\qquad v_i\ne0,
\qquad \mu_C(H_i)<\delta
\quad(1\le i\le N),
\tag{3}
\]
have target-slice interiors covering \(Y\):
\[
Y\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^N\{x:\langle v_i,x\rangle>c_i\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
r_i(z):=c_i-\langle u_i,z-m\rangle,
\qquad
K_i:=\{x:\langle v_i,x\rangle\le c_i\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then all the following conclusions hold.

1. Strict envelope separation and exact-cap contact data. For every \(i\),
\[
c_i>\phi_m(v_i).
\tag{6}
\]
For every nonzero \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), there is a globally affine function \(\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
\ell(z)\le h_z(v)\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
\ell(m)=\phi_m(v).
\tag{7}
\]
For every such exact minorant, define the exact-minorant cap body
\[
A(v,\ell):=
C\cap\{(z,x):\langle v,x\rangle\ge\ell(z)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(A(v,\ell)_z)\ge\kappa_da_z
\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
W(A(v,\ell))\ge\kappa_dW.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(\lambda\) is a vertex optimizer in (2) for \(v\), and
\[
T:=\{z:\lambda_z>0\},
\qquad
\rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}E,
\]
then \(T\) is affinely independent,
\[
1\le |T|\le\rho+1,
\qquad
\ell(z)=h_z(v)\quad(z\in T),
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(A(v,\ell)_z)\le\frac{a_z}{2}
\quad(z\in T),
\qquad
\sum_{z\in T}a_z\le G.
\tag{11}
\]
For a shallow direction \(v_i\), every vertex optimizer \(\lambda^i\) with support \(T_i\) also satisfies the exact defect identity
\[
c_i-\phi_m(v_i)
=\sum_{z\in T_i}\lambda_z^i
\bigl(r_i(z)-h_z(v_i)\bigr)>0.
\tag{12}
\]
Moreover, accepted fact \(\texttt{3d1f63abf09b2abb}\) gives, for every \(z\in E\),
\[
G\ge
\min\left\{\frac{W+a_z}{2},\ 2^na_z\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, if \(G<W/2\), then
\[
a_z\le2^{-n}G\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
\sum_{z\in T}a_z
\le\min\{G,(\rho+1)2^{-n}G\}.
\tag{14}
\]

In particular, an exact-minorant cap in a shallow direction has mixed mass at least \(\kappa_dW\), whereas the original blocker has mass less than \(\delta<\kappa_da\le\kappa_dW\). Thus the shallow blocker and its exact-minorant replacement are different cap bodies, and the shallow mass estimate is not preserved. An exact-minorant cap is not thereby a legal circuit cap: legality requires the additional globally support-minimal balanced-negative-family hypothesis of accepted fact \(\texttt{e3679dc542b23efe}\).

2. Empty target-complement branch. If
\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^NK_i=\varnothing,
\tag{15}
\]
then there are indices, relabeled \(1,\ldots,k\), and coefficients \(\theta_i>0\) such that
\[
k\le d+1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_ic_i<0.
\tag{16}
\]
Choose a vertex optimizer \(\lambda^i\) for each \(\phi_m(v_i)\), and put
\[
U_z:=\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i\lambda_z^iv_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Then \((U_z)_{z\in E}\) is nonzero and is an exact negative affine-moment stress:
\[
\sum_zU_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}=0,
\qquad
\sum_zh_z(U_z)<0.
\tag{18}
\]
It is supported on \(\bigcup_iT_i\), where each \(T_i:=\operatorname{supp}\lambda^i\) satisfies (10)-(11), and hence
\[
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{supp}U}a_z\le kG.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(G<W/2\), the right side in (19) improves to
\[
k\min\{G,(\rho+1)2^{-n}G\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Define
\[
R_{\theta}(z):=
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i
\bigl(c_i-\langle u_i,z-m\rangle\bigr),
\qquad
J_{\theta}:=\{z:R_{\theta}(z)\le0\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The set \(J_{\theta}\) is either a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\), or the whole base space, and
\[
C_z\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^k(H_i)_z
\qquad(z\in E\cap J_{\theta}).
\tag{22}
\]
If
\[
\eta_{\theta}:=
\inf_{\substack{z\in E\cap J_{\theta}\\x\in C_z}}
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i\mathbf1_{H_i}(z,x),
\tag{23}
\]
then
\[
0<\min_i\theta_i\le\eta_{\theta}<\frac{\delta}{a},
\qquad
k>\frac{a}{\delta}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus a sequence of empty-complement shallow blockers with \(\delta/a\to0\) must have diverging cardinality and degenerate smallest multiplier. Nothing in (16)-(24) makes this blocker family a legal support-minimal cap circuit or converts its stress into a same-parity matching.

3. Nonempty target-complement branch. Suppose
\[
Q:=\bigcap_{i=1}^NK_i\ne\varnothing.
\tag{25}
\]
Then there is a subfamily of \(k\le d\) of the \(K_i\)'s, again relabeled \(K_1,\ldots,K_k\), whose intersection \(Q_0\) is nonempty and disjoint from \(Y\). There are coefficients \(\theta_i\ge0\), not all zero, and a nonzero vector
\[
w:=\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_iv_i
\tag{26}
\]
such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_ic_i
=h_{Q_0}(w)
<\min_{y\in Y}\langle w,y\rangle.
\tag{27}
\]
Put \(u:=-w\). Exactly one of the following two mutually exclusive numerical cases holds.

(a) Envelope closure:
\[
\phi_m(u)\le h_Y(u),
\qquad
h_Y(u):=\max_{y\in Y}\langle u,y\rangle.
\tag{28}
\]
Then the directions \(v_i\) with \(\theta_i>0\), together with \(u\) with coefficient one, form a negative balanced envelope family:
\[
\sum_i\theta_iv_i+u=0,
\qquad
\sum_i\theta_i\phi_m(v_i)+\phi_m(u)<0.
\tag{29}
\]
Choosing vertex optimizers and collapsing as in (17) gives a nonzero field satisfying all three equations in (18). It is supported on at most \(k+1\) affine-independent contact sets, so its physical support mass is at most
\[
(k+1)G\le(d+1)G,
\tag{30}
\]
with the analogue of (20) when \(G<W/2\).

(b) Exact-minorant intersection bottleneck:
\[
\phi_m(u)>h_Y(u).
\tag{31}
\]
Choose an exact affine minorant \(\ell_u\) satisfying (7), and define
\[
A_u:=A(u,\ell_u).
\tag{32}
\]
Then there is a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}
\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z\cap(A_u)_z)<t.
\tag{33}
\]
The cap \(A_u\) has all the exact-cap and contact properties (9)-(14). It is an arbitrary exact-minorant cap; (31)-(33) alone do not make it a cap in the legal circuit which generated \(B\).

4. What legal-circuit reparameterization excludes. Suppose, in addition, that
\[
(v_j^0,\theta_j^0,\ell_j^0)_{j=1}^r
\]
is a pre-existing legal support-minimal balanced-cap circuit at the same \(m\), with legal cap bodies
\[
A_j^0:=C\cap\{(z,x):\langle v_j^0,x\rangle\ge\ell_j^0(z)\},
\]
and let \(B_{\vartheta}\) be its reparameterized legal descendants from accepted fact \(\texttt{e3679dc542b23efe}\):
\[
(B_{\vartheta})_z=\sum_{j=1}^r\vartheta_j(A_j^0)_z
\qquad
(\vartheta_j>0,\ \sum_j\vartheta_j=1).
\tag{34}
\]
Assume \(0<t<\kappa_da\). For every \(j\), there is a neighborhood \(\mathcal U_j\) of the vertex \(e_j\), relative to the closed probability simplex, such that every strictly positive \(\vartheta\in\mathcal U_j\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl((B_{\vartheta})_z\cap(A_j^0)_z\bigr)>t
\tag{35}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\). Consequently, if \(B=B_{\vartheta}\) with \(\vartheta\in\mathcal U_j\) and alternative 3(b) occurs, then its bottleneck cap body \(A_u\) cannot equal \(A_j^0\). It may still be another cap of the circuit or an exact-minorant cap outside the circuit; no stronger absorption conclusion follows.

## Proof

Accepted fact \(\texttt{2e2778e4564d7745}\) gives the nonemptiness, compactness, and convexity of \(Y\) from the assumed positive surplus. The half-mass metronoid input used in accepted facts \(\texttt{ae0161dd5d4ea672}\) and \(\texttt{8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5}\) says that every point of \(D_z\) has absolute depth at least
\[
\gamma_d\frac{a_z}{2}=\kappa_da_z
\tag{36}
\]
inside \(C_z\).

We first prove (6). Fix \(i\) and let
\[
L_i:=\{z\in E:r_i(z)\le h_z(v_i)\}.
\]
For \(z\in L_i\), choose \(q_z\in D_z\) with
\(\langle v_i,q_z\rangle=h_z(v_i)\). Then \(q_z\in(H_i)_z\), so (36) gives
\[
\delta>\mu_C(H_i)
\ge\kappa_d\sum_{z\in L_i}a_z.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{z\in L_i}a_z<\frac{\delta}{\kappa_d}<a.
\tag{37}
\]
In particular, \(E\setminus L_i\ne\varnothing\). If
\(m\notin\operatorname{conv}(E\setminus L_i)\), strict separation would give a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) with
\(E\cap J\subseteq L_i\). This would contradict (1) and (37). Thus
\(m\in\operatorname{conv}(E\setminus L_i)\). Choose
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\) supported on \(E\setminus L_i\). On its support,
\(h_z(v_i)<r_i(z)\), and therefore
\[
\phi_m(v_i)
\le\sum_z\lambda_zh_z(v_i)
<\sum_z\lambda_zr_i(z)
=r_i(m)=c_i.
\]
This is (6).

For fixed nonzero \(v\), the minimization in (2) is a feasible finite linear program over a nonempty compact polytope. Its dual maximizes \(\ell(m)\) over globally affine functions \(\ell\) satisfying \(\ell(z)\le h_z(v)\) on \(E\). Strong finite-dimensional linear-programming duality gives an optimizer and proves (7).

Let \(\lambda\) be a vertex primal optimizer and \(T=\operatorname{supp}\lambda\). A vertex of \(\Lambda_m(E)\) has affinely independent positive support: otherwise a nonzero affine dependence supported on \(T\) would perturb \(\lambda\) in both signs while preserving feasibility. Thus \(|T|\le\rho+1\). Since \(\ell\) is affine and exact at \(m\),
\[
0
=\phi_m(v)-\ell(m)
=\sum_z\lambda_z\bigl(h_z(v)-\ell(z)\bigr).
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, so equality holds termwise on \(T\). This proves (10).

For every \(z\in E\), a support-maximizing point of \(D_z\) belongs to
\(A(v,\ell)_z\). Equation (36) proves the fiberwise lower bound in (9), and summation proves its mixed-mass form. If \(z\in T\), then
\[
A(v,\ell)_z=C_z\cap\{x:\langle v,x\rangle\ge h_z(v)\}.
\]
If this set had volume strictly larger than \(a_z/2\), its strict side would have the same volume because \(v\ne0\) and a hyperplane has zero \(d\)-volume. A constant-density selection of mass \(a_z/2\) from that strict side would have normalized barycenter in \(D_z\) and support value strictly larger than \(h_z(v)\), a contradiction. This proves the first inequality in (11).

We record the physical contact-mass bound. Let \(T\subseteq E\) be any nonempty affinely independent set. Apply accepted fact \(\texttt{6d6fcf377e68c79c}\) to the atomic base measure
\[
\sum_{z\in T}a_z\delta_z.
\]
It gives \(m_T\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}T\) for which every proper closed base halfspace through \(m_T\) contains at least
\(2^{-n}\sum_{z\in T}a_z\) of this measure. Because \(T\) is affinely independent, \(\Lambda_{m_T}(T)\) is a singleton. The full-mass metronoid \(\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)\) is the singleton consisting of the centroid of \(C_z\). Hence the full profile \(t_z=a_z\) on \(T\) has nonempty complete core at \(m_T\) and has the required \(2^{-n}\) balance. It is therefore admissible in the definition of \(G\) from accepted facts \(\texttt{ae0161dd5d4ea672}\) and \(\texttt{3d1f63abf09b2abb}\). Thus
\[
\sum_{z\in T}a_z\le G,
\]
which completes (11).

For (12), use \(\sum_z\lambda_z^ir_i(z)=r_i(m)=c_i\) and
\(\sum_z\lambda_z^ih_z(v_i)=\phi_m(v_i)\), then use (6). The rank-zero one-sided augmentation in accepted fact \(\texttt{3d1f63abf09b2abb}\), applied to the singleton affine subspace \(\{z\}\), has relative complete-core capacity \(a_z\) and gives exactly (13). If \(G<W/2\), the first entry in (13) is strictly larger than \(G\); hence the second entry is at most \(G\). This proves (14). The comparison between (3) and (9) proves the final assertions in part 1, including the distinction from the additional legal-circuit hypotheses in accepted fact \(\texttt{e3679dc542b23efe}\).

Suppose now that (15) holds. Helly's theorem and the exact Farkas alternative, in the form already used in accepted fact \(\texttt{2e2778e4564d7745}\), give a subfamily of size \(k\le d+1\) and nonnegative multipliers, not all zero, satisfying the last two equations in (16). Discard zero multipliers and normalize their sum to one. By (6),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i\phi_m(v_i)
<\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_ic_i<0.
\tag{38}
\]
Choose the stated vertex optimizers. The barycentric equations and
\(\sum_i\theta_iv_i=0\) give
\[
\sum_zU_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_i\theta_iv_i
\left(\sum_z\lambda_z^iz\right)^{\mathsf T}
=\left(\sum_i\theta_iv_i\right)m^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
Subadditivity and positive homogeneity of support functions give
\[
\sum_zh_z(U_z)
\le\sum_i\theta_i\sum_z\lambda_z^ih_z(v_i)
=\sum_i\theta_i\phi_m(v_i)<0.
\]
The field cannot vanish identically because \(h_z(0)=0\). Its support lies in the union of the \(T_i\)'s, so (19) follows from (11), and (20) follows from (14). This proves (18)-(20). The zero zeroth and first moments and strict negative support sum are the collapsed affine-stress shape of accepted fact \(\texttt{8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5}\), but no edge weights or matching capacities have been constructed here.

For the blocker degeneracy, (16) gives
\(R_{\theta}(m)=\sum_i\theta_ic_i<0\). Thus (21) is either a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\), or all of \(\mathbb R^n\). If \(z\in J_{\theta}\) and a point \(x\in C_z\) lay outside every \(H_i\), then
\[
\langle v_i,x\rangle
<c_i-\langle u_i,z-m\rangle
\quad(1\le i\le k).
\]
After multiplication by \(\theta_i\) and summation, this would give
\(0<R_{\theta}(z)\le0\), proving (22). Let
\(W_{J_{\theta}}:=\sum_{z\in E\cap J_{\theta}}a_z\). If
\(J_{\theta}\) is proper, (1) gives \(W_{J_{\theta}}\ge a\); if it is the whole base, \(W_{J_{\theta}}=W\ge a\). Therefore
\[
a\le W_{J_{\theta}}
\le\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_C(H_i)<k\delta,
\]
which proves the cardinality inequality in (24). Every point in the fibers over \(J_{\theta}\) belongs to at least one \(H_i\), so
\(\eta_{\theta}\ge\min_i\theta_i>0\). Integrating the weighted covering multiplicity gives
\[
\delta
>\sum_i\theta_i\mu_C(H_i)
\ge\eta_{\theta}W_{J_{\theta}}
\ge\eta_{\theta}a.
\]
This proves the remaining inequalities in (24) and completes part 2.

Assume (25). Equation (4) gives
\(Y\cap\bigcap_iK_i=\varnothing\). Apply Helly's theorem to the family consisting of \(Y\) and the \(K_i\)'s. Because every subfamily of the \(K_i\)'s has nonempty intersection, an empty Helly subfamily must include \(Y\); hence it contains at most \(d\) of the \(K_i\)'s. This gives the nonempty polyhedron \(Q_0\) disjoint from \(Y\).

Strong separation of the compact convex set \(Y\) from the closed convex polyhedron \(Q_0\) gives a nonzero \(w\) with
\[
h_{Q_0}(w)<\min_{y\in Y}\langle w,y\rangle.
\]
The linear program maximizing \(\langle w,x\rangle\) over
\(\langle v_i,x\rangle\le c_i\) has finite optimum \(h_{Q_0}(w)\). Strong linear-programming duality gives nonnegative \(\theta_i\) satisfying (26)-(27). By (6),
\[
\sum_i\theta_i\phi_m(v_i)
<\sum_i\theta_ic_i.
\tag{39}
\]
Since \(u=-w\),
\[
h_Y(u)=-\min_{y\in Y}\langle w,y\rangle,
\]
and (27) gives
\[
\sum_i\theta_ic_i+h_Y(u)<0.
\tag{40}
\]
If (28) holds, (39)-(40) imply (29). Choose vertex optimizers for every positive-coefficient direction and define the collapsed field exactly as in (17), now including the term \(\lambda_z^uu\). The proof of (18) applies verbatim. The contact-mass estimates give (30). This proves alternative 3(a).

Suppose instead that (31) holds, and choose \(\ell_u\) by (7). If (33) failed, then for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), the finite capacities
\[
q_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z\cap(A_u)_z)
\]
would satisfy \(\sum_{z\in E\cap J}q_z\ge t\). The finite-capacity selection principle proved in accepted fact \(\texttt{2e2778e4564d7745}\) would give numbers \(0\le s_z\le q_z\) such that
\[
\sum_zs_z=t,
\qquad
\sum_zs_zz=tm.
\]
Taking in each positive-capacity intersection a constant-density portion of mass \(s_z\) produces a measure
\(\nu\in\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\) supported on \(B\cap A_u\). Its normalized barycenter \(y=(m,\bar x)\) lies in \(Y\), while affinity of \(\ell_u\) gives
\[
\langle u,\bar x\rangle
\ge\frac1t\sum_zs_z\ell_u(z)
=\ell_u(m)=\phi_m(u)>h_Y(u),
\]
a contradiction. Hence (33) holds. Part 1 supplies all stated cap and contact properties. This proves part 3.

Finally, fix a legal circuit and \(j\) as in part 4. Accepted fact \(\texttt{e3679dc542b23efe}\) gives
\[
(B_{\vartheta})_z\longrightarrow(A_j^0)_z
\]
in Hausdorff distance for every \(z\in E\) as \(\vartheta\to e_j\) through strictly positive probability vectors. The cap \(A_j^0\) is an exact-minorant cap, so (9) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((A_j^0)_z)\ge\kappa_da_z>0.
\tag{41}
\]
For compact convex bodies, Hausdorff convergence to a full-dimensional convex body implies convergence of the volume of the intersection with that limiting body. Indeed, both the approximating body and its union with the limiting body lie in an arbitrarily small outer parallel body of the limit, and volume is continuous for convex bodies. Thus
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl((B_{\vartheta})_z\cap(A_j^0)_z\bigr)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{vol}_d((A_j^0)_z)
\quad(z\in E).
\tag{42}
\]
There are only finitely many traces \(E\cap J\) of proper closed base halfspaces through \(m\). For each such trace, (1), (41), and (42) give the limiting lower bound
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}\operatorname{vol}_d((A_j^0)_z)
\ge\kappa_d\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z
\ge\kappa_da>t.
\]
Uniformly over the finitely many traces, this proves (35) for all positive \(\vartheta\) sufficiently close to \(e_j\). If the bottleneck body in (33) equaled \(A_j^0\), equations (33) and (35) would contradict each other. This proves part 4 and the theorem.
