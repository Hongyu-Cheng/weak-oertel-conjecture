## Statement

Let $T$ be a tetrahedron of volume $V>0$ and let $y$ have barycentric coordinates $(a,b,b,b)$, where $a,b>0$ and $a+3b=1$. For finitely many unit normals $u_i$, suppose $Q_i=T\cap\{u_i\cdot(x-y)=0\}$ has Euclidean section centroid $y$. Let $S_i=\mathcal H^2(Q_i)$ and $p_i=|T\cap\{u_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}|/V$. If $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\theta_i=1$, and $\sum_i\theta_i S_i u_i=0$, then $\sum_i\theta_i p_i\ge27/64$. Equality holds exactly at $a=b=1/4$ when, after omitting zero weights and aggregating repetitions, the four oriented caps are $\{\xi_j\ge1/4\}$, each of weight $1/4$. In particular any quadrilateral cap with positive weight forces strict inequality.

## Proof

Let $g$ be the tetrahedral centroid and put $s_i=S_i u_i\cdot(g-y)/V$. The assumed balance implies $\sum_i\theta_i s_i=0$. Reversing a cap sends $(p_i,s_i)$ to $(1-p_i,-s_i)$. We seek a common scalar $c$ so that the corrected value $p_i-cs_i$ of one orientation of each plane lies in $[27/64,37/64]$.

A proper cut with a vertex strictly alone on one side, possibly with other vertices on the plane, is triangular. Orient it toward the isolated vertex $j$. The three intersections on the edges from $j$ must occur at parameters $3\lambda_l$ for $l\ne j$, because $\lambda=(a,b,b,b)$ is the section centroid and the section is a triangle. This is possible exactly when each $\lambda_l\le1/3$ for $l\ne j$. Its normalized cap volume and raw margin are $p_j=27\prod_{l\ne j}\lambda_l$ and $m_j=3-\sum_{l\ne j}\xi_l/\lambda_l$. The cap is a pyramid of height $3/\lVert\nabla m_j\rVert$ even when an edge parameter equals one. Therefore its normalized coarea is $p_j$ and $s_j=p_j[3-\tfrac14\sum_{l\ne j}1/\lambda_l]$.

For $a\le3/8$ set $c=2b/(6b-1)$, so $5/24\le b<1/3$. Substituting $a=1-3b$ in the triangular formulas gives $p_j-cs_j=Q_V(b)=27b^3/[2(6b-1)]$, for $j=0$ and also for every other $j$ when available. Its difference from $27/64$ is $27(2b+1)(4b-1)^2/[64(6b-1)]$. Its derivative is $81b^2(4b-1)/[2(6b-1)^2]$, so its maximum on $[5/24,1/3]$ is the larger of $125/256$ and $1/2$, the endpoint values. Thus all vertex-side corrections are in $[27/64,1/2]$, and lower equality requires $b=1/4$.

Every other proper cut has two strict vertices on each side and is a quadrilateral. Orient it so that vertex $0$ is positive, and relabel ordinary vertices so the other positive vertex is $1$. The two negative centroid coordinates are both $b<1/3$. By tetrahedral-section-within-pair-ordering the two negative raw heights agree. Rescale their common height to $-1$ and write the margin as $m=\xi_0/A+\xi_1/B-1$ with $0<A,B<1$. Parameterize the section by $\xi_0=A(1-t)$, $\xi_1=Bt$ for $0\le t\le1$. At fixed $t$ the coordinate $\xi_2$ ranges from zero to $1-A(1-t)-Bt$, and the coordinate chart $(t,\xi_2)$ has constant Euclidean area Jacobian. Integrating gives
$$
\lambda_0=\frac{A(3-2A-B)}{3(2-A-B)},\qquad
\lambda_1=\frac{B(3-A-2B)}{3(2-A-B)},\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac{1-\lambda_0-\lambda_1}{2}.
$$
The centroid condition $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$ becomes $A^2+2AB+3B^2-3A-6B+3=0$. If $B=1/2$ this gives $A=1/2$. Otherwise put $h=(A-1/2)/(B-1/2)$. Substitution and cancellation of $B-1/2$ give
$$
A=\frac{3(h+1)^2}{2(h^2+2h+3)},\qquad B=\frac{h^2+4h+7}{2(h^2+2h+3)}.
$$
The upper bounds $A,B<1$ give respectively $-3<h<1$ and $|h|>1$, hence $-3<h<-1$. Conversely these inequalities give $0<A,B<1$, since both numerators are positive there. With $r=-h-1$, all solutions are therefore
$$
A=\frac{3r^2}{2(r^2+2)},\qquad B=\frac{r^2-2r+4}{2(r^2+2)},\qquad 0<r<2,
$$
including the case $A=B=1/2$ at $r=1$. Substituting in the centroid expressions gives $a=r^2(4-r)/[4(r^2+2)]$ and $b=(r^3+8)/[12(r^2+2)]$. In particular $a<3/8$, equivalent to $2r^3-5r^2+6>0$. The latter polynomial has minimum $37/27$ on $[0,2]$ at $r=5/3$, by its derivative. This excludes quadrilaterals for $a\ge3/8$.

For the cap $m\ge0$, the marginal barycentric density in $(\xi_0,\xi_1)$ is $6(1-\xi_0-\xi_1)$ on their coordinate triangle, obtained by integrating the constant uniform barycentric density over the remaining coordinate. Its integral over the triangle with intercepts $A,B$ is $AB(3-A-B)$, by taking the area times the mean of the affine density at its three vertices. Thus $p=1-AB(3-A-B)$. For a small threshold increase from zero to $h_0$, the complementary triangle's intercepts become $(1+h_0)A,(1+h_0)B$. Differentiating its mass gives the normalized coarea $3AB(2-A-B)$ at zero. Since $m(g)=\tfrac14(1/A+1/B)-1$, the normalized moment is $s=\tfrac34(2-A-B)(A+B-4AB)$.

Inserting the formulas for $A,B,b$ gives the corrected value
$$
Q(r)=p-cs=\frac{(r+2)^3(r^4-4r^3+7r^2-6r+4)}{4(r^2+2)^2(r^3-2r^2+4)}.
$$
The last cubic factor in the denominator has minimum $76/27$ on $[0,2]$ at $r=4/3$. Multiplying the lower and upper gaps $Q-27/64$ and $37/64-Q$ by $64(r^2+2)^2(r^3-2r^2+4)$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L(r)&=-11r^7+86r^6-188r^5+44r^4+212r^3-88r^2+80,\\
U(r)&=21r^7-106r^6+228r^5-84r^4-172r^3+168r^2+80.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $t=r/2$ the coefficients of $L,U$ in the basis $\binom7j t^j(1-t)^{7-j}$ are as follows.

| $j$ | $L$ | $U$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | 80 | 80 |
| 1 | 80 | 80 |
| 2 | $1328/21$ | 112 |
| 3 | $2736/35$ | $4784/35$ |
| 4 | $6768/35$ | $2672/35$ |
| 5 | $4432/21$ | $1136/7$ |
| 6 | $1168/7$ | $2192/7$ |
| 7 | 208 | 1232 |

The entries follow by replacing $r^k$ by $2^kt^k$, whose coefficient in position $j\ge k$ is $2^k\binom jk/\binom 7k$ by the binomial theorem. The basis is nonnegative and sums to one on $[0,1]$. Both gaps are strictly positive, proving $27/64<Q(r)<37/64$ for every quadrilateral in either orientation.

For $a\le3/8$ the triangular and quadrilateral estimates imply the result upon taking the balanced average, since the moment term cancels. The two types exhaust proper cuts: the interior point ensures both signs among the vertex heights, and unless there are two strict vertices on each side one side has just one strictly included vertex. For $a>3/8$ there are no quadrilaterals and $a>1/3$ makes the cap at vertex $0$ the only available triangular section up to orientation. Vector balance then makes the two total orientation weights equal, giving mean $1/2$.

Equality requires all positively weighted caps to be vertex-side, because the quadrilateral corrections are strict and the complementary triangular ones are at least $1/2$. The triangular formula requires $b=1/4$ and hence $a=1/4$. The margins are then $4\xi_j-1$, the fractions are $27/64$, and the section vectors divided by $V$ are a common positive multiple of $\nabla\xi_j$ by the pyramid formula. Their only linear relation is their sum zero. The aggregated weights must therefore all be $1/4$, and conversely those caps and weights give equality.
