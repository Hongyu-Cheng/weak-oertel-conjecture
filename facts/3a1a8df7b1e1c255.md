## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) be positive integers, and let \(L\ge0\) be real. Put \(D:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:\sum_iw_iz_i\le L\}\), \(N:=\#D\), and \(p(\mathbf j):=1/N\) for \(\mathbf j\in D\). For every \(\mathbf j\in D\), define \(k_{\mathbf j}:=\#(D\cap(2\mathbf j-D))\) and \(H_{\mathbf j}:=k_{\mathbf j}/N\). Then
\[
\mathbb E_p H_{\mathbf j}^2
=\frac1{N^3}\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}k_{\mathbf j}^2
\ge \frac9{32}e^{-66\sqrt n}\left(\frac8{27}\right)^n
\ge c\,g^n4^{-n},
\qquad
c:=\frac9{32}e^{-17424},\quad g:=\frac{10}{9}>1.
\]
No coordinate is required to be active in \(D\), and the dimension in both bounds is the original \(n\).

## Proof

The set \(D\) is finite and contains \(\mathbf0\), so \(N\ge1\). We first prove the bound when \(L>0\).

For \(0<r<1\), the elementary generating function is \(F(r):=\sum_{k\ge0}(2k+1)^2r^k=(1+6r+r^2)/(1-r)^3\). For \(s>0\), let \(Q_s\) be the probability law on the nonnegative integers given by \(Q_s(k):=(2k+1)^2e^{-3sk}/F(e^{-3s})\). It satisfies the uniform moment estimate
\[
s^2\mathbb E_{Q_s}[k(k+1)]\le\frac{32}{3}.
\tag{1}
\]
To verify this, write \(r=e^{-3s}\). For every nonnegative integer \(k\),
\[
\binom{k+2}{2}\le(2k+1)^2\le8\binom{k+2}{2}.
\]
The first comparison gives \(F(r)\ge(1-r)^{-3}\). Differentiating the series \(\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{k+2}{2}r^k=(1-r)^{-3}\) gives
\[
(1-r)^3\sum_{k\ge0}k(k+1)\binom{k+2}{2}r^k
=\frac{6r(1+r)}{(1-r)^2}
\le\frac{12r}{(1-r)^2}.
\]
The upper coefficient comparison therefore implies \(\mathbb E_{Q_s}[k(k+1)]\le96r/(1-r)^2\). Since \(r/(1-r)^2=1/[4\sinh^2(3s/2)]\) and \(\sinh x\ge x\) for \(x\ge0\), this proves (1).

For \(t>0\), take independent coordinates \(J_i\) with laws \(Q_{tw_i}\), and let \(\mu(t):=\mathbb E[\sum_iw_iJ_i]\). This is a finite continuous function of \(t>0\), and it tends to zero as \(t\to\infty\), directly from the displayed rational generating function. Consequently \(\mu(t)+15\sqrt n/t\) is continuous, tends to infinity as \(t\downarrow0\), and tends to zero as \(t\to\infty\). Fix \(t>0\) satisfying
\[
\mu(t)+\frac{15\sqrt n}{t}=L.
\tag{2}
\]
Write \(Q\) for this product law and \(\mu:=\mu(t)\). For every \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), put \(\sigma(\mathbf j)^2:=\sum_iw_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3\). Independence and (1) yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Q\!\left(t\sum_iw_iJ_i\right)
\le\sum_i(tw_i)^2\mathbb E_Q[J_i(J_i+1)]
\le\frac{32n}{3},
\qquad
\mathbb E_Q[t^2\sigma(\mathbf J)^2]\le\frac{32n}{9}.
\tag{3}
\]
Define the good-center set
\[
G:=\left\{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:
\left|t\left(\sum_iw_ij_i-\mu\right)\right|\le7\sqrt n,
\quad t\sigma(\mathbf j)\le4\sqrt n\right\}.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality for the first condition, Markov's inequality for the square of the second, and (3) imply
\[
Q(G)\ge1-\frac{32}{147}-\frac29>\frac12.
\tag{4}
\]
For each \(\mathbf j\in G\), (2) gives both
\[
\sum_iw_ij_i+2\sigma(\mathbf j)
\le\mu+\frac{7\sqrt n+8\sqrt n}{t}=L,
\qquad
t\left(\sum_iw_ij_i-L\right)\ge-22\sqrt n.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \(G\subseteq D\).

Fix \(\mathbf j\in G\), and let \(V_i\) be independent and uniform on the integer interval \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Their weighted sum has mean zero and variance \(\sigma(\mathbf j)^2\). If that variance is positive, Chebyshev's inequality shows that \(\mathbb P\{|\sum_iw_iV_i|\le2\sigma(\mathbf j)\}\ge3/4\). If the variance is zero, the same event has probability one. On this event, both vectors \(\mathbf j+\mathbf V\) and \(\mathbf j-\mathbf V\) have nonnegative integer coordinates, and their weighted sums are at most \(L\) by (5). They therefore both belong to \(D\). The displacement \(\mathbf V\) is uniform on a box of cardinality \(\prod_i(2j_i+1)\), and its two endpoints are reflected about \(\mathbf j\). Thus the actual oriented reflection count satisfies
\[
k_{\mathbf j}\ge\frac34\prod_i(2j_i+1)
\qquad(\mathbf j\in G).
\tag{6}
\]

Put \(q_i:=e^{-tw_i}\in(0,1)\). The uniform support cardinality obeys the elementary partition-function bound
\[
N=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}e^{t\sum_iw_iz_i}\prod_iq_i^{z_i}
\le e^{tL}\prod_i(1-q_i)^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]
For this same choice of \(t\), the product law satisfies \(Q(\mathbf j)=\prod_i[(2j_i+1)^2q_i^{3j_i}/F(q_i^3)]\). Combining (6), (7), and then the second bound in (5) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{N^3}\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}k_{\mathbf j}^2
&\ge\frac9{16}e^{-3tL}\prod_i(1-q_i)^3
\sum_{\mathbf j\in G}\prod_i(2j_i+1)^2\\
&=\frac9{16}\prod_i\bigl[(1-q_i)^3F(q_i^3)\bigr]
\sum_{\mathbf j\in G}Q(\mathbf j)e^{3t(\sum_iw_ij_i-L)}\\
&\ge\frac9{32}e^{-66\sqrt n}
\prod_i\bigl[(1-q_i)^3F(q_i^3)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The last step uses (4).

For every \(0<q<1\), one has
\[
(1-q)^3F(q^3)=\frac{1+6q^3+q^6}{(1+q+q^2)^3}\ge\frac8{27}.
\tag{9}
\]
For a direct verification, the numerator of the difference after multiplication by \(27(1+q+q^2)^3\) is
\[
27(1+6q^3+q^6)-8(1+q+q^2)^3
=(1-q)^2\bigl[19(1-q^2)^2+q(14-q+14q^2)\bigr]\ge0.
\]
Applying (9) to every factor in (8) proves the first asserted bound for \(L>0\). The proof included all \(n\) coordinates throughout, without an activity assumption.

If \(L=0\), positivity of all weights gives \(D=\{\mathbf0\}\) and \(H_{\mathbf0}=1\). The first bound also holds because \((9/32)e^{-66\sqrt n}(8/27)^n<1\).

Finally, \(\log(16/15)=\int_1^{16/15}u^{-1}\,du\ge1/16\), so
\[
n\log(16/15)-66\sqrt n
\ge\frac n{16}-66\sqrt n
=\left(\frac{\sqrt n}{4}-132\right)^2-17424
\ge-17424.
\]
Since \((8/27)^n=(16/15)^n(10/9)^n4^{-n}\), the first bound implies the second with the stated absolute constants \(c\) and \(g\).
