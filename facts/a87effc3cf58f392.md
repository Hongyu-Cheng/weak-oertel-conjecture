## Statement

An exact two-hub table will mean the following finite data. A finite atom set X is partitioned into cells of size one or two, with two distinguished, distinct atoms c_0,c_1 in distinct cells. Every two-atom cell is assigned one label a in {0,1}; there are m_a >= 1 cells of label a. A cell of label a does not contain c_a. Any cell containing c_a and another atom has label 1-a. Every atom other than c_0,c_1 is in a two-atom cell. Thus the singleton cells, if any, are precisely those distinguished atoms which are not in two-atom cells. Require that the number q_ov of distinguished atoms occurring in two-atom cells belongs to {1,2}. Put K = m_0+m_1+2-q_ov, which is exactly the number of cells. Let R_a be c_a together with all endpoints of the cells of label a, so h_a := |R_a| = 2m_a+1 and |R_0 intersect R_1| = q_ov.

Give every atom x a positive weight w_x, retain its original normalizer Z = sum_x w_x and original law p_x = w_x/Z, and require p_u p_v = p_{c_a}^2 for every cell {u,v} of label a. All scalar formulas below use the exact normalized hub and endpoint weights
\(a_a=p_{c_a}\) and \(S_a=\sum_{x\in R_a\setminus\lbrace c_a\rbrace}p_x\). This is the normalization map from unnormalized hub and endpoint weights: both are divided by the same original Z. No conditional core normalizer is used. Write each pair of label a as \(a_ae^{u_{a,r}},a_ae^{-u_{a,r}}\), with r from 1 to m_a, and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_a&=\frac{1+\sum_{r=1}^{m_a}\cosh u_{a,r}}{m_a+1}
       =\frac{S_a/a_a+2}{2(m_a+1)},
&t_a&=\log(2\mu_a),\\
V_{c_a}&=\frac{2}{h_a}\sum_{r=1}^{m_a}u_{a,r}^2,
&\Delta_a&=4m_a(m_a+1)K^2a_a^3,\\
A_0&=K^2\sum_{x\in X}p_x^3,
&A_{\mathcal S}&=A_0+\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}\Delta_a
\quad(\mathcal S\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace),\\
\widehat\nu_{\mathcal S}
&=\frac{K^2}{A_{\mathcal S}}
  \sum_{a\in\mathcal S}h_a^2a_a^3V_{c_a}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
These are the full oriented row conventions of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705: a nonzero pair is counted twice and the center once. The total pair count m_a used here equals the capacity M_a minus one in accepted fact 9e1805a461c7be2c. The overlap q_ov is the quantity called t in that predecessor, and is unrelated to the auxiliary center probabilities q_j of 445a9e668ee1a705.

Assume \(A_0<1\) and \(\max(\mu_0,\mu_1)\le2\min(\mu_0,\mu_1)\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=-\log A_0,
&g&=\log(A_0+\Delta_0+\Delta_1),\\
r_a&=\frac12\left[
 \log\frac{A_0+\Delta_a}{A_0}
 +\log\frac{A_0+\Delta_0+\Delta_1}{A_0+\Delta_{1-a}}
 \right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
All logarithms are natural. The absolute constants \(B=2^{80}\) and \(C=2B+4\) have the following properties. First, \(g>0\) and
$$
Bg\ge2\max(\mu_0,\mu_1)d,
\qquad
d\le\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{B}{B+2\mu_a}r_a.
\tag{3}
$$
Second, there are nonnegative \(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\delta_0,\delta_1\) such that, simultaneously for all four subsets \(\mathcal S\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log A_{\mathcal S}
&\ge\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}\gamma_a-
       \sum_{a\notin\mathcal S}\delta_a,\\
\delta_a&\le Ce^{-t_a}\gamma_a,
&\widehat\nu_{\mathcal S}
&\le C\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}t_a^2\gamma_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
There is no bound on either pair count or either mean. These assertions include both exact forced-spine scalar domains, with their exact Jensen-minimized cubic sums, as specified and verified in the proof.

For the geometric product consequence, let finitely many supports \(U_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), \(n_i\ge1\), have exactly two nontrivial integral midpoint centers and be closed under integral midpoints of pairs of their atoms. Suppose each U_i is the union of its two full reflected rows \(R_{i,c_0},R_{i,c_1}\), every parity class contains at most two atoms, the centers have different parity, and their overlap has cardinality one or two. Give U_i arbitrary positive weights satisfying exact midpoint equality, with the original law \(p_{i,x}=w_{i,x}/Z_i\), \(Z_i=\sum_{x\in U_i}w_{i,x}\). Require the mean comparison in (3) for every active factor, meaning every i with \(K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3<1\); inactive factors need no mean restriction. Here K_i is its number of occupied parity classes. Set \(P=\bigotimes_i p_i\), and define actual ambient-halfspace depth and its moments by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\text{ is a closed halfspace in }
 \mathbb R^{\sum_i n_i}\text{ containing }J\rbrace,\\
M_2(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^2,
&M_3(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Then, with the single absolute coefficient \(c=\tfrac14\exp(-48C^2)>0\),
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2}\ge c4^{-\sum_i n_i},
\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}\ge c^{3/2}8^{-\sum_i n_i}.
\tag{6}
$$
These statements include lower-dimensional supports and zero score variances. In particular they apply when each original complete convex support A_i equals its two-core union. For a larger complete support A, its union U = R_0 union R_1 need not itself be complete; the algebraic and reflection arguments require only the stated midpoint closure of the support being used. Applying (6) to a law whose support is U gives no transfer to an original law on a strictly larger A. No atoms of any law in (1) or (5) are deleted or renormalized during the proof.

## Proof

The two-atom cells have disjoint endpoint sets. Their labels partition those endpoints, and the only remaining atoms are the singleton hubs. When q_ov = 1, relabel the hubs if necessary so that c_1 is in a cell of label zero and c_0 is a singleton. Then \(1=a_0+S_0+S_1\) and \(K=m_0+m_1+1\). When q_ov = 2, both hubs are endpoints, so \(1=S_0+S_1\) and \(K=m_0+m_1\). In both cases there are \(2K+q_{\mathrm{ov}}-2\le2K\) atoms. Holder's inequality for the original law therefore gives \(A_0\ge1/4\), and consequently \(0<d\le\log4<2\). Also \(\mu_a\ge1\) and
$$
S_a=2a_a((m_a+1)\mu_a-1)\ge2m_aa_a\mu_a.
\tag{7}
$$

We first establish the positive full gap at occupied K, including a quantitative estimate for bounded means. This is the occupied-class version of the charge mechanism of accepted fact 530e875c56330666. Its ambient assumption K = 2^n is not substituted into; the needed charge is proved here with exactly K occupied cells.

For each atom let d_x be m_a if x = c_a and zero otherwise, and put \(z_x=Kp_x\), \(L_x=(2d_x+1)z_x\). These local quantities are a scaled atom weight and a scaled full row sum. For a cell eta put \(R_\eta=\sum_{x\in\eta}z_x\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\sum_xz_x(L_x-1)^2,
&G&=\sum_{\eta\text{ a cell}}(R_\eta-1)^2,\\
\mathcal B&=\sum_xd_xz_x^2,
&\beta&=K\sum_{a=0}^1m_aa_a^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
There are exactly K cells, \(\sum_xz_x=\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), and \(\mathcal B=K\beta\). The pair products give
\(\sum_\eta R_\eta^2=\sum_xz_x^2+2\mathcal B=\sum_xz_xL_x\). Expanding F and G, and then using (1), proves the exact identity
$$
F+2G=\sum_xz_xL_x^2-K
      =K(A_0+\Delta_0+\Delta_1-1).
\tag{9}
$$
Indeed the full row at c_a has h_a identical geometric contributions a_a, whereas a nonhub row is a singleton; equivalently \(h_a^2-1=4m_a(m_a+1)\) in the final equality.

Call a hub good if \(7/8\le L_x\le9/8\), and bad otherwise. Let \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm g}\) and \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm b}\) be their respective contributions to \(\mathcal B\), and let \(M_{\mathrm g}\) be the sum of z_x over good hubs. A good hub has \(z_x\le3/8\), because \(2d_x+1\ge3\), and
\(d_xz_x^2=z_x(L_x-z_x)/2\le9z_x/16\). Hence \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm g}\le9M_{\mathrm g}/16\). For a bad hub use \(d_xz_x^2\le z_xL_x/2\). The quotient \(L/[2(L-1)^2]\) is at most 28 for \(0\le L<7/8\), and at most 36 for \(L>9/8\). For the latter bound write L = 1+v, v > 1/8, and use \(L/[2(L-1)^2]=1/(2v^2)+1/(2v)\); for the former the quotient is increasing on [0,7/8]. Thus \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm b}\le36F\).

In a cell with \(R_\eta<7/8\), its good-hub mass is at most \(R_\eta\le56(R_\eta-1)^2\). These cells contribute at most 56G to \(M_{\mathrm g}\). If a good hub is in a cell with \(R_\eta\ge7/8\), that cell cannot be a singleton. Write its two scaled endpoint masses as \(\upsilon\le3/8\) for the good hub and \(\omega\ge1/2\) for the other endpoint. There cannot be two good hubs in the cell, because their sum would be at most 3/4. Let \(\xi\) be the scaled mass of this cell's assigned hub. Then \(\upsilon\omega=\xi^2\). If the assigned hub is good, \(\xi\le3/8\), and
$$
\omega=\frac{R_\eta+\sqrt{R_\eta^2-4\xi^2}}2
\ge\frac{7+\sqrt{13}}{16}>\frac58,
\qquad \upsilon\le\frac85\xi^2.
\tag{10}
$$
If it is bad, \(\omega\ge1/2\) gives \(\upsilon\le2\xi^2\). Distinct cells are distinct pairs; summing their assigned hub squares with the appropriate pair multiplicities proves
\(M_{\mathrm g}\le\tfrac85\mathcal B_{\mathrm g}+2\mathcal B_{\mathrm b}+56G\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\mathrm g}
&\le\frac9{10}\mathcal B_{\mathrm g}
 +\frac98\mathcal B_{\mathrm b}+\frac{63}2G,\\
\mathcal B
&\le\frac{49}4\mathcal B_{\mathrm b}+315G
 \le441F+315G\le441(F+2G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
Equations (8)-(9) imply \(A_0+\Delta_0+\Delta_1-1\ge\beta/441>0\), proving g > 0.

If both means are at most some T >= 1, then (7) gives \(S_a\le4Tm_aa_a\). In the singleton case \(a_0\le\sum_am_aa_a\), so both normalizations above imply \(1\le5T\sum_am_aa_a\). Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(K\ge m_0+m_1\), now give
$$
\beta=K\sum_am_aa_a^2
\ge\frac{K}{m_0+m_1}\left(\sum_am_aa_a\right)^2
\ge\frac1{25T^2}.
\tag{12}
$$
By (11) and \(\log(1+v)\ge v/2\) for \(0\le v\le1\), it follows that \(g\ge1/(22050T^2)\). This evaluates the logarithm at the lower bound \(1/(11025T^2)\), and does not require the actual full gap to be at most one.

We next prove the count and gain estimates for unbounded minimum mean. Put \(M=\min(\mu_0,\mu_1)\), and suppose M >= 2. For any pair of label a with total normalized mass r, its endpoint cubic sum equals \(r^3-3a_a^2r\). Jensen for r cubed and (7) therefore imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in R_a\setminus\lbrace c_a\rbrace}p_x^3
&\ge\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}-3a_a^2S_a\\
&\ge\left(1-\frac3{4\mu_a^2}\right)\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}
\ge\frac{13}{16}\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
The two endpoint sets are disjoint. Activity and (13) yield
\(\sum_aS_a^3/m_a^2<16/(13K^2)\).

For a cell eta containing a pair of label a, put \(b_\eta=Ka_a\); put \(b_\eta=0\) in a singleton cell. With the scaled masses in (8), its atomic cubic sum is \(R_\eta^3-3b_\eta^2R_\eta\). Since \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), summing these identities gives
$$
1-A_0=\frac1K\sum_\eta
\left[3b_\eta^2R_\eta-(R_\eta-1)^2(R_\eta+2)\right].
\tag{14}
$$
Let \(b=K\max(a_0,a_1)\) and choose a hub of this maximum weight. Its own cell has scaled total mass at most 2b. Indeed, if it is a singleton this is immediate. Otherwise its cell has label the other hub, so its partner's scaled weight equals the square of that other hub's scaled weight divided by b, and is at most b.

If b < 1/4, this cell has \(R_\eta<1/2\), whence \((R_\eta-1)^2(R_\eta+2)\ge5/8\). To check the last inequality, the polynomial is \(R^3-3R+2\), decreasing for \(0\le R\le1/2\), with value 5/8 at 1/2. All cell defects in (14) are nonnegative. Since its left side is positive, it follows that \(\sum_\eta b_\eta^2R_\eta>5/24\). But (7), followed by (13) and activity, gives
$$
\sum_\eta b_\eta^2R_\eta
=K^3\sum_aa_a^2S_a
\le\frac{K^3}{4M^2}\sum_a\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}
<\frac{4K}{13M^2}.
\tag{15}
$$
Thus \(K>65M^2/96\) in this case.

If b >= 1/4, let a be its hub index, so \(a_a\ge1/(4K)\). Equation (7) gives \(S_a\ge m_aM/(2K)\). A pair of total mass r has endpoint cubic sum at least \(r^3/4\), by convexity of the cube, so Jensen over its m_a pairs and activity give
$$
\sum_xp_x^3\ge\frac{S_a^3}{4m_a^2}
\ge\frac{m_aM^3}{32K^3},
\qquad K>\frac{m_aM^3}{32}\ge\frac{M^2}{16}.
\tag{16}
$$
The last inequality uses m_a >= 1 and M >= 2. Combining both cases proves \(K>M^2/16\), with no upper bound on the pair counts.

For later use, (13) also gives a sharper deficit bound before replacing its coefficient by 13/16. In the no-singleton case, Holder with capacities m_0,m_1 gives \(K^2\sum_aS_a^3/m_a^2\ge1\). In the singleton case, include its mass a_0 with capacity one: \(K^2(a_0^3+\sum_aS_a^3/m_a^2)\ge1\). The sum of the capacities is exactly K in each case. The singleton cubic contribution is also at least \((1-3/(4M^2))a_0^3\). Hence
$$
A_0\ge1-\frac3{4M^2},
\qquad
\delta:=1-A_0\le\frac3{4M^2},
\qquad
d\le\frac{12}{13M^2}<\frac1{M^2}.
\tag{17}
$$
For the last estimate use \(-\log(1-v)\le v/(1-v)\), with \(v=3/(4M^2)\le3/16\).

Now use comparability, \(M\le\mu_a\le2M\). From (7), \(S_a\le8Mm_aa_a\). The two possible normalizations imply \(1\le9M\sum_am_aa_a\). Put \(s=\sum_am_aa_a\), so \(s\ge1/(9M)\). Holder's inequality yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta:=\Delta_0+\Delta_1
&\ge4K^2\sum_a\frac{(m_aa_a)^3}{m_a}\\
&\ge\frac{4K^2s^3}{(\sqrt{m_0}+\sqrt{m_1})^2}
\ge2Ks^3>\frac1{5832M}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Here \((\sqrt{m_0}+\sqrt{m_1})^2\le2(m_0+m_1)\le2K\), and the final inequality uses the count bound (16), including the alternative (15).

When \(M\ge2^{15}>17496\), (17)-(18) give \(\delta\le\Delta/4\). Therefore
$$
A_0+\Delta\ge1+\frac34\Delta
>1+\frac1{7776M},
\qquad
g\ge\frac1{15552M}.
\tag{19}
$$
The logarithm bound again evaluates \(\log(1+v)\ge v/2\) at \(v=1/(7776M)\le1\). Since \(2\max\mu_a\,d\le4/M\), (19) proves the first inequality in (3) for any B >= 62208 in this range.

If \(1\le M<2^{15}\), comparability gives \(\mu_a\le T:=2^{16}\). Equation (12) gives \(g\ge1/(22050T^2)\). Thus every \(B\ge88200T^3\) satisfies \(Bg\ge4T>2Td\ge2\max\mu_a\,d\). The choice \(B=2^{80}\) meets this bound, because \(88200T^3<2^{65}\). Together with (19), this proves the first inequality in (3) on the entire comparable branch. In particular the proof has not imposed a fixed upper mean cutoff.

For the Shapley increments in (2), direct cancellation gives \(r_0+r_1=g+d\). Both increments are positive. Since \(B/(B+2\mu_a)\ge B/(B+2\max\mu_a)\), the first inequality in (3) implies
\(\sum_a[B/(B+2\mu_a)]r_a\ge[B/(B+2\max\mu_a)](g+d)\ge d\). This proves the exact logarithmic weighted-Shapley assertion.

We verify explicitly that both forced-spine scalar domains are included. In the one-overlap case, after the relabeling above, the forced cell of label zero has weights \(a_1,a_0^2/a_1\). Write \(L_0=a_1+a_0^2/a_1\). The domain is \(a_0+S_0+S_1=1\), \(S_1\ge2m_1a_1\), and \(S_0-L_0\ge2(m_0-1)a_0\), with equality to zero for the residual when m_0 = 1. In the two-overlap case both forced cells occur; write \(L_a=a_{1-a}+a_a^2/a_{1-a}\). The domain is \(S_0+S_1=1\) and \(S_a-L_a\ge2(m_a-1)a_a\), again with residual zero when m_a = 1. All hub weights are positive, all counts are positive integers, and these are the exact domains, with no additional bound on a ratio.

For k >= 1 and T' >= 2ka, define \(\Psi_k(T';a)=T'^3/k^2-3a^2T'\), and define the empty value \(\Psi_0(0;a)=0\). A collection of k pairs of product a squared and total mass T' has cubic sum at least this value, because each pair of total r has cubic sum \(r^3-3a^2r\) and Jensen applies to the sum of r cubed. Equality is achieved by setting all k pair totals equal to T'/k; each pair's positive weights are the roots of \(z^2-(T'/k)z+a^2=0\). Consequently the exact minimized cubic sum on the first domain is
$$
a_0^3+a_1^3+a_0^6/a_1^3+
\Psi_{m_0-1}(S_0-L_0;a_0)+\Psi_{m_1}(S_1;a_1),
\tag{20}
$$
and on the second it is
\(\sum_{a=0}^1[a_{1-a}^3+a_a^6/a_{1-a}^3+\Psi_{m_a-1}(S_a-L_a;a_a)]\).
These are exact two-hub tables as defined in the statement, so the proof of (3) applies to their scaled cubic sum A_0 directly. It also applies directly to every heterogeneous choice of the nonforced pairs. For an actual complete globally affine realization of the minimized table, accepted fact 4bb26a33ac78ee45 supplies it with additional-axis counts \((m_0-1,m_1)\) for q_ov = 1 and \((m_0-1,m_1-1)\) for q_ov = 2, center ratio \(\tau=a_1/a_0\), and each additional pair ratio chosen to give its prescribed total. Multiplying that construction's weights by a_0 gives exactly these normalized weights and Z = 1. This realization is not needed to treat an incomplete two-core union as complete.

We next prove (4), including its price estimate. Put \(f_a=B/(B+2\mu_a)\). By (3), \(d\le f_0r_0+f_1r_1\), so choose \(\delta_0=\min(d,f_0r_0)\), \(\delta_1=d-\delta_0\), and \(\gamma_a=r_a-\delta_a\). Then \(0\le\delta_a\le f_ar_a<r_a\), and
$$
\delta_a\le\frac{B}{2\mu_a}\gamma_a=Be^{-t_a}\gamma_a,
\qquad
\gamma_a\ge\frac2{B+2}r_a.
\tag{21}
$$
For the latter bound use \(1-f_a=2\mu_a/(B+2\mu_a)\ge2/(B+2)\), since \(\mu_a\ge1\). The empty-set energy is \(-d=-\delta_0-\delta_1\), and the full-set energy is \(g=\gamma_0+\gamma_1\). Diminishing increments of the logarithm give \(r_a\le\log((A_0+\Delta_a)/A_0)\), so the singleton-set lower bound is
\(\gamma_a-\delta_{1-a}=r_a-d\le\log(A_0+\Delta_a)\). These are all four energy inequalities.

The increasing concavity of \(v\mapsto\operatorname{arcosh}(v)^2\) on [1,infinity), proved in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, applies to the uniform average of 1 and all \(\cosh u_{a,r}\). Since \(\operatorname{arcosh}(\mu_a)\le t_a\) and \(2(m_a+1)/h_a\le4/3\), it gives \(V_{c_a}\le(4/3)t_a^2\). Also \(h_a^2/(h_a^2-1)\le9/8\), because h_a >= 3. Substituting in (1) proves
\(\widehat\nu_{\mathcal S}\le[3/(2A_{\mathcal S})]\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}\Delta_at_a^2\).
For a selected a, \(A_{\mathcal S}\ge A_0+\Delta_a\), and hence
$$
\frac{\Delta_a}{A_{\mathcal S}}
\le\frac{\Delta_a/A_0}{1+\Delta_a/A_0}
\le\log(1+\Delta_a/A_0)\le2r_a.
\tag{22}
$$
Equations (21)-(22) imply
\(\widehat\nu_{\mathcal S}\le3\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}t_a^2r_a\le\tfrac32(B+2)\sum_{a\in\mathcal S}t_a^2\gamma_a\). The constant C = 2B+4 dominates this coefficient and B, proving (4).

For the geometric assertion, the parity classes are the cells of the table. Distinct same-parity endpoints have an integral midpoint, which is one of the two hubs and belongs to U_i. No cell has three points by hypothesis. A cell containing a hub cannot be centered at that same hub, and the different hub parities put them in distinct cells. Because U_i is the union of its reflected rows, all nonhubs are endpoints, so the singleton cells are exactly the hubs not used as endpoints. This verifies every table hypothesis. These properties also follow for two-core unions of complete two-center supports from accepted fact 9e1805a461c7be2c. The possible incompleteness of that union introduces no new midpoint: a pair in the union already has midpoint one of the two hubs in the union. In each selected full row, exact midpoint equality makes the auxiliary geometric law uniform; every other row is a singleton. Therefore its selected energy and price are exactly (1), using its original Z_i.

We give the product composition, rather than assuming a loss once per factor. This is the bin-selection and reflected-source mechanism of accepted fact 1b91f86ed4c91107, here verified with independent choices of the two rows inside a factor. Select singleton rows throughout each inactive factor. For active factors use (4), with the same C. For each integer m >= 0, group all active hub labels (i,a) with \(m\le t_{i,a}<m+1\), and let G_m be the sum of their \(\gamma_{i,a}\). Retain every label of the bin precisely when \(G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2\), and let \(\mathcal S_i\) be the resulting selected set of hubs in factor i. Only finitely many bins occur, because there are finitely many factors and labels. A retained bin has \(W_m:=C(m+1)^2G_m\) and \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\). Its labels contribute at most W_m to the total selected price, by (4). A discarded bin loses at most
\(\sum_{(i,a)\text{ in bin}}\delta_{i,a}\le Ce^{-m}G_m<4C^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2\).
Since \(e^{-1}<1/2\) and \(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\) for \(0\le r<1\), all discarded deficits sum to at most \(D=48C^2\). Summing the four-state bounds, and including the nonnegative logarithmic energy of inactive factors, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g:=\sum_i\log A_{i,\mathcal S_i}
&\ge\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-D\\
&\ge2\sum_{m\text{ retained}}\sqrt{W_m}-D
\ge2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_{i,\mathcal S_i}}-D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
For an inactive factor its selected price is zero. The square-root inequality includes the case of no retained bins.

Finally we verify that (23) controls actual depth of the entire original product law. For each selected row \(\widehat R_{i,j}\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat b_i&=\sum_jp_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=
\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_i(j)},
&\widehat q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2}{\widehat b_i},\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat V_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
These are the canonical quantities of 445a9e668ee1a705 applied to the chosen symmetric rows. In particular \(A_{i,\mathcal S_i}=K_i^2\widehat b_i\) and \(\widehat\nu_{i,\mathcal S_i}=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j}\). Reflection preserves the row law and reverses its score, so every row score has mean zero. For a product center J, its selected row is the product of those rows; put \(\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i)\), \(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}\), \(Y_J=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}\), and \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}\). Direct substitution and independence give
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,
\qquad \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,
\qquad P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\text{ in the selected row}).
\tag{25}
$$
The probability in (25) is exactly \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,x_i}/\prod_iZ_i\).

Any ambient closed halfspace containing J contains at least one endpoint of each distinct reflected pair X,2J-X, and contains J itself. Summing over the disjoint reflection orbits, with both nonzero orientations and the central atom counted once, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\text{ in the selected row}}
       \min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
    \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
The last step is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. The factor one half uses at most the available central mass, so it also covers singleton rows. With the product auxiliary center law \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), the identity
\(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b_i)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}\)
and \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_{i,\mathcal S_i}\) now imply
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_{i,\mathcal S_i}}\right).
\tag{27}
$$
This uses Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz once more after squaring (26) and averaging under the original P. Combining (23) and (27) proves the first bound in (6). Since \(K_i\le2^{n_i}\), its ambient-dimension version follows. Jensen under the same original P gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the remaining bounds. This proof used no completeness, full rank, changed field, or changed normalizer beyond the explicit hypotheses.
