## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $d\ge3$, and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty with $\operatorname{aff}(P)=\mathbb R^n$. For each $p\in P$ let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body with nonempty interior. For each $q\in P$, let $E_q$ be the polytope of nonnegative coefficient vectors $(\lambda_p)_{p\ne q}$ with $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p=1$ and $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p p=q$. The family is strictly compatible if for every $q\in P$ and every extreme point $\lambda$ of $E_q$, the Minkowski sum $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p K_p$ is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_q$. An empty $E_q$ imposes no condition. Let $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$ and assume its actual integer fibers are the given $K_p$. Let $\mu$ be the sum of $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure on those fibers, $M$ its total mass, $\beta$ the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth over the measured points, and $I$ the bases where this maximum is attained. Assume that $C$ is a local minimum of $\beta$ among all actual positive fiber families on this fixed $P$, in the fiberwise Hausdorff topology.

For each $p\in I$ fix a finite positive circuit of actual critical caps $H_{pj}=\{(z,x):a_{pj}\cdot z+u_{pj}\cdot x\ge b_{pj}\}$, with $\lVert u_{pj}\rVert=1$, $\rho_{pj}>0$, and $\sum_j\rho_{pj}u_{pj}=0$. Each circuit passes through an interior maximizing mark of its own fiber and its caps have raw mass $Q=\beta M$. Assume each old aggregate threshold mass is $Q-S_{pj}s+o(|s|)$ when its threshold is moved by $s\to0$ from either side, with $S_{pj}>0$. Write $\kappa_{pj}=(\rho_{pj}/S_{pj})/\sum_\ell(\rho_{p\ell}/S_{p\ell})$. Let a finite collection of nonempty relatively open boundary patches $U$ be given, each in some $\partial K_q$, with $U$ a $C^1$ hypersurface containing no nontrivial line segment.

There exists one probability vector $\pi$ on $I$ such that on every patch in the collection, at each point $x$ off all restricted cap planes, $\sum_{p\in I,j}\pi_p\kappa_{pj}\mathbf1_{H_{pj}}(q,x)=\beta$. For every listed unoriented restricted plane $L$ that meets the patch and intersects $\operatorname{int}K_q$, the total weight $\pi_p\kappa_{pj}$ of the restricted caps with plane $L$ is the same in either orientation.

If a patch in fiber $q$ is the whole boundary, let $F_q$ be the total stress weight of nonproper cap restrictions that contain the whole boundary off the planes, and $P_q$ the total stress weight of properly cutting restricted caps. Then $\beta=F_q+P_q/2$. For such a fiber with $q\in I$ one has $\pi_q\le2\beta$. If the collection includes the entire boundary of every deepest fiber, then $\beta\ge 1/(2|I|)$. The two-sided positive slope hypothesis holds automatically if every $K_p$ is strictly convex.

## Proof

At any point of a given patch, its $C^1$ tangent plane determines the unique unit supporting normal of the convex body. If its own support face contained another point, the initial part of the joining segment would lie in the support face and, by relative openness, in the patch. This is excluded. The strict compatibility exposure lemma therefore makes the lifted point a singleton ambient maximizer. At every such point take the own unit supporting normal as the outward direction. This checks the pointwise conditions of the exact signed local mass theorem, fa420503475f57e1, for any finite distinct set of these points. Strict compatibility and the stipulated full-rank positive actual family check its family conditions.

List the $N$ fixed caps from all deepest circuits. Across the patches, off their planes, there are at most $2^N$ distinct cap incidence vectors. Choose one lifted point for every realized vector. The representatives are distinct and avoid all the ambient cap boundaries, since a cap has the same incidence and zero set at a lifted point as its restriction to that base. By fa420503475f57e1 they admit jointly prescribed masses of either sign, with exact changes in those cap masses even after parallel shifts of order $O(\varepsilon)$. All those actual fixed-$P$ competitors converge fiberwise to $C$ and lie in its local-minimum class. The fixed positive circuits have raw mass $Q$ and the required two-sided positive slopes, so the equality dual of ec5e140eb107957e applies to this one finite set. It produces a single probability vector $\pi$ on $I$ with the score equal to $\beta$ at every representative. This proves the same equality at every point with a represented incidence vector. If there are no representatives, any probability vector suffices for this score assertion.

On each patch apply the constant boundary score pairing lemma to the restrictions $H_{pj}|_{\{q\}\times\mathbb R^d}$ with weights $\pi_p\kappa_{pj}$. The normals are nonzero and the lemma supplies the orientation equality for each proper plane meeting the patch. All equalities use the already chosen $\pi$.

On a full boundary, off the selected planes there are points to evaluate the score. Indeed each proper plane meets the boundary in a transverse codimension-one submanifold of that boundary, and each supporting plane meets it in at most a singleton by strict convexity. The other planes miss it. A finite union of these closed sets with empty interior cannot cover the boundary. Every proper plane meets the full boundary, and a pair of equal orientation weights on that plane contributes half the total weight of its class at each point off the planes. A nonproper trace has constant incidence there, either zero or one. This gives $\beta=F_q+P_q/2$. When $q\in I$, all own caps go through the interior maximizing mark, so they properly cut $K_q$ and contribute total weight $\sum_j\pi_q\kappa_{qj}=\pi_q$ to $P_q$. Thus $\pi_q\le2\beta$. Summing this inequality when all deepest fibers have full patches gives $1\le2|I|\beta$.

To see the slope assertion, suppose all fibers are strictly convex. For a given cap with unit continuous normal $u$, Fubini expresses each fiber contribution as the integral above its restricted threshold of the $(d-1)$-volume of the sections orthogonal to $u$. Interior sections have positive volume and continuous volume as a function of their level, by the concavity of the $(d-1)$-st root of section volume on the interior support interval. At an endpoint the section is at most a singleton, and compactness forces the diameters of approaching nonempty sections to tend to zero. Since $d-1>0$, their volumes tend to zero, the same trace as outside the support interval. Summing over fibers gives the required two-sided expansion with slope the sum of section volumes, strictly positive by the proper own section. This slope argument also holds in $d=2$, where the geometric pairing lemma can fail. In $d=1$ supporting endpoints can have nonzero zero-dimensional section volume, so the automatic slope argument does not apply.
