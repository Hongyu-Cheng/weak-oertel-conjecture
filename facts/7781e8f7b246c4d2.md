## Statement

Let N and t be positive integers, let 0<=r<N, and let A be an integer r-by-N totally unimodular matrix of full row rank r with at most t distinct column vectors. Here totally unimodular means that every square minor is 0, 1, or -1. Set \(n:=N-r\) and
\[
D:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^N:A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},\qquad
Q:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Q}
\quad(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^N).
\]
Use the canonical normalized actual closed-halfspace depth and cubic moment
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=
\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]
Halfspaces have nonzero normals. The probability and depth conventions are those of accepted fact ebb695e93a721952, with \(\boldsymbol\theta\) an alias of its \(\boldsymbol\ell\). The partition Q is the exact section partition, not an independent-product partition.

The integer kernel \(\ker A\cap\mathbb Z^N\) has a saturated lattice chart of rank n whose basis vectors are ternary fundamental circuits. In that chart D is full-dimensional and complete: if \(\widehat D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) is its chart image, then \(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\). The ambient depth above equals the actual depth in this chart.

For every tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{(25/162)^N}{64(2N+1)^{7t}}
\ge \kappa_{r,t}\,8^{-n},\qquad
\kappa_{r,t}:=
\frac{(25/162)^r}{64}
\left(\frac{e\log(100/81)}{7t(2r+3)}\right)^{7t}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently some \(\mathbf z^\star\in D\) satisfies \(\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)\ge\kappa_{r,t}^{1/3}2^{-n}\).

For clarity, the original-capacity normalization is as follows. If positive capacities \(c_{\mathbf z}\) are proportional to \(e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\), write \(T:=\sum_Dc_{\mathbf z}\), \(p_{\mathbf z}=c_{\mathbf z}/T\), and \(h_c(\mathbf z):=\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{D\cap H}c_{\mathbf x}\). Then \(h_c=T\tau_p\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
=T^4M_3(p)\ge T^4\kappa_{r,t}8^{-n},\qquad
h_c(\mathbf z^\star)\ge T\kappa_{r,t}^{1/3}2^{-n}.
\tag{2}
\]
The reflected-support probabilities used below remain the exact original probabilities \(p(R_{\mathbf z})\); they are not replaced by conditional probabilities or by 1.

## Proof

First record the elementary TU box-integrality fact needed twice. If E is an integer totally unimodular matrix and b, l, u are integer vectors, every vertex of the nonempty bounded polytope \(\{x:Ex=b,\ l\le x\le u\}\) is integral. At a vertex, the equality rows and active coordinate-bound rows span the whole coordinate space: otherwise a nonzero vector annihilating them permits sufficiently small movements in both directions, contradicting extremality. Select a square independent system of these rows. Its determinant is 1 or -1, because expansion along its coordinate rows reduces it, up to sign, to a square minor of E. Its right-hand side is integral, so Cramer's rule makes the vertex integral. Every point of this bounded polytope is a convex combination of vertices. To see the latter assertion directly, a nonvertex admits a direction annihilating its active rows; extend the feasible segment in both directions to its endpoints. Each endpoint adds a row independent of the previously active rows, and the original point is a convex combination of the endpoints. Repeating this decomposition increases the active-row rank on every branch and terminates after at most the number of coordinates steps. This also proves the assertion when some coordinate intervals are singletons or equality rows are redundant.

Choose r independent columns of A and call their square matrix \(A_B\); call the remaining column-index set K. Since A is TU, \(\det A_B=\pm1\). For each j in K, let \(\mathbf h_j\) have coordinate 1 at j, coordinate 0 at the other indices in K, and basic-coordinate vector \(-A_B^{-1}\mathbf a_j\), where \(\mathbf a_j\) is column j of A. Cramer's rule expresses every entry of \(A_B^{-1}\mathbf a_j\) as a TU replacement-column determinant divided by \(\det A_B\); hence \(\mathbf h_j\in\{-1,0,1\}^N\) and \(A\mathbf h_j=0\). These are fundamental circuits: a kernel vector supported on a proper subset of \(\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf h_j)\) must vanish, because its basic coordinates are uniquely determined by its j-th coordinate, and a vector with j-th coordinate zero uses only independent basic columns.

Let \(\Phi:\mathbb R^n\to\ker A\) send the coordinate vector indexed by K to the corresponding linear combination of the \(\mathbf h_j\). Projection onto K is its inverse. If \(\mathbf z\in\ker A\cap\mathbb Z^N\), that projection is integer and its basic coordinates equal \(-A_B^{-1}A_K\mathbf z_K\); thus \(\Phi\) restricts to an isomorphism from \(\mathbb Z^n\) onto the integer kernel. This is the promised saturated chart. Saturation also follows directly: if an integer multiple of an integer vector lies in \(\ker A\), that vector itself lies there.

The polytope \(P:=\ker A\cap[-1,1]^N\) has integer vertices by the box-integrality fact, so \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). Since \(\mathbf0\) and all \(\pm\mathbf h_j\) belong to D, its chart image contains \(\mathbf0\) and all \(\pm\mathbf e_j\), and is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^n\). If \(u\in\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), then \(\Phi u\) is an integer vector in P and therefore belongs to D; hence \(u\in\widehat D\). This verifies completeness. When r=0, the basic matrix is empty with determinant 1, and these statements reduce to the identity chart of the full ternary cube.

An ambient halfspace containing a root restricts under \(\Phi\) either to a genuine chart halfspace containing that root or to the whole chart. The latter case has probability 1 and cannot decrease the minimum depth. Conversely, every chart halfspace lifts to an ambient one because \(\Phi\) has full column rank: its nonzero normal is \(\Phi^T\mathbf a\) for some ambient vector \(\mathbf a\). Such an ambient vector is nonzero. Thus the two actual depths agree, including all boundary atoms.

Next bound reflected-support cardinalities. For a root \(\mathbf z\in D\), put \(I:=\{i:z_i=0\}\), \(J:=\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus I\), and \(k:=|I|\). Coordinatewise reflection gives exactly
\[
R_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\}
=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf x_J=\mathbf z_J,\ \mathbf x_I\in\{-1,0,1\}^I,\ A_I\mathbf x_I=0\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, reflection forces a nonzero root coordinate to stay fixed, while at a zero root coordinate every ternary value is allowed. Moreover \(A_J\mathbf z_J=0\), since \(A\mathbf z=0\).

Let \(\mathbf a^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf a^{(t_0)}\), with \(t_0\le t\), be the distinct columns of A. In I, let \(k_j\) be the number of occurrences of type j. Requiring the ternary entries within every type to sum to zero is sufficient for \(A_I\mathbf x_I=0\). There are at least \(3^{k_j}/(2k_j+1)\) such assignments in a group of size \(k_j\). Here is a direct count proof. The coefficients \(f_m(a)\) of \((x^{-1}+1+x)^m\) are symmetric and nonincreasing for nonnegative a. This holds for m=0, and convolution by the ternary triple preserves it: for \(g(a)=f(a-1)+f(a)+f(a+1)\), one has \(g(a)-g(a+1)=f(a-1)-f(a+2)\ge0\) for a>=0, using symmetry at a=0. Their sum is \(3^m\) and their support has \(2m+1\) integers, so the central coefficient is at least \(3^m/(2m+1)\). This includes m=0. Multiplying the group counts gives
\[
s_{\mathbf z}:=|R_{\mathbf z}|\ge
\frac{3^k}{\prod_{j=1}^{t_0}(2k_j+1)}
\ge\frac{3^k}{(2k+1)^t}
\ge\frac{3^k}{(2N+1)^t}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(s_{\mathbf z}\) is the object denoted \(k_{\mathbf z}\) in ebb695e93a721952; k here counts zero coordinates instead. Its \(B_{\mathbf z}\) retains the exact meaning \(B_{\mathbf z}=p(R_{\mathbf z})\).

We now construct the auxiliary single-coordinate laws. For a real parameter eta define
\[
Z(\eta):=1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta},\qquad
v_+(\eta):=\frac{e^{4\eta}}{Z(\eta)^4},\qquad
v_-(\eta):=\frac{e^{-4\eta}}{Z(\eta)^4},\qquad
v_0(\eta):=\frac{729}{Z(\eta)^7}.
\tag{5}
\]
Their sum is at least 1/6. To prove this exactly, put \(\rho:=e^\eta+e^{-\eta}\ge2\) and \(s:=\rho-2\ge0\). Then \(e^{4\eta}+e^{-4\eta}=\rho^4-4\rho^2+2\), and multiplication by the positive denominator \(6(\rho+1)^7\) reduces the claimed inequality to nonnegativity of
\[
\begin{aligned}
&6(\rho^4-4\rho^2+2)(\rho+1)^3+4374-(\rho+1)^7\\
&\quad=5s^7+81s^6+525s^5+1689s^4+2577s^3
       +837s^2-2187s+2511\\
&\quad=5s^7+81s^6+525s^5+1689s^4+2577s^3
       +108s^2+729(s^2-3s+3)+324.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The last line is positive for s>=0 because \(s^2-3s+3=(s-3/2)^2+3/4\).

Keep \(w_0=v_0\). For eta>=0, set \(w_+=v_+\) and \(w_-:=\min\{v_-,v_0^2/(4v_+)\}\). For eta<=0, set \(w_-=v_-\) and \(w_+:=\min\{v_+,v_0^2/(4v_-)\}\). At eta=0 the two prescriptions agree without truncation: \(v_0=1/3\) and \(v_+=v_-=1/81\). Only the smaller endpoint is possibly decreased, and its original value is at most 1/81. Indeed, for eta>=0, \(e^{-\eta}/Z(\eta)\le1/3\), since \(2\le e^\eta+e^{2\eta}\); the other sign follows by symmetry. Consequently
\[
0<w_\sigma\le v_\sigma\quad(\sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}),\qquad
w_0^2\ge4w_+w_-,\qquad
F(\eta):=w_-+w_0+w_+\ge\frac16-\frac1{81}=\frac{25}{162}.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(w_1=w_+\), \(w_{-1}=w_-\), and likewise for v. The functions w and F are continuous. Away from zero this follows from their formulas; at zero the untruncated inequality is strict, so both branches agree in a neighborhood. They also satisfy \(w_+(-\eta)=w_-(\eta)\).

Let an auxiliary variable \(Y(\eta)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) have law \(q_\eta(\sigma):=w_\sigma(\eta)/F(\eta)\). Each such variable is a shifted sum of two independent Bernoulli variables. Indeed, the polynomial \(w_-+w_0x+w_+x^2\) has two negative real roots, counting multiplicity, by (7) and strict positivity of its coefficients. Write these roots as \(-\rho_1,-\rho_2\), with \(\rho_i>0\). Division by F gives exactly
\[
q_\eta(-1)+q_\eta(0)x+q_\eta(1)x^2
=\frac{\rho_1+x}{\rho_1+1}\frac{\rho_2+x}{\rho_2+1}.
\tag{8}
\]
The right side is the generating polynomial of two independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities \(1/(1+\rho_i)\); subtracting 1 produces Y.

The auxiliary mean \(\mu(\eta):=(w_+(\eta)-w_-(\eta))/F(\eta)\) is continuous and tends to 1 as eta tends to positive infinity, and to -1 as eta tends to negative infinity. For the positive limit, \(v_+\to1\), \(v_0\to0\), \(v_-\to0\), and the positive endpoint is retained; the negative limit follows by symmetry. No monotonicity is required.

There is a gauge \(\boldsymbol\eta=\boldsymbol\theta+A^T\boldsymbol\lambda\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf a_i\mu(\eta_i)=\mathbf0.
\tag{9}
\]
For r=0 this is automatic. For r>0, let \(f(u):=\int_0^u\mu(v)\,dv\). The two limits just proved imply \(f(u)\ge|u|/2-C\) for some finite C and every real u. Consider the continuously differentiable function
\(G(\lambda):=\sum_i f(\theta_i+\mathbf a_i\cdot\lambda)\).
Since A has full row rank, there is c>0 with \(\|A^T\lambda\|_1\ge c\|\lambda\|_2\). This follows by minimizing the continuous positive function \(\|A^T\lambda\|_1\) on the unit sphere. Thus
\(G(\lambda)\ge c\|\lambda\|_2/2-\|\theta\|_1/2-NC\), so G is coercive. It attains a global minimum, where its gradient vanishes. Differentiating G gives precisely (9).

Fix this gauge and let \(Y_i\) be independent auxiliary variables with laws \(q_{\eta_i}\). For each column type j, let \(n_j\) be its multiplicity among all N columns, and let \(S_j\) be the sum of the \(Y_i\) of that type. Its law \(\pi_j\) is strictly positive on every integer of \([-n_j,n_j]\), and is log-concave there. To verify log-concavity without an external theorem, a Bernoulli convolution takes a log-concave sequence a with interval support to \(b_k=\alpha a_k+\beta a_{k-1}\), with \(\alpha,\beta>0\). Extended by zero outside the interval,
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_k^2-b_{k-1}b_{k+1}
={}&\alpha^2(a_k^2-a_{k-1}a_{k+1})
 +\beta^2(a_{k-1}^2-a_{k-2}a_k)\\
&+\alpha\beta(a_ka_{k-1}-a_{k-2}a_{k+1})\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The first two terms are nonnegative by log-concavity. The last is nonnegative because consecutive positive ratios of a are nonincreasing; if its right product is nonzero all four relevant indices are in the interval, and otherwise the claim is immediate. Starting from a point mass and convolving the 2n_j Bernoulli variables from (8) proves the assertion. Strictly interior Bernoulli probabilities also prove positivity throughout the stated support.

Write \(\nu_j:=\mathbb E S_j\), let \(\nu=(\nu_j)\), and let \(A_{\mathrm{cat}}\) be the r-by-t_0 matrix whose j-th column is \(\mathbf a^{(j)}\). This matrix is TU, since it is a submatrix obtained by retaining one representative of each column type. Equation (9) says \(A_{\mathrm{cat}}\nu=0\). The polytope
\[
\{u\in\mathbb R^{t_0}:A_{\mathrm{cat}}u=0,
\ \lfloor\nu_j\rfloor\le u_j\le\lceil\nu_j\rceil\text{ for every }j\}
\tag{11}
\]
is nonempty because it contains nu, and has integer vertices by box integrality. Hence nu is a convex combination of integer points of this box satisfying the same equation.

For each j, linearly interpolate \(\log\pi_j\) between consecutive integers of \([-n_j,n_j]\), obtaining \(L_j\). Log-concavity makes \(L_j\) concave. Jensen's inequality and the elementary entropy bound give
\[
L_j(\nu_j)\ge\mathbb E\log\pi_j(S_j)
\ge-\log(2n_j+1).
\tag{12}
\]
For completeness, the entropy bound here is \(\sum_a\pi_j(a)\log(1/\pi_j(a))\le\log\sum_a\pi_j(a)/\pi_j(a)=\log(2n_j+1)\), by concavity of the logarithm. The means lie strictly between the support endpoints, so all floor/ceiling values in (11) remain in the support. On every floor/ceiling interval the corresponding \(L_j\) is affine, including the singleton case. Applying the convex combination from (11), some integer point u in that polytope satisfies
\[
\sum_j\log\pi_j(u_j)=\sum_jL_j(u_j)
\ge\sum_jL_j(\nu_j)
\ge-\sum_j\log(2n_j+1).
\tag{13}
\]
The groups are independent and \(A_{\mathrm{cat}}u=0\). Thus their joint event \(S_j=u_j\) is contained in \(A\mathbf Y=0\), and
\[
\mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)
\ge\prod_j\pi_j(u_j)
\ge\prod_j(2n_j+1)^{-1}
\ge(2N+1)^{-t_0}\ge(2N+1)^{-t}.
\tag{14}
\]
This proves the required rounding probability, not just feasibility of a rounded vector.

It remains to connect this auxiliary probability to actual depth. The gauge leaves the original law unchanged, since \(A\mathbf x=0\) on D. Therefore
\(Q=\sum_D e^{\boldsymbol\eta\cdot\mathbf x}\).
For the root and coordinate sets in (3), write
\[
Q_I:=\sum_{\substack{y\in\{-1,0,1\}^I\\A_Iy=0}}
 e^{\sum_{i\in I}\eta_i y_i},\qquad
Z_i:=Z(\eta_i).
\]
Empty-coordinate sums and products equal 1. Formula (3) gives the exact substitutions
\[
p_{\mathbf z}=\frac{e^{\sum_{i\in J}\eta_i z_i}}{Q},\qquad
B_{\mathbf z}=p(R_{\mathbf z})
=\frac{e^{\sum_{i\in J}\eta_i z_i}Q_I}{Q},\qquad
Q\le\prod_{i=1}^NZ_i,\qquad Q_I\le\prod_{i\in I}Z_i.
\tag{15}
\]
Apply the all-root real-halfspace bound of ebb695e93a721952 to this same probability law, using \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\eta\), \(k_{\mathbf z}=s_{\mathbf z}\), and the exact \(B_{\mathbf z}\) in (15). Dropping only the positive '+1' in its cardinality numerator yields
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge
\frac{s_{\mathbf z}^2p_{\mathbf z}^2}{4B_{\mathbf z}},\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
\ge\frac{s_{\mathbf z}^6e^{4\sum_{i\in J}\eta_i z_i}}
{64Q^4Q_I^3}.
\tag{16}
\]
This accepted bound applies to every actual containing halfspace, regardless of the restriction's dimension or equality atoms. Substituting (4) and the partition upper bounds (15), with their displayed inequality directions, gives for every root
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
&\ge\frac{1}{64(2N+1)^{6t}}
 \prod_{i\in J}\frac{e^{4\eta_i z_i}}{Z_i^4}
 \prod_{i\in I}\frac{729}{Z_i^7}\\
&=\frac{1}{64(2N+1)^{6t}}
 \prod_{i=1}^Nv_{z_i}(\eta_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, no reflected probability has been discarded; its exact Q_I factor is responsible for three of the seven zero-coordinate partition powers.

Sum (17) over D, decrease v to w, and use independence of the auxiliary variables:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac{1}{64(2N+1)^{6t}}
 \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\prod_iw_{z_i}(\eta_i)\\
&=\frac{\prod_iF(\eta_i)}{64(2N+1)^{6t}}
 \mathbb P(A\mathbf Y=0)\\
&\ge\frac{(25/162)^N}{64(2N+1)^{7t}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
This establishes the first inequality in (1).

Finally put \(a:=25/162\), \(b:=\log(8a)=\log(100/81)>0\), and \(h:=7t>0\). Since n>=1 and \(2N+1=2(n+r)+1\le(2r+3)n\), (18) gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge8^{-n}\frac{a^r}{64(2r+3)^h}\frac{e^{bn}}{n^h}.
\]
For every real x>0, \(e^{bx}/x^h\ge(eb/h)^h\): the derivative of its logarithm is \(b-h/x\), so its global minimum occurs at \(x=h/b\). This proves the explicit second inequality in (1). Since \(M_3(p)\le\max_{\mathbf z\in D}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\), the root-depth conclusion follows. Scaling each probability by T gives \(h_c=T\tau_p\) and the fourth-power identity in (2), proving all original-capacity assertions.
