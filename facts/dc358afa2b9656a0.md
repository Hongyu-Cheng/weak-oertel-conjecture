## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact and convex, with $d\ge1$, and write $w=(z,X)$. For $L>0$ on $C$, set $\Phi(w)=(Z,Y,s)=(z,X,1)/L(w)$. The inverse on the image is $(z,X)=(Z,Y)/s$. Each original closed halfspace $a\cdot z+u\cdot X\ge h$ corresponds on the image to $a\cdot Z+u\cdot Y-hs\ge0$.

If $L=b+\beta\cdot z+c\cdot X$ is affine, its image is the convex slice
$$
(Z,Y)\in sC,\qquad s>0,\qquad \beta\cdot Z+c\cdot Y+bs=1.
$$
The original label $z$ lies in $Z=zs$, $\ell(z)s+c\cdot Y=1$, where $\ell(z)=b+\beta\cdot z$. For $c\ne0$ these $d$-planes are nonparallel for distinct labels, and $Z$ varies along each full-dimensional old fiber with $z\ne0$. On any full-dimensional old $d$-fiber the intrinsic $d$-Jacobian and tilted density are, respectively,
$$
J_{\Phi,z}=L^{-d-1}\sqrt{\ell(z)^2+(1+|z|^2)|c|^2},\qquad
\frac{d\Phi_*(dX/L)}{d\mathcal H^d}=\frac{L^d}{\sqrt{\ell(z)^2+(1+|z|^2)|c|^2}}.
$$
Here the second $L$ is evaluated at the inverse point. Neither formula requires $\ell(z)\ne0$.

If $L=\min_j A_j>0$ for finitely many affine $A_j$, the image is the locus $\min_j A_j^{\rm hom}=1$ within the convex set $(Z,Y)\in sC$, $s>0$, $A_j^{\rm hom}\ge1$ for every $j$. Here $A_j^{\rm hom}(Z,Y,s)=s A_j((Z,Y)/s)$. Each active cell maps to a convex affine slice. The whole image can fail to be convex. On a single fiber $C=[-1,1]^2$ with $L=2-|X_1|$, both the homogeneous image and the injective map $X\mapsto X/L$ have nonconvex images, despite positive constants in both affine pieces.

## Proof

Because $s=1/L(w)>0$, division by $s$ recovers the original point, and multiplication by $s$ proves the exact halfspace identity. The positive cone $(Z,Y)\in sC$, $s>0$, is convex: when two cone points are averaged with any convex weights, their inverse points in $C$ are averaged with weights proportional to those weights times their respective positive scales. The equation in the affine case is $sL((Z,Y)/s)=1$, which is equivalent to belonging to the image and is affine in the cone variables. This proves the slice description.

At a fixed label the tangent space is described by $\delta Z=z\,\delta s$ and $c\cdot\delta Y=-\ell(z)\delta s$. For $c\ne0$ these equations permit a tangent with $\delta s=1$; its $\delta Z$ cannot satisfy the equation of a different label. The old fiber has nonempty interior, so $L(z,\cdot)$ and $s$ vary on it when $c\ne0$, and then $Z=zs$ varies if $z\ne0$.

To compute the Gram Jacobian at fixed label, when $c\ne0$ rotate $c$ into the first continuous coordinate. Factoring $L^{-1}$ from each derivative column, the $d-1$ transverse columns are orthonormal continuous-coordinate directions. The remaining column, after orthogonal projection away from them, has first continuous component $\ell(z)/L$ and $(Z,s)$ components $-(z,1)|c|/L$. Its norm gives the stated Jacobian. If $c=0$ the derivative is a continuous-coordinate dilation by $1/\ell(z)$, with $\ell(z)=L>0$, and the same formula holds. It follows that the Jacobian is positive. The area formula for the injective lift gives the density by dividing $1/L$ by that Jacobian.

For the finite minimum, the condition $sL((Z,Y)/s)=1$ is $\min_j A_j^{\rm hom}=1$. Each $A_j^{\rm hom}$ is linear in the cone variables. The subset with $A_j^{\rm hom}=1$ is a convex slice and corresponds to the old cell where $A_j=L$. For the square, the points $(\pm1,1)$ have $L=1$, so their homogeneous images are $(\pm1,1,1)$. The midpoint $(0,1,1)$ has minimum homogeneous value $2$, so is missing. The two images after dropping $s$ have midpoint $(0,1)$, which would require $X_1=0$, $X_2=2$, outside the square. The dropped-scale map preserves rays, and on each unit ray $X=t\omega$, $t\ge0$, its new radius $t/(2-t|\omega_1|)$ is strictly increasing. Only the origin maps to zero, proving injectivity.
