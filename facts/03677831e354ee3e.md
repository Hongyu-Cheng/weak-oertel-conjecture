## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$, $A=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(4/5,0),(0,8/25)\}$, and $s=3/128+2^{-20}$. Let $M$ be the mixed area of the hull with fibers $(2-s)A,T,sA$ at bases $-1,0,1$. For any coupling $\pi$ of the uniform probabilities of $X$ in $(2-s)A$ and $Y$ in $sA$, put $\eta_\pi=\mathbf1_T\,dx+|sA|\operatorname{Law}_\pi((X+Y)/2)$, of mass $W=M/2+1/4096000$.

No Lebesgue density representative of $\eta_\pi$ has convex inverse cube-root, extended by $+\infty$ at zeros. If $\eta_\pi$ is absolutely continuous, then at every deepest point of $\eta_\pi$, no convex body reproduces its normalized through-point halfspace masses by the corresponding through-origin halfspaces. In particular the planar Ball radialization at that point cannot be convex.

## Proof

The exact fibers and the bound $\sup_y\inf_{H\ni y}\eta_\pi(H)<2M/9-3/10^7$ over closed planar halfspaces follow from 281951e60a80792a, with exactly these endpoint laws and transported mass $b=|sA|$. Its area identities give $b=ce$ and $M=c(2+2e-\delta)$, with $c=1/2$, $e=32s^2/125$, and $\delta=1/1024000$. Thus $W=c+b=M/2+c\delta/2=M/2+1/4096000$.

Every endpoint midpoint is in $A\subset T$, and the background area measure gives $\eta_\pi$ closed support $T$. A density with convex inverse cube-root is positive almost everywhere in $T$, hence its inverse power has full-dimensional domain. The bounded representative lemma makes it bounded and upper semicontinuous on the same compact support, with convex inverse cube-root. Fact d6a3cbae1da72277 in dimension two then gives depth at least $(4/9)W>2M/9$, contradicting the uniform upper bound.

For the radialization conclusion, assume absolute continuity. Cap mass $P(u,y)=\eta_\pi\{x:u\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$ is jointly continuous in unit $u$ and $y$ by dominated convergence. Minimizing over the compact unit circle gives the depth. It is zero on the boundary of $T$ and outside by support or separation and nullity of lines, and positive inside by the uniform background. Thus it attains its maximum at an interior point.

Fix any deepest point $y_*$ with raw depth $D$. If the compact set of normals attaining $D$ had convex hull missing zero, strict separation would give unit $v$ with $u\cdot v\ge\delta_0>0$ on those normals. When the center moves to $y_*-tv$, normals with $u\cdot v>\delta_0/2$ gain a slab of thickness at least $t\delta_0/2$. For small $t$ the part inside a fixed ball centered at $y_*$ and contained in $T$ has area bounded below by a positive constant times $t$, uniformly in $u$. The uniform background gives positive cap increase on these normals. The complementary compact set has a uniform positive gap above $D$, which persists by joint continuity. This would improve depth. Thus there is a finite positively balanced family of active normals, and their through-origin closed caps cover the plane.

A convex body reproducing these cap fractions would have fraction $D/W<4/9$ in each of the covering caps. Every point of that body would have depth fraction less than $4/9$, contrary to 03bc771bddd39a8a. For a density $f$ of $\eta_\pi$, the radial set defined by $\rho(u)^2=2\int_0^\infty t f(y_*+tu)\,dt$ has area $W$ and those through-origin cap fractions by polar integration. If convex, positive finite area makes it full dimensional and bounded. The hull of an interior disk and a sequence of points escaping to infinity would have unbounded area. Its closure would then be a convex body with the same cap fractions, since its convex boundary is area-null.
