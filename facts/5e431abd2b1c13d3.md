## Statement

Let \(m\ge1\) and \(L\ge2\) be integers, and put
$$
\mathcal Q=\left\lbrace(b,i):b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,
\ 1\le i\le m,\ b_i=0\right\rbrace,
\qquad k=m+L,\qquad N=k+1.
$$
For each \(q\in\mathcal Q\), choose an arbitrary group
\(G_q\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), of size \(n_q\ge2\), subject only to
$$
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}n_q^{-2/3}\le2^m.
$$
The groups may overlap with arbitrary incidence. Write the active and neutral coordinates as \((y,t)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^L\), and, for \(q=(b,i)\), set
$$
E_q=\left\lbrace(b+2e_i,t):
\begin{array}{l}
\text{exactly one neutral coordinate is two, its index belongs to }G_q,\\
\text{and every other neutral coordinate is binary}
\end{array}\right\rbrace,
\qquad E=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q}E_q.
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th active coordinate unit vector.
Suppose that the finite support \(D\) satisfies
$$
\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
$$
every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two, and
$$
\left\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace j:x_j=2\rbrace=2\right\rbrace=E.
$$
Thus the actual points with at most one two may be arbitrary, subject to these hypotheses.

For every original field \(\beta=(\gamma,\eta)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^L\), define
$$
A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,
\qquad w_{(y,t)}=\exp(-\gamma\cdot y-\eta\cdot t),
\qquad Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
\qquad p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z.
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) solely as an abbreviation, and use the actual quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
\qquad T(p)=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum is over ordered pairs and includes the diagonal once. Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; additionally allowing the whole space leaves the depth unchanged. Then, for every such \(D\) and every \(\beta\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-52}8^{-N},
\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-52}8^{-N}.
\tag{1}
$$
In particular, for the same arbitrary groups,
$$
D=\operatorname{conv}(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\cup E)\cap\mathbb Z^k
\tag{2}
$$
satisfies every support hypothesis above and hence (1).

## Proof

We use accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 only for its balanced private-leaf theorem with private size at least two, and accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 only for its final one-step assertion. Their normalizations and embeddings are matched explicitly below. All other arguments are proved here.

First consider a normalized law \(\pi\) on a finite set \(V\subseteq\mathbb R^h\) and an affine injection \(\Phi:V\to D\), extending affinely to the ambient spaces, such that
$$
p_{\Phi(u)}=\lambda\pi_u\quad(u\in V),
\qquad \lambda=p(\Phi(V)).
$$
Use the canonical extra-zero embeddings for both supports. The affine map
\((u,s)\mapsto(\Phi(u),s)\) pulls back every original closed halfspace containing \((\Phi(c),0)\) to either a closed halfspace containing \((c,0)\) with nonzero normal or the whole residual space. It cannot be empty because it contains the center. In the whole-space case its conditional mass is one, which is at least the conditional depth. Consequently
$$
\tau_p((\Phi(c),0))\ge\lambda\tau_\pi((c,0)).
$$
Every ordered reflected pair \(u+v=2c\) maps injectively to an original ordered reflected pair, because \(\Phi\) is affine and injective. Its minimum probability is multiplied by exactly \(\lambda\). Thus
$$
s_p(\Phi(c))\ge\lambda s_\pi(c).
$$
Multiplying the cubes of these inequalities by the center probability gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)&\ge\lambda^4M_3(\pi),\\
T(p)&\ge\lambda^4T(\pi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
If several such images are disjoint, their contributions on the right can be summed. This uses actual containing closed halfspaces and ordered reflected pairs, in every affine rank. Whole-space pullbacks require no new convention: the depth is at most one, and a nonzero-normal halfspace containing the entire finite support already has mass one. We will apply (3) both to sections and to subsets of a section.

Put \(\rho_i=\exp(-\gamma_i)>0\) and \(a_h=\exp(-\eta_h)>0\). Fix any set \(J\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\) of deleted neutral indices and any prescribed binary values \(v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^J\). Write
$$
I=\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\setminus J,
\qquad r=|J|,\qquad d=|I|=L-r,
\qquad K=m+d,\qquad N'=K+1=N-r.
$$
Here \(r\) counts deletions; the original neutral vector remains denoted by \(t\). Let \(D'\) be the support obtained from the actual section \(t_J=v\) by deleting its fixed coordinates. Its coordinates are \((y,u)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^I\). It contains the full Boolean cube in these coordinates. It is complete: an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D')\), lifted by insertion of the fixed binary coordinates, is an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\), hence belongs to \(D\) and to the prescribed section.

The normalized conditional law on this section is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
w'_{(y,u)}&=\prod_{i=1}^m\rho_i^{y_i}\prod_{h\in I}a_h^{u_h},
&Z'&=\sum_{x\in D'}w'_x,
&p'_x&=w'_x/Z',\\
\mu&=p(t_J=v)=\left(\prod_{h\in J}a_h^{v_h}\right)\frac{Z'}Z,
&p_{\Phi(x)}&=\mu p'_x,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
where \(\Phi\) reinserts the prescribed coordinates. Thus \(p'\) uses precisely the surviving original field coefficients; the fixed-coordinate factor cancels only in the conditional normalization. Define its depths and cubics on \(D'\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{N'}\) by the same formulas as in the statement. Equation (3) gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge\mu^4M_3(p'),
\qquad T(p)\ge\mu^4T(p').
\tag{5}
$$

Since every deleted coordinate is binary, deletion preserves the number of twos. For each label put \(G'_q=G_q\cap I\) and \(n'_q=|G'_q|\). The double points of \(D'\) are exactly the disjoint union of the sets \(E'_q\) with \(n'_q>0\), where \(E'_q\) has the same fixed active vector as \(E_q\), its single neutral two lies in \(G'_q\), and its other surviving neutral coordinates are arbitrary binary values. Indeed every original double point in the section has its neutral two in \(I\); conversely every listed residual point can be completed by the prescribed binary values because all other neutral entries in \(E_q\) are arbitrary. Labels with \(n'_q=0\) contribute no double points. Arbitrary original one-step points remain one-step points. This proves the exact structural stability needed at every deletion, including when a group loses all its indices.

We next give two stopping estimates on any such current conditional law \(p'\). Let
$$
D'_1=\left\lbrace x\in D':\#\lbrace j:x_j=2\rbrace\le1\right\rbrace,
\qquad \alpha=p'(D'_1).
$$
The actual core \(D'_1\) is complete. To see this, an integral point of its convex hull lies in \(D'\). If it had two coordinates equal to two, the extremality of two in \([0,2]\) would force every point with positive coefficient in a convex representation by \(D'_1\) to have both those coordinates equal to two, which is impossible. Hence it lies in \(D'_1\).

If \(\alpha\ge1/32\), take the normalized restriction \(\pi_x=p'_x/\alpha\) on \(D'_1\). It is exactly the original affine law with coefficients \(\beta'=(\gamma,(\eta_h)_{h\in I})\), normalized on that actual support. Apply the final assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 with its integer dimension \(k=K\ge1\), support \(D'_1\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^K\), field \(\beta'\), and embedding \(D'_1\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{K+1}\). Its occupied parity count is at most \(2^K\), without any rank assumption. Both conditional cubics are therefore at least \(1/(512\,8^K)\). Using the exact mass \(\alpha\) in (3) yields
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\,8^K}
\ge2^{-26}8^{-N'}.
\tag{6}
$$

Alternatively, let \(j\in I\) and suppose \(\nu_j=p'(u_j=2)\ge1/32\). Delete this fixed-two coordinate from its actual section. The projected support is nonempty and complete by the same lifting argument used above. It is ternary and has at most one remaining two. Its integer dimension is \(K-1\ge m\ge1\). Its law is the normalized restriction \(p'/\nu_j\), with the original surviving coefficients; the factor \(a_j^2\) cancels in that normalization. Apply the same final assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 with integer dimension \(K-1\), that projected support, and its extra-zero embedding in \(\mathbb R^K\). Its occupied parity count is at most \(2^{K-1}\); no Boolean base is required by that assertion. Equation (3) gives
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge\frac{\nu_j^4}{512\,8^{K-1}}
\ge2^{-23}8^{-N'}.
\tag{7}
$$

Now suppose \(\alpha<1/32\) and all surviving neutral activities belong to \([1/2,2]\). If \(d=0\), the exact double-point description makes every point one-step, contradicting \(\alpha<1/32\); so \(d\ge1\). For every live label \(q\), meaning \(n'_q\ge1\), order the indices of \(I\) by a bijection \(\sigma_q:\lbrace1,\ldots,d\rbrace\to I\) whose first \(n'_q\) values enumerate \(G'_q\). For an integer \(a\ge1\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_a^{\mathrm{leaf}}&=\left\lbrace z\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^a:
\#\lbrace h:z_h=2\rbrace=1\right\rbrace,\\
S_a&=E_a^{\mathrm{leaf}}\cup
\left\lbrace z\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^a:\sum_hz_h\ge2\right\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular \(S_1=\lbrace2\rbrace\). Form the product cell \(C'_q\subseteq D'\) by fixing the active vector to \(b+2e_i\), using \(S_{n'_q}\) on \(G'_q\), and using the full Boolean cube on \(I\setminus G'_q\). Its double points are \(E'_q\). Its other points also belong to \(D'\): choose two of their private ones, replace them by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\) in the two endpoints, and keep every other coordinate fixed. The endpoints lie in \(E'_q\), so completeness puts their integral midpoint in \(D'\). When \(n'_q=1\) there are no such extra Boolean private points, and the cell is simply \(E'_q\). No cell is used when \(n'_q=0\).

Different labels have different active vectors, so the cells are disjoint even for arbitrary group overlap. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_q&=S_{n'_q}\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{d-n'_q},
&Z_q&=\sum_{z\in R_q}\prod_{h=1}^d a_{\sigma_q(h)}^{z_h},\\
\pi_q(z)&=\frac{\prod_{h=1}^d a_{\sigma_q(h)}^{z_h}}{Z_q},
&\lambda_q&=p'(C'_q)
=\frac{\left(\prod_{j=1}^m\rho_j^{b_j+2\mathbf1_{j=i}}\right)Z_q}{Z'}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The affine injection \(\Phi_q:R_q\to C'_q\) inserts the fixed active vector and puts neutral entry \(z_h\) at \(\sigma_q(h)\). Thus
$$
p'_{\Phi_q(z)}=\lambda_q\pi_q(z),
\qquad \Lambda:=\sum_{q:n'_q>0}\lambda_q
\ge p'(E')=1-\alpha>31/32>1/2.
\tag{8}
$$
Here \(E'=\bigcup_q E'_q\) is the exact double-point set of \(D'\). Additional one-step points outside the cells are allowed; equality between their union and a fixed-active slice is not asserted.

For \(n'_q\ge2\), apply accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 with its
\(\ell=n'_q\), \(f=d-n'_q\), and ambient \(N=d+1\). Its support is exactly \(R_q\), its private activities are \(a_{\sigma_q(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma_q(n'_q)}\in[1/2,2]\), and its free activities are the other \(a_{\sigma_q(h)}>0\). Its normalization \(Z\) is exactly \(Z_q\), and its original probability law is exactly \(\pi_q\). The coordinate permutation and fixed-active insertion are covered by (3). Since the constant in that accepted fact is greater than \(2^{-38}\), it gives the common bound
$$
M_3(\pi_q),\ T(\pi_q)\ge2^{-38}(n'_q)^2 8^{-(d+1)}.
\tag{9}
$$
For \(n'_q=1\), we do not apply that accepted fact. The private coordinate is fixed to two, and its constant weight cancels, so \(\pi_q\) is the Boolean product law on the \(d-1\) free coordinates. Every containing closed halfspace contains the center atom, and the diagonal reflected pair occurs once; hence both cubics are at least \(\sum_z\pi_q(z)^4\). For every \(a>0\), convexity of the fourth power gives \((1+a)^4\le8(1+a^4)\). Therefore
$$
\sum_z\pi_q(z)^4
=\prod_{h=2}^d\frac{1+a_{\sigma_q(h)}^4}{(1+a_{\sigma_q(h)})^4}
\ge8^{-(d-1)}
\ge2^{-38}8^{-(d+1)}.
$$
This proves (9) also for \(n'_q=1\), including \(d=1\), when the free product is empty and the law is a singleton. This argument uses no depth factorization.

For every live label,
$$
n_q=n'_q+|G_q\cap J|\le n'_q+r\le(r+1)n'_q.
$$
Consequently the residual inverse-size budget satisfies
$$
\sum_{q:n'_q>0}(n'_q)^{-2/3}
\le(r+1)^{2/3}\sum_qn_q^{-2/3}
\le(r+1)^{2/3}2^m.
$$
Hölder's inequality, applied to
\(\lambda_q=(\lambda_q^4(n'_q)^2)^{1/4}(n'_q)^{-1/2}\), now gives
$$
\sum_{q:n'_q>0}\lambda_q^4(n'_q)^2
\ge\frac{\Lambda^4}{\left(\sum_{q:n'_q>0}(n'_q)^{-2/3}\right)^3}
\ge\frac{2^{-4}}{(r+1)^2 8^m}.
$$
The collection is nonempty by (8). Summing (3) over its disjoint cells and using (9), we obtain the balanced stopping estimate
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')
\ge\frac{2^{-42}}{(r+1)^2}8^{-(m+d+1)}
=\frac{2^{-42}}{(r+1)^2}8^{-N'}.
\tag{10}
$$

We now construct the deletions rather than assume their existence. Start with \(J=\varnothing\), so \(p'=p\) and \(\mu=1\). At any current section, stop if \(\alpha\ge1/32\), using (6). Otherwise stop if all surviving neutral activities belong to \([1/2,2]\), using (10). If neither condition holds, choose a surviving neutral index \(j\) with \(a_j<1/2\) or \(a_j>2\). If \(\nu_j=p'(u_j=2)\ge1/32\), stop using (7). It remains to treat
$$
\alpha<1/32,\qquad \nu_j<1/32,
\qquad a_j\notin[1/2,2].
$$
For \(s\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), let \(E'_s=\lbrace x\in E':u_j=s\rbrace\). Exact binary completion of \(E'\) gives a bijection between \(E'_0\) and \(E'_1\) by changing only the \(j\)-th entry. Indeed their neutral two lies elsewhere, and every other surviving neutral entry of every \(E'_q\) is an arbitrary binary value. The original affine weights therefore give the exact identity
$$
p'(E'_1)=a_jp'(E'_0).
$$
Also
$$
p'(E'_0)+p'(E'_1)
=1-\alpha-p'(E'_2)>1-\frac1{32}-\frac1{32}=\frac{15}{16}.
$$
If \(a_j<1/2\), this implies
\(p'(E'_0)>\frac23\frac{15}{16}=5/8\). If \(a_j>2\), it implies
\(p'(E'_1)>\frac23\frac{15}{16}=5/8\). Thus the favored actual binary section \(u_j=s\), with \(s=0\) in the first case and \(s=1\) in the second, has conditional mass
$$
\theta=p'(u_j=s)>5/8.
\tag{11}
$$
Adjoin \(j\) to \(J\) with its prescribed value \(s\), and continue with this normalized conditional law. All geometry and exact weight identities proved for binary sections remain valid. The new original section mass is precisely \(\mu\theta\), by (4) or conditional probability.

Each continuation deletes a distinct neutral coordinate. Hence the process stops after some \(0\le r\le L\) deletions. If all neutral coordinates have been deleted, there are no double points, so \(\alpha=1\) and (6) applies. At termination the original section mass satisfies
$$
\mu\ge(5/8)^r,
$$
and every stopping alternative gives the common estimate
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge\frac{2^{-42}}{(r+1)^2}8^{-N'}.
$$
For (6) and (7) this is weaker than the respective displayed estimates; for the balanced alternative it is (10). Combining with the exact original mass factor (5) and \(N'=N-r\) yields
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)
\ge2^{-42}\frac{(625/512)^r}{(r+1)^2}8^{-N}.
\tag{12}
$$
The gain per deletion is \(8(5/8)^4=625/512>6/5\), since \(3125>3072\). The geometric-series identity, obtained by differentiating the convergent geometric series twice, gives
$$
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^2(5/6)^j
=\frac{1+5/6}{(1-5/6)^3}=396<512.
$$
Each summand is at most this sum, so
$$
\frac{(625/512)^r}{(r+1)^2}
\ge\frac{(6/5)^r}{(r+1)^2}
\ge\frac1{396}>2^{-9}.
$$
Equation (12) is therefore stronger than \(2^{-51}8^{-N}\) for each cubic, and in particular proves the advertised bound (1). Every probability restriction in the argument carries its exact fourth-power mass factor. No change to the original affine field, original support, or all-containing-halfspace definition was made.

Finally consider the source support (2). Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\cup E)\) and \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\). Every generator lies in \([0,2]^k\), has at most one active two, and has at most one neutral two. If a coordinate of a point in \(P\) equals two, every generator with positive coefficient in a convex representation has that coordinate equal to two. A lattice point of \(P\) therefore has at most one active two and at most one neutral two, and has at most two twos in total.

Suppose such a lattice point has two twos. One is active, say at \(i\), and the other is neutral, say at \(h\). The active value two forces all generators used in its representation to come from families with active two at \(i\). Their other active entries are binary. Each integral value zero or one of the lattice point in those other active coordinates is an extreme value in \([0,1]\), so all these generators have the same other active bits \(b\). Thus they all belong to the single family \(E_q\), \(q=(b,i)\). The neutral value two at \(h\) forces each such generator to have its neutral two at \(h\), so \(h\in G_q\). Every other neutral coordinate is binary in the generators, and its integral average is binary. The point is therefore itself a member of \(E_q\). Conversely all \(E_q\) are generators and are double points. This proves the required exact double-point identity for arbitrary group incidence.

The set \(D\) is finite and ternary, and contains the Boolean cube. Moreover its generating set is contained in \(D\subseteq P\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=P\); hence \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). It satisfies all the hypotheses already proved sufficient for (1), completing the consequence (2).
