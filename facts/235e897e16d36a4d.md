## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be nonempty and finite. Let the compact positive $(d-1)$-bodies $B_z$ satisfy all anchored Minkowski inclusions, with a singleton of measure one for $d=1$. Let $H_z>0$ satisfy the sampled concavity inequalities $H_q\ge\sum_z\lambda_zH_z$ for every anchored representation $\lambda$ of $q$. The actual fibers are $K_z=B_z\times[-H_z,H_z]$ in their canonical hull. Write $E_H$ for the nontrivial extreme anchored representations with equality and $v_z=(z,H_z)$. Let $C_H$ be the cone of vectors $h$ satisfying $h\ge0$ and $h_q\le\sum_z\lambda_zh_z$ on $E_H$, and let $C_H^{\mathrm{sgn}}$ omit $h\ge0$.

For any bounded Borel boundary load $W$ and scalar $\beta$ use $c_z^\pm=\int_{B_z}(W(z,X,\pm H_z)-\beta)\,dX$ to denote the upper and lower coefficients in this paragraph, and put $c_z=c_z^++c_z^-$. For every $h\in C_H$ there is an actual nested height path $H_z-th_z$ and a decreasing bounded sequence of globally finite continuous nonnegative convex physical tests on the old measured boundary with limit $h_z$ on both roofs and zero almost everywhere on the side. The integral against $W-\beta$ tends to $c\cdot h$. In particular a positive limit gives one finite test with positive response.

The condition $c\cdot h\le0$ for all $h\in C_H$ is equivalent both to $c=\sum_{(q,\lambda)\in E_H}\gamma_{q\lambda}(e_q-\sum_z\lambda_ze_z)-s$, with $\gamma_{q\lambda}\ge0$ and $s\ge0$, and to a capacitated transport between the upper nodes. Its rows from $c_i>0$ have mass $c_i$ and barycenter $v_i$, and columns at $c_j<0$ have capacity $-c_j$. Each row remains in the minimal lifted face containing its source. Thus $\sum_{v_z\in F}c_z\le0$ for every face $F$, and $c_q\le0$ at each lifted vertex. The alternative to this polar condition is a strictly positive $c\cdot h$ for some admissible $h$.

If the same load obeys $\int(W-\beta)\Phi\,dS\le0$ for every jointly continuous globally finite signed normal-dependent field convex in the physical variables at each normal, then for each $h\in C_H^{\mathrm{sgn}}$ such a field has exact compatible first speed $h_z$ on both roofs, or on just one of them, and zero elsewhere almost everywhere. For each of the upper, lower and combined coefficient vectors the polar representation holds with $s=0$. It has zero total, base and height moments and a lifted transport with every column saturated. In particular, if every lifted node is a vertex, all three coefficient vectors vanish identically.

## Proof

The anchored criterion 6d64b23cb5b24ea1 applies because the prism support at $(u_X,u_y)$ is $h_{B_z}(u_X)+H_z|u_y|$. There are finitely many extreme anchored representations. Under $H-th$ the tight height rows persist for $h$ in the stated cones and all strict inequalities and positive heights persist for small $t$. Thus the perturbed prism families have their exact canonical hulls, nested when $h\ge0$. The same reasoning allows varying just the upper height or just the lower height in the signed case: both signed roof magnitudes remain positive and concave in the sampled sense.

Place the nodes $(z,\pm H_z)$ with prescribed values $h_z$. In any convex representation of $(q,H_q)$ by these nodes, sampled concavity gives $H_q\ge\sum_j\lambda_jH_{z_j}\ge\sum_j\lambda_j\operatorname{sign}_jH_{z_j}=H_q$. Positivity forces every positive-weight sign to be plus and the height relation to be tight. Reflection handles the lower nodes. Every such tight representation is a combination of tight extreme representations of the base, since its nonnegative height gap averages to zero. Therefore the average of the prescribed values is at least $h_q$. At each node minimize that average over its representations. The minimum equals its own value by the trivial representation. Finite linear programming duality gives an affine minorant of all data touching that node. Taking the maximum of all those minorants gives a finite convex piecewise-affine function $\phi(z,y)$ with the prescribed values. For nonnegative data include zero in the maximum.

Let $H(z)=\min_r a_r(z)$ be the upper polyhedral envelope of the lifted upper nodes on the base hull, so $H(z)=H_z$ on $P$. The finite supporting upper planes exist by polyhedrality, with one plane in the affine-graph case. The functions $\max(0,\max_{r,\sigma=\pm1}[\phi(z,y)-R(a_r(z)-\sigma y)])$ are finite convex tests, independent of $X$. On the old body their penalties are nonnegative and on each roof an active gap is zero. Away from both roofs all gaps are positive. This proves the bounded decreasing limit and the claimed integral by dominated convergence against $dS$.

Finite cone duality gives exactly the displayed polar from the rows $h_q-\sum_z\lambda_zh_z\le0$ and $-h_z\le0$. At a vertex there is no nontrivial tight representation targeting it, so its coordinate unit vector belongs to $C_H$. For the transport equivalence use the compact-measure capacity criterion proved in 37b32ccd92db0405. Every nonnegative convex function on the ambient space of the upper nodes restricts to an $h$ in $C_H$. Conversely the same finite minorant argument on only the upper nodes extends every $h\in C_H$ to such a test. Thus the polar is exactly the nonnegative convex comparison between the atomic measures $\sum_{c_i>0}c_i\delta_{v_i}$ and $\sum_{c_j<0}(-c_j)\delta_{v_j}$. The criterion applies to these finite measures, including the zero-source case, and gives the row equations and column bounds in the statement. A supporting affine gap vanishing at a source has row average zero and is nonnegative on all targets, so every target belongs to the minimal source face. The total positive mass in a face is bounded by its negative capacity.

For signed data use the same interpolant without the zero term. Write $u_y$ for the last component of the continuous unit normal. The fields $u_y^2\phi$ and $(\pm u_y)_+^2\phi$ are finite jointly continuous and convex at fixed $u$. By 8820984cf6a6942e each gives an actual compatible signed path with that marked inward speed. At regular side points $u_y=0$ and at the roof interiors $u_y=\pm1$. These assertions include the endpoint convention. The three tests use the same prescribed $W$ and give the three signed polar inequalities. Finite cone duality now has no rows $-h_z\le0$, so $s=0$. Every tight generator annihilates $1,z,H$, giving the moment identities. The capacitated transports already proved for the inward cone have equal total positive and negative masses, hence all columns saturate. If all lifts are vertices, each coefficient is nonpositive and their sum is zero, forcing every coefficient to be zero.
