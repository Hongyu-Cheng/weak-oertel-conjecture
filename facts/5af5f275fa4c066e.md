## Statement

Let \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) be integrable and log-concave, where \(n\ge1\) and \(0\) is interior to \(\{f>0\}\). For every \(0<p<1\), the function
$$
q_p(x)=\left(\int_0^\infty t^{p-1} f(tx)\,dt\right)^{-1/p}\quad (x\ne0),\qquad q_p(0)=0
$$
is finite, continuous, convex and positively homogeneous. Its unit sublevel set is a compact convex body containing \(0\) in its interior.

## Proof

There are \(m,\delta>0\) with \(f\ge m\) on \(B(0,\delta)\), since the finite concave function \(\log f\) is continuous in the interior of its domain. For \(v\) in that ball, \(f((x+v)/2)\ge\sqrt{mf(x)}\). Integrating on the image ball gives a global upper bound on \(f\). The superlevel set \(\{f\ge f(0)/2\}\) is bounded. It contains a ball, and if it contained points arbitrarily far away, the convex hulls of that ball and those points would have arbitrarily large volume. Choose \(R\) strictly greater than its outer radius. For \(\|x\|\ge R\) with \(f(x)>0\), log-concavity on the segment from \(0\) to \(x\) gives
$$
f(0)/2>f(Rx/\|x\|)\ge f(0)^{1-R/\|x\|}f(x)^{R/\|x\|}.
$$
Together with the global upper bound this gives \(f(x)\le A e^{-c\|x\|}\) everywhere for some \(A,c>0\). These lower and upper bounds bound the ray integral uniformly above and away from zero on the unit sphere. Substitution gives \(q_p(cx)=cq_p(x)\) for \(c>0\).

Take \(x,y\) with \(q_p(x)=q_p(y)=1\), and let \(z=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\) with \(0<\lambda<1\). We can assume \(z\ne0\). The densities \(r^{p-1}f(rx)\) and \(s^{p-1}f(sy)\) each integrate to one. On the interiors of their positive rays they are continuous and positive. Their increasing quantiles \(r(u),s(u)\), \(0<u<1\), have derivatives \(r'=1/[r^{p-1}f(rx)]\) and \(s'=1/[s^{p-1}f(sy)]\). Put
$$
t=\left(\frac{\lambda}{r}+\frac{1-\lambda}{s}\right)^{-1},\qquad
a=\frac{\lambda t}{r},\qquad b=\frac{(1-\lambda)t}{s}.
$$
Then \(a+b=1\), \(t=ar+bs\), \(tz=arx+bsy\), and \(t'=t(ar'/r+bs'/s)>0\). Write \(f_1=f(rx)\) and \(f_2=f(sy)\). Log-concavity and the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality give
$$
t^{p-1}f(tz)t'\ge
t^p f_1^a f_2^b\left(\frac{a}{r^pf_1}+\frac{b}{s^pf_2}\right)
\ge\frac{t^p}{r^{pa}s^{pb}}\ge1.
$$
The last inequality uses \(t=ar+bs\ge r^a s^b\). Integration in \(u\), with the increasing change of variable \(t=t(u)\), shows that the ray integral at \(z\) is at least one. The integral over the image interval of \(t\) is bounded by the integral over the whole ray, and any finite endpoint of the positive ray has zero weight in the change of variable.

Thus every segment between points of \(q_p=1\) stays in the unit sublevel set. By homogeneity each point of that set is a combination of such a point and zero. Any convex combination of two sublevel points is therefore a combination of zero and a point on one of the proved segments. The unit sublevel set is convex, so its gauge \(q_p\) is subadditive and convex. The bound \(q_p(v)\le C\|v\|\) from the ray estimates now gives \(|q_p(x)-q_p(y)|\le C\|x-y\|\), using subadditivity in both directions. The upper and lower radial bounds and continuity give the claimed compact convex body.

The construction is Ball's radial-integral body. Ball's original article treats the even case. Klartag's Theorem 2.2 gives the non-even form for \(p\ge1\). The argument here uses \(p>0\) directly at any interior positive point. [Ball, *Logarithmically concave functions and sections of convex sets in \(\mathbb R^n\)*](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm-88-1-69-84), [Klartag, *On convex perturbations with a bounded isotropic constant*](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e86e750a29c3eccb630f05f14574cf63ab704b23&repid=rep1&type=pdf)
