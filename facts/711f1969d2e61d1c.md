## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). Use mixed-integer volume and halfspace depth exactly as in the locked problem, and retain the actual-fiber notation of accepted facts \(\texttt{3c78088dd84bbbdf}\) and \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\):

\[
C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

For every \(z\in D_+\), let \(b_z\) and \(\Sigma_z\) be the true centroid and uniform-volume covariance of the actual fiber, with exactly their meanings in \(\texttt{3c78088dd84bbbdf}\):

\[
b_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
\Sigma_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}(y-b_z)(y-b_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dy.
\]

Assume \(W>0\) and that there exist a matrix \(L\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\), a vector \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), and a number \(0\le\epsilon\le1/(4e)\), fixed independently of all halfspaces, such that

\[
\bigl|\beta\cdot(b_z-Lz-b)\bigr|
\le\epsilon\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}
\qquad(z\in D_+,\ \beta\in\mathbb R^d).
\tag{1}
\]

The matrix called \(A\) in this subgoal's affine map \(Az+b\) is denoted \(L\) here, since \(A\) in accepted fact \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\) denotes the true centroid affine space. Its centroid \(b_z\), the covariance \(\Sigma_z\), and the approximation vector \(\widetilde b_z=Lz+b\) have the same respective meanings as in the two predecessors. No hypothesis on the centroid affine rank or on base symmetry is imposed.

There exists one \(z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), chosen before any halfspace, such that \(\mathbf y^\star=(z^\star,Lz^\star+b)\in S\) and, writing \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\) as in the predecessors,

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d-\epsilon}{2^n}W
\ge\frac{W}{2e\,2^n}.
\tag{2}
\]

For the remaining branch \(W=0\), any \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) satisfies the locked depth inequality. The affine-graph selection above is asserted for \(W>0\).

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes its nonempty integer fibers a finite collection. Each \(C_z\) with \(z\in D_+\) is a compact convex body of positive \(d\)-dimensional volume. The other fibers contribute zero mixed-integer volume, giving the stated identity \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). If \(W=0\), the assumed nonemptiness of \(S\) lets us choose any actual point, and every containing halfspace has nonnegative mixed-integer volume. This proves the stated zero-volume branch. Henceforth assume \(W>0\), and use the original probabilities \(p_z=w_z/W\), exactly as in \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\).

Let \(Z\) have law \(\mathbb P(Z=z)=p_z\), and conditional on \(Z=z\) let \(Y\) be uniform on the actual \(C_z\). The actual-volume identity proved in \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\), with this unchanged normalization, is

\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in F)
=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in F\}}{w_z}
=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)}W
\tag{3}
\]

for every measurable ambient set \(F\).

We use only the scalar shifted-centroid estimate in the proof of accepted fact \(\texttt{3c78088dd84bbbdf}\), equation (2) there. Its exact applicability map is the actual convex body \(C_z\), its uniform conditional variable \(Y\), its true centroid \(b_z\), the same continuous normal \(\beta\), and its own directional standard deviation \(s_z=\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}\). For every \(\beta\ne0\), compactness gives finite variance, and the nonempty interior of the positive-volume fiber gives \(s_z>0\). In that predecessor's scalar argument, \(X=\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\) has a log-concave density with essential supremum at most \(1/s_z\), while the true-centroid cap inequality gives \(\mathbb P(X\ge0\mid Z=z)\ge\gamma_d\). Subtracting the integral of that density on \([0,ts_z]\) gives

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge ts_z\mid Z=z\bigr)
\ge\gamma_d-t\qquad(t\ge0).
\tag{4}
\]

This local derivation uses neither base-negation invariance nor the common covariance lower bound from that predecessor's global theorem. Both were used later for its different point construction. Thus (4), rather than that global theorem, is the accepted scalar input used here. The conditional projection has a density, so endpoints introduce no atoms.

For each \(z\in D_+\), put \(\widetilde b_z=Lz+b\). By (1), the event \(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge\epsilon s_z\) implies \(\beta\cdot(Y-\widetilde b_z)\ge0\). Equation (4) therefore gives

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\beta\cdot(Y-\widetilde b_z)\ge0\mid Z=z\bigr)
\ge\gamma_d-\epsilon>0\qquad(\beta\ne0).
\tag{5}
\]

Here \(\gamma_d>1/e\), as proved in \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\), and \(\epsilon\le1/(4e)\). For \(\beta=0\), the conditional probability in (5) is one.

These inequalities establish \(\widetilde b_z\in C_z\) for every positive fiber. Indeed, otherwise choose a nearest point \(k\in C_z\) to \(\widetilde b_z\), and set \(\beta=\widetilde b_z-k\ne0\). Minimizing distance along the segment from \(k\) to any \(y\in C_z\) gives \(\beta\cdot(y-k)\le0\), hence \(\beta\cdot(y-\widetilde b_z)\le-\|\beta\|_2^2<0\). The cap in (5) would then be empty, a contradiction. Consequently every graph point \((z,Lz+b)\) with \(z\in D_+\) belongs to \(C\).

Now apply the accepted finite-atomic centerpoint construction in the proof of \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\) to \(\eta=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z\delta_z\), with ambient affine space \(\mathcal E=\mathbb R^n\), point-location set \(T=\mathbb Z^n\), support hull \(P_\eta=\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\), and Helly number \(h=2^n\). This is the \((a,b)=(0,n)\) specialization of the mixed-integer Helly theorem explicitly stated and checked there, with only the coordinate-block permutation. The construction gives one \(z^\star\in P_\eta\cap T\) such that every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z^\star\) has \(\eta\)-mass at least \(2^{-n}\). The construction includes finite atoms and fixes this point before any direction is tested. Thus, for every \(u\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\u\cdot(z-z^\star)\ge0}}p_z\ge2^{-n}.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(u=0\), all support points satisfy the condition and the sum is one, so this case is also included.

Choose a convex-hull representation \(z^\star=\sum_{z\in D_+}\lambda_z z\), with \(\lambda_z\ge0\) and \(\sum_z\lambda_z=1\). Affineness gives
\((z^\star,Lz^\star+b)=\sum_z\lambda_z(z,Lz+b)\in C\), because all points in that convex combination were just shown to belong to \(C\). Since \(z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\), this is the required actual point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\). No positivity assumption on its chosen fiber is needed for this membership.

Fix this point. For arbitrary \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), let \(I_{\alpha,\beta}=\{z\in D_+:(\alpha+L^{\mathsf T}\beta)\cdot(z-z^\star)\ge0\}\). Equation (6), with exactly the sheared normal \(u=\alpha+L^{\mathsf T}\beta\), gives \(\sum_{z\in I_{\alpha,\beta}}p_z\ge2^{-n}\). For \(z\in I_{\alpha,\beta}\),

\[
\alpha\cdot(z-z^\star)+\beta\cdot(Y-Lz^\star-b)
=(\alpha+L^{\mathsf T}\beta)\cdot(z-z^\star)
+\beta\cdot(Y-\widetilde b_z).
\tag{7}
\]

If \(\beta\ne0\), the last term is nonnegative with conditional probability at least \(\gamma_d-\epsilon\) by (5). The first term is nonnegative on the selected bases. Averaging over precisely those bases, using only the original probabilities, proves

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\alpha\cdot(Z-z^\star)+\beta\cdot(Y-Lz^\star-b)\ge0\bigr)
\ge(\gamma_d-\epsilon)\sum_{z\in I_{\alpha,\beta}}p_z
\ge\frac{\gamma_d-\epsilon}{2^n}.
\tag{8}
\]

If \(\beta=0\), each selected fiber is retained in full, so the probability in (8) is at least \(2^{-n}\), which is at least its displayed lower bound since \(\gamma_d-\epsilon\le1\). If the sheared normal vanishes, \(I_{\alpha,\beta}=D_+\), and the same proof applies with total selected mass one. These arguments also cover \(\alpha=0\).

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) contains the parallel closed halfspace with boundary through \(\mathbf y^\star\). The latter is the event in (8) for a nonzero pair \((\alpha,\beta)\). Equation (3) therefore gives the first inequality in (2) for every containing halfspace. If the whole ambient space is admitted as a halfspace, its mixed-integer volume is \(W\), which also satisfies this bound. Taking the infimum proves the depth assertion at this single fixed actual point.

Finally, \(\gamma_d>1/e\) and \(\epsilon\le1/(4e)\) imply \(\gamma_d-\epsilon>3/(4e)>1/(2e)\), proving the second inequality in (2). The absolute constant \(c_0=1/(2e)\) belongs to \((0,1/e)\) and is independent of \(n,d,C,L,b\), and \(\epsilon\).
