## Statement

Let $W=pY_p+qY_q-\sum_j r_jY_j$, where $p>q>0$, the list of $r_j\ge0$ is finite and may be empty, and all variables are independent unit-rate exponentials. Add an independent unit-rate exponential $Y_*$ and set $V_a=W-aY_*$ for $a>0$. Write $F_a=\Pr(V_a\ge0)$ and $\mu_i(a)$ for the coarea conditional mean of $Y_i$ at $V_a=0$. Set $P=\Pr(W>0)$, $M=\mathbb E(W\mid W>0)$ and $\nu_i=\mathbb E(Y_i\mid W>0)$ for every old index $i$. If $I$ is all old indices or all old indices except one, then
$$
\lim_{a\to\infty} a\left[\mu_*(a)\prod_{i\in I}\mu_i(a)-F_a(1-F_a)\right]
=M\left[\prod_{i\in I}\nu_i-P\right]\ge0.
$$

## Proof

The old sum has no atom at zero by conditioning on all variables except $Y_p$. Its positive event has positive probability since $Y_p$ is unbounded independently of the other variables. The expectation of $W_+$ is finite and positive. Conditional on the old variables, the cap probability is $\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}(1-e^{-W/a})$. Changing the last coordinate from $Y_*$ to $v=W-aY_*$ has Jacobian factor $1/a$. At $v=0$ the resulting density of the old vector relative to its original law is proportional to $e^{-W/a}\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}$, and $Y_*=W/a$. This is the coarea conditional law at zero since $W$ has no atom there. In particular, with $J_a=\mathbb E[e^{-W/a}\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}]$, the density at zero is $J_a/a$ and
$$
\mu_i(a)=J_a^{-1}\mathbb E[Y_i e^{-W/a}\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}],\qquad
a\mu_*(a)=J_a^{-1}\mathbb E[W e^{-W/a}\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}].
$$
The density at zero is positive. Dominated convergence gives $J_a\to P$, $\mu_i(a)\to\nu_i$, and $a\mu_*(a)\to M$, using the integrable bounds $1$, $Y_i$, and $W_+$. Also $a(1-e^{-W/a})\mathbf1_{\{W>0\}}\le W_+$ and tends pointwise to $W_+$. Hence $aF_a\to PM$ and $F_a\to0$. The stated identity follows by multiplication.

The event $\{W>0\}$ agrees up to a null set with $\{W\ge0\}$. If one old index is omitted, apply accepted fact 716fa7580a098deb to the old variables, allowing arbitrary signs and zeros on their coefficients. For the full old product, let $N$ be the number of old variables. Their sum and normalized vector are independent, with mean sum $N$ and normalized vector uniform on an $(N-1)$-simplex. The old event depends only on the normalized vector, so its simplex centroid has coordinates $\nu_i/N$, all strictly positive. Its indicator is feasible for the retained-volume objective at this centroid in accepted fact efa4558174ce60cf. Dividing that fact's bound by the full simplex volume gives $P\le N^N\prod_i(\nu_i/N)=\prod_i\nu_i$. This applies also when the old event has probability one.
