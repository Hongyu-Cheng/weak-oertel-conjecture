## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $0<a<1$, $Q=[0,1]^{d-1}$ (a singleton transverse factor when $d=1$), and $k=(3-a)/4$. At bases $-1,0,1$ take the actual fibers of the lifted hull
$$
A=[k-a/2,k+a/2]\times Q,\qquad K=[0,1]\times Q,\qquad B=[k-1/2,k+1/2]\times Q.
$$
Their original mixed volume measure $\mu$ has mass $M=2+a$. Base $0$ is the unique integer base of weighted closed halfspace depth at least $M/2$. For $D=d+2$, the escort maximizes $(\int\rho^{(D-1)/D}\,d\mu)^D$ over probability densities with base mean zero. Its mass on each endpoint is $w/(1+2w)$ and on the middle fiber $1/(1+2w)$, where $w=((a^{1/D}+1)/2)^D$. Its continuous mean is $(y,1/2,\ldots,1/2)$ with
$$
y=\frac{1/2+2wk}{1+2w}.
$$
The actual cap $x_1-z\ge y$ through this point has original raw mass $a+1-y$. The normalized depth of the selected point is therefore at most $991/4012<1/4$ for $(d,a)=(1,1/8)$ and at most $8099/36838<2/9$ for $(d,a)=(2,1/81)$. In particular both filtered escort rules fail the sharp depth target at their unique qualifying base.

## Proof

The lifted hull has the indicated fibers and no others by 750432756479bf6a, with its three named fibers relabeled to $A,K,B$ here. In particular the midpoint of the endpoint boxes is $[(1-a)/2,1]\times Q\subseteq K$ and the individual masses are $a,1,1$. The extreme bases have weighted depths $a$ and $1$, by isolating them with closed base halfspaces. Base zero has depth $\min(a+1,2)=a+1>M/2$, hence it is the only eligible base.

For a probability with fixed fiber masses, strict Jensen for $q=(D-1)/D\in(0,1)$ shows that the maximizing density is constant on each positive-mass fiber and gives the finite objective $(\sum_z v_z^{1/D}\pi_z^q)^D$ with $v_z=|K_z|$. Base mean zero forces equal endpoint probabilities $u$, so the objective before the power is
$$
(a^{1/D}+1)u^q+(1-2u)^q,\qquad 0\le u\le 1/2.
$$
It is strictly concave. Its derivative vanishes at the unique interior solution of $u/(1-2u)=((a^{1/D}+1)/2)^D=w$. Thus $u=w/(1+2w)$ and the maximum value after raising to $D$ is $1+2w$. The continuous mean follows by weighting the fiber centroids, whose first coordinates are $k,1/2,k$ and whose other coordinates are all $1/2$.

Since $1/2<y<k<1$, the cap $x_1-z\ge y$ includes all of $A$: the threshold there is $y-1<0$, below $(3-3a)/4$. Its threshold at the other endpoint is $y+1>3/2$, above $(5-a)/4$, so it misses $B$. Its middle trace has mass $1-y$. In particular the exact substitutions are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c|c}
(d,a)&k&w&1+2w&y&M&\mu\{x_1-z\ge y\}\\ \hline
(1,1/8)&23/32&27/64&59/32&1133/1888&17/8&991/1888\\
(2,1/81)&121/162&16/81&113/81&10433/18306&163/81&8099/18306.
\end{array}
$$
The two normalized cap masses are $1/4-3/1003$ and $2/9-785/331542$, respectively. For comparison the separate mark at the center of the middle unit box has normalized depth at least $(1+2a)/(2(2+a))$ by a93e9443922f47dc §1: the reflected first-coordinate interval of $A$ is $[(1-a)/4,(1+3a)/4]\subseteq [k-1/2,k+1/2]$, giving overlap $a$, and the middle box contributes half its unit volume. This gives $5/17$ and $83/326$ for the two examples.
