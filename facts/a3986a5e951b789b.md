## Statement

Let \(n\geq2\) and \(d\geq1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]
For a bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(m>0\), use the canonical metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_Kf=m
 \right\},
\tag{2}
\]
and, for \((z,x)\in C\), define
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}\\x\in J}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J).
\tag{3}
\]
For a Borel set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
 \mu_C(B):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \mu_C(H).
\tag{5}
\]

Retain the canonical transverse atlas and the rescaled internal atlas of accepted facts 036a276c54ec5e66 and 2fe46f47d19112ea. Every atlas label \(\sigma\) has two distinct opposite endpoints \(c(\sigma),d(\sigma)\), with original endpoint contacts denoted
\[
 x_{\sigma,c(\sigma)},\qquad x_{\sigma,d(\sigma)}.
\tag{6}
\]
For an internal label, its rescaled weight is \(\eta_\sigma>0\), and there are source densities
\[
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y}
 \quad
 (y\in\{c(\sigma),d(\sigma)\})
\tag{7}
\]
of mass \(\eta_\sigma\) and barycenter \(x_{\sigma,y}\). For every physical endpoint \(y\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\ \mathrm{internal}\\
                   y\in\{c(\sigma),d(\sigma)\}}}
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y}
 \leq\frac12
 \quad\text{pointwise on }C_y.
\tag{8}
\]
The original whole-label point is
\[
 X_\sigma=
 \frac{x_{\sigma,c(\sigma)}+x_{\sigma,d(\sigma)}}2
 \in
 \mathsf M_{\eta_\sigma}
 \left(C_{(c(\sigma)+d(\sigma))/2}\right).
\tag{9}
\]

For every pooled opposite endpoint \(y\), retain the full, unrescaled atlas source-density pool
\[
 G_y:=
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\ \mathrm{in\ the\ full\ transverse\ atlas}\\
                   y\in\{c(\sigma),d(\sigma)\}}}
 g_{\sigma,y}.
\tag{10}
\]
Its exact mass is the pooled endpoint weight \(\tau_y\), and
\[
 0\leq G_y\leq\frac12,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_y}G_y=\tau_y.
\tag{11}
\]
When \(\tau_y>0\), write
\[
 p_y:=\frac1{\tau_y}\int_{C_y}xG_y(x)\,dx.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix one missed edge
\[
 e_0=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}},
 \qquad
 t_0:=t_{e_0}=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Suppose there are \(n\) distinct rescaled internal labels
\[
 \sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1},
 \qquad
 \eta_j:=\eta_{\sigma_j}>0,
\tag{14}
\]
such that the opposite-endpoint pair of \(\sigma_j\) is
\[
 \{a_j,b_j\},
 \qquad
 a_j\in\{u,w\},
\tag{15}
\]
and \(b_j\) is an endpoint of a missed edge
\[
 \widehat e_j\in
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}\setminus\{e_0\},
\tag{16}
\]
where \(\widehat e_0,\ldots,\widehat e_{n-1}\) are pairwise distinct. Assume the exact product-chart rank condition
\[
 w-u,\quad b_1-b_0,\quad\ldots,\quad b_{n-1}-b_0
 \quad\text{are linearly independent in }\mathbb R^n.
\tag{17}
\]
Condition (17), rather than merely full affine rank of the endpoint set \(\{u,w,b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}\}\), is the hypothesis used below.

Put \(u_0:=u\), \(u_1:=w\), and let \(\varepsilon_j\in\{0,1\}\) be determined by \(a_j=u_{\varepsilon_j}\). Then there are actual points
\[
 Y_{ij}\in
 \mathsf M_{\eta_j}
 \left(C_{(u_i+b_j)/2}\right)
 \qquad
 (i\in\{0,1\},\ 0\leq j\leq n-1)
\tag{18}
\]
such that
\[
 \boxed{Y_{\varepsilon_j,j}=X_{\sigma_j}}
 \qquad(0\leq j\leq n-1).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus the corner in the row originally met by \(\sigma_j\) preserves that whole label, rather than being replaced by an aggregate with the same base. All \(2n\) points satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_C\left(\frac{u_i+b_j}{2},Y_{ij}\right)
 \geq\gamma_d\eta_j.}
\tag{20}
\]

The endpoint witnesses for all memberships in (18) can be allocated simultaneously with pointwise total at most one in every physical source fiber. This assertion concerns only the endpoint source densities used in the separate positive-root interpolations. It does not add, or assert simultaneous feasibility of, independently produced target-fiber densities.

The \(2n\) bases in (18) form one integral injective full-rank product chart as follows. Put
\[
 h:=\frac{w-u}{2},
 \qquad
 k_j:=\frac{b_j-b_0}{2}\quad(1\leq j\leq n-1),
 \qquad
 V:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{k_1,\ldots,k_{n-1}\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Choose \(B\in\mathbb Z^{n\times(n-1)}\) whose columns are a lattice basis of the saturated lattice \(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and write
\[
 k_j=Bv_j\quad(1\leq j\leq n-1),
 \qquad v_0:=0,
 \qquad v_j\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}.
\tag{22}
\]
Define
\[
 \Phi(s,v):=
 \frac{u+b_0}{2}+s h+Bv,
 \qquad
 \Delta_1:=[0,1],
 \qquad
 \Delta_2:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Then \(\Phi:\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{n-1}\to\mathbb R^n\) is an integral affine injection, its two factor ranks are \(1\) and \(n-1\), and
\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi(i,v_j)=\frac{u_i+b_j}{2}}
 \qquad(i\in\{0,1\},\ 0\leq j\leq n-1).
\tag{24}
\]

Let \(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{n-1}\) be the barycentric-coordinate functions of \(\Delta_2\), put
\[
 \mathcal D_2:=\Delta_2\cap\mathbb Z^{n-1},
\tag{25}
\]
and define
\[
 \mathcal B_\eta:=
 \sum_{v\in\mathcal D_2}
 \left(
  \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\beta_j(v)\eta_j^{1/d}
 \right)^d.
\tag{26}
\]
The chart (23) and the actual points (18) determine one admissible full-rank two-factor lattice-polysimplex packet \(\Pi\), in the exact sense of accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5, with exact energy
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E(\Pi)=2\gamma_d\mathcal B_\eta.}
\tag{27}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 \geq2\gamma_d\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\eta_j,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq2^{-n}\mathcal E(\Pi)
 \geq2^{1-n}\gamma_d\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\eta_j.}
\tag{28}
\]
If \(\mathcal D_2=\{v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}\}\), in particular when \(\Delta_2\) is a standard unimodular simplex, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E(\Pi)=
 2\gamma_d\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\eta_j.}
\tag{29}
\]
Every additional lattice point of \(\Delta_2\) contributes a strictly positive summand to (26), and hence only improves (27). Non-unimodularity by itself need not imply the existence of an additional lattice point in dimensions at least three; equality in (29) is controlled by the actual lattice-point set, not by normalized volume alone.

This theorem is conditional on the one displayed downstream-edge star and (17). It does not assert that such a star exists, carries a fixed fraction of the internal mass, or can be extracted from every canonical hard instance. It also does not sum the energies of different stars.

## Proof

We first complete the partner row with the exact downstream capacities. For \(y\in\{u,w\}\), let
\[
 J_y:=\{j:u_{1-\varepsilon_j}=y\},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_y:=\sum_{j\in J_y}\eta_j.
\tag{30}
\]
If \(y=u\), every label indexed by \(J_y\) has original endpoint \(w\); if \(y=w\), every such label has original endpoint \(u\). The downstream endpoint-capacity inequality in accepted fact 2fe46f47d19112ea, applied at that opposite endpoint of \(e_0\), therefore gives
\[
 \Lambda_y\leq t_0\leq\tau_y.
\tag{31}
\]
For \(j\in J_y\), define the partner-completion density
\[
 r_{j,y}:=\frac{\eta_j}{\tau_y}G_y.
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (11) and (31) show that \(r_{j,y}\) is admissible, has mass \(\eta_j\), and has barycenter \(p_y\). Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{j\in J_y}r_{j,y}
 =\frac{\Lambda_y}{\tau_y}G_y
 \leq G_y\leq\frac12
 \quad\text{pointwise on }C_y.
\tag{33}
\]

For each selected label, abbreviate its two original contacts by
\[
 x_{j,a}:=x_{\sigma_j,a_j},
 \qquad
 x_{j,b}:=x_{\sigma_j,b_j}.
\tag{34}
\]
Define
\[
 Y_{\varepsilon_j,j}:=
 \frac{x_{j,a}+x_{j,b}}2=X_{\sigma_j},
 \qquad
 Y_{1-\varepsilon_j,j}:=
 \frac{p_{u_{1-\varepsilon_j}}+x_{j,b}}2.
\tag{35}
\]
The equality in the first definition is the original whole-label formula from accepted facts 036a276c54ec5e66 and 2fe46f47d19112ea. The two rescaled densities \(\widetilde g_{\sigma_j,a_j}\) and \(\widetilde g_{\sigma_j,b_j}\) have the common positive mass \(\eta_j\) and barycenters \(x_{j,a}\) and \(x_{j,b}\). Accepted positive-root metronoid convexity dfd25e72c1f45a4e, applied at their common root \(\eta_j^{1/d}\), proves the first membership in (18). For the second membership, use \(r_{j,u_{1-\varepsilon_j}}\) and a second copy of \(\widetilde g_{\sigma_j,b_j}\), again at common mass \(\eta_j\), and apply the same accepted fact. This proves every membership in (18), including the exact bases shown there. Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d turns those memberships into (20).

We verify simultaneous source feasibility. At either \(y\in\{u,w\}\), the original-row occurrences have total density at most \(1/2\) by (8), while the partner-completion occurrences have total density at most \(1/2\) by (33). Their sum is pointwise at most one. The matching is endpoint-disjoint and every \(\widehat e_j\ne e_0\), so no \(b_j\) equals \(u\) or \(w\). At a physical endpoint used as some \(b_j\), the two row constructions use two copies of the corresponding rescaled density. Equation (8) gives
\[
 2\sum_{j:b_j=y}\widetilde g_{\sigma_j,y}\leq1
 \quad\text{pointwise on }C_y.
\tag{36}
\]
There are no other selected source occurrences. This proves the simultaneous source-density assertion. Each target membership was nevertheless obtained separately; no target-fiber densities have been summed.

We next prove integrality and rank. Every edge in the retained dyadic matching has same-parity endpoints, while the two opposite endpoints of every transverse atlas label have the same parity by accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66. Since \(a_j\in\{u,w\}\), all of
\[
 u,w,b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}
\tag{37}
\]
belong to one parity class. Thus the base point and all vectors in (21) are integral. Condition (17) is equivalent, after multiplication by \(1/2\), to linear independence of
\[
 h,k_1,\ldots,k_{n-1}.
\tag{38}
\]
In particular, \(V\) has dimension \(n-1\), the lattice basis \(B\) in (22) exists, and \(v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}\) are linearly independent. Hence \(\Delta_2\) is a full-dimensional lattice simplex and the concatenated matrix
\[
 [h\ B]
\tag{39}
\]
has rank \(n\). This proves that \(\Phi\) is integral and injective. Direct substitution of (21)--(23) gives
\[
 \Phi(i,v_j)
 =\frac{u+b_0}{2}
  +i\frac{w-u}{2}
  +\frac{b_j-b_0}{2}
 =\frac{u_i+b_j}{2},
\tag{40}
\]
which proves (24). In particular, the \(2n\) product-vertex bases are pairwise distinct.

Because \(B\mathbb Z^{n-1}=V\cap\mathbb Z^n\), the map \(v\mapsto Bv\) gives a bijection
\[
 \mathcal D_2
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{conv}\{0,k_1,\ldots,k_{n-1}\}
 \cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{40a}
\]
Thus the factorwise-primitive second chart retains every lattice point of the physical column simplex; no available column-simplex lattice base is omitted from the exact energy below.

We now apply the factorized packet theorem. On the two vertices of \(\Delta_1=[0,1]\), choose the constant first-factor roots
\[
 a_{1,0}=a_{1,1}:=\gamma_d^{1/d},
\tag{41}
\]
and on the vertices of \(\Delta_2\) choose
\[
 a_{2,j}:=\eta_j^{1/d}
 \qquad(0\leq j\leq n-1).
\tag{42}
\]
Equation (20) says exactly that every product vertex satisfies
\[
 \delta_C\bigl(\Phi(i,v_j),Y_{ij}\bigr)^{1/d}
 \geq a_{1,i}a_{2,j}.
\tag{43}
\]
All hypotheses of accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 now hold: both simplices are full dimensional and integral, the chart is integral and injective, its total rank is \(1+(n-1)=n\), and (43) supplies an actual point at every product vertex. That theorem produces one admissible full-rank factorized packet.

The first factor has the exact energy
\[
 \sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}(\gamma_d^{1/d})^d=2\gamma_d.
\tag{44}
\]
The second factor has exact energy \(\mathcal B_\eta\) by (26). Their product is (27), and the depth conclusion in accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 gives the first inequality for \(h_{\max}(C)\) in (28).

Every vertex \(v_j\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_2\), and at that vertex the summand in (26) is exactly \(\eta_j\). All barycentric coordinates are nonnegative on \(\Delta_2\), and all \(\eta_j\) are positive. Therefore every nonvertex lattice point contributes a strictly positive additional term, proving the energy bound and the equality criterion in (28)--(29). This also proves that lattice points of a non-unimodular second simplex can only improve the guaranteed energy. The stated qualification about non-unimodularity is necessary because an empty lattice simplex can have normalized volume greater than one in dimension at least three.

We record the exact rank wording. Full affine rank of the \(n+2\)-point endpoint set alone does not imply (17). For \(n=2\), take
\[
 u=(0,0),\quad w=(2,0),\quad
 b_0=(0,2),\quad b_1=(2,2).
\tag{45}
\]
These four same-parity points have affine rank two, but \(w-u=b_1-b_0\). Their four row-column midpoint bases lie on one line, and the two middle bases coincide. Thus a chart based only on the weaker affine-rank assertion can fail. The exact repair test is
\[
 \operatorname{rank}
 [\,w-u\ \ b_1-b_0\ \cdots\ b_{n-1}-b_0\,]=n,
\tag{46}
\]
which is precisely (17).

Finally, this theorem is not a restatement of the previously accepted star packets. Accepted fact 33a5a1bf70d5c5f1 completes a star of loaded stress edges sharing one common source anchor and uses its incidence loads. Accepted fact 6c37fd58639bf070 likewise assumes one common triangle anchor together with a complete cross-edge family. The labels here may have different original common stress endpoints; their common structure is instead the downstream dyadic edge \(e_0\), and (31)--(33) create the missing row from its partner endpoint pool. Accepted fact f44e38de6bcf9626 constructs a rank-two square from one transverse contribution and explicitly does not give full ambient rank when \(n>2\). Accepted fact 1c54f5dc74c15059 assumes a complete grid of distinct atlas labels and an externally supplied rank-one minorant. Here only the original-row corner of each column is an atlas label; the other corner is constructed by downstream partner completion, and the factorization in (41)--(43) follows directly from the column weights \(\eta_j\). None of these comparisons supplies, or is used to claim, existence of a macroscopic downstream-edge star or additivity across stars.
