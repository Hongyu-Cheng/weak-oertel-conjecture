## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]
For a positive-volume convex body \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(E)\), use the canonical metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_t(E):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_E xg(x)\,dx:
 0\leq g\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_E g(x)\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard instance of accepted facts
`6bfe50fed9a5540b` and `7ab0a03cf9189637`. Thus \(Q>0\), and the global complete-core capacity
\[

\[
 G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{3}

There are finite target loads \((\lambda_m)\), target-fiber volumes

and an exact minimum-unmatched off-diagonal same-parity target matching
\(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\). Put
\[
 d_m:=d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}}),
 \qquad
 \Lambda:=\sum_m\lambda_m,
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}:=|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|.
\tag{5}
\]
The accepted conclusions used here are
\[
 0\leq d_m\leq\lambda_m\leq\frac{a_m}{2},
 \qquad
 \Lambda>\frac{49}{50}Q,
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}>\frac{381}{800}Q.
\tag{6}
\]

For tags \(j\in\{\lambda,d\}\), define
\[
 w_m^\lambda:=\frac{\lambda_m}{2},
 \qquad
 w_m^d:=\frac{d_m}{2},
 \qquad
 A_j:=\{m:w_m^j>0\}.
\tag{7}

Both \(A_j\) are finite and nonempty, and

Put \(W_j:=\sum_{m\in A_j}w_m^j\). Then

Fix a total order on the parity classes. For each tag \(j\), parity class
\(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), and \(t\in\mathbb Z^n\), put

\tag{10}

\[

 \frac1{M_{j,\xi}}

 w_u^jw_v^j,&M_{j,\xi}>0,\\[3mm]

 \end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
The sum is ordered and includes all diagonal pairs. If
\(B_{j,\xi,t}>0\), define
\[
 p_{j,\xi,t}:=
 \frac1{M_{j,\xi}B_{j,\xi,t}}
 \sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_{j,\xi}\times A_{j,\xi}\\u+v=2t}}
 w_u^jw_v^j\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}.
\tag{12}

Leave zero-mass centers undefined. These quantities have finite support and

 \boxed{
 \sum_tB_{j,\xi,t}=M_{j,\xi},
 \qquad
 \sum_{\xi,t}B_{j,\xi,t}=W_j.}
\tag{13}

Define the common centered body
\[
 K_j:=\bigcap_{m\in A_j}
 \left(\mathsf M_{a_m/2}(C_m)-c_m\right).
\tag{14}

Accepted fact `35ca05c7683b7d12`, under the alias map

 M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j:=\sum_{t:\alpha_{j,t}>0}b_{j,t},

## Proof

Accepted fact `6bfe50fed9a5540b` gives the target loads, the strict estimate
\(\Lambda>49Q/50\), and the hard ledger containing \(G<Q/100\).
Accepted fact `7ab0a03cf9189637` identifies the same target system and gives
\(0\leq d_m\leq\lambda_m\leq a_m/2\) and
\(P_{\mathrm{tar}}>381Q/800\). This proves (3), (6), and (8).

The first identity in (9) follows from (7). Each edge of the finite
off-diagonal matching \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\) contributes its weight to its two
distinct endpoints, so
\[
 \sum_md_m=2|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|=2P_{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{45}
\]
This proves the second identity and the strict inequalities in (9).

Fix \(j\) and a parity class with \(M_{j,\xi}>0\). Every ordered pair
\((u,v)\in A_{j,\xi}^2\), including a diagonal pair, occurs in exactly one
sum (11), at the integral point \(t=(u+v)/2\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_tB_{j,\xi,t}
 =\frac1{M_{j,\xi}}
 \sum_{u,v\in A_{j,\xi}}w_u^jw_v^j
 =M_{j,\xi}.
\tag{46}
\]
The same identity is trivial for an empty class. Summing over \(\xi\) proves
(13). Equations (14)-(16) are the conclusions of accepted fact
`35ca05c7683b7d12` under (15). Equations (17)-(20) follow from the dominant
color definition and (13).

Apply accepted fact `ce3ac24a3aedd198` separately to the two admissible
weight systems in (7)-(8). Its definitions are (21)-(26), and its occurrence
ledger and cross-color estimate are exactly (27). In particular, the zero
orbit in (22) contributes two tagged occurrences of \(t\) to (25). The same
accepted fact gives (28)-(32), including coincident cloud atoms and reuse of
one source across distinct labels. Thus the square cloud is one dominated
measure, not a sum of separately dominated measures.

From (19) and (27),
\[
 M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j+\Theta_\square^j
 \geq M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j+C_{\mathrm{col}}^j
 =W_j.
\tag{47}
\]
This proves (33), and (9) gives (34)-(35). If the square term supplies the
half-mass alternative, (32) gives the stated strict cloud-mass bound. All
selectable-source choices are legal because
\[
 4w_m^\lambda=2\lambda_m\leq a_m,
 \qquad
 4w_m^d=2d_m\leq a_m.
\tag{48}
\]

We prove the common-gauge affine estimate carefully at the empty-complement
boundary. Fix \(j\) and an affine map
\(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and put
\[
 J_a:=\left\{t:\alpha_{j,t}>0,
 \ \|a(t)-q_{j,t}\|_{K_j}\leq\frac65\right\}.
\tag{49}
\]
For \(t\in J_a\), (20) gives
\[
 a(t)\in\mathsf M_{b_{j,t}}(C_t).
\tag{50}
\]
If \(J_a\ne\varnothing\), apply accepted fact `10f1feebe06e22d0` with
\(L=\mathbb R^n\), \(E=J_a\), masses \(b_{j,t}\), and affine selector
\(a\). That theorem allows \(J_a\) to span a proper affine subspace and
gives
\[
 \sum_{t\in J_a}b_{j,t}\leq G.
\tag{51}
\]
The same inequality is immediate when \(J_a=\varnothing\).

For each \(t\notin J_a\), the summand in (36) is strictly larger than
\(b_{j,t}/5\). If the complement is empty, both the complementary mass and
the full objective are nonnegative. Thus the estimate valid in every case is
the non-strict inequality
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_tb_{j,t}
 \left(\|a(t)-q_{j,t}\|_{K_j}-1\right)_+
 &\geq\frac15\sum_{t\notin J_a}b_{j,t}\\
 &=\frac15\left(
 M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j-\sum_{t\in J_a}b_{j,t}
 \right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{52}
\]
If \(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j>G\), (51) also shows that the complement cannot be
empty, because its mass is at least \(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j-G>0\). Combining
(51)-(52), uniformly in \(a\), gives
\[
 \mathfrak A_j\geq\frac15(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j-G).
\tag{53}
\]
If \(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^j\leq G\), nonnegativity proves the zero right side
of (37). This proves the unconditional estimate (37).

In the load-weighted dominant branch, (3), (6), and (53) give
\[
 \mathfrak A_\lambda
 \geq\frac15(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^\lambda-G)
 >\frac15\left(\frac{49}{200}-\frac1{100}\right)Q
 =\frac{47}{1000}Q.
\tag{54}
\]
In the degree-weighted dominant branch, the same argument gives
\[
 \mathfrak A_d
 \geq\frac15(M_{\mathrm{dom}}^d-G)
 >\frac15\left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac1{100}\right)Q
 =\frac{73}{1600}Q.
\tag{55}
\]
This proves (38)-(39). The strict numerical conclusions come from the strict
mass and hard-ledger inequalities; they do not require an invalid universal
strictness assertion in (52).

Finally, fix \(L_\xi>0\) in the degree-weighted system. Then
\(M_{d,\xi}=L_\xi/2\). For a nonzero orbit with endpoints
\(u=t+h\) and \(v=t-h\), (7) and (22) give
\[
 \beta_{d,\xi,t,[h]}
 =\frac{2(d_u/2)(d_v/2)}{L_\xi/2}
 =\frac{d_ud_v}{L_\xi}
 =q_{\{u,v\}}^{\mathrm{prod}}.
\tag{56}
\]
For the zero orbit,
\[
 \beta_{d,\xi,t,[0]}
 =\frac{(d_t/2)^2}{L_\xi/2}
 =\frac{d_t^2}{2L_\xi}.
\tag{57}
\]
These equations prove (42). Summing the orbit decomposition proves (43),
and multiplying each orbit mass by its centroid midpoint proves (44). This
accounts for every diagonal and off-diagonal multiplicity.
