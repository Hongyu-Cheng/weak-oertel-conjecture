## Statement

For an integer \(m\ge2\), let \(D_m=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:x+y\le m\}\). For \(\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\theta_2)\in\mathbb R^2\), put

\[
Z_m(\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{(x,y)\in D_m}e^{\theta_1x+\theta_2y},
\qquad
p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(x,y)=\frac{e^{\theta_1x+\theta_2y}}{Z_m(\boldsymbol\theta)}.
\]

This is the log-affine law of accepted fact \(\texttt{bf1ff94487296374}\) with \(A=D_m\) and the exact tilt alias \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\theta\). Use its canonical capacity and second-moment normalization:

\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^2}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_m}
 p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},
\qquad
\mathcal B_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2.
\]

Define \(k_{\mathbf j}=|D_m\cap(2\mathbf j-D_m)|\) and
\(\mathcal R_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_m}k_{\mathbf j}^2p_{\mathbf j}^3\). Thus \(\mathcal R_2\) is exactly the surrogate called \(S\) in the assignment, not the first moment called \(S\) in the proof of the accepted fact.

Set

\[
L_\star=1344e^{96},
\qquad
\varepsilon_\star=\frac{e^{-6L_\star}}{4\cdot384^3},
\qquad
g=1+16\varepsilon_\star
 =1+\frac{4e^{-6L_\star}}{384^3}>1.
\]

For every \(m\ge2\) and every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_2(p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
\ge\mathcal R_2(p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
\ge\frac1{16}+\varepsilon_\star=\frac g{16}.}
\]

There is also a complete reflection-count formula. For \(\mathbf j=(x,y)\), put \((a_1,a_2,a_3)=(x,y,m-x-y)\), and interpret \(\binom{t}{2}_{+}\) as \(t(t-1)/2\) for integers \(t\ge2\) and as zero otherwise. Then

\[
k_{\mathbf j}
=\binom{m+2}{2}
 -\sum_{i=1}^3\binom{m-2a_i+1}{2}_+
 +\sum_{i=1}^3\binom{2a_i-m}{2}_+.
\tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, if \(a_i\ge m/2\) and \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), then \(k_{\mathbf j}=(2a_j+1)(2a_k+1)\). If every \(a_i\le m/2\), then \(k_{\mathbf j}=m^2+m+1-2\sum_i a_i^2\). These formulas agree on their overlaps.

## Proof

The reflection argument in accepted fact \(\texttt{bf1ff94487296374}\) gives
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}\), where its canonical reflected Hellinger mass is

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in D_m\\2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\in D_m}}
 \sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}}
=k_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{2}
\]

The last equality follows from log-affinity. Thus \(\mathcal B_2(p)\ge\mathcal R_2(p)\). We prove the stronger surrogate bound using exact finite inequalities.

First, the barycentric coordinates of an endpoint and its reflection about \(\mathbf j\) are respectively \((b_1,b_2,b_3)\) and \((2a_1-b_1,2a_2-b_2,2a_3-b_3)\). Therefore
\(k_{\mathbf j}\) counts the integer triples satisfying \(\sum_i b_i=m\) and \(0\le b_i\le2a_i\) for every \(i\). Without the upper bounds there are \(\binom{m+2}{2}\) triples. Violating the \(i\)-th upper bound subtracts \(\binom{m-2a_i+1}{2}_+\). Violating the bounds at two distinct indices adds \(\binom{m-2a_i-2a_j}{2}_+=\binom{2a_k-m}{2}_+\). The three violations cannot occur together, since their minimum total is \(2m+3>m\). Inclusion-exclusion proves (1).

If \(a_i\ge m/2\), the other two coordinates have total at most \(m/2\). Every independent choice \(0\le b_j\le2a_j\), \(0\le b_k\le2a_k\) then gives \(b_i=m-b_j-b_k\) between zero and \(2a_i\). This proves the rectangular formula. If all \(a_i\le m/2\), the positive pair-intersection terms in (1) vanish, and expanding its three single-intersection terms gives \(m^2+m+1-2\sum_i a_i^2\). Substituting \(a_i=m/2\) shows that the two formulas agree there.

For completeness, the numerator of the surrogate has an exact representation by four polynomial-weighted triangular geometric sums. Put \(h=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) and \(W_{\boldsymbol\theta}(a_1,a_2,a_3)=e^{3\theta_1a_1+3\theta_2a_2}\). Then \(Z_m(\boldsymbol\theta)^3\mathcal R_2(p_{\boldsymbol\theta})\) equals

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\substack{a_1,a_2,a_3\ge0\\a_1+a_2+a_3=m}}
 W_{\boldsymbol\theta}(a_1,a_2,a_3)
 \left(m^2+m+1-2\sum_{i=1}^3a_i^2\right)^2\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^3
 \sum_{\substack{a_j,a_k\ge0\\a_j+a_k\le h\\a_i=m-a_j-a_k}}
 W_{\boldsymbol\theta}(a_1,a_2,a_3)
 \left[(2a_j+1)^2(2a_k+1)^2
 -\left(m^2+m+1-2\sum_{i=1}^3a_i^2\right)^2\right],
 \qquad \{j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Outside the three corner triangles, the first summand already uses the correct count. Inside a corner, its correction replaces that count by the rectangular one. For even \(m\), two corner triangles intersect only where two barycentric coordinates equal \(m/2\) and the third is zero; the corrections vanish at those points. Thus no overlap correction is needed. Every domain in (3) is an integer triangle, and its weight is a monomial in two positive geometric parameters times a constant. This identity is valid at all positive parameters, including coincident or unit parameters, without a limiting convention.

We will use two consequences of the parity midpoint argument. They are stated here for the reflection second moment, rather than only for capacities. For a finite complete rank-\(r\) support \(A\) and a log-affine law, let \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\) denote the mass of intrinsic parity class \(\mathbf c\). The oriented same-parity midpoint bijection and log-affinity give

\[
\overline{\mathsf H}:=\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}
=\sum_{\mathbf c}\pi_{\mathbf c}^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2
=\overline{\mathsf H}^{2}
 +\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}
       (\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}-\overline{\mathsf H})^2
\ge4^{-r}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\overline{\mathsf H}\) is exactly the first moment denoted \(S\) in the accepted fact's parity argument. In rank two, if the support has more than four points and every atom is at least \(\eta>0\), the same argument gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2
\ge\frac1{16}+\frac{\eta^3}{4}.
\tag{5}
\]

Here are the details of the strict version, to make its application to the surrogate explicit. Let \(R_0\) consist of points in parity classes containing at least two support points, and choose a vertex \(\mathbf j\) of \(\operatorname{conv}R_0\). The set \(R_0\) is nonempty. Any two distinct reflected endpoints about \(\mathbf j\) would belong to \(R_0\) and contradict that extremality. Hence \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=p_{\mathbf j}\). If \(\mathbf c\) is its parity class, then \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\ge p_{\mathbf j}+\eta\). If \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\ge1/4+\eta/2\), then
\(\overline{\mathsf H}=1/4+\sum_{\mathbf d}(\pi_{\mathbf d}-1/4)^2\ge1/4+\eta^2/4\). Consequently the second moment is at least \(1/16+\eta^2/8\ge1/16+\eta^3/4\), since \(\eta\le1/|A|<1/4\). Otherwise, \(p_{\mathbf j}<1/4-\eta/2\le\overline{\mathsf H}-\eta/2\), so the variance term in (4) is at least \(p_{\mathbf j}\eta^2/4\ge\eta^3/4\). This proves (5).

Permuting the three barycentric coordinates is an affine lattice bijection of \(D_m\). Such a map preserves reflection counts, reflected masses, capacities, and their second moments. Choose a vertex of maximal tilt score as the origin and order the other two vertices by their score deficits. We may therefore assume

\[
\boldsymbol\theta=(-\gamma_1,-\gamma_2),
\qquad
\gamma_1\ge\gamma_2\ge0,
\qquad
p(x,y)=\frac{e^{-\gamma_1x-\gamma_2y}}{Z_m},
\qquad
Z_m=\sum_{x+y\le m}e^{-\gamma_1x-\gamma_2y}.
\tag{6}
\]

We next establish the one-dimensional geometric estimates used in the two anisotropic cases. For \(0\le\zeta<1\), elementary differentiation of the geometric series gives

\[
\mathcal G(\zeta):=\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}(2x+1)^2\zeta^x
=\frac{1+6\zeta+\zeta^2}{(1-\zeta)^3},
\qquad
\Phi(\zeta):=(1-\zeta)^3\mathcal G(\zeta^3)
=\frac{1+6\zeta^3+\zeta^6}{(1+\zeta+\zeta^2)^3}
\ge\frac8{27}.
\tag{7}
\]

The last inequality follows from the exact polynomial identity

\[
27(1+6\zeta^3+\zeta^6)-8(1+\zeta+\zeta^2)^3
=(1-\zeta)^2
 \left[19(1-\zeta^2)^2+14\zeta^3+\zeta(14-\zeta)\right]\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

For \(\gamma>0\), let \(\omega_\gamma\) be the probability on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) proportional to \((2x+1)^2e^{-3\gamma x}\). With \(z=e^{-3\gamma}\), its mean satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega_\gamma}X
=\frac{z(6+2z)}{1+6z+z^2}+\frac{3z}{1-z}
\le\frac{9z}{1-z}
=\frac9{e^{3\gamma}-1}
\le\frac3\gamma.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, \((6+2z)(1-z)\le6(1+6z+z^2)\), and \(e^{3\gamma}-1\ge3\gamma\). Its exponential moment satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega_\gamma}e^{\gamma X}
=\frac{\mathcal G(e^{-2\gamma})}{\mathcal G(e^{-3\gamma})}
\le27,
\qquad
\omega_\gamma(X>t)\le27e^{-\gamma t}\quad(t\ge0).
\tag{10}
\]

For the moment bound, write \(\zeta=e^{-\gamma}\). The ratio of the polynomial numerators in \(\mathcal G(\zeta^2)/\mathcal G(\zeta^3)\) is at most eight, while
\((1-\zeta^3)/(1-\zeta^2)=(1+\zeta+\zeta^2)/(1+\zeta)\le3/2\). Their product is at most \(8(3/2)^3=27\). Markov's inequality gives the tail bound.

There are three exhaustive parameter regimes.

First suppose \(m\gamma_2\ge32\). Both \(\gamma_i\) are positive. The triangular partition function is at most the full-quadrant partition function:
\(Z_m\le[(1-e^{-\gamma_1})(1-e^{-\gamma_2})]^{-1}\). On \(x+y\le m/2\), the rectangular reflection formula gives \(k_{(x,y)}=(2x+1)(2y+1)\). Under independent \(X\sim\omega_{\gamma_1}\), \(Y\sim\omega_{\gamma_2}\), the event \(X+Y>m/2\) is contained in \(\{X>m/4\}\cup\{Y>m/4\}\). Retaining only this corner triangle in the numerator and using (7) and (10) therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_2(p)
&\ge\Phi(e^{-\gamma_1})\Phi(e^{-\gamma_2})
 \left(1-27e^{-m\gamma_1/4}-27e^{-m\gamma_2/4}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{64}{729}(1-54e^{-8})
\ge\frac{101}{1458}
\ge\frac1{16}+\frac1{256}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The penultimate bound uses \(e>2\), so \(e^{-8}<1/256\). The final comparison is exact: \(101/1458-1/16=79/11664>1/256\).

Next suppose \(m\gamma_2<32\) and \(m\gamma_1\ge L_\star\). Extend \(k_{(x,y)}\) by zero outside \(D_m\), and put \(k^{[1]}_{m,y}=2\min(y,m-y)+1\) for \(0\le y\le m\). With \((\cdot)_+\) denoting positive part, for all integers \(x\ge0\), \(0\le y\le m\),

\[
(2x+1)(k^{[1]}_{m,y}-2x)_+
\le k_{(x,y)}\le(2x+1)k^{[1]}_{m,y}.
\tag{12}
\]

To prove this, first take \(x+y\le m\). For a fixed reflected endpoint's first coordinate \(X\in\{0,\ldots,2x\}\), its second coordinate must lie in the integer interval
\([\max(0,2x+2y-m-X),\min(2y,m-X)]\). If \(x>m/2\), the claimed lower bound is zero. If \(x\le m/2\) and \(y\le m/2\), put \(d=m-2y\ge0\). Relative to the interval \([0,2y]\), the two endpoint losses are \((X-d)_+\) and \((2x-d-X)_+\), whose sum is at most \(2x\). Thus there are at least \((2y+1-2x)_+\) choices. If \(y\ge m/2\), the number is exactly \(2(m-y)+1-2x\). Summing over \(X\) proves the lower bound. The upper bound follows by counting at most \(2y+1\) choices when \(y\le m/2\), and from the exact rectangular formula when \(y\ge m/2\). If \(x+y>m\), then \(k^{[1]}_{m,y}-2x\le0\), so the extended inequality remains valid.

Since \((b-2x)_+^2\ge b^2-4xb\) for \(b\ge0\), (12) gives

\[
k_{(x,y)}^2
\ge(2x+1)^2
 \left[(k^{[1]}_{m,y})^2-4xk^{[1]}_{m,y}\right].
\tag{13}
\]

Here \(\gamma_1>0\), so the sums over all \(x\ge0\) are absolutely convergent. Also
\(Z_m\le(1-e^{-\gamma_1})^{-1}\sum_{y=0}^m e^{-\gamma_2y}\). Define the reflection second moment of the tilted integer interval by

\[
\mathcal R_2^{[1]}(m,\gamma_2)
=\frac{\sum_{y=0}^m(k^{[1]}_{m,y})^2e^{-3\gamma_2y}}
 {\left(\sum_{y=0}^m e^{-\gamma_2y}\right)^3}
\ge\frac14.
\tag{14}
\]

The lower bound is (4) in intrinsic rank one, applied to the complete interval \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Summing (13), then using the product partition-function bound, yields

\[
\mathcal R_2(p)
\ge\Phi(e^{-\gamma_1})\mathcal R_2^{[1]}(m,\gamma_2)
\left[
1-4\mathbb E_{\omega_{\gamma_1}}X
 \frac{\sum_{y=0}^m k^{[1]}_{m,y}e^{-3\gamma_2y}}
      {\sum_{y=0}^m(k^{[1]}_{m,y})^2e^{-3\gamma_2y}}
\right].
\tag{15}
\]

In using an upper bound on the partition function here, the bracket is nonnegative by the following quantitative estimates. The exact unweighted sums of squares are

\[
\sum_{y=0}^m(k^{[1]}_{m,y})^2
=\begin{cases}
(m^3+3m^2+5m+3)/3,&m\text{ even},\\
(m^3+3m^2+2m)/3,&m\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\sum_{y=0}^m k^{[1]}_{m,y}\le(m+1)^2.
\tag{16}
\]

These follow by summing the successive odd integers and their squares on the two halves of the interval. Since \(m\gamma_2<32\) and \(m\ge2\), the ratio in (15) is at most
\(3e^{96}(m+1)^2/m^3\le7e^{96}/m\). By (9), the quantity subtracted in that bracket is at most
\(84e^{96}/(m\gamma_1)\le1/16\). Hence

\[
\mathcal R_2(p)
\ge\frac8{27}\cdot\frac14\cdot\frac{15}{16}
=\frac5{72}
=\frac1{16}+\frac1{144}
\ge\frac1{16}+\frac1{256}.
\tag{17}
\]

Finally, the remaining regime has \(m\gamma_2<32\) and \(m\gamma_1<L_\star\). Let \(N_m=|D_m|=(m+1)(m+2)/2\). Because \(0\le\gamma_1x+\gamma_2y\le m\gamma_1<L_\star\) throughout the support,

\[
\frac{e^{-L_\star}}{N_m}\le p(x,y)\le\frac{e^{L_\star}}{N_m},
\qquad
\frac{m^2}{2}\le N_m\le\frac{3m^2}{2}\quad(m\ge2).
\tag{18}
\]

Put \(\delta=e^{-L_\star/2}/16\). If \(m\delta<1\), then \(N_m<384e^{L_\star}\) and every atom is at least \(\eta=e^{-2L_\star}/384\). The support is complete of rank two and has at least six points. Applying (5) gives
\(\mathcal R_2(p)\ge1/16+\eta^3/4=1/16+\varepsilon_\star\).

If \(m\delta\ge1\), put \(t=\lfloor\delta m\rfloor\) and let \(E=\{(x,y):0\le x,y\le t\}\). This square lies in \(D_m\), since \(2t\le2\delta m\le m\). Its mass satisfies

\[
p(E)\ge\frac{e^{-L_\star}(t+1)^2}{N_m}
\ge\frac23e^{-L_\star}\delta^2
=\frac{e^{-2L_\star}}{384}.
\tag{19}
\]

For \(\mathbf j\in E\), endpoint coordinate bounds give \(k_{\mathbf j}\le(2t+1)^2\). Since \(2t+1\le3\delta m\), (18) implies

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=k_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}
\le18e^{L_\star}\delta^2
=\frac9{128}<\frac18.
\tag{20}
\]

But (4) gives \(\overline{\mathsf H}\ge1/4\). The variance contribution from \(E\) is therefore at least \(p(E)/64\), and

\[
\mathcal R_2(p)
\ge\frac1{16}+\frac{e^{-2L_\star}}{24576}
\ge\frac1{16}+\frac{e^{-6L_\star}}{4\cdot384^3}
=\frac1{16}+\varepsilon_\star.
\tag{21}
\]

The comparison uses only \(L_\star\ge0\) and \(24576<4\cdot384^3\). In the first two regimes, (11) and (17) give the stronger gain \(1/256\), while \(\varepsilon_\star\le1/(4\cdot384^3)<1/256\). Thus all three regimes give the same claimed surrogate gap. Combining this with (2) proves the capacity gap for all finite \(m\ge2\) and all real tilts. Every reduction is an inequality at the original finite dilation and positive parameters; no asymptotic exclusion of a boundary class is used.
