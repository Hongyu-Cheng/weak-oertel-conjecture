## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), use exactly the standard block objects of accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409, with the reflection description of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2. Thus \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), the matching scans from left to right and matches each one to the last previously unmatched zero, \(D_m\) is its nonterminal set, \(i_m(c)\) is the first unmatched-zero position, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_m(c)&=c+e_{i_m(c)},\\
E_m&=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\\
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\\
T_m&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
 =E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},\\
J_m&=F_m(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For \(j=F_m(c)\in J_m\), write \(u_j=c\) and \(v_j=c+2e_{i_m(c)}\). Here \(e_h\) is a coordinate unit vector.

Let \(L\ge1\), and independently for each \(1\le\ell\le L\) choose \(m_\ell\ge1\) and a finite real table \(\phi_\ell:T_{m_\ell}\to\mathbb R\). Require only the midpoint inequalities
$$
\phi_\ell(j)\le\frac{\phi_\ell(u_j)+\phi_\ell(v_j)}2
\qquad(j\in J_{m_\ell}).
\tag{2}
$$
Define the entire original factor laws and their product by
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{\ell,x}&=e^{-\phi_\ell(x)},\qquad
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in T_{m_\ell}}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=\frac{w_{\ell,x}}{Z_\ell}\quad(x\in T_{m_\ell}),\\
N&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L m_\ell,\qquad P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,\\
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}&=
\frac{\exp(-\sum_\ell\phi_\ell(x_\ell))}{\prod_\ell Z_\ell}
\quad((x_1,\ldots,x_L)\in\prod_\ell T_{m_\ell}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
All laws are zero outside their displayed supports. Retain the actual depths and moments of 4d36c889dd0a9ba2:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
 \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Every ambient containing closed halfspace is included, even for lower-dimensional supports. With natural logarithms, set
$$
D_*=384\,(26624)^2,\qquad
c_* =\frac14\exp\left[-29952
 -16D_*\left(2+12(\log2)^2\right)\right]>0.
\tag{5}
$$
Then every choice in (1)--(3) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_*4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_*^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$

In particular, (6) holds for all independent finite fields \(\gamma_\ell\in\mathbb R^{m_\ell}\) and all independent finite coordinate penalties \(\alpha_{\ell,h}\ge0\), with the full original factor weights
$$
w_{\ell,x}=\exp\left(\gamma_\ell\cdot x
 -\sum_{h=1}^{m_\ell}\alpha_{\ell,h}\max(0,x_h-1)\right)
\qquad(x\in T_{m_\ell}).
\tag{7}
$$
The conclusion also applies to every fully convex-admissible finite potential table on the same blocks, since full convex-admissibility implies (2). No assertion is made here for other complete convex lattice support families.

## Proof

We first prove one-block estimates, keeping the original normalization. Suppress the block index and write \(m,T_m,J_m,p,w,Z\) as in the statement, and put \(K=2^m\). The normalization map to accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 is \(n_1=m\), \(A_1=T_m\), \(w_{1,x}=e^{-\phi(x)}\), \(Z_1=Z\), and \(p_1=p\). That fact gives the following exact properties. Every parity class has at most two supported points. The distinct same-parity pairs are precisely \(\lbrace u_j,v_j\rbrace\), \(j\in J_m\); their centers are distinct and their endpoint sets are pairwise disjoint. Every supported reflected row is either \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) or \(\lbrace u_j,j,v_j\rbrace\). Every spike is a singleton-row center, because the nontrivial centers are Boolean. Also \(|T_m|\le2K\). Accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409 specifies that these are exactly the standard blocks in (1).

For \(j\in J_m\), define
$$
s_j=\frac{\sqrt{p_{u_j}p_{v_j}}}{p_j}\in(0,1],\qquad
z_j=\frac12\log\frac{p_{v_j}}{p_{u_j}},\qquad
\rho_j=\log(1/s_j)\ge0,\qquad
\beta_j=Ks_jp_j^2.
\tag{8}
$$
The upper bound on \(s_j\) is exactly (2). The full canonical quantities remain
\(H_p(x)=\sum_y\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}\), \(b(p)=\sum_xp_xH_p(x)^2\), and \(\chi(p)=\sum_xp_xH_p(x)\), with both orientations of a nonzero displacement and the zero displacement once. In particular, \(H_p(j)=(1+2s_j)p_j\) on \(J_m\), and \(H_p(x)=p_x\) otherwise.

Choose any subset \(\mathcal S\subseteq J_m\). At centers in \(\mathcal S\), retain the full three-point reflected row, and at every other supported center retain only its singleton. Denote these symmetric auxiliary sets by \(\widehat R_x\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H(x)&=\sum_{y\in\widehat R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},\qquad
\widehat b=\sum_xp_x\widehat H(x)^2,\qquad
\widehat\chi=\sum_xp_x\widehat H(x),\\
\widehat\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{\widehat H(x)}
 \quad(y\in\widehat R_x),\qquad
\widehat Y_x(y)=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
\widehat V_x&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_x}\widehat Y_x^2,\qquad
\widehat q_x=\frac{p_x\widehat H(x)^2}{\widehat b},\qquad
\widehat\nu=\sum_x\widehat q_x\widehat V_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
All sums over supported atoms use \(T_m\). Hats always designate auxiliary restricted quantities; the canonical full quantities are unchanged. In particular the original law throughout is \(p_x=w_x/Z\), including atoms whose auxiliary row is a singleton.

Introduce the normalized cubic atom sum and normalized restricted reflected sum
\(A_0=K^2\sum_xp_x^3\) and \(A_{\mathcal S}=K^2\widehat b\). These are not the canonical quantity \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\). The central term and Jensen give
$$
\frac14\le A_0\le A_{\mathcal S}\le K^2b(p)\le9A_0.
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed \(|T_m|\le2K\), and \(H_p(x)\le3p_x\) because \(s_j\le1\).

For each parity \(\eta\in B_m\), let \(r_\eta=p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^m)\). Define the nonnegative parity and restricted reflected deviations, together with the selected convexity slack, by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=K\sum_{\eta\in B_m}r_\eta^2-1,\qquad
\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x(K\widehat H(x)-1)^2,\\
\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}&=4K\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}s_j(1-s_j)p_j^2,\qquad
\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}=\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}+2\mathcal D+\mathcal T_{\mathcal S},\\
\beta_{\mathcal S}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}\beta_j,\qquad
\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}=\sum_{j\notin\mathcal S}s_j\beta_j
 =K\sum_{j\notin\mathcal S}s_j^2p_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
Here \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\) is a deviation energy, distinct from the score variances \(\widehat V_x\). Nonnegativity of \(\mathcal D\) follows because the \(K\) parity masses sum to one.

Exact expansion of the pair classes gives
$$
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in J_m}s_j^2p_j^2,
\qquad
\widehat\chi=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}s_jp_j^2.
\tag{12}
$$
Expanding the square in \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\) and substituting (12) proves the restricted-row parity-energy identity
$$
A_{\mathcal S}=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}-4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
\tag{13}
$$
Thus deleting a row has a quadratic-ratio loss. This identity does not compare the cubic reflected numerator to the cubic parity numerator.

We next prove a charge for every selected row, including ratios arbitrarily near one:
$$
\beta_{\mathcal S}\le1024\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}.
\tag{14}
$$
For selected rows with \(s_j\le7/8\), \(4s_j(1-s_j)\ge s_j/2\), so their total \(\beta_j\) is at most \(2\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}\).

For a selected row with \(s=s_j>7/8\), put \(\xi=Kp_j\), \(\upsilon=Kp_{u_j}\), \(\omega=Kp_{v_j}\), and \(r=\upsilon+\omega=Kr_{u_j}\), where the parity subscript on the last expression means the parity of \(u_j\). Then \(\upsilon\omega=s^2\xi^2\). Write
$$
v_j^{\mathrm{dev}}=\xi((1+2s)\xi-1)^2,\qquad
v_v^{\mathrm{dev}}=\omega(\omega-1)^2,\qquad
d_j^{\mathrm{dev}}=(r-1)^2.
\tag{15}
$$
These are \(K\) times the contributions of the center, its singleton spike endpoint, and its source parity to the corresponding energies. We claim
$$
s\xi^2\le1024\bigl(v_j^{\mathrm{dev}}+v_v^{\mathrm{dev}}+d_j^{\mathrm{dev}}\bigr).
\tag{16}
$$
If \(\xi\ge4/9\), then \((1+2s)\xi-1\ge\xi/2\), since \(1+2s\ge11/4\). Hence \(v_j^{\mathrm{dev}}\ge\xi^3/4\ge\xi^2/9\), which proves (16). If \(\xi\le1/4\), then \((1+2s)\xi\le3/4\), so \(v_j^{\mathrm{dev}}\ge\xi/16\) and \(s\xi^2\le\xi^2\le4v_j^{\mathrm{dev}}\).

It remains that \(1/4<\xi<4/9\), which implies \(\upsilon\omega>49/1024\). Suppose both \(d_j^{\mathrm{dev}}<1/4096\) and \(v_v^{\mathrm{dev}}<1/4096\). The first inequality gives \(r<65/64\). If \(\omega\le1/2\), then \(\omega(\omega-1)^2\ge\omega/4\), so \(\omega<1/1024\) and
\(\upsilon\omega\le r\omega<65/65536<49/1024\), a contradiction. If \(\omega>1/2\), the second inequality implies \(|\omega-1|<1/32\). Thus \(\omega>31/32\), \(\upsilon=r-\omega<3/64\), and \(\omega\le r<65/64\). Consequently
\(\upsilon\omega<195/4096<196/4096=49/1024\), again a contradiction. At least one of these two deviations is therefore at least \(1/4096\). Since \(s\xi^2<16/81<1/4\), this also proves (16).

Sum (16) over the selected high-ratio rows and divide by \(K\). Their centers are distinct Boolean points, their spike endpoints are distinct spikes, and these two sets are disjoint. Their source parities are distinct. Therefore
\(\sum_{j\in\mathcal S:s_j>7/8}\beta_j\le1024(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}+\mathcal D)\).
Combining this with the low-ratio charge yields (14), since
\(1024(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}+\mathcal D)+2\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}\le1024\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\).

We also need a score-price estimate that does not charge every row a fixed amount:
$$
\widehat\nu\le96\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}(1+\rho_j^2)\beta_j
 +1152\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}.
\tag{17}
$$
At a selected row the exact formulas are
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{u_j}&=s_jp_je^{-z_j},\qquad p_{v_j}=s_jp_je^{z_j},\\
\widehat H(j)&=(1+2s_j)p_j,\qquad
\widehat V_j=\frac{2s_jz_j^2}{1+2s_j},\\
p_j\widehat H(j)^2\widehat V_j&=2s_j(1+2s_j)p_j^3z_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Every other score variance is zero. For one selected row again put \(\xi=Kp_j\), \(\upsilon=Kp_{u_j}\), \(\omega=Kp_{v_j}\), and abbreviate \(s,z,\rho\). Its dimensionless numerator is
\(G_j=2s(1+2s)\xi^3z^2\).

First suppose \(\max(\xi,\upsilon,\omega)\le2\). Since \(\upsilon\omega=s^2\xi^2\), the larger endpoint is at most two and the smaller is at least \(s^2\xi^2/2\). Thus
\(|z|\le\rho+\log(2/\xi)\). Also \(\xi\le2\) and
\(\xi\log^2(2/\xi)\le8/e^2<2\), as follows by setting \(t=\log(2/\xi)\ge0\) and maximizing \(2t^2e^{-t}\). Therefore
$$
G_j\le12s\xi^3\bigl(\rho^2+\log^2(2/\xi)\bigr)
 \le24s\xi^2(1+\rho^2).
\tag{19}
$$

For the remaining rows let \(M=\max(\xi,\upsilon,\omega)>2\). For all \(s\in(0,1]\) and real \(z\), one has
$$
z^2\le2\rho^2+4s^3\cosh(3z).
\tag{20}
$$
If \(|z|\le\rho\), this is immediate. Otherwise write \(|z|=\rho+t\) with \(t>0\), use \((\rho+t)^2\le2\rho^2+2t^2\), \(e^{3t}\ge t^2\), and \(s^3\cosh(3z)\ge e^{3t}/2\). This proves (20). Since \(\upsilon^3+\omega^3=2s^3\xi^3\cosh(3z)\), (20) and \(1+2s\le3\) give
$$
G_j\le12s\rho^2\xi^3+12s(\upsilon^3+\omega^3)
 \le12(\xi^3+\upsilon^3+\omega^3)\le36M^3.
\tag{21}
$$
Here \(s\rho^2\le4/e^2<1\), by maximizing \(t^2e^{-t}\).

Choose one of the row's three atoms at which \(Kp_x=M\). Since \(\widehat H(x)\ge p_x\), that atom contributes at least
\(M(M-1)^2\ge M^3/4\) to \(K\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\). Thus (21) is at most 144 times this contribution. Each atom can be chosen by at most two rows: it is a center of at most one row and an endpoint of at most one row. This uses the two distinctness properties separately and permits a Boolean center also to be another row's endpoint. Consequently the total contribution from (21), divided by \(K\), is at most \(288\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\). Dividing (19) by \(K\) gives at most \(24\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}(1+\rho_j^2)\beta_j\). Finally (18) and (10) give
$$
\widehat\nu
=\frac{K^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}G_j}{A_{\mathcal S}}
\le4\left(24\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}(1+\rho_j^2)\beta_j
 +288\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\right),
\tag{22}
$$
which is (17).

For a block with \(A_0<1\), (13)--(14) imply the useful logarithmic gain estimate
$$
\log A_{\mathcal S}\ge\frac1{26}\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}
 +\frac1{26624}\beta_{\mathcal S}-16\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
\tag{23}
$$
To check its constants, Cauchy--Schwarz under \(p\) gives
\(K\sum_xp_x^2\le\sqrt{A_0}<1\), hence \(\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}<1\). By (10) and (13),
\(0\le\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}=A_{\mathcal S}-1+4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}<12\).
Since \(A_{\mathcal S}\ge1/4\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log A_{\mathcal S}
&=\log(1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S})
 -\log\left(1+\frac{4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}}{A_{\mathcal S}}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}{1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}
 -16\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}
\ge\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}{13}-16\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
The nonnegative energy is at least \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\) and, by (14), at least \(\beta_{\mathcal S}/1024\). Its lower bound by half the sum of these two quantities proves (23).

We now choose the auxiliary rows across all factors. Compute \(K_\ell=2^{m_\ell}\) and \(A_{0,\ell}=K_\ell^2\sum_xp_{\ell,x}^3\) from the full original probabilities. If \(A_{0,\ell}\ge1\), use only singleton rows in that factor. Then \(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell=A_{0,\ell}\ge1\) and \(\widehat\nu_\ell=0\).

For all remaining factors, form bands of their nontrivial rows by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_k&=\lbrace(\ell,j):A_{0,\ell}<1,\ j\in J_{m_\ell},
 \ 2^{-k-1}<s_{\ell,j}\le2^{-k}\rbrace,\\
\beta_k&=\sum_{(\ell,j)\in\mathcal B_k}K_\ell s_{\ell,j}p_{\ell,j}^2,
\qquad A_k=1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\qquad(k\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
Only finitely many bands contain rows. Select a band exactly when \(\beta_k\ge D_*A_k\), and retain a full row exactly when its band is selected. This determines \(\mathcal S_\ell\) independently as a subset of each factor's centers; bands themselves need not be product factors.

Let \(U=\sum_{\ell:A_{0,\ell}<1}\mathcal V_{\mathcal S_\ell}\). Since \(\rho_{\ell,j}<(k+1)\log2\) and \(s_{\ell,j}\le2^{-k}\) in band \(k\), (17) and (23) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_\ell\log(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell)
&\ge\frac U{26}+\frac1{26624}\sum_{k\text{ selected}}\beta_k
 -16\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k,\\
\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell
&\le1152U+96\sum_{k\text{ selected}}A_k\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
All auxiliary rows, including the choices made for blocks with \(A_{0,\ell}\ge1\), still use all original probabilities in their normalizers.

For completeness we prove the actual product conversion for these restricted rows. For every supported target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), set
\(\widehat R_J=\prod_\ell\widehat R_{\ell,j_\ell}\),
\(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_\ell\widehat\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell}\), and
\(\widehat Y_J(X)=\sum_\ell\widehat Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(X_\ell)\).
Direct factorization and reflection symmetry give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_P(J)&:=\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}
 =\prod_\ell\widehat H_\ell(j_\ell),\\
P_X&=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{\widehat Y_J(X)}
 \quad(X\in\widehat R_J),\\
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J&=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J^2
 =\sigma_J^2:=\sum_\ell\widehat V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The first quantity is a restricted reflected sum; it need not equal the full canonical \(H_P(J)\). The second identity has exactly the original probabilities and original product normalizer from (3).

Every closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) in the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) contains \(J\) and at least one endpoint of each distinct pair \(X,2J-X\) in \(\widehat R_J\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
 \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|\widehat Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$
The factor one half counts each distinct reflected orbit once and uses only half the central atom. The last inequality is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. Take the infimum over all these halfspaces, square, and average with respect to the full original law \(P\). The auxiliary center law \(\bigotimes_\ell\widehat q_\ell\) and (27) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
 \mathbb E_{\bigotimes_\ell\widehat q_\ell}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
The first equality of weights used here is
\(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell)\prod_\ell\widehat q_{\ell,j_\ell}\).
Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz prove the second line, because the expectation of \(\sigma_J^2\) under that auxiliary law is \(\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell\). This conversion remains valid for singleton rows, zero variances, and lower-dimensional supports.

Combining (26) and (29), and using the subadditivity of square roots on nonnegative sums, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(4^NM_2(P))\ge{}&-\log4+\frac U{26}-2\sqrt{1152U}\\
&+\sum_{k\text{ selected}}
 \left(\frac{\beta_k}{26624}-2\sqrt{96A_k\beta_k}\right)
 -16\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
$$
Completing the square gives
\(U/26-2\sqrt{1152U}\ge-29952\).
The definition \(D_*=384(26624)^2\) ensures that every selected summand is nonnegative. Every unselected band has \(\beta_k<D_*A_k\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(4^NM_2(P))
&\ge-\log4-29952-16D_*\sum_{k=0}^\infty
 2^{-k}\bigl(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\bigr)\\
&=-\log4-29952-16D_*\bigl(2+12(\log2)^2\bigr)
 =\log c_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$
The two numerical series are \(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=2\) and \(\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^22^{-k}=12\), obtained by differentiating \(\sum_{k\ge0}t^k=(1-t)^{-1}\) twice for \(|t|<1\) and setting \(t=1/2\). There are only finitely many actual factors and selected bands; the infinite series bounds their total possible loss. This proves the second-moment assertion in (6). Jensen under the original law gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the third-moment assertion.

Finally, for (7) the potential is
\(\phi_\ell(x)=\sum_h\alpha_{\ell,h}\max(0,x_h-1)-\gamma_\ell\cdot x\).
It is convex, as a nonnegative sum of maxima of affine functions plus an affine function. At a reflected pair with selected coordinate \(h=i_{m_\ell}(c)\), the Boolean endpoint and midpoint have zero penalty, while the spike has penalty \(\alpha_{\ell,h}\). The affine terms cancel at the midpoint, giving exactly
\(s_{\ell,j}=e^{-\alpha_{\ell,h}/2}\in(0,1]\).
Thus all fields and all coordinate spectra satisfy (2), without any ratio-spread assumption. Full convex-admissibility for any other table also implies (2) by its application to this midpoint combination. Completeness of each original support and its product follows from (1); no auxiliary row selection alters those supports. Every estimate above retained every original atom and every original normalizer. This proves all assertions.
