## Statement

Fix a real number r_0 >= 0, and put
\(c(r_0):=\min\{e^{-6r_0}/10200,\ e^{-41r_0}/(1+82r_0)\}>0\).
Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let A be a real symmetric positive-definite n by n matrix, let \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), and let R be finite. Write \(Q(x)=x^\top A x\), and define weights on \(\mathbb Z^n\) by \(w_z=e^{-Q(z-a)}\) if \(Q(z-a)\le R\), and \(w_z=0\) otherwise. Assume \(W:=\sum_z w_z>0\) and \(r:=\min_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}Q(z-a)\le r_0\).

For each integer j with \(w_j>0\), use the canonical objects from accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`: \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), and \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\). Logarithms are natural, and the sum counts oriented displacements, including v=0. Then
\[
\max_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z^n:\ w_j>0\\ L\ge0}}
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}
\ \ge\ c(r_0)\,2^{-n}W.
\]
The constant depends only on the fixed bound r_0. No diagonal or product representation of the lattice Gaussian is assumed. For example, if \(A\preceq I/n\), then \(r\le1/4\) for every a, so the displayed inequality holds with the one absolute constant c(1/4), for every real shift and every finite R with W>0.

For any positive integer d, if these weights are the integer-fiber volumes of a convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\), with \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` consequently gives a point \(p^\star\in S\) such that \(h_S(p^\star)\ge(\ell c(r_0)/16)2^{-n}W\), where \(\ell=-\log(1-e^{-1})\). This conclusion has the same order of quantifiers over the point and all containing halfspaces as that accepted fact. The finite table above is realized by an actual convex body for every R>0 and every positive integer d with d>=2R; the single-fiber case R=0 is realizable as well.

## Proof

Positive definiteness implies that the minimum defining r is attained and that \(K:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:Q(z-a)\le R\}\) is finite. The assumption W>0 gives \(0\le r\le R\) and \(K\ne\varnothing\). For every supported reflected pair, the quadratic identity
\(Q(j+v-a)+Q(j-v-a)=2Q(j-a)+2Q(v)\)
gives exactly \(\Delta_j(v)=2Q(v)\). Thus these curvatures have the canonical normalization, even though the auxiliary Gaussian parameters used below will change. For each of the finitely many j in K the cutoff sum has finitely many jumps, so its quotient by 1+L attains its maximum at a curvature value; consequently the joint maximum in the statement exists.

First we record a parity estimate that handles a bounded energy range. Put N=|K|, and let \(N_b\) count the members of K in parity class \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\). If x,y are in the same parity class, then \(j=(x+y)/2\) and \(v=(x-y)/2\) are integer vectors. Convexity of the ellipsoid gives j in K, and v belongs to \(\mathcal U_j\). This is a bijection between such ordered pairs (x,y) and the pairs (j,v) with j in K and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{j\in K}|\mathcal U_j|
=\sum_b N_b^2\ge 2^{-n}N^2.
\]
There is therefore a j in K with \(|\mathcal U_j|\ge2^{-n}N\). For every supported pair at that j, its two endpoint energies are at most R, so \(Q(j-a)+Q(v)\le R\), and hence \(\Delta_j(v)\le2(R-r)\). Also \(\alpha(v)\ge e^{-R}\), while \(W\le Ne^{-r}\). Taking the cutoff \(L=2(R-r)\) proves
\[
\max_{j,L}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}
\ge \frac{e^{-(R-r)}}{1+2(R-r)}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, if \(R\le42r\), then \(0\le R-r\le41r\le41r_0\), and (1) gives the desired bound with constant \(e^{-41r_0}/(1+82r_0)\). This includes R=r=0.

We next derive the Gaussian estimates needed for the remaining case. For \(t>0\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^n\), define \(\theta_t(b):=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-tQ(v-b)}\), and abbreviate \(\theta_t:=\theta_t(0)\). Periodizing the function \(x\mapsto e^{-tQ(x)}\) gives
\[
\theta_t(b)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{t^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}
e^{-\pi^2 k^\top A^{-1}k/t}\cos(2\pi k^\top b).
\tag{2}
\]
Here is a direct justification of the formula and the operations with it. The periodization and its derivatives converge uniformly for b in a fundamental cube and for t in a compact subinterval of (0,infinity), since a positive-definite quadratic form bounds a positive multiple of the squared Euclidean norm. Its kth Fourier coefficient is the integral of \(e^{-tQ(x)}e^{2\pi i k^\top x}\) over \(\mathbb R^n\). Diagonalizing A in this integral and evaluating the one-dimensional Gaussian Fourier integrals yields \(\pi^{n/2}t^{-n/2}(\det A)^{-1/2}e^{-\pi^2 k^\top A^{-1}k/t}\). The one-dimensional evaluation follows, for example, by differentiating the Fourier integral in its frequency and integrating by parts, obtaining the equation \(I'(\xi)=-2\pi^2\xi I(\xi)/(t\lambda)\), with \(I(0)=\sqrt{\pi/(t\lambda)}\) for an eigenvalue lambda of A. These Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable. Their Fourier series equals the smooth periodization, and pairing k with -k gives (2). The same quadratic decay justifies termwise differentiation in t.

All coefficients in (2) are positive. Thus \(\theta_t(b)\le\theta_t\) for every b. Moreover, for \(\beta\ge1\), replacing t by \(\beta t\) in (2) at b=0 enlarges every exponential in the dual sum, and therefore \(\theta_{\beta t}\ge\beta^{-n/2}\theta_t\). Finally, differentiating (2) at b=0 gives
\[
\frac{\sum_v Q(v)e^{-tQ(v)}}{\theta_t}
=\frac{n}{2t}
-\frac{\pi^2}{t^2}
\frac{\sum_k (k^\top A^{-1}k)e^{-\pi^2 k^\top A^{-1}k/t}}
{\sum_k e^{-\pi^2 k^\top A^{-1}k/t}}
\le\frac{n}{2t}.
\tag{3}
\]
These estimates allow arbitrary coupling: the diagonalization occurs inside a Euclidean Fourier integral and imposes no restriction on the lattice coordinates.

Assume now \(R>42r\), and choose an integer minimizer j with \(Q(j-a)=r\). Put \(u=j-a\). The inner product defined by A and the elementary inequality \(2xy\le x^2/5+5y^2\) give, for every integer v,
\[
Q(u\pm v)\le r+Q(v)+2\sqrt{rQ(v)}
\le6r+\frac65Q(v).
\tag{4}
\]
Set \(\beta=6/5\), \(s=\max\{1,51n/[100(R-6r)]\}\), and \(T=51n/(100\beta s)\). These are finite and positive, and they satisfy \(T\le(R-6r)/\beta\) and \(2T\le17n/20\). Every v with \(Q(v)\le T\) therefore has both reflected endpoints in K by (4). At this j its reflected weight satisfies
\(\alpha(v)=e^{-r-Q(v)-2|u^\top A v|}\ge e^{-6r}e^{-\beta Q(v)}\),
and its curvature is at most 2T. Since s>=1, this gives
\[
B_j^\Delta(2T)
\ge e^{-6r}\sum_{Q(v)\le T}e^{-\beta sQ(v)}.
\tag{5}
\]
Under the probability distribution assigning mass \(e^{-\beta sQ(v)}/\theta_{\beta s}\) to v, (3) bounds the mean of Q(v) by \(n/(2\beta s)\). Markov's inequality and \(\beta sT=51n/100\) show that the mass with \(Q(v)>T\) is at most 50/51. Thus the sum in (5) is at least \(\theta_{\beta s}/51\), and Gaussian scaling gives
\(B_j^\Delta(2T)\ge e^{-6r}\beta^{-n/2}\theta_s/51\).

For the original finite table, s>=1 and \(Q(z-a)\le R\) on K imply
\[
W\le e^{(s-1)R}\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(z-a)}
=e^{(s-1)R}\theta_s(a)
\le e^{(s-1)R}\theta_s.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining these inequalities yields
\(B_j^\Delta(2T)\ge(e^{-6r}/51)\beta^{-n/2}e^{-(s-1)R}W\).
If s=1 then \((s-1)R=0\). Otherwise, the condition \(R>42r\) gives \(R/(R-6r)\le7/6\), and hence
\((s-1)R\le51nR/[100(R-6r)]\le119n/200\).
The elementary estimates \(\log(6/5)\le137/750\) and \(\log2\ge56/81\) imply
\(\tfrac12\log(6/5)+119/200\le\log2-1/200\).
For completeness, the first logarithmic estimate follows by integrating \(1/(1+x)\le1-x+x^2\) from 0 to 1/5. The second follows from \(\log2=2\int_0^{1/3}(1-t^2)^{-1}\,dt\ge2\int_0^{1/3}(1+t^2)\,dt\). The rational bounds leave margin \(407/81000>1/200\) for the difference \(\log2-\tfrac12\log(6/5)-119/200\).

Consequently
\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(2T)}{1+2T}
\ge \frac{e^{-6r}}{51}\,2^{-n}W
\frac{e^{n/200}}{1+17n/20}
\ge \frac{e^{-6r_0}}{10200}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{7}
\]
The final inequality uses \(e^{n/200}\ge1+n/200\ge(1+17n/20)/200\) and r<=r_0. Equations (1) and (7) cover every finite R with W>0 and prove the stated constant c(r_0).

If \(A\preceq I/n\), round each coordinate of a to an integer to obtain some j with \(\|j-a\|_2^2\le n/4\). Then \(Q(j-a)\le1/4\), so the minimum r is at most 1/4. This proves the asserted all-shift matrix subcase. More intrinsically, the same theorem works for all shifts whenever every point of \(\mathbb R^n\) has an integer point at Q-distance squared at most r_0; the proof requires no restriction on off-diagonal entries beyond positive definiteness and this distance condition.

We finish by verifying the geometric realization and consumer. Let R>0, let d be a positive integer with d>=2R, and let \(B_d\) denote the Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb R^d\). On the ellipsoid \(E:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:Q(z-a)\le R\}\), define
\(\rho(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)^{-1/d}e^{-Q(z-a)/d}\),
and put \(C:=\{(z,x):z\in E,\ \|x\|_2\le\rho(z)\}\).
For \(u=z-a\) and any direction h, the second directional derivative of the unscaled factor \(e^{-Q(u)/d}\) is
\[
e^{-Q(u)/d}\left(\frac{4(h^\top Au)^2}{d^2}
-\frac{2h^\top Ah}{d}\right)
\le e^{-Q(u)/d}\left(\frac{4R}{d^2}-\frac2d\right)h^\top Ah
\le0.
\]
The first inequality is the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the A inner product. Thus rho is concave on the convex set E. If two points satisfy the defining ball-fiber inequalities, the norm triangle inequality followed by concavity of rho shows that their convex combination satisfies them as well. Therefore C is convex. It is compact because E is compact and rho is continuous and bounded, and it has nonempty interior because R>0 and rho(a)>0. It is consequently a convex body. Its fiber at any z in E is a ball of volume \(\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)\rho(z)^d=e^{-Q(z-a)}\), and its other fibers are empty. This realizes the exact finite lattice table, including the positive boundary fibers. If R=0 and W>0, then a is an integer vector and the table has just one positive weight, equal to 1. A base cube of side length 1/2 centered at a, times a d-dimensional ball of volume 1, realizes that table as a convex body.

For any convex body with this table, the quantities w_z, W, alpha, \(\mathcal U_j\), \(\Delta_j\), and \(B_j^\Delta\) are exactly those of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; no change of their normalization is made. Its cutoff conclusion gives a single point selected before the halfspace with depth at least \((\ell/16)\max_{j,L}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\). Applying the inequality just proved yields \(h_S(p^\star)\ge(\ell c(r_0)/16)2^{-n}W\). Taking \(c_0=\min\{\ell c(r_0)/16,1/(2e)\}\) gives a constant in (0,1/e) for this subclass, independent of n, d, A, a, and R once r_0 is fixed. The proof imposes r<=r_0 throughout and does not establish the unrestricted all-shift claim when these nearest lattice energies grow without bound.
