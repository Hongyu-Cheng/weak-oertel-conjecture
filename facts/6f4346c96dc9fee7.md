## Statement

On the actual body $C=[-1,1]_z\times[-1,1]_x\times[-1,1]_y$ with one integer coordinate, take sources $(-1,-8/13,0)$ and $(1,8/13,0)$, both with flat base support slopes. Their unique optimal source-centered projective denominators on their own squares are $L_\pm^{\rm own}=(13/9)(1\mp x/2)$, with the minus source using $1+x/2$. With zero base shifts the joint physical speed on every old top facet is $W_z(x)=\kappa/(1-|x|/2)^2$, where $\kappa=81/169$. Take endpoint marginals with their own projective speed densities on $x\in[-1,-3/4]$ and $x\in[3/4,1]$, respectively. Each is dominated by the actual endpoint speed and has mass $4\kappa/5$. Every midpoint coupling is on the old middle top facet with $x\in I=[-1/8,1/8]$, and reflection gives a normal-preserving coupling. The actual middle capacity on $I$ is $4\kappa/15$, so every domination factor is at least $3$.

More generally keep the sources, flat slopes and marginals and extend the two denominators to $(13/9)\ell_\pm$ on $C$, with $\ell_-(z,x)=1+x/2+b_-(z+1)$ and $\ell_+(z,x)=1-x/2+b_+(z-1)$. For any pair of extensions positive on $C$, the capacity on $I$ is strictly less than $16\kappa/33$, forcing every domination factor to exceed $33/20$. If both denominators $(13/9)\ell_\pm$ are at least $1/3$ on $C$, the capacity on $I$ is at most $(2704/7515)\kappa$, forcing every domination factor to be at least $1503/676>2$.

## Proof

In dimension two the centered projective-volume objective at an interior source $Y$ is $\int_K(1+a\cdot(X-Y))^{-3}\,dX$, where $K=[-1,1]^2$ and the denominator is positive on $K$. This is the volume of the projective image of $K-Y$ under $v\mapsto v/(1+a\cdot v)$ by its Jacobian determinant $(1+a\cdot v)^{-3}$. The objective is strictly convex on the positive chart domain, because $t^{-3}$ is strictly convex and different parameters give different affine arguments off a null line. For the minus source, $(13/9)(1+x/2)=1+(13/18)(x+8/13)$. Direct integration gives
$$
\int_{-1}^1(1+x/2)^{-4}\,dx=416/81,\qquad
\int_{-1}^1 x(1+x/2)^{-4}\,dx=-256/81 .
$$
The ratio is $-8/13$ and the $y$-mean vanishes by symmetry. Differentiating under the integral at this positive denominator gives zero gradient, so strict convexity makes it the unique minimum. Reflect in $x=0$ for the plus source.

To realize the fields, use fact 84cd8a85417b0d7a with the constant-in-$z$ majorant $(u,z)\mapsto h_K(u)$ and field $V_i(u,z,X)=h_{K-Y_i}(u)L_i(z,X)^{-2}$ for each source $i$. The source is interior so its support height is positive. On $C$ the denominators are positive affine, so the fields are jointly continuous, nonnegative and convex in $(z,X)$ for each $u$. All majorants are in contact, and the top facet has unique normal $(0,1)$ away from its endpoints with $h_{K-Y_i}(0,1)=1$. Therefore the physical inward speed there is the maximum of the two inverse squares, using arclength as its capacity measure. For zero shifts it is the displayed $W_z$. For the extensions the speed is $\kappa\max(\ell_-^{-2},\ell_+^{-2})$ on the same facets.

The endpoint base labels $-1,1$ have the same parity, and the marginal supports force every midpoint to have label zero and $x\in I$. Reflection preserves their common normal and sends the tangential midpoint to zero. The minus marginal has mass $\kappa\int_{-1}^{-3/4}(1+x/2)^{-2}\,dx=4\kappa/5$, as does the reflected plus marginal. With zero shifts the middle capacity is $2\kappa\int_0^{1/8}(1-x/2)^{-2}\,dx=4\kappa/15$. Domination by a factor times the target capacity forces that factor to be at least the ratio of the fixed mass to the target capacity on $I$.

For positive extensions evaluate $\ell_-$ at $(z,x)=(1,-1)$ and $\ell_+$ at $(-1,1)$. This gives $b_->-1/4$ and $b_+<1/4$, so the middle speed is pointwise below $\kappa/(3/4-|x|/2)^2$. Its integral on $I$ is bounded strictly by $4\kappa[(3/4-1/16)^{-1}-(3/4)^{-1}]=16\kappa/33$. The source denominators and their marginal domination are unchanged at their own bases. With the normalized floor $1/3$ one has $\ell_\pm\ge3/13$ at the same outer vertices, yielding $b_-\ge-7/52$ and $b_+\le7/52$. The speed is then bounded by $\kappa/(45/52-|x|/2)^2$, whose integral is $4\kappa[(45/52-1/16)^{-1}-(45/52)^{-1}]=(2704/7515)\kappa$. The two mass-to-capacity ratios give the asserted factors.
