## Statement

Retain the complete common canonical setting and notation of accepted fact 6e6a3b541bc64f05. In particular,

\[
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{1}
\]

the positive whole-label family \(\Gamma\) has total mass

\[
 \Theta_{\mathcal P}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\theta_\sigma
 >\frac{1143}{12800}Q,
\tag{2}
\]

and the five atomic measures
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}},\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}},\Xi_\square,
\Xi_{\mathrm{blk}},\Xi_{\mathrm{cp}}\) satisfy

\[
 |\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}|
 =|\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}|
 =|\Xi_\square|
 =\Theta_{\mathcal P},
 \qquad
 |\Xi_{\mathrm{blk}}|
 =|\Xi_{\mathrm{cp}}|
 =\frac{\Theta_{\mathcal P}}4,
\tag{3}
\]

with the equal full first moments asserted there. The packet family representing
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}+\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}\) in its physical source fibers has total pointwise density at most \(17/32\), and
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{blk}}+\Xi_{\mathrm{cp}}\) is a subfamily of the same ledger.

Define

\[
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}
 :=\frac{32\gamma_d}{17}
 \left(\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}+\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}\right),
 \qquad
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{cp}}
 :=\frac{32\gamma_d}{17}
 \left(\Xi_{\mathrm{blk}}+\Xi_{\mathrm{cp}}\right).
\tag{4}
\]

Then both are carried by
\(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}(H)\leq\mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{cp}}(H)\leq\mu_C(H).}
\tag{5}
\]

Their exact masses and dimension-free lower bounds are

\[
 \boxed{
 |\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}|
 =\frac{64\gamma_d}{17}\Theta_{\mathcal P}
 >\frac{1143}{9350}Q
 >\frac Q9,}
\tag{6}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 |\Sigma_{\mathrm{cp}}|
 =\frac{16\gamma_d}{17}\Theta_{\mathcal P}
 >\frac{1143}{37400}Q
 >\frac{3Q}{100}.}
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\rho_{\mathrm{src}},r_{\mathrm{src}}\) and
\(\rho_{\mathrm{cp}},r_{\mathrm{cp}}\) be exactly the intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks defined in 6e6a3b541bc64f05. Thus positive rescaling does not change these ranks, and the coupled parent-child identity there still gives

\[
 r_{\mathrm{cp}}
 =
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal A^+)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_z-s(z):z\in\mathcal A^+\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The dominated-measure theorem of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 and (5) give

\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \frac{64\gamma_d\Theta_{\mathcal P}}{17}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}}{r_{\mathrm{src}}+1},
 \qquad
 D\geq
 \frac{16\gamma_d\Theta_{\mathcal P}}{17}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{cp}}}}{r_{\mathrm{cp}}+1}.}
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently the hard inequality in (1) forces

\[
 \boxed{
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}(r_{\mathrm{src}}+1)
 >\frac{2286}{187}\,2^n,
 \qquad
 r_{\mathrm{src}}\geq12,}
\tag{10}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{cp}}}(r_{\mathrm{cp}}+1)
 >\frac{1143}{374}\,2^n,
 \qquad
 r_{\mathrm{cp}}\geq3.}
\tag{11}
\]

There is also an exact convex-order structure which does not use the rank bounds. For equal-mass finite measures write
\(\mu\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}\nu\) when
\(\int\Phi\,d\mu\leq\int\Phi\,d\nu\) for every finite-valued convex function \(\Phi\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Xi_\square
 \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}
 \Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}
 \preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}
 \Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Both comparisons are realized by explicit finite martingale kernels. In particular,

\[
 \operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\Xi_\square)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}})
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}).
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, there is a measurable mixed density \(F^{\mathrm{rep}}\) on the physical fibers, relative to \(\mu_C\), such that

\[
 0\leq F^{\mathrm{rep}}\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int F^{\mathrm{rep}}\,d\mu_C
 =\frac{32}{17}\Theta_{\mathcal P},
\tag{14}
\]

and

\[
 \int pF^{\mathrm{rep}}(p)\,d\mu_C(p)
 =\frac{32}{17}
 \int p\,d\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}(p)
 =\frac{32}{17}
 \int p\,d\Xi_\square(p).
\tag{15}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
 \bar p:=\frac1{\Theta_{\mathcal P}}
 \int p\,d\Xi_\square(p),
\tag{16}
\]

then \(\bar p\) is the barycenter of an actual mixed subdensity of mass
\(32\Theta_{\mathcal P}/17\). More generally, every finite-valued nonnegative convex function \(\Phi\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{32}{17}\int\Phi\,d\Xi_\square
 \leq
 \frac{32}{17}\int\Phi\,d\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}
 \leq
 \int\Phi\,d\mu_C.}
\tag{17}
\]

No targetwise disintegration of \(F^{\mathrm{rep}}\), no integrality of the base coordinate of \(\bar p\), and no affine selector for its fiberwise barycenters is asserted.

## Proof

Scale every representing density in the one physical source ledger of accepted fact 6e6a3b541bc64f05 by \(32/17\). The total pointwise density in every source fiber is then at most

\[
 \frac{32}{17}\frac{17}{32}=1.
\tag{18}
\]

Every packet mass and first moment is scaled by the same factor, while every packet barycenter is unchanged. The blocker-complement-parent packet family is a literal subfamily, so (18) holds for it as well.

Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\) and one physical source fiber \(C_y\). From the scaled packet family for
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}+\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}\), retain exactly the packets whose atoms \((y,p_j)\) lie in \(H\), and let \(g_{y,H}\) be the sum of their representing densities. By (18),
\(0\leq g_{y,H}\leq1\). If its mass \(T_{y,H}\) is positive, its barycenter is a convex combination of the selected points \(p_j\), hence belongs to the convex fiber section \(H_y\). It also belongs to
\(\mathsf M_{T_{y,H}}(C_y)\), by the definition of a metronoid. Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d therefore gives

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \geq\gamma_dT_{y,H}
\tag{19}
\]

when \(H_y\) is a proper closed halfspace. If \(H_y=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from
\(T_{y,H}\leq a_y\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\); an empty section selects no atom. Summing (19) over the distinct integer source bases proves the first inequality in (5). Restricting the argument to the blocker-complement-parent subledger proves the second.

Equations (3)-(4) give the two exact masses in (6)-(7). The elementary inequalities

\[
 \gamma_d>\frac1{\mathrm e}>\frac4{11}
\tag{20}
\]

hold for every positive integer \(d\): the first follows from
\(d\log(1+1/d)<1\), and the second follows, for example, from
\(\mathrm e<11/4\). Combining (2), (4), and (20) gives

\[
 \frac{64\gamma_d}{17}\Theta_{\mathcal P}
 >\frac{64}{17}\frac4{11}\frac{1143}{12800}Q
 =\frac{1143}{9350}Q,
\tag{21}
\]

and

\[
 \frac{16\gamma_d}{17}\Theta_{\mathcal P}
 >\frac{16}{17}\frac4{11}\frac{1143}{12800}Q
 =\frac{1143}{37400}Q.
\tag{22}
\]

The final comparisons in (6)-(7) are the exact integer inequalities
\(1143\cdot9>9350\) and
\(1143\cdot100>3\cdot37400\).

Positive rescaling leaves both the affine support and the intrinsic residual ranks unchanged. Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 to the two measures in (4), use (5), and multiply its lower bounds for \(h_{\max}(C)\) by \(2^n\). This proves (9). Comparing the first inequality in (9) with \(D<Q/100\) gives

\[
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}(r_{\mathrm{src}}+1)
 >\frac{6400\gamma_d\Theta_{\mathcal P}}{17Q}\,2^n
 >\frac{2286}{187}\,2^n,
\tag{23}
\]

where the last inequality uses
\(\gamma_d\Theta_{\mathcal P}/Q>1143/35200\). Since
\(\rho_{\mathrm{src}}\leq n\) and the rank is integral, (23) gives
\(r_{\mathrm{src}}+1>2286/187>12\), hence
\(r_{\mathrm{src}}\geq12\). The second inequality in (9) similarly gives

\[
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{cp}}}(r_{\mathrm{cp}}+1)
 >\frac{1600\gamma_d\Theta_{\mathcal P}}{17Q}\,2^n
 >\frac{1143}{374}\,2^n.
\tag{24}
\]

Thus \(r_{\mathrm{cp}}+1>1143/374>3\), proving
\(r_{\mathrm{cp}}\geq3\). Identity (8) is exactly the accepted parent-child rank identity, with unchanged support under positive rescaling.

We prove the convex-order chain. For one label \(\sigma\), its square atom is the average of its four tagged occurrence atoms in the full lifted space:

\[
 \widehat r_\sigma^c
 =\frac14\sum_{o=1}^4
 \widehat c_{y_{\sigma,o}}.
\tag{25}
\]

Jensen's inequality, multiplied by \(\theta_\sigma\) and summed over labels, proves
\(\Xi_\square\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}\). This is an explicit martingale kernel: a labelled square atom is sent with probability \(1/4\) to each of its four tagged occurrences, with repeated tags retained.

For a blocker \(m\in\mathcal B^+\), accepted fact 6e6a3b541bc64f05 gives

\[
 \alpha_ma_m\widehat c_m
 =\alpha_mV_m\widehat q_m
 +\frac{\alpha_m}{2}
 \sum_{e=\{u,v\}:m_e=m}b_e
 \left(\widehat c_u+\widehat c_v\right).
\tag{26}
\]

The measure on the right of (26) has mass
\(\alpha_m(V_m+\lambda_m)=\alpha_ma_m\). Jensen therefore says that replacing the atom
\(\alpha_ma_m\delta_{\widehat c_m}\) by that measure can only increase the integral of a convex function. These are exactly the local replacements which turn
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{occ}}\) into \(\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}\); every nonchosen occurrence is fixed. This proves the second comparison in (12). To make the martingale kernel labelwise, distribute the right side of (26) among the chosen blocker occurrences at \(m\) in the proportions
\(\theta_\sigma/\ell_m\). Their sum is the displayed replacement measure, and every conditional barycenter remains \(\widehat c_m\).

Equation (25) puts every square atom in the convex hull of the occurrence support. Equation (26) puts every removed occurrence atom in the convex hull of the replacement support, while every nonremoved atom remains present. This proves (13).

Let \(F^{\mathrm{rep}}\) be the sum of the packet densities representing
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}\), after the common scaling \(32/17\). It is a subfamily of (18), so \(0\leq F^{\mathrm{rep}}\leq1\). Summing its packet masses and full first moments proves (14) and the first equality in (15); the second is the accepted equal-moment identity. This proves the exact mixed-density representation.

Finally let \(\Phi\) be finite-valued, nonnegative, and convex. For every scaled packet with mass \(t_j\), barycenter \(p_j\), and density \(f_j\), Jensen gives

\[
 t_j\Phi(p_j)\leq\int\Phi(x)f_j(x)\,dx.
\tag{27}
\]

Sum (27) over the scaled replacement packets and physical source fibers. Their total density is at most one, so nonnegativity of \(\Phi\) gives

\[
 \frac{32}{17}\int\Phi\,d\Xi_{\mathrm{rep}}
 \leq\int\Phi\,d\mu_C.
\tag{28}
\]

The first inequality in (17) follows from (12). Equations (14)-(17) are global statements relative to the mixed measure: they do not assign the density to the physical target fiber of each square label. This is precisely why they do not, without an additional target-local kernel or affine-selector theorem, remove the residual denominators in (9). All asserted conclusions are now proved.
