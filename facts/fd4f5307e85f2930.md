## Statement

Let \(m,L\ge1\) be integers, let \(k=m+L\), \(N=k+1\), and put
$$
\mathcal Q=\left\lbrace(b,i):b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\ 1\le i\le m,\ b_i=0\right\rbrace.
$$
For every \(q\in\mathcal Q\), choose an arbitrary nonempty group \(G_q\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), and put \(n_q=|G_q|\). There is no restriction on overlap or on the numbers \(n_q\). Write points as \((y,t)\), with active coordinates \(y\in\mathbb R^m\) and neutral coordinates \(t\in\mathbb R^L\). For \(q=(b,i)\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_q=\bigl\lbrace&(b+2e_i,t):\text{exactly one neutral coordinate equals two,}\\
&\text{its index lies in }G_q,\text{ and all other neutral coordinates are binary}\bigr\rbrace,
\qquad E=\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal Q}E_q.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the active coordinate unit vector, and binary means belonging to \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\). Suppose that
$$
\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
$$
that every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two, and that its points with exactly two coordinates equal to two are exactly \(E\). The actual points with at most one two are otherwise arbitrary, subject to these hypotheses.

Choose arbitrary positive activities \(\rho_i\) and \(a_h\), and use the original affine law
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,\\
w_{(y,t)}&=\prod_{i=1}^m\rho_i^{y_i}\prod_{h=1}^L a_h^{t_h}
=\exp(-\gamma\cdot y-\eta\cdot t),\\
\gamma_i&=-\log\rho_i,\qquad \eta_h=-\log a_h,\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) as an abbreviation. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
\qquad T(p)=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum counts ordered pairs and counts the diagonal once. Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal. The meanings of \(k,N,D,A,w,Z,p,\tau_p,M_3,s_p,T\) are exactly those in accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7, with its field \(\beta=(\gamma,\eta)\).

If all neutral activities belong to \([1/2,2]\), then, for all active activities,
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-110}8^{-N},\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-110}8^{-N}.
\tag{1}
$$
More generally, (1) holds for arbitrary positive neutral activities provided
$$
\text{for every }q\in\mathcal Q\text{ there is }h\in G_q\text{ with }a_h\in[1/2,2].
\tag{2}
$$
In particular the support obtained by taking
\(D=\operatorname{conv}(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\cup E)\cap\mathbb Z^k\)
always has the required support geometry, including when some \(n_q=1\); thus both assertions apply to that construction under their respective field hypotheses.

## Proof

We first establish the support, restriction, and reflection facts needed below.
The actual one-step core is
$$
D_1=\left\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace j:x_j=2\rbrace\le1\right\rbrace.
$$
It is nonempty and complete. Indeed, an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) belongs to \(D\). If two of its coordinates were two, every member with positive coefficient in a convex representation by points of \(D_1\) would have both coordinates equal to two: all coordinates lie in \([0,2]\), and their average can equal two only when every positive-coefficient value is two. That is impossible for a point of \(D_1\). Hence the integer point belongs to \(D_1\).

For \(q=(b,i)\), define the actual subsets
$$
F_q=\left\lbrace(b+2e_i,t):t\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L,
\ \sum_{h\in G_q}t_h\ge2\right\rbrace,
\qquad C_q=E_q\cup F_q.
\tag{3}
$$
Every point of \(F_q\) is in \(D\): choose two of its private one-coordinates and replace them by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\) to obtain two points of \(E_q\) with that midpoint. Completeness puts the midpoint in \(D\). For a singleton group \(F_q\) is empty. The sets \(C_q\) are pairwise disjoint, since the active vector \(b+2e_i\) determines its label. Their union contains \(E\). They need not contain every actual point with an active two; additional one-step points are allowed.

For an integer \(n\ge2\), write \(S_n\) for the private support \(S_\ell\) of accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 at \(\ell=n\). Explicitly, it consists of all ternary vectors with exactly one two, together with the Boolean vectors having at least two ones. Define additionally \(S_1=\lbrace2\rbrace\). After a permutation of neutral coordinates placing \(G_q\) first, the set \(C_q\) is the affine image of
$$
R_q=S_{n_q}\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{L-n_q}.
\tag{4}
$$
The affine map \(\Phi_q\) fixes the active vector to \(b+2e_i\) and restores the neutral coordinate order. The support in (4) is complete for \(n_q\ge2\) by accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7, and for \(n_q=1\) because it is a fixed coordinate times a Boolean cube.

Here is the probability and embedding comparison used for these subsets and for later sections. Suppose \(R\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), \(\Phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k\) is an injective affine map with \(\Phi(R)\subseteq D\), and the original probabilities on its image are \(\mu\pi_u\), where \(\pi\) is normalized on \(R\) and \(\mu\) is the original mass of the image. Define the residual cubics on \(R\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{d+1}\). A containing original halfspace pulls back to a containing residual halfspace, using zero coefficient on the last residual coordinate. If the restricted normal is zero, its pullback is the whole residual space, since it contains the center; its conditional mass is then one, which is at least the residual depth. Thus
$$
\tau_p((\Phi(u),0))\ge\mu\tau_\pi((u,0)).
\tag{5}
$$
An ordered reflected residual pair maps to an original reflected pair, and its minimum probability is multiplied by exactly \(\mu\). Consequently restriction to one subset gives both cubic lower bounds with the factor \(\mu^4\). For several pairwise disjoint image sets one may sum these lower bounds over their centers. This argument includes coordinate permutations, fixed coordinates, restrictions to sections, and supports of lower affine rank. A nonempty support on zero residual coordinates is a singleton and has both cubics one; it needs no positive-dimensional predecessor theorem.

For every finite positive law, distinct unordered non-diagonal reflected pairs about a center have disjoint endpoints, none equal to the center. Every containing closed halfspace contains at least one endpoint of each pair, since it contains their midpoint. Hence
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+
\frac12\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y)
=\frac{p_c+s_p(c)}2\ge\frac{s_p(c)}2,
\qquad M_3(p)\ge\frac18T(p).
\tag{6}
$$
Also \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c\) and \(s_p(c)\ge p_c\). For a Boolean product law with arbitrary positive activities \(u_h\), convexity of the fourth power gives
$$
\sum_v\left(\prod_h\frac{u_h^{v_h}}{1+u_h}\right)^4
=\prod_h\frac{1+u_h^4}{(1+u_h)^4}\ge8^{-d},
\tag{7}
$$
where \(d\) is the number of Boolean coordinates. Empty products are one. Indeed, \((1+u)^4\le8(1+u^4)\).

We now assume that all \(a_h\in[1/2,2]\). Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_q&=\prod_{j=1}^m\rho_j^{b_j+2\mathbf1_{j=i}}\quad(q=(b,i)),\\
B_t&=\prod_{h=1}^L(1+a_h),\qquad
B_y=\prod_{i=1}^m(1+\rho_i),\qquad
V_y=\prod_{i=1}^m(1+\rho_i^{2/5}),\\
\Sigma_\rho&=\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{\rho_i^2}{1+\rho_i},\qquad
\theta_h=\frac{a_h^2}{1+a_h}\in[1/6,4/3].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The asserted interval for \(\theta_h\) follows because the derivative of \(a^2/(1+a)\) is \(a(2+a)/(1+a)^2>0\).
For each label, let \(Z_q^{\mathrm{res}}\) be the sum of its neutral monomial weights over the support (4), with the original neutral activities in the permuted order. Let \(\pi_q\) be those weights divided by \(Z_q^{\mathrm{res}}\). Then
$$
\lambda_q:=p(C_q)=\frac{c_q Z_q^{\mathrm{res}}}{Z},
\qquad p_{\Phi_q(u)}=\lambda_q\pi_q(u),
\qquad \sum_q\lambda_q\ge p(E).
\tag{9}
$$
The permutation, field and normalization map to accepted fact 128f3b41bb80c5e7 are exact: its \(\ell=n_q\), \(f=L-n_q\), \(N=L+1\), its private activities are the \(a_h\) for \(h\in G_q\), and its free activities are the other \(a_h\). Thus, for \(n_q\ge2\), that accepted fact and (5) give the applicable conditional bounds
$$
M_3(\pi_q),\ T(\pi_q)\ge2^{-38}n_q^2 8^{-(L+1)}.
\tag{10}
$$
When \(n_q=1\), the private coordinate is fixed to two and \(\pi_q\) is a Boolean product law on the other \(L-1\) coordinates. The atom bounds preceding (7) give each cubic at least \(8^{-(L-1)}\), so (10) holds in this case too, including \(L=1\). Since the image center sets are disjoint, (5), (9), and (10) give
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)\ge2^{-38}8^{-(L+1)}
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\lambda_q^4 n_q^2.
\tag{11}
$$

The exact neutral partition formula is
$$
\frac{Z_q^{\mathrm{res}}}{B_t}
=\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h+
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{h\in G_q}U_h\ge2\right),
\tag{12}
$$
where the \(U_h\) are independent Boolean variables with success probabilities \(a_h/(1+a_h)\). The first term counts the unique private two, and the second term counts the Boolean private shadow. For \(n_q\ge2\), the right side is at most \(4n_q/3+1\le2n_q\); for \(n_q=1\), its second term is zero and its first is at most \(4/3\). Therefore
$$
Z_q^{\mathrm{res}}\le2n_qB_t,
\qquad n_q\ge\frac{\lambda_q Z}{2c_qB_t}.
\tag{13}
$$
The Boolean cube in \(D\) has weight \(B_yB_t\). The total weight of \(E_q\) is exactly \(c_qB_t\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h\). The full label family and every group's nonemptiness give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}c_q
&=\sum_{i=1}^m\rho_i^2\prod_{j\ne i}(1+\rho_j)=B_y\Sigma_\rho,\\
\frac Z{B_t}&\ge B_y\max(1,\Sigma_\rho/6).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
For the second lower bound on \(Z\), we used \(\sum_{h\in G_q}\theta_h\ge n_q/6\ge1/6\). Thus (14) does not presume anything about overlap or inverse group sizes, and does use all labels.

Let \(\alpha=p(D_1)\). If \(\alpha\ge1/4\), use the last, one-step assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 on the complete support \(D_1\) in \(k\) integer coordinates, with the normalized restriction \(\pi_1(x)=w_x/\sum_{v\in D_1}w_v\) and field \((\gamma,\eta)\). Its occupied parity count is at most \(2^k\), so both conditional cubics are at least \(1/(512\,8^k)\). The exact original relation is \(p_x=\alpha\pi_1(x)\), and (5) gives
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\,8^k}
\ge2^{-14}8^{-N}.
\tag{15}
$$
This is stronger than (1).

Assume now \(\alpha<1/4\), so \(p(E)>3/4\). Call a label \(q=(b,i)\) good if \(\rho_i\in[1/2,2]\), and bad otherwise. Let \(E_{\mathrm g}\) and \(E_{\mathrm b}\) be the unions of their respective sets \(E_q\).

Suppose first that \(p(E_{\mathrm g})\ge1/4\). In one parity class, write its active and neutral binary parity vectors as \(b,s\). Every member of \(E_{\mathrm g}\) in that class is obtained from \((b,s)\) by adding two in one active coordinate \(i\) and one neutral coordinate \(h\), both at zero positions of the parity vector. Its log probability is
$$
-\log Z+\sum_j b_j\log\rho_j+\sum_l s_l\log a_l
+2\log\rho_i+2\log a_h.
$$
The first three terms are fixed in the class. The last two terms together belong to \([-4\log2,4\log2]\). Thus for any two such points
$$
\exp\left(-\frac{|\log p_x-\log p_y|}{2}\right)\ge\frac1{16}.
\tag{16}
$$
Completeness puts the integral midpoint of every same-parity pair in \(D\). The affine law gives \(p_{(x+y)/2}=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\). Grouping all ordered pairs by midpoint therefore proves the exact identity
$$
\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)
=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
p_xp_y\exp\left(-\frac{|\log p_x-\log p_y|}{2}\right).
\tag{17}
$$
Indeed \(\sqrt{p_xp_y}\min(p_x,p_y)\) equals the displayed summand, including on the diagonal. Restrict (17) to endpoints in \(E_{\mathrm g}\). By (16) and Cauchy over at most \(2^k\) parity classes,
$$
\sum_c p_c s_p(c)\ge\frac1{16}
\sum_{b,s}p(E_{\mathrm g}\cap\text{class }(b,s))^2
\ge\frac{p(E_{\mathrm g})^2}{16\,2^k}
\ge2^{-8}2^{-k}.
$$
Jensen for the probability weights \(p_c\), followed by (6), gives
$$
T(p)\ge2^{-24}8^{-k},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge2^{-27}8^{-k}=2^{-24}8^{-N}.
\tag{18}
$$
Again (1) follows.

In the remaining case, \(p(E_{\mathrm b})>1/2\). Put
$$
H=\lbrace i:\rho_i\notin[1/2,2]\rbrace,
\qquad h=|H|\ge1,\qquad
U=\sum_{i\in H}\frac{\rho_i^{4/5}}{1+\rho_i^{2/5}},
\qquad \Lambda_{\mathrm b}=\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\lambda_q>\frac12.
$$
Here \(h\) denotes the number of bad active indices, not a neutral index. Direct summation over every binary label with fixed two-index gives
$$
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}c_q^{2/5}=V_yU.
\tag{19}
$$
From (13) and Hölder with exponents \(6\) and \(6/5\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\lambda_q^4n_q^2
&\ge\left(\frac Z{2B_t}\right)^2
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\frac{\lambda_q^6}{c_q^2},\\
\Lambda_{\mathrm b}
&=\sum_{q\text{ bad}}
\left(\frac{\lambda_q^6}{c_q^2}\right)^{1/6}c_q^{1/3}
\le\left(\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\frac{\lambda_q^6}{c_q^2}\right)^{1/6}
\left(\sum_{q\text{ bad}}c_q^{2/5}\right)^{5/6},\\
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\lambda_q^4n_q^2
&\ge\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm b}^6 B_y^2\max(1,\Sigma_\rho/6)^2}
{4V_y^5U^5}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

For \(r>0\), define the scalar function
$$
\mathcal F(r)=\frac{8(1+r)^2}{(1+r^{2/5})^5}.
$$
Concavity of the power \(2/5\) gives
\(1+r^{2/5}\le2^{3/5}(1+r)^{2/5}\), so \(\mathcal F(r)\ge1\). Also
$$
\mathcal F(1/r)=\mathcal F(r),\qquad
\frac{d}{dr}\log\mathcal F(r)
=\frac{2(1-r^{-3/5})}{(1+r)(1+r^{2/5})}.
$$
Consequently \(\mathcal F\) is increasing above one, and reciprocity treats arguments below one. Since
\(2^{2/5}<4/3\), as follows by taking fifth powers and using \(4<1024/243\),
$$
\mathcal F(2)>\frac{17496}{16807}>\frac{33}{32}.
$$
The last comparison is \(559872>554631\). Hence
$$
\frac{B_y^2}{V_y^5}
=8^{-m}\prod_{i=1}^m\mathcal F(\rho_i)
\ge8^{-m}(33/32)^h.
\tag{21}
$$

For \(\rho_i<1/2\), its summand in \(U\) is less than one. For \(\rho_i>2\), the summand is at most \(\rho_i^{2/5}\), while \(\rho_i\le2\rho_i^2/(1+\rho_i)\). Hölder, or concavity of the power \(2/5\), therefore gives
$$
U\le h+h^{3/5}(2\Sigma_\rho)^{2/5}.
$$
Convexity of the fifth power gives \((x+y)^5\le16(x^5+y^5)\), so
$$
\begin{aligned}
U^5&\le16h^5+64h^3\Sigma_\rho^2
\le80h^5\max(1,\Sigma_\rho^2),\\
\max(1,\Sigma_\rho/6)^2&\ge\frac1{36}\max(1,\Sigma_\rho^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting these bounds and (21) into (20) yields
$$
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\lambda_q^4n_q^2
\ge\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm b}^6}{11520}\,8^{-m}
\frac{(33/32)^h}{h^5}.
\tag{22}
$$
For completeness, the following exact geometric-series calculation controls the remaining polynomial for every integer \(h\ge1\). For \(0<r<1\), applying \((r\,d/dr)^5\) to \((1-r)^{-1}\) gives
$$
\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^5r^j
=\frac{r(1+26r+66r^2+26r^3+r^4)}{(1-r)^6}
<\frac{120}{(1-r)^6}.
$$
Termwise differentiation is valid on every compact subinterval of \((0,1)\). At \(r=32/33\),
$$
h^5(32/33)^h<120\cdot33^6
=154976156280<2^{38}.
$$
As \(\Lambda_{\mathrm b}>1/2\) and \(11520<2^{14}\), (22) implies
$$
\sum_{q\text{ bad}}\lambda_q^4n_q^2>2^{-58}8^{-m}.
$$
Equation (11) now gives both original cubics greater than
\(2^{-96}8^{-(m+L+1)}\), proving (1) in the balanced-neutral case. Empty good or bad collections cause no exception: the bad calculation is used only when \(\Lambda_{\mathrm b}>1/2\), so \(h\ge1\) and \(U>0\).

We prove the extension (2) by actual binary restrictions, preserving a balanced coordinate in every group. Initially choose one such coordinate in each group. A coordinate may serve as the choice for many groups. Delete only coordinates whose activities lie outside \([1/2,2]\), so none of these chosen coordinates is ever deleted.

At any stage, some previously deleted neutral coordinates have fixed binary values. Project the actual section onto the remaining coordinates, keeping all \(m\) active coordinates. Let its remaining neutral count be \(L'\), its integer dimension \(k'=m+L'\), and its normalized original conditional law be \(p'\). The remaining activities have not changed: the weights of the fixed coordinates are a common factor and cancel in the conditional normalization. This support is complete, because an integer point of its convex hull lifts to an integer point of the convex hull of the preceding actual section. It contains the full Boolean cube, since all fixed values are binary. Its double points are exactly the same full family with groups restricted to the remaining neutral indices: fixing a binary value at a neutral coordinate only removes the spikes whose two was at that coordinate. All other neutral binary entries remain free in every \(E_q\). Thus these groups remain nonempty, with their chosen balanced coordinates retained; in particular \(L'\ge1\).

Its actual one-step core is complete by the earlier extremality argument. If its core mass \(\alpha'\ge1/32\), apply the last assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 to that core with the retained field and its normalized restriction. Equations (5) and the parity bound \(2^{k'}\) give, for each current cubic,
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge\frac{(1/32)^4}{512\,8^{k'}}
=2^{-26}8^{-(k'+1)}.
\tag{23}
$$
This terminates the procedure.

Otherwise, if a remaining biased coordinate \(j\) is chosen, let \(\mu_2\) be the current probability of its actual section \(t_j=2\). This section is complete by the same lifting argument. After deleting \(j\), it has at most one two, since every original point has at most two and one has already been used at \(j\). If \(\mu_2\ge1/32\), apply the final one-step assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7 to its normalized conditional law in \(k'-1\) integer coordinates, with the retained field. Its occupied parity count is at most \(2^{k'-1}\). In the stated setting \(k'-1\ge1\); a zero-coordinate residual would instead be a singleton with cubics one. The comparison (5) gives
$$
M_3(p'),\ T(p')\ge\frac{(1/32)^4}{512\,8^{k'-1}}
\ge2^{-26}8^{-(k'+1)}.
\tag{24}
$$
This also terminates the procedure. No Boolean-containing hypothesis is needed for this application of the one-step assertion.

It remains to justify a profitable binary restriction when both \(\alpha'<1/32\) and \(\mu_2<1/32\). Let \(E'\) be the current actual set of double points. For its two binary sections, the map that changes \(t_j=0\) to \(t_j=1\) is an exact bijection: a double point in either section has its neutral two at another index, and the definition of every \(E_q\) permits both binary values at \(j\). Their current probabilities consequently satisfy
$$
p'(E'\cap\lbrace t_j=1\rbrace)
=a_j p'(E'\cap\lbrace t_j=0\rbrace).
\tag{25}
$$
Also
$$
p'(E'\cap\lbrace t_j\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\rbrace)
\ge1-\alpha'-\mu_2>15/16.
$$
Since \(a_j\notin[1/2,2]\), the larger of the two fractions \(1/(1+a_j)\) and \(a_j/(1+a_j)\) is greater than \(2/3\). Equation (25) therefore implies that the binary section favored by this activity has total current mass \(\mu>5/8\). Restrict to that actual section, normalize its law, and delete \(j\). Its support and nonempty groups have the properties already checked. Its original-to-conditional comparison has the exact factor \(\mu^4\), and its dimension has decreased by one. In dimension-normalized cubic units the resulting factor is
$$
8\mu^4>8(5/8)^4=625/512>1.
\tag{26}
$$

Repeat while biased neutral coordinates remain, unless (23) or (24) terminates earlier. The process is finite because each binary step deletes one biased coordinate. If neither early termination occurs, the final remaining neutral activities are all balanced, its groups are still nonempty, and the already proved balanced-neutral theorem applies with coefficient \(2^{-110}\). The retained support still has the full label family, irrespective of all group overlaps or how small the remaining groups become.

To check the original normalization through the complete procedure, suppose there were \(r\) binary steps with successive current conditional masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r\). The probability in the original law of the final current section is exactly their product, and its conditional affine probabilities are its original probabilities divided by that product. Iterating (5) therefore multiplies each cubic lower bound on the final current support by \(\prod_{l=1}^r\mu_l^4\). If termination was by (23) or (24), the current law already has a bound with coefficient \(2^{-26}\ge2^{-110}\); otherwise it has the balanced-neutral bound. Its ambient dimension is \(N-r\). In all cases the original bound is at least
$$
2^{-110}8^{-(N-r)}\prod_{l=1}^r\mu_l^4
=2^{-110}8^{-N}\prod_{l=1}^r(8\mu_l^4)
\ge2^{-110}8^{-N}
$$
by (26), proving (1) under (2) for both actual cubics. This reasoning uses actual section masses, never a separately chosen product normalization.

Finally consider the convex-completion source in the statement. Every generator of \(P=\operatorname{conv}(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\cup E)\) has at most one active two and at most one neutral two. If an integer point of \(P\) has a coordinate equal to two, every generator with positive coefficient in its convex representation has that coordinate equal to two. Thus any lattice point of \(P\) has at most one active two and at most one neutral two. If it has an active two at index \(i\), all representing generators have that same active two. All their other active entries are binary, so an integer value of zero or one fixes each of those bits in every positive-coefficient generator. Hence a single family \(E_q\), with active vector \(b+2e_i\), supplies the representation. If the lattice point also has a neutral two, its index must be in \(G_q\); all other neutral entries are binary, so it is already in \(E_q\). Therefore no additional double points arise, for arbitrary overlaps and singleton groups. The set \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\) is finite, contains the full Boolean cube and \(E\), and satisfies \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=P\), hence is complete. This verifies every support hypothesis for that source. All additional lattice points are one-step points and are already permitted in the theorem.
