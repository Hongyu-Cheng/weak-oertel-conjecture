## Statement

Retain the canonical hard-branch objects and normalizations of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66. Thus \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body, \(a_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\),

\[

 \qquad

\[
 d_z(r):=\sum_{e\ni z}r_e\leq\frac{a_z}{4}.
\tag{1}

For \(e=\{u,v\}\), write

\[
 m_e=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 D_e=\frac12(D_u+D_v),
 \qquad
 L_e=\frac12(\mathcal K_u+\mathcal K_v).
\tag{2}

Put \(R:=R_C(r)\). Retain the original load-minimal optimizer \(v=(v_e)\) and its edge loads

\[

\tag{4}

so in particular

This includes \(\beta_e=q_e^{\mathrm{mid}}+q_e^{\mathrm{ctr}}\), or either nonzero one of those two summands, from accepted fact 084a2e888b32fd2d.

Apply the exact weighted dual of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d afresh to the scalar matching \(\beta\). To distinguish it from the original reservoir optimization, denote its load-minimal dual optimizer and loads by

 \widehat v=(\widehat v_e),

## Proof

From (3) and (6),

\[
 d_z(\beta)
 \leq\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e
 \leq\sum_{e\ni z}r_e
 =d_z(r)
 \leq\frac{a_z}{4}.
\tag{36}
\]

The binomial theorem gives

\[
 \frac1{\gamma_d}
 =\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 =\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk d^{-k}
 \leq\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3,
\tag{37}
\]

so \(\gamma_d>1/3>1/4\). This proves (7). All positive reservoir edges are unordered distinct same-parity pairs, and \(\beta\) is supported on them. Thus \(\beta\) satisfies every other matching hypothesis of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d.

By the exact characterization in that accepted fact, \(R_C(\beta)\) is the maximum mass of an affinely half-metronoid-realizable submatching \(\beta'\leq\beta\). Every such \(\beta'\) satisfies

\[
 0\leq\beta'\leq\beta\leq\omega\leq r.
\tag{38}
\]

It is therefore an admissible realizable submatching in the definition of \(R_C(r)=R\). Hence \(|\beta'|\leq R\), and maximizing over \(\beta'\) proves (8).

Apply accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d to \(\beta\). Its exact dual gives (10), and its gauge-load inequality gives

\[
 B-R_C(\beta)\leq\widehat\Omega\leq B.
\tag{39}
\]

Adding \(R_C(\beta)\) to the first inequality and using (8) proves (11). The strong endpoint bounds (7) permit the stronger contact measure in the same accepted fact. With the fresh contacts from its support decomposition, that measure is (13), proving its domination and mass.

We next dispose of the zero-load case before introducing any intrinsic rank. Suppose \(\widehat\Omega=0\). Equation (11) gives \(B\leq R_C(\beta)\), while the definition gives \(R_C(\beta)\leq B\). Hence \(R_C(\beta)=B\), and (8) gives \(R\geq B\). The exact zero-defect characterization in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d says that \(R_C(\beta)=B\) is equivalent to one affine map hitting every \(L_e\) on the positive support of \(\beta\), and this is equivalent to \(\Delta_C(\beta)=0\). The zero dual field is then optimal. Since \(\widehat v\) minimizes the nonnegative gauge load among all optimizers, its load is zero. Every \(D_e\) is full-dimensional with the origin in its interior, so \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm; therefore \(\widehat v=0\).

Conversely, if one affine map hits every charged \(L_e\), then \(\Delta_C(\beta)=0\), the zero dual field is optimal, and load minimality gives \(\widehat\Omega=0\). This proves (14)-(15). A singleton support is hit by any affine map whose value at its one midpoint belongs to the corresponding nonempty body \(L_e\).

Now suppose \(\widehat\Omega>0\). The intrinsic edge-contact ranks (16)-(17) are therefore defined on a nonempty support. The intrinsic residual bound for the stronger contact measure in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d, multiplied by \(2^n\), is exactly (18).

Equations (19)-(20) are the objects of accepted fact 6e66cd69e25232b7 under the exact alias map

\[
 q=\beta,\quad
 v=\widehat v,\quad
 \omega_e=\widehat\omega_e,\quad
 r_z=\widehat d_z,\quad
 p_z=\widehat p_z.
\tag{40}
\]

That accepted fact assumes positive load, which is exactly the present case. Its simultaneous metronoid, dominated-selector, and residual-selector conclusions give (21)-(23) and the first inequality in (27). Equation (39) gives the second inequality in (27).

Write \(x:=R_C(\beta)\) and \(L:=\widehat L_\beta\). Equations (8), (27), and (39) give

\[
 R+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq x+L(B-x).
\tag{41}
\]

Here \(0\leq x\leq B\). If \(L\geq1\), the right side is at least \(B\); if \(0<L\leq1\), it is at least \(LB\). This proves (28). If \(\widehat r_\beta=0\), an affine map sends each \(z\in E_\beta\) to \(\widehat p_z\). The exact affine case of accepted fact 6e66cd69e25232b7 gives \(G\geq2\widehat\Omega\), and (39) proves (29). The two disjoint cases (15) and (28), together with the piecewise definition (30), prove (31) without defining an affine rank for the empty set.

Assume \(B>R\). Equations (8) and (39) give (32), so the positive-load definitions and conclusions just proved apply. Combining (27) with \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<Q/100\) yields

\[
 2^{n-\widehat\rho_\beta}
 \frac{B-R_C(\beta)}{\widehat r_\beta+1}
 <\frac Q{100}.
\]

Rearranging and using \(R_C(\beta)\leq R\) proves (33). Similarly, (18), \(D<Q/100\), and (39) give

\[
 2^{n-\widehat\rho_\beta^{\mathrm{edge}}-1}
 \frac{B-R_C(\beta)}
 {\widehat r_\beta^{\mathrm{edge}}+1}
 <\frac Q{100},
\]

which proves (34).

For each \(z\in E_\beta\), the one-atom submeasure

\[
 \frac{\widehat d_z}{2}
 \delta_{(z,\widehat p_z)}
 \leq\widehat\eta_\beta
\]

is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\). Hence

\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\frac{\widehat d_z}{2},
 \qquad
 D\geq2^{n-1}\widehat d_z.
\tag{42}
\]

The bound \(D<Q/100\) gives

\[
 \widehat d_z<\frac{Q}{50\,2^n}
 \qquad(z\in E_\beta).
\tag{43}
\]

Using (21), (32), and (43),

\[
 2\bigl(B-R_C(\beta)\bigr)
 \leq2\widehat\Omega
 =\sum_{z\in E_\beta}\widehat d_z
 <|E_\beta|\frac{Q}{50\,2^n},
\]

and (8) then proves (35). Thus every downstream screen that mentions an intrinsic edge or endpoint rank is reached only after (32) has established \(\widehat\Omega>0\).

For completeness, (11) gives the following exact threshold audit without invoking any rank. If \(B>Q/4000\), then

\[
 R_C(\beta)>\frac Q{8000}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \widehat\Omega>\frac Q{8000}.
\tag{44}
\]

If \(B>Q/1000\), then

\[
 R_C(\beta)>\frac Q{2000}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \widehat\Omega>\frac Q{2000}.
\tag{45}
\]

The guaranteed quantities in (44) and (45) remain below \(Q/100\). In the positive-load alternative, the lower bound in (27) is guaranteed to reach \(Q/100\) at the minimal displayed mass only with the additional bounds \(\widehat L_\beta\geq80\), respectively \(\widehat L_\beta\geq20\). In the retention alternative, (14)-(15) permit \(\widehat\Omega=0\) and give only \(R\geq B\). Thus the first missing step is a charged-support theorem quantitatively bounding \(R_C(\beta)\) away from \(B\); after that, (27), (33), (34), and (35) give the remaining rank alternatives. This completes the proof.
