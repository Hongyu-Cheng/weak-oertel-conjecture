## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^k\) be compact and convex, and suppose
$$
\varnothing\ne D=P\cap\mathbb Z^k
\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad
\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\quad(x\in D).
$$
For any \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1},
&w_x&=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),\\
Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
&p_{(x,0)}&=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \(z\in A\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)
&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then, with \(N=k+1\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{512}\,8^{-k}
=\frac1{64}\,8^{-N}.
$$
All halfspaces in this statement may be required to have nonzero normal; allowing the whole ambient space as well does not change the depths. No affine-rank assumption or restriction on the signs of the coordinates of \(\beta\) is imposed. An additive constant in the affine field cancels upon normalization.

The following is its conditional embedding consequence. Let \(n\ge1\), let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be compact and convex, and suppose
$$
\varnothing\ne\widehat D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n
\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^n,
\qquad
\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le2\quad(x\in\widehat D).
$$
For arbitrary \(\widehat\beta\in\mathbb R^n\), define the original distribution \(\widehat p\) on \(\widehat A=\widehat D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\) by
$$
\widehat Z=\sum_{x\in\widehat D}\exp(-\widehat\beta\cdot x),
\qquad
\widehat p_{(x,0)}=\frac{\exp(-\widehat\beta\cdot x)}{\widehat Z},
$$
and define \(\tau_{\widehat p}\) and \(M_3(\widehat p)\) using all containing closed affine halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\).

Fix an index set \(I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\) and a pattern \(a\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^I\) having at least one coordinate equal to two. Assume the coordinate section
$$
F=\lbrace x\in\widehat D:x_I=a\rbrace
$$
is nonempty. Thus this assertion applies, in particular, to every nonempty coordinate face of the support with such a fixed pattern. Set
$$
J=\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\setminus I,
\qquad k=|J|,
\qquad
\lambda=\sum_{x\in F}\widehat p_{(x,0)}.
$$
Here the symbol \(k\) is the residual dimension, and the first assertion will be applied with this value when it is positive. Let
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_a&=\lbrace y\in\mathbb R^J:(a,y)\in Q\rbrace,\\
D_a&=\lbrace y\in\mathbb Z^J:(a,y)\in F\rbrace,\\
A_a&=D_a\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1},\\
Z_a&=\sum_{y\in D_a}\exp(-\widehat\beta_J\cdot y),
\qquad
q_{(y,0)}=\frac{\exp(-\widehat\beta_J\cdot y)}{Z_a}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The notation \((a,y)\) means the vector in \(\mathbb R^n\) having coordinates \(a\) on \(I\) and \(y\) on \(J\), in their original order. Define \(\iota:\mathbb R^{k+1}\to\mathbb R^{n+1}\) by \(\iota(y,r)=((a,y),r)\). With \(\tau_q\) and \(M_3(q)\) defined in \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\), one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat p_{\iota(z)}&=\lambda q_z\quad(z\in A_a),\\
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\\iota(z)\in H}}
\frac1\lambda\sum_{u\in\iota(A_a)\cap H}\widehat p_u
&=\tau_q(z),\\
\tau_{\widehat p}(\iota(z))&\ge\lambda\tau_q(z),\\
\sum_{x\in F}\widehat p_{(x,0)}\tau_{\widehat p}((x,0))^3
&\ge\lambda^4M_3(q).
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular, for \(k\ge1\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\widehat p)
&\ge\frac{\lambda^4}{64}\,8^{-(k+1)}\\
&=\frac{8^{|I|}\lambda^4}{64}\,8^{-(n+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \(k=0\), the exact conditional value is \(M_3(q)=1\), and the stronger conclusion is
$$
M_3(\widehat p)\ge\lambda^4.
$$
Hence the displayed bound with constant \(1/64\) also holds for \(k=0\).

More generally, fix any nonempty \(I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\), put \(k=n-|I|\), and define the set of actual patterns containing a two and their original marginal probabilities by
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_I&=\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^I:
\exists x\in\widehat D\text{ with }x_I=u,
\ \exists i\in I\text{ with }u_i=2\rbrace,\\
p_I(u)&=\sum_{\substack{x\in\widehat D\\x_I=u}}\widehat p_{(x,0)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then the aggregated block bound is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\widehat p)
&\ge\frac1{64}\,8^{-(k+1)}\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4\\
&=\frac1{64}\,8^{-(n+1)}
\left(8^{|I|}\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
For \(k=0\) it has the stronger form \(M_3(\widehat p)\ge\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4\). Empty pattern sets have sum zero. Taking the maximum over blocks gives the direct score implication
$$
M_3(\widehat p)\ge\frac1{64}\,8^{-(n+1)}
\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace}
\left(8^{|I|}\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4\right).
$$
No uniform positive lower bound on this maximum is asserted here.

The claim permits missing Boolean points, missing parity sources, and lower affine rank in both the original and residual supports. The scalar finite inequality from accepted fact \(50c34c5bfb703ba9\) and the conditional comparison mechanism from accepted fact \(deffd679a4e4439e\) are reproduced below with their exact probability normalizations; the full-cube theorem of the former is not applied to \(D\) or \(D_a\).

## Proof

First prove the one-step assertion. Throughout this part, \(p_x\) and \(\tau_p(x)\), for \(x\in D\), abbreviate \(p_{(x,0)}\) and \(\tau_p((x,0))\), respectively. These are aliases of the original probabilities and depths, with no change of normalization. If \(x,y,c\in D\) and \(x+y=2c\), the affine field gives
$$
p_c^2=p_xp_y.
$$

Write \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\). For any \(b\in B_k\), a point in the parity class
$$
D_b=\lbrace x\in D:x\equiv b\pmod2\rbrace
$$
must be either \(b\), or \(b+2e_i\) for one index \(i\) with \(b_i=0\), where \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th coordinate unit vector. This is a description of the possible coordinates, and does not assert that \(b\in D\). Indeed, a coordinate with \(b_i=1\) must equal one, a coordinate with \(b_i=0\) must equal zero or two, and at most one coordinate can equal two.

It follows that the midpoint of any two distinct points of \(D_b\) is Boolean. The possibilities are
$$
\frac{b+(b+2e_i)}2=b+e_i,
\qquad
\frac{(b+2e_i)+(b+2e_j)}2=b+e_i+e_j
\quad(i\ne j).
$$
In the second possibility \(b_i=b_j=0\), so its midpoint is Boolean as well. Both endpoints lie in the convex set \(P\), so their midpoint lies in \(P\); it is integral, hence lies in \(P\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\). Consequently every distinct same-parity pair has its midpoint in
$$
C=D\cap B_k,
\qquad |C|\le2^k.
$$
This is precisely where completeness replaces the missing full Boolean cube. In particular, no Boolean parity source is needed in this argument.

For each \(c\in C\), define the ordered-pair sum
$$
R(c)=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y).
$$
Each unordered reflected pair occurs twice. Distinct unordered pairs reflected about \(c\) have disjoint endpoints, because the partner of \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\). The center \(c\) is distinct from all endpoints in these pairs. Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains at least one endpoint of each pair: if both endpoints satisfied the strict reverse of its defining inequality, their midpoint would also satisfy that strict reverse. Therefore
$$
\tau_p(c)\ge p_c+\frac12R(c)\ge\frac12R(c).
$$
Every containing halfspace also contains its center atom at any point of \(D\). Thus, setting
$$
S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4,
$$
we have
$$
M_3(p)\ge S_4.
$$

We use the following exact scalar inequality from the proof of accepted fact \(50c34c5bfb703ba9\): for any positive numbers \(a_1,\ldots,a_q\),
$$
K(a)=\sum_{i,j=1}^q
\min(a_i,a_j)^{7/6}\max(a_i,a_j)^{1/6}
\ge\frac34\left(\sum_{i=1}^qa_i\right)^{4/3}.
$$
Here is its proof, which requires no support geometry. Reorder the entries so that \(a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_q\). For each \(i\), there are \(2i-1\) ordered pairs whose largest index is \(i\), and each contributes at least \(a_i^{4/3}\). Hence
$$
K(a)\ge\sum_{i=1}^q(2i-1)a_i^{4/3}
\ge\sum_{i=1}^qi\,a_i^{4/3}.
$$
Hölder's inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^qa_i
&=\sum_{i=1}^q(i\,a_i^{4/3})^{3/4}i^{-3/4}\\
&\le\left(\sum_{i=1}^qi\,a_i^{4/3}\right)^{3/4}
\left(\sum_{i=1}^qi^{-3}\right)^{1/4}\\
&\le K(a)^{3/4}(3/2)^{1/4},
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(\sum_{i=1}^qi^{-3}\le1+\int_1^\infty t^{-3}\,dt=3/2\). Raising to the power \(4/3\) and rearranging yields
$$
K(a)\ge(2/3)^{1/3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^qa_i\right)^{4/3}
\ge\frac34\left(\sum_{i=1}^qa_i\right)^{4/3}.
$$
The last comparison follows from \(2/3\ge27/64\).

For the application, enumerate each nonempty class \(D_b\) as \(x_1,\ldots,x_q\) and set \(a_i=p_{x_i}\). This is the exact map to the scalar lemma in accepted fact \(50c34c5bfb703ba9\); its inputs are original atom probabilities, without conditioning on \(D_b\). Put
$$
P_b=\sum_{x\in D_b}p_x
\quad(b\in B_k),
$$
and set \(P_b=0\) for an empty class. Then \(\sum_{b\in B_k}P_b=1\). Define
$$
L=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}.
$$
Applying the scalar inequality only to the nonempty parity classes, followed by Hölder on the \(2^k\) possible classes, gives
$$
L\ge\frac34\sum_{b\in B_k}P_b^{4/3}
\ge\frac34\,2^{-k/3}.
$$
Explicitly, the second inequality follows from
$$
1=\sum_{b\in B_k}P_b
\le\left(\sum_{b\in B_k}P_b^{4/3}\right)^{3/4}(2^k)^{1/4}.
$$

The diagonal contribution is
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\mathrm{diag}}
&=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^{4/3}
=\sum_{x\in D}(p_x^4)^{1/9}p_x^{8/9}\\
&\le\left(\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4\right)^{1/9}
\left(\sum_{x\in D}p_x\right)^{8/9}
=S_4^{1/9}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(L_{\mathrm{off}}=L-L_{\mathrm{diag}}\). Every pair contributing to \(L_{\mathrm{off}}\) has a midpoint in \(C\), as proved above. Conversely, every integral reflected pair about a center in \(C\) has equal parity. For each such pair, \(p_c^2=p_xp_y\), so
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\mathrm{off}}
&=\sum_{c\in C}
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}
(p_xp_y)^{1/6}\min(p_x,p_y)\\
&=\sum_{c\in C}p_c^{1/3}R(c).
\end{aligned}
$$
To handle absent Boolean centers, including the possibility \(C=\varnothing\), define numbers indexed by the full set \(B_k\) by
$$
t_c=\begin{cases}
p_c^{1/3}R(c),&c\in C,\\
0,&c\in B_k\setminus C.
\end{cases}
$$
No probability is assigned to a point outside \(D\) in this definition. The preceding identity and the halfspace bound imply
$$
\sum_{c\in B_k}t_c=L_{\mathrm{off}},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac18\sum_{c\in B_k}t_c^3.
$$
Hölder on the \(2^k\) entries, including zero entries, yields
$$
\left(\sum_{c\in B_k}t_c\right)^3
\le(2^k)^2\sum_{c\in B_k}t_c^3.
$$
Consequently
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{L_{\mathrm{off}}^3}{8\cdot4^k}.
$$
This estimate uses only at most \(2^k\) occupied Boolean midpoint centers, rather than the presence of all Boolean atoms.

If \(S_4\ge2^{-9}8^{-k}\), the inequality \(M_3(p)\ge S_4\) proves the claim. Otherwise
$$
S_4^{1/9}<\frac12\,2^{-k/3},
$$
and hence
$$
L_{\mathrm{off}}
\ge\frac34\,2^{-k/3}-S_4^{1/9}
\ge\frac14\,2^{-k/3}.
$$
It follows that
$$
M_3(p)
\ge\frac{(\frac14\,2^{-k/3})^3}{8\cdot4^k}
=2^{-9}8^{-k}
=\frac1{64}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
This proves the first assertion, including supports of lower affine rank.

We now establish the conditional assertion. The section \(Q_a\) is compact and convex: it is obtained from the intersection of \(Q\) with the affine subspace \(x_I=a\) by the coordinate identification with \(\mathbb R^J\). Because the fixed coordinates are integral,
$$
Q_a\cap\mathbb Z^J=D_a.
$$
Indeed, an integral \(y\) belongs to \(Q_a\) exactly when \((a,y)\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\) and has the specified fixed coordinates. Nonemptiness of \(F\) gives nonemptiness of \(D_a\). Every point of \(D_a\) is ternary, and it has at most one coordinate equal to two, since at least one of the at most two such coordinates of \((a,y)\) is already fixed in \(I\). This reasoning also applies when the fixed pattern contains two coordinates equal to two, in which case no residual coordinate can equal two. No assertion about Boolean parity sources is needed.

Factoring the original affine weights gives
$$
\lambda
=\frac{\exp(-\widehat\beta_I\cdot a)Z_a}{\widehat Z},
\qquad
\widehat p_{\iota(y,0)}
=\lambda\frac{\exp(-\widehat\beta_J\cdot y)}{Z_a}
=\lambda q_{(y,0)}.
$$
Thus \(q\) is exactly the original distribution conditioned on the section, under \(\iota\), with residual field \(\widehat\beta_J\). This is the normalization map for the conditional comparison of accepted fact \(deffd679a4e4439e\): its ambient distribution is here \(\widehat p\), its conditional distribution is \(q\), its face mass is \(\lambda\), and its embedding is the present \(\iota\). We prove the comparison for the arbitrary fixed pattern directly.

Fix \(z\in A_a\), and let a containing ambient halfspace with nonzero normal be written as
$$
H=\lbrace(x,r)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:u\cdot x+v r\ge t\rbrace,
\qquad (u,v)\ne0.
$$
Its pullback is
$$
\iota^{-1}(H)
=\lbrace(y,r)\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:
u_J\cdot y+v r\ge t-u_I\cdot a\rbrace.
$$
If \((u_J,v)\ne0\), this is a closed affine halfspace containing \(z\), so its \(q\)-mass is at least \(\tau_q(z)\). If \((u_J,v)=0\), containment of \(\iota(z)\) implies \(0\ge t-u_I\cdot a\), so the pullback is the whole space and has \(q\)-mass one, again at least \(\tau_q(z)\). In particular, a containing ambient halfspace cannot have empty pullback. This accounts for zero pullback normal even when several coordinates are fixed.

Conversely, every closed affine halfspace with nonzero normal in \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) extends to an ambient halfspace by using its coefficients on \(J\) and on \(r\), setting the coefficients on \(I\) to zero, and keeping its threshold. The extended normal is still nonzero. Since \(\widehat p_{\iota(z)}=\lambda q_z\), these two directions establish exactly
$$
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\\iota(z)\in H}}
\frac1\lambda\sum_{u\in\iota(A_a)\cap H}\widehat p_u
=\tau_q(z).
$$
Allowing the whole space among halfspaces does not change either infimum, because its mass is one. The full original mass of any ambient halfspace is at least its original mass on this section, so
$$
\tau_{\widehat p}(\iota(z))\ge\lambda\tau_q(z).
$$
Keeping precisely the section terms in the original moment then gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\widehat p)
&\ge\sum_{z\in A_a}\widehat p_{\iota(z)}
\tau_{\widehat p}(\iota(z))^3\\
&\ge\sum_{z\in A_a}\lambda q_z
\bigl(\lambda\tau_q(z)\bigr)^3
=\lambda^4M_3(q).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus one factor of \(\lambda\) comes from the averaging measure and three from depth. The same calculation proves the stated bound for the sum over section points alone.

If \(k\ge1\), the already-proved first assertion applies to \(D_a=Q_a\cap\mathbb Z^J\), after ordering the coordinates of \(J\), with its residual field \(\widehat\beta_J\) and its original conditional normalization \(Z_a\). Hence
$$
M_3(q)\ge\frac1{64}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Since \(k=n-|I|\), substituting this bound proves both residual-dimension and original-dimension forms in the statement.

If \(k=0\), all coordinates are fixed, \(D_a\) consists of the unique vector of \(\mathbb R^0\), and \(A_a\) is a single point in \(\mathbb R^1\). Its conditional probability is one. Every containing halfspace contains that atom, so its depth is one and \(M_3(q)=1\). The proved embedding comparison therefore gives \(M_3(\widehat p)\ge\lambda^4\) directly, without invoking a theorem requiring positive residual dimension. The contribution of the section itself is at least \(\lambda^4\) as well. This also implies the coarser bound \((\lambda^4/64)8^{-1}\).

For the aggregated claim, fix a nonempty block \(I\). For each \(u\in U_I\), apply the proved section comparison to \(a=u\). Its original mass is \(\lambda=p_I(u)\), and its residual dimension is the common value \(k=n-|I|\). The sets \(\lbrace x\in\widehat D:x_I=u\rbrace\), for distinct \(u\), are disjoint. If \(k\ge1\), summing their original sampling contributions therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\widehat p)
&\ge\sum_{u\in U_I}
\sum_{\substack{x\in\widehat D\\x_I=u}}
\widehat p_{(x,0)}\tau_{\widehat p}((x,0))^3\\
&\ge\frac1{64}\,8^{-(k+1)}\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \(k=0\), the same disjointness and the stronger singleton-section contribution instead give \(M_3(\widehat p)\ge\sum_{u\in U_I}p_I(u)^4\). This implies the displayed aggregated bound with constant \(1/64\) for \(k=0\) too. If \(U_I\) is empty, the asserted inequality follows from nonnegativity. Finally, \(8^{-(k+1)}=8^{|I|}8^{-(n+1)}\), and the inequality holds for every nonempty block \(I\). There are finitely many such blocks, so it also holds with the maximum of their bracketed scores. This proves the score implication without assuming or proving any separate finite inequality for that score.
