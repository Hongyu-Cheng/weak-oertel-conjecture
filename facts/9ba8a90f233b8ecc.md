## Statement

Under the hypotheses on the positive actual family, unrestricted local minimum and selected finite critical and slack covers in the finite-list moving-cap separation lemma, for every prescribed Borel inside body subdensity system there is one collection of consistently allocated facet subdensities and $\lambda_j\ge0$, $\sum_j\lambda_j=1/\beta$, such that for $\nu=(\sum_j\lambda_j\alpha_j)\eta$,
$$
\int\psi(u)A(z,x)\,d(\eta-\nu)=0,\qquad
\int\psi_+(u)\Phi(z,x)\,d(\eta-\nu)\ge0
$$
for every continuous $\psi$, nonnegative continuous $\psi_+$, affine $A$, and finite continuous convex $\Phi$. The same certificate satisfies the corresponding polar inequalities on admissible cap velocities. The certificate may depend on the chosen cover and inside subdensities.

## Proof

For each finite list of body tests, apply the finite-list separation lemma the finite-list moving-cap separation lemma, including both homothety signs, common erosion and the same finite-dimensional cone of cap velocities. A certificate is recorded by the multiplier vector in the fixed compact simplex $\sum_j\lambda_j=1/\beta$ and the finite list of weighted body and section facet measures. Each weighted measure is dominated by $\lambda_j$ times its fixed compact facet's surface measure. Weak compactness holds in the space of finite measures on those facets; nonnegativity, domination, prescribed inside measures proportional to $\lambda_j$, and the consistency relations are weakly closed. On a coincident facet its normal is constant almost everywhere, so both physical and marked continuous moment tests give weakly continuous constraints on these measures. Strict boundary and proper section terms are fixed measures times $\lambda_j$.

On signed affine body tests with zero cap velocity the polar inequality holds in both signs, giving the stated identity. A nonnegative normal weight times a convex potential is feasible with outward velocity $-\psi_+\Phi$ and zero cap velocity, giving the displayed inequality with its indicated sign. The cap-velocity constraints are closed by evaluation on each admissible velocity, and are satisfied on every finite-list certificate. Thus every finite list of the desired constraints has a solution in one compact space independent of the list. The finite-intersection property gives one solution for them all. If a multiplier is zero any consistent unweighted density on its facet has no effect; otherwise divide the weighted measures by the multiplier to recover the claimed allocations.
