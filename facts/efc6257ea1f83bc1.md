## Statement

Let $\Lambda=c+L$ be an affine lattice and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\Lambda$ be finite, with positive point weights of total mass $M$. Let $I\subseteq P$ be nonempty and closed under midpoints of two points congruent modulo $2L$. Suppose every $p\in I$ has the same closed atomic halfspace depth $Q\in(0,M)$, computed using all weights on $P$. Then $I$ can be covered by $1\le m\le k(I)$ closed base halfspaces, where $k(I)$ counts occupied classes modulo $2L$. Each halfspace has mass exactly $Q$ and contains a point of $I$ on its boundary lying strictly outside every other retained halfspace.

## Proof

For each point of $I$, choose one closed containing cap of minimum weight $Q$, attained because only finitely many traces on $P$ occur. Write the chosen caps as $\{a_i\ge b_i\}$ for affine $a_i$ on the base span. Take $U_i>\max(b_i,\max_P a_i)$ and maximize $\sum_i c_i$ over the compact box $b_i\le c_i\le U_i$ subject to the caps $\{a_i\ge c_i\}$ covering $I$. The constraint is closed because $I$ and the index set are finite, and $c=b$ is feasible.

A row with $c_i<U_i$ has an exclusive witness $p_i\in I$ at equality $a_i(p_i)=c_i$. Indeed, otherwise every point of $I$ outside the other caps has a positive gap $a_i(p)-c_i$, or there are no such points. A small increase of $c_i$ would preserve the cover, a contradiction. Discard saturated rows, which are empty on $P$. The retained list is nonempty and still covers $I$. If two witnesses have the same parity, their midpoint belongs to $I$, and at every retained row at least one witness satisfies its inequality strictly in the excluded direction while the other satisfies the non-strict excluded inequality. Their midpoint lies strictly outside every cap, a contradiction. This gives $m\le k(I)$. A retained cap has mass at most $Q$ because it is contained in its original cap and mass at least $Q$ because it contains an exclusive witness whose atomic depth is $Q$.
