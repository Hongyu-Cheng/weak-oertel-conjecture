## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\), and \(h_S\). Use exactly the fiber notation and normalization of accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`: \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}}\), \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), \(D=\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S)\). The original finite Borel mixed-volume measure is denoted \(\mu_C\), so \(\mu_C(H)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\mu_{\mathbf I}(H_{\mathbf I})\) for an ambient halfspace \(H\), with \(H_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in H\}\). All logarithms are natural.

Assume first that \(W>0\), and put \(a_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\). For each \(\mathbf J\in D\), use the predecessor's finite family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) of nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\). Write \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}>0\) for the unique barycentric coefficients of \(\mathbf J\) on \(E\). The same root-volume cost and its cutoff family are

\[
L_E=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}\right)^d},
\qquad
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:L_E\le L\}
\quad(L\ge0).
\tag{1}
\]

Every cost is finite and nonnegative, and the singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\) has cost zero. Define the integral hitting mass and its optimized score by

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_d(\mathbf J,L)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing
\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\right\},\\
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The exact notation map from the second archived response is \(A_{\mathrm{source}}=D\), its hitting or deleted set \(D_{\mathrm{source}}=F\), and \(\mathcal R_d(w)_{\mathrm{source}}=\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\). The last alias applies only to that response's optimized integral hitting score. It does not identify this score with the average-root-cost quantity called \(\mathcal R_d\) in another source. The predecessor's \(\mathcal B_d(w)\) continues to mean its fractional cutoff score. Explicitly, with full source occurrence charges,

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_d(\mathbf J,L)
&=\max\left\{\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D)\right\},\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Thus every participating source in a component of mass \(x_E\) is charged \(x_E\), independently of its positive barycentric coefficient. The singleton variable is the predecessor's central mass. All components share the original capacities \(w_{\mathbf I}\).

All extrema in (2)-(3) are attained. At each target, the optimized cutoff can be chosen among the finitely many \(L_E\). Moreover, \(w_{\mathbf J}\le m_d(\mathbf J,L)\le W\), and deleting a set of original weight strictly less than \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) always leaves some \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\) completely undeleted.

Put \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), and let \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\). For every \(\mathbf J\in D\), every \(L\ge0\), and every integer \(k\ge1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf J}}h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y))
&\ge \frac{\bigl(1-e^L(1-\gamma_d)^k\bigr)_+}{k}
m_d(\mathbf J,L),\\
\max_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf J}}h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y))
&\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L)}{6(1+L)},\\
h_{\max}(C):=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)
&\ge\frac{\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The maxima over points are attained. In particular each asserted lower bound is witnessed by one point selected before the arbitrary closed ambient halfspace, including vertical halfspaces. For the specific original fiber centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf J}=w_{\mathbf J}^{-1}\int_{C_{\mathbf J}}\mathbf y\,d\mathbf y\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf c_{\mathbf J}))
&\ge \bigl(1-e^L(1-\gamma_d)\bigr)_+m_d(\mathbf J,L),\\
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf c_{\mathbf J}))
&\ge\gamma_d m_d(\mathbf J,0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

For \(r=e^{-L/d}a_{\mathbf J}>0\), with \(r\le a_{\mathbf J}\), the exact finite geometric characterization is

\[
m_d(\mathbf J,L)
=\min_{\substack{(\mathbf b,c)\in(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R)\setminus\{(\mathbf0,0)\}\\c\ge0}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+c(a_{\mathbf I}-r)\ge0}}
w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{6}
\]

Here the minimum is over closed upper halfspaces through \( (\mathbf J,r)\) in the lifted configuration \(\{(\mathbf I,a_{\mathbf I}):\mathbf I\in D\}\). The case \(c=0\) is allowed, every equality point is included, and an optimal normal exists. At each target and cutoff, and also after optimization,

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_d(\mathbf J,L)&\le m_d(\mathbf J,L)\le(n+1)P_d(\mathbf J,L),\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&\le\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\le(n+1)\mathcal B_d(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

For every integer \(N\ge16\), let \(n=N-1\), \(d=1\), and

\[
C_N=N\Delta_{N-1}\times[0,1],
\qquad
\Delta_{N-1}=\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{N-1}:
\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}x_i\le1\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

These are full-dimensional rational convex bodies. Their complete positive lattice support is \(N\Delta_{N-1}\cap\mathbb Z^{N-1}\); every fiber on that support, including its boundary lattice points, is exactly \([0,1]\) and has weight one. Appending the slack coordinate identifies that support with

\[
\Omega_N=\left\{\mathbf K\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{N}:\sum_{i=1}^N K_i=N\right\},
\qquad W=|\Omega_N|=\binom{2N-1}{N-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

Every barycentric cost is zero, and for this exact weight table,

\[
\mathcal B_1^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge\frac W{152},
\qquad
\mathcal B_1(w)\le\frac{4W}{\log N},
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal B_1^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}{\mathcal B_1(w)}
\ge\frac{\log N}{608}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{10}
\]

The fractional upper bound holds uniformly over every lattice target in (9). Consequently the separation concerns the optimized scores, and not only scores at a prescribed center. It is a separation between two certificates, not a counterexample to the locked conjecture.

If an absolute \(c>0\) satisfied \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge c2^{-n}W\) for every actual convex-body fiber table in the statement, then (4) would prove the locked conjecture with \(c_0=\min\{c/6,1/(2e)\}\). No such uniform scalar inequality is asserted here. If \(W=0\), define \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\mathcal B_d(w)=0\); then the global zero lower bound holds at every point of the assumed nonempty \(S\), without defining a positive-fiber centroid or a positive-support optimization.

## Proof

We first identify precisely the mathematical input consumed from accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`. Its root-volume coupling bound implies, for any convex body of the prescribed mixed dimensions, any positive source fibers with a barycentric representation at an integer target, and their actual current volumes, that the target volume is at least the \(d\)-th power of the corresponding barycentric mean of the volume roots. Applied to \(C\), this proves all finiteness and nonnegativity assertions about \(L_E\) in (1). We will also apply that same accepted statement to cut remainders, after checking that they remain convex bodies with positive source fibers. The exact LP, its dual, and its source-preserving reduction to affinely independent supports are consumed with their original full occurrence capacities.

The following continuous centroid inequality is also a direct consequence of the same accepted fact. For any convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\), apply its zero-cost central-mass statement to the auxiliary body \([-1/2,1/2]\times K\), with one integer coordinate. Its only integer fiber is \(K\) at zero. Giving its central mass the value \(\mathcal L^d(K)\), and evaluating the conclusion on the ambient halfspace \(\mathbb R\times G\), gives

\[
\mathcal L^d(K\cap G)\ge\gamma_d\mathcal L^d(K)
\quad\text{for every closed halfspace }G\subset\mathbb R^d
\text{ containing the uniform centroid of }K.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus (11) is available from the declared predecessor alone. Applying it to both through-centroid sides of a proper cutting hyperplane gives positive volume on each side. No new external centroid or volume-concavity assertion is being assumed.

Compactness of the projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty. Therefore \(D\) is finite, the total measure \(\mu_C\) is finite, and every positive fiber is a compact convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\). Work with \(W>0\) until the final zero-volume observation.

We record attainment of pointwise depth maxima without deleting ambient boundary mass. For a unit vector \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define \(f_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf p)=\mu_C(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)\ge0\})\). If \(\mathbf p_j\to\mathbf p\), then the pointwise upper limit of these closed-halfspace indicators is at most the indicator at \(\mathbf p\). Reverse Fatou for the finite measure \(\mu_C\) yields \(\limsup_j f_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf p_j)\le f_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf p)\). Thus each \(f_{\mathbf u}\) is upper semicontinuous. Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p\) contains the parallel closed halfspace through \(\mathbf p\), so \(h_S(\mathbf p)=\inf_{\|\mathbf u\|=1}f_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf p)\). An arbitrary infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, since its strict sublevel sets are unions of open sets. Compactness of \(C_{\mathbf J}\) gives the first maxima in (4), and compactness of \(S=C\cap\Lambda\) gives \(h_{\max}(C)\). An optional improper whole-space halfspace has mass \(W\) and does not affect this infimum. This argument remains valid when a vertical boundary carries positive mixed volume.

The minimum in (2) exists because there are finitely many subsets of \(D\), and the whole set is a hitting set. The low-cost singleton forces every hitting set to contain \(\mathbf J\), proving \(w_{\mathbf J}\le m_d(\mathbf J,L)\le W\). A set of weight strictly less than the minimum cannot hit all low-cost supports, which proves the stated deletion property. For any \(L\ge0\), the largest support cost \(t\le L\) exists because the singleton has cost zero. The cutoff families at \(L\) and \(t\) coincide, so both their hitting masses and their fractional packing values coincide. Replacing \(L\) by \(t\) weakly increases each ratio with denominator \(1+L\). This proves finite attainment of the outer optimization in (2), and matches the predecessor's attainment statement for (3).

Fix \(\mathbf J,L\), and abbreviate \(m=m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) and \(q=1-\gamma_d\in(0,1)\). Every closed base halfspace through \(\mathbf J\) hits every support in \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\): if its defining normal is \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), the identity \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=0\) prevents all its positively weighted source scores from being strictly negative. Its original source weight is therefore at least \(m\). A vertical ambient halfspace through any point of the target fiber has exactly this source weight. A vertical halfspace merely containing such a point contains the parallel through-point halfspace, so it also has original mixed volume at least \(m\).

Suppose \(e^Lq^k<1\); otherwise the first inequality in (4) is the trivial nonnegative bound. To prove it in the nontrivial case, suppose its attained fiberwise maximum were strictly less than \((1-e^Lq^k)m/k\). Choose \(\delta\) and \(\theta\) with

\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf J}}h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y))
<\delta<\frac{1-e^Lq^k}{k}m,
\qquad
e^Lq^k<\theta<1-\frac{k\delta}{m}.
\tag{12}
\]

These choices are possible, and satisfy \(0<\delta<m\) and \(0<\theta<1\). Start with \(C^0=C\). At a stage \(t<k\), let \(\mathbf c_t\) be the uniform centroid of the positive-volume target fiber \(C^t_{\mathbf J}\). Its membership in \(C_{\mathbf J}\) and (12) supply a proper closed ambient halfspace of original measure less than \(\delta\) containing \((\mathbf J,\mathbf c_t)\). Shrink it parallel until its boundary passes through that point. Write the resulting halfspace as

\[
H_t=\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y):
\mathbf b_t\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf J)+
\mathbf v_t\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf c_t)\ge0\},
\qquad \mu_C(H_t)<\delta.
\tag{13}
\]

The vertical observation and \(\delta<m\) force \(\mathbf v_t\ne\mathbf0\). In particular, the boundary of \(H_t\) is null for the original \(d\)-dimensional measure in every fiber, and hence for \(\mu_C\). Define \(C^{t+1}\) by intersecting \(C^t\) with the opposite closed halfspace, whose displayed score is at most zero.

Here is a noncircular verification that every step is available. Initially \(C^0\) is a convex body and \(w_{\mathbf J}^0=w_{\mathbf J}>0\). If \(C^t\) is a convex body with a positive target fiber, (11) applied to both through-centroid sections in that fiber, with their common hyperplane null, gives

\[
\gamma_d w_{\mathbf J}^t\le w_{\mathbf J}^{t+1}\le q w_{\mathbf J}^t.
\tag{14}
\]

In particular the retained strict side contains a point of \(C^t\). A convex combination of that point with an interior point of \(C^t\), using a sufficiently small positive weight on the latter, is an interior point on the retained strict side: an interior ball at the latter point scales to an interior ball at the combination. Consequently \(C^{t+1}\) is again full dimensional, as well as compact and convex, and (14) proves positivity of its target fiber. This induction constructs all \(k\) cuts. It also gives \(w_{\mathbf J}^t\le q^t w_{\mathbf J}\) for \(0\le t\le k\).

Set \(w_{\mathbf I}^t=\mathcal L^d(C^t_{\mathbf I})\) for the original indices \(\mathbf I\in D\). Fibers outside \(D\) remain of zero volume under intersection. At each cut the part discarded from \(C^t\) lies in \(H_t\), so its original mixed volume is strictly less than \(\delta\). Therefore

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}(w_{\mathbf I}-w_{\mathbf I}^0)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}(w_{\mathbf I}-w_{\mathbf I}^t)<t\delta
\quad(1\le t\le k).
\tag{15}
\]

The first equality is kept separate: a strict inequality with right side \(t\delta=0\) would be false at \(t=0\). All weights in the deletion threshold are the original weights. Define \(F_t=\{\mathbf I\in D:w_{\mathbf I}^t<\theta w_{\mathbf I}\}\). At \(t=0\), \(F_0=\varnothing\), since \(\theta<1\), and its weight is less than \(m\). At every \(1\le t\le k\), (12) and (15) give

\[
(1-\theta)\sum_{\mathbf I\in F_t}w_{\mathbf I}
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}(w_{\mathbf I}-w_{\mathbf I}^t)
<t\delta\le k\delta<(1-\theta)m.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus \(F_t\) has original weight less than \(m\) at every stage, including the separately checked initial stage. Some \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\) is disjoint from \(F_t\). Its sources have positive current volumes at least \(\theta w_{\mathbf I}\). Apply the accepted root-volume bound to the actual convex body \(C^t\), these sources, and their unchanged coefficients. Its hypotheses hold by the full-dimensionality and source positivity just proved. It gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{\mathbf J}^t
&\ge\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}
(w_{\mathbf I}^t)^{1/d}\right)^d\\
&\ge\theta\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}
w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d
\ge\theta e^{-L}w_{\mathbf J}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The surviving support may depend on \(t\); neither a common support nor altered source capacities are required. At \(t=k\), (12), (14), and (17) imply \(q^k w_{\mathbf J}\ge w_{\mathbf J}^k\ge\theta e^{-L}w_{\mathbf J}>q^k w_{\mathbf J}\), a contradiction. This proves the first inequality in (4), with the exact original boundary measure throughout.

To obtain the numerical constant, \(\log(1+1/d)\le1/d\) gives \(\gamma_d\ge e^{-1}>1/3\). For example \(e<3\) follows from \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\), with a strict inequality for \(j\ge3\), in the exponential series. Thus \(q\le2/3\). Choose

\[
k=\left\lfloor\frac{L+\log2}{\log(3/2)}\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{18}
\]

Then \(e^Lq^k<1/2\). Also \(\log(3/2)\ge1/3\), by integrating \(1/x\ge2/3\) on \([1,3/2]\), and \(\log2<2\log(3/2)\), since \(2<9/4\). Hence \(k\le (L+\log2)/\log(3/2)+1\le3(1+L)\). The first inequality of (4) gives a bound greater than \(m/(2k)\), and therefore at least \(m/[6(1+L)]\). Choose an attaining target and cutoff in (2), then a point attaining the fiberwise depth maximum. Its depth is at least \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)/6\). All these choices precede the arbitrary containing halfspace.

For (5), the case \(e^Lq\ge1\) is trivial. Suppose \(e^Lq<1\) and that the depth at the original centroid were less than \((1-e^Lq)m\). There is a through-centroid closed halfspace \(H\) with \(\mu_C(H)<(1-e^Lq)m<m\). It is nonvertical by the same argument. Choose \(\theta\) with \(e^Lq<\theta<1-\mu_C(H)/m\), and let \(C'\) be the opposite closed remainder. The preceding one-cut body argument applies, and (11) gives \(w'_{\mathbf J}\le q w_{\mathbf J}\). For \(F=\{\mathbf I:w'_{\mathbf I}<\theta w_{\mathbf I}\}\), the removed original mass is at most \(\mu_C(H)<(1-\theta)m\), so \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}<m\). A surviving low-cost support and the accepted root-volume bound give \(w'_{\mathbf J}\ge\theta e^{-L}w_{\mathbf J}>q w_{\mathbf J}\), the same contradiction. This proves the fixed-centroid claim independently of the fiberwise maximization. Setting \(L=0\) yields its second inequality.

We prove (6), keeping the closed boundary. A low-cost support has \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}\ge r\). For any allowed \( (\mathbf b,c)\),

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}
\bigl[\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+c(a_{\mathbf I}-r)\bigr]
=c\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}-r\right)
\ge0.
\tag{19}
\]

At least one source has score at least zero. Thus the source set of every displayed closed halfspace is a hitting set, giving the inequality that its weight is at least \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\). This includes \(c=0\) and equality in the root threshold.

For the converse, let \(F\subseteq D\) be a minimum hitting set, and put \(B=D\setminus F\). There is no barycentric representation of \(\mathbf J\) using \(B\) with root average at least \(r\). Indeed, if one existed, maximize the linear root average over its barycentric coefficient polytope on \(B\). An optimal vector with a smallest positive support is affinely independent: an affine dependence permits both small signed perturbations; optimality forces zero change of the linear objective, and continuing until a coefficient first vanishes reduces that support. Its positive support would therefore belong to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\) and be disjoint from \(F\), a contradiction. This is also the source-preserving support-reduction mechanism in the declared predecessor.

When \(B\ne\varnothing\), consider

\[
K_B=\operatorname{conv}\{(\mathbf I,a_{\mathbf I}):\mathbf I\in B\}
-\{(\mathbf0,s):s\ge0\}.
\tag{20}
\]

It is a nonempty closed convex set. For closedness, in any convergent sequence of its points the convex-hull summands have a convergent subsequence, by compactness; the corresponding nonnegative downward coordinates then also converge. Membership of \( (\mathbf J,r)\) in \(K_B\) is exactly a barycentric representation on \(B\) with root average at least \(r\), which has just been excluded.

For completeness, strict separation here can be obtained from a nearest point of the closed convex set to \( (\mathbf J,r)\). Such a point exists by restricting the distance minimization to a large closed ball. The nonzero vector from that nearest point to \( (\mathbf J,r)\), say \( (\mathbf b,c)\), has strictly negative inner product with \(\mathbf x-(\mathbf J,r)\) for every \(\mathbf x\in K_B\). The projection inequality follows by differentiating squared distance along each segment from the nearest point into \(K_B\); the strict gap at the target is the square of the distance. Since \(K_B\) is unbounded downward, this separating normal must satisfy \(c\ge0\). Its closed upper halfspace through \( (\mathbf J,r)\) contains no lifted point of \(B\), even on its boundary. Its source set is consequently contained in \(F\), so its weight is at most \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\).

If \(B=\varnothing\), then \(F=D\) and \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)=W\). The admissible normal \( (\mathbf0,1)\) has a source set of weight at most \(W\), and (19) gives the opposite bound. This handles the empty complement explicitly. These arguments produce an attaining normal in all cases and prove (6); no minimization continuity claim for the discrete mass function is required.

The attained dual of the exact fractional program, from accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`, is

\[
P_d(\mathbf J,L)=\min\left\{
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}:
u_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}u_{\mathbf I}\ge1
\quad(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L))\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]

The indicator of a hitting set is a feasible dual vector, proving \(P_d\le m_d\). For an optimal dual vector, let \(F=\{\mathbf I:u_{\mathbf I}\ge1/(n+1)\}\). Every eligible support has at most \(n+1\) elements. If it missed \(F\), all of its coordinates would be strictly less than \(1/(n+1)\), and its dual sum would be strictly less than one. Thus \(F\) is a hitting set, and its weight is at most \( (n+1)\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}=(n+1)P_d\). This proves the pointwise inequalities in (7); divide by \(1+L\) and maximize to get the score inequalities. In particular the full charge on an occurrence has not been replaced by its barycentric coefficient at any point of this comparison.

We now verify the family (8) and the entire all-target separation. The affine map

\[
\mathbf x\longmapsto
\left(x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1},N-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}x_i\right)
\tag{22}
\]

identifies its base lattice support bijectively with \(\Omega_N\). Whenever a composition \(\mathbf j\in\Omega_N\) is used as the target argument of \(m_1\) or \(P_1\) below, it means the value at the corresponding original target \((j_1,\ldots,j_{N-1})\). Convexity and full dimensionality are immediate from the simplex inequalities and the interval factor. Every included integer fiber is precisely \([0,1]\), and every integer fiber outside the listed support is empty. Thus all forced lattice fibers and all boundary fibers are present, each with original mixed weight one. Stars and bars, equivalently extraction of the coefficient of \(t^N\) in \( (1-t)^{-N}\), gives the cardinality in (9). All root averages equal one at every barycentric component, so every cost is zero. Consequently both scores optimize at \(L=0\).

Let \(\mathbf j^0=(1,\ldots,1)\in\Omega_N\), the image of the original target with all \(N-1\) coordinates equal to one. At cost zero, every lifted height and the threshold in (6) equal one. The term \(c(a_{\mathbf I}-r)\) vanishes identically. A nonzero base normal therefore gives exactly a closed base halfspace through the target; a normal with zero base part gives the entire support. It follows from (6) that \(m_1(\mathbf j^0,0)\), with the target written in composition coordinates, is exactly the minimum number of sources in a closed base halfspace through \(\mathbf j^0\).

Let \(\mathbf K\) be uniform on \(\Omega_N\). A nontrivial base halfspace through \(\mathbf j^0\) has an equation \(\sum_i v_iK_i\ge0\) for some nonzero \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^N\) with \(\sum_i v_i=0\). To see the normalization explicitly, append a zero coefficient to its \(N-1\) original coefficients, then subtract the mean of the resulting \(N\) coefficients. On the affine hyperplane \(\sum_iK_i=N\) this leaves the through-target score unchanged, and a nonzero original normal cannot become the zero vector. Conversely any such zero-sum vector is nonconstant and defines a nontrivial affine functional on the composition hyperplane. In the following calculation these coefficients \(v_i\) are the source response's coefficients \(a_i\); they are distinct from the volume roots \(a_{\mathbf I}\).

Put \(Z=\sum_i v_iK_i\), \(s_2=\sum_i v_i^2>0\), and \(s_4=\sum_i v_i^4\). Exchangeability and \(\sum_iK_i=N\) give \(\mathbb EK_i=1\), hence \(\mathbb EZ=0\). We compute the second and fourth moments exactly. For an integer \(s\ge0\), use the factorial conventions \((x)_{\underline s}=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(x-j)\) and \((x)^{\overline s}=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(x+j)\), with empty products equal to one. For integers \(r_i\ge0\), with \(s=\sum_i r_i\le N\), the exact factorial moment identity is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\prod_i(K_i)_{\underline{r_i}}
&=\frac{(N)_{\underline s}}{(N)^{\overline s}}\prod_i r_i!,\\
\prod_i\sum_{k\ge0}(k)_{\underline{r_i}}t^k
&=\frac{t^s\prod_i r_i!}{(1-t)^{N+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Indeed the one-variable identity follows by differentiating the geometric series \(r_i\) times and multiplying by \(t^{r_i}\). Extracting the coefficient of \(t^N\) in the second line and dividing by \(W\) gives the factor
\(\binom{2N-1}{N-s}/\binom{2N-1}{N-1}=N!(N-1)!/[(N-s)!(N+s-1)!]\), which is exactly the first line. Define \(c_s=(N)_{\underline s}/(N)^{\overline s}\) for \(1\le s\le4\); \(N\ge16\) ensures all these factors are positive and at most one, with \(c_1=1\) and \(c_2=(N-1)/(N+1)\).

Using \(K_i^2=(K_i)_{\underline1}+(K_i)_{\underline2}\) in (23) gives \(\mathbb EK_i^2=c_1+2c_2\) and \(\mathbb EK_iK_j=c_2\) for \(i\ne j\). Since \(\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j=-s_2/2\),

\[
\mathbb EZ^2=(c_1+2c_2)s_2+2c_2\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j
=(c_1+c_2)s_2=\frac{2N}{N+1}s_2.
\tag{24}
\]

The cubic and quartic falling-factorial expansions are \(x^3=(x)_{\underline1}+3(x)_{\underline2}+(x)_{\underline3}\) and \(x^4=(x)_{\underline1}+7(x)_{\underline2}+6(x)_{\underline3}+(x)_{\underline4}\). Consequently, with all indices distinct whenever different letters occur,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EK_i^4&=c_1+14c_2+36c_3+24c_4,\\
\mathbb EK_i^3K_j&=c_2+6c_3+6c_4,\\
\mathbb EK_i^2K_j^2&=c_2+4c_3+4c_4,\\
\mathbb EK_i^2K_jK_k&=c_3+2c_4,\\
\mathbb EK_iK_jK_kK_l&=c_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

The zero-sum condition on \(\mathbf v\) gives the following exact coefficient identities:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i\ne j}v_i^3v_j&=-s_4,\\
\sum_{i<j}v_i^2v_j^2&=\frac{s_2^2-s_4}{2},\\
\sum_i\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\ne i}}v_i^2v_jv_k
&=s_4-\frac{s_2^2}{2},\\
\sum_{i<j<k<l}v_iv_jv_kv_l&=\frac{s_2^2}{8}-\frac{s_4}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

The first two follow by summing \(\sum_{j\ne i}v_j=-v_i\) and by expanding \(s_2^2\). For the third, the inner sum equals \( [(-v_i)^2-(s_2-v_i^2)]/2=v_i^2-s_2/2\). For the fourth, expand \( (\sum_i v_i)^4=0\), group its terms by multiplicities \(4\), \(3+1\), \(2+2\), \(2+1+1\), and \(1+1+1+1\), and substitute the first three identities; the result is \(0=6s_4-3s_2^2+24\sum_{i<j<k<l}v_iv_jv_kv_l\).

The same full multiplicity expansion, now with (25), is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EZ^4
&=(c_1+14c_2+36c_3+24c_4)s_4\\
&\quad+4(c_2+6c_3+6c_4)\sum_{i\ne j}v_i^3v_j\\
&\quad+6(c_2+4c_3+4c_4)\sum_{i<j}v_i^2v_j^2\\
&\quad+12(c_3+2c_4)\sum_i\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\ne i}}v_i^2v_jv_k\\
&\quad+24c_4\sum_{i<j<k<l}v_iv_jv_kv_l\\
&=(c_1+7c_2+12c_3+6c_4)s_4
+(3c_2+6c_3+3c_4)s_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

In particular \(0\le c_s\le1\), \(s_4\le s_2^2\), and (24) show \(\mathbb EZ^4\le26s_4+12s_2^2\le38s_2^2\le38(\mathbb EZ^2)^2\). Write \(\sigma^2=\mathbb EZ^2>0\). Hölder with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), followed by the fourth-moment bound, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2
&=\mathbb E\bigl(|Z|^{2/3}|Z|^{4/3}\bigr)
\le(\mathbb E|Z|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Z|^4)^{1/3},\\
\mathbb E|Z|
&\ge\frac{\sigma^3}{(\mathbb EZ^4)^{1/2}}
\ge\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{38}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

Let \(Z_+=\max(Z,0)\) and \(Z_-=\max(-Z,0)\). The mean-zero identity gives \(\mathbb EZ_+=\mathbb EZ_-=\mathbb E|Z|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz yields \(\mathbb EZ_+\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Z>0)}\) and \(\mathbb EZ_-\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Z<0)}\). Therefore both probabilities are at least \(1/152\). Each closed base halfspace through the target contains its corresponding strict event, so its mass is at least \(W/152\). Any atoms on \(Z=0\) are retained in the closed halfspace; they are never assumed null. Formula (6) now proves \(\mathcal B_1^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge m_1(\mathbf j^0,0)\ge W/152\).

To bound the fractional score at all centers, let \(\mathbf j\in\Omega_N\) be arbitrary and define, for \(\mathbf K\in\Omega_N\),

\[
u_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf K)=\min_{i:K_i>0}\frac{j_i}{K_i}.
\tag{29}
\]

The minimum has a nonempty index set because \(\sum_iK_i=N>0\); every ratio has a positive denominator, and the value is finite and nonnegative even when some \(j_i=0\). If \(\mathbf j=\sum_{\mathbf K\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf K}\mathbf K\) with positive coefficients, coordinatewise nonnegativity gives \(\lambda_{\mathbf K}K_i\le j_i\) for every source and coordinate. Thus \(\lambda_{\mathbf K}\le u_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf K)\), and summing over \(E\) proves its full-occurrence dual constraint \(\sum_{\mathbf K\in E}u_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf K)\ge1\). This works for every barycentric support, in particular for all supports of (21). For the central singleton, \(u_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf j)=1\), including targets on the simplex boundary.

Extend \(\mathbf j\) in (29) to the real simplex \(\{\mathbf j\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^N:\sum_i j_i=N\}\). For each fixed \(\mathbf K\), its expression is a minimum of finitely many linear functions and is therefore concave. The function \(\mathbf j\mapsto\sum_{\mathbf K\in\Omega_N}u_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf K)\) is consequently concave, and reindexing \(\Omega_N\) shows that it is invariant under all coordinate permutations. The average over permutations of any such \(\mathbf j\) is \(\mathbf j^0\). Concavity therefore bounds each constructed dual objective by its value at \(\mathbf j^0\), which is \(\sum_{\mathbf K}1/\max_iK_i\). Applying (21) at every target, and recalling that all costs vanish, gives

\[
\mathcal B_1(w)=\max_{\mathbf j\in\Omega_N}P_1(\mathbf j,0)
\le W\,\mathbb E\frac1{K_{\max}},
\qquad K_{\max}=\max_i K_i.
\tag{30}
\]

We supply the complete conditional maximum estimate. Let \(G_1,\ldots,G_N\) be independent with \(\mathbb P(G_i=k)=2^{-(k+1)}\) for integers \(k\ge0\). Each composition of total \(N\) has exactly the same joint probability \(2^{-2N}=4^{-N}\). Thus the conditional law given \(\sum_iG_i=N\) is exactly uniform on \(\Omega_N\), and

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sum_iG_i=N\right)
=\binom{2N-1}{N-1}4^{-N}
=\frac12\binom{2N}{N}4^{-N}
\ge\frac1{4\sqrt N}.
\tag{31}
\]

For the last inequality, set \(b_N=\binom{2N}{N}4^{-N}\). At \(N=1\), \(b_1=1/2\), and \(b_{N+1}/b_N=(2N+1)/(2N+2)\ge\sqrt{N/(N+1)}\), because the squared numerators differ by one after cross-multiplication. Induction gives \(b_N\ge1/(2\sqrt N)\), proving (31).

For any integer \(k\ge1\), independence and \(\mathbb P(G_i\ge k)=2^{-k}\) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(K_{\max}<k)
&=\mathbb P\left(\max_iG_i<k\,\middle|\,\sum_iG_i=N\right)\\
&\le\frac{\mathbb P(\max_iG_i<k)}{\mathbb P(\sum_iG_i=N)}
\le4\sqrt N(1-2^{-k})^N
\le4\sqrt N e^{-N2^{-k}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]

Take \(k=\lfloor\tfrac12\log_2N\rfloor\ge2\). Since \(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\) for \(x\ge2\), one has \(1/k\le4\log2/\log N\); also \(N2^{-k}\ge\sqrt N\). The identity \(\sum_iK_i=N\) implies \(K_{\max}\ge1\) everywhere. Splitting according to \(K_{\max}\ge k\), (32) therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E\frac1{K_{\max}}
\le\frac1k+\mathbb P(K_{\max}<k)
\le\frac{4\log2}{\log N}+4\sqrt N e^{-\sqrt N}.
\tag{33}
\]

To verify the remaining numerical estimate exactly, write \(x=\sqrt N\ge4\). The function \(f(x)=8x\log x\,e^{-x}\) decreases on this interval because \(f'(x)/f(x)=1/x+1/(x\log x)-1<0\), using \(\log x\ge\log4>1\). Also \(\log4<3/2\): the first four terms of \(e^{3/2}\) sum to \(67/16>4\). The terms through degree six in \(e^4\) sum to \(2185/45>48\), so \(f(4)=32\log4/e^4<48/e^4<1\). Consequently \(4\sqrt N e^{-\sqrt N}<1/\log N\). The same inequality \(\log4<3/2\) gives \(\log2<3/4\), and (33) implies

\[
\mathbb E\frac1{K_{\max}}
\le\frac{4\log2+1}{\log N}<\frac4{\log N}.
\tag{34}
\]

Equations (30) and (34) prove the uniform fractional upper bound in (10). Its denominator is positive, since a central singleton of mass one is feasible at every target. Combining the fractional bound with the integral lower bound gives the ratio \(\log N/608\). All computations used the exact distribution on the complete support (9), not a continuous approximation to the simplex or an unconditioned geometric law.

Finally, the conditional scalar bridge in the statement follows directly from (4): the assumed score inequality would give a point of depth at least \( (c/6)2^{-n}W\), and decreasing that coefficient to \(c_0=\min\{c/6,1/(2e)\}\in(0,1/e)\) preserves the bound. Neither the comparison (7) nor the separation (10) establishes this uniform premise. When \(W=0\), all containing-halfspace masses are nonnegative, so every point of the nonempty \(S\) satisfies the stipulated global zero bound. Zero-volume fibers were never normalized or given positive source capacity.
