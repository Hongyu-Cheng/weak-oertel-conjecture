## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and $d\ge0$. In any fixed unimodular integer-base coordinates, let $\pi_I$ delete integer coordinates indexed by $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$. For a compact convex set $K\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, including the empty set, put $A_I(K)=\mu_{n-|I|,d}(\pi_I K)$, where $\mu_{j,d}$ sums $d$-volume over the $\mathbb Z^j$ fibers. Use $\mu_{0,d}=\operatorname{vol}_d$ and count the nonempty singleton in dimension zero. Then
$$
|\mu_{n,d}(K)-\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(K)|\le \sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}} A_I(K).
$$
In particular, every closed ambient halfspace $H$ satisfies $\mu_{n,d}(C\cap H)\ge \operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C\cap H)-\sum_{I\ne\varnothing}A_I(C)$. Lower-dimensional sets and boundary fibers are included.

## Proof

On a nonempty closed interval $[a,b]$, the lattice count is $\lfloor b\rfloor-\lceil a\rceil+1$, which is nonnegative even when the interval has no integer point. Its difference from length lies between $-1$ and $1$. An empty section has no error. For $n=1$, integrate this comparison over the actual projection using Fubini, including counting in dimension zero.

For $n>1$, replace the sum in coordinate $1$ by its integral with the other base coordinates still sampled. The interval comparison costs at most $A_{\{1\}}(K)$. By induction the resulting partially continuous measure differs from ordinary volume by at most the sum, over nonempty $J\subseteq\{2,\ldots,n\}$, of the partially continuous measures of $\pi_J K$. Each of these is at most $A_J(K)+A_{J\cup\{1\}}(K)$ by the one-coordinate comparison. These contributions account for every nonempty index set exactly once. Compact convex projections and sections are measurable throughout, and closed endpoints retain the sampled boundary mass. Apply the result to $C\cap H$ and use monotonicity of each projected measure.
