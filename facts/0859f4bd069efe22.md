## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let n,d be arbitrary positive integers and let C be a convex body in R^n x R^d such that (x,y) belongs to C if and only if (-x,y) belongs to C. Only simultaneous base negation is required. Put Lambda=Z^n x R^d and S=C intersect Lambda. For a measurable A subset Lambda use the original mixed-integer volume
\[
\mathcal H_d(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y:(z,y)\in A\},
\]
and define h_S(a) as the infimum of H_d(S intersect H) over all closed ambient halfspaces H containing a, exactly as in the locked problem.

Retain the actual-fiber objects of 705d93afab077ade without changing their meaning or normalization:
\[
C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\},\quad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\quad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\},\quad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]
Here C_0=C_{0_n} and w_0=w_{0_n}. Let M be any finite real number with M>=1 such that w_z>=w_0/M for every z in D_+. Then w_0>0 and W>0. For z in D_+ set
\[
b_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
p_z=\frac{w_z}{W},\qquad
m=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_zb_z
  =\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}\int_{C_z}y\,dy.
\]
These are the original volume centroids, original probabilities, and original continuous marginal mean, as in 705d93afab077ade. The point (0_n,m) belongs to S and satisfies
\[
h_S((0_n,m))\ge\frac{W}{1024M(1+\log M)}.
\tag{1}
\]
More precisely, let Z have the original probabilities p_z and, conditional on Z=z, let Y be uniform volume on the actual C_z. The same m, chosen before any direction, satisfies for every nonzero beta in R^d
\[
\min\{\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0),
        \mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\le0)\}
\ge\frac1{512M(1+\log M)}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if M(1+log M)<=2^n, then every closed ambient halfspace containing (0_n,m) has original mixed-integer volume at least (1/1024)2^{-n}W. Thus c_0=1/1024, an absolute constant in (0,1/e), works for this class for every n,d. It suffices, more explicitly, that every positive original fiber obey w_z>=((1+n log 2)/2^n)w_0. Empty or zero-volume fibers require no volume comparison and contribute zero to all these volumes.

## Proof

We first verify the geometric and original-law hypotheses used in the accepted inputs. Compactness of C makes the set of integer bases with nonempty fiber finite. Each C_z is compact and convex. The symmetry gives C_{-z}=C_z and w_{-z}=w_z. If y belongs to C_z, the midpoint of (z,y) and (-z,y) belongs to C, so y belongs to C_0. Therefore C_z is contained in C_0 for every integer z, including those with zero-volume fibers, and D_+ is symmetric.

The central fiber has positive volume independently of the comparison assumption. Since C is a convex body, an open ambient ball of some radius r>0 centered at (x_*,y_*) is contained in C. For every v in R^d with norm less than r, (x_*,y_*+v) belongs to that ball. Its base reflection (-x_*,y_*+v) belongs to C. Their midpoint (0_n,y_*+v) belongs to C, so C_0 contains the open d-dimensional ball of radius r centered at y_*. Consequently w_0>0, 0_n belongs to D_+, and W>=w_0>0. Every positive-volume C_z is a full-dimensional convex body in R^d. In particular, the normalized means in the statement are defined. Under these symmetry hypotheses the zero-total-volume case of the locked problem cannot occur.

For every z in D_+, b_z belongs to C_z: otherwise strict separation from that compact convex set would give a linear functional whose value at b_z exceeds its maximum over C_z, contradicting the defining integral average. Thus every b_z belongs to C_0. Since the p_z are nonnegative and sum to one, convexity puts m in C_0. This proves (0_n,m) belongs to S. This choice uses only the original fibers and their volumes and is made before beta, alpha, or a halfspace is chosen.

For every measurable ambient set F, the original conditional law gives
\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in F)
=\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}
  \operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in F\}
=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)}W.
\tag{3}
\]
The omitted zero-volume fibers have zero contribution, even if nonempty. Retain the section count N and multivariate marginal density f of 705d93afab077ade: N(y)=#{z in Z^n:y in C_z} and
\[
f(y)=\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}\mathbf1_{C_z}(y)
    =\frac{N(y)}W\quad\hbox{almost everywhere}.
\]
The latter equality holds because the union of the finitely many nonempty zero-volume fibers is a Lebesgue-null subset of R^d. Integration shows f is a probability density and its mean is m. Put k=#D_+. The comparison gives W>=k w_0/M, and containment in C_0 gives
\[
f(y)\le\frac{k}{W}\mathbf1_{C_0}(y)
     \le\frac{M}{w_0}\mathbf1_{C_0}(y)
\quad\hbox{almost everywhere}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the original marginal is dominated by M times uniform volume on the actual central fiber. There is no replacement or equalization of original fiber weights.

Fix an arbitrary nonzero beta in R^d. Retain exactly the projection objects from 705d93afab077ade: U is uniform volume on C_0, q_beta is the density of beta dot Y, g_beta is the density of beta dot U, and mu_beta=beta dot m. An invertible linear change of coordinates with first coordinate beta dot y followed by integration over the remaining d-1 coordinates proves that both projected laws have real Lebesgue densities. For d=1 this is the usual one-dimensional change of variables. Fubini's theorem applied to (4) proves q_beta<=M g_beta almost everywhere. Both are probability densities with finite first and second absolute moments, because Y and U are supported on the compact C_0. The mean of q_beta is mu_beta, by linearity of the original mean.

The reference projection is nondegenerate. A nonzero linear functional takes a nontrivial interval of values on the ball contained in C_0, and uniform volume assigns positive mass to open subsets of that ball, so beta dot U is not almost surely constant. Its variance is finite and positive. The scalar projection assertion established and used in 705d93afab077ade states that such a nondegenerate projection of uniform volume on the actual convex body has a log-concave density. It applies here to the identical U, C_0, beta, and g_beta; there is no new normalization or reference body. We use this log-concavity conclusion, together with (4), rather than that predecessor's cubic cap estimate.

Apply 715faf86f9347fdb with the following exact alias map: its scalar original density f is q_beta here, its scalar reference density g is g_beta here, its original true mean m_f is mu_beta=beta dot m here, and its finite domination constant M is the same M. Its scalar f is distinct from the multivariate f(y) just retained from 705d93afab077ade. The required probability-density, upper-domination, log-concavity, and finite-moment hypotheses have all been checked. That theorem yields both original cap inequalities
\[
\int_{\mu_\beta}^{\infty}q_\beta(t)\,dt
\ge\frac1{512M(1+\log M)},\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\mu_\beta}q_\beta(t)\,dt
\ge\frac1{512M(1+\log M)}.
\]
These are precisely (2). Since q_beta is a Lebesgue density, the event beta dot Y=mu_beta has zero probability, so the closed endpoints are covered. Every cap is measured under the original law of Y, and mu_beta is the projection of the one already chosen m.

We now verify the paired mixed-normal transfer from 705d93afab077ade with this stronger cap estimate. For beta in R^d, including beta=0, let B_beta={y:beta dot (y-m)>=0}. For alpha in R^n and beta in R^d not both zero, define the closed halfspace through the fixed point
\[
H_0=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}.
\]
Partition D_+ minus {0_n} into unordered pairs {z,-z}. If y belongs to B_beta, at least one of alpha dot z+beta dot (y-m) and -alpha dot z+beta dot (y-m) is nonnegative. Therefore C_{-z}=C_z gives, for each pair,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}
 +\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_{-z}:-\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\\
&\qquad\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
 =\frac12\bigl(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
              +\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{-z}\cap B_\beta)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The pointwise inequality includes all boundary cases. The singleton zero base contributes exactly vol_d(C_0 intersect B_beta), at least half of its own marginal-cap contribution. Summing over the pairs and this singleton, with zero-volume fibers contributing zero, yields
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12\sum_{z\in D_+}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
=\frac W2\mathbb P(Y\in B_\beta).
\tag{5}
\]
For beta nonzero, (2) and (5) give W/[1024M(1+log M)]. For beta=0, B_beta is all of R^d, so (5) gives W/2, which is at least the same bound because M>=1 and 1+log M>=1. Thus pure base normals, pure continuous normals, and arbitrary mixed normals are all covered.

Every proper closed ambient halfspace H containing (0_n,m) can be written as {(x,y):alpha dot x+beta dot y>=t} for alpha,beta not both zero. Point containment gives t<=beta dot m. Its parallel halfspace H_0 through the point is therefore contained in H. Monotonicity of original mixed-integer volume and (5) give the asserted lower bound for H. If the whole ambient space is included as an improper closed halfspace, it has volume W and obeys the bound too; an empty halfspace cannot contain the point. Taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves (1).

If M(1+log M)<=2^n, (1) is at least (1/1024)2^{-n}W. The constant 1/1024 belongs to (0,1/e), for example because e<3<1024. For the explicit sufficient comparison, set M_n=2^n/(1+n log 2). The inequality exp(a)>=1+a at a=n log 2 gives M_n>=1. Also M_n<=2^n, hence 1+log M_n<=1+n log 2 and M_n(1+log M_n)<=2^n. If the stated original volumes obey w_z>=((1+n log 2)/2^n)w_0, they obey w_z>=w_0/M_n and the theorem applies with M=M_n. Equivalently, their original ratio w_0/min_{z in D_+}w_z may be as large as 2^n/(1+n log 2). No such comparison or symmetry has been deduced for arbitrary C, so the conclusion retains its stated class hypotheses.
