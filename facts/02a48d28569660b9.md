## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), let \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^n\), let \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\) be the standard basis, and set \(P_n=\operatorname{conv}\{-\mathbf1,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\}\) and \(C_n=P_n\times[-1,1]\). Then \(C_n\) is a full-dimensional rational convex body in \(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R\).

Use the locked problem's mixed-integer measure and depth. Thus \(S=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), \(\mathcal H_1(A)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{length}\{t:(\mathbf z,t)\in A\}\) for measurable \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\), \(W(C_n)=\mathcal H_1(S)\), and \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_1(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Write \(\mathcal D(C_n)=W(C_n)^{-1}\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)\). This normalized quantity \(\mathcal D(C_n)\) is the quantity denoted \(D(C_n)\) in the archived source; the unadorned \(D\) below denotes lattice support.

The entire lattice support is \(D=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{\mathbf0,-\mathbf1,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\}\). Every fiber at a base in \(D\) is exactly \([-1,1]\), and every other integer-base fiber is empty. Consequently \(W(C_n)=2(n+2)\), and the entire normalized fiber-volume table is \(p_{\mathbf z}=1/(n+2)\) for \(\mathbf z\in D\), extended by zero outside \(D\). Using natural logarithms, define

\[
I(\mathbf j)=-\log\inf_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n}
   \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}
       e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}
\quad(\mathbf j\in D),
\qquad
I_*=\min_{\mathbf j\in D}I(\mathbf j),
\qquad E(p)=e^{-I_*}.
\]

For this family, \(E(p)=1\), the fixed point \((\mathbf0,0)\in S\) has \(h_S((\mathbf0,0))=3\), and every \(\mathbf y\in S\) satisfies \(h_S(\mathbf y)\le3\). In particular, the supremum defining \(\mathcal D(C_n)\) is attained at that point and

\[
\mathcal D(C_n)=\frac{3}{2(n+2)},
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal D(C_n)}{E(p)}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
2^n\mathcal D(C_n)=\frac{3\,2^{n-1}}{n+2}\ge1.
\]

It follows that no absolute constant \(c>0\) can satisfy \(\mathcal D(C)\ge cE(p)\) for every convex body with positive mixed-integer volume and its entire normalized fiber-volume table, even when restricted to rational bodies with continuous dimension one. Every \(C_n\), however, satisfies the locked weak Oertel conclusion for every \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\).

## Proof

First verify the geometry and entire support. The vectors \(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf1\), \(1\le i\le n\), are linearly independent: if \(\sum_i c_i(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf1)=\mathbf0\) and \(s=\sum_i c_i\), each coordinate gives \(c_i+s=0\); summing these equations gives \((n+1)s=0\), and hence all \(c_i=0\). Thus the displayed vertices of \(P_n\) are affinely independent. The simplex \(P_n\), and therefore its product with \([-1,1]\), is compact, convex, and full-dimensional in its ambient space. All vertices of this product are integral, so \(C_n\) is rational.

For any \(\mathbf z\in P_n\), choose nonnegative barycentric coefficients \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\) summing to one such that \(\mathbf z=-\alpha_0\mathbf1+\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i\mathbf e_i\). Then \(z_i=\alpha_i-\alpha_0\in[-1,1]\). If \(\mathbf z\) is integral and some \(z_i=-1\), the equation \(\alpha_0=1+\alpha_i\) and the bounds \(0\le\alpha_0\le1\) force \(\alpha_0=1\) and all other coefficients to be zero. Hence \(\mathbf z=-\mathbf1\). Otherwise every coordinate of this integer point is nonnegative, and \(\sum_{i=1}^n z_i=1-(n+1)\alpha_0\le1\), so \(\mathbf z\) is \(\mathbf0\) or one of the \(\mathbf e_i\). Conversely, the listed vertices belong to \(P_n\), and \(\mathbf0\) is their average because \(-\mathbf1+\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf e_i=\mathbf0\). This proves the exact support assertion, including its converse. The product definition gives all the stated fibers, their volumes, and the normalized table.

For \(\mathbf j\in D\), the term with \(\mathbf z=\mathbf j\) bounds its exponential infimum below by \(p_{\mathbf j}>0\), while \(\mathbf a=\mathbf0\) bounds it above by one. Hence \(I(\mathbf j)\ge0\). The uniform law on \(D\) has mean \(\mathbf0\). For every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\), the elementary inequality \(e^u\ge1+u\) therefore gives \(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z}\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}(1+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z)=1\). Equality holds at \(\mathbf a=\mathbf0\), so \(I(\mathbf0)=0\). Together with the nonnegativity of all \(I(\mathbf j)\), this proves \(I_*=0\) and \(E(p)=1\).

We next prove the lower bound at the fixed point \((\mathbf0,0)\). Any closed halfspace containing that point can be written \(H=\{(\mathbf z,t):\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z+bt\ge c\}\), where \((\mathbf a,b)\ne(\mathbf0,0)\) and \(c\le0\). Its parallel halfspace \(H_0=\{(\mathbf z,t):\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z+bt\ge0\}\) is contained in \(H\), so it suffices to bound the mixed volume of \(S\cap H_0\).

Suppose first that \(b=0\). The entire fiber above \(\mathbf0\) belongs to \(H_0\). Write \(\mathbf v_0=-\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf v_i=\mathbf e_i\) for \(1\le i\le n\). Since \(\sum_{i=0}^n\mathbf v_i=\mathbf0\), the scores \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf v_i\) sum to zero. At least one of them is nonnegative, so \(H_0\) contains at least one entire vertex fiber as well. These two distinct fibers have total length four. Thus \(\mathcal H_1(S\cap H_0)\ge4\) in this case.

Suppose now that \(b\ne0\). Put \(s_{\mathbf z}=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z/|b|\) and \(F(s)=\min\{1,\max\{0,(1+s)/2\}\}\). Replacing \(t\) by \(\operatorname{sgn}(b)t\) preserves \([-1,1]\) and length; in the fiber above \(\mathbf z\), the halfspace becomes \(t\ge-s_{\mathbf z}\). Therefore its retained fraction is exactly \(F(s_{\mathbf z})\). The vertex scores satisfy \(\sum_{i=0}^n s_{\mathbf v_i}=0\). If one score is at least one, its retained fraction is one. If no score is at least one, then each satisfies \(F(s_{\mathbf v_i})\ge(1+s_{\mathbf v_i})/2\), including scores below minus one. Summing and using the zero score sum gives \(\sum_{i=0}^nF(s_{\mathbf v_i})\ge(n+1)/2\ge1\).

In both cases the vertex fibers contribute total retained length at least two. The central score is zero, so its fiber contributes length \(2F(0)=1\). This proves \(\mathcal H_1(S\cap H)\ge\mathcal H_1(S\cap H_0)\ge3\) for every containing halfspace. Thus \(h_S((\mathbf0,0))\ge3\).

The closed halfspace \(\{(\mathbf z,t):-\sum_{i=1}^n z_i+t\ge0\}\) contains \((\mathbf0,0)\) and has mixed volume exactly three. Indeed, at \(-\mathbf1\) its inequality is \(n+t\ge0\), which holds throughout \([-1,1]\) for every \(n\ge1\). At each \(\mathbf e_i\) it retains only the endpoint \(t=1\), of length zero. At \(\mathbf0\) it retains \([0,1]\), of length one. This proves the matching equality \(h_S((\mathbf0,0))=3\), including \(n=1\).

It remains to bound the depth at every other point. Fix \(t_0\in[-1,1]\), and choose \(b=1\) if \(t_0\ge0\), and \(b=-1\) if \(t_0<0\). Then \(\{t\in[-1,1]:b(t-t_0)\ge0\}\) has length \(1-|t_0|\le1\), and \(|b(t-t_0)|\le2\) throughout \([-1,1]\). Fix any real \(L>2\).

At the central point \((\mathbf0,t_0)\), use the containing closed halfspace

\[
H=\left\{(\mathbf z,t):-L\sum_{i=1}^n z_i
                              +b(t-t_0)\ge0\right\}.
\]

At \(-\mathbf1\) the left-hand side is at least \(Ln-2>0\), so the whole fiber is retained. At each \(\mathbf e_i\) it is at most \(-L+2<0\), so the fiber is excluded. At \(\mathbf0\) the retained length is \(1-|t_0|\). Therefore \(h_S((\mathbf0,t_0))\le\mathcal H_1(S\cap H)=3-|t_0|\le3\).

For a vertex \(\mathbf v\in\{-\mathbf1,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\}\), choose an explicit exposing linear functional: let \(\ell_{-\mathbf1}(\mathbf z)=-\sum_{i=1}^n z_i\), and let \(\ell_{\mathbf e_i}(\mathbf z)=z_i\). On the complete support \(D\), each satisfies \(\ell_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf z)-\ell_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf v)\le-1\) whenever \(\mathbf z\ne\mathbf v\). For \(\mathbf v=-\mathbf1\), its value at that vertex is \(n\), versus zero at the origin and minus one at every \(\mathbf e_i\). For \(\mathbf v=\mathbf e_i\), its value there is one, versus zero at the origin and at every other \(\mathbf e_j\), and minus one at \(-\mathbf1\). The containing closed halfspace

\[
H=\left\{(\mathbf z,t):
L\bigl(\ell_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf z)-\ell_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf v)\bigr)
+b(t-t_0)\ge0\right\}
\]

excludes every other support fiber, because its left-hand side there is at most \(-L+2<0\). In the selected fiber it retains precisely length \(1-|t_0|\). Thus \(h_S((\mathbf v,t_0))\le1-|t_0|\le1\). The established support classification exhausts every point of \(S\), proving the all-point upper bound. Combined with the fixed-point equality and \(W(C_n)=2(n+2)\), it gives \(\mathcal D(C_n)=3/[2(n+2)]\).

Since \(E(p)=1\), the ratio \(\mathcal D(C_n)/E(p)=3/[2(n+2)]\) tends to zero as \(n\) tends to infinity. For any proposed absolute \(c>0\), an integer \(n\) with \(n+2>3/(2c)\) violates \(\mathcal D(C_n)\ge cE(p)\), proving the stated obstruction.

Finally, \(3\,2^{n-1}\ge n+2\) for all integers \(n\ge1\): equality holds at \(n=1\); if the inequality holds at \(n\), doubling yields \(3\,2^n\ge2(n+2)\ge n+3\), which is the next case. Consequently \(h_S((\mathbf0,0))/W(C_n)=\mathcal D(C_n)\ge2^{-n}\). For every \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), this fixed point therefore satisfies \(h_S((\mathbf0,0))\ge c_0 2^{-n}W(C_n)\). The vanishing ratio to \(E(p)\) does not contradict the locked statement.
