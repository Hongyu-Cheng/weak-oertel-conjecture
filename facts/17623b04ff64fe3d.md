## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).  Let \(P=-P\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be a convex body, let \(\rho:P\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous, concave, even, and satisfy \(\rho(0)>0\), and let \(\mathcal E=T B_2^d\) for an invertible linear map \(T:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d\).  Let \(\phi:P\to\mathbb R^d\) be any map for which
\[
 \mathcal C:=\{(u,y):u\in P,\ y\in\phi(u)+\rho(u)\mathcal E\}
\]
is a convex body.  For every integer \(L\ge1\), define the base dilate
\[
 C_L:=\{(x,y):x/L\in P,\ y\in\phi(x/L)+\rho(x/L)\mathcal E\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 (C_L)_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C_L\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d((C_L)_z),\qquad
 W_L:=\sum_z a_z.
\]
Thus, writing \(v_{\mathcal E}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(\mathcal E)\),
\[
 (C_L)_z=\phi(z/L)+\rho(z/L)\mathcal E,
 \qquad a_z=v_{\mathcal E}\rho(z/L)^d
\]
when \(z/L\in P\), with \(a_z=0\) otherwise.
For \(k\ge1\), put
\[
 \gamma_k:=\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^k.
\]

For every \(z\) with \(a_z>0\), put
\(\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}((C_L)_z)\).  As in accepted fact
`8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, a free matching is a nonnegative family \(q=(q_e)\)
on unordered pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\) of distinct positive fibers with
\(z\equiv z'\pmod2\), subject to
\[
 d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\le a_z/2.
\]
It is affinely half-metronoid realizable if there are an affine map
\(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) and points
\(x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z\), \(x_{e,z'}\in\mathcal K_{z'}\) on every
positive edge such that
\[
 \frac{x_{e,z}+x_{e,z'}}2=s\left(\frac{z+z'}2\right).
\]
Let \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_L)\) and
\(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)\) be the suprema of \(\sum_eq_e\)
over all free and all affinely realizable free matchings, respectively.

For completeness, use the exact reflected-cloud normalization of accepted fact
`c1e3b8400d5b5b10`.  For every occupied \(m\), let \(\mathcal R_m\)
contain one representative from each unordered nonzero pair \(\{v,-v\}\)
with \(a_{m+v}a_{m-v}>0\), put
\[
 c_z:=\phi(z/L),\quad
 \alpha_v:=\min\{a_{m+v},a_{m-v}\},\quad
 q_v:=\frac{c_{m+v}+c_{m-v}}2,\quad q_0:=c_m,
\]
and, for nonempty \(J\subseteq\{0\}\sqcup\mathcal R_m\), put
\[
 r_{m,J}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{q_i:i\in J\},
\]
\[
 E_{m,J}:=\gamma_da_m\mathbf1_{\{0\in J\}}
 +\gamma_{2d}\sum_{v\in J\cap\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v.
\]
Then
\[
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_L)
 :=2^n\sup_{m:a_m>0}\ \sup_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{0\}\sqcup\mathcal R_m}
 \frac{E_{m,J}}{r_{m,J}+1}.
\]
Let \(h_{\max}(C_L)\) denote maximum mixed-integer halfspace depth.  Put
\[
 \beta_d:=\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{(d+1)\omega_d}
 =\frac{2\Gamma(d/2+1)}{(d+1)\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d+1)/2)},
\]
where \(\omega_j=\operatorname{vol}_j(B_2^j)\).

Then the following assertions hold.

1.  For every Euclidean ball \(B(c,r)\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) with \(r>0\) and volume \(a\),
\[
 \mathsf M_{a/2}(B(c,r))=B(c,\beta_dr).
\]
More generally, for \(r>0\) and \(a=\operatorname{vol}_d(c+r\mathcal E)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_{a/2}(c+r\mathcal E)=c+\beta_dr\mathcal E.
\]
For every \(d\ge1\),
\[
 \beta_d\ge\frac{\sqrt{2d/\pi}}{d+1}\ge\frac1{3\sqrt d},
 \qquad
 \log(1+\beta_d)\ge\frac1{4\sqrt d}.
\]

2.  Fix \(L\), an integral occupied midpoint \(m\), and one representative from each unordered nonzero pair \(\{v,-v\}\) for which \(a_{m+v}a_{m-v}>0\).  Write
\[
 r_0:=\rho(m/L),\quad r_\pm:=\rho((m\pm v)/L),\quad
 \overline r_v:=\frac{r_++r_-}{2}.
\]
Call \(v\) flat at \(m\) when
\[
 r_0\le(1+\beta_d)\overline r_v,
\]
and put
\[
 \mathcal O_{\mathrm{flat}}(m)
 :=\sum_{v\text{ flat at }m}\min\{a_{m+v},a_{m-v}\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)
 \ge\frac12\mathcal O_{\mathrm{flat}}(m).
\]
Every nonflat reflected pair satisfies
\[
 a_m>(1+\beta_d)^d\sqrt{a_{m+v}a_{m-v}}
 \ge e^{\sqrt d/4}\sqrt{a_{m+v}a_{m-v}}.
\]

3.  Let \(\rho_0:=\max_P\rho=\rho(0)\), and define the probability measure
\[
 d\pi_d(u):=\frac{\rho(u)^d\,du}{\int_P\rho^d}.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\pi_d}\!\left[\log\frac{\rho_0}{\rho(U)}\right]\le\frac nd.
\]
Consequently, for every \(\beta>0\),
\[
 \pi_d\left\{u:\rho(u)\ge\frac{\rho_0}{1+\beta}\right\}
 \ge1-\frac{n}{d\log(1+\beta)}.
\]

4.  Exactly one of the following two parameter regimes applies.

(a) If \(d\log(1+\beta_d)\ge2n\), then, as \(L\to\infty\) through the positive integers,
\[
 \liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{W_L}\ge\frac18,
 \qquad
 \liminf_{L\to\infty}
 \frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_L)}\ge\frac12.
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{W_L}>\frac19,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_L)}>\frac25.
\]

(b) If \(d\log(1+\beta_d)<2n\), then \(d<64n^2\), and for every \(L\),
\[
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_L)}{W_L}
 \ge\frac{\gamma_{2d}2^{n-1}}{d+1}
 >\frac1{400}.
\]

Hence, for the fixed data above and all sufficiently large \(L\),
\[
 \max\left\{
 \frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{W_L},
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_L)}{W_L}
 \right\}>\frac1{400},
\]
and
\[
 \frac{h_{\max}(C_L)}{2^{-n}W_L}>\frac1{400}.
\]

5.  For the same fixed data, all sufficiently large \(L\) satisfy the strict no-rank-drop condition of accepted fact `c1e3b8400d5b5b10`:
\[
 \sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
 <2^{\dim A-n}W_L
\]
for every proper primitive affine subspace \(A\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\).  No threshold uniform over varying \(n,d,P,\rho,\phi\), or \(\mathcal E\) is asserted.

## Proof

The linear map \((u,y)\mapsto(Lu,y)\) takes \(\mathcal C\) onto \(C_L\), so every \(C_L\) is a convex body and its fibers and their volumes are exactly as stated.  Evenness and concavity give
\[
 \rho(0)\ge\frac{\rho(u)+\rho(-u)}2=\rho(u),
\]
so \(\rho_0=\rho(0)>0\).

We first determine the half-metronoid of a ball.  Translation and positive scalar covariance reduce to \(B_2^d\), whose volume is \(\omega_d\).  The set \(\mathsf M_{\omega_d/2}(B_2^d)\) is compact and convex by accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, and it is invariant under every orthogonal map, hence it is a centered Euclidean ball.  In a fixed unit direction, the exact support-function bathtub argument of `6769574fc1bd9f8d` selects the upper half-ball.  Its normalized first moment is
\[
 \frac{2}{\omega_d}
 \int_{B_2^d\cap\{x_1\ge0\}}x_1\,dx
 =\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_d}
 \int_0^1t(1-t^2)^{(d-1)/2}\,dt
 =\frac{2\omega_{d-1}}{(d+1)\omega_d}=\beta_d.
\]
This proves the exact ball formula.  Applying an invertible affine map to the representing densities proves the ellipsoid formula.

The gamma-function identity for \(\omega_j\) gives the displayed expression for \(\beta_d\).  Log-convexity of \(\Gamma\), which follows directly from Holder's inequality applied to its defining integral, gives, for \(x>0\),
\[
 \Gamma(x+1/2)^2\le\Gamma(x)\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)^2.
\]
Thus \(\Gamma(x+1)/\Gamma(x+1/2)\ge\sqrt x\).  With \(x=d/2\),
\[
 \beta_d\ge\frac{\sqrt{2d/\pi}}{d+1}.
\]
The latter is at least \(1/(3\sqrt d)\), because
\(3d\sqrt{2/\pi}\ge d+1\) for every integer \(d\ge1\).  Finally, for
\(x=1/(3\sqrt d)\le1/3\),
\[
 \log(1+\beta_d)\ge\log(1+x)\ge\frac{x}{1+x}\ge\frac1{4\sqrt d}.
\]
This proves assertion 1.

For assertion 2, abbreviate
\(c_0=\phi(m/L)\) and \(c_\pm=\phi((m\pm v)/L)\).  Convexity gives
\[
 \frac12\bigl((C_L)_{m+v}+(C_L)_{m-v}\bigr)
 =\frac{c_++c_-}{2}+\overline r_v\mathcal E
 \subseteq c_0+r_0\mathcal E.
\]
After applying \(T^{-1}\), inclusion of these two Euclidean balls implies
\[
 \left\|T^{-1}\left(\frac{c_++c_-}{2}-c_0\right)\right\|_2
 \le r_0-\overline r_v.
\]
If \(v\) is flat, the right side is at most \(\beta_d\overline r_v\).  Assertion 1 therefore gives
\[
 c_0\in\frac12\left(
 \mathsf M_{a_{m+v}/2}((C_L)_{m+v})
 +\mathsf M_{a_{m-v}/2}((C_L)_{m-v})
 \right).
\]
For every flat pair \(e=\{m+v,m-v\}\), set
\[
 q_e:=\frac12\min\{a_{m+v},a_{m-v}\}.
\]
These are eligible same-parity off-diagonal edges, every endpoint occurs in only one pair about the fixed midpoint \(m\), and its degree is at most \(a_z/2\).  The constant affine map \(s\equiv c_0\) and the preceding membership realize every such edge in the sense of `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`.  Their total matching mass is \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{flat}}(m)/2\), proving the affine-matching bound.

If \(v\) is nonflat, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 r_0>(1+\beta_d)\overline r_v
 \ge(1+\beta_d)\sqrt{r_+r_-}.
\]
Raising to the \(d\)-th power and multiplying by \(v_{\mathcal E}\) proves the first volume jump.  Assertion 1 gives
\(d\log(1+\beta_d)\ge\sqrt d/4\), proving the exponential form.

We next prove assertion 3.  On \(\{\rho>0\}\), define
\[
 V(u):=d\log\frac{\rho_0}{\rho(u)},
\]
and extend \(V\) by \(+\infty\) where \(\rho=0\).  Since the negative logarithm is convex and decreasing and \(\rho\) is concave, \(V\) is convex.  It has minimum zero.  For \(t>0\), put
\[
 A(t):=\operatorname{vol}_n\{u\in P:V(u)\le t\}.
\]
If \(0<s<t\) and \(u_0\) is a minimizer, convexity shows
\[
 u_0+\frac{s}{t}\bigl(\{V\le t\}-u_0\bigr)\subseteq\{V\le s\}.
\]
Hence \(A(t)/t^n\) is nonincreasing.  Write \(A(t)=t^nb(t)\), with \(b\) nonincreasing.  Layer-cake integration gives
\[
 \int_Pe^{-V}\,du=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}A(t)\,dt
\]
and
\[
 \int_PVe^{-V}\,du=\int_0^\infty(t-1)e^{-t}A(t)\,dt.
\]
Under the measure with density \(t^ne^{-t}\,dt\), the mean of \(t\) is \(n+1\).  Since \(b\) is nonincreasing, the reversed covariance inequality yields
\[
 \int_0^\infty(t-(n+1))t^ne^{-t}b(t)\,dt\le0.
\]
Indeed, after normalizing that measure, twice the covariance of \(t\) and
\(b(t)\) is the double integral of
\((t-s)(b(t)-b(s))\), which is nonpositive pointwise.
Therefore
\[
 \int_PVe^{-V}\,du\le n\int_Pe^{-V}\,du.
\]
Because \(e^{-V}=(\rho/\rho_0)^d\), division by \(d\) and normalization prove the expectation bound.  Markov's inequality applied to \(\log(\rho_0/\rho(U))\) proves the probability bound.

For assertion 4, define
\[
 \Omega:=\left\{u\in P:\rho(u)\ge\frac{\rho_0}{1+\beta_d}\right\},
 \qquad
 W_{\Omega,L}:=\sum_{z/L\in\Omega}a_z,
 \qquad
 a_0=v_{\mathcal E}\rho_0^d.
\]
The set \(\Omega\) is a symmetric convex body: it is a convex superlevel set, and continuity together with its level being strictly below \(\rho_0\) puts a neighborhood of zero inside it.  Every nonzero reflected pair \(\{z,-z\}\) with \(z/L\in\Omega\) is flat at midpoint zero.  Assertion 2 therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)\ge\frac{W_{\Omega,L}-a_0}{4}.
\]
The degree constraints in the definition from `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` give, for every free matching \(q\),
\[
 2|q|=\sum_zd_z(q)\le\frac{W_L}{2},
\]
so \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_L)\le W_L/4\).  For all sufficiently large \(L\), this free capacity is positive, since the positive neighborhood of zero contains a nonzero pair \(\{z,-z\}\).

Standard lattice Riemann sums, applicable because boundaries of convex bodies have Lebesgue measure zero, give
\[
 \frac{W_L}{v_{\mathcal E}L^n}\longrightarrow\int_P\rho^d,
 \qquad
 \frac{W_{\Omega,L}}{v_{\mathcal E}L^n}\longrightarrow\int_\Omega\rho^d,
 \qquad
 \frac{a_0}{W_L}\longrightarrow0.
\]
If \(d\log(1+\beta_d)\ge2n\), assertion 3 with \(\beta=\beta_d\) gives
\[
 \frac{\int_\Omega\rho^d}{\int_P\rho^d}\ge\frac12.
\]
The last four displays yield the two liminf bounds and hence the stated eventual strict bounds.

Suppose instead that \(d\log(1+\beta_d)<2n\).  Assertion 1 implies
\(\sqrt d/4<2n\), hence \(d<64n^2\).  The volume profile is reflection symmetric about zero.  In the notation defining \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}\) in `c1e3b8400d5b5b10`, take \(m=0\) and the full reflected cloud.  Its affine dimension is at most \(d\), and
\[
 \gamma_da_0+\gamma_{2d}\sum_{v/\pm}a_v
 =\gamma_da_0+\frac{\gamma_{2d}}2(W_L-a_0)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_{2d}}2W_L,
\]
where \(\gamma_d\ge\gamma_{2d}\).  Directly from the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}\), without any small-capacity assumption, this gives
\[
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_L)}{W_L}
 \ge\frac{\gamma_{2d}2^{n-1}}{d+1}
 >\frac{2^{n-1}}{e(d+1)}.
\]
Here \(\gamma_{2d}>1/e\), as also proved in `a0f7746b5927f3d9`.  Since \(d<64n^2\) and \(d\) is integral, \(d+1\le64n^2\).  Moreover
\[
 \min_{n\ge1}\frac{2^{n-1}}{n^2}=\frac49.
\]
Thus the last display is greater than \(1/(144e)>1/400\); the final numerical inequality follows, for example, from
\[
 e=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac1{k!}
 <\frac52+\frac{1/6}{1-1/4}=\frac{49}{18}<\frac{25}{9}.
\]
This proves the dichotomy.

For the depth conclusion in regime (a), assertion 1 of `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` gives
\[
 \frac{h_{\max}(C_L)}{2^{-n}W_L}
 \ge\gamma_d\frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_L)}{W_L}
 >\frac1{9e}>\frac1{400}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(L\).  In regime (b), the definition in `c1e3b8400d5b5b10` and the componentwise depth theorem `a0f7746b5927f3d9` give
\[
 h_{\max}(C_L)\ge2^{-n}R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_L),
\]
so the normalized depth is again greater than \(1/400\).

It remains to prove assertion 5.  Choose \(R>0\) with \(P\subseteq B_2^n(0,R)\).  If \(A\) is any affine subspace of dimension \(q\), distinct points of \(A\cap\mathbb Z^n\) have Euclidean distance at least one.  The intrinsic \(q\)-balls of radius \(1/2\) about the points of \(A\cap LP\cap\mathbb Z^n\) are disjoint and lie in \(A\cap B_2^n(0,LR+1/2)\).  Comparing intrinsic volumes gives the uniform bound
\[
 \#(A\cap LP\cap\mathbb Z^n)\le(2LR+1)^q,
\]
with the same conclusion for \(q=0\).  Also
\[
 W_L\sim v_{\mathcal E}L^n\int_P\rho^d,
 \qquad
 \max_za_z\le v_{\mathcal E}\rho_0^d.
\]
Uniformly over all proper affine \(A\), therefore,
\[
 \frac{\sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z}{W_L}=O(L^{-1})\longrightarrow0.
\]
For sufficiently large \(L\), this ratio is smaller than \(2^{-n}\), and hence smaller than \(2^{q-n}\) for every \(q<n\).  Continuity and \(\rho(0)>0\) also imply that the positive lattice support contains \(0,e_1,\ldots,e_n\) for all sufficiently large \(L\), so it has full affine rank.  This is exactly the strict no-rank-drop condition in `c1e3b8400d5b5b10` and completes the proof.
