## Statement

For every integer \(k\geq2\), put \(n=k^2\), \(t=k^{-1/4}\), and
\(\theta=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^k\). Let \(\beta\in\mathbb R^n\) consist of \(k\) consecutive copies of \(\theta\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_k&=\{0,1\}^k\cup\{0,-1\}^k,
&K_k&=\operatorname{conv}(\Omega_k),\\
P_k&=\mathbf1_n+K_k^k,
&D&=P_k\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(K_k^k\) denotes the Cartesian product of \(k\) copies and \(\mathbf1_n\) is the all-one vector. Then \(P_k\) is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope and
\(D=\mathbf1_n+\Omega_k^k\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^n\).

For \(I\in D\), set \(w_I=e^{-\beta\cdot I}\), extend \(w_I=0\) outside \(D\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=\sum_{I\in D}w_I,\\
\tau_w(J)&=\inf\left\{\sum_{I\in D\cap H}w_I:
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\right\},\\
S_J&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min(w_{J+v},w_{J-v}),\\
R&=\sum_{J\in D}w_JS_J,
&A&=\sum_{J\in D}w_J\tau_w(J).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(S_J\) is the full oriented sum, including \(v=0\). For every \(J\in D\),
\(\tau_w(J)=(S_J+w_J)/2\). Consequently

\[
A=\frac12\left(R+\sum_{J\in D}w_J^2\right)\leq R,
\qquad
0\leq\frac{2^nA}{W^2}
\leq\frac{2^nR}{W^2}
\leq
3e^{5/2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi(3+\cosh2)}8}\,k^{-1/4}.
\tag{1}
\]

Both normalized averages therefore tend to zero as \(k\to\infty\). In particular, there is no absolute \(c>0\) for which \(A\geq cW^2/2^n\) holds throughout the registered ternary class.

## Proof

First we verify the complete lattice support. Every point \(x\in K_k\) can be written as \(x=\lambda u_+-(1-\lambda)u_-\), where \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\) and \(u_+,u_-\in[0,1]^k\): group a convex combination defining \(K_k\) according to its two cubes, whose convex hulls are \([0,1]^k\) and \([-1,0]^k\). Thus \(-1\leq x_i\leq1\) and \(x_i-x_j\leq1\) for all coordinates \(i,j\). An integral \(x\in K_k\) cannot have both a coordinate \(1\) and a coordinate \(-1\). It must belong to \(\{0,1\}^k\) or to \(\{0,-1\}^k\). The reverse inclusion is part of the definition of \(K_k\), so \(K_k\cap\mathbb Z^k=\Omega_k\). Products and the integral translation give the asserted formula for \(D\). The points \(\pm e_i\) belong to \(K_k\), so \(K_k\), and hence \(P_k\), is full-dimensional.

We will use centered coordinates \(x=I-\mathbf1_n\in\Omega_k^k\). This is the exact coordinate change relating the construction to the stated ternary support. Since every block of \(\beta\) has coordinate sum zero, \(w_I=e^{-\beta\cdot x}\). Translation also preserves containing closed halfspaces, reflected displacements, and all the stated sums.

Every nonzero point of \(\Omega_k\) is an exposed vertex of \(K_k\). To prove this explicitly, for a nonempty \(E\subseteq[k]\) consider the positive Boolean point \(\mathbf1_E\), and take a vector \(a\) whose coordinates are \(2k\) on \(E\) and \(-1\) off \(E\). Among positive Boolean points, \(a\cdot\mathbf1_F=2k|E\cap F|-|F\setminus E|\) has its unique maximum \(2k|E|\) at \(F=E\). Among negative Boolean points,
\(a\cdot(-\mathbf1_F)\leq k-|E|<2k|E|\). Thus \(\mathbf1_E\) uniquely maximizes \(a\) on \(\Omega_k\), hence on \(K_k\). The values on \(\Omega_k\) are integers, so every other support point has a gap of at least one. Changing the sign of \(a\) exposes \(-\mathbf1_E\), with the same gap property.

Fix an arbitrary center \(J=\mathbf1_n+x\in D\), and write its \(k\) centered blocks as \(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(k)}\in\Omega_k\). Let \(s\) be the number of its zero blocks. Keep every nonzero block fixed and let the zero blocks vary in \(\Omega_k^s\). This gives a subset of the original support containing \(J\). If \(y\in\Omega_k^s\) denotes those varying blocks and \(\beta_s\) is the concatenation of \(s\) copies of \(\theta\), their original weights are \(w_Je^{-\beta_s\cdot y}\). Put
\(T_s=\sum_{y\in\Omega_k^s}e^{-|\beta_s\cdot y|}\), with \(T_0=1\).

If \(J+v,J-v\in D\), each nonzero block of \(x\) is the midpoint of the corresponding endpoint blocks. Its exposing functional forces both endpoint blocks to equal that block of \(x\). Thus the displacement vanishes on every nonzero block. On a zero block, every pair \(y,-y\) with \(y\in\Omega_k\) is allowed. It follows, with the stated orientation and the central displacement included, that

\[
S_J=w_JT_s.
\tag{2}
\]

Any closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one member of each pair indexed by \(y,-y\) in the fixed-block subset. Its mass on this subset is therefore at least the central mass \(w_J\), plus \(w_Je^{-|\beta_s\cdot y|}\) for each unordered nonzero antipodal pair. Its total original mass is at least that subset mass. Hence
\(\tau_w(J)\geq w_J(T_s+1)/2\).

We next construct one original closed halfspace attaining this bound, including all zero-energy boundary pairs. For \(s>0\), choose a vector \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^{ks}\) satisfying \(\gamma\cdot y\neq0\) for every nonzero \(y\in\Omega_k^s\). The explicit choice \(\gamma_j=2^{j-1}\) for \(1\leq j\leq ks\) works: in a nonzero vector with coordinates in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), the highest nonzero coordinate contributes more in absolute value than the sum of all preceding contributions. Since the support is finite, one may choose \(\eta>0\) so small that \(b=\beta_s+\eta\gamma\) satisfies \(\operatorname{sign}(b\cdot y)=\operatorname{sign}(\beta_s\cdot y)\) on every \(y\) with \(\beta_s\cdot y\neq0\). For \(\beta_s\cdot y=0\) and \(y\neq0\), the sign of \(b\cdot y\) is the nonzero sign of \(\gamma\cdot y\).

Thus the closed halfspace \(b\cdot y\geq0\) contains the lighter endpoint of every pair with unequal weights, exactly one endpoint of every nonzero equal-weight antipodal pair, and the origin. Its mass on the varying blocks is exactly \((T_s+1)/2\).

To realize that restriction by one halfspace in the full original space, select the above integer exposing vector \(a_r\) for each nonzero block \(x^{(r)}\), and in centered coordinates \(X\in\Omega_k^k\) set
\(L(X)=\sum_{r:x^{(r)}\neq0}a_r\cdot(X^{(r)}-x^{(r)})\).
Every summand is nonpositive. Consequently \(L(X)=0\) precisely when all the selected blocks equal their prescribed points; otherwise \(L(X)\leq-1\). Write \(X_{\mathrm{zero}}\) for the remaining blocks and put \(B=\max_{y\in\Omega_k^s}|b\cdot y|\). The single affine closed halfspace
\(L(X)+(1+B)^{-1}b\cdot X_{\mathrm{zero}}\geq0\)
contains \(x\), excludes every support point with \(L(X)\leq-1\), and restricts to \(b\cdot y\geq0\) on the fixed-block subset. Translating it by \(\mathbf1_n\) gives the required original halfspace. If \(s=0\), the halfspace \(L(X)\geq0\) selects exactly \(x\) from the support, so it gives the same conclusion with \(T_0=1\). If all blocks are zero, the sum defining \(L\) is zero and the construction is simply the already defined halfspace on all varying blocks.

We have proved at every original center, without discarding any boundary site,

\[
\tau_w(J)=\frac{w_J(T_s+1)}2
=\frac{S_J+w_J}2.
\tag{3}
\]

Multiplying by \(w_J\) and summing proves the identity for \(A\) in (1). Also \(S_J\geq w_J\) because its \(v=0\) term is \(w_J\), so \(\sum_Jw_J^2\leq R\) and \(A\leq R\).

It remains to bound the full reflected average. The assignments \(I=J+v\), \(K=J-v\) give a bijection between its contributing pairs \((J,v)\) and ordered pairs \(I,K\in D\) with equal coordinatewise parity. For the converse, equal parity makes their midpoint integral, convexity places it in \(P_k\), and lattice completeness places it in \(D\). Log-affinity therefore gives the exact identity

\[
R=\sum_{\substack{I,K\in D\\I\equiv K\pmod2}}
w_Iw_K
\exp\!\left(-\frac{|\beta\cdot(I-K)|}{2}\right).
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, the absolute value is taken only after adding all block energies.

We calculate the relevant one-block quantities by an exact subset count. Put \(M=2^k\) and \(u=\cosh t\). In centered coordinates, the parity class indexed by a nonempty subset \(E\subseteq[k]\) contains exactly \(\mathbf1_E,-\mathbf1_E\). Its total weight is \(2\cosh(\theta\cdot\mathbf1_E)\). The empty parity class contains only the origin, with weight one. Of the \(M\) subsets, exactly \(M/2\) contain either both or neither of the first two coordinates, and \(M/4\) contain just each one of them. Thus their energies \(\theta\cdot\mathbf1_E\) are respectively \(0,t,-t\). The first count includes the empty subset. The one-block total mass \(W_0\) and squared parity-mass sum \(Q\) are consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_0&=1+2(M/2-1)+2u(M/2)=M(1+u)-1,\\
Q&=1+4(M/2-1)+4u^2(M/2)\\
&=2M(1+u^2)-3=M(3+\cosh(2t))-3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, \(W=W_0^k\) and \(2^n=M^k\).

Choose an ordered equal-parity endpoint pair in a single block with probability proportional to the product of its two weights, whose total is \(Q\). Let \(V\) be its energy difference divided by \(2t\). Every diagonal pair has \(V=0\). For a nonempty parity class with energy \(t\) or \(-t\), its two oppositely oriented off-diagonal pairs have \(V=1\) and \(V=-1\); each has weight product one. There are exactly \(M/2\) such classes. All other pairs have \(V=0\). Therefore

\[
\mathbb P(V=1)=\mathbb P(V=-1)=\frac{M}{2Q},
\qquad
\mathbb P(V=0)=1-\frac{M}{Q}.
\tag{6}
\]

Equal parity is a blockwise condition and endpoint weights factor over the \(k\) blocks. Hence, if \(V_1,\ldots,V_k\) are independent with law (6) and \(Z=V_1+\cdots+V_k\), equation (4) becomes

\[
\frac{2^nR}{W^2}
=\left(\frac{MQ}{W_0^2}\right)^k\mathbb E e^{-t|Z|}.
\tag{7}
\]

This is an exact identity for the global energy kernel.

We first bound the prefactor. Since \(0<t\leq1\), the power series for \(\cosh\) gives \(u<2\) and \(u-1\leq t^2\): indeed, \(\cosh1<2\) and
\(\cosh t-1\leq t^2(\cosh1-1)\). Direct expansion of (5) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
MQ-W_0^2
&=M^2(u-1)^2+M(2u-1)-1,\\
\frac{MQ}{W_0^2}
&\leq1+t^4+\frac3M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

For the second line, use \(W_0\geq M\), \((u-1)^2\leq t^4\), and \(2u-1<3\). Our choices give \(kt^4=1\). Also \(k/2^k\leq1/2\) for all \(k\geq2\), by induction from \(2^k\geq2k\). Consequently

\[
\left(\frac{MQ}{W_0^2}\right)^k
\leq\exp\!\left(kt^4+\frac{3k}{M}\right)
\leq e^{5/2}.
\tag{9}
\]

For completeness, we prove the required concentration estimate for \(Z\). Write \(\rho=M/Q\). Formula (5), \(M\geq4\), and \(0<t\leq1\) give
\[
\frac1{3+\cosh2}\leq\rho\leq\frac4{13}<\frac12.
\tag{10}
\]
Its characteristic function is
\(\mathbb E e^{i\alpha V}=1-\rho+\rho\cos\alpha\), which is nonnegative for every real \(\alpha\). Fourier inversion for the integer-valued finite sum \(Z\) follows by integrating each monomial \(e^{i\alpha(Z-j)}\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\); its integral divided by \(2\pi\) equals one when \(Z=j\) and zero otherwise. For any integer \(j\) it yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(Z=j)
&\leq\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(1-\rho+\rho\cos\alpha)^k\,d\alpha\\
&\leq\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{\mathbb R}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\rho k\alpha^2}{\pi^2}\right)\,d\alpha\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8\rho k}}
\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi(3+\cosh2)}{8k}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(1-x\leq e^{-x}\) for \(x\geq0\), and
\(1-\cos\alpha=2\sin^2(|\alpha|/2)\geq2\alpha^2/\pi^2\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\). The latter follows from concavity of \(\sin\) on \([0,\pi/2]\), which places it above the chord \(2x/\pi\). The Gaussian integral used in (11) follows by scaling from \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi\); squaring that positive integral and using polar coordinates gives \(2\pi\int_0^\infty r e^{-r^2}\,dr=\pi\).

Finally, \(e^t-1\geq t\) and \(t\leq1\) imply
\(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-t|j|}=1+2/(e^t-1)\leq3/t\). Summing (11) with these factors gives

\[
\mathbb E e^{-t|Z|}
\leq\frac3{t\sqrt k}\sqrt{\frac{\pi(3+\cosh2)}8}
=3\sqrt{\frac{\pi(3+\cosh2)}8}\,k^{-1/4}.
\tag{12}
\]

Equations (7), (9), and (12), together with (3), prove (1). All lattice sites, diagonal endpoint pairs, zero-energy antipodal pairs, and containing closed halfspaces have been included. Since the right side of (1) tends to zero and the examples already have full rank, the full registered affine-average assertion fails even on full-dimensional members of its class.
