## Statement

Let $r\ge1$, $s\ge0$, $m=r+s$, and let $(K_z)_{z\in P}$ be a finite family of compact convex sets in $RB_2^m$ placed at a fixed finite set $P\subset\mathbb R^k$ with $J=|P|$ and $R>0$. The sets may have zero $m$-volume. Write the continuous coordinates as $(u,x)\in\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^s$. Let $a+L$ be a full-rank lattice coset in $\mathbb R^r$ with covolume $v$ and a measurable fundamental cell $F$ for $L$, with translates tiling up to null sets and $F\subseteq[-\delta,\delta]^r$. Define $\nu$ as the summed coarse $m$-volume and $\mu$ as $v$ times the sum of $s$-volumes at fine bases in $a+L$ within each coarse fiber, with $\operatorname{vol}_0$ counting on $\mathbb R^0$. Set $M=\nu(\mathrm{total})>0$ and $E=2r\delta(2R+2\delta)^{m-1}$. Then $|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|\le JE$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$, and $|\mu(\mathrm{total})-M|\le JE$. If $M>JE$, the two normalized measures differ by at most $2JE/(M-JE)$ on all closed halfspaces, and their normalized depths at every same ambient point differ by at most that quantity. For a sequence with common $J,R$ and $\delta_\ell/M_\ell\to0$, the fine mass is eventually positive and this normalized error tends to zero.

## Proof

We give the cell estimate from 0639bd50a01557c4 §1. For any compact convex $Q\subseteq[-R,R]^m$, the measurable union $U=\bigcup_{\lambda\in a+L}(\lambda+F)\times Q_\lambda$ has $m$-volume $v\sum_\lambda |Q_\lambda|_s$ by disjointness of the cells up to null sets and Fubini. Put $A=[-\delta,\delta]^r\times\{0\}$. Then $Q\ominus A\subseteq U\subseteq Q+A$ up to a null base cylinder, where $Q\ominus A=\{x:x+A\subseteq Q\}$. For the upper inclusion the sample point differs by a vector of $A$. For the lower inclusion almost every base belongs to some cell, and its sample base differs by a vector of $A$, so the sample point at the same $x$ belongs to $Q$.

For a coordinate segment of length $2\delta$, dilation increases each nonempty line-section length by $2\delta$, and erosion removes at most that length, since convex line sections are intervals, possibly singletons. Thus the absolute change of $m$-volume in either operation is at most $2\delta$ times the $(m-1)$-volume of the preceding orthogonal projection. In $m=1$ a nonempty projection counts as one; for an empty set the change is zero. Perform the $r$ coordinate operations successively, noting that successive erosions give erosion by the sum of the segments. Dilated sets lie in $[-R-\delta,R+\delta]^m$ and eroded sets remain in $[-R,R]^m$. This bounds each projection volume by $(2R+2\delta)^{m-1}$ and gives $\bigl||U|_m-|Q|_m\bigr|\le E$, including lower-dimensional and empty $Q$.

Apply the estimate to each coarse fiber and separately to each trace of a closed ambient halfspace in its coarse fiber. Both are compact convex subsets of the required box. Summing gives the two absolute discrepancies. In particular all zero coarse-volume fibers are included before passing to normalized laws. For $T=\mu(\mathrm{total})>0$,
$$
\left|\frac{\mu(H)}{T}-\frac{\nu(H)}M\right|
\le\frac{|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|}{T}
+\frac{\nu(H)|M-T|}{TM}
\le\frac{2JE}{M-JE}.
$$
Taking infima over the same containing closed cuts gives the same-point depth comparison. Since $M_\ell\le J|RB_2^m|_m$, the relative mesh assumption implies $\delta_\ell\to0$ and $JE_\ell/M_\ell\to0$. This proves the last assertion.
