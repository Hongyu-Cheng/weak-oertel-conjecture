## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and $N\ge0$ be integers. Put $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$ and $V=\operatorname{vol}_d(T)$. Let the complete integer fibers of $S\subset\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d$ be $r_kT$ at $k=-N,\ldots,N$, with $0\le r_{-k}=r_k\le r_0=R$ and $R>0$. Write $\mu$ for the sum of $d$-volume on these fibers, $M=V\sum_k r_k^d$ and $y=(0,Rt,\ldots,Rt)$, where $t=1/(d+1)$. Fix a nonnegative continuous normal $u$ with $\sum_i u_i=1$. After relabeling, put $a=u_1=\max_i u_i$, $b=\max_{j\ge2}u_j$ and $q=2^{-1/d}$. For $\beta\in\mathbb R$ write $H_\beta=\lbrace(z,x):\beta z+u\cdot x\ge Rt\rbrace$.

Assume that the radii form a concave sequence, meaning the successive differences are nonincreasing.

For a normal with $a>b$ and $(a-t)/(a-b)\le q$, put $c=Rt/a$ and $C_u=a^{d-1}/\prod_{j=2}^d(a-u_j)\ge1$. Its cap mass satisfies
$$
\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\mu(S\cap H_\beta)
\ge C_u(1-t/a)^{d+1}M+
\frac{V C_u\,c(R-c)^d}{R(2N+1)}.
$$

Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and set
$$
A_d=\frac{q-t}{2q-1},\qquad
B_d=\frac{t}{1-(\Gamma_d/2)^{1/(d+1)}},\qquad
a_d=\max\lbrace A_d,B_d\rbrace.
$$
Then $1/2<a_d<1$. For every such chain, every closed halfspace containing $y$ with nonnegative normalized continuous normal satisfying $a\ge a_d$ has mass strictly greater than $(\Gamma_d/2)M$. The same strict bound holds for pure base-normal halfspaces containing $y$. The strict bound also holds for a signed continuous normal when the barycentric swap in accepted fact 6f616779219fd76c, followed by normalization, gives $a\ge a_d$.

## Proof

The directional minimum in integer-centered-shared-tilt is $VC_u Q_d(c)$, where $Q_d(c)=\sum_k(r_k-c)_+^d$. Apply accepted fact facc699f7957afd2 with $p=d\ge2$, $L=2N+1$ and chosen peak $k=0$ (index $N$ after reindexing). The radius sequence is nonnegative, concave and nonzero, and its maximum is $R$. The ratio condition gives $a>t>0$, hence $0<c<R$. The fact yields
$$
Q_d(c)\ge (1-c/R)^{d+1}Q_d(0)
+\kappa_N(0)\frac{c(R-c)^d}{R},
\qquad
\kappa_N(0)\ge\frac1{2N+1}.
$$
Here $VQ_d(0)=M$. This proves the cap estimate with the stated positive lower bound for the correction. If an endpoint has zero radius it contributes zero to both power sums. At each level below $R$ the strict superlevel interval is symmetric and contains the chosen peak even if the peak lies in a plateau. The integral form in facc699f7957afd2 uses the constant reciprocal side-length coefficient on the open intervals between consecutive distinct knots, so a knot at $c$ and repeated levels have the same endpoint convention. The two terms specialize to $Q_d(0)$ and zero when $c=0$, and both vanish at $c=R$. Neither degenerate truncation level occurs for the cap under consideration. For $N=0$, the two terms give the identity $Q_d(c)=(R-c)^d$.

For $d\ge2$ the first three binomial terms give $(1+1/d)^d>2$, hence $\Gamma_d<1/2$ and $q>1-t>1/2$. We have
$$
A_d-(1-t)=\frac{(1-q)(1-2t)}{2q-1}>0,
\qquad
A_d<1
$$
where the second inequality follows from $q>1-t$. Also $(1-t)^{d+1}=\Gamma_d\,d/(d+1)>\Gamma_d/2$. Thus $(\Gamma_d/2)^{1/(d+1)}<1-t$ and $B_d<1$. These bounds give $1/2<a_d<1$. In the normalized nonnegative simplex the region $a\ge a_d$ contains every coordinate vertex and has nonempty relative interior near each such vertex.

If $a\ge a_d$, then $a>1/2$ and $b\le1-a<a$. Since $(a-t)/(2a-1)$ decreases for $a>1/2$ and equals $q$ at $A_d$, we have
$$
\frac{a-t}{a-b}\le\frac{a-t}{2a-1}\le q.
$$
The inequality $a\ge B_d$ gives $(1-t/a)^{d+1}\ge\Gamma_d/2$. The first term of the cap bound is at least $(\Gamma_d/2)M$ because $C_u\ge1$, and the correction is strictly positive even when $a=a_d$. Every containing halfspace with this continuous normal contains the halfspace of the same orientation translated to pass through $y$. Both pure base-normal halfspaces through $y$ have mass $(M+VR^d)/2$, since each takes the whole own fiber and exactly one side of every reflected pair. A containing pure base-normal halfspace has at least this mass.

For a signed continuous normal, take $P=\lbrace k:r_k>0\rbrace$, $j=0$ and the touching radius majorant of slope zero, $r_k\le R$. These are the positive-radius hypotheses of accepted fact 6f616779219fd76c. After moving the boundary through $y$, choose the negative minimum coefficient $i$ as in its proof. The volume-preserving swap with the simplex slack changes the signed vector $v$ to the nonnegative vector with coefficients $-v_i$ at $i$ and $v_j-v_i$ at $j\ne i$. The replacement through $y$ keeps one shared tilt and its cap mass on each positive-radius fiber is at most the original cap mass. Zero-radius fibers have zero mass for either cap. If the replacement's normalized largest coefficient is at least $a_d$, the bound just proved for the replacement transfers to the original halfspace. For other replacement directions this argument supplies no bound.

For $N\ge1$, piecewise-linear interpolation of the radii gives a concave function $\rho$ on $[-N,N]$. The compact convex set defined by $-N\le z\le N$, $x_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i x_i\le\rho(z)$ has interior near a positive-radius slice and has precisely the stated integer fibers, including singleton endpoints if a radius is zero. Its mixed measure is therefore exactly the sum used above. For $N=0$, the product $[-1/2,1/2]\times RT$ realizes the single-fiber census as well. Under a nonsingular continuous affine coordinate change with an affine translation depending on the base, $y$ and all halfspaces are mapped to their actual images and every fiber mass is multiplied by the same determinant factor. The cone conditions are imposed in the simplex coordinates.
