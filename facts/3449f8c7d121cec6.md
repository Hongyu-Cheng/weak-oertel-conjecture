## Statement

Take $C=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1/2,-1),(3/2,-1),(1/2,3)\}$ in one integer and one continuous dimension. Both integer fibers are $[-1,1]$, and mixed mass and ambient area both equal four. For $z\in[0,1]$ the maximum of normalized mixed depth over the vertical line at $z$ is $1/(4\max(z,1-z))$. At level $1/3$ the four closed caps
$$
\pm x-\tfrac43 z\ge-\tfrac13,\qquad
\pm x+\tfrac43 z\ge1
$$
have attained supporting contacts at $(1/4,0)$ and $(3/4,0)$, equal raw mixed budgets $4/3$, and full normals summing to zero. Their closed complementary rhombus is bounded and mixed-lattice-free. The sum of their normalized mixed-minus-ambient fractions is $-11/48$, and their total sawtooth boundary flux is $11/12$.

## Proof

The floor is $-1$ and the roof is $4z+1$ on the left and $5-4z$ on the right of $z=1/2$. These give the stated fibers and area. The sampled law is symmetric in $x$, so convexity of its halfspace depth upper-level sets gives a maximizing height zero at every fixed real base. Indeed, any halfspace containing a convex combination contains at least one endpoint. For $0\le z\le1/2$ a nonvertical through-$(z,0)$ cap can be reflected to $x\ge a(t-z)$, where $t$ is the base variable. With $L(c)=\min(2,\max(0,1-c))$, its mass is $L(-az)+L(a(1-z))$. For $a\ge0$ and $a\le1/(1-z)$ both terms are unclipped and their sum decreases from two to $1/(1-z)$. Beyond that threshold the second term vanishes and the first is nondecreasing. For $a\le0$, the second term either equals two, or both terms are unclipped and sum to $2+|a|(1-2z)$, or the first is clipped to zero with the second already two. Vertical caps have mass at least two. Reflecting the bases about $1/2$ proves the profile.

The projection at level $1/3$ is therefore $[1/4,3/4]$ and its two height-zero endpoints are actual contacts in $C$. At each displayed cap the integer mass is $4/3$ by clipping. A point strictly on its cap side has smaller depth, by shifting the cap a sufficiently small positive amount, losing positive mass in its proper interval cut. Thus the complementary inequalities contain the actual depth region. They form the asserted rhombus, and the four displayed ambient normals sum to zero. For the first pair the clipped triangles have vertices $(-1/2,-1),(1,1),(1/2,3)$ and $(-1/2,-1),(1,-1),(-1/8,1/2)$, respectively. Their areas are $2,9/8$ and base reflection gives the other pair. Consequently the signed fraction sum is $4/3-25/16=-11/48$. The endpoint correction of closed-endpoint-sampling-flux vanishes on each cap: every integer endpoint of a cap's projection has a zero-length fiber. The total flux is therefore the sum of ambient minus mixed raw masses, $11/12$.
