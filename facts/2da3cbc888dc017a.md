## Statement

In the buffered limit setting of the sampled shift and depth limit theorem, suppose there is a limiting maximizing mark at a base in $Z$. Then the limiting ordinary maximum equals the limiting buffered maximum $Q$, and every ordinary maximizing base belongs to $Z$.

In particular the hypothesis holds if the weights are logarithmically normalized as in the logarithmic zero-fiber lemma and the marks in the original-volume row split theorem are limits of sampled buffered maximizing marks.

## Proof

At the given zero-source mark the buffer is zero and ordinary depth is $Q$. At every mark ordinary depth is bounded above by the limiting buffered depth, proving equality of the maxima. A boundary mark has ordinary depth zero by the compatible ambient support argument in the sampled shift and depth limit theorem, and $Q>0$, so all ordinary maximizing marks are own-interior. At an ordinary maximizing mark over a base outside $Z$, the limiting weight is positive there. By d90ba05577427854 its own energy is positive as well. The shift is thus a positive scalar times the norm $\Gamma$. The own-interior strict buffer bound 9583fbdf100e75ef applies and would make the limiting buffered depth strictly exceed $Q$, a contradiction.

For the particular application the logarithmic zero-fiber lemma makes $Z$ nonempty. Every limiting fiber has positive volume. The original-volume row split theorem gives $\sum_{p\in J\cap Z}\sigma_p=V(Z)/M>0$, yielding a zero-class source. Its limiting mark is maximizing by the shift and depth limit theorem.
