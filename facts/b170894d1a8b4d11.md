## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty, compact and convex, $d\ge1$, $P=\operatorname{proj}_z C$, $K_z=C_z$, $r(z)=|K_z|^{1/d}$, and $L(z,x)=a\cdot z+u\cdot x-b$ with $u\ne0$. All volumes in a fiber are Lebesgue $d$-volumes. On the domain $z\in P,\ 0\le t\le r(z)$, set $U(z,0)=\max_{K_z}L(z,\cdot)$ and, for $t>0$, set
$$
U(z,t)=\sup\{s:|K_z\cap\{L(z,\cdot)\ge s\}|\ge t^d\}.
$$
The domain is convex and $U$ is finite, bounded, upper semicontinuous and jointly concave. For the cap $H=\{L\ge0\}$, its root $c_z=|K_z\cap\{x:L(z,x)\ge0\}|^{1/d}$ is concave on the projection of $C\cap H$ if that set is nonempty. On a positive fiber a proper cap has the unique root $U(z,c_z)=0$ and the zero and full cases are decided by the fiber support extrema. Its actual mixed cap mass is $\sum_z c_z^d$, summed over the integer bases in $P$.

For each positive fiber,
$$
\int_{K_z} L(z,x)\,dx=d\int_0^{r(z)}t^{d-1}U(z,t)\,dt.
$$
At a mixed centroid $(j,y)$ with integer $j$, $L(j,y)=0$ and positive total mixed mass, the exact moment equations are $\sum_z(z-j)r(z)^d=0$ and $\sum_z d\int_0^{r(z)}t^{d-1}U(z,t)\,dt=0$. Null fibers contribute zero.

For $H_v=\{L(z,x)+v\cdot(z-j)\ge0\}$, the inverse is $U_v(z,t)=U(z,t)+v\cdot(z-j)$. At a parameter with no endpoint contacts on positive fibers, let $c_z$ now be the cap roots for $H_v$ and $\beta_z=-\partial_t U_v(z,c_z)$ at proper roots. These derivatives exist and are strictly positive. The mixed cap tilt gradient is
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{proper}\ z}(z-j)\frac{dc_z^{d-1}}{\beta_z}
=\sum_{\mathrm{proper}\ z}(z-j)\frac{\mathcal H^{d-1}(K_z\cap\partial H_v)}{\|u\|}.
$$
It vanishes at a tilt minimum. In dimension one, when the oriented continuous coefficient is normalized to one, each proper section has weight one.

## Proof

The hypograph consists of the triples $(z,t,s)$ with $t\ge0$ for which the closed cap $K_z\cap\{L\ge s\}$ is nonempty and has volume at least $t^d$. The nonempty clause applies even at $t=0$. Convexity of $C$ and affinity of $L$ put a Minkowski average of two such caps in the cap at the averaged $(z,s)$. Brunn–Minkowski for nonempty compact sets, including null sets, proves convexity of the hypograph. In any convergent sequence of triples, compactness gives a point in the limiting cap and forces the approximating caps into each fixed closed neighborhood of the limiting cap eventually. Continuity of volume from above in those neighborhoods bounds their volume limsup by the limiting volume. This proves closedness and upper semicontinuity, and projection gives the stated domain. Boundedness follows from the extrema of $L$ on $C$. Fixing $s=0$ in the same inclusion proves concavity of $c_z$ on the nonempty cap projection.

On a positive fiber the cap volume is continuous because its boundary is null. It strictly decreases between the extrema because every open subinterval there meets the image of the fiber interior and therefore has a positive-volume preimage. Consequently $U(z,r(z))=\min_{K_z}L$, the proper root is unique, cap volume is zero when the maximum is at most zero and full when the minimum is at least zero. The pushforward of uniform fiber measure by its normalized cap survival function is uniform on $[0,1]$. Integrating its inverse and substituting the survival variable $t^d/r(z)^d$ proves the moment formula. Summation gives both centroid equations and the stated mass formula.

At a proper section the coarea density is positive and continuous in its level by d4e455a28be6cde4, applied with constant full-dimensional body $K_z$ and the fixed normal $u$. Thus the derivative of the parallel tail is the negative of that density. Differentiating the equation that this tail at $U_v(z,t)$ equals $t^d$ gives the positive value of $\beta_z$ and the coarea identity. The tilt is a level change of $-(z-j)\cdot v$ on fiber $z$. Proper traces remain proper locally, strictly saturated traces remain constant, and null fibers have no measured response. The gradient follows by adding the finitely many level derivatives. In $d=1$ the section has counting measure one; a reflection if needed and a positive rescaling normalize the coefficient.
