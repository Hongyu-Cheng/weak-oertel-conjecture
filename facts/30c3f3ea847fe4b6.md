## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body. If $h$ is nonnegative affine on $D$ and vanishes somewhere on $D$, then $\int_Dh^{-r}\,dx=+\infty$ for every $r\ge d$. When $h$ is nonconstant, the cone lower bound has logarithmic divergence at $r=d$. If convex bodies $D_j$ converge in Hausdorff distance to $D$ and affine $h_j>0$ on $D_j$ converge coefficientwise to such an $h$, then $\int_{D_j}h_j^{-r}\,dx\to+\infty$.

For each $0<r<d$ the standard simplex $D=\{x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le1\}$ has positive affine $h_\delta=\delta+\sum_i x_i$, $\delta>0$, with $|D|^{-1}\int_D h_\delta^{-r}\,dx\to d/(d-r)$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Thus inverse-power-mean-one positive affine functions can converge to a finite affine function vanishing at a vertex in that exponent range.

## Proof

If $h$ vanishes identically the integral is infinite. Otherwise its continuous coefficient $c$ is nonzero and it is positive at every interior point. Let $x_0\in D$ be a zero and choose a positive-level $(d-1)$-disk $B$ inside $D$ at level $s_0>0$. Its cone with vertex $x_0$ is in $D$. The level-$s$ section of this cone has area $(s/s_0)^{d-1}\mathcal H^{d-1}(B)$ for $0<s<s_0$. In dimension one use a point with $\mathcal H^0$-mass one. The affine coarea formula gives the lower bound
$$
\frac{\mathcal H^{d-1}(B)}{\lVert c\rVert s_0^{d-1}}\int_0^{s_0}s^{d-1-r}\,ds=+\infty .
$$
The exponent is $-1$ at $r=d$.

Every interior point of the limiting $D$ eventually belongs to $D_j$. In fact Hausdorff convergence implies uniform support-function convergence, and an interior point has a strictly positive support margin on the unit sphere. Apply Fatou's lemma to the nonnegative functions equal to $h_j^{-r}$ on $D_j$ and zero outside. The pointwise limit on the interior of $D$ is $h^{-r}$ with $0^{-r}=+\infty$. The boundary is Lebesgue-null, so the divergent integral yields $\liminf_j\int_{D_j}h_j^{-r}\,dx=+\infty$.

For the standard simplex the relative volume of $\{\sum_i x_i\le t\}$ is $t^d$ for $0\le t\le1$ by homothety. The density of $t$ under uniform probability is $d\,t^{d-1}$. By monotone convergence the stated mean tends to $\int_0^1 d\,t^{d-1-r}\,dt=d/(d-r)$. Multiplying $h_\delta$ by the $1/r$ power of its inverse-power mean gives a normalized affine parameter with a finite nonzero limiting scale and a zero at the simplex vertex.
