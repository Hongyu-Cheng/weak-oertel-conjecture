## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-branch of accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70` and `7ec63df101c30343`. Use all their canonical normalizations, in particular
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained matching, let \(\mathcal E_I\) be the canonical family of \(I\)-bad opposite edges, and write
\[
 I:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I}s_e>\frac Q4.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(t_e\) be the actual opposite-edge triangle masses. Let \(\mathcal B\) be the exceptional family from accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`, so that
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal B}s_e\le G,
 \qquad t_e\ge\frac18s_e\quad(e\notin\mathcal B).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 \Theta_I(\{c\}):=
 \sum_\xi\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
 \qquad
 E:=\{c\in\mathbb Z^n:\Theta_I(\{c\})>0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
 \Theta_I(E):=\sum_{c\in E}\Theta_I(\{c\})>\frac{3Q}{100}.
\tag{5}
\]
At each \(c\in E\), choose a retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing its contribution \(\Theta_{\xi,c}\), put \(q_c:=q^\circ_{\xi(c),c}\), and define
\[
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\]
\[
 r_E:=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-a(c):c\in E\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then no such canonical hard \(I\)-branch configuration can satisfy
\[
 \rho_E=2,
 \qquad r_E\le2,
 \qquad d\le47.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, the subcase \(\rho_E=2\), \(r_E=2\), and \(d\le47\) is impossible.

## Proof

Assume (7). Accepted fact `e2263a787ace07a4`, applied to the full target support \(E\), gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \ge
 \frac{4\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\Theta_I(E)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_d}{3}\Theta_I(E)
 >\frac{\gamma_d}{100}Q.
\tag{8}
\]
This uses one heaviest parity label and one actual metronoid point in each physical target fiber. No target densities belonging to different labels are added.

Put \(P:=\operatorname{aff}(E)\). Every \(c\in E\) is an occupied fiber base by the actual metronoid realization in accepted fact `e2263a787ace07a4`. Hence \(E\subseteq Z\cap P\), where \(Z\) is the occupied base support, and
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(Z\cap P)=2.
\]
Accepted fact `07889c9b4686d8b4`, applied to this rank-two slice, gives
\[
 \Theta_I(E)=\Theta_I(P\cap\mathbb Z^n)\le16G.
\tag{9}
\]
The equality holds because \(E\) is the complete positive target support. Equations (5) and (9) imply
\[
 \frac GQ>\frac3{1600}.
\tag{10}
\]

We also have \(n\ge3\). Indeed, \(\rho_E=2\) first gives \(n\ge2\). If \(n=2\), then \(E\subseteq Z\) and \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)=2\) imply \(\operatorname{aff}(Z)=\mathbb R^2\). The full-rank clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` then gives
\[
 G\ge\frac W{64}.
\]
Accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` gives \(Q\le W/8\), and therefore \(G\ge Q/8\), contradicting (1). Thus
\[
 n\ge3.
\tag{11}
\]

Now remove the exceptional edges exactly as prescribed by (3). Put
\[
 J:=\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B,
 \qquad S:=\sum_{e\in J}s_e.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) give
\[
 S
 =I-\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I\cap\mathcal B}s_e
 \ge I-G
 >\frac Q4-G.
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, \(J\ne\varnothing\). If \(e\in J\) has \(s_e>0\), then (3) gives \(t_e\ge s_e/8>0\). Hence \(\Theta_I(\{m_e\})>0\), so
\[
 m_e\in E\subseteq P
 \qquad(e\in J,\ s_e>0).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus all measures constructed below have base affine rank at most two.

We first charge \(S\) to \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\). Retain the canonical centroid notation \(\bar c_e\) and reservoir weights \(r_e\) from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. That fact gives \(s_e/16\le r_e\). Consequently
\[
 \sigma_M:=
 \sum_{e\in J}\frac{s_e}{16}\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}
\tag{15}
\]
is a nonzero positive submeasure of its reservoir centroid measure \(\bar\nu_r\). Let \(\rho_M,r_M\) be the intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks of \(\sigma_M\). Equation (14) gives \(\rho_M\le2\), while the definition of residual rank gives only the general bound \(r_M\le d\). In particular, no rank of the centroid points \(\bar c_e\) is inferred from the heaviest-label hypothesis \(r_E\le2\). The exact centroid visibility inequality in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 &\ge2^{n-\rho_M}\frac{|\sigma_M|}{r_M+1}\\
 &\ge 2^{n-2}\frac{S/16}{d+1}
 =\frac{2^{n-6}}{d+1}S.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

We next charge the same edge family to the separate summand \(D\). Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` proves that
\[
 \gamma_d\sum_es_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\). Its restriction
\[
 \sigma_F:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in J}s_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}
\tag{17}
\]
is therefore also halfspace-dominated. Let \(\rho_F,r_F\) be its intrinsic ranks. Again (14) gives \(\rho_F\le2\), and only the automatic bound \(r_F\le d\) is used. The dominated-measure residual theorem in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \ge\frac{|\sigma_F|}{2^{\rho_F}(r_F+1)}
 \ge\frac{\gamma_dS}{4(d+1)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-2}}{d+1}S.
\tag{18}
\]

For clarity about capacity accounting, accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70`, `8aac56c49321466c`, and `e2263a787ace07a4` also give
\[
 D\ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C),
 \qquad D\ge M_{\mathrm{off}}(C),
 \qquad D\ge R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C),
 \qquad D\ge\gamma_dG.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus \(D\) is at least the maximum of the lower bounds in (18)--(19). We use only (18) for the single \(D\)-summand. We do not add any two lower bounds inside \(D\), and we do not need a separate \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}\) estimate.

Normalize
\[
 g:=\frac GQ.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (1) and (10) give
\[
 \frac3{1600}<g<\frac1{100}.
\tag{21}
\]
The quantities \(G\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}\), and \(D\) are four distinct nonnegative summands in (1). Using (8), (13), (16), and (18), while discarding only the other nonnegative summands, gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal A(C)}Q
 > L(n,d,g),
\tag{22}
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L(n,d,g)
 &=g+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
 +\frac{2^{n-6}}{d+1}\left(\frac14-g\right)
 +\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-2}}{d+1}\left(\frac14-g\right)\\
 &=g+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
 +\frac{2^{n-6}(1+16\gamma_d)}{d+1}
 \left(\frac14-g\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

It remains to minimize (23) exactly on the whole parameter range. For fixed \(d,g\), the last term increases with \(n\), so (11) gives
\[
 L(n,d,g)\ge L(3,d,g).
\tag{24}
\]
Put
\[
 k_d:=\frac{1+16\gamma_d}{8(d+1)}.
\]
Then
\[
 L(3,d,g)=\frac{\gamma_d}{100}+g+k_d\left(\frac14-g\right).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(d=1\), \(\gamma_1=1/2\) and \(k_1=9/16<1\). For \(d\ge2\), \(\gamma_d<1\), and hence
\[
 k_d<\frac{17}{8(d+1)}\le\frac{17}{24}<1.
\]
Thus (25) is increasing in \(g\), and (21) gives
\[
 L(3,d,g)>
 \frac{\gamma_d}{100}+\frac3{1600}
 +k_d\frac{397}{1600}.
\tag{26}
\]

The sequence \(\gamma_d\) is decreasing. Indeed,
\[
 \gamma_d^{-1}
 =\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 =\sum_{j=0}^d\frac1{j!}
 \prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(1-\frac{i}{d}\right),
\tag{27}
\]
and passage from \(d\) to \(d+1\) does not decrease any pre-existing product, strictly increases every one with \(j\ge2\), and adds one positive term. Therefore both \(\gamma_d\) and \(k_d\) decrease as \(d\) increases. Since all their coefficients in (26) are positive, the right side of (26) is minimized over every integer \(1\le d\le47\) at \(d=47\). This also handles \(d\le4\) directly, without importing any separate low-dimensional assertion.

Write \(\gamma_{47}=(47/48)^{47}\). Equations (24)--(26) now give
\[
 L(n,d,g)>
 \frac{1549+12496\gamma_{47}}{614400}.
\tag{28}
\]
We prove the remaining comparison with rational inequalities. By the binomial theorem,
\[
 \gamma_{47}^{-1}
 =\left(\frac{48}{47}\right)^{47}
 <\sum_{j=0}^{47}\frac1{j!}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{47}\frac1{j!}
 <\frac{163}{60}
 +\frac{1/720}{1-1/7}
 =\frac{11743}{4320}
 <\frac{87}{32}.
\tag{29}
\]
Here \(163/60\) is the sum through \(j=5\), and the tail beginning at \(j=6\) is bounded by a geometric series whose successive ratios are at most \(1/7\). Hence
\[
 \gamma_{47}>\frac{32}{87}>\frac{4595}{12496},
\tag{30}
\]
where the last comparison is the exact integer inequality
\[
 32\cdot12496-87\cdot4595=107>0.
\]
Substitution in (28) yields
\[
 L(n,d,g)>
 \frac{1549+4595}{614400}
 =\frac1{100}.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (22) and (31) contradict the hard inequality (1). Therefore no configuration satisfying (7) exists.
