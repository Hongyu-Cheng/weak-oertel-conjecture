## Statement

Let n be any positive integer. For each i in {1,...,n}, let q_i be a probability law on a nonempty finite space Omega_i, and let K_i be a symmetric probability law on Omega_i x Omega_i whose two marginals both equal q_i. Fix beta in (0,1), and suppose

K_i(x,y) >= beta q_i(x)q_i(y)

for every i and every x,y in Omega_i. Let the paired coordinates (X_i,Y_i) be independent over i, with respective joint laws K_i, and put q = tensor_i q_i. Then, for every event E contained in product_i Omega_i,

Pr(X in E, Y in E) >= q(E)^{C_beta},

where C_beta = 2/(1-sqrt(1-beta)). This exponent is independent of n, all space sizes, all positive atom masses, and E.

## Proof

First restrict each Omega_i to the support of q_i. This changes no probability: if q_i(x)=0, the nonnegative row and column of K_i at x have sum zero by its marginal assumptions. Thus all remaining q_i(x) are strictly positive.

Define three Markov operators on functions on Omega_i by

(P_i h)(x) = sum_y K_i(x,y)h(y)/q_i(x),
(J_i h)(x) = sum_y q_i(y)h(y),
(R_i h)(x) = sum_y [K_i(x,y)-beta q_i(x)q_i(y)]h(y)/[(1-beta)q_i(x)].

The entries of R_i are nonnegative by minorization. Its rows sum to one because the first marginal of K_i equals q_i. It preserves q_i because, for every y,

sum_x q_i(x) [K_i(x,y)-beta q_i(x)q_i(y)]/[(1-beta)q_i(x)] = q_i(y).

In particular, R_i J_i = J_i, since R_i fixes constant functions. Put S_i = (1-beta)Id + beta J_i. Direct multiplication gives the exact identity

R_i S_i = (1-beta)R_i + beta J_i = P_i.

Let R = tensor_i R_i, S = tensor_i S_i, and P = tensor_i P_i. Tensor multiplication yields P=RS, and R is a q-preserving Markov operator. Independence of the pairs gives

E_q[f(X)(P g)(X)] = E[f(X)g(Y)]

for all functions f,g. Here and below E_q denotes expectation under the product law q.

Set t = -log(1-beta). Since J_i is a projection, S_i = exp[-t(Id-J_i)]. Consequently S is the time-t operator of the tensor product of simple Markov semigroups with underlying laws q_i. Corollary 1.11 of Mossel, Oleszkiewicz and Sen, cited below, states that for every strictly positive h and real exponents b<a<1,

||S h||_b >= ||h||_a whenever t >= log[(1-b)/(1-a)],

where ||h||_u = (E_q h^u)^{1/u} for u != 0. This theorem is applied only to S.

Choose a = 1-sqrt(1-beta), so 0<a<1, and b=a/(a-1)<0. Then

1-b = 1/(1-a),
log[(1-b)/(1-a)] = -2log(1-a) = -log(1-beta) = t.

The cited theorem therefore gives ||S h||_b >= ||h||_a for this choice. For any strictly positive v, convexity of u -> u^b and the Markov property of R imply pointwise

(R v)^b <= R(v^b).

Taking E_q, using q-preservation of R, and raising both positive sides to the negative power 1/b gives

||R v||_b >= ||v||_b.

Apply this to v=S h to obtain the discrete-time reverse hypercontractive inequality

||P h||_b = ||RS h||_b >= ||S h||_b >= ||h||_a.

For completeness, the required reverse Holder inequality follows directly from ordinary Holder. For strictly positive f and v, use the conjugate exponents 1/a and 1/(1-a) on f^a=(fv)^a v^{-a} to get

E_q f^a <= (E_q fv)^a (E_q v^{-a/(1-a)})^{1-a}.

Since b=-a/(1-a), rearrangement gives E_q fv >= ||f||_a ||v||_b. Taking v=P g for strictly positive g and applying the preceding inequality yields

E[f(X)g(Y)] >= ||f||_a ||P g||_b >= ||f||_a ||g||_a.

Now take f=g=1_E+epsilon, with epsilon>0, and let epsilon decrease to zero. All sums are finite, so the left side converges to Pr(X in E,Y in E), and both a-pseudonorms converge to q(E)^{1/a}. Therefore

Pr(X in E,Y in E) >= q(E)^{2/a} = q(E)^{C_beta}.

This limit also covers q(E)=0 and q(E)=1. The proof uses the identical-marginal and minorization assumptions; the additional symmetry assumption is permitted and does not require another condition. A product of length zero, if included, is a one-point space and satisfies the same inequality directly.

## External sources

Elchanan Mossel, Krzysztof Oleszkiewicz and Arnab Sen, "On reverse hypercontractivity," Geometric and Functional Analysis 23 (2013), 1062-1097, Corollary 1.11 (page 5 of arXiv v5).
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.1210v5#page=5
Published article: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-013-0229-4

The corollary states that a simple semigroup generated by Id-E, or a tensor product of such semigroups, satisfies ||T_t h||_b >= ||h||_a for strictly positive h, b<a<1, and t>=log[(1-b)/(1-a)]. Its setup assumes finite probability spaces with positive atom masses. Restriction to supports ensures that condition here. Each J_i is precisely expectation under q_i, so S is the stated tensor product. The chosen exponents include a negative b, which the corollary explicitly allows. The time requirement holds with equality, and epsilon regularization ensures strict positivity. The theorem imposes no lower atom bound. Applicability to the actual discrete-time P is established by the proved factorization P=RS and Jensen inequality, not by a semigroup comparison.
