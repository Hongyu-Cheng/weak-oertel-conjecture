## Statement

Let $S$ be a nondegenerate triangle with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$. If $|\lambda_i(y)-1/3|\le1/10000$ for all $i$, every closed halfspace through $y$ capturing at most $(4/9+1/10000)|S|$ strictly isolates one vertex $i$ and has the equation $A_j\lambda_j+A_k\lambda_k\le1$ for the other indices $j,k$, with equality at $y$ and $29/20<A_j,A_k<31/20$. In particular a direction through a vertex of $S$ cannot satisfy the area threshold.

## Proof

A cap containing two vertices (including a vertex on its cut line) has a complementary cap with one strictly captured vertex and incident intercept fractions $t_j,t_k\in(0,1]$. A direction through a vertex has one complementary intercept equal to one; a cut through the interior point $y$ cannot pass through two vertices. Put $z=\lambda_j(y)/t_j$. The relation $\lambda_j(y)/t_j+\lambda_k(y)/t_k=1$ implies $z\in[\lambda_j(y),1-\lambda_k(y)]$. The complementary fraction $t_jt_k=\lambda_j(y)\lambda_k(y)/[z(1-z)]$ is maximized at an endpoint because $z(1-z)$ is concave. The maximum is at most $\max\{\lambda_j(y)/(1-\lambda_k(y)),\lambda_k(y)/(1-\lambda_j(y))\}\le17/33$, since each coordinate is less than $17/50$. Thus the original cap fraction is at least $16/33>4/9+1/10000$. If the cap line passes through a vertex then it contains two vertices with the boundary one counted, since it also passes through the interior of $S$. Therefore every cap under the threshold has one strictly captured vertex and two strictly excluded ones.

In this case the intercepts are $1/A_j,1/A_k$, with $A_j,A_k>1$, and the fraction is $1/(A_j A_k)$. The lower bound $9997/30000$ on each coordinate and the area threshold imply $A_j+A_k\le U=30000/9997$ and $A_jA_k\ge P=90000/40009$. The product with either coefficient equal to $a$ is at most $a(U-a)$. This bound increases up to $U/2$ and decreases beyond it, so a coefficient $\le29/20$ or $\ge31/20$ would contradict, respectively,
$$
P-(29/20)(U-29/20)=110147293/159987989200>0,\qquad
P-(31/20)(U-31/20)=95744053/159987989200>0.
$$
This proves both strict bounds in all directions.
