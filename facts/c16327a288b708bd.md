## Statement

Let \(N\ge 1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in D}\) be a strictly positive probability law. For \(\mathbf z\in D\), define
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}: H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf z\in H\right\},
\qquad t_{\mathbf z}:=\tau_p(\mathbf z),
\]
and, for \(q>0\), define
\[
T:=\max_{\mathbf z\in D}t_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
M_q(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}t_{\mathbf z}^{q}.
\]

The following assertions hold without assuming that \(D\) is full-dimensional.

1. For every \(\mathbf z\in D\), there is a depth-minimizing cap
\[
H_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a_{\mathbf z}\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\},
\qquad \mathbf a_{\mathbf z}\ne\mathbf0,
\]
such that \(D\cap\partial H_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf z\}\). Every such choice satisfies the strict-order implication
\[
\mathbf x\in D\cap H_{\mathbf z},\quad \mathbf x\ne\mathbf z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_{\mathbf x}\le t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}<t_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{1}
\]
Closed halfspaces are used throughout, so the definition retains all boundary atoms; the generic choice merely ensures that a selected minimizing boundary has no atom other than its center.

2. If \(p\) is a local minimum of \(M_q\) on the full open probability simplex on \(D\), then
\[
M_q(p)\ge \frac{T^q}{q+1}.
\tag{2}
\]
If, in addition, \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N\), then the accepted integer-centerpoint theorem \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\) gives
\[
T\ge2^{-N}
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
M_q(p)\ge\frac{2^{-qN}}{q+1}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, \(q=3\) gives \(M_3(p)\ge 2^{-3N}/4\).
This conclusion requires local minimality against all probability-simplex directions.

3. In contrast, suppose
\[
p_{\boldsymbol\ell}(\mathbf z)
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}}
{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}
\]
and \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\ell}\) is a local minimum of \(M_q\) only within this log-affine family. For any choice of depth-minimizing caps \((H_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in D}\), set
\[
\Phi(r):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_{\mathbf z}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H_{\mathbf z}}r_{\mathbf x}\right)^q,
\qquad
g_{\mathbf x}:=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r_{\mathbf x}}(p),
\qquad
\overline{\mathbf z}:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x.
\]
Then
\[
g_{\mathbf x}
=t_{\mathbf x}^q
+q\sum_{\mathbf z:\,\mathbf x\in H_{\mathbf z}}
p_{\mathbf z}t_{\mathbf z}^{q-1},
\tag{4}
\]
and log-affine stationarity gives exactly
\[
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}
\bigl(g_{\mathbf x}-(q+1)M_q(p)\bigr)
(\mathbf x-\overline{\mathbf z})=\mathbf0.
\tag{5}
\]
This vector equation does not imply the coordinatewise identity \(g_{\mathbf x}=(q+1)M_q(p)\). If the caps are chosen as in part 1 and \(\mathbf z_\star\) satisfies \(t_{\mathbf z_\star}=T\), then
\[
g_{\mathbf z_\star}=T^q+q\,p_{\mathbf z_\star}T^{q-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) yields (2) only when the unrestricted simplex stationarity used in part 2 is actually available.

4. Suppose now that \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\) is finite for a convex set \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), that \(p\) is log-affine as above, and that the caps are chosen as in part 1. Define
\[
E_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D\cap H_{\mathbf z}:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\},
\qquad
K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}:=p_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}\}},
\qquad
\Delta:=\operatorname{diag}(t_{\mathbf z}).
\]
Then
\[
K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf z}=p_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{\mathbf x}\le t_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}>0,\ \mathbf x\ne\mathbf z
\Longrightarrow t_{\mathbf x}<t_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus any ordering of \(D\) by nondecreasing \(t_{\mathbf z}\), with the same order on rows and columns, makes \(K\) lower triangular. Moreover,
\[
K^{\mathsf T}\Delta^{-1}K\preceq I
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\frac12\left(
\sum_{\boldsymbol\gamma\in\mathbb Z^N/2\mathbb Z^N}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap(\boldsymbol\gamma+2\mathbb Z^N)}p_{\mathbf x}\right)^2
+\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2
\right)
\ge2^{-N-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
These are identities and inequalities for the actual minimizing-cap matrix. They do not establish, for any dimension-free \(A_q\), the further estimate
\[
\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le A_q M_q(p)^{1/q}.
\tag{10}
\]

5. For every fixed \(q>0\), the algebraic properties in (7)--(8), even with equality in the selected row masses, do not imply (10). Explicitly, for every integer \(k\ge2\), there are a finite index set \(D_k\), a strictly positive probability law \(p\), positive numbers \((t_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in D_k}\), subsets \(E_{\mathbf z}\subseteq D_k\), and a matrix \(K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}\}}\) such that
\[
\mathbf z\in E_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
t_{\mathbf z}=\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}\setminus\{\mathbf z\}
\Longrightarrow t_{\mathbf x}<t_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
K^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(t)^{-1}K\preceq I,
\tag{11}
\]
but
\[
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_k}p_{\mathbf z}t_{\mathbf z}^q\right)^{1/q}}
\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(k\to\infty).
\tag{12}
\]
This last family is an abstract incidence-matrix construction. It is not a genuine lattice support, does not arise from minimizing halfspace caps, and does not encode the midpoint/parity relations behind (9). Hence it refutes only an inference from the listed algebraic properties, not (10) for genuine cap matrices and not the locked conjecture.

## Proof

For fixed \(\mathbf z\), only finitely many intersections \(D\cap H\) can be cut out by closed halfspaces \(H\ni\mathbf z\), so the minimum defining \(t_{\mathbf z}\) is attained. Translate a minimizing boundary parallel toward \(\mathbf z\) until the boundary passes through \(\mathbf z\); the new cap is contained in the old one, remains minimizing, and contains \(\mathbf z\) on its boundary. Write it as \(H=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\}\). If another point of \(D\) lies on its boundary, perturb \(\mathbf a\) slightly while keeping the boundary through \(\mathbf z\). All strict signs are preserved, every former boundary point remains included or is excluded, and one of the two signs of a generic perturbation excludes at least one former boundary point. The resulting cap is a strict subset of \(D\cap H\), contradicting strict positivity and minimality. This proves the asserted generic choice, including when \(D\) lies in a proper affine subspace.

If \(\mathbf x\in(D\cap H_{\mathbf z})\setminus\{\mathbf z\}\), then \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf z}\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)>0\). Translate the boundary of \(H_{\mathbf z}\) parallel to itself until it passes through \(\mathbf x\). The resulting closed halfspace contains \(\mathbf x\), is contained in \(H_{\mathbf z}\), and excludes \(\mathbf z\). Its mass is at most \(t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}\), and therefore the defining minimum for \(t_{\mathbf x}\) proves (1).

Fix depth-minimizing caps and consider \(\Phi\) from part 3. For every positive probability law \(r\) on \(D\), each fixed cap is an admissible halfspace in the definition of \(\tau_r(\mathbf z)\), so
\[
M_q(r)\le\Phi(r),
\qquad
M_q(p)=\Phi(p).
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, if \(p\) is a local minimum of \(M_q\) on the full open simplex, then it is a local minimum of the differentiable function \(\Phi\) there. Its coordinate derivatives are (4). The first-order condition on the simplex makes all \(g_{\mathbf x}\) equal to a common value \(\lambda\). Since \(\Phi\) is homogeneous of degree \(q+1\), Euler's identity gives
\[
\lambda=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}g_{\mathbf x}
=(q+1)\Phi(p)=(q+1)M_q(p).
\tag{14}
\]
Choose \(\mathbf z_\star\) with \(t_{\mathbf z_\star}=T\) and use generic caps. If \(\mathbf z_\star\in H_{\mathbf z}\) for some \(\mathbf z\ne\mathbf z_\star\), then (1) gives \(T\le t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}<t_{\mathbf z}\le T\), a contradiction. Thus the only cap containing \(\mathbf z_\star\) is its own, and (4) gives (6). Combining (6) with (14) yields \((q+1)M_q(p)\ge T^q\), proving (2).

For the consequence (3), apply accepted theorem \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\), with its discrete parameters \(n=N,d=0\), to the Borel probability measure \(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\delta_{\mathbf z}\) supported on \(\mathbb Z^N\). It supplies \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb Z^N\) for which every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y\) has mass at least \(2^{-N}\). If \(\mathbf y\notin\operatorname{conv}(D)\), strict separation from the compact convex hull gives a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y\) and disjoint from \(D\), a contradiction. Hence \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\), so \(T\ge t_{\mathbf y}\ge2^{-N}\). Substitution in (2) proves (3). This checks the predecessor's support, finiteness, and point-location hypotheses.

Now suppose only that \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\ell}\) is locally minimizing along the log-affine family. For every fixed active-cap selection, (13) shows that \(\boldsymbol\ell\) is a local minimum of \(\boldsymbol\ell'\mapsto\Phi(p_{\boldsymbol\ell'})\). Along a direction \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^N\),
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}p_{\boldsymbol\ell+s\mathbf b}(\mathbf x)\right|_{s=0}
=p_{\mathbf x}\,\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf x-\overline{\mathbf z}).
\]
The chain rule and arbitrariness of \(\mathbf b\) give \(\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}g_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf x-\overline{\mathbf z})=\mathbf0\). Subtracting \((q+1)M_q(p)\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf x-\overline{\mathbf z})=\mathbf0\) proves (5). This works for every active-cap selection, even when the depth minimum is attained by several caps. The deepest-point argument above proves (6) for a generic selection, but (5) supplies no coordinatewise upper bound for its left-hand side. The distinction is algebraically strict: on \(D=\{-1,0,1\}\) with uniform \(p\), the nonconstant deviations \((1,-2,1)\) have both zero \(p\)-mean and zero \(p\)-weighted first moment. Thus the Euler average and (5) alone do not force the deviations to vanish. This proves part 3 without deriving the full-simplex bound from constrained stationarity.

For part 4, convexity of \(P\) implies that if \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D\) and \(\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod 2\), then their integer midpoint \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) lies in \(D\). Log-affinity gives the exact midpoint identity
\[
p_{\mathbf z}^2=p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
\qquad(2\mathbf z=\mathbf x+\mathbf y).
\tag{15}
\]
The diagonal and row-mass statements in (7) are immediate from \(\mathbf z\in E_{\mathbf z}\subseteq D\cap H_{\mathbf z}\); the strict-order statement follows from (1), and it gives triangularity.

For each unordered pair of distinct points \(\{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\}\subseteq D\) in the same parity class, let \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\). Genericity of \(H_{\mathbf z}\) and the equality \(\mathbf y-\mathbf z=-(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\) imply that exactly one of \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\) lies in \(H_{\mathbf z}\). Thus exactly one is selected in row \(\mathbf z\), and its squared matrix entry is \(p_{\mathbf z}^2=p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}\). The central choices contribute \(\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2\). Therefore
\[
2\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2-\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D\\\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}}p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y},
\]
which is the equality in (9). The masses of the at most \(2^N\) parity classes sum to one, so Cauchy--Schwarz makes the parity sum at least \(2^{-N}\). This proves the lower bound in (9).

For any real vector \(v=(v_{\mathbf x})_{\mathbf x\in D}\), (15), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(E_{\mathbf z}\subseteq D\cap H_{\mathbf z}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|(Kv)_{\mathbf z}|^2
&=\left|\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}
\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}}\,v_{\mathbf x}\right|^2\\
&\le\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{\mathbf x}\right)
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}v_{\mathbf x}^2\right)\\
&\le t_{\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}v_{\mathbf x}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For fixed \(\mathbf x\), the map \(\mathbf z\mapsto2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\) is injective and takes every contributing \(\mathbf z\) into \(D\). Dividing by \(t_{\mathbf z}>0\), summing first over \(\mathbf z\), and then grouping by \(\mathbf x\), yields
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\frac{|(Kv)_{\mathbf z}|^2}{t_{\mathbf z}}
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}v_{\mathbf x}^2
\sum_{\mathbf z:\,\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}v_{\mathbf x}^2.
\]
This is exactly (8).

It remains to prove the algebraic obstruction. Fix \(k\ge2\) and take disjoint site groups
\[
A_k=[k]^2,
\qquad
B_k=[k]^3,
\qquad
C_k=[k]^4,
\qquad
D_k=A_k\sqcup B_k\sqcup C_k.
\]
Give each site in these groups probabilities
\[
a:=\frac1{3k^2},
\qquad
b:=\frac1{3k^3},
\qquad
c:=\frac1{3k^4},
\]
respectively, so each group has total probability \(1/3\). For sites in \(A_k\) or \(C_k\), let \(E_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf z\}\). For \(\mathbf z=(i,j,r)\in B_k\), let
\[
E_{(i,j,r)}:=\{(i,j,r)\}\cup\{(i,j,u,v)\in C_k:u,v\in[k]\}.
\]
Set
\[
t_{\mathbf z}=a\quad(\mathbf z\in A_k),
\qquad
t_{\mathbf z}=a+b\quad(\mathbf z\in B_k),
\qquad
t_{\mathbf z}=c\quad(\mathbf z\in C_k),
\]
and \(K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}\}}\). The selected probabilities in every row sum exactly to the displayed \(t_{\mathbf z}\). Every off-diagonal selection goes from \(B_k\) to \(C_k\), and \(c<a+b\); ordering \(C_k,A_k,B_k\) makes \(K\) lower triangular.

Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz in each row gives
\[
\frac{|(Kv)_{\mathbf z}|^2}{t_{\mathbf z}}
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}}
\frac{p_{\mathbf z}^2}{p_{\mathbf x}}v_{\mathbf x}^2.
\]
After summing rows, the coefficient of \(v_{\mathbf x}^2\) is \(a\) on \(A_k\), \(b\) on \(B_k\), and
\[
c+k\frac{b^2}{c}=c+\frac1{3k}\le1
\]
on \(C_k\). Since also \(a,b\le1\), this proves the contraction in (11). Direct counting gives
\[
\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\frac1{9k}+\frac{k^{-2}+k^{-3}+k^{-4}}9
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_k}p_{\mathbf z}t_{\mathbf z}^q
=\frac{k^{-2q}}{3^{q+1}}
\left(1+(1+1/k)^q+k^{-2q}\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently, the ratio in (12) is asymptotic to
\[
\frac13\left(\frac32\right)^{1/q}k,
\]
and diverges. This proves part 5 and, by construction, proves only an abstract matrix no-go.
