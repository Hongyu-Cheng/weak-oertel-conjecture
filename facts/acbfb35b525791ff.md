## Statement

Let $C$ be compact convex, $L=\min_i A_i>0$ on $C$ for finitely many affine functions, and $d\ge1$ an integer. Let two tuple laws $\pi,\pi_0$ on $C^2$ have the same endpoint marginals $\gamma_-,\gamma_+$ of mass $m>0$. For $b=(w_-+w_+)/2$, write $\rho=b_\#\pi$, $\rho_0=b_\#\pi_0$ and suppose $\rho_0\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}\rho$ in full physical coordinates. Put $V=(L(w_-)+L(w_+))/2$, $a=V/L(b)$ and $E=b_\#((1-a^d)\pi)$. If
$$
\Delta_0=\int\!\left(\log L(b)-\tfrac12[\log L(w_-)+\log L(w_+)]\right)d\pi_0,
$$
then $E(C)\le m[1-\exp(-d\Delta_0/m)]\le d\Delta_0$.

For $0<p\le1$ put $T_p=\int L^p\,d\rho$, $T_{p,0}=\int L^p\,d\rho_0$ and $c_p=\tfrac12\int L^p\,d(\gamma_-+\gamma_+)$. Then $c_p\le T_p\le T_{p,0}$ and
$$
\int L^p\,dE\le T_p[1-(c_p/T_p)^{d/p}]
\le T_{p,0}[1-(c_p/T_{p,0})^{d/p}]
\le (d/p)(T_{p,0}-c_p).
$$

For any nonnegative affine $q$ on $C$ put $F_q=q\log(q/L)$ with $0\log0=0$, $T_q=\int q\,d\rho=\tfrac12\int q\,d(\gamma_-+\gamma_+)$, and $\Delta_q(\pi')=\tfrac12\int F_q\,d(\gamma_-+\gamma_+)-\int F_q\,d(b_\#\pi')$. If $T_q>0$, then
$$
\int q\,dE\le T_q[1-\exp(-d\Delta_q(\pi)/T_q)]
\le T_q[1-\exp(-d\Delta_q(\pi_0)/T_q)]
\le d\Delta_q(\pi_0).
$$
At each tuple with $q_0=q(b)>0$ the tuple gap divided by $q_0$ is $-\log a+\mathrm{KL}((q_-,q_+)/(2q_0)\,\Vert\,(L_-,L_+)/(2V))$. The relative-entropy term vanishes exactly when $q_-/L_-=q_+/L_+$. For $T_q=0$ the weighted error and gaps are zero. All bounds use the same $\pi$. For $m=0$ all errors and bounds are understood as zero.

## Proof

Concavity gives $0<a\le1$. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives $-\log a\le\log L(b)-\tfrac12(\log L(w_-)+\log L(w_+))$. For any $0<p\le1$, the functions $-\log L$ and $-L^p$ agree on $C$ with finite globally convex functions. Indeed extend respectively $-\log t$ or $-t^p$ by its tangent line to the left of $\min_C L/2$, retaining a convex decreasing function on the whole real line. Taking the maximum over its compositions with the affine $A_i$ gives the desired extension. Convex order and the fixed endpoints therefore make the integrated logarithmic gap of $\pi$ at most $\Delta_0$. Applying concavity of $x\mapsto1-\exp(-dx)$ to that nonnegative tuple gap under $\pi/m$ gives the first bound. The linear bound follows from $1-e^{-x}\le x$.

Concavity of $L^p$ and convex order give $c_p\le T_p\le T_{p,0}$. Under the probability $L(b)^p\,d\pi/T_p$, the expectation of $a^p$ is $\int V^p\,d\pi/T_p\ge c_p/T_p$ by concavity of the $p$th power. The power $d/p$ is convex, so Jensen bounds $\int L^p a^d\,d\pi$ below by $T_p(c_p/T_p)^{d/p}$. Subtract this from $T_p$. For fixed $c>0$ and $r\ge1$, $T-c^r/T^{r-1}$ is increasing on $T\ge c$. Substitution of $T_{p,0}$ then gives the second bound. Convexity of $x^r$ at $x=1$ gives $1-x^r\le r(1-x)$ for $x\in[0,1]$, giving the last bound.

For strictly positive affine $q$ on $C$, the formula
$$
q\log(q/L)=\sup_t(qt-L\exp(t-1))
$$
has maximizing $t=1+\log(q/L)$ in one bounded interval on $C$. Restrict the supremum to this interval and replace $-L\exp(t-1)$ by the maximum over $-A_i\exp(t-1)$. This gives a finite globally convex extension as a supremum of affine functions. For nonnegative $q$, approximate by $q+\epsilon$ and use uniform convergence on $C$ of the corresponding entropies, since $x\log x$ is continuous at zero and $\log L$ is bounded. Thus in all cases convex order gives $\Delta_q(\pi)\le\Delta_q(\pi_0)$.

Affineness gives $q_0=(q_-+q_+)/2$. At a tuple with $q_0>0$, put $p_i=q_i/(2q_0)$ and $s_i=L_i/(2V)$, $i\in\{-,+\}$. Expanding the logs gives the stated gap $-\log a+\sum_i p_i\log(p_i/s_i)$. Terms with $p_i=0$ have value zero. The relative entropy is nonnegative, with equality exactly for equal probability vectors. This follows from $\log x\le x-1$ on the positive entries, including the strict missing mass if an entry of $p$ is zero while $s$ is positive. Equality is equivalent to the stated two ratios. At $q_0=0$ both endpoint $q$ values and the gap vanish. Therefore $q_0(1-a^d)\le q_0[1-\exp(-d\delta_q/q_0)]$ where $\delta_q$ is the nonnegative tuple gap, with both terms zero if $q_0=0$. The probability $q_0\,d\pi/T_q$ and concavity of $1-\exp(-dx)$ give the first affine-weighted inequality. The fixed endpoints and the convex-order comparison give the second, and $1-e^{-x}\le x$ gives the last. If $T_q=0$, affineness and nonnegativity force $q$ to vanish on both endpoint and midpoint laws almost everywhere.
