## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(p_{\mathbf I}>0\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\), with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}=1\). Set \(p_{\mathbf I}=0\) off \(D\). Assume that \(p\) has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with all lattice values, including zeros, in the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Write \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\). No condition on the affine rank of \(D\) is imposed.

Use the arrays and row masses of accepted fact `5d0f9e78ced711d6`, with both indices in \(D\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\quad(\mathbf J\in D)
\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2,\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(0\log0=0\). Let \(\mathcal F_{\min}\) be the set minimizing \(V\) on \(\mathcal F\), and define

\[
\mathcal T=\{(\mathbf J,\mathbf I):r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0
\text{ for some }r\in\mathcal F_{\min}\},\qquad
\mathcal J=\{\mathbf J:(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T
\text{ for some }\mathbf I\}.
\]

There is a unique maximizer \(r^\star\) of \(H\) on \(\mathcal F_{\min}\), and \(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0\) holds exactly on \(\mathcal T\). The set \(\mathcal J\) is exactly the positive-row target set of \(r^\star\). Every point of \(\mathcal J\) is a vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J)\), and

\[
\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J,
\qquad
|\mathcal J|\le2^k\le2^n,
\qquad k=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal J).
\]

For each \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J\), put \(D_{\mathbf J}=\{\mathbf I:(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T\}\). This is the complete positive support of the row, it satisfies \(D_{\mathbf J}=\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathbf J})\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and it contains \(\mathbf J\). The assignment-share potential
\(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=-\log(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D_{\mathbf J}\) is convex-admissible: whenever \(E\subseteq D_{\mathbf J}\) is finite and nonempty, \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}>0\) sum to one, and \(\mathbf z=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\) is integral, one has \(\mathbf z\in D_{\mathbf J}\) and

\[
\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf z)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I),
\qquad
\log r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf z}
\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}.
\]

The row \(\mathbf I\mapsto r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), extended by zero to all other lattice points, has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension. Its normalization by \(q^\star_{\mathbf J}\) is consequently a globally extendable probability table of mean \(\mathbf J\).

For a positive barycentric component at \(\mathbf J\) within \(D_{\mathbf J}\), use the scale-invariant logarithmic cost of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, written for a table \(u\) as \(G_\lambda(u)=\log u_{\mathbf J}-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log u_{\mathbf I}\). Thus the normalization map to that fact is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log((\max_{\mathbf K\in D}p_{\mathbf K})/p_{\mathbf I})\) for the original table. Then

\[
0\le G_\lambda(p)
\le G_\lambda(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\cdot})
=G_\lambda(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\cdot}/q^\star_{\mathbf J}).
\]

These conclusions include boundary targets, rank-zero supports, and all forced-zero entries. They impose no affine-independence condition on the barycentric component.

## Proof

The diagonal array belongs to \(\mathcal F\), and every feasible coordinate lies in \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). Thus \(\mathcal F\) is a nonempty compact convex set, and \(\mathcal F_{\min}\) is a nonempty compact convex face. For each coordinate in \(\mathcal T\), choose one minimizing array positive there, and average these finitely many arrays. The resulting \(r^\circ\in\mathcal F_{\min}\) is positive at every coordinate of \(\mathcal T\) and zero elsewhere. In particular, its positive row targets are precisely \(\mathcal J\).

We first strengthen the midpoint merging argument of accepted fact `5d0f9e78ced711d6`. Suppose that \(\mathbf K\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) has a representation \(\mathbf K=\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J\), where \(E\subseteq\mathcal J\), all coefficients are positive, and at least one used target differs from \(\mathbf K\). Since \(\mathbf K\in P\), lattice convexity gives \(\mathbf K\in D\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathbf J}\le q^\circ_{\mathbf J}\) for every used target. Remove the subrow \(\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathbf J}r^\circ_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/q^\circ_{\mathbf J}\) from each such row and add the sum of these subrows to row \(\mathbf K\). This preserves nonnegativity and every column sum. The sum added has mass \(\varepsilon\) and mean \(\mathbf K\), so it also preserves every row identity, including if row \(\mathbf K\) was among the removed rows. The decrease in \(V\) is

\[
\varepsilon\left(\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2-\|\mathbf K\|^2\right)
=\varepsilon\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J-\mathbf K\|^2>0,
\]

contradicting minimality. Therefore every lattice point in this hull belongs to \(\mathcal J\), and no member of \(\mathcal J\) is a convex combination of the other members. This proves both assertions about its hull.

For the intrinsic parity bound, fix \(\mathbf J_0\in\mathcal J\) and use the lattice \(\mathscr L=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(\mathcal J-\mathbf J_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). It has a basis of \(k\) elements as an additive group. The elementary basis assertion follows by induction for subgroups of \(\mathbb Z^n\): a nonzero first-coordinate image is \(a\mathbb Z\) for its least positive element \(a\); selecting a subgroup vector with that coordinate splits off one integer generator, leaving a subgroup in the remaining \(n-1\) coordinates. If the first-coordinate image is zero, omit that coordinate. The resulting basis is real-linearly independent, so its size equals the real span dimension, which here is \(k\). Hence \(\mathscr L/2\mathscr L\) has \(2^k\) classes. Two distinct targets in one class would have an integer midpoint in their hull, contrary to the preceding strict decrease. This proves \(|\mathcal J|\le2^k\), including \(k=0\), and in particular the accepted \(2^n\) bound for these general table arrays.

We next prove a strict supporting dual, including the structural zeros. Flatten the array coordinates as \(e=(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\), write its equalities as \(\mathsf A r=b\), and let \(c_e=\|\mathbf J\|^2\). Here \(\mathsf A\) is just the finite constraint matrix, with all column-sum equations and all row moment equations; redundant equations are retained. Let \(\mathcal Z=(D\times D)\setminus\mathcal T\). Subscripts on \(\mathsf A\) and \(c\) in this paragraph restrict their columns or coordinates to the indicated set.

If \(h\) is supported on \(\mathcal T\) and \(\mathsf A h=0\), then both \(r^\circ+th\) and \(r^\circ-th\) are feasible for sufficiently small \(t>0\). Minimality gives \(c\cdot h=0\). The equality of the row space with the orthogonal complement of the kernel therefore gives a finite vector \(y_0\) with \(c_{\mathcal T}=\mathsf A_{\mathcal T}^{\mathsf T}y_0\). If \(\mathcal Z\) is empty, this already supplies the desired dual. Otherwise put \(s^0_{\mathcal Z}=c_{\mathcal Z}-\mathsf A_{\mathcal Z}^{\mathsf T}y_0\) and consider

\[
Q=\left\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal Z}:
\sum_{e\in\mathcal Z}z_e=1,\quad
\mathsf A_{\mathcal Z}z\in\operatorname{range}(\mathsf A_{\mathcal T})\right\}.
\]

This is a closed subset of a finite simplex, hence compact. For \(z\in Q\), choose \(h_{\mathcal T}\) with \(\mathsf A_{\mathcal T}h_{\mathcal T}=-\mathsf A_{\mathcal Z}z\), and set \(h_{\mathcal Z}=z\). The array \(r^\circ+th\) is feasible for all sufficiently small \(t>0\). Its objective change divided by \(t\) is \(c\cdot h=s^0_{\mathcal Z}\cdot z\). This number cannot be negative by optimality, and it cannot be zero: equality would give an optimal array positive in a coordinate of \(\mathcal Z\), contrary to its definition. Thus it is strictly positive. If \(Q\ne\varnothing\), compactness gives \(\delta=\min_Q s^0_{\mathcal Z}\cdot z>0\); choose \(\varepsilon=\delta/2\). If \(Q=\varnothing\), choose \(\varepsilon=1\).

Let \(c'=c-\varepsilon\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z}\), where the indicator is zero on \(\mathcal T\). For every \(h\) with \(\mathsf A h=0\) and \(h_{\mathcal Z}\ge0\), one has \(c'\cdot h\ge0\). Indeed, when \(h_{\mathcal Z}=0\), both \(c\cdot h\) and the subtracted term are zero. Otherwise divide \(h_{\mathcal Z}\) by its positive coordinate sum to obtain an element of \(Q\), and use the bound \(\delta\); this case is impossible when \(Q\) is empty.

For completeness, the finite cone

\[
\mathcal C=\left\{\mathsf A^{\mathsf T}y+s:
s_{\mathcal T}=0,\quad s_{\mathcal Z}\ge0\right\}
\]

is closed. It is generated by finitely many vectors: a basis of \(\operatorname{range}(\mathsf A^{\mathsf T})\), their negatives, and the coordinate vectors of \(\mathcal Z\). A conic representation with the smallest positive support uses linearly independent generators, since otherwise a signed dependence can be subtracted until one positive coefficient vanishes. For a convergent sequence of conic combinations, pass to a subsequence using one fixed independent subset of generators. Its coefficients converge by the linear inverse on their span and remain nonnegative. The limit is in the cone, proving closedness.

If \(c'\notin\mathcal C\), let \(u_0\) be its closest point in \(\mathcal C\). Existence follows from closedness by a bounded minimizing sequence. Differentiating squared distance along segments in the convex cone gives \((c'-u_0)\cdot(u-u_0)\le0\) for all \(u\in\mathcal C\). Taking \(u=0,2u_0\) shows \((c'-u_0)\cdot u_0=0\). Consequently \(h=u_0-c'\) satisfies \(h\cdot u\ge0\) on the cone and \(h\cdot c'=-\|c'-u_0\|^2<0\). The cone contains \(\operatorname{range}(\mathsf A^{\mathsf T})\) with both signs and all coordinate vectors of \(\mathcal Z\), so these inequalities imply \(\mathsf A h=0\) and \(h_{\mathcal Z}\ge0\), contradicting the preceding paragraph. Thus \(c'\in\mathcal C\).

Adding \(\varepsilon\mathbf1_{\mathcal Z}\) to this representation, or using \(y_0\) directly when \(\mathcal Z\) is empty, proves that finite vectors \(y,s\) exist with

\[
c=\mathsf A^{\mathsf T}y+s,\qquad
s_e=0\ (e\in\mathcal T),\qquad
s_e>0\ (e\in\mathcal Z).
\]

For every feasible array, \(V(r)=b\cdot y+s\cdot r\), while \(V(r^\circ)=b\cdot y\). Hence the entire optimal face is exactly the feasible arrays supported on \(\mathcal T\). This proves both dual attainment and the required strictness without a linear programming theorem or an interiority assumption.

Write the column multipliers in \(y\) as \(\alpha_{\mathbf I}\) and the row moment multipliers as \(\boldsymbol\eta_{\mathbf J}\in\mathbb R^n\). Define the affine functions \(\ell_{\mathbf J}(x)=\|\mathbf J\|^2-\boldsymbol\eta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(x-\mathbf J)\). The strict dual says

\[
\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)-\alpha_{\mathbf I}
\begin{cases}
=0,&(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T,\\
>0,&(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\notin\mathcal T.
\end{cases}
\]

Every column has positive sum \(p_{\mathbf I}\), so some entry in that column belongs to \(\mathcal T\). It follows that \(\alpha_{\mathbf I}=\min_{\mathbf K\in D}\ell_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf I)\). In particular these lattice values are restrictions of the concave function \(x\mapsto\min_{\mathbf K\in D}\ell_{\mathbf K}(x)\). For each row define its convex equality cell

\[
\Omega_{\mathbf J}=\{x\in P:\ell_{\mathbf J}(x)\le\ell_{\mathbf K}(x)
\text{ for every }\mathbf K\in D\}.
\]

We have \(D_{\mathbf J}=D\cap\Omega_{\mathbf J}\) for a positive row. Therefore every integer point in \(\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathbf J})\) lies in \(P\cap\Omega_{\mathbf J}\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_{\mathbf J}\). The positive row of \(r^\circ\), divided by its mass, expresses \(\mathbf J\) as a convex combination of \(D_{\mathbf J}\). Thus \(\mathbf J\in D_{\mathbf J}\). These arguments include cells in proper affine subspaces and cells meeting any boundary face of \(P\).

The function \(H\) is continuous on the compact optimal face, so it attains a maximum. For fixed \(p_{\mathbf I}>0\), the function \(-t\log(t/p_{\mathbf I})\) is strictly concave on \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\): its second derivative is \(-1/t\) for \(t>0\), and a segment with a zero endpoint has the same strict inequality directly from the term \(-t\log t\). Hence the sum is strictly concave in the array, proving uniqueness of the maximizer \(r^\star\).

This maximizer is positive on all of \(\mathcal T\). Otherwise mix it with \(r^\circ\): at previously zero coordinates, the entropy change for \((1-t)r^\star+tr^\circ\) is \(-t\log t\) times the positive sum of the corresponding \(r^\circ\) coordinates, plus \(O(t)\). At previously positive coordinates the change is \(O(t)\) by differentiability. Since there are finitely many coordinates, the total change is positive for sufficiently small \(t>0\), a contradiction. Off \(\mathcal T\), all optimal arrays are zero by definition. This proves the exact support assertion.

There are finite entropy multipliers on this exact support. Indeed, every \(h\) supported on \(\mathcal T\) with \(\mathsf A h=0\) permits both signs of a sufficiently small perturbation of \(r^\star\). Such perturbations remain optimal for \(V\), by the dual characterization above. Stationarity of \(H\) and the row-space/kernel identity give finite scalars \(a_{\mathbf I}\) and finite vectors \(\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\in\mathbb R^n\) such that

\[
-\log\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}-1
=a_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
\qquad((\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T).
\]

Put \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}=-\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\), and define the nonempty active set and its normalizing sum at each source by

\[
\mathcal A(\mathbf I)=\{\mathbf K:(\mathbf K,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T\},\qquad
N(\mathbf I)=\sum_{\mathbf K\in\mathcal A(\mathbf I)}
\exp\!\left(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)\right).
\]

Summing the exponentiated stationarity equations in the column and using its sum \(p_{\mathbf I}\) determines the column factor. Thus the exact formula is

\[
r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle p_{\mathbf I}\,
\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J))}{N(\mathbf I)},
&(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T,\\
0,&(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\notin\mathcal T.
\end{cases}
\]

No term for a forced-zero entry is included in the denominator. Redundant moment equations and boundary targets cause no problem, because the multipliers were obtained from the kernel of the restricted finite matrix, not from a full-support distribution on \(D\).

Fix a positive row \(\mathbf J\) and a positive barycentric combination \(\mathbf z=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(E\subseteq D_{\mathbf J}\). We already proved that \(\mathbf z\in D_{\mathbf J}\). If \(\mathbf K\in\mathcal A(\mathbf z)\), the affine difference \(\ell_{\mathbf K}-\ell_{\mathbf J}\) is nonnegative at every \(\mathbf I\in E\) and has value zero at \(\mathbf z\). Positivity of all coefficients forces its value to be zero at every one of those sources. By the strict dual characterization, \(\mathbf K\in\mathcal A(\mathbf I)\) for every \(\mathbf I\in E\). Therefore \(\mathcal A(\mathbf z)\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathbf I\in E}\mathcal A(\mathbf I)\), even when active sets are larger at some boundary sources.

Weighted Hölder's inequality on the common active set now gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N(\mathbf z)
&=\sum_{\mathbf K\in\mathcal A(\mathbf z)}
\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}
\left(\exp(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K))\right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\\
&\le\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf K\in\mathcal A(\mathbf z)}
\exp(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K))\right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\\
&\le\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}N(\mathbf I)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality follows directly by dividing each positive family by its sum, applying weighted arithmetic-geometric mean to its normalized entries for each \(\mathbf K\), and summing over \(\mathbf K\). The last inequality uses the proved active-set inclusion. Thus no common active set on an entire neighborhood, or across a boundary, is presumed. Taking logarithms and subtracting the affine function \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(x-\mathbf J)\) proves the desired convex-admissibility, since the exact entropy formula gives \(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=\log N(\mathbf I)-\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\).

Global log-concavity of the extension of \(p\), applied repeatedly to this finite convex combination, gives \(\log p_{\mathbf z}\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}\). Subtracting the just-proved inequality for \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\) yields the claimed inequality for \(\log r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\).

To verify an extension with all zeros retained, take the lower convex roof on \(\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathbf J})\) of the finite values \(-\log r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\). Explicitly, minimize their coefficient-weighted sum over the nonnegative coefficient vectors of sum one and prescribed barycenter. Each feasible coefficient set is compact, so the roof is finite and attained; combining minimizers proves convexity. The inequality already proved at lattice barycenters, together with the singleton representation, makes the roof equal to the prescribed value at every \(\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}\). Its negative exponential on that convex hull, extended by zero elsewhere in \(\mathbb R^n\), is finite-valued and globally log-concave. It agrees with the row at every lattice point, because the hull's complete lattice intersection is \(D_{\mathbf J}\). This is the convex-roof extension mechanism of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, applied to the row table. The same construction with the values \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\) gives a finite convex extension of the share potential on that hull. Multiplying the row extension by \(1/q^\star_{\mathbf J}\) preserves log-concavity, and the row identity gives mean \(\mathbf J\).

Finally, a component at \(\mathbf J\) within \(D_{\mathbf J}\) has \(\mathbf J\) in that positive support. Scale invariance of the logarithmic cost gives its equality under row normalization, and direct subtraction gives

\[
G_\lambda(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\cdot})-G_\lambda(p)
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)
-\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf J)\ge0.
\]

The original cost is nonnegative by log-concavity of \(p\). This proves the cost comparison for every stated component in the original table normalization.
