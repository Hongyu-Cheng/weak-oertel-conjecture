## Statement

Let n>=2 and d>=1 be integers, and let L, alpha, B be positive real numbers. Define
\[
K=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}: |x_i|\le L\text{ for every }i,\quad |x_i|+\alpha|x_j|\le B\text{ for every ordered pair }i\ne j\right\}.
\]
Let D be any convex body in \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\), and let \(C=K\times D\). Write \(v=\mathcal{H}_{d}(D)>0\), and, for each coordinate i and each real t, set
\[
a_i(t)=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{1}_{C}(x_i=t,\mathbf{x}_{-i}=\mathbf{z},\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y},
\qquad W=a_i(0)+2\sum_{k\ge1}a_i(k).
\]
For an integer \(k\ge0\), the mixed mass of \(P_{i,k}(\mathbf{x}_{-i},\mathbf{y})=(2k+1)\mathbf{1}_{C}(x_i=k,\mathbf{x}_{-i},\mathbf{y})\) is \((2k+1)a_i(k)\). The mixed mass of \(Q_i(\mathbf{x}_{-i},\mathbf{y})=2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{C}(x_i=t,\mathbf{x}_{-i},\mathbf{y})\,dt\) is \(q_i=2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}a_i(t)\,dt\). An all-coordinate half-mass failure would require \((2k+1)a_i(k)<W/2\) for every i and k, and \(q_i<W/2\) for every i.

Put \((\beta,b)=(\alpha,B)\) if \(\alpha\ge1\), and \((\beta,b)=(1/\alpha,B/\alpha)\) if \(0<\alpha<1\). Define the integer
\(r=\min\{\lfloor L\rfloor,\lfloor b/(\beta+1)\rfloor\}\).
Then, simultaneously for every coordinate i,
\[
2(2r+1)a_i(r)-W\ \ge\ v\left((2r+1)^n-(2r)^n\right)>0.
\]
Consequently the above C never exhibits an all-coordinate half-mass failure. In particular this holds for every rational choice of L, alpha, and B in the proposed symmetric base family with n>=3.

## Proof

First normalize the pair constraints. When \(\alpha<1\), dividing \(|x_i|+\alpha|x_j|\le B\) by alpha and then interchanging the ordered indices shows that their entire collection is equivalent to \(|x_i|+(1/\alpha)|x_j|\le B/\alpha\) for all ordered pairs. Hence in both cases the normalized parameters \(\beta\ge1\) and b describe exactly the same K.

For a vector with largest and second-largest absolute coordinates m and s, respectively, the largest left side among its ordered pair constraints is \(\beta m+s\). Indeed, if the coefficient beta falls on a largest coordinate, the best distinct partner has absolute value s. If it falls on any other coordinate, the left side is at most \(\beta s+m\le\beta m+s\). Thus an integer vector belongs to K exactly when its largest magnitude is at most \(\lfloor L\rfloor\) and \(\beta m+s\le b\).

The entire integer cube \(\{-r,\ldots,r\}^{n}\) lies in K, because \((\beta+1)r\le b\) and \(r\le L\). If \(r=\lfloor L\rfloor\), there are no integer vectors outside this cube. Otherwise \(r=\lfloor b/(\beta+1)\rfloor<\lfloor L\rfloor\). For any integer m>r, define \(j_m=\lfloor b-\beta m\rfloor\). An outside vector with largest magnitude m is feasible if and only if \(m\le\lfloor L\rfloor\), \(j_m\ge0\), and every other magnitude is at most \(j_m\). Moreover,
\(b<(\beta+1)(r+1)\) gives \(j_m\le r\), and therefore its largest coordinate is unique. Set
\[
\mathcal{M}=\left\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:r<m\le\lfloor L\rfloor,\ j_m\ge0\right\}
\]
in this second case, and set \(\mathcal{M}=\varnothing\) in the first case. The numbers \(j_m\), for \(m\in\mathcal{M}\), belong to \(\{0,\ldots,r\}\) and are distinct: since \(\beta\ge1\), one has \(j_{m+1}\le j_m-1\). This also proves finiteness.

We have therefore partitioned all integer vectors in K into the core cube and the following disjoint sets: choose \(m\in\mathcal{M}\), choose its unique largest coordinate and its sign, and choose all remaining coordinates independently in \(\{-j_m,\ldots,j_m\}\). Their exact count is
\[
\frac{W}{v}=(2r+1)^n+2n\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(2j_m+1)^{n-1}.
\]
The identity follows also directly from the definition of mixed volume, because every nonempty integer fiber of C is precisely D.

Let \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether some \(m\in\mathcal{M}\) has \(j_m=r\). Fix any coordinate i to the value r. The core gives \((2r+1)^{n-1}\) vectors. An outside vector in this slice must have its unique largest coordinate among the other n-1 coordinates, and it contributes precisely when \(j_m\ge r\), equivalently \(j_m=r\). There is at most one such m. Thus the exact slice census is
\[
\frac{a_i(r)}{v}=(2r+1)^{n-1}+2(n-1)\varepsilon(2r+1)^{n-2}.
\]
This formula is valid also when r=0. Coordinate permutation symmetry gives the same formula for every i.

It remains to bound the lower-width outside sets. The function \(t\mapsto t^{n-1}\) is convex on \([0,\infty)\) because \(n\ge2\). Its midpoint inequality on each interval \([2j,2j+2]\) gives
\[
2(2j+1)^{n-1}\le\int_{2j}^{2j+2}t^{n-1}\,dt.
\]
This follows by adding its values at equally spaced points on either side of the midpoint, using convexity, and integrating. Summing the exact integrals for \(j=0,\ldots,r-1\) yields
\[
2n\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(2j+1)^{n-1}\le n\int_0^{2r}t^{n-1}\,dt=(2r)^n.
\]
When r=0, both sides are zero. Since the \(j_m\) are distinct, all terms with \(j_m<r\) are bounded by this sum. Hence
\[
\frac{W}{v}\le(2r+1)^n+2n\varepsilon(2r+1)^{n-1}+(2r)^n.
\]
Combining this with the exact slice census gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2(2r+1)a_i(r)-W}{v}
&\ge (2r+1)^n-(2r)^n+2(n-2)\varepsilon(2r+1)^{n-1}\\
&\ge (2r+1)^n-(2r)^n>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(P_{i,r}\) exceeds half the mass for every i, independently of the values of q_i. Finally, positivity of L, alpha, and B ensures that K contains a neighborhood of the origin, so K and C are actual full-dimensional convex bodies. All floors and census terms are exact for rational parameters; the only integral used in the bound was evaluated exactly above. No finite-search conclusion is used.
