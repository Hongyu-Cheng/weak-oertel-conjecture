## Statement

Fix a real number \(T\ge0\), and define
$$
C_T=2^{13}T^2e^{4T},\qquad
c_T=\frac14e^{-C_T},\qquad
\gamma_T=\frac18e^{-3C_T/2}=c_T^{3/2}.
\tag{1}
$$
Let \(L\ge1\) be an integer. For every \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(n_\ell\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A_\ell\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty, and choose positive weights \(w_{\ell,x}\) on its entire support. Assume that every coordinatewise parity class contains at most two atoms of \(A_\ell\), and that the midpoint of every unordered distinct same-parity pair belongs to \(A_\ell\). Retain the original laws and normalizers
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=w_{\ell,x}/Z_\ell,\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,\qquad
N=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell.
\tag{2}
$$
Extend each law by zero outside its support. For every unordered distinct same-parity pair \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\), write \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\), and assume
$$
p_{\ell,j_e}^{\,2}=p_{\ell,u_e}p_{\ell,v_e},\qquad
\left|\frac12\log\frac{p_{\ell,v_e}}{p_{\ell,u_e}}\right|\le T.
\tag{3}
$$
There is no upper bound on the number of pairs having a given midpoint, no global comparison of distinct atom masses, and no convex-completeness or global-affinity assumption.

For one factor suppress \(\ell\). Use exactly the original-law full-row definitions of accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705 and c00bd51b9e53801c:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P(A)&=\lbrace\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n:
A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\ne\varnothing\rbrace,
&K&=|\mathcal P(A)|,\\
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_xp_xH_p(x)^2,
&\chi(p)&=\sum_xp_xH_p(x),\\
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)},
&Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,
&q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},\qquad
\nu=\sum_xq_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
The occupied count \(K\) is not replaced by the ambient capacity \(2^n\). Every row retains both orientations of each noncentral reflected pair and its central atom once. Then every factor satisfies
$$
K^2b(p)\ge1,\qquad
\nu\le C_T\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{5}
$$
For any finite lattice law \(p\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of its original mass over all ambient closed halfspaces containing \(j\), and let \(M_k(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^k\). These are exactly the depth and moment conventions in 445a9e668ee1a705 and c00bd51b9e53801c. For the original product (2), with halfspaces taken in \(\mathbb R^N\),
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_T\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\gamma_T\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3}.
\tag{6}
$$
In particular, one common coefficient gives \(M_2(P)\ge\gamma_T4^{-N}\) and \(M_3(P)\ge\gamma_T8^{-N}\). The coefficient is paid once for the entire product, and singleton and lower-dimensional supports are included.

## Proof

Fix one factor. We retain the canonical quantities \(p,Z,h_x,t_x\) from c00bd51b9e53801c throughout. Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of all unordered distinct same-parity pairs, and let \(\mathcal J=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr Q\rbrace\). Set \(m_x=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q:j_e=x\rbrace|\), \(h_x=2m_x+1\), and \(t_x=Kp_x\), as in that predecessor. Every \(h_x\) is an odd positive integer, but it is unbounded here. In particular we impose no bound on the midpoint multiplicity called \(D\) in c00bd51b9e53801c. This midpoint multiplicity is different from the endpoint-incidence parameter \(D_i\) of 445a9e668ee1a705: under the present parity-two hypothesis, an atom is an endpoint of at most one pair in \(\mathscr Q\), so that endpoint-incidence parameter is at most one.

A noncentral reflected pair about a supported center \(x\) is exactly a pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(x\). Different such pairs at a fixed center have disjoint endpoints. By (3) each geometric-mean term in the full row equals \(p_x\). Consequently \(|R_x|=h_x\), \(H_p(x)=h_xp_x\), and \(\kappa_x\) is uniform on its entire row. In particular \(b(p)>0\).

For \(\eta\in\mathcal P(A)\), use the canonical class quantities \(r_\eta=\sum_{x\equiv\eta\pmod2}p_x\), \(R_\eta=Kr_\eta\), and \(d_\eta=(R_\eta-1)^2\) from c00bd51b9e53801c. Expanding every occupied parity square and using all pair equalities yields
$$
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}p_{u_e}p_{v_e}
=\sum_x(2m_x+1)p_x^2
=\chi(p).
\tag{7}
$$
No bound on \(m_x\) occurs in this identity. Retain exactly the full-row energy notation of c00bd51b9e53801c:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),
&\mathcal D&=K\sum_\eta r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_\eta d_\eta,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\sum_xp_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2,
&\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}&=\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The symbol \(\mathcal D\) in (8) is a parity-mass energy, not either incidence parameter discussed above. Since there are exactly \(K\) occupied classes and their masses sum to one, \(\mathcal D\ge0\). By (7), direct expansion gives
$$
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}=A_{\mathcal J}-2K\chi(p)+1,
\qquad A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}\ge1.
\tag{9}
$$
This proves the first assertion in (5), independently of any selected-row identity.

For an odd positive integer \(h\) and \(t>0\), set \(\Psi_h(t)=t(ht-1)^2\), and abbreviate \(\Psi_x=\Psi_{h_x}(t_x)\). Then \(\sum_x\Psi_x=K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\). For a pair \(e\), choose either endpoint ordering, and write
$$
\xi=t_{j_e},\qquad \upsilon=t_{u_e},\qquad
\omega=t_{v_e},\qquad
z_e=\frac12\log\frac{\omega}{\upsilon},\qquad
G_e=2h_{j_e}\xi^3z_e^2.
\tag{10}
$$
Thus \(\upsilon\omega=\xi^2\), \(|z_e|\le T\), and \(\upsilon=\xi e^{-z_e}\), \(\omega=\xi e^{z_e}\). Uniformity of the full geometric row gives the exact score identities
$$
V_x=\frac2{h_x}\sum_{e:j_e=x}z_e^2,
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}\nu=\frac1K\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}G_e.
\tag{11}
$$
We now bound the latter sum without ever multiplying one center deviation by an uncontrolled number of incident pairs.

First consider all pairs at a center \(x\) satisfying \(h_xt_x\notin[1/2,2]\). Put \(a=h_xt_x\) only in this calculation. The complete contribution at this center satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e:j_e=x}G_e
&\le 2T^2h_xt_x^3m_x
=T^2h_x(h_x-1)t_x^3\\
&\le T^2t_xa^2
\le4T^2t_x(a-1)^2
=4T^2\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
The penultimate inequality holds because \(a^2/(a-1)^2\le1\) for \(0<a\le1/2\) and is at most four for \(a\ge2\). Equation (12) charges the entire row to its center once, regardless of how large \(h_x\) is.

It remains to treat pairs whose centers satisfy \(1/2\le h_{j_e}\xi\le2\). Their endpoint class has exactly the two atoms \(u_e,v_e\), because the class contains the distinct endpoints and has size at most two. Let \(\eta(e)\) denote this class. Define only for this pair
\(F_e=\Psi_{u_e}+\Psi_{v_e}+d_{\eta(e)}\). From (10) and the upper bound on \(h_{j_e}\xi\),
$$
G_e\le4T^2\xi^2
\le T^2(\upsilon+\omega)^2
=T^2R_{\eta(e)}^2.
\tag{13}
$$
If \(R_{\eta(e)}\le1/2\) or \(R_{\eta(e)}\ge2\), then \(R_{\eta(e)}^2\le4(R_{\eta(e)}-1)^2\), and hence \(G_e\le4T^2d_{\eta(e)}\). This explicitly charges tiny endpoint-class mass, as well as large endpoint-class mass, to its class deviation.

Suppose instead that \(1/2\le R_{\eta(e)}\le2\). Define
$$
\rho_T=\frac1{2(1+e^{2T})},\qquad
\delta_T=\frac{\rho_T^2}{64}.
\tag{14}
$$
Since \(\omega/\upsilon\) lies between \(e^{-2T}\) and \(e^{2T}\), both endpoints satisfy \(\upsilon,\omega\ge R_{\eta(e)}/(1+e^{2T})\ge\rho_T\). We claim that \(F_e\ge\delta_T\), uniformly over every odd endpoint multiplier, including unbounded ones.

For completeness, the root estimate used in c00bd51b9e53801c is valid without an upper bound on \(h\):
$$
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(t,\lbrace0,1/h\rbrace\bigr)^2
\le2\Psi_h(t)\qquad(h\ge1,\ t>0).
\tag{15}
$$
Indeed, if \(t\le1/(2h)\), then \(\Psi_h(t)\ge t/4\ge t^2/2\). If \(t\ge1/(2h)\), then \(\Psi_h(t)=h^2t(t-1/h)^2\ge(h/2)(t-1/h)^2\ge(t-1/h)^2/2\). These two cases prove (15).

If \(F_e<\delta_T\), choose roots \(a_u\in\lbrace0,1/h_{u_e}\rbrace\) and \(a_v\in\lbrace0,1/h_{v_e}\rbrace\) with respective distances at most \(\sqrt{2\Psi_{u_e}}\) and \(\sqrt{2\Psi_{v_e}}\). Each distance is less than \(\sqrt2\rho_T/8<\rho_T/2\), so both roots are positive and both exceed \(\rho_T/2\). They are therefore reciprocals of odd positive integers. If either is one, their sum exceeds one by more than \(\rho_T/2\). If neither is one, both are at most \(1/3\), so their sum is at most \(2/3\). Since \(0<\rho_T\le1/4\), both cases imply \(|a_u+a_v-1|\ge\rho_T/2\). On the other hand,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|a_u+a_v-1|
&\le |a_u-\upsilon|+|a_v-\omega|
+|R_{\eta(e)}-1|\\
&\le(2\sqrt2+1)\sqrt{F_e}
<\frac{2\sqrt2+1}{8}\rho_T
<\frac{\rho_T}{2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
a contradiction. This proves \(F_e\ge\delta_T\). It is precisely here that parity occupancy at most two and the pair-score bound are used together: the bound keeps both endpoints away from zero when their class mass is near one, and two positive odd reciprocal roots cannot sum to one.

In this middle range, (13) and \(R_{\eta(e)}\le2\) give \(G_e\le4T^2\le(4T^2/\delta_T)F_e\). Define
\(B_T=4T^2/\delta_T=1024T^2(1+e^{2T})^2\).
Since \(\delta_T\le1\), the two outer ranges following (13) also give \(G_e\le B_TF_e\). Thus every pair at one of the remaining centers satisfies
$$
G_e\le B_T\bigl(\Psi_{u_e}+\Psi_{v_e}+d_{\eta(e)}\bigr).
\tag{17}
$$
This estimate does not charge its center deviation, and does not require any upper bound on its center or endpoint multipliers.

An atom can occur among all endpoint terms in (17) at most once, because its parity class contains at most one other atom. Likewise a parity class supplies at most one pair, so its \(d_\eta\) occurs at most once. Summing (12) over its centers and (17) over the other pairs therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}G_e
&\le(4T^2+B_T)\sum_x\Psi_x+B_T\sum_\eta d_\eta,\\
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
&\le(4T^2+B_T)\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+B_T\mathcal D
\le C_T\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
For the last inequality, \((1+e^{2T})^2\le4e^{4T}\) gives
\(4T^2+B_T\le4100T^2e^{4T}\le2^{13}T^2e^{4T}=C_T\), and \(B_T\le2C_T\). These comparisons include \(T=0\); in that case every \(G_e\) and \(\nu\) is zero. Centers that are themselves endpoints are already covered: their center charge in (12) and their single endpoint charge in (17) have exactly the combined coefficient shown in (18). No degree cutoff has been assumed or inferred globally.

Finally (9), (18), and \(\log a\ge(a-1)/a\) for \(a\ge1\) imply
$$
\nu\le C_T\frac{A_{\mathcal J}-1}{A_{\mathcal J}}
\le C_T\log A_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{19}
$$
This proves (5), also when there are no nontrivial pairs.

We give the original-product conversion explicitly, preserving the normalizations of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. For a supported product center \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), the full row is \(R_J=\prod_\ell R_{\ell,j_\ell}\). Direct factorization gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_P(J)&=\prod_\ell H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell),
&\kappa_J&=\bigotimes_\ell\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\\
Y_J(X)&=\sum_\ell Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),
&\sigma_J^2&=\sum_\ell V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Reflection preserves each geometric row law and negates its score. The factor scores therefore have mean zero, and independence gives \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2\). There is also the exact likelihood identity \(P_X=H_P(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}\) for \(X\in R_J\), where \(P_X=\prod_\ell w_{\ell,x_\ell}/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\) remains the original product probability.

Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each distinct noncentral reflected pair \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\), as well as \(J\) itself. Summing the smaller mass of each noncentral pair and half the central mass, and then using Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
The oriented sum counts every noncentral pair twice before its factor \(1/2\). Thus (21) holds also for singleton and lower-dimensional rows.

Under the auxiliary product center law \(q=\bigotimes_\ell q_\ell\), the exact identity \(P_JH_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell))q_J\) holds. Squaring (21), summing under the original probability \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q\), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
Here \(\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\), and none of the original laws has been conditioned or replaced. Put \(s_{\log}=\sum_\ell\log(K_\ell^2b(p_\ell))\ge0\). By (5), \(\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\le C_Ts_{\log}\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2}\right)
\exp\bigl(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_Ts_{\log}}\bigr)\\
&\ge\frac14e^{-C_T}\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
because \(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_Ts_{\log}}=(\sqrt{s_{\log}}-\sqrt{C_T})^2-C_T\ge-C_T\). Finally Jensen under the same original probability \(P\) gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving (6). Since \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\) and \(0<\gamma_T\le c_T\), the two ambient bounds follow with the common coefficient \(\gamma_T\). All constants depend only on \(T\).
