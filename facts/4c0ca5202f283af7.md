## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer. Let \(w=(w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be a nonzero, nonnegative, finitely supported admissible table: there is a finite-valued function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing with \(w\) at every lattice point, including all zero sites, and satisfying \(f((1-t)x+ty)\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(y)^t\) for all \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\). Use the normalizations of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160:
\[
W=\sum_z w_z,\qquad p_z=w_z/W,\qquad
H_j=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},\qquad
H_*=\max_jH_j,\qquad G=\log(2^nH_*/W).
\]
Displacement sums are oriented and include zero, and logarithms are natural. For \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), set \(\pi_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}p_z\) and \(q=\sum_b\pi_b^2\).

Define the original mean and covariance by \(m=\sum_zp_zz\) and \(\Sigma=\sum_zp_z(z-m)(z-m)^{\mathsf T}\). The symbol \(m\) is the original mean of accepted fact a41dc1e8d1a966c2, not the integer slicing dimension called \(m\) in part (iv) of ab57fac07c0d3160; no slicing parameter is used here. The exact normalization map to the directional variance in a41dc1e8d1a966c2 is \(\sigma_a^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a=\operatorname{Var}_p(a\cdot X)\), with \(\sigma_a^4=(a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a)^2\).

For \(\pi_b>0\), define the conditional class mean and covariance by
\[
\mu_b=\frac1{\pi_b}\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}p_zz,\qquad
\Sigma_b=\frac1{\pi_b}\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}
p_z(z-\mu_b)(z-\mu_b)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For \(\pi_b=0\), set \(\mu_b=m\) and \(\Sigma_b=0\). Set
\[
T_{\mathrm{orig}}=\sum_b\pi_b\Sigma_b,\qquad
T_{\mathrm{sq}}=\sum_b\frac{\pi_b^2}{q}\Sigma_b.
\]
The scalar \(q\) is the canonical parity-collision probability. The original class weights sum to one, and the squared class weights also sum to one.

Fix \(\kappa\ge0\) and assume that, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\mathbb E_p[(a\cdot(X-m))^4]
\le\kappa(a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a)^2.
\]
Then, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|a^{\mathsf T}(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a\right|
&\le\varepsilon_\kappa(G)\,a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a,\\
\varepsilon_\kappa(G)
&=\frac9{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,e^{G/2}(e^G-1)^{1/4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the same function \(\varepsilon_\kappa\) as in a41dc1e8d1a966c2. In particular, the requested absolute comparison holds with the absolute constant \(C=9/\sqrt2\), and \(\varepsilon_\kappa(G)\to0\) as \(G\downarrow0\) at fixed \(\kappa\).

The upper comparison has the sharper form
\[
a^{\mathsf T}(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a
\le\frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}\,a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a.
\]
Consequently, for any \(\eta\ge0\), if the additional input \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\eta\Sigma\) holds for this table, then
\[
T_{\mathrm{orig}}
\preceq\left(\eta+\frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}\right)\Sigma.
\]
Here \(A\preceq B\) means \(a^{\mathsf T}Aa\le a^{\mathsf T}Ba\) for every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\). The inequality \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\eta\Sigma\) is explicitly conditional, not a claim proved or cited in this packet.

## Proof

Accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160, part (i), gives \(q\ge2^{-n}>0\), \(2^nq\le e^G\), and \(G\ge0\). Let \(\Gamma\) be the law of independent \(p\)-distributed \(X,Y\), conditioned on \(X\equiv Y\pmod2\), exactly as in both accepted predecessors. Its joint probabilities are \(p_xp_y/q\) when \(x\equiv y\pmod2\), and zero otherwise. Its endpoint marginal is therefore
\[
p^{\mathrm{end}}_z=\Gamma\{X=z\}
=\frac{p_z\pi_{z\bmod2}}q.
\]
Thus the endpoint class mass is \(\pi_b^2/q\). Within a class with \(\pi_b>0\), its conditional endpoint probabilities are
\[
\frac{p^{\mathrm{end}}_z}{\pi_b^2/q}
=\frac{p_z}{\pi_b}\quad(z\equiv b\pmod2),
\]
the same as the original conditional probabilities. Classes with \(\pi_b=0\) have zero mass under both laws. In particular, the conditional covariance remains \(\Sigma_b\); only the class weights change. Also \(\sum_zp^{\mathrm{end}}_z=q/q=1\).

Let \(\tau=\operatorname{TV}(p^{\mathrm{end}},p)\), with the canonical convention \(\operatorname{TV}(u,v)=\tfrac12\sum_z|u_z-v_z|\). Summing the absolute differences class by class gives the exact identity
\[
\tau=\frac12\sum_b\left|\frac{\pi_b^2}q-\pi_b\right|
=\frac1{2q}\sum_b\pi_b|\pi_b-q|.
\]
Indeed the sign of \(p^{\mathrm{end}}_z-p_z=p_z(\pi_b/q-1)\) is constant within each positive class. There are \(2^n\) classes, including the zero-mass ones, so \(\sum_b\pi_b=1\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_b(\pi_b-q)^2
&=q-2q+2^nq^2=q(2^nq-1),\\
2q\tau
&\le\left(\sum_b\pi_b^2\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\sum_b(\pi_b-q)^2\right)^{1/2}
=q\sqrt{2^nq-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz and the accepted bound \(2^nq\le e^G\) therefore give \(\tau\le\tfrac12\sqrt{e^G-1}\). This is also the endpoint total-variation estimate proved in a41dc1e8d1a966c2.

Fix \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), put \(Z(z)=a\cdot(z-m)\), and use the normalization \(\sigma_a^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a\). Define the nonnegative class function \(s_b=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_ba\). For \(\pi_b>0\), subtracting the conditional mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
s_b
&=\mathbb E_p[Z(X)^2\mid X\bmod2=b]
  -(a\cdot(\mu_b-m))^2\\
&\le\mathbb E_p[Z(X)^2\mid X\bmod2=b],\\
s_b^2
&\le\left(\mathbb E_p[Z(X)^2\mid X\bmod2=b]\right)^2
 \le\mathbb E_p[Z(X)^4\mid X\bmod2=b].
\end{aligned}
\]
The last step is conditional Cauchy-Schwarz. Zero-mass classes have \(s_b=0\) and contribute nothing. Consequently,
\[
\sum_b\pi_bs_b^2\le\mathbb E_p Z(X)^4\le\kappa\sigma_a^4.
\]

For the squared class weights, the unchanged conditional probabilities computed above give
\[
\sum_b\frac{\pi_b^2}{q}s_b^2
\le\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{end}}}Z(X)^4
\le81e^G\mathbb E_p Z(X)^4
\le81e^G\kappa\sigma_a^4.
\]
The middle inequality is the accepted triple fourth-moment transfer in a41dc1e8d1a966c2, with exactly its endpoint law \(p^{\mathrm{end}}\), original center \(c=m\), and direction \(a\). That transfer is valid without an additional endpoint regularity hypothesis. Its source class weights are \(\pi_b^2/q\), and its within-class probabilities are \(p_z/\pi_b\), exactly the weights used here.

Set \(d_b^+=(\pi_b^2/q-\pi_b)_+\) and \(d_b^-=(\pi_b-\pi_b^2/q)_+\), where \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\). Both discrepancy tables have mass \(\tau\), since the two class-weight tables are probability tables. They satisfy \(d_b^+\le\pi_b^2/q\) and \(d_b^-\le\pi_b\). Applying Cauchy-Schwarz separately to their integrals of the nonnegative function \(s_b\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_bd_b^+s_b
&\le\sqrt{\tau\sum_bd_b^+s_b^2}
 \le9e^{G/2}\sqrt{\kappa\tau}\,\sigma_a^2,\\
\sum_bd_b^-s_b
&\le\sqrt{\tau\sum_bd_b^-s_b^2}
 \le\sqrt{\kappa\tau}\,\sigma_a^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The desired difference has the exact signed decomposition
\[
a^{\mathsf T}(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a
=\sum_bd_b^-s_b-\sum_bd_b^+s_b.
\]
Both sums on the right are nonnegative. Hence their difference lies between minus the first displayed upper bound and the second displayed upper bound. Its absolute value is at most the larger bound, not their sum. Since \(G\ge0\), this proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|a^{\mathsf T}(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a\right|
&\le9e^{G/2}\sqrt{\kappa\tau}\,\sigma_a^2
 \le\frac9{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,e^{G/2}(e^G-1)^{1/4}\sigma_a^2,\\
a^{\mathsf T}(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a
&\le\sqrt{\kappa\tau}\,\sigma_a^2
 \le\frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}\sigma_a^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
These inequalities hold for every direction \(a\), so adding the latter inequality to the explicitly assumed \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\eta\Sigma\) proves the conditional matrix comparison.

All moments and sums are finite by finite support. No step divides by \(\sigma_a\). If \(\sigma_a^2=0\), then \(Z(z)=0\) at every positive site, so all positive-class directional variances and both quadratic forms vanish. If \(G=0\), the total-variation bound gives \(\tau=0\), so the two class-weight tables and the two covariance averages coincide. Finally, \(e^{G/2}(e^G-1)^{1/4}\to0\) as \(G\downarrow0\), establishing the stated dimension-independent limit for any fixed hypothesized \(\kappa\).
