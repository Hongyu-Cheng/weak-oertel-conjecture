## Statement

Take independent unit exponentials with coefficients $(1,1/2,-1/400,\ldots,-1/400)$, with forty negative coefficients. Let $F$ be the probability that the signed sum is nonnegative and $\mu_1$ the conditional mean of the first exponential on the zero section, using the coarea conditional law. On $-400<t<1$ let $F_t$ denote the corresponding cap probability when each exponential has rate $1-tc_i$. Put $G(t)=F_t(1-F_t)/\prod_i(1-tc_i)$ and $D_-=\lim_{t\downarrow-400}(1-t)G(t)$. Then
$$
\frac{997}{1000}<\frac{\mu_1D_-}{F(1-F)}<\frac{998}{1000}<1.
$$
Thus the proposed universal payment $\mu_1D_-\ge F(1-F)$ from the left tilt endpoint fails for actual two-positive-phase coefficients.

## Proof

Under tilt the positive terms, including their coefficient scales, have rates $1-t$ and $2-t$. The sum $N$ of the forty absolute negative terms is a sum of exponentials with common rate $400+t$. At $s\ge0$, the survival probability for the sum of the two positive terms is
$(2-t)e^{-(1-t)s}-(1-t)e^{-(2-t)s}$, by convolution of their two exponential densities. Integrating this formula at $s=N$ gives
$$
F_t=(2-t)\left(\frac{400+t}{401}\right)^{40}
     -(1-t)\left(\frac{400+t}{402}\right)^{40}.
$$
Let $X=(400/401)^{40}$ and $Y=(200/201)^{40}$. At $t=0$ this yields $F=2X-Y$. The product of tilt rates is $(1-t)(1-t/2)(1+t/400)^{40}$. Cancel $1-t$ in the scaled expression, substitute $F_t$, and cancel $(1+t/400)^{40}$. Since $F_t\to0$ as $t\downarrow-400$, the remaining expression has limit $D_-=2X-(401/201)Y$.

At zero tilt, condition first on $N$ and eliminate the first unit exponential from the signed-sum equation. If $s$ is the value of the second positive term, the first exponential is forced to $N-s$. The coefficient of the first exponential is one, so its Jacobian factor is one. The conditional density at zero of the signed sum given $N$ is $2e^{-N}\int_0^N e^{-s}\,ds=2(e^{-N}-e^{-2N})$, since the second term has rate two. Weighting the integral by the forced value $N-s$ gives $2[(N-1)e^{-N}+e^{-2N}]$. Averaging and using $\mathbb E[Ne^{-N}]=(40/401)X$ gives section density $2(X-Y)$ and first-moment density $2[(40/401-1)X+Y]$. Thus $\mu_1=(40/401)/(1-Y/X)-1$.

The following exact rational arithmetic verifies positivity of the denominator and the claimed two strict bounds.

```python
from fractions import Fraction

X = Fraction(400, 401)**40
Y = Fraction(200, 201)**40
F = 2*X - Y
mu = Fraction(40, 401)/(1 - Y/X) - 1
D = 2*X - Fraction(401, 201)*Y
assert 0 < F < 1 and mu > 0 and D > 0
assert 997*F*(1 - F) < 1000*mu*D < 998*F*(1 - F)
```
