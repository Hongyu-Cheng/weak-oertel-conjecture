## Statement

Let $\mu$ be normalized mixed length of a compact convex source in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R$ with $N$ positive sampled interval fibers and total raw length $M>0$, and let $\eta$ be its integer marginal. Fix a positive fiber $I_{z_0}=[l,u]$ whose marginal closed-halfspace depth at $z_0$ is $\beta>0$. For $t\in\mathbb R$ set
$$
L(t)=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R^n}\mu\{(z,x):x\le t+a\cdot(z-z_0)\},\qquad
R(t)=\inf_{b\in\mathbb R^n}\mu\{(z,x):x\ge t+b\cdot(z-z_0)\}.
$$
Then $L,R$ are $N/M$-Lipschitz, respectively nondecreasing and nonincreasing, and satisfy $L(l)=0$, $R(u)=0$, and $L(t)+R(t)\ge\beta$ for all $t$. The cumulative distribution function $F(t)=\min\{L(t)/\beta,1\}$ defines a nonatomic probability $\nu$ carried by $\{z_0\}\times[l,u]$ with $\nu(H)\le\mu(H)/\beta$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$.

## Proof

On each sampled interval a length cap changes by at most $|\Delta|$ when its threshold changes by $\Delta$. Null fibers contribute nothing. The common bound $N|\Delta|/M$ and the individual monotonicities persist under the two infima. For every pair $a,b$ the two caps cover each entire fiber with $(a-b)\cdot(z-z_0)\ge0$, since the left threshold is at least the right threshold there. That base halfspace has marginal mass at least $\beta$, or is all of the base space when $a=b$. The sum of the two cap masses is therefore at least $\beta$ for every pair. Taking independent infima gives $L(t)+R(t)\ge\beta$.

Take the convex hull of the lifted positive intervals. It is a polytope, contained in the inducing source, and its fibers at retained sites contain their original intervals and hence equal them. The polytope is full-dimensional in the product of the affine hull of the retained bases with $\mathbb R$, since its projection spans the base affine hull and a positive interval supplies the vertical direction. Use a finite inequality description there. At $(z_0,l)$ at least one active inequality has a negative coefficient of $x$: otherwise moving a short distance downward would satisfy all active inequalities, and by finiteness still satisfy all strict ones. After solving for $x$ this gives an affine lower support graph through $(z_0,l)$. Extend its affine base function to $\mathbb R^n$. Its closed lower cap can meet any retained interval only in an endpoint, so $L(l)=0$. At $(z_0,u)$ the upward motion argument gives an active inequality with positive coefficient of $x$, and its upper cap gives $R(u)=0$.

Thus $F$ is continuous and nondecreasing, equals zero on $(-\infty,l]$, and equals one on $[u,\infty)$ because $L(u)\ge\beta$. Its probability has no atoms. For a nonvertical left cap tracing $x\le t$ at $z_0$, the target mass is $F(t)\le L(t)/\beta\le\mu(H)/\beta$. For a nonvertical right cap tracing $x\ge t$, it is $1-F(t)\le R(t)/\beta\le\mu(H)/\beta$, using $L+R\ge\beta$ when $L<\beta$ and zero target right mass when $L\ge\beta$. Nonatomicity justifies the closed right ray formula. A vertical cap contains the whole target fiber or misses it. In the former case $\mu(H)\ge\beta$ by marginal depth and in the latter case the target mass vanishes. In particular, a median of $\nu$ has original depth at least $\beta/2$.
