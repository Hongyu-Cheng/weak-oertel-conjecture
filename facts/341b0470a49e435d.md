## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be a strictly convex body with positive mixed $d$-volume, $d\ge1$, and let $P$ be the finite set of all its nonempty integer fibers $K_z=C_z$. For each target $z$ admitting a convex representation by sites other than $z$, there is a common positive inward margin in $K_z$ for every Minkowski interpolant using such a representation. In particular each zero-volume fiber lies over an extreme base of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$. There is an $\epsilon>0$ such that all independent compact convex $d$-body replacements $K'_z$ of the positive-volume fibers with $d_H(K'_z,K_z)<\epsilon$, retaining every zero-volume fiber, give exactly the integer fibers of their canonical convex hull and of a full-dimensional ambient convex body. Both have precisely the integer base set $P$.

## Proof

For fixed $z$, take any convex representation $z=\sum_{p\ne z}\lambda_p p$ with $\lambda_p\ge0$ and sum one. At least two distinct sites have positive weights. For every selection $x_p\in K_p$, combine two such positively weighted lifted points first. Their strict combination is in $\operatorname{int} C$ by strict convexity. An interior point combined with other points of $C$, retaining positive weight on the interior point, remains interior because its own open ball scales to an open ball about the combination. Thus $\sum_{p\ne z}\lambda_p x_p\in\operatorname{int}K_z$. If $K_z$ has zero volume, the representation polytope is therefore empty, so $z$ is extreme.

At each vertex of the nonempty representation polytope the interpolant is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_z$. Taking the least of finitely many positive distances gives $\delta_z>0$ such that the interpolant plus $\delta_z B_d$ is contained in $K_z$ at every vertex, with $B_d$ the closed unit ball. The equivalent inequality on support functions is linear in $\lambda$, so holds on the whole representation polytope. Take $\epsilon$ less than half the least $\delta_z$ for all nonempty polytopes, or any positive $\epsilon$ if all are empty. Each perturbation changes the support in each unit direction by at most $\epsilon$, and the same bound holds on each weighted source sum because the weights sum to one. Hence each perturbed excluded-target interpolant is contained in $K'_z$ by the support inequalities with slack at least $\delta_z-2\epsilon>0$. A representation using the target itself can be handled by removing that coefficient, applying the excluded-target inequality to the renormalized rest, then using convexity of $K'_z$. The sole-target representation is immediate. The compact convex families at all sites, including the retained lower-dimensional ones, now meet the anchored compatibility hypothesis of ba0f3db1bf4f62c6. That fact gives the exact hull sections. The hull projection is $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, whose integer points are exactly $P$, since it lies in the original convex projection of $C$.

Let $E$ be the direction space of $\operatorname{aff}P$. One positive fiber supplies all continuous directions to the affine span of the perturbed hull, and differences of lifts at the spanning bases supply the remaining base directions of $E$. If $E=\mathbb R^n$, this hull is full-dimensional. Otherwise add the radius-$\eta$ ball of $E^\perp$ in the base coordinates, times $\{0\}$, to the hull. For $\eta\le1$ the candidate integer points in the new projection belong to a fixed bounded set, so there are finitely many outside $\operatorname{aff}P$. Choose $\eta>0$ smaller than each of their positive distances from that affine space. At a base in $\operatorname{aff}P$ the perpendicular component of the added vector must be zero, so each of the old sections remains exactly the same and no other base in $\operatorname{aff}P$ enters. Adding the missing perpendicular directions makes the convex realization full-dimensional.
