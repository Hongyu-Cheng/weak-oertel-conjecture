## Statement

Let $K$ be a full-dimensional compact planar convex body with exposed point $v$. The probability $(2/3)\operatorname{Unif}(K)+(1/3)\delta_v$ has maximum closed-halfspace depth exactly $1/3$. For two full-dimensional convex endpoint fibers $A,B$ with uniform probability marginals and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, their midpoint law gives zero mass to $\partial K$ for every coupling.

## Proof

At $v$, the atom gives a lower bound of $1/3$. The exposing closed halfspace meets $K$ only at $v$, so it gives equality. At a point $y\ne v$ take the line through $y,v$ and the closed side whose uniform mass is at most $1/2$, using that the common line has zero area. Rotate this halfspace boundary by an arbitrarily small angle about $y$ in the direction that excludes $v$. Its uniform mass converges to that of the original side by dominated convergence, and it omits the atom for every nonzero small rotation. The infimum of masses of halfspaces containing $y$ is therefore at most $(2/3)(1/2)$.

For the compatibility statement, each endpoint lies in the interior of its fiber with probability one under its given marginal, so both are interior almost surely under any coupling. If $x\in\operatorname{int}A$ and $x'\in\operatorname{int}B$, their midpoint lies in the interior of $(A+B)/2$, since averaging a small ball around $x$ with $x'$ gives a neighborhood of the midpoint. Containment in $K$ then puts it in $\operatorname{int}K$.
