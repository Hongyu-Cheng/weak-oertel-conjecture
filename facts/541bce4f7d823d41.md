## Statement

For the actual integer fibers of a convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, let $w_z$ be their $d$-volumes in units of the volume of the standard simplex $T_d$. At an integer anchor $j$ define $E_j=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}\sum_z w_z e^{\alpha(z-j)}$. Let $T_j$ denote the maximum actual mass, in the same units, of a convex subbody whose positive unweighted mixed volume has centroid base $j$. In each of the following examples one ambient closed cap attains $T_j$.

1. For the five integer fibers $zT_4$, $z=1,\ldots,5$, at anchor $j=3$, $T_3=116$ and $E_3^{16}\ge 2^{90}3^{18}5^4>232^{16}$.
2. For the three integer fibers $(1+z/10)T_{40}$, $z=-1,0,1$, at anchor $j=0$, $T_0=1+2(9/10)^{40}$ and $E_0=1+2(99/100)^{20}\ge13/5>5/2>2T_0$.
3. For integers $N\ge1$ and $L\ge2S_2$, where $S_2=\sum_{k=1}^N k^2$, set $d=L^3$ and take the integer fibers $(1+z/L^2)T_d$, $z=-N,\ldots,N$, at anchor $j=0$. With $K=2N+1$,
$$
K(1-S_2/L)^{1/K}\le E_0\le K,\qquad
1\le T_0\le 1+\frac{N(N+3)}2\,2^{-L}.
$$
For each fixed $N$, the ratio $E_0/T_0$ tends to $K$ as $L$ tends to infinity through integers. In particular the ratio is unbounded over actual affine-root homothetic simplex frusta at fixed integer dimension one.

## Proof

Each example is an actual full-dimensional compact convex body: impose the stated closed real base interval and the inequalities $x_i\ge0$, $\sum_i x_i\le r(z)$ for the specified positive affine radius $r(z)$. Its integer weights are $r(z)^d$.

Any positive-mass convex subbody whose centroid base is $j$ has retained masses $0\le t_z\le w_z$ satisfying $\sum_z(z-j)t_z=0$. For every real $b$, the balance identity gives the upper bound
$$
\sum_z t_z=\sum_z[1+b(z-j)]t_z\le\sum_z w_z[1+b(z-j)]_+ .
$$
The maximum over the feasible mass box equals $T_j$ when positive by b1274a369dfa9af7, applied with intrinsic dimension $d$ to the unweighted measure on the actual source. The accepted theorem realizes its total mass and base moment by a single cap and puts the full centroid in the retained mixed set. Dividing all masses and moments by $|T_d|$ leaves the balance and the assertion unchanged. In the first two examples explicit caps also verify attainment directly.

For any balanced positive probability row $\lambda_z$ with $\sum_z z\lambda_z=j$, weighted arithmetic-geometric mean applied to $w_z e^{\alpha(z-j)}/\lambda_z$ gives $E_j\ge\prod_z(w_z/\lambda_z)^{\lambda_z}$, since the tilt cancels in the product.

For the five-fiber example, use $b=-1$ in the upper bound to get $T_3\le3+2(16)+81=116$. The cap $\sum_i x_i+3(z-4)\le18^{1/4}$ retains masses $(1,16,81,18,0)$. In fact $2<18^{1/4}<3$ ensures full capture at $z\le3$, zero at $z=5$, and radius $18^{1/4}$ at $z=4$. The row balances by $-2-16+18=0$, and its sum is 116. For $\lambda=(1,4,6,4,1)/16$, its mean is 3 and the weighted product gives
$$
E_3^{16}\ge\prod_{z=1}^5
\left(\frac{16z^4}{\binom4{z-1}}\right)^{\binom4{z-1}}
=2^{90}3^{18}5^4.
$$
Dividing by $2^{48}$ reduces the strict comparison with $232^{16}$ to $2^{42}3^{18}5^4>29^{16}$. The two integers are $1064933331235409756160000$ and $250246473680347348787521$.

In the three-fiber example the endpoint retained masses must be equal. Their common value is at most $(9/10)^{40}$ and the middle mass is at most one. The closed cap $\sum_i x_i\le 1-z/10$ attains both bounds. Minimizing the exponential endpoint pair gives twice the geometric mean of its weights, so $E_0=1+2(99/100)^{20}$. Bernoulli gives $(99/100)^{20}\ge4/5$, and $8\cdot9^{20}=97261323672455430408<10^{20}$ implies $(9/10)^{40}<(9/10)^{20}<1/8$. Thus $E_0\ge13/5>5/2>2T_0$.

In the family in item 3, $L>N$ and the radii are positive. Use the uniform balanced probability row. Pairing the opposite weights gives
$$
E_0\ge K\left[\prod_{k=1}^N (1-k^2/L^4)^{L^3}\right]^{1/K}
\ge K(1-S_2/L)^{1/K}.
$$
For the last step use $\prod(1-t_k)\ge1-\sum t_k$ for $0\le t_k\le1$, then Bernoulli to bound $(1-S_2/L^4)^{L^3}$. At tilt $\alpha=-L$ all terms are at most one, by $\log(1+z/L^2)\le z/L^2$, proving $E_0\le K$.

The feasible balanced row retaining only the central fiber has mass one and ensures $T_0\ge1$ by the accepted cap realization. Taking $b=-1$ gives
$$
T_0\le1+\sum_{k=1}^N(k+1)(1-k/L^2)^{L^3}.
$$
For $t=k/L^2\in(0,1)$, $(1-t)^{-1}\ge1+t$, and Bernoulli implies $(1-t)^{-L^2}\ge(1+t)^{L^2}\ge1+k$. Thus each power in the sum is at most $(1+k)^{-L}\le2^{-L}$. This yields the asserted bounds and limit. For any fixed factor depending only on integer dimension one, choose $N$ so that $K$ exceeds it and then choose finite $L$ large enough.
