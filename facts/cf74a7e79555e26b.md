## Statement

Let \(P,J\) be disjoint finite sets, with \(m=|P|\ge1\). Let \(T\ge0\) be an integer and let \(\beta\ge0\) be a finite real number. For each \(i\in P\cup J\), let \(B_i\ge2\) be an integer. For \(i\in P\), also let \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) be an integer. Define the allowed site sets and energies by
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_i&=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace,
&\bigl(g_i(0),g_i(1),g_i(2),g_i(3)\bigr)&=(0,1,B_i,C_{4,i})
&& (i\in P),\\
D_i&=\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace,
&\bigl(g_i(0),g_i(1),g_i(2)\bigr)&=(0,1,B_i)
&& (i\in J).
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(D_{\rm core}\) be any nonempty finite set. Assign each \(\mathbf z\in D_{\rm core}\) a positive finite spatial weight \(w_{\mathbf z}\) and an energy \(h_{\rm core}(\mathbf z)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with at least one energy-zero core site. The case of no additional core is represented by one site of energy zero and weight one. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm sel}&=D_{\rm core}\times\prod_{i\in P\cup J}D_i,
&\Gamma(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)&=h_{\rm core}(\mathbf z)+\sum_{i\in P\cup J}g_i(y_i),\\
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&E\in\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace,\\0,&E\in\mathbb Z,\ E>T,\end{cases}
&Z_{\rm sel}&=\sum_{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in D_{\rm sel}}w_{\mathbf z}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus \(\Gamma\) is the original additive spatial energy, now allowing a weighted core, and \(f_E\) is the original complete height tail at the original \(\beta,T\). The complete selective law assigns mass \(w_{\mathbf z}e^{-\beta t}/Z_{\rm sel}\) to every \((\mathbf z,\mathbf y,t)\) with \(\Gamma(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\le t\le T\), for integer \(t\). Its spatial marginal is
\(\mathbb P_{\rm sel}(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)=w_{\mathbf z}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)}/Z_{\rm sel}\).

Use the canonical occupation counts
$$
Q_{P,J}(\mathbf y)=|\lbrace i\in P\cup J:y_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,
\qquad
A_P(\mathbf y)=|\lbrace i\in P:y_i=1\rbrace|.
$$
Write \(Q=Q_{P,J}\). For each \(i\in P\cup J\), define the untruncated one-coordinate Gibbs high-occupation probability
$$
\rho_i=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{s\in D_i\cap\lbrace2,3\rbrace}e^{-\beta g_i(s)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{s\in D_i}e^{-\beta g_i(s)}},
\qquad
\mu=\sum_{i\in P\cup J}\rho_i.
$$
For every finite real \(a\ge0\) and every finite real \(b\ge2\), the assumptions \(\mu^2/m\le a^2\) and \(\mu/b\le a\) imply
$$
\mathbb E_{\rm sel}\exp\!\left[64\min\left\lbrace\frac{Q(Q+A_P/b)}m,Q\right\rbrace\right]
\le K(a),
\qquad
K(a)=\sqrt{\frac e{e-1}}\,
\exp\!\left(64e^{260}a^2+a(e^{64}-1)\right).
$$
The bound is independent of the numbers of sites, all costs, \(\beta,T,b\), and the core. In particular, it applies when \(B_i=b\) on \(P\) and \(B_i\ge b\) on \(J\).

## Proof

The energy-zero core site together with all coordinate sites equal to zero contributes a positive multiple of \(f_0\ge1\). Hence \(Z_{\rm sel}>0\). Summing the displayed complete law over its height coordinate gives exactly the stated spatial law.

First prove the needed conditional comparison under this spatial law. Fix \(i\in P\cup J\), a core site, and every coordinate other than \(i\), at values having positive joint marginal probability. Their energy
\(E=h_{\rm core}(\mathbf z)+\sum_{j\ne i}g_j(y_j)\) satisfies \(E\le T\), because all site energies are nonnegative. The exact conditional distribution is
$$
\mathbb P_{\rm sel}(y_i=s\mid\mathbf z,(y_j)_{j\ne i})
=\frac{f_{E+g_i(s)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{u\in D_i}f_{E+g_i(u)}}
\quad(s\in D_i).
\tag{1}
$$
The denominator is positive because its site-zero term is \(f_E>0\). The fixed core weight cancels in (1).

For an integer \(u\), set \(H(u)=\sum_{v=0}^u e^{-\beta v}\) when \(u\ge0\), and \(H(u)=0\) when \(u<0\). This is nondecreasing in \(u\). For every nonnegative integer \(d\), including \(E+d>T\),
$$
f_{E+d}=e^{-\beta(E+d)}H(T-E-d).
\tag{2}
$$
Consequently \(d\mapsto H(T-E-d)\) is nonincreasing. In particular, (2) also proves \(f_{E+d}/f_E\le e^{-\beta d}\), but the following group comparison uses the full monotonicity.

The low sites are \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), and every high site in \(D_i\cap\lbrace2,3\rbrace\) has greater energy than either low site. For this fixed coordinate, write
\(a_s=e^{-\beta g_i(s)}\) and \(u_s=H(T-E-g_i(s))\). Define
\(G_{\rm low}=\sum_{s=0}^1a_s\), \(G_{\rm high}=\sum_{s\in D_i\cap\lbrace2,3\rbrace}a_s\),
\(\widetilde G_{\rm low}=\sum_{s=0}^1a_su_s\), and \(\widetilde G_{\rm high}=\sum_{s\in D_i\cap\lbrace2,3\rbrace}a_su_s\).
Here \(\widetilde G_{\rm low}>0\). For every high site \(s\) and low site \(t\), monotonicity gives \(u_s\le u_t\). Summing \(a_sa_tu_s\le a_sa_tu_t\) over all these pairs gives \(\widetilde G_{\rm high}G_{\rm low}\le G_{\rm high}\widetilde G_{\rm low}\). By (1)-(2),
$$
\mathbb P_{\rm sel}(y_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\mid\mathbf z,(y_j)_{j\ne i})
=\frac{\widetilde G_{\rm high}}{\widetilde G_{\rm low}+\widetilde G_{\rm high}}
\le\frac{G_{\rm high}}{G_{\rm low}+G_{\rm high}}=\rho_i.
\tag{3}
$$
All denominators in this comparison are positive.

Next derive domination of the whole occupation count without asserting independence under the original law. Enumerate the indices of \(P\cup J\) as \(i_1,\ldots,i_{|P|+|J|}\), and let
\(I_j=\mathbf1_{\lbrace y_{i_j}\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace}\).
Conditioning (3) further only on \(I_1,\ldots,I_{j-1}\), by the tower property, shows that for every history of positive probability,
$$
p_j(I_1,\ldots,I_{j-1})
:=\mathbb P_{\rm sel}(I_j=1\mid I_1,\ldots,I_{j-1})
\le\rho_{i_j}.
\tag{4}
$$
This use of the tower property is valid because the previous indicators are functions of coordinates other than \(i_j\). Assign \(p_j=0\) to zero-probability histories.

Take independent uniform random variables \(U_j\) on \([0,1]\) and recursively define
\(I'_j=\mathbf1_{\lbrace U_j\le p_j(I'_1,\ldots,I'_{j-1})\rbrace}\).
Induction on \(j\), using the conditional transition probabilities, proves that \((I'_j)_j\) has the same joint law as \((I_j)_j\). Define also
\(V_j=\mathbf1_{\lbrace U_j\le\rho_{i_j}\rbrace}\).
These are independent Bernoulli variables with means \(\rho_{i_j}\), and (4) gives \(I'_j\le V_j\) for every \(j\), almost surely. Therefore, for
\(R=\sum_jV_j\), one has \(Q\le R\) in this coupling. In particular,
$$
\mathbb E_{\rm sel}\phi(Q)\le\mathbb E\phi(R)
\quad\text{for every nonnegative increasing function }\phi.
\tag{5}
$$
The mean of \(R\) is \(\mu\).

We give all tail and moment estimates for this independent Bernoulli sum. For every integer \(\ell\ge1\), independence and expansion over subsets give
$$
\mathbb P(R=\ell)
\le\mathbb E\binom R\ell
=\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,|P|+|J|\rbrace\\|A|=\ell}}\prod_{j\in A}\rho_{i_j}
\le\frac{\mu^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{6}
$$
The first inequality holds because \(\binom R\ell\ge\mathbf1_{\lbrace R=\ell\rbrace}\). For the last inequality, expand \(\mu^\ell\) over ordered \(\ell\)-tuples: every product using distinct indices occurs \(\ell!\) times, and all repeated-index terms are nonnegative. For \(\ell\) exceeding the number of variables, the subset sum and probability are both zero. Monotonicity of the logarithm gives
\(\log(\ell!)\ge\int_1^\ell\log x\,dx=\ell\log\ell-\ell+1\ge\ell\log\ell-\ell\).
Thus (6) implies
$$
\mathbb P(R=\ell)\le\left(\frac{e\mu}{\ell}\right)^\ell
\quad(\ell\ge1).
\tag{7}
$$

For any fixed real \(c>0\), set \(\psi_m(x)=\min\lbrace x^2/m,x\rbrace\) for \(x\ge0\). We claim
$$
\mathbb E e^{c\psi_m(R)}
\le\frac e{e-1}\exp\!\left(ce^{2c+4}a^2\right)
\quad\text{whenever }\mu^2/m\le a^2.
\tag{8}
$$
If \(\mu=0\), all Bernoulli variables vanish almost surely and the left side is 1, so (8) holds. Suppose \(\mu>0\), and split at the positive real number \(D=e^{c+2}\mu\). On \(R\le D\),
\(\psi_m(R)\le R^2/m\le e^{2c+4}\mu^2/m\le e^{2c+4}a^2\).
Hence this part of the expectation is at most \(\exp(ce^{2c+4}a^2)\). For each integer \(\ell>D\), use \(\psi_m(\ell)\le\ell\) and (7) to obtain
$$
e^{c\psi_m(\ell)}\mathbb P(R=\ell)
\le\left(\frac{e^{c+1}\mu}{\ell}\right)^\ell
\le e^{-\ell}.
$$
Summing these nonnegative bounds over integers \(\ell>D\), and extending to all \(\ell\ge1\), bounds the remaining expectation by
\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}e^{-\ell}=1/(e-1)\).
Since \(\exp(ce^{2c+4}a^2)\ge1\), the sum of these two bounds is at most
\((1+1/(e-1))\exp(ce^{2c+4}a^2)\), proving (8). This split is valid whether \(D\) is integral or lies below 1.

For the linear moment, independence and \(1+x\le e^x\) for \(x\ge0\) give
$$
\mathbb E e^{cR/b}
=\prod_j\left[1+\rho_{i_j}(e^{c/b}-1)\right]
\le\exp\!\left(\mu(e^{c/b}-1)\right)
\le\exp\!\left(2a(e^{c/2}-1)\right).
\tag{9}
$$
To verify the last inequality, put \(t=2/b\in(0,1]\). The exponential series and \(t^k\le t\) for integers \(k\ge1\) imply
\(e^{c/b}-1=\sum_{k\ge1}t^k(c/2)^k/k!\le(2/b)(e^{c/2}-1)\).
Now use \(\mu/b\le a\). The elementary inequality \(1+x\le e^x\) follows from the same series.

Finally \(0\le A_P\le m\). For every realized configuration,
$$
\min\left\lbrace\frac{Q(Q+A_P/b)}m,Q\right\rbrace
\le\min\lbrace Q^2/m+Q/b,Q\rbrace
\le\psi_m(Q)+Q/b.
\tag{10}
$$
For the second inequality, if \(Q^2/m\le Q\), the left member is at most \(Q^2/m+Q/b\); otherwise it is at most \(Q\le Q+Q/b\). Both \(\psi_m(x)\) and \(x/b\) are increasing on \([0,\infty)\), so (5), (10), and Cauchy-Schwarz yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\rm sel}\exp\!\left[64\min\left\lbrace\frac{Q(Q+A_P/b)}m,Q\right\rbrace\right]
&\le\mathbb E\exp\!\left[64\psi_m(R)+64R/b\right]\\
&\le\left(\mathbb E e^{128\psi_m(R)}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\mathbb E e^{128R/b}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le\sqrt{\frac e{e-1}}\,
\exp\!\left(64e^{260}a^2+a(e^{64}-1)\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
All variables here have finite support, so Cauchy-Schwarz involves finite moments. In its application of (8)-(9), the choice is \(c=128\).

There is no division by \(\mu\) in the zero-mean case; then (5) forces \(Q=0\) almost surely and the claimed expectation equals 1. The case \(a=0\) is covered by this observation whenever its hypotheses hold. For finite \(\beta\) and nonempty \(P\), in fact \(\mu>0\), so no such parameter choice has \(a=0\). If \(T=0\), the original law has only zero coordinate energies and therefore \(Q=A_P=0\), though it can retain several energy-zero core sites. For \(\beta=0\), (2) uses \(H(u)=u+1\) for \(u\ge0\); the same monotonicity proof applies. Empty \(J\), equality in either mean assumption, \(b=2\), and equality in the cost inequalities are all included. Feasibility and height endpoints throughout are weak inequalities, and no comparison in this proof requires an independent or reweighted representation of the truncated original law.
