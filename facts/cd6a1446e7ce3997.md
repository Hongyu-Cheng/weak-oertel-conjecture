## Statement

Let \(r,s,h_x,h_y\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(n=r+s\), let \(a\ge0\), and let \(B\in\mathbb Z^{s\times r}\). Use the geometric objects of accepted fact `6dd47958954d6d0a` with exactly the same dimensions and side lengths:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,h_x]^r\cup\{-\mathbf1_r\}\bigr),
&Q&=[-h_y,h_y]^s,\\
P&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((R\times\{\mathbf0\})\cup(\{\mathbf0\}\times Q)\bigr),
&D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Write an integer index as \(\mathbf I=(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb Z^s\). Let \(V:R\to\mathbb R\) be finite and convex, with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(x,y)&=V(x)+a\|y-Bx\|_\infty\quad((x,y)\in P),
&\phi_{\mathbf I}&=\phi(x,y),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
&w_{\mathbf I}&=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),
&W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Assume that the original weighted mean is \(\mathbf J_0=W^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). No transformation of \(P\), of the lattice indices, or of these weights is part of this hypothesis.

Use the original full-capacity logarithmic hitting quantity of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, a barycentric support at \(\mathbf0\) consists of a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) and positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) with \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). Its logarithmic gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}\). For \(L\ge0\), let \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)\) be the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every barycentric support with gap at most \(L\). Each selected source is charged its full original weight. That accepted fact proves that this is the same quantity as its finite definition using affinely independent supports and their unique positive coefficients. All cutoff inequalities are weak, so equality cases are included.

The symbols \(n,D,P,w,W\) here have their canonical meanings in `488f0a583886b5fc`. We normalize at the origin, with \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\); the potential called \(\phi\) in that fact is therefore \(\phi-\min_D\phi\) here, and its \(w_{\max}\) is \(e^{-\min_D\phi}\). These substitutions leave every original weight and every gap unchanged. Our integer matrix \(B\) is a different object from the scalar amplitude called \(B\) in that fact; the latter would equal \(\max_D\phi-\min_D\phi\), and is not used in this proof. The auxiliary function \(V\) has the first-block meaning of `6dd47958954d6d0a`; we do not identify the new weights with that fact's additive weights.

The exact complete support is

\[
D=\{(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}
\cup\left\{(x,y):
x\in\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r,\quad y\in\mathbb Z^s,\quad
\frac{\|x\|_\infty}{h_x}+\frac{\|y\|_\infty}{h_y}\le1
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

The table is globally extendable in the sense of `488f0a583886b5fc`, \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) has dimension \(n\), \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Put \(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}w_{(\mathbf0,y)}\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&\le2^{r+1}Z_{\mathrm{cube}},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)&\ge\frac{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}{4},\\
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)}{1+L}
&\ge\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}
\ge\frac{W}{20\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The original-stationarity hypothesis has genuinely coupled examples. Specifically, for \(r=2\), \(s=1\), every \(h_x,h_y\ge2\), every \(a>0\), and every \(b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), there is an explicit finite convex \(V\) satisfying the hypothesis for \(B=(b,-b)\), such that the resulting values \(\phi_{(x,y)}\) on \(D\) cannot be written as \(U(x)+T(y)\) for any two real-valued functions on the corresponding projections of \(D\).

For the actual-body conclusion, retain the integer dimension \(n=r+s\) and take any continuous dimension \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, write \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{z\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,z)\in C\}\), and suppose for some \(\alpha>0\) that its complete volume table satisfies

\[
w^C_{\mathbf I}:=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})=\alpha w_{\mathbf I}
\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{3}
\]

This equality includes all zero-volume integer fibers. Put \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(W_C=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{\mathbf I}w^C_{\mathbf I}=\alpha W\). There is one \(z^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf0,z^\star)\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\frac{W_C}{120\,2^{r+s}};
\qquad
h_S((\mathbf0,z^\star))\ge\frac{W_C}{120\,2^{r+s}}.
\tag{4}
\]

In applying `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, its \(C,S,\Lambda,n,d\) are exactly the actual objects just specified, its \(D\) is (1), its weights and total are \(w^C\) and \(W_C\), and its target \(\mathbf J\) is \(\mathbf0\). Its continuous fiber variable is denoted \(z\) here to distinguish it from the second integer block \(y\). In particular, the continuous dimension \(d\) is independent of both integer block dimensions \(r,s\). Its volume roots \(a_{\mathbf I}\) are \((w^C_{\mathbf I})^{1/d}\), and are distinct from the coefficient \(a\) in the potential. We write its \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) as \(m_d(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\) when making the target and original table explicit.

## Proof

The support-only census of `6dd47958954d6d0a` applies to the unchanged \(R,Q,P\), independently of any potential. We verify it directly as well. A point of \(R\) has the form \(t u-(1-t)\mathbf1_r\), with \(0\le t\le1\) and \(u\in[0,h_x]^r\). Each coordinate is at least \(t-1\) and at most \((h_x+1)t-1\). A negative integer coordinate is necessarily \(-1\), and the lower bound forces \(t=0\), so the entire point is \(-\mathbf1_r\). Every other integer point of \(R\) lies in \(\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r\), and all of that integer cube belongs to \(R\).

Every point of \(P\) has a representation \((x,y)=(\lambda u,(1-\lambda)v)\), with \(u\in R\), \(v\in Q\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Since \(\mathbf0\in R\), its first projection belongs to \(R\). If an integer first projection is \(-\mathbf1_r\), the bound \(x_i\ge-\lambda\) forces \(\lambda=1\), hence \(y=\mathbf0\). Otherwise the first projection is nonnegative; the inequalities \(x_i\le\lambda h_x\) and \(\|y\|_\infty\le(1-\lambda)h_y\) give (1). Conversely, for a nonnegative integer \(x\) satisfying (1), choose \(\lambda=\|x\|_\infty/h_x\). If \(0<\lambda<1\), then \(x/\lambda\in[0,h_x]^r\) and \(y/(1-\lambda)\in Q\), giving the required representation. The endpoint cases \(\lambda=0,1\) belong directly to the two defining blocks. This proves the complete census, including its boundary sites.

All vertices used to generate \(P\) are integer points, so \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). The origin is the strictly positive barycenter with coefficients \(1/(r+1)\) of the affinely independent first-block points \(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\). Thus \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}R\). Also \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}Q\), and \((R/2)\times(Q/2)\subseteq P\), so \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\). The origin and the coordinate unit vectors in both blocks belong to \(D\), giving affine rank \(n\).

On \(P\), the potential \(\phi(x,y)\) is convex: its first term is convex under projection, and its second term is a nonnegative multiple of a norm composed with a linear map. Its value at \((\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\) is zero. Define a function on all of \(\mathbb R^n\) by \(f(x,y)=e^{-\phi(x,y)}\) on \(P\) and \(f(x,y)=0\) outside \(P\). It is finite-valued at every point. For two endpoints in \(P\), convexity of \(P\) and of \(\phi\) proves the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). If at least one endpoint is outside \(P\), the right side of that inequality is zero for an interpolation parameter strictly between zero and one, so the inequality still holds. Equation (1) shows that this global extension agrees with every lattice value, including all zeros. Convexity also gives every barycentric convex-admissibility inequality and hence \(G_\lambda\ge0\).

We establish the new integer-translation comparison before using it. For any \(h\in\mathbb Z^s\) and any integer \(k\ge0\), let \(N_h(k)\) count the \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) with \(\|y-h\|_\infty\le k\). Coordinatewise independence gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_h(k)
&=\prod_{j=1}^s
\left|\{-h_y,\ldots,h_y\}\cap\{h_j-k,\ldots,h_j+k\}\right|\\
&\le\prod_{j=1}^s\min\{2h_y+1,2k+1\}
=(2\min\{h_y,k\}+1)^s
=N_{\mathbf0}(k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

The entries in both sets in each intersection are integers; the estimate holds whether or not the translated interval meets the original interval. At zero translation the smaller centered interval is contained in the larger, giving the last equality. This proves (5) for every integer shift, including shifts outside the cube and cutoff zero.

If \(a>0\), set \(\tau=e^{-a}\in(0,1)\). For every integer \(m\ge0\), the geometric-series identity is \(\tau^m=(1-\tau)\sum_{k=m}^\infty\tau^k\). Since each \(\|y-h\|_\infty\) is an integer, summing this identity over the finite cube and using (5) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y-h\|_\infty}
&=(1-\tau)\sum_{k=0}^\infty\tau^kN_h(k)\\
&\le(1-\tau)\sum_{k=0}^\infty\tau^kN_{\mathbf0}(k)
=Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

All terms are nonnegative and the finite cube permits the displayed interchange directly. For \(a=0\), both sides of (6) are exactly \((2h_y+1)^s\). Thus (6) holds for every \(a\ge0\) and every \(h\in\mathbb Z^s\). No assertion for noninteger translations is used.

On \(\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\), define \(\rho_x=2^{-(r+1)}2^{-(x_1+\cdots+x_r)}\), with the same auxiliary first-block normalization as in `6dd47958954d6d0a`. Its mass at \(-\mathbf1_r\) is \(1/2\), while its total mass on the nonnegative orthant is \(2^{-(r+1)}(\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j})^r=1/2\). Thus \(\rho\) is a probability. On the original finite support \(D\), define the auxiliary numbers

\[
q_{(x,y)}=\frac{\rho_x e^{-a\|y-Bx\|_\infty}}{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}.
\]

By (1), every original first projection belongs to the domain of \(\rho\), and every second projection belongs to \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\). Since \(B\) and \(x\) are integer, (6) applies with \(h=Bx\), including at the negative apex. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<\sum_{(x,y)\in D}q_{(x,y)}
&\le\sum_{x\in\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}
\rho_x\frac{\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y-Bx\|_\infty}}{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}\\
&\le\sum_x\rho_x=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

These numbers are positive on \(D\) and form a subprobability there. They are used solely in the following comparison; the source capacities stay equal to \(w\).

Let \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\), the original probability law. Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives \(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(q_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\le\log\sum_Dq_{\mathbf I}\le0\). Substituting the exact expressions for \(p,q,\rho\) cancels the entire coupled norm term and yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\log\frac{p_{(x,y)}}{q_{(x,y)}}\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
+(r+1)\log2+\log Z_{\mathrm{cube}}
+(\log2)\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
+(r+1)\log2+\log Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The last equality uses the first-block coordinates of the stipulated original mean. Jensen's inequality for the finite convex function \(V\), evaluated at the same original law, gives \(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)\ge V(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x)=V(\mathbf0)=0\). Equation (8) therefore proves \(W\le2^{r+1}Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\). There has been no reweighting in either the mean hypothesis or the hitting capacities.

The central cube is unaffected by the coupling because \(B\mathbf0=\mathbf0\). For every \(L\ge0\), consider the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) and the mutually disjoint unordered pairs \(\{(\mathbf0,y),(\mathbf0,-y)\}\) with \(y\ne\mathbf0\) and \(a\|y\|_\infty\le L\). Each pair belongs to \(D\), has positive barycentric coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), and has gap \(a\|y\|_\infty\). Its endpoints have the same original weight \(e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}\). Each pair and the singleton are affinely independent supports. Every hitting set must contain the singleton and at least one complete source from each eligible pair, so

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
\ge1+\frac12
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s,\ y\ne\mathbf0\\a\|y\|_\infty\le L}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}.
\tag{9}
\]

This lower bound applies to the all-support hitting definition, since these supports are members of that full family. It includes every pair at exact gap \(L\).

For completeness, a direct halving map controls the central tail without any parameter restriction. Define \(\eta(y)_j=\operatorname{sgn}(y_j)\lfloor |y_j|/2\rfloor\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\). Its image stays in \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), and \(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty\le\|y\|_\infty/2\). A zero image in one coordinate has at most the preimages \(-1,0,1\), and a positive or negative image has at most two preimages. Truncating the domain to the cube cannot increase these numbers, so every image vector has at most \(3^s\) preimages. Consequently

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}
\le\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|\eta(y)\|_\infty}
\le3^sZ_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\tag{10}
\]

If \(a>0\), the mass at gap strictly larger than \(4s\) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\\a\|y\|_\infty>4s}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}
&\le e^{-2s}\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}\\
&\le(3e^{-2})^sZ_{\mathrm{cube}}
<\frac12Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(e^2>1+2+2+8/6>6\), so \(3e^{-2}<1/2\), and \(s\ge1\). For \(a=0\), the tail is empty. In every case at least half of the central-cube mass has gap at most \(4s\). The right side of (9) is one half of this eligible cube mass plus \(1/2\), so it gives \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)\ge Z_{\mathrm{cube}}/4\). Combining with the proved mass bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}
&\ge\frac{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}{4(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{W}{2^{r+3}(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{W}{20\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The final inequality uses \(1+4s\le5\cdot2^{s-1}\). This is equality at \(s=1\); if it holds at \(s\), adding four on the left is bounded by doubling the right because \(5\cdot2^{s-1}\ge5>4\). This proves it for all \(s\ge1\). The cutoff maximum in (2) exists by the finite-support result in `488f0a583886b5fc` and is at least the value at \(4s\). Thus all of (2) follows.

We now construct the genuinely coupled stationary examples. Fix \(r=2\), \(s=1\), \(h_x,h_y\ge2\), \(a>0\), \(b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), and \(B=(b,-b)\), with \(D\) still exactly (1). Define the following finite constants and function:

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac1{2h_x(h_x+1)^2(2h_y+1)},\\
c_-&=\sum_{\substack{(x,y)\in D\\x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2}}
x_1u^{x_1+x_2}e^{-a|y-b(x_1-x_2)|},
&\delta&=-\log(c_-u^2),\\
V(x)&=-(\log u)(x_1+x_2)+\delta\max\{0,-x_1,-x_2\}
\quad(x\in R).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

One has \(0<u<1\). The site \((\mathbf e_1,0)\in D\) contributes \(u e^{-a|b|}>0\) to \(c_-\). In every nonzero term of that sum, \(1\le x_1\le h_x\), \(u^{x_1+x_2}\le u\), and the exponential factor is at most one. The number of sites is at most \((h_x+1)^2(2h_y+1)\), so \(0<c_-\le h_x(h_x+1)^2(2h_y+1)u=1/2\). Hence \(c_-u^2<1\) and \(\delta>0\). The maximum of the three displayed affine functions is convex, so (13) is finite and convex on all of \(\mathbb R^2\), in particular on \(R\); also \(V(\mathbf0)=0\).

For every nonnegative projected lattice site, the original weight from this \(V\) is exactly \(u^{x_1+x_2}e^{-a|y-b(x_1-x_2)|}\). At the unique negative projected site, \(B(-1,-1)=0\), and
\(V(-1,-1)=2\log u+\delta=-\log c_-\). Its original weight is therefore exactly \(c_-\).

The involution \((x_1,x_2,y)\mapsto(x_2,x_1,-y)\) preserves the nonnegative part of (1), and preserves its original weights because the expression inside the absolute value changes sign. It exchanges the first two coordinates and negates the last coordinate. Thus the original first moment of the nonnegative part equals \(c_-\) in each first-block coordinate, and its last-coordinate first moment is zero. The negative apex contributes \((-c_-,-c_-,0)\), so the total original moment is exactly \((0,0,0)\). Dividing by the positive total mass proves the required original mean without changing the table.

To verify actual coupling on the lattice support, let \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}(b)\in\{-1,1\}\). The four sites \((\mathbf0,0)\), \((\mathbf0,\epsilon)\), \((\mathbf e_1,0)\), and \((\mathbf e_1,\epsilon)\) belong to \(D\); for the last site, \(1/h_x+1/h_y\le1\). Since \(|\epsilon-b|=|b|-1\), their exact potential differences are

\[
\phi_{(\mathbf e_1,\epsilon)}-\phi_{(\mathbf e_1,0)}=-a,
\qquad
\phi_{(\mathbf0,\epsilon)}-\phi_{(\mathbf0,0)}=a.
\tag{14}
\]

They differ because \(a>0\). An additive representation \(U(x)+T(y)\) would make these two differences both equal to \(T(\epsilon)-T(0)\), a contradiction. This proves the stated nonvacuity with genuine coupling and the original support unchanged.

Finally let \(C\) satisfy (3), with the actual continuous dimension \(d\). Its positive-volume support is exactly \(D\), its total volume is \(W_C=\alpha W\), and its original normalized source law is \(w^C/W_C=w/W\). Thus its original mean is \(\mathbf0\), and its target fiber has positive volume \(w^C_{\mathbf0}=\alpha\).

The normalized potential of `488f0a583886b5fc` for this actual table is
\(\log(w^C_{\max}/w^C_{\mathbf I})=\log(\alpha w_{\max}/(\alpha w_{\mathbf I}))=\phi_{\mathbf I}-\min_D\phi\). Its logarithmic eligible supports are therefore identical to those of \(w\). Every hitting set has its total capacity multiplied by \(\alpha\), which proves, for every cutoff,

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)=\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w).
\tag{15}
\]

For an affinely independent support \(E\) at \(\mathbf0\) with its unique positive coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\), the original root cost from `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_E^C
&=\log\frac{w^C_{\mathbf0}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}(w^C_{\mathbf I})^{1/d}\right)^d}\\
&=\log\frac{1}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}/d}\right)^d}
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
=G_E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The common scale cancels exactly. The inequality is weighted arithmetic-geometric mean, or equivalently convexity of the exponential. The root cost is nonnegative by the actual-body conclusion on costs in `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. Thus every logarithmic eligible independent support is root eligible at the same cutoff, with exactly the same original capacities. This rederives the pointwise comparison in `488f0a583886b5fc` with the required actual dimension and normalization:
\(m_d(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\). The direction follows because hitting the larger root family requires at least as much weight. It holds also for tied costs and cutoff zero.

Apply the original-fiber bound (4) of `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` at target \(\mathbf0\) and cutoff \(4s\), with the exact object map in the statement. Equations (12), (15), and (16) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{z\in C_{\mathbf0}}h_S((\mathbf0,z))
&\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf0,4s;w^C)}{6(1+4s)}\\
&\ge\frac{\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{6(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{\alpha W}{120\,2^{r+s}}
=\frac{W_C}{120\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

That accepted fact proves attainment of this original-fiber depth maximum. Choose an attaining \(z^\star\) before the ambient halfspace is specified. Its closed-halfspace conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and every equality atom, giving (4) with the locked definition of \(h_S\).
