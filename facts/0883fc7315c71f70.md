## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $d\ge1$, and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty. Let $(K_z)_{z\in P}$ be full-dimensional compact convex fibers satisfying all anchored Minkowski inclusions. Write $Q=\operatorname{conv}P$, $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, and $\eta=\sum_z\mathcal H^{d-1}|_{\{z\}\times\partial K_z}$. Use the relative physical space $\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d$. A finite contact action is specified by finitely many functions $\ell_i(u,z)$ continuous in $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ and affine in $z$ with $\ell_i(u,z)\ge h_{K_z}(u)$ on $P$, and jointly continuous nonnegative fields $V_i(u,z,x)$ convex in $(z,x)$ for each $u$ on a common open convex neighborhood of $C$. It cuts $C$ by $u\cdot x+\varepsilon V_i(u,z,x)\le\ell_i(u,z)$ for all $i,u$. Let $v$ denote its physical inward speed at regular old measured boundary points.

For $L\ge0$, set $g_i(u,z,x)=\ell_i(u,z)-u\cdot x$ and $\Phi_L(z,x)=\max_{i,u}[V_i(u,z,x)-Lg_i(u,z,x)]_+$. An empty index set gives the zero action and $\Phi_L=0$. Each $\Phi_L$ extends to a globally finite continuous nonnegative convex function. On $C$ these potentials are uniformly bounded and decrease as $L$ increases. At regular old measured boundary points they decrease to $v$, and $\|\Phi_L-v\|_{L^1(\eta)}\to0$. In particular every bounded measurable boundary response converges, uniformly over any family of tests with a common supremum bound. For $d=1$, the boundary measure counts the two endpoints of each interval.

Write $h(z,u)=h_{C_z}(u)$ for the support of the actual real section over $z\in Q$. For any fixed $L$, impose $u\cdot x+\varepsilon\Phi_L(z,x)\le h(z,u)$ for every $u$ inside $C$. This gives an actual nested compact convex deformation with projection $Q$ and the same positive integer bases for small $\varepsilon\ge0$. Its measured inward speed is $\Phi_L$. Its first total and fixed closed-cap losses are respectively $\int\Phi_L\,d\eta$ and $\int\kappa_H\Phi_L\,d\eta$, where $\kappa_H$ equals one on a wholly captured fiber, zero when the cut misses the fiber interior, and the open cap indicator on a proper cut. Every strict finite list of fixed signed cap responses transfers by choosing one finite $L$ first and then sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$ for that deformation.

Conversely, for any one globally finite continuous nonnegative convex potential $\Phi(z,x)$, a finite contact action realizes the old-section roof erosion by $\Phi$ on every measured fiber, including equality of its deformed fibers for each $\varepsilon\ge0$. Thus the two speed classes have the same $L^1(\eta)$ closure. Their response vectors for any finite list of bounded measurable tests have the same closure and the same polar inequalities. The converse concerns the measured integer fibers. The approximation concerns strict inequalities and closure.

## Proof

Every point of $C$ is a convex combination of the generating fibers, so the anchored inclusions give $C_z=K_z$ on $P$. Each $g_i$ is affine in the physical variables and nonnegative on every generating fiber, hence nonnegative throughout $C$. The index set is compact. Taking its maximum of jointly continuous functions gives a finite continuous function on the common neighborhood, and the positive part and the maximum preserve convexity. Let $B=\max_{i,u,(z,x)\in C}V_i(u,z,x)$. The whole-body gap inequality gives $0\le\Phi_{L'}\le\Phi_L\le B$ on $C$ for $L'\ge L$.

For each fixed $L$, choose a compact convex thickening of $C$ inside its domain. A finite convex function on an open convex set is locally Lipschitz and has subgradients there. On the thickening the subgradients are uniformly bounded, by a finite local cover or the common positive distance to the boundary of the neighborhood. The supremum of the supporting affine functions based on the thickening, together with zero, is therefore globally finite, nonnegative and convex, and agrees with $\Phi_L$ on the thickening. Extend constantly in the orthogonal complement if the relative base is proper. This extension is made separately for each $L$ and preserves all assertions on $C$.

At a regular old boundary point $(z,x)$ let $\nu$ be its unique outward normal. The radial loss lemma 84cd8a85417b0d7a applies to the original action, so $v(z,x)$ is the maximum of zero and the values $V_i(\nu,z,x)$ for which $\ell_i(\nu,z)=h_{K_z}(\nu)$. Indeed $g_i=0$ exactly when both nonnegative quantities $\ell_i-h_{K_z}$ and $h_{K_z}-u\cdot x$ vanish, which here forces $u=\nu$. The zero-gap values are unchanged by the penalty and give $\Phi_L\ge v$. If the decreasing limit is positive, take maximizing pairs along $L\to\infty$. Their gaps are at most $B/L$. Along a subsequence the index is constant and the normal converges, so the limiting pair has zero gap. The values have upper limit at most the limiting field value, hence at most $v$. When the decreasing limit is zero the lower bound already gives equality. Almost-everywhere regularity and finiteness of $\eta$ give the $L^1$ limit by dominated convergence. For any test $w$, the response error is at most $\|w\|_\infty\|\Phi_L-v\|_1$.

Grouping convex representations by base gives
$$
h(z,u)=\max\left\{\sum_{p\in P}\alpha_ph_{K_p}(u):\alpha_p\ge0,\ \sum_p\alpha_p=1,\ \sum_p\alpha_pp=z\right\}.
$$
The upper bound follows by applying the support inequality to each generating point, and equality follows by choosing support maximizers for an optimizing weight vector. Mixing weight vectors proves concavity in $z$. For each fixed $u$, compactness of $C$ and a convergent subsequence of support maximizers give upper semicontinuity in $z$. Consequently each old-roof inequality defines a closed convex set within $C$. The bodies are nested by nonnegativity. If $K_z$ contains a ball of radius $r_z>0$ about $o_z$, the homothet $o_z+(1-\varepsilon B/r_z)(K_z-o_z)$ stays in the eroded section at $z$ for small $\varepsilon$, since its support is at most $h_{K_z}(u)-\varepsilon B$. Convexity and preservation of every site of $P$ then give projection $Q$. Lattice completeness excludes all other integer sites.

For the measured radial formula, apply 155691fc3768c06f at each $p\in P$ using the given anchored inclusions. This gives a continuous touching affine majorant $\ell_p(u,z)$ for every $p$. For $z\in P$, $\min_p\ell_p(u,z)=h_{K_z}(u)=h(z,u)$, because each is a majorant and $p=z$ attains equality. Placing the same field $\Phi$ on every majorant therefore gives exactly the same integer fibers as the roof construction, for all $\varepsilon\ge0$. This proves the converse. For $\Phi=\Phi_L$, 84cd8a85417b0d7a now supplies the homothet and radial hypotheses, the speed $\Phi_L$ at all regular measured boundary points, and the stated fixed closed-cap expansions with the full inward selectors. The whole captured case includes inside-supporting and pure-base boundary caps. The excluded-interior case includes outside-supporting traces. No equality of the two ambient bodies between sampled bases is needed.

For a finite list of fixed caps, each desired first signed response $\int(\kappa_{H_j}-\beta_j)\Phi_L\,d\eta$ tends to the corresponding response for $v$. If all target rows are positive, choose a finite $L$ at which every row is positive. For this one actual deformation the fixed-cap and total-loss remainders are $o_L(\varepsilon)$, uniformly over the fixed list. Thus the physical loss differences are positive for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. The same $L^1$ estimate transfers a positive uniform first-response margin over uniformly bounded boundary tests. The roof speeds belong to the finite action speed class, and every finite action speed is a limit of roof speeds. Continuous finite linear response maps preserve the resulting two inclusions of closures, and continuous linear inequalities have the same validity on either response class.
