## Statement

Let a planar convex body have exactly five nonempty integer fibers, all positive intervals, and suppose its true mixed length centroid has integer base. That base is the middle index. Translate it to zero. Scale continuous lengths to make the common first absolute moment on each strict side equal to one. The resulting widths at $-2,-1,0,1,2$ are exactly the arrays $(a,1-2a,c,1-2b,b)$ satisfying $0<a,b\le1/3$ and $1-a-b\le c\le\min(2-5a,2-5b)$. After a shear and reflection taking any through-centroid cap of nonzero continuous normal to $x\ge0$ and the centroid to $(0,0)$, the middle endpoints satisfy $r_0\ge Q/(2M)>0$ and $l_0\le-Q/(2M)<0$, where $Q$ is the sum of squared widths and $M$ the total length.

## Proof

Convexity makes the five bases consecutive, the lower endpoints convex, the upper endpoints concave and the widths concave. The positive weights exclude the extreme sites as possible mean. If the mean is the second index from the left, translate to sites $-1,0,1,2,3$ with mean zero, and scale lengths so that $w_1=1$. Write $w_2=t>0,w_3=u>0,w_0=s$. Balance forces $w_{-1}=1+2t+3u$. Concavity at $0,1,2$ gives $s\ge1+t+3u/2$, $s\le2-t$ and $t\ge(1+u)/2$. The first two give $t\le1/2-3u/4$, contradicting the third. Base reflection excludes the second index from the right.

At the middle, balance gives $w_{-1}+2w_{-2}=w_1+2w_2>0$. Scaling by the reciprocal gives the claimed form. The three second-difference inequalities are exactly $c\ge1-a-b$, $c\le2-5a$ and $c\le2-5b$. Comparing them gives $4a-b\le1$ and $4b-a\le1$, hence $3\max(a,b)\le1$. Conversely the listed parameters give positive concave widths. Piecewise linear interpolation of their positive half widths gives a concave upper roof and, by negation, a convex lower roof. The resulting centered body realizes these widths exactly, with base mean zero.

A containing cap with nonzero continuous normal can be expressed, after the indicated length-preserving affine change of coordinates, as $x\ge0$. The true zero height moment gives $\sum_i w_i r_i=Q/2$ and $\sum_i w_i l_i=-Q/2$. Use Jensen for the concave and convex piecewise linear endpoint roofs with weights $w_i/M$ of mean base zero. This gives the asserted middle endpoint bounds.
