## Statement

For a bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(m>0\), set
\[
\mathsf M_m(K):=\left\{\frac1m\int_{\mathbb R^d}y f(y)\,dy:f\ \text{measurable},\ 0\le f\le\mathbf 1_K\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_{\mathbb R^d}f=m\right\}.
\]
Let \(r,d\ge1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let \(C_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\). Then
\[
\mathfrak M(C):=\overline{\{(z,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_z)\}}
\]
is convex.

## Proof

Write \(G:=\{(z,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_z)\}\). Take \((z_i,x_i,s_i)\in G\), \(i=0,1\), and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\). By the definition of the metronoid, choose measurable \(f_i\) with \(0\le f_i\le\mathbf1_{C_{z_i}}\), \(\int f_i=s_i^d\), and \(s_i^{-d}\int y f_i(y)\,dy=x_i\). Thus \(\rho_i:=s_i^{-d}f_i\) is a compactly supported probability density, \(\|\rho_i\|_\infty\le s_i^{-d}\), and \(\int y\rho_i(y)\,dy=x_i\).

Let \(\mu_i:=\rho_i\,dy\). McCann's displacement interpolation gives a convex \(\phi\) with \((\nabla\phi)_\#\mu_0=\mu_1\) and
\[
\mu_\lambda:=\bigl((1-\lambda)\operatorname{id}+\lambda\nabla\phi\bigr)_\#\mu_0=\rho_\lambda\,dy.
\]
His Theorem 2.3 with \(q=\infty\) yields
\[
\|\rho_\lambda\|_\infty^{-1/d}\ge(1-\lambda)\|\rho_0\|_\infty^{-1/d}+\lambda\|\rho_1\|_\infty^{-1/d}\ge(1-\lambda)s_0+\lambda s_1=:s_\lambda.
\]

For \(\mu_0\)-almost every \(y\), one has \(y\in C_{z_0}\) and \(\nabla\phi(y)\in C_{z_1}\). Hence the defining pushforward is carried by \((1-\lambda)C_{z_0}+\lambda C_{z_1}\subseteq C_{z_\lambda}\), where \(z_\lambda:=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1\); the inclusion follows directly from convexity of \(C\). Compact support makes all first moments finite, and the same pushforward identity gives
\[
\int y\rho_\lambda(y)\,dy=(1-\lambda)\int y\rho_0(y)\,dy+\lambda\int y\rho_1(y)\,dy=(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1=:x_\lambda.
\]

Now \(f_\lambda:=s_\lambda^d\rho_\lambda\) satisfies \(0\le f_\lambda\le\mathbf1_{C_{z_\lambda}}\) almost everywhere, \(\int f_\lambda=s_\lambda^d\), and \(s_\lambda^{-d}\int y f_\lambda(y)\,dy=x_\lambda\). Therefore \((z_\lambda,x_\lambda,s_\lambda)\in G\). Thus \(G\) is convex, and its closure \(\mathfrak M(C)=\overline G\) is convex. This closure argument includes every zero-mass boundary point without requiring a separate characterization.

## External sources

Robert J. McCann, “A Convexity Principle for Interacting Gases,” Advances in Mathematics 128 (1997), 153-179, DOI https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1997.1634, author-hosted primary-source PDF https://www.math.toronto.edu/~mccann/papers/advances.pdf. Definition 1.1 and equation (7) state that for \(\mu_0,\mu_1\in\mathcal P_{ac}(\mathbb R^d)\), there is a convex \(\phi\) with \((\nabla\phi)_\#\mu_0=\mu_1\), and the displacement interpolant is \(\mu_\lambda=[(1-\lambda)\operatorname{id}+\lambda\nabla\phi]_\#\mu_0\). The \(q=\infty\) case of Theorem 2.3 states exactly \(\|\rho_\lambda\|_\infty^{-1/d}\ge(1-\lambda)\|\rho_0\|_\infty^{-1/d}+\lambda\|\rho_1\|_\infty^{-1/d}\). Here each \(\mu_i=\rho_i\,dy\) is an absolutely continuous probability measure; each \(\rho_i\) is merely measurable but belongs to \(L^\infty\); no smoothness or strict positivity is assumed by the cited statement. The fibers are compact, so the endpoint measures have compact support and finite first moments. Equation (7), together with \((\nabla\phi)_\#\mu_0=\mu_1\), gives both the asserted support containment and the barycenter computation.
