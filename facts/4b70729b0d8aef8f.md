## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, put \(N=k+1\) and \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), and suppose
$$
B_k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad \#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le2\quad(x\in D).
$$
For every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), define the original affine law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i&=\exp(-\beta_i),&w_x&=\exp(-\beta\cdot x)=\prod_{i=1}^k r_i^{x_i},\\
Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,& A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,
&p_{(x,0)}&=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) only as an abbreviation. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{K\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in K}}
\sum_{v\in A\cap K}p_v,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus \(s_p\) counts ordered reflected pairs, the diagonal once and each unordered distinct pair twice. Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; additionally allowing the whole space does not change the depths. These objects and the original normalization agree exactly with accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7. Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-256}8^{-N},\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-256}8^{-N}.
$$
In fact the proof gives the larger coefficient \(2^{-222}\). No star, activity, incidence, size, anchor, product-fiber, or additional one-step-containment hypothesis is imposed.

## Proof

We use only the final one-step assertion of accepted fact f27ee487069e27e7: for a finite nonempty complete \(S\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^d\) with at most one two per point, its normalized affine law on \(S\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{d+1}\), with \(q\) actually occupied parity classes, has both cubics at least \(1/(512q^3)\). It does not require Boolean containment and covers every affine rank. Every use below specifies this support, field, normalization, and parity count. All other estimates are proved here.

1. Reflection and restriction comparisons.

For any finite positive law on an extra-zero embedding and any center \(c\), distinct unordered non-diagonal reflected pairs have disjoint endpoints, none equal to \(c\): the partner of \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\). Every containing closed halfspace contains at least one endpoint of each such pair. If both were outside a defining closed inequality, their midpoint would be strictly outside. With \(R_p(c)=s_p(c)-p_c\), this proves
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\tfrac12R_p(c)
=\tfrac12(p_c+s_p(c))\ge\tfrac12s_p(c).
\tag{1}
$$
Consequently \(M_3(p)\ge T(p)/8\), and both cubics are at least \(\sum_xp_x^4\). The latter follows from the center atom in every containing halfspace and the diagonal in the ordered reflection sum. These arguments include halfspaces with boundary through the center and have no rank requirement.

More generally, let \(V\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be finite and nonempty, let \(\Phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k\) be an affine injection with \(\Phi(V)\subseteq D\), and suppose a normalized law \(\nu\) on \(V\) satisfies
$$
p_{\Phi(u)}=\lambda\nu_u\quad(u\in V),
\qquad \lambda=p(\Phi(V)).
$$
The conditional cubics are defined on \(V\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{d+1}\). Pull back an original containing halfspace under \((u,v)\mapsto(\Phi(u),v)\). A nonzero restricted normal gives a containing conditional closed halfspace. A zero restricted normal gives the whole conditional space, since the center is contained. Its conditional mass is one, at least the conditional depth. Also, the affine injection sends ordered reflected pairs injectively to original ordered reflected pairs, preserving their midpoint. Therefore, for every \(c\in V\),
$$
\tau_p((\Phi(c),0))\ge\lambda\tau_\nu((c,0)),
\qquad s_p(\Phi(c))\ge\lambda s_\nu(c).
$$
Multiplying the cubes by \(p_{\Phi(c)}=\lambda\nu_c\) and summing proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c\in V}p_{\Phi(c)}\tau_p((\Phi(c),0))^3&\ge\lambda^4M_3(\nu),\\
\sum_{c\in V}p_{\Phi(c)}s_p(\Phi(c))^3&\ge\lambda^4T(\nu).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
For disjoint image sets these lower contributions can be summed. The factor \(\lambda^4\) consists of one center factor and three depth or reflection factors. This also covers \(d=0\), when \(V\) is a singleton and both conditional cubics are one. In every extra-zero ambient space the halfspace defined by the last coordinate being nonnegative has nonzero normal and contains the whole support. Thus adjoining the whole space to admissible halfspaces changes none of these infima.

2. A partition surplus for arbitrary complete one-step sections.

Let \(d\ge0\), let \(S\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^d\) be nonempty and complete, and suppose every point of \(S\) has at most one two. Let \(a_j\in[1/16,16]\). Define
$$
Z_S=\sum_{u\in S}a^u,\qquad B_0=\prod_{j=1}^d(1+a_j),
\qquad \zeta_S=Z_S/B_0,\qquad \nu_{(u,0)}=a^u/Z_S.
$$
Here \(a^u=\prod_ja_j^{u_j}\), and \(\nu\)'s cubics use exactly the stated halfspace and ordered-reflection definitions on \(S\times\lbrace0\rbrace\). We prove
$$
M_3(\nu),T(\nu)\ge2^{-96}\max\lbrace1,\zeta_S^2\rbrace\,8^{-(d+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$
For \(d=0\), \(S\) consists of the empty vector, \(Z_S=B_0=1\), and both cubics are one. Suppose \(d\ge1\). If \(\zeta_S\le514\), apply the one-step assertion of f27ee487069e27e7 with dimension \(d\), support \(S\), field \((-\log a_j)_j\), normalization \(Z_S\), probabilities \(\nu\), and actual parity count \(q\le2^d\). It gives both cubics at least
$$
\frac1{512\,8^d}=2^{-6}8^{-(d+1)}.
$$
Since \(\max\lbrace1,\zeta_S^2\rbrace\le514^2<2^{20}\), this is stronger than (3).

Suppose now \(\zeta_S\ge514\). For each \(b\in B_d\), put \(v_b=a^b\), and let \(n_b\) be the number of actual one-two points of parity \(b\) in \(S\). Each is uniquely \(b+2e_h\) for an index with \(b_h=0\), and its weight is \(v_ba_h^2\le16^2v_b\). The total weight of actual Boolean points is at most \(B_0=\sum_bv_b\). Hence
$$
\sum_b(n_b-1)_+v_b
\ge\sum_bn_bv_b-B_0
\ge\frac{Z_S-B_0}{16^2}-B_0
\ge\frac{Z_S}{2\cdot16^2}.
\tag{4}
$$
The last inequality is exactly \(Z_S\ge514B_0\). No assertion that a Boolean point \(b\) lies in \(S\) was made in this enumeration.

For distinct actual endpoints \(b+2e_h,b+2e_\ell\) in one such class, completeness puts their integral Boolean midpoint \(b+e_h+e_\ell\) in \(S\). Let \(L_S\) be the sum, over all these ordered distinct endpoint pairs, of
$$
\min(\nu_x,\nu_y)^{7/6}\max(\nu_x,\nu_y)^{1/6}.
$$
Both factors \(a_h^2,a_\ell^2\) belong to \([16^{-2},16^2]\), so
$$
L_S\ge16^{-8/3}Z_S^{-4/3}\sum_bn_b(n_b-1)v_b^{4/3}.
$$
Write \(V_0=\sum_{b\in B_d}v_b^{2/3}=\prod_j(1+a_j^{2/3})\). Since \(n(n-1)\ge(n-1)_+^2\), Cauchy's inequality and (4) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_bn_b(n_b-1)v_b^{4/3}
&\ge\frac{(\sum_b(n_b-1)_+v_b)^2}{V_0}
\ge\frac{Z_S^2}{4\cdot16^4V_0},\\
L_S&\ge\frac{16^{-20/3}}4\,\frac{Z_S^{2/3}}{V_0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
For each selected midpoint \(c\), affine weights give \(\nu_c^2=\nu_x\nu_y\), and the selected summand equals \(\nu_c^{1/3}\min(\nu_x,\nu_y)\). Let \(R_\nu(c)\) be the full non-diagonal ordered reflection sum. Grouping just the selected pairs, and then applying Hölder on at most \(2^d\) actual Boolean centers, yields
$$
L_S\le\sum_{c\in S\cap B_d}\nu_c^{1/3}R_\nu(c),
\qquad
L_S^3\le4^d\sum_{c\in S\cap B_d}\nu_cR_\nu(c)^3.
$$
Missing Boolean centers can equivalently be assigned zero contribution. By (1) and by \(s_\nu(c)\ge R_\nu(c)\), both cubics are at least \(L_S^3/(8\cdot4^d)\). Hölder on two terms gives
$$
1+a_j^{2/3}\le2^{1/3}(1+a_j)^{2/3},
\qquad V_0^3\le2^dB_0^2.
$$
Combining this with (5) proves the stronger large-partition bound
$$
M_3(\nu),T(\nu)
\ge\frac{16^{-20}}{512}\,\frac{Z_S^2}{4^dV_0^3}
\ge\frac{16^{-20}}{64}\,\zeta_S^2 8^{-(d+1)}
=2^{-86}\zeta_S^2 8^{-(d+1)}.
$$
This proves (3) for arbitrary missing Boolean fibers and every affine rank.

3. A density cap on a block consisting entirely of bad coordinates.

Let \(m\ge1\), and let each \(\rho_i\) lie in \((0,1/16)\cup(16,\infty)\). Define the full one-step label family, its weights, and their Boolean normalization by
$$
\mathcal Q=\lbrace(b,i):b\in B_m,\ b_i=0,\ 1\le i\le m\rbrace,
\quad c_{(b,i)}=\rho^{b+2e_i},
\quad B_y=\prod_i(1+\rho_i),
\quad g_q=c_q/B_y.
$$
For every \(\epsilon>0\), we prove
$$
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\min(g_q,\epsilon2^{-m})
\le400\epsilon^{3/4}+\epsilon.
\tag{6}
$$
This full finite family will only upper-bound sums over actual labels; no support containment is implicit.

Call a binary bit wrong if it is zero at a high coordinate \(\rho_i>16\), or one at a low coordinate \(\rho_i<1/16\). A wrong binary factor \(\rho_i^{b_i}/(1+\rho_i)\) is at most \(1/16\), and a preferred binary factor is at most one. Order the high indices \(i_1,\ldots,i_u\) so that their activities decrease. A high two at \(i_j\) is unmatched when all earlier high bits are one. Exactly \(2^{m-j}\) labels have this two-index and these prescribed earlier bits. Their total capped contribution is at most
$$
\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^u2^{-j}\le\epsilon.
\tag{7}
$$
If there are no high coordinates this is an empty sum.

Every remaining label has either a low two, or a high two with an earlier high zero. Let \(w\) be its number of wrong bits among the other \(m-1\) coordinates. A low two contributes at most \(16^{-2}\), so \(g_q\le16^{-2-w}\le16^{1-w}\). For a matched high two at \(i_j\), choose an earlier high zero at \(s\). Since \(\rho_s\ge\rho_{i_j}\),
$$
\frac{\rho_{i_j}^2}{1+\rho_{i_j}}\frac1{1+\rho_s}
\le\frac{\rho_{i_j}}{\rho_s}\le1.
$$
The remaining \(w-1\) wrong bits cost at most \(16^{-(w-1)}\). Thus again \(g_q\le16^{1-w}\). Use \(\min(g,t)\le g^{1/4}t^{3/4}\) on these labels and enlarge the enumeration of wrong-bit subsets after imposing this bound. Their sum is at most
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\epsilon^{3/4}2^{-3m/4}m(3/2)^{m-1}
&=2\cdot2^{-3/4}\epsilon^{3/4}m
\left(\frac{3/2}{2^{3/4}}\right)^{m-1}\\
&<400\epsilon^{3/4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed \(((3/2)/2^{3/4})^4=81/128<(9/10)^4\), and
\(m(9/10)^{m-1}\le\sum_{j\ge1}j(9/10)^{j-1}=100\), while \(2\cdot2^{-3/4}<2\). Counting extra subsets only increases the upper bound; the pointwise estimate was required only on the remaining labels themselves. Together with (7), this proves (6) for every positive \(\epsilon\).

An integrated consequence will handle arbitrary section partition ratios. For \(v>0\), let \(N_g(v)=\#\lbrace q\in\mathcal Q:g_q\ge v\rbrace\). Applying (6) with \(\epsilon=2^mv\) gives
$$
N_g(v)\le400\,2^{3m/4}v^{-1/4}+2^m.
\tag{8}
$$
For every \(u>0\), the finite layer-cake identity, followed by (8), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{q:g_q\le u}g_q^{1/3}
&=\frac13\int_0^u v^{-2/3}
\#\lbrace q:v\le g_q\le u\rbrace\,dv\\
&\le\frac13\int_0^u v^{-2/3}N_g(v)\,dv\\
&\le1600\,2^{3m/4}u^{1/12}+2^m u^{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The first integrand has exponent \(-11/12\), so its integral at zero is finite. This argument places no upper restriction on \(u\).

4. The cross-only case, using actual sections.

For an arbitrary field on a support as in the statement, partition the indices into
$$
G=\lbrace i:1/16\le r_i\le16\rbrace,
\qquad F=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\setminus G.
\tag{10}
$$
Suppose for the moment that every point with two twos has one two-index in each block. Let
$$
D_1=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace,
\qquad \alpha=p(D_1).
$$
This actual core is complete. An integer point of its convex hull belongs to \(D\). If it had two coordinates equal to two, extremality of two in \([0,2]\) would force every positive-coefficient generator to have both coordinates equal to two, contrary to membership in \(D_1\). It therefore belongs to \(D_1\). Its occupied parity count is \(2^k\), because it contains \(B_k\).

The conditional law \(\widehat p_x=w_x/(\alpha Z)=p_x/\alpha\) on \(D_1\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) has the same field \(\beta\). Apply the one-step assertion of f27ee487069e27e7 with dimension \(k\), support \(D_1\), normalization \(\alpha Z\), this conditional law, and parity count \(2^k\). By (2), with the identity injection,
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\,8^k}.
\tag{11}
$$
If \(\alpha\ge1/4\), both are at least \(2^{-14}8^{-N}\).

Otherwise the actual double points have mass greater than \(3/4\). In particular \(m=|F|\ge1\) and \(d=|G|\ge1\). Relabel coordinates into these blocks, with activities \(\rho_i\) on \(F\) and \(a_j\) on \(G\). This simultaneous coordinate permutation of support and field preserves both cubics, the extra-zero embedding, and every normalization. Put
$$
B_y=\prod_{i\in F}(1+\rho_i),\qquad B_0=\prod_{j\in G}(1+a_j),
\qquad z_B=B_yB_0/Z\in(0,1].
$$
The last inequality uses the full Boolean cube of the original support.

Use the full bad-block family \(\mathcal Q\) in part 3. For each label \(q=(b,i)\) whose bad vector \(y_q=b+2e_i\) actually occurs in \(D\), define its actual good-coordinate section
$$
S_q=\lbrace t:(y_q,t)\in D\rbrace,
\quad Z_q=\sum_{t\in S_q}a^t,
\quad \zeta_q=Z_q/B_0,
\quad \lambda_q=p(\lbrace(y_q,t):t\in S_q\rbrace).
$$
Only nonempty sections are used. Each \(S_q\) is complete: an integral point of its convex hull lifts to an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) with bad coordinates \(y_q\), and hence belongs to the same section by completeness of \(D\). It has at most one two because the fixed bad vector has one. Its Boolean points may be arbitrary or absent. Its conditional law is
$$
\nu^q_{(t,0)}=a^t/Z_q,
\qquad p_{(y_q,t)}=\lambda_q\nu^q_t,
\qquad \lambda_q=c_qZ_q/Z=z_Bg_q\zeta_q.
\tag{12}
$$
Here the injection reinserts the fixed bad coordinates and the extra zero coordinate. These actual sections have disjoint images, and together contain every double point. Thus
$$
\sum_q\lambda_q>3/4.
\tag{13}
$$
Apply (3) to each actual section with dimension \(d\), activities \(a\), normalization \(Z_q\), and conditional law \(\nu^q\), then apply (2). It follows that both original cubics are at least
$$
2^{-96}8^{-(d+1)}
\sum_q\lambda_q^4\max\lbrace1,\zeta_q^2\rbrace.
\tag{14}
$$

We bound the sum in (14) using original, unrenormalized section masses. Set
$$
\delta=2^{-40},\qquad t_0=\delta2^{-m},
\qquad \xi_q=\lambda_q\max\lbrace1,\sqrt{\zeta_q}\rbrace.
$$
For labels with \(\xi_q\le t_0\) and \(\zeta_q\le1\), equation (12) gives
\(\lambda_q\le\min(z_Bg_q,t_0)\). Their total mass is, by (6) with \(\epsilon=\delta/z_B\), at most
$$
z_B\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}\min(g_q,t_0/z_B)
\le400\delta^{3/4}z_B^{1/4}+\delta.
\tag{15}
$$
For labels with \(\xi_q\le t_0\) and \(\zeta_q>1\), equation (12) implies \(z_Bg_q\le t_0\) and
$$
\lambda_q=(z_Bg_q)^{1/3}\xi_q^{2/3}
\le t_0^{2/3}(z_Bg_q)^{1/3}.
$$
Enlarge this sum to all labels with \(g_q\le t_0/z_B\), and apply (9). Their total mass is at most
$$
\begin{aligned}
t_0^{2/3}z_B^{1/3}
\left(1600\,2^{3m/4}(t_0/z_B)^{1/12}
+2^m(t_0/z_B)^{1/3}\right)
=1600\delta^{3/4}z_B^{1/4}+\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
These bounds hold even when \(z_B\) is arbitrarily small: (6) and (9) hold for every positive argument. Since \(z_B\le1\), (15) and (16) give
$$
\sum_{q:\xi_q\le t_0}\lambda_q
\le2000\delta^{3/4}+2\delta
=2000\cdot2^{-30}+2^{-39}<1/32.
\tag{17}
$$
For example, \(2000<2^{11}\) bounds the first term by \(2^{-19}\), and the second term is less than \(2^{-19}\). By (13), the labels with \(\xi_q>t_0\) have total original mass greater than \(1/4\). For every label,
$$
\lambda_q^4\max\lbrace1,\zeta_q^2\rbrace\ge\lambda_q\xi_q^3:
$$
there is equality for \(\zeta_q\le1\), while \(\zeta_q^2\ge\zeta_q^{3/2}\) for \(\zeta_q\ge1\). Therefore
$$
\sum_q\lambda_q^4\max\lbrace1,\zeta_q^2\rbrace
\ge t_0^3\sum_{q:\xi_q>t_0}\lambda_q
\ge\frac{\delta^3}{4}8^{-m}=2^{-122}8^{-m}.
\tag{18}
$$
Together with (14), this proves the cross-only bound
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-218}8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{19}
$$
It also holds in the heavy-core case of (11). If either block in (10) is empty, the cross-only hypothesis gives no double points, so \(D=D_1\) and (11) applies. This covers all zero-sized-block cases without introducing artificial sections.

5. A mixed good/bad estimate on a general support.

Return to an arbitrary support and field in the statement, and keep (10). Let \(X\) be the actual double points with one two-index in each of \(G,F\). Define disjoint actual sets
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm g}&=\lbrace x\in D:\text{every two-index of }x\text{ lies in }G\rbrace,\\
W_{\mathrm b}&=\lbrace x\in D:x\text{ has at least one two and all its two-indices lie in }F\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
The max-two assumption gives the partition \(D=W_{\mathrm g}\mathbin{\dot\cup}W_{\mathrm b}\mathbin{\dot\cup}X\). Put \(\mu=1-p(X)>0\), so \(p(W_{\mathrm g})+p(W_{\mathrm b})=\mu\). We prove
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-80}\mu^6 8^{-N}.
\tag{20}
$$

For same-parity \(x,y\in W_{\mathrm g}\), write their common parity as \(b\in B_k\). The identity
$$
\log p_x=\sum_i b_i\log r_i+2\sum_{i:x_i=2}\log r_i-\log Z
$$
has its correction term in \([-16\log2,16\log2]\), since every two-index is good and there are at most two. Hence
\(\exp(-|\log p_x-\log p_y|/2)\ge2^{-16}\). Completeness puts the integral midpoint of every same-parity pair in \(D\), and the original affine law gives \(p_{(x+y)/2}^2=p_xp_y\). Thus the exact ordered-pair identity, including the diagonal, is
$$
\sum_cp_cs_p(c)
=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
p_xp_y\exp(-|\log p_x-\log p_y|/2).
$$
Retaining the pairs from \(W_{\mathrm g}\) and applying Cauchy over the \(2^k\) possible parity classes gives
$$
\sum_cp_cs_p(c)\ge2^{-16-k}p(W_{\mathrm g})^2.
$$
Jensen for the probability weights \(p_c\), and then (1), yield
$$
T(p)\ge2^{-48}p(W_{\mathrm g})^6 8^{-k},
\qquad M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-48}p(W_{\mathrm g})^6 8^{-N}.
\tag{21}
$$
In particular, \(p(W_{\mathrm g})\ge\mu/2\) proves (20), with the stronger coefficient \(2^{-54}\).

For the other case we establish actual-section consumers. Let \(D_1\) and \(\alpha\) be the actual core and its mass as in part 4, and let
$$
B=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+r_i)=\sum_{b\in B_k}w_b.
$$
The earlier completeness proof for \(D_1\) and the original-normalization consumer (11) apply to this general support too. In particular \(B/Z\le\alpha\).

For each nonempty coordinate block \(I\) and each actual pattern \(u\in\pi_I(D)\) containing a two, define
$$
F_u=\lbrace x\in D:x_I=u\rbrace,\quad
p_I(u)=p(F_u),\quad D_u=\lbrace x_{I^c}:x\in F_u\rbrace,
$$
and let \(q_u\) be the number of actually occupied parity classes in \(D_u\). When \(I\) is the whole block, \(D_u\) is the singleton empty vector and \(q_u=1\). Put
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}=
\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace}
8^k\sum_{\substack{u\in\pi_I(D)\\u\text{ contains a two}}}
\frac{p_I(u)^4}{q_u^3},
\tag{22}
$$
where an empty sum is zero.

The section \(D_u\) is nonempty, complete by lifting integral convex combinations with fixed coordinates \(u\), and has at most one two. Put \(J=I^c\), \(s=|J|\), and define its conditional affine law by
$$
Z_u=\sum_{y\in D_u}\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y),
\qquad \sigma_{(y,0)}=\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y)/Z_u.
$$
Inserting \(u\) in the original coordinate positions is an affine injection, and with \(\lambda=p_I(u)\) the exact normalization is
$$
\lambda=\exp(-\beta_I\cdot u)Z_u/Z,
\qquad p_{(u,y)}=\lambda\sigma_y.
$$
For \(s\ge1\), apply f27ee487069e27e7 with dimension \(s\), support \(D_u\), field \(\beta_J\), normalization \(Z_u\), probabilities \(\sigma\), and actual parity count \(q_u\). Both conditional cubics are at least \(1/(512q_u^3)\), regardless of missing Boolean points or affine rank. For \(s=0\), the singleton cubics equal one and give the same bound. Apply (2), sum over the disjoint sections for fixed \(I\), and maximize over \(I\). Together with (11), this proves
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge
\frac{\max\lbrace\alpha^4,P_{\mathrm{par}}\rbrace}{512\,8^k}
=\frac{\max\lbrace\alpha^4,P_{\mathrm{par}}\rbrace}{64}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{23}
$$

We next prove the purely finite mixed high/low mass estimate
$$
p(W_{\mathrm b})\le2^{14}\alpha+2^{10}P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}.
\tag{24}
$$
If \(F\) is empty its left side is zero. Otherwise let \(h=|F|\ge1\), \(g=|G|=k-h\). Split \(F\) into high and low coordinates, and order the high ones \(i_1,\ldots,i_u\) so their activities decrease. Call a bad-coordinate pattern matched if every high prefix has at most as many twos as zeros; otherwise call it unmatched. This partitions \(W_{\mathrm b}\) according to its bad-coordinate pattern. Scanning a matched pattern pairs every high two to a distinct earlier high zero. Low twos require no pairing.

For a bad pattern \(v\) with \(t\in\lbrace1,2\rbrace\) twos, let \(w\) be the number of wrong binary bits among its other coordinates, with the same high-zero/low-one convention as part 3. Define
$$
d_v=\prod_{i\in F}\frac{r_i^{v_i}}{1+r_i},
\qquad \pi_G(b)=\prod_{i\in G}\frac{r_i^{b_i}}{1+r_i}
\quad(b\in B_g).
$$
When \(g=0\), this is the unit law on the empty vector. If \(x\in W_{\mathrm b}\), its good coordinates are binary, and with \(g_x=w_x/B\) we have the exact weight factorization and original-probability bound
$$
g_x=d_{x_F}\pi_G(x_G),\qquad p_x=(B/Z)g_x\le\alpha g_x.
\tag{25}
$$
This is an identity for each actual point, not a claim that additional binary fibers are present.

For a matched pattern with \(a\) high twos and \(b\) low twos, \(a+b=t\). Each high-two/earlier-high-zero pair has product at most one by the same ordered-activity calculation as part 3. The other \(w-a\) wrong bits each cost at most \(1/16\), and each low two costs at most \(16^{-2}\). Preferred bits cost at most one. Thus
$$
d_v\le16^{a-w-2b}\le16^{t-w}.
\tag{26}
$$
Put \(\theta=2^{-k}\) and \(C=10^4\). We claim
$$
\sum_{\substack{x\in W_{\mathrm b}\\x\text{ matched}}}\min(g_x,\theta)\le C.
\tag{27}
$$
For each fixed bad pattern, enlarging its actual good assignments to all binary ones gives
$$
\sum_{b\in B_g}\min(d_v\pi_G(b),2^{-k})
\le\min(d_v,2^g2^{-k})=\min(d_v,2^{-h}).
$$
This is an upper bound, using only positivity, the number of assignments, and \(\sum_b\pi_G(b)=1\). Use (26), \(\min(v,2^{-h})\le\sqrt{v2^{-h}}\), and enlarge wrong-bit subsets after applying that bound. Write \(\varrho=5/(4\sqrt2)<9/10\). The one-two contribution is at most
$$
4h2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-1}=2\sqrt2\,h\varrho^{h-1}<300.
$$
For \(h\ge2\), the two-two contribution is at most
$$
16\binom h2 2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-2}
=4h(h-1)\varrho^{h-2}\le8000.
$$
For these constants, \(h\varrho^{h-1}\le100\) and
\(h(h-1)\varrho^{h-2}\le\sum_{j\ge2}j(j-1)(9/10)^{j-2}=2000\). The second family is empty for \(h=1\). This proves (27).

By (25), the actual matched points with \(g_x\le\theta\) have original mass at most \(C\alpha\). Those with \(g_x>\theta\) number at most \(C2^k\), by (27). The full coordinate block in (22) gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge8^k\sum_{\substack{x\in D\\x\text{ has a two}}}p_x^4.
$$
Hölder on the high-density actual matched points therefore shows
$$
p(\lbrace x\in W_{\mathrm b}:x\text{ matched}\rbrace)
\le C\alpha+(C2^k)^{3/4}(8^{-k}P_{\mathrm{par}})^{1/4}
=C\alpha+C^{3/4}P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}.
\tag{28}
$$

For each unmatched point, let \(j\) be its first high-prefix length with more twos than zeros. Its last high-prefix entry is two. Since the whole point has at most two twos, this prefix has one of exactly two forms. Either every earlier entry is one, or there is a zero at a position \(a\), a two at a position \(b\), with \(a<b<j\), and all other entries before \(j\) are one. Indeed, just before the first excess the numbers of zeros and twos agree and are either both zero or both one; in the second case the zero must precede the first two to avoid earlier excess. Thus the number of possible first-excess prefixes of length \(j\) is
$$
n_j=1+\binom{j-1}{2},
$$
with the binomial term zero for \(j=1,2\). Every such pattern \(v\) actually present defines an actual section on \(I_j=\lbrace i_1,\ldots,i_j\rbrace\), with original mass \(\lambda_v\). It is included in (22), since it contains a two, and its actual residual parity count is at most \(2^{k-j}\). Hence
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge8^j\lambda_v^4,
\qquad \lambda_v\le P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}8^{-j/4}.
$$
Every unmatched point lies in its actual first-excess section. These sections are disjoint: all completions of a specified such prefix have their first positive high-prefix excess at its specified length and pattern. Their sections may contain additional points outside \(W_{\mathrm b}\), which only increases their original masses as upper bounds. With \(a_0=8^{-1/4}<3/5\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\lbrace x\in W_{\mathrm b}:x\text{ unmatched}\rbrace)
&\le P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}\sum_{j\ge1}n_ja_0^j\\
&=P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}
\left(\frac{a_0}{1-a_0}+\frac{a_0^3}{(1-a_0)^3}\right)
<5P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
The expression at \(a_0=3/5\) is \(39/8<5\); it is increasing on \((0,1)\). With no high coordinates the unmatched family is empty and the bound holds. Since \(C^{3/4}=1000\), (28) and (29) prove (24): \(10000\le2^{14}\) and \(1005\le2^{10}\).

If \(p(W_{\mathrm g})<\mu/2\), the partition gives \(p(W_{\mathrm b})>\mu/2\). By (24),
$$
\frac\mu2\le(2^{14}+2^{10})\max\lbrace\alpha,P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}\rbrace
<2^{15}\max\lbrace\alpha,P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}\rbrace.
$$
It follows that \(\max\lbrace\alpha^4,P_{\mathrm{par}}\rbrace\ge2^{-64}\mu^4\). Substitution into (23) bounds both cubics below by \(2^{-70}\mu^4 8^{-N}\), at least \(2^{-70}\mu^6 8^{-N}\) because \(0<\mu\le1\). This and the good branch (21) prove (20), with the weaker common coefficient \(2^{-80}\).

6. Completion of the unrestricted theorem.

If \(p(X)\le1/2\), then \(\mu\ge1/2\), and (20) gives both original cubics at least \(2^{-86}8^{-N}\). Suppose \(p(X)>1/2\). Form the exact actual-subset completion
$$
D_X=\operatorname{conv}(B_k\cup X)\cap\mathbb Z^k.
$$
It is contained in \(D\), because its generating set lies in the complete support \(D\). It is finite, ternary, and satisfies the max-two condition. Since its convex hull equals \(\operatorname{conv}(B_k\cup X)\), it is complete and contains the Boolean cube.

Its double points are exactly \(X\). The inclusion of \(X\) is immediate. Conversely, let \(y\in D_X\) have coordinates \(i,j\) both equal to two. In a convex representation using generators from \(B_k\cup X\), extremality of two in \([0,2]\) forces every positive-coefficient generator to have both these coordinates two. Every such generator lies in \(X\), uses its full two-budget there, and has only binary entries elsewhere. The other coordinates of the integral point \(y\) are therefore zero or one. Extremality of either endpoint in \([0,1]\) forces every positive-coefficient generator to agree with \(y\) on every remaining coordinate. Each such generator thus equals \(y\), proving \(y\in X\). Any other points created by this completion have at most one two; no binary-fiber completion was assumed.

Let
$$
\lambda=p(D_X)\ge p(X)>1/2,
\qquad Z_X=\sum_{x\in D_X}w_x=\lambda Z,
\qquad p^X_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z_X=p_x/\lambda.
$$
This is the original affine law on \(D_X\) with the unchanged field \(\beta\), so its good/bad partition remains (10). Its double points are cross-only, and part 4 gives
$$
M_3(p^X),T(p^X)\ge2^{-218}8^{-N}.
$$
Apply (2) to the identity injection of \(D_X\) into the original support. At the same ambient centers it restricts the original containing halfspaces and full ordered reflected pairs to actual points of \(D_X\), with the exact probability factor \(\lambda\). Consequently
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\lambda^4 2^{-218}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-222}8^{-N}.
$$
This is the weaker of the two final branches and implies the asserted \(2^{-256}8^{-N}\). All conditional laws retained their original affine field on their actual complete support, all transferred masses entered to the fourth power, and all reflection sums retained the ordered-pair convention. The argument includes singleton and lower-rank sections, boundary activities \(r_i=1/16,16\) (which belong to \(G\)), empty good or bad blocks, and arbitrary missing Boolean points inside sections.
