## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(R>0\). Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body centrally symmetric about \(0_d\). Let \(E:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, even, convex, and coercive, with \(E(0)=0\). Let \(\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^d\) be continuous and even, with \(\gamma(0)=0_d\) and \(\gamma(t)\in E(t)K\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\). Assume that the actual set

\[
C=\{(t,y)\in\mathbb R^{1+d}:E(t)\le R,\ y\in\gamma(t)+(R-E(t))K\}
\]

is convex. Define \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z:E(z)<R\}\), and, for \(z\in D_+\), define \(a_z=R-E(z)\), \(C_z=\gamma(z)+a_zK\), \(w_z=a_z^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z\), and \(p_z=w_z/W\). Halfspace depth and mixed-integer volume have their locked-problem meanings. Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body, \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), and there is one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), chosen before all containing closed halfspaces, such that

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{(3/5)^{10}}8\,W.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(d\ge10\), the point \((0,0_d)\) satisfies (1). For \(1\le d\le9\), some \(m\in RK\) gives \(\mathbf y^\star=(0,m)\) with depth at least \(W/[2(d+1)]\).

For \(Z\) with exactly the original fiber-volume law \(p\), every \(u\ge0\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb P_p\!\left(\frac{E(Z)}{a_Z}\ge u\right)
\le3(1+u/2)^{-d}.
\tag{2}
\]

This is the one-base, arbitrary-shape extension of the original-volume contraction argument in accepted fact 57d7623173030446. The objects \(E,\gamma,a_z,C_z,w_z,W,p_z\) keep their energy, center, actual-radius, actual-fiber, actual-volume, total-volume, and original-volume-law meanings. The shape normalization here is \(C_z=\gamma(z)+a_zK\). When \(K=[-1,1]^d\), it gives \(w_z=(2a_z)^d\), and the normalized uniform variable used here is twice the uniform variable on \([-1/2,1/2]^d\) used in that accepted fact.

A sufficient condition for the assumed convexity is that, for every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), the function \(t\mapsto h_K(\beta)E(t)-\beta\cdot\gamma(t)\) is convex, where \(h_K(\beta)=\max_{v\in K}\beta\cdot v\). For every \(d\) and every such \(K\), the hypotheses include actual bodies whose positive-volume integer-fiber centers have affine hull of dimension \(d\).

## Proof

Because \(K\) is a centrally symmetric convex body, \(0_d\) lies in its interior, and \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)>0\). A finite convex function on \(\mathbb R\) is continuous. Thus \(E\) and \(\gamma\) are continuous. The fiber domination gives

\[
\gamma(t)+(R-E(t))K\subseteq E(t)K+(R-E(t))K=RK
\quad\text{whenever }E(t)\le R.
\tag{3}
\]

The equality follows from convexity of \(K\): for nonnegative scalars \(b,c\), \(bK+cK=(b+c)K\). Coercivity bounds the base \(\{t:E(t)\le R\}\). The set \(C\) is closed: if \((t_j,y_j)\in C\) converges to \((t,y)\), write \(y_j=\gamma(t_j)+(R-E(t_j))v_j\) with \(v_j\in K\); a convergent subsequence of the \(v_j\), available by compactness of \(K\), proves the defining membership at \((t,y)\). Together with (3), these observations prove compactness.

Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) with the Euclidean ball \(B(0_d,\varepsilon)\subseteq K\). Near \(t=0\), continuity gives \(E(t)<R/2\) and \(\|\gamma(t)\|_2<R\varepsilon/4\). For all such \(t\) and all \(y\) with \(\|y\|_2<R\varepsilon/4\), one has \(\|y-\gamma(t)\|_2<R\varepsilon/2<(R-E(t))\varepsilon\), so \((t,y)\in C\). Hence \(C\) has nonempty interior. It is convex by assumption.

For completeness, the stated sufficient condition proves this convexity. Membership of a vector \(v\) in a compact convex body \(K\) is equivalent to \(\beta\cdot v\le h_K(\beta)\) for every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\). Consequently, when \(E(t)\le R\), the fiber membership defining \(C\) is equivalent to

\[
\beta\cdot y+h_K(\beta)E(t)-\beta\cdot\gamma(t)
\le Rh_K(\beta)
\quad\text{for every }\beta\in\mathbb R^d.
\tag{4}
\]

This equivalence also holds when \(E(t)=R\), since the inequalities then force \(y=\gamma(t)\). Under the sufficient condition, each inequality in (4) defines a convex set, and \(E(t)\le R\) defines a convex set. Their intersection is \(C\).

Coercivity makes the integer support finite. Fibers with \(E(z)<R\) have precisely the positive volumes \(w_z\). Fibers with \(E(z)=R\) are singletons and have zero \(d\)-dimensional volume because \(d>0\); fibers with \(E(z)>R\) are empty. The origin fiber is \(RK\), whose positive volume is \(R^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\). The locked mixed-volume definition therefore gives \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\).

Let \(Z\sim p\), independently let \(U\) be uniform on \(K\), and set \(Y=\gamma(Z)+a_ZU\). Conditional on \(Z=z\), this is exactly the uniform law on the actual fiber \(C_z\). Thus for every measurable ambient set \(A\),

\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in A)=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)}W.
\tag{5}
\]

Every probability below uses this original law. Evenness gives \(p_{-z}=p_z\), \(\gamma(-z)=\gamma(z)\), and \(a_{-z}=a_z\). Hence the joint law of \((Z,Y)\) is invariant under \((Z,Y)\mapsto(-Z,Y)\). The marginal law of \(Y\) is absolutely continuous, as a finite mixture of uniform laws on positive-volume fibers, and (3) supports it on \(RK\).

We reproduce the original global contraction of accepted fact 57d7623173030446 with the actual \(K\)-fiber weights. Even convexity makes \(E\) nondecreasing in absolute value. Indeed, for \(0\le s\le t\) with \(t>0\), \(s\) is a convex combination of \(t\) and \(-t\), which have equal energy, so \(E(s)\le E(t)\). Define \(Qz=\operatorname{sgn}(z)\lfloor|z|/2\rfloor\), with \(Q0=0\). Since \(|Qz|\le|z|/2\), monotonicity and convexity at zero give

\[
E(Qz)\le E(z/2)\le E(z)/2,
\qquad a_{Qz}\ge a_z+E(z)/2.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus \(Q\) maps \(D_+\) into \(D_+\). Its zero image has only the possible preimages \(-1,0,1\). An image \(k>0\) has possible preimages \(2k,2k+1\), and an image \(k<0\) has possible preimages \(2k,2k-1\). Every image has at most three preimages. For \(A_u=\{z\in D_+:E(z)/a_z\ge u\}\), (6) gives \(a_{Qz}\ge a_z(1+u/2)\) on \(A_u\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in A_u}a_z^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)
&\le(1+u/2)^{-d}\sum_{z\in A_u}a_{Qz}^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\\
&\le3(1+u/2)^{-d}\sum_{v\in D_+}a_v^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K).
\end{aligned}
\]

Division by \(W\) proves (2).

The required cap bound uses the actual shape. For \(0\le r<1\) and \(\eta\in rK\), convexity gives \(\eta+(1-r)K\subseteq K\). For every nonzero \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), the hyperplane \(\beta\cdot v=0\) has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume, and central symmetry gives \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap\{v:\beta\cdot v\ge0\})=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)/2\). In particular,

\[
\eta+(1-r)\bigl(K\cap\{v:\beta\cdot v\ge0\}\bigr)
\subseteq K\cap\{v:\beta\cdot(v-\eta)\ge0\},
\]

so the uniform law on \(K\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\beta\cdot(U-\eta)\ge0\bigr)
\ge\tfrac12(1-r)^d.
\tag{7}
\]

Suppose \(d\ge10\). The function \(s\mapsto(1+2/s)^s\) is increasing for \(s>0\): the derivative of its logarithm is \(\log(1+v)-v/(1+v)>0\), with \(v=2/s>0\), because this expression vanishes at \(v=0\) and its derivative with respect to \(v\) is \(v/(1+v)^2>0\). Applying (2) with \(u=4/d\) gives

\[
\mathbb P_p\!\left(\frac{E(Z)}{a_Z}\ge\frac4d\right)
\le3(1+2/d)^{-d}
\le3(5/6)^{10}<\tfrac12.
\tag{8}
\]

The strict inequality is exact: \(6^9=10077696>9765625=5^{10}\). Thus \(G=\{z\in D_+:E(z)/a_z<4/d\}\) has original mass greater than one half.

For \(z\in G\), central symmetry and \(\gamma(z)\in E(z)K\) give \(\eta_z=-\gamma(z)/a_z\in(4/d)K\). Taking \(r=4/d\) in (7) is legal because \(r\le2/5<1\). The function \(s\mapsto(1-4/s)^s\) is increasing for \(s>4\): the derivative of its logarithm is \(\log(1-v)+v/(1-v)>0\), with \(v=4/s\in(0,1)\), since this expression vanishes at zero and has derivative \(v/(1-v)^2>0\). Hence \((1-4/d)^d\ge(3/5)^{10}\). Because \(Y=a_z(U-\eta_z)\) conditional on \(Z=z\), (7) yields, simultaneously for every nonzero \(\beta\),

\[
\mathbb P(\beta\cdot Y\ge0\mid Z=z)
\ge\tfrac12(1-4/d)^d
\ge\tfrac12(3/5)^{10}
\quad(z\in G).
\tag{9}
\]

Averaging (9) under the original \(p\) over \(G\) gives \(\mathbb P(\beta\cdot Y\ge0)\ge(3/5)^{10}/4\). For \(\beta=0_d\), this probability is one.

We now transfer any marginal halfspace bound at a selected \(m\in RK\) to the actual ambient point \((0,m)\). For every \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), the pointwise inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf 1_{\{\alpha Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf 1_{\{-\alpha Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}\\
&\hspace{10mm}\ge\mathbf 1_{\{\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

holds: when the right-hand scalar is nonnegative, at least one of the two left-hand scalars is nonnegative. Reflection of \(Z\) makes the expectations of the two left-hand indicators equal. Every closed halfspace through \((0,m)\) therefore has probability at least half the probability of its corresponding marginal halfspace through \(m\). A closed halfspace containing \((0,m)\) contains the parallel halfspace through \((0,m)\), so the same bound applies to all containing halfspaces.

For \(d\ge10\), select \(m=0_d\). The bound following (9), together with (10) and (5), proves (1). The point \((0,0_d)\) belongs to the origin fiber and is fixed independently of the halfspace normal.

For \(1\le d\le9\), use the continuous centerpoint assertion recorded in accepted fact 57d7623173030446: any absolutely continuous probability measure supported on a cube \([-B,B]^d\), with \(B>0\), has a point in that cube at which every containing closed halfspace has probability at least \(1/(d+1)\). Choose \(B\) large enough that \(RK\subseteq[-B,B]^d\), and apply that assertion to the actual marginal law of \(Y\). Its absolute continuity and support were established above. Let \(m\) be the resulting centerpoint.

This \(m\) must belong to \(RK\). If it did not, take a nearest point \(q\in RK\) and put \(v=m-q\ne0_d\). Minimality of \(q\), applied along every segment from \(q\) to \(x\in RK\), gives \(v\cdot(x-q)\le0\). Hence \(v\cdot(x-m)\le-\|v\|_2^2<0\) for every \(x\in RK\). The closed halfspace \(\{y:v\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\) would contain \(m\) but have zero marginal probability, contradicting its positive centerpoint depth. Thus \((0,m)\in S\). Selecting this single \(m\) before all halfspaces, (10) and (5) yield

\[
h_S((0,m))\ge\frac{W}{2(d+1)}\ge\frac W{20}
>\frac{(3/5)^{10}}8\,W.
\]

For the last comparison, \((3/5)^{10}\le(3/5)^2=9/25\), and \((9/25)/8=9/200<1/20\). This completes the proof of (1) for every positive \(d\). For the subclass with one integer coordinate, the locked depth form follows with \(c_0=(3/5)^{10}/4\in(0,1/e)\), since \(c_0<1/4<1/e\). The proven quantifiers here keep the integer dimension equal to one.

Finally, unrestricted affine rank is realized for every fixed \(d\) and every fixed centrally symmetric \(K\). Choose linearly independent \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\in K\), which is possible because \(0_d\) is interior, and define

\[
E(t)=\sum_{l=1}^d t^{2(l+1)},
\qquad\gamma(t)=\sum_{l=1}^d t^{2(l+1)}v_l.
\tag{11}
\]

The energy is finite, even, convex, coercive, and zero at the origin. The center map is continuous, even, and zero at the origin. For \(t\ne0\), the vector \(\gamma(t)/E(t)\) is a convex combination of the \(v_l\), so \(\gamma(t)\in E(t)K\); the membership also holds at zero. For every \(\beta\),

\[
h_K(\beta)E(t)-\beta\cdot\gamma(t)
=\sum_{l=1}^d\bigl(h_K(\beta)-\beta\cdot v_l\bigr)t^{2(l+1)}
\]

is convex, since all coefficients are nonnegative and the even powers are convex. Thus the actual body is convex by (4). Choose \(R>E(d)\). Then \(0,1,\ldots,d\) belong to \(D_+\). The coefficient matrix expressing \(\gamma(k)\), \(1\le k\le d\), in the basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) has entries \(k^{2(l+1)}\). Factoring \(k^4\) from row \(k\) leaves the Vandermonde matrix \(((k^2)^{l-1})_{k,l}\), whose determinant is \(\prod_{1\le i<j\le d}(j^2-i^2)\ne0\), with empty product one for \(d=1\). Therefore \(\gamma(1),\ldots,\gamma(d)\) are linearly independent, and together with \(\gamma(0)=0_d\) their affine hull has dimension \(d\). The center map is nonlinear: it is even and nonzero, whereas an even affine map vanishing at zero is identically zero. This supplies actual examples with the claimed unrestricted center rank.
