## Statement

Let \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(p\) be a positive log-affine probability law on \(D\). Define actual closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\), moments \(M_q=\sum p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q\), and the canonical erosion quantities
\[
t_{\mathbf z}=\tau_p(\mathbf z),\quad
r_s(\mathbf z)=\min\{p_{\mathbf z},(t_{\mathbf z}-s)_+\},\quad
R_s=\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z),\quad
G(s)=\int_s^\infty R_u\,du.
\]

Put \(e_{\mathbf z}=-\log p_{\mathbf z}\). In each occupied parity class \(\mathbf c\), list the distinct energies as
\(e_{\mathbf c,1}<\cdots<e_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}}\), and define the canonical parity-energy capacity
\[
\kappa_{\mathbf c}
=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\mathbf c}-1}
\tanh\frac{e_{\mathbf c,i+1}-e_{\mathbf c,i}}4,
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)=\sum_{\mathbf c}\kappa_{\mathbf c}.
\]
This is exactly the capacity \(\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}\) of accepted fact \(61062ccd228bf31b\) under \(w=p\). Equivalently, if the distinct log-probabilities in class \(\mathbf c\) are listed as
\(y_{\mathbf c,1}<\cdots<y_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}}\), then
\(e_{\mathbf c,i}=-y_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}+1-i}\); reversing the order preserves the adjacent gaps. Thus the notation in the registered subgoal is the exact alias
\[
\mathcal L(p):=\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)
=\sum_{\mathbf c}\left[
1+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\mathbf c}-1}
\tanh\frac{y_{\mathbf c,i+1}-y_{\mathbf c,i}}4
\right].
\]
Then, for every \(s\ge0\),
\[
G(s)\ge\frac12
\sum_{\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\;(\mathrm{mod}\;2)}
r_s(\mathbf x)r_s(\mathbf y)
e^{-|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|/2}
\ge\frac{R_s^2}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)}. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{4\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)^3}
+\frac34\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^4. \tag{2}
\]
If each parity class has at most \(K\) log-weight levels, the first term is at least \(8^{-N}/(4K^3)\).

For any nonempty \(B\subseteq D\), let \(\rho=p(B)\). In each parity class meeting \(B\), list only the distinct energies attained on \(B\), define its capacity by the same formula, and let \(\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)\) be the sum of those capacities. Equivalently, this is the registered notation \(\mathcal L_B(p):=\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)\), obtained by using only the log-weight levels occurring in \(B\), separately within each occupied parity class. Then
\[
M_1(p)-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2
\ge\frac{\rho^2}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)}, \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\rho^6}{8\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)^3}
+\frac34\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^4. \tag{4}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact \(01a4100542206b94\) gives
\[
r_s(H)\le(p(H)-s)_+,
\]
\[
G(s)=\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)(t_{\mathbf z}-s)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2,
\]
and, after integration by parts in its cubic layer-cake formula,
\[
M_3(p)\ge6\int_0^\infty sG(s)\,ds
+\frac34\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^4. \tag{5}
\]

Suppose \(\mathbf x\ne\mathbf y\) have the same parity and both residual masses are positive. Their midpoint \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) belongs to \(D\). A generic minimizing cap at \(\mathbf z\) contains exactly one endpoint strictly. By the strict-order statement in accepted fact \(c16327a288b708bd\),
\[
t_{\mathbf z}>s+p_{\mathbf z},
\]
so \(r_s(\mathbf z)=p_{\mathbf z}\). Log-affinity gives
\(p_{\mathbf z}^2=p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}\).

Writing \(r_s(\mathbf x)=a p_{\mathbf x}\) and
\(r_s(\mathbf y)=b p_{\mathbf y}\), with \(0<a,b\le1\), one has
\[
p_{\mathbf z}\min\{r_s(\mathbf x),r_s(\mathbf y)\}
\ge
r_s(\mathbf x)r_s(\mathbf y)
e^{-|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|/2},
\]
because \(\min\{au,bv\}\ge ab\min\{u,v\}\). For the diagonal case \(\mathbf x=\mathbf y=\mathbf z\), the same inequality follows directly from \(r_s(\mathbf z)\le p_{\mathbf z}\).

For any residual measure \(r\), reflection about \(\mathbf z\) gives
\[
\tau_r(\mathbf z)\ge
\frac12\left[
r(\mathbf z)+\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^N}
\min\{r(\mathbf z+\mathbf v),r(\mathbf z-\mathbf v)\}
\right],
\]
where \(r\) is extended by zero outside its support and the oriented sum includes \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\). If \(r_s(\mathbf z)>0\), a minimizing \(p\)-cap and the erosion halfspace inequality imply
\(t_{\mathbf z}-s\ge\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)\).
Substitute the reflected bound into the formula for \(G(s)\). The central square terms cancel, leaving one half the oriented reflected sum weighted by \(r_s(\mathbf z)\). Reindexing by
\(\mathbf x=\mathbf z+\mathbf v\), \(\mathbf y=\mathbf z-\mathbf v\),
and applying the preceding midpoint estimate proves the first inequality in (1), including its diagonal terms.

Under the identification \(w=p\), \(e_{\mathbf z}=-\log p_{\mathbf z}\), accepted fact \(61062ccd228bf31b\) gives the exact finite-kernel minimum: for any distinct energies \(e_1<\cdots<e_k\), nonnegative masses \(\beta_i\) of total mass \(A\), and
\[
\kappa=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\tanh\frac{e_{i+1}-e_i}{4},
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{i,j=1}^k\beta_i\beta_j e^{-|e_i-e_j|/2}\ge\frac{A^2}{\kappa}.
\tag{6}
\]
Group the residual masses by parity and equal energy. Applying (6) in each parity class gives \(\sum_{\mathbf c}R_{s,\mathbf c}^2/\kappa_{\mathbf c}\), and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf c}\frac{R_{s,\mathbf c}^2}{\kappa_{\mathbf c}}
\ge\frac{R_s^2}{\sum_{\mathbf c}\kappa_{\mathbf c}}
=\frac{R_s^2}{\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)}.
\]
Because \(|e_{\mathbf x}-e_{\mathbf y}|=|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|\), this proves the second inequality in (1).

Write \(\kappa=\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)\). Since \(R_0=1\), (1) gives \(G(0)\ge1/(2\kappa)\). Since \(G'=-R_s\), it also gives
\[
\frac d{ds}\sqrt{G(s)}\ge-\sqrt{\kappa/2}
\]
where \(G>0\). Therefore
\[
G(s)\ge(1-\kappa s)_+^2/(2\kappa).
\]
Using (5),
\[
6\int_0^\infty sG(s)\,ds
\ge\frac3\kappa\int_0^{1/\kappa}s(1-\kappa s)^2\,ds
=\frac1{4\kappa^3}.
\]
This proves (2). The level-count consequence follows from
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)\le\sum_{\mathbf c}k_{\mathbf c}\le K2^N\).

For \(B\subseteq D\), restrict the nonnegative kernel sum in the first inequality of (1) at \(s=0\) to \(B\times B\). Apply the exact kernel bound (6) to the retained energy levels in each parity class and then weighted Cauchy--Schwarz across those classes. This gives
\[
G(0)\ge\frac{\rho^2}{2\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)}.
\]
The accepted erosion identity gives
\(G(0)=M_1(p)-\frac12\sum p_{\mathbf z}^2\), proving (3).
Finally, \(R_s\le1\) implies \(G(s)\ge(G(0)-s)_+\). Hence (5) gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge G(0)^3+\frac34\sum p_{\mathbf z}^4,
\]
which proves (4). Completeness was used only for the original support and its midpoints, never for \(B\).
