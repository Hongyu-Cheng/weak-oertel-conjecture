## Statement

Consider a continuous path of positive finite-fiber bodies on a fixed finite base, with common compact candidate charts and actual depth jointly continuous in chart coordinates and time. Let $\beta(t)$ be its maximum normalized depth and $M(t)>0$ its raw mass, differentiable at the time under consideration. Suppose there are finitely many globally maximizing candidates $Y_i$ at that time, all own-interior. On compact chart neighborhoods of each $Y_i$, assume genuine through-candidate cap tests bound raw depth from above by a finite branch minimum and satisfy the hypotheses of the uniform finite-envelope lemma, with all branch values at $Y_i$ equal to $Q=\beta(t)M(t)$. Let $b_{ij}$ be the complete raw branch derivative at fixed chart coordinate along the actual body path and $\theta_{ij}$ any balanced probabilities there. Then
$$
D^+\beta(t)\le \frac{1}{M(t)}\max_i\left(\sum_j\theta_{ij}b_{ij}-\beta(t)M'(t)\right).
$$
A nonpositive right side at every time before the right endpoint of an interval makes $\beta$ nonincreasing there. A uniform upper bound $-c$ gives $\beta(b)-\beta(a)\le-c(b-a)$ on any subinterval $[a,b]$.

## Proof

At the given time the compact complement of the chosen neighborhoods has a strict depth gap, which persists for small time changes by joint continuity. The old maximum is approached at some current candidate, so the future maximum is attained in one of the finitely many neighborhoods for all sufficiently small positive time increments. On each neighborhood the actual raw depth is bounded by its cap envelope, and the uniform finite-envelope lemma bounds its supremum by $Q+s\sum_j\theta_{ij} b_{ij}+o(s)$. The finite maximum preserves $o(s)$. Divide by $M(t+s)=M(t)+sM'(t)+o(s)$ and take the upper right derivative. This argument at a tie time includes every source tied then; the branch derivatives use all actual body, chart and smoothing changes built into the tests.

For comparison, if a continuous function with $D^+f\le0$ increased from $a$ to $b$, take a small $\epsilon>0$ so $f(t)-\epsilon t$ still increased between these endpoints. Its minimum on $[a,b]$ is attained at some $t_0<b$ and has nonnegative upper right Dini derivative, contradicting the bound $-\epsilon$. Apply this also to $\beta(t)+ct$ for the rate statement.
