## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A\) be a real symmetric positive-definite \(n\times n\) matrix, and let \(a\in\mathbb R^n\). Use the quadratic and Gaussian notation of accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15`, now without a finite truncation: \(Q(x):=x^{\mathsf T}Ax\), \(w_z:=e^{-Q(z-a)}\) for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(W:=\sum_z w_z\). Choose any integer minimizer \(j\) of \(Q(j-a)\), and put \(u:=j-a\), \(r:=Q(u)\), and \(F:=W/w_j\). Define the signed linear functional \(T(v):=-2u^{\mathsf T}Av\). The scalar cutoff called T inside the proof of `a7090e5d5aa26b15` is not reused here; throughout this packet, \(T(v)\) denotes only this linear functional.

For \(s>0\), let \(\theta_s:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(v)}\), let \(D_s\) be the probability law with mass \(D_s(v):=e^{-sQ(v)}/\theta_s\), and let \(\mathbb P_s\), \(\mathbb E_s\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_s\) mean probability, expectation, and variance under this law. Set \(m_s:=\mathbb E_s Q(v)\). This \(D_s\) is a discrete Gaussian probability law, not a halfspace-depth function.

Use exactly the reflected-weight and curvature normalization of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`: \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}=\mathbb Z^n\), \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\), and \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\) for \(L\ge0\). These are auxiliary infinite-table analogues of that fact's finite-table objects; its convex-body conclusion is not applied to the infinite table. Every displacement sum is oriented: v and -v both occur, and v=0 occurs once. Define the actual normalized cutoff score by
\[
\mathcal K_j:=\frac{2^n}{W}\sup_{q\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(2q)}{1+2q}.
\]

All logarithms are natural. Write \(h_{\rm cert}:=\log(\theta_1/\theta_2)\), \(G_{\rm cert}:=\log(2^n\theta_1/F)\), and \(\eta:=\log(F/\theta_1)\). The subscripted gaps are exact aliases for the scalars h and G in the external consultation, and are distinct from the depth and halfspace notation in `280b299bc1e87c14`. Define
\[
\nu_2^{\rm cert}:=\inf_{0<b<2}\frac{2}{b^2}\log\frac{\theta_{2-b}}{\theta_2}.
\]
This scalar is precisely the consultation's variance proxy \(\nu_2\); it is not any of the midpoint probability measures \(\nu_v\) from `280b299bc1e87c14`. With \([x]_+:=\max\{x,0\}\), define all four certificates by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_0&:=\frac{e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}}{\theta_2}=\frac{2^n}{F},\\
\Gamma_1&:=\sup_{q\ge0}\frac{e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}}{1+2q}\mathbb P_2(Q\le q),\\
\Gamma_2&:=\frac{\exp(G_{\rm cert}-2\sqrt{2\eta})}{2(1+4n)},\\
\Gamma_3&:=\sup_{\substack{0\le p\le q\\\tau>0}}
\frac{e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}+p-\tau}}{1+2q}
\left[\mathbb P_2(p\le Q\le q)-\frac{\nu_2^{\rm cert}}{\tau^2}\right]_+.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here p and q are real energy thresholds, and \(\tau\) is a real threshold for \(|T|\). For every input just specified, these expressions are well-defined, nonnegative, and satisfy \(\mathcal K_j\ge\max_{0\le i\le3}\Gamma_i\). In particular, \(\eta\ge0\). The auxiliary bounds \(G_{\rm cert}\ge h_{\rm cert}\ge0\), \(\operatorname{Var}_2(T)\le\nu_2^{\rm cert}\le2h_{\rm cert}\), and \(m_2\le h_{\rm cert}\) also hold. For any such input, the additional condition \(\nu_2^{\rm cert}\ge\mathbb E_2[Q^2]\) implies the annular-collapse bound \(\Gamma_3\le\Gamma_1\).

Nevertheless their universal infimum is zero. Specifically, for each integer \(k\ge5\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_k&:=\left\lfloor\tfrac12(\log k)e^{2k}\right\rfloor,&
M_k&:=\left\lfloor k^{-2}e^{2k^3}\right\rfloor,&
n_k&:=N_k+M_k,\\
A_k&:=\operatorname{diag}(kI_{N_k},k^3I_{M_k}),&
a_k&:=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{n_k},&
j_k&:=\mathbf0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The integers \(N_k,M_k\) are block dimensions; no midpoint map or thinned mass from `280b299bc1e87c14` is denoted by them. Evaluate all the preceding objects at \((n_k,A_k,a_k,j_k)\). Then \(j_k\) is a nearest integer point, \(r_k=(kN_k+k^3M_k)/4\to\infty\), and, for all sufficiently large k,
\[
\Gamma_1=\Gamma_0,\qquad \Gamma_3\le\Gamma_1,\qquad
\max_{0\le i\le3}\Gamma_i=\Gamma_0=(1+o(1))k^{-1/2}.
\]
Moreover \(\Gamma_2=o(\Gamma_0)\), and the optimized proxy itself satisfies
\[
\nu_2^{\rm cert}\ge3k^4-3k^6e^{-2k^3},\qquad
\mathbb E_2[Q^2]=2k^4+O(k^2\log^2k).
\]
Consequently
\(\inf_{n,A,a,j\ {
m nearest}}\max_{0\le i\le3}\Gamma_i=0\).
For this same family the genuine score satisfies
\[
\mathcal K_{j_k}\ge
\Gamma_0\frac{N_k(N_k-1)e^{-2k}}{1+4k}
=(1+o(1))\frac{e^{2k}(\log k)^2}{16k^{3/2}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The family also satisfies both stated residual conditions:
\(\theta_2e^{h_{\rm cert}-G_{\rm cert}}\sim\sqrt{k}\) and
\((1+m_2)e^{h_{\rm cert}-G_{\rm cert}}\sim\sqrt{k}\log k\).

For precision about finite tables, if \(R\ge r\) is finite and
\(w_z^{(R)}:=e^{-Q(z-a)}\mathbf1_{\{Q(z-a)\le R\}}\), a displacement v has both reflected endpoints retained exactly when \(r+Q(v)+|T(v)|\le R\). A whole cutoff \(Q(v)\le q\) is therefore retained if \(R\ge r+2q\). The event \(p\le Q(v)\le q\), \(|T(v)|\le\tau\) is retained if \(R\ge r+q+\tau\). For the displayed family, the particular vectors proving divergence are retained already when \(R\ge r_k+2k\), and give the same lower bound for the score normalized by the truncated total mass \(W_R:=\sum_z w_z^{(R)}\). This last assertion concerns those vectors, not every vector of the cutoff \(Q\le2k\). The four vanishing certificates above are the untruncated certificates as defined; no assertion that they vanish after recomputing them from a hard truncation is made. This is a counterexample to the proposed four-certificate assertion, not a counterexample to weak Oertel.

## Proof

Positive definiteness bounds Q below by a positive multiple of the squared Euclidean norm. It follows that the nearest integer point exists, \(0<W<\infty\), and \(0<\theta_s<\infty\) for every \(s>0\). All lattice sums used below, including polynomial moments, and their derivatives converge uniformly when s ranges in a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\). Sums with an additional bounded-parameter linear exponential tilt enjoy the same convergence, by absorbing its linear growth into a smaller positive quadratic coefficient. These facts justify the differentiations and moment-generating functions used below. Nonnegative double sums may be rearranged by Tonelli. Every bounded energy cutoff contains finitely many lattice points.

The nearest-point identities are
\(Q(u+v)=r+Q(v)-T(v)\) and \(Q(u-v)=r+Q(v)+T(v)\).
Both are at least r. Thus \(|T(v)|\le Q(v)\), \(\Delta_j(v)=2Q(v)\), and
\(\alpha(v)=w_j e^{-Q(v)-|T(v)|}=w_j e^{-2Q(v)}e^{Q(v)-|T(v)|}\).
In particular the curvature has exactly the normalization of `280b299bc1e87c14`, with no temperature factor inserted. Also \(\alpha(v)\le w_{j+v}\), so \(\sum_v\alpha(v)\le W\) and \(\mathcal K_j\le2^n\); its defining supremum is finite.

For each parity class \(c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(W_c\) be its total Gaussian weight. Cauchy--Schwarz, the bijection \((x,y)=(z+v,z-v)\) between ordered pairs of equal parity and \((z,v)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z^n\), and the maximality of \(w_j\) give
\[
2^{-n}W^2\le\sum_c W_c^2
=\sum_{z,v}e^{-2Q(z-a)-2Q(v)}
=\theta_2\sum_z w_z^2
\le\theta_2 w_j W.
\]
Therefore \(F\le2^n\theta_2\), which is \(G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}\ge0\). Termwise \(\theta_1\ge\theta_2\), so \(G_{\rm cert}\ge h_{\rm cert}\ge0\). Translating z by j in F gives \(F/\theta_1=\mathbb E_1e^T\). The law \(D_1\) is invariant under \(v\mapsto-v\), whereas T changes sign, hence \(F/\theta_1=\mathbb E_1\cosh T\ge1\) and \(\eta\ge0\).

We next verify the covariance estimate with its normalization. The primary external input is Regev--Stephens-Davidowitz, Corollary 3.2, whose exact covariance statement and Gaussian normalization are recorded in EXTERNAL SOURCES. Put \(J_s(b):=\log\mathbb E_s e^{bT}\) for real b. The probability law proportional to \(e^{-sQ(v)+bT(v)}\) is the exponential tilt used to differentiate \(J_s\). Completing the square gives
\[
-sQ(v)+bT(v)=-sQ\bigl(v+(b/s)u\bigr)+(b^2/s)r.
\]
Let \(A^{1/2}\) be the symmetric positive-definite square root, and set \(P_s:=\sqrt{s/\pi}\,A^{1/2}\). Under this tilted law, the vector \(P_s(v+(b/s)u)\) has the source's unit-parameter discrete Gaussian law on the coset \(P_s\mathbb Z^n+P_s(b/s)u\), since its mass is proportional to \(e^{-\pi\|P_s(v+(b/s)u)\|_2^2}\). This is a full-rank lattice coset. The source's centered law corresponds to \(D_s\) under \(v\mapsto P_sv\). Its covariance inequality, followed by the invertible linear map \(P_s^{-1}\), says that the covariance of v under the tilt is at least its covariance under \(D_s\). Translation does not change covariance. Applying the linear functional \(v\mapsto-2u^{\mathsf T}Av\) therefore gives \(J_s''(b)\ge\operatorname{Var}_s(T)\) for every real b. Symmetry gives \(J_s(0)=J_s'(0)=0\), so for \(b\ge0\), integration on \([0,b]\) gives \(J_s(b)\ge(b^2/2)\operatorname{Var}_s(T)\).

For \(0<b<s\), the bound \(T\le Q\) also gives \(\mathbb E_s e^{bT}\le\theta_{s-b}/\theta_s\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}_s(T)\le
\inf_{0<b<s}\frac{2}{b^2}\log\frac{\theta_{s-b}}{\theta_s}.
\]
At \(s=2\) this proves \(\operatorname{Var}_2(T)\le\nu_2^{\rm cert}\); choosing \(b=1\) in the infimum proves \(\nu_2^{\rm cert}\le2h_{\rm cert}<\infty\). The infimum is nonnegative because \(\theta_{2-b}\ge\theta_2\). Its attainment is not needed anywhere. Also \(m_s'= -\operatorname{Var}_s(Q)\le0\) and \((\log\theta_s)'=-m_s\). Consequently \(h_{\rm cert}=\int_1^2m_s\,ds\ge m_2\).

We use the centered Poisson identity derived in the proof of accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15`, with exactly the same matrix A and centered parameter s. Its derivation applies to every positive-definite A: periodize \(e^{-sQ(x)}\) over \(\mathbb Z^n\); diagonalization and the one-dimensional Gaussian Fourier integral give its Fourier coefficient at z as \(\pi^{n/2}s^{-n/2}(\det A)^{-1/2}e^{-\pi^2z^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}z/s}\). The periodization and its Fourier series converge absolutely, and evaluation at zero gives
\[
\theta_s=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{s^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-\pi^2 z^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}z/s}.
\]
Its termwise derivative, justified either by the convergence above on the dual lattice or by that accepted derivation, gives
\[
m_s=\frac{n}{2s}-\frac{\pi^2}{s^2}
\frac{\sum_z(z^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}z)e^{-\pi^2z^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}z/s}}
{\sum_z e^{-\pi^2z^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}z/s}}
\le\frac{n}{2s}.
\]
Finally the earlier covariance argument at \(s=1,b=1\), using \(J_1(1)=\eta\) directly rather than a comparison with \(\theta_0\), gives \(\operatorname{Var}_1(T)\le2\eta\). All tilted sums here converge even at b=1; the restriction \(b<s\) was required only for the separate upper comparison with \(\theta_{s-b}\).

The formula for \(\alpha\) gives the exact score representation
\[
\mathcal K_j=e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}
\sup_{q\ge0}\frac{\mathbb E_2[e^{Q-|T|}\mathbf1_{\{Q\le q\}}]}{1+2q}.
\]
Taking q=0 gives \(\mathcal K_j\ge\Gamma_0\), because positive definiteness makes v=0 the sole zero-energy vector and \(D_2(0)=1/\theta_2\). Dropping the nonnegative slack \(Q-|T|\) gives \(\mathcal K_j\ge\Gamma_1\).

On the event \(p\le Q\le q\), \(|T|\le\tau\), the factor \(e^{Q-|T|}\) is at least \(e^{p-\tau}\). Since \(\mathbb E_2T=0\), Chebyshev and the proved proxy inequality give
\[
\mathbb P_2(p\le Q\le q,\ |T|\le\tau)
\ge\left[\mathbb P_2(p\le Q\le q)-\frac{\nu_2^{\rm cert}}{\tau^2}\right]_+.
\]
Every expression in the supremum defining \(\Gamma_3\) is therefore at most \(\mathcal K_j\), proving its validity without assuming a maximizing triple exists.

For \(\Gamma_2\), first suppose \(\eta>0\). Markov gives \(\mathbb P_1(Q>2n)\le1/4\), and Chebyshev gives \(\mathbb P_1(|T|>2\sqrt{2\eta})\le1/4\). Thus the intersection \(Q\le2n\), \(|T|\le2\sqrt{2\eta}\) has probability at least 1/2. If \(\eta=0\), then \(\operatorname{Var}_1(T)=0\) and symmetry forces \(T=0\) almost surely, so the same lower bound holds. The equivalent \(D_1\) representation \(\alpha(v)=w_je^{-Q(v)}e^{-|T(v)|}\), at cutoff \(q=2n\), now gives \(\mathcal K_j\ge e^{G_{\rm cert}}e^{-2\sqrt{2\eta}}/[2(1+4n)]=\Gamma_2\). This completes verification of all four sufficient certificates.

We record an annular-collapse observation valid for any of the inputs in the statement. If \(\nu_2^{\rm cert}\ge\mathbb E_2[Q^2]\), then \(\Gamma_3\le\Gamma_1\). Indeed, fix \(0\le p\le q\) and \(\tau>0\), and put \(P_{\rm ann}:=\mathbb P_2(p\le Q\le q)\). If the positive-part bracket vanishes, its contribution is zero. Otherwise \(P_{\rm ann}>0\) and
\[
\tau^2>\frac{\nu_2^{\rm cert}}{P_{\rm ann}}
\ge\frac{\mathbb E_2[Q^2]}{P_{\rm ann}}\ge p^2.
\]
The last inequality follows by retaining the indicated annulus in the nonnegative expectation of \(Q^2\). Hence \(\tau>p\), including when p=0. The annular contribution is at most \(e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}P_{\rm ann}/(1+2q)\), which is at most \(\Gamma_1\) because \(P_{\rm ann}\le\mathbb P_2(Q\le q)\). Taking the supremum proves the observation.

We now analyze the explicit family. Its matrices are positive definite. Each coordinate of \(a_k\) equals 1/2, and every nearest integer coordinate is 0 or 1. Thus \(j_k=0\) is nearest and \(r_k=(kN_k+k^3M_k)/4\). All nearest points have the same weight, so all four certificates have the same values regardless of which nearest point is selected. Define the one-dimensional sums \(\phi(t):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}e^{-tz^2}\) and \(\psi(t):=\sum_{l=0}^\infty e^{-tl(l+1)}\) for \(t>0\). Pairing \(z=-l\) with \(z=l+1\), whose half-shift energies are \(t(l+1/2)^2=t/4+tl(l+1)\), proves exactly
\[
\theta_s=\phi(sk)^{N_k}\phi(sk^3)^{M_k},\qquad
F=2^{n_k}\psi(k)^{N_k}\psi(k^3)^{M_k}.
\]

All O estimates in the remainder of the proof have absolute constants independent of t, k, and the block dimensions. They are estimates for real numbers, not numerical computations with these very large dimensions. For \(t\ge1\), the tails after the terms z=0,1,-1 in \(\phi(t)\), and after l=0,1 in \(\psi(t)\), satisfy
\(\phi(t)=1+2e^{-t}+O(e^{-4t})\) and \(\psi(t)=1+e^{-2t}+O(e^{-6t})\).
For example the first tail divided by \(e^{-4t}\) is at most \(2\sum_{z\ge2}e^{-(z^2-4)}<\infty\), and the second divided by \(e^{-6t}\) is at most \(\sum_{l\ge2}e^{-(l(l+1)-6)}<\infty\). Applying \(\log(1+x)=x+O(x^2)\) on the resulting fixed bounded small-x interval, in particular for \(t\ge5\), gives uniformly
\[
\log\psi(t)=e^{-2t}+O(e^{-4t}),\quad
\log\phi(2t)=2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-4t}),\quad
\log\phi(t)=2e^{-t}+O(e^{-2t}).
\]

The floors obey the exact error bounds
\[
\left|N_ke^{-2k}-\tfrac12\log k\right|\le e^{-2k},\qquad
\left|M_ke^{-2k^3}-k^{-2}\right|\le e^{-2k^3}.
\]
Also \(N_ke^{-4k}\le\tfrac12(\log k)e^{-2k}\) and \(M_ke^{-4k^3}\le k^{-2}e^{-2k^3}\). Thus multiplying the uniform logarithmic estimates by the actual block dimensions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(F/2^{n_k})
&=\tfrac12\log k+k^{-2}+O((\log k)e^{-2k}+e^{-2k^3}),\\
\log\theta_2
&=\log k+2k^{-2}+O((\log k)e^{-2k}+e^{-2k^3}),\\
G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}
&=\tfrac12\log k+k^{-2}+O((\log k)e^{-2k}+e^{-2k^3}).
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular \(\Gamma_0=(1+o(1))k^{-1/2}\) and \(\theta_2\sim k\).

The smallest positive lattice value of Q is k. For \(0\le q<k\), \(\mathbb P_2(Q\le q)=1/\theta_2\) and the \(\Gamma_1\) expression is at most \(\Gamma_0\), with equality at q=0. For \(q\ge k\), that expression is at most \(e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}/(1+2k)\). Since \(\theta_2<1+2k\) for sufficiently large k, the latter is less than \(e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}/\theta_2=\Gamma_0\). Hence \(\Gamma_1=\Gamma_0\) eventually.

The same one-dimensional expansions, with their dimension factors retained, give
\[
G_{\rm cert}=2N_ke^{-k}+2M_ke^{-k^3}
+O(N_ke^{-2k}+M_ke^{-2k^3}).
\]
Here \(n_k\sim M_k\sim k^{-2}e^{2k^3}\), so \(\sqrt{n_k}\sim e^{k^3}/k\). The first main term divided by \(\sqrt{n_k}\) is \(O(k(\log k)e^{k-k^3})=o(k^{-1})\). The second divided by \(\sqrt{n_k}\) is \((2+o(1))/k\), and the error is \(O(\log k)/\sqrt{n_k}=o(k^{-1})\). Therefore \(G_{\rm cert}/\sqrt{n_k}=(2+o(1))/k\to0\). Since \(\eta=n_k\log2-G_{\rm cert}\), it follows that \(\eta\sim n_k\log2\). As \(\log(2(1+4n_k))=o(\sqrt{n_k})\), we obtain
\[
\log\Gamma_2
=G_{\rm cert}-2\sqrt{2\eta}-\log(2(1+4n_k))
=-(2\sqrt{2\log2}+o(1))\sqrt{n_k}.
\]
This implies \(\Gamma_2=o(\Gamma_0)\).

To control the fully optimized proxy, we prove a lower bound uniform in its optimization parameter: for every \(t\ge5\) and every \(0<b<2\),
\[
\frac{2}{b^2}\log\frac{\phi((2-b)t)}{\phi(2t)}\ge3t^2e^{-2t}.
\]
For \(x\ge t\ge5\), the inequality \(z^2\ge z\) for positive integers z gives \(\phi(x)\le1+2/(e^x-1)\le4/3\). If \(0<b\le1\), differentiate \(\log\phi\), retain the z=1,-1 terms in the numerator, and use this bound in the denominator throughout \(0\le y\le bt\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{\phi((2-b)t)}{\phi(2t)}
&=\int_0^{bt}\frac{\sum_z z^2e^{-(2t-y)z^2}}{\phi(2t-y)}\,dy\\
&\ge\tfrac32 e^{-2t}(e^{bt}-1)
\ge\tfrac32 e^{-2t}b^2t^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the last step, \(e^x-1\ge x^2\) for \(x\ge0\): the derivative of \(e^x-1-x^2\) is at least \(1-x+x^2/2>0\), using the first three Taylor terms of \(e^x\). Multiplying by \(2/b^2\) proves the desired bound in this first range.

If \(1\le b<2\), monotonicity of the theta numerator in b and \(2/b^2\ge1/2\) give
\[
\frac{2}{b^2}\log\frac{\phi((2-b)t)}{\phi(2t)}
\ge\tfrac34 e^{-2t}(e^t-1)
\ge3t^2e^{-2t}.
\]
Here \(e^t-1\ge4t^2\) for \(t\ge5\). For an elementary check, the first seven Taylor terms give \(e^5>101\); the function \(e^t-1-4t^2\) is positive at 5, its derivative is positive there, and its second derivative is positive for all \(t\ge5\). This proves the second range and hence the bound for every b in the full interval.

For the two-block product, the logarithm of \(\theta_{2-b}/\theta_2\) is the sum of \(N_k\) copies at t=k and \(M_k\) copies at \(t=k^3\); each is nonnegative. Retaining only the second block, the just-proved bound holds before taking an infimum, for every \(0<b<2\). Consequently
\[
\nu_2^{\rm cert}\ge3M_k k^6e^{-2k^3}
\ge3k^4-3k^6e^{-2k^3}.
\]
No exchange of an infimum and a sum, and no restriction of the infimum to a subinterval, is used in this lower bound.

For comparison with \(\mathbb E_2[Q^2]\), let \(Z_t\) have mass \(e^{-2tz^2}/\phi(2t)\) on the integers. Uniformly for \(t\ge5\),
\[
\mathbb E Z_t^2=2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-4t}),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Z_t^2)=2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-4t}).
\]
Indeed the numerators for both \(\mathbb E Z_t^2\) and \(\mathbb E Z_t^4\) are \(2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-8t})\); the tails are bounded by fixed convergent sums after division by \(e^{-8t}\), as above with the extra polynomial factors z^2 or z^4. Their common denominator is \(1+2e^{-2t}+O(e^{-8t})\). Division and then subtraction of \((\mathbb E Z_t^2)^2\) give the stated estimates.

Under \(D_2\) the coordinates are independent and Q is the sum of their coefficients times their squares. Applying the floor bounds and the uniform moment errors gives explicitly
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_2
&=k\log k+2k
+O(k(\log k)e^{-2k}+k^3e^{-2k^3})
=k\log k+2k+o(1),\\
\operatorname{Var}_2(Q)
&=k^2\log k+2k^4
+O(k^2(\log k)e^{-2k}+k^6e^{-2k^3})
=k^2\log k+2k^4+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example the first-block error in the second line consists of the floor error \(O(k^2e^{-2k})\) and the accumulated moment error \(O(k^2N_ke^{-4k})\); both are included in the displayed bound. The second-block terms are bounded the same way before taking a limit. Squaring the estimate for \(m_2\) and adding the variance now gives
\(\mathbb E_2[Q^2]=2k^4+O(k^2\log^2k)=(2+o(1))k^4\).
The optimized-proxy lower bound is \((3+o(1))k^4\), so \(\nu_2^{\rm cert}\ge\mathbb E_2[Q^2]\) for all sufficiently large k. The annular-collapse observation proves \(\Gamma_3\le\Gamma_1=\Gamma_0\) eventually. Together with \(\Gamma_2=o(\Gamma_0)\), this proves the asserted maximum and its zero infimum. The zero value is an infimum, not an asserted attained minimum, since \(\Gamma_0>0\) for each input.

Also \(\theta_2e^{h_{\rm cert}-G_{\rm cert}}=F/2^{n_k}\sim\sqrt{k}\). Combining \(m_2\sim k\log k\) with \(e^{G_{\rm cert}-h_{\rm cert}}\sim\sqrt{k}\) gives the other residual asymptotic. Clearly \(r_k\to\infty\), so the sequence has no fixed bound on the nearest energy of the sort assumed in `a7090e5d5aa26b15`.

It remains to check the actual cutoff score and the finite-table claim. At \(j_k=0\), the signed functional is \(T(v)=k\sum_{i\le N_k}v_i+k^3\sum_{i>N_k}v_i\).
