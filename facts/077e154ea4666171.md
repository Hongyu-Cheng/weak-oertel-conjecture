## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be convex bodies at the only positive-area integer fibers $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $0\in\operatorname{int}K$. Set $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$ and $v_3=(-1,-1)$, and assume $B\subseteq T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1,\ i=1,2,3\}$. Suppose each closed ambient cap $H_i=\{(z,x):v_i\cdot x\ge z\}$ attains the true raw mixed area depth $Q$ at $(0,0)$, with proper $A$ cuts and zero $B$ captures. Proper means each cutting line meets the interior of $A$. Write $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $b=|B|$ for the planar areas, and $u\le v\le w$ for the ordered $A$ cap areas. Assume $b\le a+k$ and $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$. Then $a<b/6$.

## Proof

The three original $A$ halfplanes cover the plane because their thresholds sum to $-3$ and the normals sum to zero. The three middle halfplanes cover the plane by the same argument with thresholds zero. The middle cap areas in decreasing order are $Q-u,Q-v,Q-w$ by the common mixed area and the zero $B$ captures. Write $q=Q-w$, $\delta=a-u-v$, $r=\delta/w$, and $F=u+v+w/4$. The three-cap formula (3) of accepted fact `ac7ba1e1e0ebc955` applies to these ordinary planar bodies and covers with $\gamma_2=4/9$, so
$$
a\le w+v+u/4,\qquad k\le 9Q/4-F.
$$
Using $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$ with the second inequality gives both $k\le a+b-2F$ and $Q\le4(a+b-F)/9$. Then $b\le a+k$ gives $a\ge F$, so $r\ge1/4$. Also
$$
b\ge 9Q/2-a-k\ge9Q/4+F-a=(9/4)q+(5/2-r)w. \tag{1}
$$

Let $v_\ell$ be the normal of the largest $A$ cap. Replace its threshold $-1$ by $2$ and keep the two other thresholds at $-1$. These three halfplanes still cover the plane because their thresholds and normals both sum to zero. If the shifted cap has area $\tau$, the same three-cap formula gives $\tau\ge\delta+\tfrac34\min(u,\tau)$. If $\tau<u$, this implies $\tau\ge4\delta\ge w\ge u$, a contradiction. Hence $\tau\ge\delta+3u/4$. Put $x=u/w$ and $\theta=r+3x/4$. With $h=\max_{p\in A}v_\ell\cdot p$, positivity of $\tau\ge\theta w$ implies $h>2$ because the line at level $2$ has area zero. Write $H=h+1>3$. Contract the original largest cap about any of its points at level $-1$ by $3/H$. The contracted cap is still in the original cap, lies at levels at most $2$, and has area $9w/H^2$. Thus $\theta\le1-9/H^2$ and
$$
1/4\le r<\theta<1,\qquad H\ge3/\sqrt{1-\theta}. \tag{2}
$$

Take the segment $E$ joining two points of $A$ at the minimum and maximum of $t=v_\ell\cdot p$. Its range in $t$ is $[t_0,h]$ with $t_0<-1$ by properness. Choose a transverse linear coordinate $s$ of unit absolute Jacobian together with $t$, then shear $s$ by a multiple of $t$ so that $E$ has constant $s$. For a nonempty $t$-section of $B$ at fixed $s$, write $[L,U]$ for its interval. The three normal coordinates of $B$ sum to zero and are at most $1$, so $-2\le L\le U\le1$. The corresponding section of $E+B$ is $[t_0+L,h+U]$, with its lower endpoint negative and its upper endpoint positive. Its length at levels $t\ge0$ is $h+U$. The pointwise bound
$$
h+U-\frac{H}{3}(U-L)=\frac{(h-2)(1-U)+H(L+2)}3\ge0
$$
and integration in the area-preserving coordinates give $|(E+B)\cap\{t\ge0\}|\ge Hb/3$. By compatibility, $4q\ge Hb/3$. Combining this bound with (1) and (2) shows
$$
0<(5/2-r)w\le b(1-3H/16)\le b\left(1-\frac{9}{16\sqrt{1-\theta}}\right).
\tag{3}
$$
In particular $\theta<175/256$.

The actual upper part of $A$ adds area to this sweep. For this area calculation translate $A$ in the transverse coordinate, and $K$ by half that translation, so that $E$ has $s=0$. This preserves the tested $t$-cut, areas and midpoint containment. Write the $s$-interval of $A$ at height $t$ as $[a_-(t),a_+(t)]$, where $a_-(t)\le0\le a_+(t)$ for $t\in[t_0,h]$. Let $(s_+,t_+)$ and $(s_-,t_-)$ be points of $B$ with respectively largest and smallest $s$ coordinate. For $t\in[2,h]$, both heights $t+t_+$ and $t+t_-$ are nonnegative. At height $t+t_+$, the right endpoint of $E+B$ is $s_+$ because $(0,t)\in E$ and $s_+$ is the maximum transverse coordinate of $B$. The right endpoint of $A+B$ at that height is at least $s_++a_+(t)$. At height $t+t_-$, the left endpoint of $E+B$ is $s_-$ and that of $A+B$ is at most $s_-+a_-(t)$. At each height the two endpoint gains are nonnegative and add to the section-width gain, since $E+B\subseteq A+B$. Integrating the right gain on $[2+t_+,h+t_+]$ and the left gain on $[2+t_-,h+t_-]$ therefore adds at least $\int_2^h(a_+(t)-a_-(t))\,dt=\tau$ to the captured area of $E+B$. Returning to the original bodies, it follows that
$$
4q\ge Hb/3+\tau\ge Hb/3+\theta w. \tag{4}
$$

Set $\alpha=a/w>1$, since the largest original cut is proper. For the lower parts of $A$ at levels $-1$ and $h$, their Minkowski interpolation with weight $3/H$ is contained in the lower part at level $2$. These endpoint sets are compact convex with positive area $a-w$ and $a$. Apply the Brunn–Minkowski form recorded in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d` with $d=2$. Since the lower part at level $2$ has area at most $a-\tau\le a-\theta w$, this gives
$$
H\ge J(\alpha,\theta)
=\frac{3(\sqrt{\alpha}-\sqrt{\alpha-1})}{\sqrt{\alpha-\theta}-\sqrt{\alpha-1}}.
$$
The denominator is positive by (2). Combining (1) and (4) gives
$$
\frac{a}{b}\le\frac{\alpha N}{D},\qquad
N=1-\frac3{16}J,\quad D=\frac52-r+\frac9{16}\theta. \tag{5}
$$
Here $N>0$ because the bound follows from $Dw\le bN$ with $D>0$. By $v/w\le1$ and $\alpha=r+x+v/w$, one has $\theta\ge3(\alpha-1)/4+r/4$.

To bound (5), write $e=\sqrt{\alpha-1}$, $X=\sqrt{\alpha}$ and $Y=\sqrt{\alpha-\theta}$. Differentiating the radical expression, or using $J=3(Y+e)/((1-\theta)(X+e))$, gives
$$
\frac{J_\alpha}{J}=\frac{1/Y-1/X}{2e}>0,\qquad
\frac{J_\theta}{J}=\frac{1+e/Y}{2(1-\theta)}.
$$
In particular $J\ge3/\sqrt{1-\theta}$ by taking the limit $\alpha\downarrow1$. On $\theta\ge1/4$ these formulas yield $J\ge2\sqrt3$ and $J_\theta\ge3/2$. If also $\alpha\ge5/4$, then $e/Y\ge1/2$ since $3\alpha+\theta\ge4$, so $J_\theta\ge J$.

For fixed $\alpha,r$, decreasing $\theta$ to $\theta_0=\max\{r,3(\alpha-1)/4+r/4\}$ raises $N$ and lowers $D$, so it increases the bound while keeping it positive. Along this choice of $\theta$, $D\ge33/16$ because $r\le\theta<1$. On the piece $\theta_0=r$, the derivative of $N/D$ in $r$ has the sign of $-3J_\theta D+7N$, which is negative since $3J_\theta D\ge297/32>7$. On the piece $\theta_0=3(\alpha-1)/4+r/4$, one has $\alpha\ge1+r\ge5/4$. The derivative has the sign of $-3J_\theta D+55N$. This is negative because $3J_\theta D-55N\ge(33/2)J-55\ge33\sqrt3-55>0$. The pieces agree at their meeting point. Decreasing $r$ to $1/4$ also decreases $\theta_0$, so the numerator remains positive. It suffices to bound (5) at $r=1/4$ and $\theta=\max\{1/4,3\alpha/4-11/16\}$.

For $1<\alpha\le5/4$ the latter value is $1/4$ and $D=153/64$. If $\alpha\le9/8$, using $J\ge2\sqrt3$ gives $\alpha N/D\le(8-3\sqrt3)/17<1/6$ since $31^2<3\cdot18^2$. If $9/8\le\alpha\le5/4$, use $J\ge J(9/8,1/4)=1+\sqrt7$ to get $\alpha N/D\le(65-15\sqrt7)/153<1/6$ since $79^2<7\cdot30^2$.

For $\alpha\ge5/4$ with $\theta=3\alpha/4-11/16<1$, one has $D=(477+108\alpha)/256$, $D'=27/64$, and $N'\le-9J/64$ by the derivative bounds for $J$. Where $N>0$, the numerator of the derivative of $\alpha N/D$ is at most
$$
N(D-\alpha D')-\frac9{64}\alpha JD
\le \frac{477}{512}-\frac{1215}{1024}<0,
$$
using $N\le1/2$, $\alpha\ge5/4$, $J\ge3$, and $D\ge9/4$. Here $N$ decreases along this piece and $\theta$ increases, so the relevant interval is attached to $\alpha=5/4$. The bound decreases from the value there, which was already strictly below $1/6$. This proves $a<b/6$.
