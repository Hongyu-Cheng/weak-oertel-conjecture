## Statement

Use the diagonal coordinates $(x,y,z,w)$ of a strict tetrahedral quadrilateral section for split $\{0,1\}\mid\{2,3\}$. Let $\lambda$ be its actual centroid in barycentrics, $a=xy$, $b=zw$ and $\Delta=(x+z)(x+w)(y+z)(y+w)$. Then
$$
3(\lambda_0-\lambda_1)=\frac{(x-y)[b-(a-b)^2]}{\Delta},\qquad
3(\lambda_2-\lambda_3)=\frac{(z-w)[a-(a-b)^2]}{\Delta}.
$$
In each pair separately, if both centroid coordinates of that pair are at most $1/3$, the bracket for that pair is strictly positive. Thus the within-pair ordering, including ties, agrees with that of the corresponding diagonal coordinates. In particular equal centroid coordinates at most $1/3$ within either pair force the two signed raw heights in that pair to coincide.

## Proof

From tetrahedral-quadrilateral-section-calculus,
$3\lambda_0=x[1/(x+z)+1/(x+w)-1]$ and similarly for the other entries. Subtract the first two and factor $x-y$. The coefficient is
$$
\frac{z}{(x+z)(y+z)}+\frac{w}{(x+w)(y+w)}-1
=\frac{(z+w)(a-b)+2b-\Delta}{\Delta}
=\frac{b-(a-b)^2}{\Delta}.
$$
Here the numerator in the first equality uses $x+y+z+w=1$ and the last uses the expanded expression for $\Delta$. The second difference follows by interchanging pairs. The factored identities hold when the difference vanishes as well.

Also, $(1-3\lambda_0)(x+z)(x+w)=b(1+x)-ax$. To see this, use $(x+z)(x+w)=x(1-y)+b$ and $2x+z+w=1+x-y$ in the centroid formula. Replacing $x$ by $y$ gives the analogous identity. Thus for the first pair the hypotheses imply $(a-b)\max(x,y)\le b$. If $a\le b$, then $(a-b)^2\le b^2<b$. If $a>b$ and $(a-b)^2\ge b$, then $(a-b)\max(x,y)\ge\sqrt{ab}>b$, a contradiction. The proof for the other pair interchanges $a,b$. The reciprocal-height formula then gives the equal-height implication.
