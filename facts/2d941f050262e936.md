## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(w_{\mathbf I}>0\) on \(D\), with \(w_{\mathbf I}=0\) off \(D\). Write \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\), \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) on \(D\), \(p_{\max}=\max_Dp_{\mathbf I}\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\). All logarithms are natural. Both indices of a feasible array range over \(D\), and feasibility means

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
\quad(\mathbf J\in D),\qquad r\ge0.
\]

Set \(q_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) and \(T=\{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0\}\). For a finite probability law \(v\), write \(S(v)=-\sum_xv_x\log v_x\). Interpret zero summands in all entropy expressions by continuity. Use the original potential and the canonical quantities

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(r)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       \log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},
& I(r)&=S(q)-H(r),\\
G(r)&=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       (\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J})
=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
       \log\frac{p_{\mathbf J}}{p_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The archived response's \(\mathcal A(r)\), \(\mathcal G(r)\), and \(H(q)\) are \(H(r)\), \(G(r)\), and \(S(q)\), respectively.

If the original mean \(\mathbf m=\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\) is integral, there is exactly one feasible array satisfying

\[
T=\operatorname{vert}(\operatorname{conv}T)
 =\operatorname{conv}T\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\]

It has \(T=\{\mathbf m\}\), \(q=\delta_{\mathbf m}\), \(r_{\mathbf m,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\), and all other entries zero. In particular, \(S(q)=H(r)=I(r)=0\) and \(G(r)=\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(p_{\mathbf m}/p_{\mathbf I})\).

For each \(n\ge1\), consider the complete lattice simplex and table

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n&=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:x_i\ge-1\ (1\le i\le n),
                       \ \textstyle\sum_i x_i\le1\},\\
D_n&=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
N_n=|D_n|=\binom{2n+1}{n},\qquad
\varepsilon_n=\frac{n}{N_n-1},\\
w_{\mathbf0}&=1,\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=\varepsilon_n\quad
(\mathbf I\in D_n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}),\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D_n).
\end{aligned}
\]

In this family use \(D=D_n\). Here \(\mathbf0\) is the only interior lattice point of \(P_n\), \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_n}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\), and \(W=n+1\). Thus \(p_{\mathbf0}=1/(n+1)\), every other positive probability is \(n/((n+1)(N_n-1))\), and the original mean is \(\mathbf0\). Every feasible array satisfying the target condition has exactly

\[
S(q)=H(r)=I(r)=0,\qquad
G(r)=\frac{n}{n+1}\log\frac{N_n-1}{n}.
\]

Consequently, if a constant \(\kappa>0\), independent of \(n\) and the table, guarantees for every complete table with a finite-valued globally log-concave extension the existence of a feasible array satisfying the target condition and

\[
G(r)\le\kappa\bigl[n\log2-S(q)+H(r)\bigr]
      =\kappa\bigl[n\log2-I(r)\bigr],
\]

then necessarily

\[
\kappa\ge
\frac{\log((N_n-1)/n)}{(n+1)\log2}\quad(n\ge1),
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\log((N_n-1)/n)}{(n+1)\log2}=2.
\]

In particular \(\kappa\ge2\). Coefficient one already fails at \(n=5\), where \(N_5=462\) and every such array has \(G(r)=\frac56\log(461/5)>5\log2\).

The necessity remains true when the tables are restricted to all integer fiber lengths of rational full-dimensional convex bodies in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). An explicit realization is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_n(\mathbf x)&=
 \max\{-x_1,\ldots,-x_n,\textstyle\sum_i x_i\},\\
a_n(\mathbf x)&=1-(1-\varepsilon_n)\gamma_n(\mathbf x),\\
C_n&=\{(\mathbf x,y):\mathbf x\in P_n,\ 0\le y\le a_n(\mathbf x)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(S_n=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), one has \(\mathcal H_1(S_n)=n+1\) and

\[
h_{S_n}((\mathbf0,1/2))=\frac12,\qquad
\frac{h_{S_n}((\mathbf0,1/2))}{\mathcal H_1(S_n)}
=\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\ge\frac12\,2^{-n}.
\]

## Proof

Summing the feasible row identities and using the column sums gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J
=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf m\).
The positive row masses sum to one, so this expresses \(\mathbf m\) as a convex combination with a strictly positive coefficient at every member of \(T\). If \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n\), the target condition places \(\mathbf m\) in \(T\) and makes it a vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}T\). If \(T\) contained any other point, then \(q_{\mathbf m}<1\), and rearrangement would give
\(\mathbf m=\sum_{\mathbf J\ne\mathbf m}
q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J/(1-q_{\mathbf m})\).
This contradicts the vertex property. Hence \(T=\{\mathbf m\}\); nonnegativity makes every other row zero, and the column sums force the displayed array. Conversely, completeness of \(D\) places the integral mean \(\mathbf m\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) in \(D\). The forced array is feasible by the original mean identity and has singleton target support, so it exists. The formulas for its entropies and energy follow directly from their definitions. This proves rigidity for the original mean, without any tilt or alteration of the source probabilities.

The change of variables \(k_i=x_i+1\) for \(1\le i\le n\) identifies \(P_n\) with the simplex \(k_i\ge0\), \(\sum_{i=1}^n k_i\le n+1\). In particular, \(P_n\) is bounded, full-dimensional, and has integer vertices. Adjoining \(k_0=1-\sum_i x_i\) identifies all of \(D_n\) bijectively with nonnegative integer \((n+1)\)-tuples satisfying \(\sum_{i=0}^n k_i=n+1\). Placing \(n\) separators among \(n+1\) identical units gives \(\binom{2n+1}{n}\) such tuples. Their set is invariant under every coordinate permutation. The sum of any one coordinate over all tuples is therefore \(N_n\), since the sum of all \(n+1\) coordinates over all tuples is \((n+1)N_n\). Thus, for \(1\le i\le n\),
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_n}I_i=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_n}(k_i-1)=0\).

An interior lattice point satisfies \(x_i>-1\), hence \(x_i\ge0\), and \(\sum_i x_i<1\), hence \(\sum_i x_i\le0\). It must be \(\mathbf0\), which satisfies all the defining inequalities strictly and is therefore interior. The points \(\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\pm\mathbf e_n\) all belong to \(D_n\), giving \(N_n-1\ge2n\) and \(0<\varepsilon_n\le1/2\). Since every vertex of \(P_n\) is in \(D_n\), its convex hull is exactly \(P_n\), so \(D_n\) is complete.

The weight sum is \(1+(N_n-1)\varepsilon_n=n+1\). Since the origin contributes zero to a vector sum and every other site has the same weight, the arithmetic-sum identity proves that the original weighted mean is zero. Also \(p_{\max}=p_{\mathbf0}\), so \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\) at every other site. Rigidity leaves just the origin row. Its total nonorigin source mass is \(n/(n+1)\), proving the exact energy formula.

For this forced array the bracket in the proposed inequality is \(n\log2>0\). Dividing the energy by that bracket gives the stated necessary bound on \(\kappa\). To calculate its limit without numerical estimates, note that \(N_n\) is a largest coefficient in the binomial expansion of \((1+1)^{2n+1}\). The \(2n+2\) coefficients have sum \(2\cdot4^n\), whence
\(4^n/(n+1)\le N_n\le2\cdot4^n\).
As \(N_n\ge3\), also \(N_n/2\le N_n-1\le N_n\). It follows that

\[
\frac{n\log4-\log(2n(n+1))}{(n+1)\log2}
\le
\frac{\log((N_n-1)/n)}{(n+1)\log2}
\le
\frac{n\log4+\log2-\log n}{(n+1)\log2}.
\]

Both outside expressions tend to two. Thus every \(\kappa<2\) fails for some \(n\). At \(n=5\), the exact count is \(\binom{11}{5}=462\), and \(461>5\cdot2^6=320\) gives \(\frac56\log(461/5)>5\log2\).

It remains to verify the actual-body realization and the depth calibration. The \(n+1\) linear expressions defining \(\gamma_n\) sum to zero, so their maximum is nonnegative. On \(P_n\) each is at most one, so \(0\le\gamma_n\le1\). Moreover, \(\gamma_n(\mathbf x)=0\) forces \(x_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i x_i\le0\), hence \(\mathbf x=\mathbf0\). At every lattice site \(\gamma_n\) is an integer; consequently it equals one at each member of \(D_n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\).

The function \(\gamma_n\) is convex as a maximum of linear functions. Since \(0<\varepsilon_n<1\), its negative multiple plus one, \(a_n\), is concave, with \(\varepsilon_n\le a_n\le1\) on \(P_n\). The body \(C_n\) is thus compact and convex. More explicitly its upper constraints are \(y\le1+(1-\varepsilon_n)x_i\) for every \(i\), and \(y\le1-(1-\varepsilon_n)\sum_i x_i\); together with the rational constraints of \(P_n\) and \(y\ge0\), these make it a rational polytope. All these constraints are strict at \((\mathbf0,1/2)\), so it is full-dimensional. Its fiber at \(\mathbf0\) is \([0,1]\), at every other \(\mathbf I\in D_n\) it is \([0,\varepsilon_n]\), and outside \(D_n\) its integer fibers are empty. These are exactly the original weights at every lattice point, including zeros.

Extend \(a_n\) by zero off \(P_n\). If both endpoints lie in \(P_n\), its concavity and the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality prove log-concavity. If either endpoint lies outside \(P_n\), the log-concavity lower bound is zero for each interpolation parameter strictly between zero and one, and therefore holds; at the endpoints it is equality. This gives a finite-valued globally log-concave extension of the entire table. Normalizing by \(W\) gives one for \(p\) as well.

Finally, the intersection of any closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,1/2)\) with the central vertical line contains at least one of the half-intervals \([0,1/2]\) and \([1/2,1]\). Its mixed-integer volume is therefore at least \(1/2\). The closed halfspace \(y\ge1/2\) attains this value, because all noncentral fibers have length at most \(1/2\) and contribute zero length to that halfspace. Thus the depth is exactly \(1/2\). Dividing by \(W=n+1\) and using \(n+1\le2^n\) proves the relative-depth statement. In particular these bodies satisfy the weak-Oertel lower bound for every allowed \(c_0<1/e\). The energy obstruction concerns the additional target condition only and proves no universal upper bound with coefficient two.
