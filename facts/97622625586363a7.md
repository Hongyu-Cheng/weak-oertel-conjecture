## Statement

Let \(a:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) be log-concave, write \(a_k=a(k)\), and assume \(0<W:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}a_k<\infty\). For every integer \(m\), define \(L:=\sum_{p\ge1}a_{m-p}\), \(R:=\sum_{q\ge1}a_{m+q}\), and \(M:=\sum_{r\ge1}\min(a_{m-r},a_{m+r})\). Then \(LR\le WM\).

An integer weighted median means an integer \(m\) satisfying \(L\le W/2\) and \(R\le W/2\). Such a median exists, and every such median satisfies \(a_m>0\) and \(S_m:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\min(a_k,a_{2m-k})\ge W/2\). The constant \(1/2\) is optimal, even if the integer center may be chosen freely.

Consequently, let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and let \(a:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) be the product weight \(a(\mathbf x)=\prod_{i=1}^n b_i(x_i)\), where each \(b_i:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) is log-concave and \(0<W_i:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}b_i(k)<\infty\). Write \(a_{\mathbf z}=a(\mathbf z)\) and \(W:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}\). For any choice of integer weighted median \(m_i\) of each factor, the center \(\mathbf m=(m_1,\ldots,m_n)\) satisfies \(a_{\mathbf m}>0\) and \(S_{\mathbf m}:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\min(a_{\mathbf z},a_{2\mathbf m-\mathbf z})\ge2^{-n}W\). The constant \(2^{-n}\) is optimal for this product class, even with a freely chosen integer center.

## Proof

First, log-concavity implies an inward-pair inequality: whenever \(x\le s,t\le y\) and \(s+t=x+y\), one has \(a(x)a(y)\le a(s)a(t)\). If the left side is zero, the inequality follows from nonnegativity. If \(x=y\), it is equality. Otherwise, when \(a(x),a(y)>0\), write \(s=(1-\theta)x+\theta y\) and \(t=\theta x+(1-\theta)y\) for some \(\theta\in[0,1]\). For \(0<\theta<1\), multiplying the two log-concavity inequalities gives the claim; for \(\theta=0,1\), it is equality. Thus the inward-pair inequality includes zero weights and needs no division by a weight.

Fix an arbitrary integer center \(m\). Replace the function by \(x\mapsto a(x+m)\) and henceforth denote its lattice values by \(a_k\). This translation preserves \(W,L,R,M\), and its kernel \(S_0\) equals the original \(S_m\). In this notation \(L=\sum_{p\ge1}a_{-p}\), \(R=\sum_{q\ge1}a_q\), and \(M=\sum_{r\ge1}\min(a_{-r},a_r)\).

Partition all positive integer pairs \((p,q)\) according to \(r=\min(p,q)\). Within each shell, use the disjoint cases \(q=r,p\ge r\) and \(p=r,q>r\); the diagonal pair \((r,r)\) occurs only in the first case. Consequently,

\[
LR
=\sum_{r\ge1}\left(
\sum_{p\ge r}a_{-p}a_r
+\sum_{q>r}a_{-r}a_q
\right).
\]

For a fixed \(r\ge1\), suppose first that \(a_{-r}\le a_r\). If \(p\ge r\), both \(-r\) and \(2r-p\) belong to \([-p,r]\), and their sum is \(-p+r\). The inward-pair inequality therefore gives \(a_{-p}a_r\le a_{-r}a_{2r-p}\). The other branch is already \(a_{-r}a_q\) for \(q>r\). The indices \(2r-p\), as \(p\) runs through \(r,r+1,\ldots\), run through every integer at most \(r\), exactly once. The indices \(q>r\) run through the remaining integers, exactly once. Thus this shell is at most \(a_{-r}W=W\min(a_{-r},a_r)\).

If instead \(a_r<a_{-r}\), retain \(a_{-p}a_r\) for \(p\ge r\). For \(q>r\), the points \(r\) and \(q-2r\) both belong to \([-r,q]\) and have sum \(-r+q\). Hence \(a_{-r}a_q\le a_ra_{q-2r}\). Now \(-p\), for \(p\ge r\), covers exactly the integers at most \(-r\), while \(q-2r\), for \(q>r\), covers exactly the integers at least \(1-r\). These disjoint sets again exhaust \(\mathbb Z\). The shell is therefore at most \(a_rW=W\min(a_{-r},a_r)\).

Summing the shell bounds proves \(LR\le W\sum_{r\ge1}\min(a_{-r},a_r)=WM\). All series have nonnegative terms, so the product expansion, shell partition, reindexings, and summation of inequalities are valid by Tonelli's theorem for series. Moreover, \(L,R\le W<\infty\) and \(M\le\min(L,R)\), so the quantities are finite. This proves the tail inequality for finite or infinite support, including centers of zero weight.

To establish existence of a median, let \(F(j)=\sum_{k\le j}a_k\) for the original unshifted function. Summability gives \(F(j)\to0\) as \(j\to-\infty\) and \(F(j)\to W\) as \(j\to+\infty\). Thus the set of integers with \(F(j)\ge W/2\) is nonempty and bounded below. Its smallest element \(m\) satisfies \(\sum_{k<m}a_k=F(m-1)<W/2\) and \(\sum_{k>m}a_k=W-F(m)\le W/2\).

Every integer median has positive weight. Indeed, if a median had \(a_m=0\), its two tails would obey \(L+R=W\) and \(L,R\le W/2\), forcing \(L=R=W/2>0\). There would then be integers \(i<m<j\) with \(a_i,a_j>0\). Log-concavity applied to \(m\) as a strict convex combination of \(i,j\) would give \(a_m>0\), a contradiction. This also covers medians at the equality boundaries of the defining inequalities.

Now take any integer median and translate it to zero as above. Put \(u=a_0\). Then \(W=u+L+R\), and the median conditions are equivalent to \(|L-R|\le u\). Pairing the positive and negative indices gives \(S_0=u+2M\). The tail inequality yields the following calculation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2W\left(S_0-\frac W2\right)
&=2uW+4WM-W^2\\
&\ge2uW+4LR-W^2\\
&=u^2-(L-R)^2\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(W>0\), this proves \(S_m=S_0\ge W/2\) for the original median.

For the product assertion, each median satisfies \(b_i(m_i)>0\) by the one-dimensional result, so \(a_{\mathbf m}>0\). For any nonnegative numbers \(u_i,v_i\), the product \(\prod_i\min(u_i,v_i)\) is bounded above by each of \(\prod_i u_i\) and \(\prod_i v_i\). Therefore \(\min(\prod_i u_i,\prod_i v_i)\ge\prod_i\min(u_i,v_i)\). Applying this pointwise and then factoring nonnegative sums gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathbf m}
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\prod_{i=1}^n\min\bigl(b_i(z_i),b_i(2m_i-z_i)\bigr)\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^n\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\min\bigl(b_i(k),b_i(2m_i-k)\bigr)\\
&\ge\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{W_i}{2}
=2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here Tonelli's theorem also gives \(W=\prod_{i=1}^nW_i\in(0,\infty)\); all product-factor sums are consequently finite.

Finally, take \(a(x)=\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(x)\) in dimension one. This is log-concave, its only nonzero lattice weights are \(a_0=a_1=1\), and \(W=2\). Its integer medians are \(0,1\), with \(S_0=S_1=1\); every other integer center has zero kernel mass. Thus \(1/2\) cannot be improved. In dimension \(n\), take the product \(a(\mathbf x)=\mathbf1_{[0,1]^n}(\mathbf x)\). Then \(W=2^n\). If \(\mathbf m\in\{0,1\}^n\), the conditions \(\mathbf z,2\mathbf m-\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^n\) force \(\mathbf z=\mathbf m\) coordinate by coordinate, so \(S_{\mathbf m}=1\). If an integer center lies outside \(\{0,1\}^n\), no such reflected pair exists and its kernel mass is zero. Hence \(\max_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n}S_{\mathbf m}=1=2^{-n}W\), proving sharpness in the product class.
