## Statement

Put $T=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le1\}$, $V=|T|_4=1/24$, $r=1/10$, $\epsilon=r^4$, $B=rT$, and $D=\operatorname{conv}((\{-1\}\times B)\cup(\{0\}\times T)\cup(\{1\}\times B))$. Let $K=\operatorname{diag}(3+2\epsilon,1,1,1)T$. Consider
$$
C_{\mathrm{bin}}=[0,1]\times D,\qquad
C_{\mathrm{fac}}=\operatorname{conv}((\{0\}\times D)\cup(\{1\}\times\{0\}\times K)).
$$
These are full-dimensional rational convex bodies in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^4$ with all sampled fibers positive. The six fibers of $C_{\mathrm{bin}}$ are $B,T,B$ in each new layer, with total $M_{\mathrm{bin}}=2(1+2\epsilon)V$. The four fibers of $C_{\mathrm{fac}}$ are $B,T,B$ over $(0,-1),(0,0),(0,1)$, and $K$ over $(1,0)$, with total $M_{\mathrm{fac}}=4(1+\epsilon)V$. Write $\gamma_4=(4/5)^4$. At base $(0,0)$ the normalized marginal depths are $10001/20004<1/2$ and $1/4$, respectively. Over that entire base fiber, the normalized mixed depth has maximum strictly below $\gamma_4(10001/20004)$ in $C_{\mathrm{bin}}$ and strictly below $\gamma_4/4$ in $C_{\mathrm{fac}}$. Thus the first refutes lifting to $\gamma_d\min(1/2,\beta)$ at a specified base of marginal depth $\beta$, and the second refutes lifting at every specified base of marginal depth at least $2^{-n}$ to $\gamma_d 2^{-n}$.

## Proof

The projection of $D$ is $[-1,1]$. The extreme fibers are exactly $B$, while the middle fiber contains $T$ and is contained in $T$ by convexity of $T$ and $B\subset T$. The four continuous directions of $T$ and one endpoint base difference give full dimension. The exact two-layer census of w32-two-layer-depth-transfer proves the stated fibers after either padding, including the absence of further integer fibers. The mass of $B$ is $\epsilon V$ and that of $K$ is $(3+2\epsilon)V$, giving the claimed totals. The old marginal depth at zero is $\beta=(1+\epsilon)/(1+2\epsilon)$ because a closed tail through zero contains the central site and at least one endpoint, and either orientation of through-zero tail takes exactly that mass. The two transfer factors yield $\beta/2=10001/20004$ and $(1+\epsilon)V/M_{\mathrm{fac}}=1/4$.

Write the barycentric coordinates on $T$ as $\lambda_i(x)=x_i$ for $1\le i\le4$ and $\lambda_0(x)=1-\sum_{i=1}^4x_i$. The required raw comparison threshold is the same for both paddings, namely $\tau V$ with $\tau=\gamma_4(1+\epsilon)=160016/390625$. For each $x\in T$ and $i=0,\ldots,4$, use the proper closed containing caps
$$
\lambda_i(w)-2u\ge\lambda_i(x)\quad\text{in }C_{\mathrm{bin}},
\qquad
\lambda_i(w)-4u\ge\lambda_i(x)\quad\text{in }C_{\mathrm{fac}},
$$
where $u$ is the new integer coordinate and $w$ is the continuous coordinate of a tested point. In the product's added layer $\lambda_i(w)\le1$. In the facet's added layer each $\lambda_i(w)\le3+2\epsilon<4$, using $w\in K$ and $\lambda_0(w)\le1$. Thus both families of caps strictly miss their added layer. Their common old middle trace is the simplex homothet about vertex $i$ of factor $1-\lambda_i(x)$, giving mass $(1-\lambda_i(x))^4V$.

Suppose all five caps at a given $x$ had raw mass at least $\tau V$. On the endpoint fibers $\lambda_i\le r$ for $i=1,\ldots,4$, so for $\lambda_i(x)>r$ the endpoint contributions vanish and $\lambda_i(x)\le1-\tau^{1/4}$. For $\lambda_i(x)\le r$ this bound holds because $\tau<(1-r)^4$. The $i=0$ cap receives at most $2\epsilon V$ from the endpoints, so $\lambda_0(x)\le1-(\tau-2\epsilon)^{1/4}$. The rational witnesses $a=8000199/10^7$ and $b=7999223/10^7$ satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau-a^4&=2032792963819263760799/10^{28}>0,\\
\tau-2\epsilon-b^4&=664183074753367294559/10^{28}>0,\\
4a+b-4&=19/10^7>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
It follows that $4\tau^{1/4}+(\tau-2\epsilon)^{1/4}>4$, so summing the five barycentric upper bounds gives a sum strictly below one, impossible. Thus at every $x$ at least one cap is strictly below the threshold. Each cap mass is continuous in $x$ because the continuous normal is nonzero and the sampled fibers have full continuous dimension. Their minimum is continuous on the compact fiber $T$ and has maximum strictly below $\tau V$. The actual depth is bounded by this minimum. Its own maximum exists by upper semicontinuity, obtained for any fixed finite law by bounding moving through-point caps by fixed parallel enlargements and taking their closed limit.

For scope, the centroid in any of the two product fibers of volume $V$ pays at least $\gamma_4V$ from that fiber alone by the full-volume case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d. This exceeds $\gamma_4 M_{\mathrm{bin}}/4$. In the facet example the centroid of the added fiber pays at least $\gamma_4(3+2\epsilon)V>\gamma_4 M_{\mathrm{fac}}/4$ by the same argument. In both cases a containing cap restricts to a containing continuous halfspace or the entire fiber.
