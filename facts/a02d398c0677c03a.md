## Statement

There exists a full-dimensional rational polytope $C\subset\mathbb R^{209}\times\mathbb R^{1178}$ with positive finite mixed-integer volume $\mu(C)$ such that every point of $\mathbb Z^{209}\times\mathbb R^{1178}$ lies in a proper closed halfspace $H$ satisfying $\mu(C\cap H)/\mu(C)<36787944/(10^8\,2^{209})<1/(e\,2^{209})$. Here $\mu$ sums $1178$-dimensional fiber volumes over the integer bases.

Counterexample to the specific constant 1/e in total dimension 1387. The locked existence claim for a smaller positive universal constant remains open.

## Proof

# A rational counterexample in total dimension 1387

We construct a full-dimensional rational polytope
$C\subset\mathbb R^{209}\times\mathbb R^{1178}$ such that, for every
$x\in\mathbb Z^{209}\times\mathbb R^{1178}$, there is a proper closed
halfspace $H_x$ containing $x$ with

$$
\frac{\mu(C\cap H_x)}{\mu(C)}
<\frac{36787944}{10^8\,2^{209}}
<\frac1{e\,2^{209}}.
\tag{1}
$$

Here $\mu(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{209}}\operatorname{vol}_{1178}(A_z)$,
where $A_z=\{v:(z,v)\in A\}$. Thus (1) is a uniform upper bound on
the normalized mixed-integer halfspace depth, including at boundary
points and at points outside $C$.

All parameters defining $C$ are rational. The generating points,
the exact vertex set, every integer fiber, and the halfspaces used in
the proof are described below. The accompanying [checker](../counterexamples/one-over-e-1387/check.py)
verifies the finite selector identities and the exact arithmetic
inequalities. No data outside this directory are needed.

## 1. Parameters and continuous polytopes

Throughout, put

$$
b=19,\qquad d=1178,\qquad f=d-3=1175,\qquad
K=2048,\quad N=2047,\quad Q=220,\quad U=1827.
$$

There are $b$ integer blocks of dimension eleven, so $n=11b=209$.
Block indices are $0,\ldots,18$ and owner labels are $0,1,2,3$.
For a nonnegative integer $m$, write
$\Gamma_m=(m/(m+1))^m$ when $m>0$. Set
$\tau=3/32$, $\beta=27/64$, and $H=\tau\Gamma_f$.

Use centered barycentric coordinates
$x=(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and $y=(y_0,\ldots,y_f)$, with
$\sum x_l=\sum y_q=0$. Dropping $x_3$ and $y_f$ identifies their
product hyperplane rationally with $\mathbb R^d$. In these coordinates
let

$$
B=\{(x,y):x_l\ge-1/4\ (0\le l\le3),\quad
                 y_q\ge-1/(f+1)\ (0\le q\le f)\}.
$$

Thus $B$ is the product of centered standard simplices of dimensions
three and $f$. Its volume in the dropped coordinates is
$|B|=1/(3!f!)$. All fiber masses below are measured in units of $|B|$.
For owner $l$ define
$T_l=B\cap\{x_j\le0:j\ne l\}$. Its first factor is the cube given
by $0\le\lambda_j\le1/4$ for $j\ne l$, where
$\lambda_j=x_j+1/4$. Consequently

$$
\frac{|T_l|}{|B|}=\frac{(1/4)^3}{1/3!}=\tau,
\qquad
\frac{|B\cap\{x_l\ge0\}|}{|B|}=(3/4)^3=\beta.
\tag{2}
$$

The vertices of $B$ are the pairs
$(e_l-\tfrac14\mathbf1,e_q-\tfrac1{f+1}\mathbf1)$.
The vertices of $T_l$ are obtained by choosing
$\epsilon_j\in\{0,1\}$ for $j\ne l$ and $q\in\{0,\ldots,f\}$,
and setting

$$
x_j=(\epsilon_j-1)/4\ (j\ne l),\qquad
x_l=3/4-\sum_{j\ne l}\epsilon_j/4,\qquad
y=e_q-\tfrac1{f+1}\mathbf1.
\tag{3}
$$

In particular $T_l$ has $8(f+1)$ rational vertices. On $T_l$ its
owner score $x_l$ is nonnegative, every other score is nonpositive,
and every score lies in $[-1/4,3/4]$.

The radii use the primary grid $G=10^{14}$. For each
$k\in\{55,\ldots,70\}$ let $p_k$ be the largest integer
$p\in[G/2,G]$ satisfying

$$
\bigl(27p^d+6k(2p-G)^d\bigr)(f+1)^f
\le6f^fG^d,
\qquad r_k=\frac{p_k-1}{G}.
\tag{4}
$$

This is an explicit finite integer definition, equivalently the binary
search in `check.py`. The left side is strictly increasing on this
interval. Its value at $G/2$ satisfies the inequality, while its value
at $G$ does not. The subtraction of one in the numerator is part of
the construction.

The inactive-fiber mass uses a separate grid $G_*=10^{16}$:

$$
m_*=
\frac{\left\lfloor G_*H/\Gamma_d\right\rfloor-1}{G_*}.
\tag{5}
$$

In the dropped $d$ coordinates define the rational simplex

$$
\Sigma=\operatorname{conv}
\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_{d-1},m_*\tbinom d3 e_d\}.
\tag{6}
$$

Its volume divided by $|B|$ is $m_*$, since
$\binom d3=d!/(3!f!)$. The exact arithmetic in Section 8 gives
$m_*>0$ and $1/2<r_k<1$, so every continuous polytope used here is
full-dimensional.

## 2. An eleven-dimensional integer block

A local block is $z=(v,s)\in\mathbb R^8\times\mathbb R^3$.
Put $u=\sum_{t=0}^7v_t$ and define four rows and a guard by

$$
A(z)=(u-s_0,u-s_1,u-s_2,u),\qquad
g(z)=\sum_{l=0}^3A_l(z).
\tag{7}
$$

The eight sign forms on the selector coordinates have coefficient
vectors

$$
L_0,\ldots,L_3=(2,2,-3),\qquad
L_4,L_5,L_6=(-2,-2,3),\qquad L_7=(-2,-2,5).
\tag{8}
$$

None vanishes on a nonzero Boolean selector.
The finite set $V\subset\mathbb Z^{11}$ is defined as follows.
At selector zero include all $(v,0)$ with $v\in\{0,1\}^8$.
For each nonzero $s\in\{0,1\}^3$ and each Boolean mask
$a\in\{0,1\}^8$, start with
$v_t=\operatorname{sign}(L_t(s))a_t$ and retain exactly one of
$(v,s)$ and $-(v,s)$: the one with positive guard.

This choice is unique because $g(v,s)=4u-|s|\ne0$.
If $u\le0$, every row in (7) is nonpositive; if $u\ge1$, every
row is nonnegative. Hence

$$
A(z)\ge0\quad(z\in V),\qquad
g(z)\ge1\quad(z\in V\setminus\{0\}),\qquad g(0)=0.
\tag{9}
$$

Changing signs does not change parity. There is exactly one element
of $V$ in every parity class of $\mathbb Z^{11}$, so $|V|=2^{11}=K$.
Call $z\in V$ eligible when $g(z)=1$, and let $E$ be the eligible set.
Its row vector is one of the four coordinate vectors. The owner is
the index $l$ with $A_l(z)=1$.

Here is an explicit coding of the eligible elements. Set

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^{(0)}=d^{(1)}&=(1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0),\\
d^{(2)}&=(0,0,0,0,1,1,1,1),\\
d^{(3)}&=(0,0,0,0,1,1,1,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

For every $w\in\{0,1\}^8$ with $\sum w_t=4$, define

$$
e_l(w)=
\begin{cases}
(w-d^{(l)},-e_l),&l=0,1,2,\\
(w-d^{(3)},(1,1,1)),&l=3.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
$$

These are precisely the eligible elements of owner $l$. Indeed,
$g=1$ forces a negative singleton selector with original ordinary
sum zero, or a positive triple selector with ordinary sum one.
Complementing the four folded positions in the singleton cases, or
the three folded positions in the triple case, gives (10). Thus every
owner has $\binom84=70$ eligible sites.

An eligible site is **high** if $w_0+w_1>0$ and is a **tail** otherwise.
There are $70-\binom64=55$ high codes and 15 tail codes per owner.
There are therefore $Q=220$ high sites, 60 tail sites, and
$U=N-Q=1827$ nonzero nonhigh sites.

For every nonzero $z\in V$, let $\ell(z)$ be the least index with
$A_{\ell(z)}(z)>0$; put $\ell(0)=0$. This agrees with the owner on $E$.
The coded bits have linear extensions

$$
c_t(z)=v_t+\sum_{l=0}^3d_t^{(l)}A_l(z).
\tag{11}
$$

On $e_l(w)$ they equal $w_t$, and on $-e_l(w)$ they equal $-w_t$.
Write $J=c_0+c_1$ and $L=\sum_{t=2}^72^{t-2}c_t$.
For block $i\in\{0,\ldots,18\}$ define the rational linear priority

$$
h_i(z)=100\,3^{18-i}J(z)+L(z)+\frac{19-i}{20}g(z).
\tag{12}
$$

On eligible sites, all high scores in an earlier block exceed all high
scores in a later block, and every high score exceeds every tail score.
For example, successive high scales differ by a factor of three,
whereas $J\in\{1,2\}$ on highs and $0\le L\le63$.
The smallest scale is 100, so the bands are disjoint. Tails are ordered
by decreasing binary score $L$, then by increasing block index.
Priorities in different blocks cannot tie: their fractional parts are
the distinct numbers $(19-i)/20$.

## 3. Baselines, lights, and their sources

Every tuple $z=(z_0,\ldots,z_{18})\in V^b$ is a baseline.
Its sector label is
$\ell(z)=\sum_i\ell(z_i)\pmod4$, represented in $\{0,1,2,3\}$.
A light is obtained from a baseline by negating one eligible block.
Use exactly the following selection rules.

* At an all-nonzero baseline, negate the eligible block of greatest
  priority (12), if there is an eligible block. Equivalently, negate
  the least-index high block if one exists; otherwise choose the
  greatest-priority eligible tail.
* At a baseline with exactly one zero block $j$, let $S$ be the set of
  high blocks. If $|S|\ge2$, put $T=S\cup\{j\}$ and let its first
  three indices be $a_1<a_2<a_3$. Add no light when $j=a_1$; negate
  block $a_3$ when $j=a_2$; otherwise negate block $a_1$.
* If such a one-zero baseline has exactly one high block $r<j$, the
  preceding rule is replaced by the following tail rule. Among eligible
  tails in blocks $i$ satisfying $(i<r\text{ or }i>j)$, negate the one of greatest priority,
  if any exists.

There are no other lights. Each baseline produces at most one, and
each light determines its baseline partner uniquely by undoing its
unique negative-guard block.

For a finite index set $T$ of size at least three and $j\in T$, denote
the high-rule choice by $f_T(j)$, with $f_T(a_1)$ undefined. Its arrows
$j\to f_T(j)$ are acyclic: give $a_1$ rank 0, $a_3$ rank 1, and all
other elements rank 2; every arrow decreases rank. In particular there
are no reverse arrows.

We next define active centers and their pools; these definitions use
only the finite integer data above.

An **ordinary active center** has exactly one zero block $i$ and no
high block of index less than $i$. Its pool consists of the eligible
values for block $i$ that beat every eligible fixed block under (12).
Every pool contains all 55 high codes per owner, and possibly an initial
segment of the 15 tail codes in their priority order. Write $k$ for its
number of codes per owner, so $55\le k\le70$.

For a baseline with exactly two zero blocks $i,j$, let $R$ be its set
of fixed high blocks. It is an **extra active center** if $R\ne\varnothing$
and one of $f_{R\cup\{i,j\}}(j)=i$ and
$f_{R\cup\{i,j\}}(i)=j$ holds. At most one holds. In the first case
call $i$ the varying block and $j$ the sentinel. Its pool contains every
high code in block $i$. Tail codes can also belong to the pool only
when $R=\{r\}$, $r<j$, and $(i<r\text{ or }i>j)$. They are exactly the tail
codes whose priority at $i$ exceeds every fixed eligible tail in
$\{t:t<r\text{ or }t>j\}\setminus\{i\}$.
Again this gives $k\in\{55,\ldots,70\}$ codes per owner.

The equivalence

$$
f_{\{r,i,j\}}(j)=i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r<j\ \hbox{and}\ (i<r\ \hbox{or}\ i>j)
\tag{13}
$$

follows by ordering the three indices. Thus every tail light also
belongs to the extra active center just described. Conversely, every
eligible value in any active pool produces the corresponding light.
Pools are balanced across owners because all selection comparisons use
the coded mask and the block index, not its owner.

Prescribe the following continuous sources:

| Integer base | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Active center with pool size $k$ | $r_kB$ |
| All-nonzero baseline | $T_{\ell(z)}$ |
| Inactive one-zero baseline | $T_{\ell(z)}$ |
| Every other inactive baseline | $\Sigma$ |
| Light associated with a pool of size $k$ | $(2r_k-1)T_{\ell(z^+)}$, where $z^+$ is its baseline partner |

Let $C$ be the convex hull of these lifted sources. Equations
(3), (4), (6), and (10) give a finite rational generating-point list.
The exact vertex list is obtained by omitting the active-center
generating points, as proved in Section 6.

Every baseline partner of an extra light is an inactive one-zero
baseline. To see this for a high arrow $j\to i$, observe that the
fixed high set contains an index below $j$: it contains $a_1$ when
$j=a_2,i=a_3$, and contains $a_2$ otherwise. The same assertion is
part of the tail rule. Such an earlier high prevents every ordinary
eligible value at $j$ from winning. This fact will also identify the
noncenter sources in extra active slices.

## 4. The complete integer census and elementary exposures

We use the following finite lexicographic observation. Successive
rational linear maximizations on a finite set can be replaced by one
rational linear maximization with the same final maximizing set.
Clear denominators in the objectives, bound their absolute values by
an integer $B_0$, and combine them with successive powers of
$2B_0+2$. A one-unit improvement in an earlier objective exceeds the
sum of all possible later losses. Minimization is maximization of
the negative objective. This also makes every exposing form used below
constructively rational.

Let $P_{\rm loc}=\operatorname{conv}(V\cup(-E))$.
Its integer points are exactly $V\cup(-E)$, and every nonzero one
is exposed. Here is a direct proof.

All coordinates of its generators lie in $[-1,1]$. A nonzero integer
selector has a coordinate equal to $1$ or $-1$. Saturating that
coordinate forces all contributing selectors to have its sign, so
their remaining coordinates lie in the same signed Boolean cube.
The integral target selector then forces each selector coordinate.
At that selector the ordinary coordinates lie in the signed cube
specified by (8). An integral ordinary target forces every coordinate
of its cube vertex, and therefore must be a listed generator.
These saturations, in order, expose the site.

If the target selector is zero and an ordinary target coordinate is
$\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}$, first saturate that coordinate. Every remaining
generator with nonzero selector $s'$ satisfies
$\epsilon L_t(s')>0$ at that coordinate $t$. Their average therefore
cannot have selector zero unless all their weights vanish. If
$\epsilon=-1$ there is no zero-selector generator left, a contradiction.
If $\epsilon=1$, minimizing $L_t(s')$ forces selector zero; the ordinary
Boolean coordinates can then be exposed individually. The only
remaining integer target is the origin, which is already in $V$.
This proves the local assertion, including its exposure statement.

The integer projection of $C$ is contained in $P_{\rm loc}^b$.
At an integer target, each nonzero local block can therefore be fixed
by its local exposing form. If a fixed block is in $-E$, all compatible
generators are lights flipped in that block. Their other guards are
nonnegative, and each such light has at most one zero block.
Minimizing the guards at any remaining zero slots forces precisely
those zeros. Thus the target is one prescribed light, with at most one
zero slot, and is exposed. Two fixed negative blocks are impossible.

If there is no negative block, every block lies in $V$, so the target
is a prescribed baseline. All-nonzero baselines are exposed by fixing
all their blocks. We have proved the exact integer census:

$$
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^{209}}(C)\cap\mathbb Z^{209}
=V^b\ \cup\ \{\text{the prescribed lights}\}.
\tag{14}
$$

In particular there are no additional integer bases with zero-volume
fibers. Each listed source has positive volume.

At a one-zero target with zero block $j$, fix every other block.
No extra light is compatible: its sentinel or its negative block
would disagree with one of those fixed blocks. The only possible
negative guard at $j$ comes from an ordinary light flipped there.
If none exists, minimizing $g_j$ exposes the baseline. Otherwise this
is exactly the ordinary active slice, already exposed as a slice by
the fixed blocks.

At a two-zero target with zeros $i,j$, fix all outsiders. Extra lights
on the two axes exist exactly when there is a high arrow between
$i,j$; (13) shows that a tail light can use an axis only when that
same high arrow exists. If $j\to i$, there is a fixed high below $j$.
No ordinary light can flip $j$, whatever the value at $i$.
No extra high or tail light can flip $j$ with sentinel $i$, since that
would require the reverse arrow. Hence $g_j\ge0$ on the fixed-outsider
face. Minimizing $g_j$ exposes the slice with $j=0$ and $i$ varying.
This is the extra active slice.

If neither arrow exists, there are no compatible extra lights.
Minimize $g_i+g_j$ on the fixed-outsider face. Its minimum is zero.
Besides the baseline target, only ordinary lights with guards
$(-1,1)$ or $(1,-1)$ can remain. Both sites were eligible in their
partner, and the flipped one had strictly greater original priority.
Thus $h_i+h_j<0$ at each such light, while it is zero at the target.
Maximizing $h_i+h_j$ exposes this inactive baseline.

## 5. Exposing baselines with three or more zero blocks

Fix the nonzero blocks of an inactive baseline and let $Z$ be its
zero slots. Minimize $\sum_{i\in Z}g_i$. A compatible ordinary light
has guard sum at least $|Z|-2$. A compatible extra light has one
sentinel in $Z$ and guard sum at least $|Z|-3$. Baselines have
nonnegative guards, vanishing only at zero. Consequently, when
$|Z|\ge4$ this exposes the baseline immediately.

Suppose $Z=\{a,b,c\}$ with $a<b<c$. After the guard minimization,
an additional generator can only be an extra light with one sentinel
and two eligible sites, of which one is negated. We construct linear
priorities $\phi_a,\phi_b,\phi_c$ such that the negated site always
has greater priority than the other eligible site. Then
$\sum_{i\in Z}\phi_i$ is strictly negative on every such light and
zero at the baseline target, and its maximization exposes the target.

Let $R$ be the set of fixed high blocks outside $Z$.

**No fixed high.** For two high sites, sentinel $b$ selects $c$ over
$a$, sentinel $c$ selects $a$ over $b$, and sentinel $a$ selects
nothing. For one high and one tail, the only possible comparisons are
a tail at $c$ over a high at $a$ with sentinel $b$, and a tail at $a$
over a high at $b$ with sentinel $c$. With two tails there is no light.
Thus putting the entire eligible bands in order $c>a>b$ suffices.
For example, use $\phi_i=100J+L+1000q_i g$ with
$q_c=2,q_a=1,q_b=0$.

**At least one fixed high.** A high among the two eligible sites
cannot lose to a tail: their baseline then has at least two highs,
so the high rule applies. For two high sites, all comparisons use
the same index set $R\cup Z$. Each pair of zero slots has a unique
sentinel, so there is no pair of opposite comparisons. A directed
three-cycle among these comparisons would give a directed three-cycle
among the sentinel arrows: the sentinel of each comparison is the
third zero slot. The arrow rule is acyclic, so the high comparisons
admit a topological order. This argument applies to every number of
fixed highs, not only to one or two.

If $|R|\ge2$, two tails cannot produce a compatible light: the high
rule would have to flip a fixed outsider. There are no tail-tail
comparisons to satisfy. If $R=\{r\}$, all tail-tail comparisons are
realized as follows, using the original tail order $L+(19-i)/20$.

| Position of $r$ | Tail ordering to use |
| --- | --- |
| $r>c$ | No tail-tail comparisons occur. |
| $b<r<c$ | Interleave the tails at $a,b$ in their original order. |
| $a<r<b$ | Interleave $a,c$ in their original order, above the entire tail band at $b$. |
| $r<a$ | Interleave $b,c$ in their original order, above the entire tail band at $a$. |

For example, in the third row sentinel $b$ allows both $a,c$ as tail
candidates, whereas sentinel $c$ allows only $a$. In the last row,
sentinel $a$ allows both $b,c$, sentinel $b$ allows only $c$, and
sentinel $c$ allows neither. With only one zero slot above $r$ there
is at most the one pair shown in the second row. Fixed tail competitors
can suppress a light but cannot add a different comparison.

These orders have explicit linear realizations. Give the interleaved
bands the same offset and a lower band an offset smaller by 100;
write the offsets as $q_i\in\{0,100\}$. Let $t_i\in\{0,1,2\}$
be a topological ranking of the high-comparison graph, increasing
toward every required winner. Set

$$
\phi_i=1000\,3^{t_i}J+L+
\left(q_i+\frac{19-i}{20}\right)g.
\tag{15}
$$

All tail scores lie below 164. Each high band lies above all tails,
and a high band of greater rank lies wholly above a lower one because
$1000\,3^{t+1}>2\cdot1000\,3^t+164$.
The two high-versus-tail possibilities and all tail-tail comparisons
therefore hold simultaneously. Linearity of (11) makes the score at
a negated eligible site the negative of its positive score. This
completes the three-zero exposure proof in all fixed-high cases.

Combining these maximizations with those fixing the outsiders, using
the finite lexicographic observation, gives a rational exposing form.
We have proved that every baseline other than an active center is
exposed, and that every active slice is exposed as a slice.

## 6. Actual fibers, exact vertices, and full dimension

Every nonactive prescribed base is exposed. Its actual fiber in $C$
is therefore exactly its prescribed source.

Consider an ordinary or extra active slice with varying block $i$,
pool size $k$, and radius $r=r_k$. At an extra slice the sentinel
$j$ is zero and the other outsiders include a high below $j$.
Every noncenter baseline in this slice is consequently an inactive
one-zero baseline, and hence has a unit sector source. At an ordinary
slice the noncenter baselines are all-nonzero and likewise have unit
sectors. The only lights in either slice are the $4k$ pool lights.

On the slice define the affine radius envelope
$R(z)=r+(1-r)g_i(z)$. It equals $2r-1$ on a light, equals $r$ at
the center, and is at least one on a noncenter baseline. Every source
in the slice satisfies the affine inequalities

$$
x_l\ge-R(z)/4\quad(0\le l\le3),\qquad
y_q\ge-R(z)/(f+1)\quad(0\le q\le f).
\tag{16}
$$

They hold throughout its convex hull. At the center $g_i=0$, (16)
gives precisely $rB$ as an upper bound on the actual fiber, and its
prescribed source gives the reverse inclusion. Thus the actual center
fiber is exactly $r_kB$. This proves all actual-fiber assertions, not
just inclusions for the sources.

There is also a useful exact description of the vertices. Let $c$ be
the fixed blocks' label sum modulo four. For each owner $l$, choose a
winning eligible value of that owner. Its baseline and light have
opposite varying bases and sources $T_{l+c}$ and $(2r-1)T_{l+c}$.
Their midpoint contains $rT_{l+c}$. Moreover,
$\operatorname{conv}(T_0\cup T_1\cup T_2\cup T_3)=B$:
each tetrahedron vertex, together with the entire free simplex,
belongs to its corresponding sector.

In particular every generating vertex of an active-center $rB$ is
the midpoint of two distinct lifted generating vertices. None is a
vertex of $C$. Every vertex of every other prescribed source is a
vertex of $C$, because its integer base is exposed. Since every vertex
of a finite convex hull belongs to its generating list, the actual
vertex set is exactly:

* the vertices (3) at every all-nonzero or inactive one-zero baseline;
* the vertices (6) at every other inactive baseline;
* the vertices (3), multiplied by $2r_k-1$, at every light in a
  pool of size $k$.

Thus the construction specifies the actual rational vertices, and the
active-center generators can be omitted without changing $C$.

Finally, $V$ contains the eight positive ordinary coordinate vectors
and the three negative selector coordinate vectors. Global baselines
with just one of these nonzero blocks, and the all-zero baseline, have
at least three zero blocks and carry $\Sigma$, which contains the
continuous origin. These bases span all 209 integer directions.
The all-zero fiber $\Sigma$ spans all $d$ continuous directions.
Hence $C$ is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^{209+d}$.

## 7. A proper closed cap at every mixed-integer point

We first record precisely how exposing tilts are used. If an exposed
base or slice is the zero face of an integer affine form $F\le0$
on the integer-coordinate projection, then every other integer base
satisfies $F\le-1$. Given a rational affine score $G(z,x,y)$ with a
nonzero continuous linear part, choose a positive integer $M>\max\{0,\max_C G\}$; the maximum is rational and can
be obtained from the
finite generating-point list. Then the proper closed halfspace

$$
G(z,x,y)+MF(z)\ge0
\tag{17}
$$

has trace $G\ge0$ on the exposed face and misses every integer fiber
off that face. The exposing forms above can be made integer by clearing
denominators and normalized to zero on the desired integer base or
slice. It is the integer exposing gap and the global score bound that
justify (17); negativity only at outside source vertices would not
suffice.

At an active center, let $i$ be its varying block and let $c$ be its
fixed label sum. For $l=0,1,2,3$, use on the active slice the score
$G_l=x_{l+c}-3A_l(z_i)$, with subscripts modulo four, and isolate it
by (17). These scores have nonzero continuous linear parts, so the
resulting halfspaces are proper. On the center the four thresholds are zero and
$\sum_lx_{l+c}=0$, so these four closed traces cover every center point.
Each has center mass $\beta r_k^d$ by (2).

A noncenter baseline has a unit sector. If $A_l>0$, its threshold is
at least 3, whereas every continuous score is at most $3/4$, so the
cap misses that source. If $A_l=0$, its local label differs from $l$;
the trace lies in a nonowner score hyperplane of its sector and has
zero $d$-volume. A light of owner $l$ has $A_l=-1$ and is captured
in full by row $l$, whose threshold is $-3$. Each other row meets it
only on a null score hyperplane. There are exactly $k$ lights per
owner. Thus each of the four center caps has mass

$$
\beta r_k^d+k\tau(2r_k-1)^d.
\tag{18}
$$

At a point of an exposed unit sector, some free coordinate $y_q$ is
nonnegative because their sum is zero. The closed trace $y_q\ge0$
contains the point and cuts the fraction $\Gamma_f$ of its free
simplex, by the elementary simplex cap formula. Its mass is exactly
$\tau\Gamma_f=H$. Use (17) to isolate it.

At a point of an inactive simplex $\Sigma$, some barycentric coordinate
is at least $1/(d+1)$. Its corresponding closed coordinate cap has
mass $\Gamma_dm_*$, and can again be isolated by (17).
At a light, its entire exposed fiber can be isolated by the proper
closed halfspace $F\ge0$, at mass $\tau(2r_k-1)^d$.

The simplex cap formula used here is immediate: in an $m$-simplex,
the region where one barycentric coordinate is at least $t$ is a
homothetic copy of ratio $1-t$, and hence has volume fraction
$(1-t)^m$. All traces above are closed. The coordinate-at-least-average
argument also applies on every face and at every vertex, and the four
center traces include their zero-score boundaries. Thus no boundary
points have been omitted.

An exterior point admits a proper closed halfspace disjoint from $C$
by strict separation. Since $C$ is a full-dimensional rational polytope,
one can also use a violated rational facet inequality and a rational
threshold strictly between the facet and the exterior point. Its mass
is zero. This covers every point outside $C$ as well.

## 8. Exact census, total mass, and the strict comparison with $e$

Let $O_{\ge k}$ and $E_{\ge k}$ count ordinary and extra active centers
whose pool contains at least $k$ codes per owner. Set
$O_{\ge71}=E_{\ge71}=0$. For ordinary centers,

$$
\begin{aligned}
O_{\ge55}&=\sum_{i=0}^{18}U^iN^{18-i}
          =\frac{N^{19}-U^{19}}Q,\\
O_{\ge k}&=\frac{(N-4(k-1))^{19}-(N-4k)^{19}}4
          \quad(56\le k\le70).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$

For the first identity, earlier blocks must be nonhigh and later blocks
may be arbitrary nonzero sites. For $k=55+t$, the tail of rank $t$
in block $i$ beats all fixed competitors exactly when earlier blocks
avoid the first $t$ tail ranks and later blocks avoid the first $t-1$,
with all highs excluded. Their numbers are $N-4k$ and $N-4(k-1)$.
Summing their products over $i$ gives the second identity.

For extra centers, each index set $T$ of size $t\ge3$ supplies $t-1$
arrows. An arrow's two endpoints are its zero slots; its remaining
$t-2$ blocks are high and all other fixed blocks are nonhigh. There are
no reverse arrows, so no center is counted twice. Therefore

$$
E_{\ge55}=\sum_{t=3}^{19}(t-1)\binom{19}{t}Q^{t-2}U^{19-t}.
\tag{20}
$$

Extra tails require a sole fixed high $r$ and sentinel $j>r$.
Their candidate axes are $i<r$ or $i>j$. If their number is
$m=r+18-j$, there are $m+1$ choices of $(r,j)$, and $1\le m\le17$
when a tail can be chosen. The fixed high and noncandidate blocks
give $Q U^{17-m}$ choices. At tail rank $t$, summing the products
before and after each of the $m$ candidate axes telescopes to
$[(U-4(t-1))^m-(U-4t)^m]/4$. Hence

$$
E_{\ge55+t}=\sum_{m=1}^{17}(m+1)Q U^{17-m}
 \frac{(U-4(t-1))^m-(U-4t)^m}{4}
\quad(1\le t\le15).
\tag{21}
$$

As a direct count check, the number of these extra tail lights is
both $4\sum_{t=1}^{15}E_{\ge55+t}$ and
$\sum_{m=1}^{17}(m+1)Q U^{17-m}(U^m-(U-60)^m)$.

Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_k&=O_{\ge k}-O_{\ge k+1}+E_{\ge k}-E_{\ge k+1},\\
R&=19N^{18}-O_{\ge55},\\
I&=K^{19}-N^{19}-R-O_{\ge55}-E_{\ge55}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$

Here $C_k$ counts all centers of pool size $k$, $R$ counts inactive
one-zero baselines, and $I$ counts the other inactive baselines.
Every light determines its center and one pool value, so the number
of lights is $\sum_k4kC_k$. All actual fibers were identified in
Sections 4–6. Thus their exact total mass is

$$
W=(N^{19}+R)\tau+Im_*+
\sum_{k=55}^{70}C_k\bigl(r_k^d+4k\tau(2r_k-1)^d\bigr).
\tag{23}
$$

All divisions in (19)–(21) are exact integer divisions, also expressible
as the geometric sums used in their derivations. The counts are
nonnegative and $R,I>0$.

For completeness, the exact rational comparisons used in the final
step are as follows, with the parameters (4)–(5):

$$
\begin{gathered}
\tfrac12<r_k<1,\qquad r_k^d<\tau<m_*,\\
\tau(2r_k-1)^d<H,\qquad
\beta r_k^d+k\tau(2r_k-1)^d<H,\qquad \Gamma_dm_*<H.
\end{gathered}
\tag{24}
$$

Define the rational number

$$
e_+=\sum_{j=0}^{32}\frac1{j!}+\frac1{32\cdot32!}.
$$

Every omitted exponential-series term is at most the corresponding
term of the geometric series with ratio $1/33$, with strict inequality
after the first omitted term. Its sum is $1/(32\cdot32!)$, so
$e<e_+$. Direct integer arithmetic using (4)–(5) and (19)–(23) gives

$$
\frac{W}{e_+K^{19}}-H>\frac{15}{10^{11}},
\qquad
\frac{HK^{19}}W<\frac{36787944}{10^8}<\frac1{e_+}.
\tag{25}
$$

These comparisons, including the floor-minus-one choices in both grids,
are checked without floating-point arithmetic by [check.py](../counterexamples/one-over-e-1387/check.py).
They are finite integer inequalities after clearing denominators.
The checker also verifies the local eligible census and the count
identities above. Equations (4), (5), and (19)–(23) specify every
integer and rational input, so (24)–(25) are reproducible independently
of any external parameter file.

By Section 7 and (24), every mixed-integer point has a containing
proper closed cap of mass at most $H$ in units of $|B|$.
Since $K^{19}=2^{209}$, (25) proves (1), uniformly over all such points.
The same inequalities show that $m_*$ is the largest fiber mass and
$\Gamma_dm_*<W/(e_+2^{209})$, consistent with the simplex centroid
bound. This completes the construction and the all-point proof.
