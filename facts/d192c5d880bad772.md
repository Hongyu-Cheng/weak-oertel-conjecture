## Statement

Let $L_i(z,x)=v_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$, $1\le i\le k$, be ambient affine cap margins on $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$. Suppose the continuous normals form a minimal positive dependence, with weights $\lambda_i>0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i u_i=0$. Then their rank is $k-1$, so $k\le d+1$. After rescaling the margins by the respective $\lambda_i$, there is a single affine shear $x'=x+Az-y$ fixing $z$ such that the rescaled margins are
$$
\lambda_iL_i(z,x)=\lambda_i u_i\cdot x'+\ell(z),\qquad
\ell(z)=k^{-1}\sum_i\lambda_i(v_i\cdot z-b_i).
$$
This shear preserves the mixed lattice, each cap incidence, and full mixed volume on the fibers.

At a fixed base $z$, the open caps cover the entire continuous plane exactly when $\ell(z)>0$. For $\ell(z)=0$ the closed caps cover the plane, and the open caps miss exactly the common kernel of the continuous normals in the new coordinates. For $\ell(z)<0$ the closed caps miss $x'=0$. If $k=d+1$ and $\ell(z)>0$, the intersection of the closed caps in that fiber is the scale-$\ell(z)$ copy of the simplex at common term $1$.

## Proof

Write $u_i'=\lambda_i u_i$, $v_i'=\lambda_i v_i$, and $b_i'=\lambda_i b_i$. The column matrix of the $u_i'$ has the positive kernel vector $(1,\ldots,1)$. If it had another independent kernel direction, move along that direction, with the sign chosen to make some coordinate decrease, until the first coordinate is zero. The resulting kernel vector is nonnegative and nonzero, with smaller support, contradicting minimality. Thus the image of $x\mapsto (u_i'\cdot x)_i$ is exactly the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane in $\mathbb R^k$. Each coordinate column of $(v_i'-\bar v)_i$, where $\bar v=k^{-1}\sum_i v_i'$, lies in that image, as does $(b_i'-\bar b)_i$ for $\bar b=k^{-1}\sum_i b_i'$. Consequently choose $A,y$ satisfying $u_i'\cdot Az=(v_i'-\bar v)\cdot z$ and $u_i'\cdot y=b_i'-\bar b$. Substitution gives the formula. The shear is invertible and acts on each fiber by translation, so it preserves cap incidences, the product lattice and mixed volume.

The rescaled margins sum to $k\ell(z)$. If $\ell(z)>0$, some margin is positive at every $x'$; if $\ell(z)=0$, some margin is nonnegative and all are nonpositive exactly when all vanish. This happens precisely on the common kernel. When $\ell(z)<0$, the origin misses every closed cap. Finally if $k=d+1$, the continuous normal map is bijective onto the sum-zero hyperplane. For $\ell(z)>0$, its affine translate $x'\mapsto (u_i'\cdot x'+\ell(z))_i$ maps the closed intersection bijectively onto the simplex $\{t_i\ge0:\sum_i t_i=k\ell(z)\}$. Equivalently in the new fiber coordinates the closed intersection is $\ell(z)\{x':u_i'\cdot x'+1\ge0\ \forall i\}$.
