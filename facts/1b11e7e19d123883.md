## Statement

Let \(n,d\) be positive integers, let \(Q=\{-1,0,1\}^n\), let \(\Gamma\) be a real symmetric positive-definite \(d\times d\) matrix, and let \(\eta\geq0\). For \(u\in\mathbb R^d\), write \(\|u\|_\Gamma=\|\Gamma^{-1/2}u\|_2\). Suppose \(f:Q\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfies

\[
\|f(z+v)+f(z-v)-2f(z)\|_\Gamma\leq\eta
\quad\text{whenever }z,z+v,z-v\in Q,
\]

where \(v\in\mathbb R^n\). Let \(e_i\) denote the \(i\)-th standard basis vector, set \(b=f(0)\), and define \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) by \(Ae_i=(f(e_i)-f(-e_i))/2\). If \(z\in Q\) has exactly \(m\) nonzero coordinates, then

\[
\|f(z)-Az-b\|_\Gamma
\leq
\begin{cases}
0,&m=0,\\
(m-\tfrac12)\eta,&m\geq1.
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\inf_{\substack{A'\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\\b'\in\mathbb R^d}}
\max_{z\in Q}\|f(z)-A'z-b'\|_\Gamma
\leq\max_{z\in Q}\|f(z)-Az-b\|_\Gamma
\leq(n-\tfrac12)\eta\leq\tfrac12 n^2\eta.
\]

There is also the following conditional stock interpretation. Put \(R=(n-\tfrac12)\eta\). For any set \(\rho\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), the invertible affine shear \(\Phi(z,y)=(z,y-Az-b)\) sends each stock fiber \(\{z\}\times(f(z)+\rho)\), for \(z\in Q\), to \(\{z\}\times(r(z)+\rho)\), where \(r(z)=f(z)-Az-b\) satisfies \(\|r(z)\|_\Gamma\leq R\). For every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), \(w\in\mathbb R^d\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), and \(z\in Q\), its halfspace thresholds obey

\[
\begin{aligned}
\{u\in\rho:a\cdot z+w\cdot u\geq t+R\sqrt{w^{\mathsf T}\Gamma w}\}
&\subseteq\{u\in\rho:a\cdot z+w\cdot(u+r(z))\geq t\}\\
&\subseteq\{u\in\rho:a\cdot z+w\cdot u\geq t-R\sqrt{w^{\mathsf T}\Gamma w}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These conclusions require no assumption on the existence or size of \(\rho\). If a common stock is separately known to lie in the fibers of a convex body, its inclusions persist under \(\Phi\); no positive volume ratio or depth lower bound follows from the present statement alone.

## Proof

The map \(\Gamma^{-1/2}\) is linear and invertible, so \(\|\cdot\|_\Gamma\) is a norm. Its triangle inequality and homogeneity follow immediately from those of the Euclidean norm. All estimates below are estimates in this norm, with no dependence on \(d\).

Define the odd part \(g:Q\to\mathbb R^d\) by \(g(z)=(f(z)-f(-z))/2\). Thus \(g(-z)=-g(z)\), \(g(0)=0\), and \(g(e_i)=Ae_i\). Applying the original midpoint condition at center \(0\) with direction \(z\) gives

\[
f(z)-b-g(z)=\tfrac12\bigl(f(z)+f(-z)-2f(0)\bigr),
\qquad \|f(z)-b-g(z)\|_\Gamma\leq\tfrac12\eta.
\]

The odd part also satisfies every reflected-midpoint bound with the same \(\eta\). Indeed, for every feasible \(z,v\), the points \(-z,-z+v,-z-v\) also lie in \(Q\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
g(z+v)+g(z-v)-2g(z)
={}&\tfrac12\bigl(f(z+v)+f(z-v)-2f(z)\bigr)\\
&-\tfrac12\bigl(f(-z+v)+f(-z-v)-2f(-z)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Each bracket has norm at most \(\eta\), so the displayed expression has norm at most \(\eta\).

We next prove approximate additivity for disjoint coordinate supports. Let \(p,q\in Q\) satisfy \(p_iq_i=0\) for every \(i\). This ensures \(p+q,p-q,q-p\in Q\). The midpoint bounds for \(g\) at centers \(p\) and \(q\), with directions \(q\) and \(p\), respectively, give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g(p+q)+g(p-q)-2g(p)\|_\Gamma&\leq\eta,\\
\|g(p+q)+g(q-p)-2g(q)\|_\Gamma&\leq\eta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(g(q-p)=-g(p-q)\), one-half of the sum of these two defect vectors is exactly \(g(p+q)-g(p)-g(q)\). The triangle inequality therefore proves
\(\|g(p+q)-g(p)-g(q)\|_\Gamma\leq\eta\).
These midpoint centers need not be \(0\). This is the step that uses the full face-center hypothesis rather than only central antipodal pairs.

Fix \(z\in Q\) with \(m\geq1\) nonzero coordinates, listed as \(i_1,\ldots,i_m\). Define \(p_k=\sum_{j=1}^k z_{i_j}e_{i_j}\) for \(1\leq k\leq m\). For each \(k\geq2\), the vectors \(p_{k-1}\) and \(z_{i_k}e_{i_k}\) have disjoint supports. The preceding estimate bounds the norm of \(g(p_k)-g(p_{k-1})-g(z_{i_k}e_{i_k})\) by \(\eta\). Telescoping, using \(p_m=z\), yields

\[
g(z)-\sum_{j=1}^m g(z_{i_j}e_{i_j})
=\sum_{k=2}^m\bigl(g(p_k)-g(p_{k-1})-g(z_{i_k}e_{i_k})\bigr).
\]

For \(m=1\), the right side is an empty sum and the identity still holds. Oddness and \(z_{i_j}\in\{-1,1\}\) give \(g(z_{i_j}e_{i_j})=z_{i_j}g(e_{i_j})\), so the sum on the left is \(Az\). Thus \(\|g(z)-Az\|_\Gamma\leq(m-1)\eta\). Combining this with \(\|f(z)-b-g(z)\|_\Gamma\leq\eta/2\) proves the asserted \((m-\tfrac12)\eta\) bound. At \(z=0\), the residual is exactly \(f(0)-b=0\). Taking the maximum and then the infimum gives the claimed universal affine-approximation bound. Finally, \(n-\tfrac12\leq n^2/2\) is equivalent to \((n-1)^2\geq0\).

For \(n=1\), the two nonzero residuals are both exactly \((f(1)+f(-1)-2f(0))/2\), and hence have norm at most \(\eta/2\). For \(\eta=0\), all the above residuals vanish, so \(f(z)=Az+b\) throughout \(Q\). These conclusions include every positive \(d\); the proof did not use scalar-valuedness or coordinatewise estimates in the target space.

To check the odd nonlinear example explicitly, suppose \(n\geq3\), choose \(u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), and set \(f(z)=u z_1z_2z_3\). All central antipodal defects vanish. Nevertheless, at center \(e_3\) with direction \(e_1+e_2\), the defect is \(2u\). In fact the largest defect norm over all feasible centers and directions is exactly \(2\|u\|_\Gamma\). To see this exhaustively, a coordinate of a feasible center equal to \(1\) or \(-1\) forces the corresponding direction coordinate to be \(0\); a center coordinate equal to \(0\) allows direction coordinate \(-1,0,1\). Among the first three coordinates, if none are zero then the defect vanishes. If at least one is zero, the center product vanishes. A zero direction coordinate at one of these zero center coordinates makes both endpoint products vanish. Otherwise, with an odd number of zero center coordinates the endpoint products cancel, and with two zero center coordinates they coincide and have absolute value \(1\). This proves the asserted maximum, including every face center.

For this example, \(A=0\) and \(b=0\), and the concrete residual radius is \(\|u\|_\Gamma\), consistent with \(\eta=2\|u\|_\Gamma\). It is also the best possible affine residual radius: for any affine fit \(A'z+b'\), take the signed average of its residuals over the eight points \(z=\sigma_1e_1+\sigma_2e_2+\sigma_3e_3\), where each \(\sigma_i\) is \(1\) or \(-1\), with weights \(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3/8\). The average of \(f(z)\) is \(u\). Every constant or linear term has signed average zero, by pairing the signs of a coordinate absent from that term. Thus the signed average of the residuals is \(u\), and the triangle inequality bounds \(\|u\|_\Gamma\) by their maximum norm. This example therefore refutes exact affine control from central antipodal information alone, but does not contradict the full-hypothesis bound.

For the stock conclusion, the inverse of \(\Phi\) is \((z,y)\mapsto(z,y+Az+b)\), and substitution directly gives \(\Phi(z,f(z)+u)=(z,r(z)+u)\) for \(u\in\rho\). The residual estimate is already proved. Since \(\Gamma\) is symmetric positive-definite, Euclidean Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
|w\cdot r(z)|
=|(\Gamma^{1/2}w)\cdot(\Gamma^{-1/2}r(z))|
\leq\sqrt{w^{\mathsf T}\Gamma w}\,\|r(z)\|_\Gamma
\leq R\sqrt{w^{\mathsf T}\Gamma w}.
\]

Adding this bound to the scalar threshold inequality proves the first set inclusion; subtracting it proves the second. When \(w=0\), the same proof applies and both threshold shifts are zero. If the original stock fibers are contained in another set, applying the bijection \(\Phi\) preserves their containments. The argument assumes a stock only for this conditional interpretation and contains no mechanism establishing its existence, positive mass, retained fraction, or a depth bound.
