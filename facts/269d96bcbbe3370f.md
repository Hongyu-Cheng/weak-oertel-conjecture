## Statement

Let $\Phi(h,\theta)$ be the original mixed cap mass of a finite-dimensional body family at a specified source in one fixed integer fiber, with $\theta$ a local chart of through-source nonvertical normal directions. Suppose $\Phi$ is $C^2$ near $(h_0,\theta_0)$, $\Phi_\theta(h_0,\theta_0)=0$, and $K=\Phi_{\theta\theta}(h_0,\theta_0)$ is positive definite. There is a unique nearby stationary branch $\theta(h)$, a strict local minimum in $\theta$. With $V(h)=\Phi(h,\theta(h))$, $A=\Phi_{hh}$ and $B=\Phi_{h\theta}$ at the base, one has $DV=\Phi_h(h,\theta(h))$ and $D^2V(h_0)=A-BK^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}$.

Suppose moreover that a fixed compact set of normal parameters gives the full minimum for all nearby $h$, that cap mass is jointly continuous there, and that its complete minimizing menu at $h_0$ is finite. If each member is interior to a chart in that domain and satisfies the preceding differentiability and positive-definiteness hypotheses, the full minimum near $h_0$ is the minimum of the corresponding local branches.

## Proof

Apply the implicit function theorem to $\Phi_\theta(h,\theta)=0$. It gives a $C^1$ branch with $D\theta(h_0)=-K^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}$. After shrinking the chart, the Hessian in the normal variables stays positive definite, so the branch is a strict local normal minimum and is the only stationary branch there. The chain rule and stationarity give $DV=\Phi_h(h,\theta(h))$. This is $C^1$, and differentiating it at the base gives the asserted reduced Hessian.

For the full minimum take disjoint chart neighborhoods about the finitely many original minimizers, with closures inside their original charts. At $h_0$ their compact complement, if nonempty, has strictly larger minimum than the common optimal mass. Joint continuity on the compact domain preserves a positive gap from the nearby branch values for all sufficiently small changes in $h$. Any new global minimizer must therefore lie inside one of these neighborhoods and be stationary there. The neighborhoods can be chosen within the branch uniqueness neighborhoods. The full minimum is consequently the smallest branch value.
