## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite, nonempty and complete, so $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$. Give $P$ a hyperedge $\{p,q,(p+q)/2\}$ for each distinct same-parity pair $p,q\in P$. If a connected component $C$ has affine dimension at most two, then $\operatorname{conv}(C)\cap\mathbb Z^n=C$.

## Proof

Different components occupy disjoint parity classes: any distinct same-parity pair has its lattice midpoint in $P$ by completeness, and the corresponding edge joins them. Also a segment between points of one component contains only lattice points of that component. Indeed, with its primitive lattice step $v$ the segment has points $a+jv$ for $0\le j\le m$. For $m\ge2$ the overlapping hyperedges from $a+jv$ and $a+(j+2)v$ connect all these points, which are in $P$ by completeness. For $m\le1$ there are no additional points. This proves ranks zero and one.

In rank two take $c\in C$, $V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(C-c)$ and the saturated lattice $\Lambda=V\cap\mathbb Z^n$. It has rank two since $V$ is spanned by integer vectors, and $\operatorname{aff}(C)\cap\mathbb Z^n=c+\Lambda$. A basis identifies this affine lattice with $\mathbb Z^2$. Parity is preserved: an ambient integral half-difference lies in $V$ and so belongs to $\Lambda$, while an even difference in $\Lambda$ is also ambient even. Set $R=\operatorname{conv}(C)$ and $T=R\cap\mathbb Z^n$ and work in these coordinates. We have $T\subseteq P$. Every old edge within $C$ survives and no edge in $T$ can join $C$ to a different original component. Thus $C$ is a connected component of the hypergraph on $T$.

Choose a minimum-area nondegenerate triangle with vertices in $C$. If $C$ occupied at most two parities, two of these vertices would have the same parity. Replacing one by the midpoint gives a nondegenerate component triangle of half the area. Hence $C$ occupies at least three of the four plane parities. Any set $T\setminus C$ uses at most the one remaining parity class. It is closed under integral midpoints, since an edge between two of its points and a point of $C$ is impossible. If it had two or more points, a closest distinct pair would give a closer distinct pair with its midpoint, a contradiction. A sole omitted point would be isolated in $T$, since any edge through it would use points of $C$. It would lie in the interior of $R$, since every boundary edge has endpoints in $C$ and contains only component lattice points by the primitive-step argument.

Apply the isolated-interior-point classification to that point. Then $T$ has exactly four distinct-parity points and no edges, so its three other points cannot form the connected component $C$. This contradiction leaves $T=C$. Since the plane lattice was the whole ambient integer trace of the affine plane, the conclusion is in the original lattice.
