## Statement

Fix $d\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $0\le\rho<1$, and write $x=q+1/2$. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1(q)&=\tfrac12\bigl(x^2-(\rho-\tfrac12)^2\bigr),\\
P_2(q)&=\tfrac13\bigl(x^3-x/4-c\bigr),\qquad c=\rho^3-\tfrac32\rho^2+\tfrac12\rho,\\
P_3(q)&=\tfrac14\bigl(x^4-x^2/2+1/16-\rho^2(1-\rho)^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
For every integer $m\ge0$, $P_d(\rho+m)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\rho+m-j)_+^d$. For each fixed $\rho$, the real-variable interpolant $P_d(q)^{1/(d+1)}$ is concave on $q\ge\rho$, including the continuous value zero at $q=0$ for $\rho=0$.

## Proof

For each $d$, substitution gives $P_d(\rho)=\rho^d$, and expanding the displayed formulas gives $P_d(q+1)-P_d(q)=(q+1)^d$. Induction proves the sampled identity. Each polynomial is strictly increasing for $q>0$ by differentiating in $x=q+1/2>1/2$. It is therefore positive on $q\ge\rho>0$, and on $q>0$ when $\rho=0$.

For a positive polynomial $P$, the sign of $(P^{1/(d+1)})''$ is the sign of $PP''-\frac{d}{d+1}(P')^2$. Positive constant factors in $P$ do not affect the sign. For $d=1$, write $c_1=(\rho-1/2)^2$. Taking $P=x^2-c_1$ gives $-2c_1\le0$. For $d=2$, $c=\rho(\rho-1/2)(\rho-1)$ has $|c|\le1/(12\sqrt3)$, by evaluating the endpoints and the stationary points $\rho=(1\pm1/\sqrt3)/2$. Taking $P=x^3-x/4-c$ gives
$$
-\frac{x^2}{2}-6cx-\frac1{24}
=-\frac{(x+6c)^2}{2}+18c^2-\frac1{24}\le0.
$$
For $d=3$ write $c_3=1/16-\rho^2(1-\rho)^2$, so $0\le c_3\le1/16$. Taking $P=x^4-x^2/2+c_3$ gives
$$
(12x^2-1)c_3-x^4-x^2/4
\ \le\ \frac{12x^2-1}{16}-x^4-x^2/4
=-(x^2-1/4)^2\le0
$$
since $x\ge1/2$. The second derivative tests prove concavity wherever the polynomials are positive. The limiting value at the zero endpoint extends concavity there by continuity.
