## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \([a_i,b_i]\), \(1\le i\le n\), be real intervals with \(a_i<b_i\) and all \(2n\) endpoints distinct. Define the strict partial order \(i<_P j\) by \(b_i<a_j\), and put
$$
D_P=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n:x_i=2,\ x_j=0\Longrightarrow i<_P j\}.
$$
Then \(D_P\) is a complete convex lattice census. For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), use the original law on this entire support, with
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in D_P}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z},\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}p(H),\qquad
M_r(p)=\sum_{x\in D_P}p_x\tau_p(x)^r,
$$
where \(p(H)=\sum_{y\in D_P\cap H}p_y\). These are the depth and moment normalizations of accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf. Use its same parameters
\(\delta=1/16\), \(\rho=1+3\tanh^2(1/32)\), and \(d_*=\log\rho\), and define \(c_{\mathrm{int}}=d_*^3/11664>0\). Uniformly in \(n\), the interval configuration, and all fields \(\ell\),
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{int}}4^{-n},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{int}}^{3/2}8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
$$
Every depth in (1) uses all original ambient containing halfspaces. No boundary point or conditioning assignment that actually occurs is discarded.

## Proof

The interval relation is a strict partial order. It is irreflexive because \(a_i<b_i\); and if \(b_i<a_j\) and \(b_j<a_k\), then \(b_i<a_j<b_j<a_k\), proving transitivity.

First prove completeness, in a form valid for every strict partial order. In the present interval case define
$$
K_P=\{x\in[0,2]^n:x_i-x_j\le1\text{ whenever }i\ne j\text{ and }i\not<_P j\}.
\tag{2}
$$
At an integer point in the box, a displayed inequality fails exactly when \(x_i=2,x_j=0\). Hence \(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_P\), with all boundaries included. The bounded polytope \(K_P\) is full-dimensional because \(\mathbf1\) satisfies every defining inequality strictly. Since \(D_P\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\subseteq K_P\), it follows that \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_P\).

We prove an auxiliary small-field estimate inside this proof. Let \(R\) be any strict partial order on a finite label set \(I\), with \(m=|I|\), and write
\(D_R=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{I}:x_i=2,x_j=0\Longrightarrow i<_Rj\}\).
For zero labels, the support and law are a singleton, and its depth and moments are 1. A linear extension \(i_1,\ldots,i_m\) of \(R\) sends \(D_R\), under the coordinate permutation \(z_s=x_{i_s}\), into the ordered-chain support \(D_m\) used in accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf: a 2 at \(i_s\) and a 0 at \(i_t\) require \(i_s<_R i_t\), hence \(s<t\). Thus that fact's cardinality formula gives
\(|D_R|\le(m+2)2^{m-1}\), including the singleton convention at \(m=0\). Both cubes \(\{0,1\}^{I}\) and \(\{1,2\}^{I}\) lie in \(D_R\).

Suppose first that \(D_R\) has no masks, and that \(|h_i|\le\delta\) for every \(i\in I\). Define
$$
Z_h=\sum_{z\in D_R}e^{h\cdot z},\qquad
q_h(z)=\frac{e^{h\cdot z}}{Z_h},\qquad
T_h=\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^{I}}e^{-|h\cdot u|}.
\tag{3}
$$
For every nonzero Boolean \(u\), the points \(\mathbf1-u\) and \(\mathbf1+u\) are in \(D_R\), have midpoint \(\mathbf1\), and form pairs disjoint as \(u\) varies. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf1\) contains at least one endpoint of every pair, with mass at least \(q_h(\mathbf1)e^{-|h\cdot u|}\). Include the central atom once, corresponding to \(u=0\). This proves \(\tau_{q_h}(\mathbf1)\ge q_h(\mathbf1)T_h\) for the actual depth in \(\mathbb R^{I}\).

The exact normalization in (3) and the cardinality bound give
\(q_h(\mathbf1)\ge e^{-\delta m}/|D_R|\) and \(T_h\ge2^m e^{-\delta m}\). Consequently
$$
4^mM_2(q_h)
\ge4^m q_h(\mathbf1)^3T_h^2
\ge\frac{8e^{(\log2-5/16)m}}{(m+2)^3}
\ge\frac1{16}.
\tag{4}
$$
For completeness, the last numerical inequality follows from \(\log2>2/3\), which is the strict convexity bound for the integral of \(1/x\) on \([1,2]\). Thus \(\log2-5/16>1/3\). The maximum of \((s+2)^3e^{-s/3}\) on \(s\ge0\) occurs at \(s=7\) and equals \(729e^{-7/3}<128\); indeed, \(e>5/2\) gives \(729e^{-7/3}<729\cdot4/25<128\). This proves (4) also at \(m=0\).

Now permit arbitrary masks
\(A_i\in\{\{1\},\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,1,2\}\}\), and set \(E=D_R\cap\prod_{i\in I}A_i\). Give the entire \(E\) the original small-field law \(q_E(x)=e^{h\cdot x}/Z_E\), where \(Z_E=\sum_{x\in E}e^{h\cdot x}\) and \(|h_i|\le\delta\). We claim
$$
M_2(q_E)\ge\frac1{16}4^{-m}.
\tag{5}
$$
The masks all contain 1, so every such support is nonempty.

Here is an exact recursive partition of \(E\). At any stage the remaining support has the same masked form on an induced partial order. If a coordinate has mask \(\{1\}\), delete it and remember its fixed value. A coordinate fixed to 1 imposes no further restriction. Otherwise, if a coordinate \(i\) has a binary mask \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{1,2\}\), partition according to its two values and delete it. Both fibers are nonempty: the chosen value, with every other remaining coordinate equal to 1, is supported.

The updates to the other masks are exact. If the chosen value at \(i\) is 0, remove 2 from \(A_j\) precisely for those remaining labels \(j\) with \(j\not<_R i\). If the chosen value is 2, remove 0 from \(A_j\) precisely when \(i\not<_R j\). A chosen value 1 imposes no restriction. The constraints involving only remaining labels are exactly those of the induced partial order. Each updated mask still contains 1 and is one of the four permitted masks. This proves inductively the asserted form of every fiber, without any independence assertion about its binary coordinates. Every step deletes one label, so the process terminates after at most \(m\) steps on each branch.

At a terminal leaf \(\lambda\), all remaining masks are \(\{0,1,2\}\). Let their label set be \(I_\lambda\), of size \(r_\lambda\), and let \(R_\lambda\) be the induced partial order. The leaf support is exactly an affine insertion \(\iota_\lambda(D_{R_\lambda})\), which places fixed values \(\eta_{\lambda,i}\in\{0,1,2\}\) on \(I\setminus I_\lambda\). The leaf supports are disjoint and cover \(E\). Their dimensions obey
$$
\sum_\lambda 2^{r_\lambda}\le2^m.
\tag{6}
$$
To verify (6), induct on the number of remaining labels at any node. A terminal node on \(r\) labels contributes \(2^r\). A fixed-one deletion has one child on one fewer label, whose total contribution is at most half the parent's bound. A binary split has two children on one fewer label, each contributing at most half the parent's bound. These cases establish (6) at the root, including zero labels.

For each leaf retain the exact normalizers
$$
Z_\lambda=\sum_{y\in D_{R_\lambda}}
 e^{\sum_{i\in I_\lambda}h_i y_i},\qquad
w_\lambda=
\frac{e^{\sum_{i\notin I_\lambda}h_i \eta_{\lambda,i}}Z_\lambda}{Z_E},\qquad
q_\lambda(y)=\frac{e^{\sum_{i\in I_\lambda}h_i y_i}}{Z_\lambda}.
\tag{7}
$$
The weights are positive, sum to 1, and satisfy \(q_E(\iota_\lambda y)=w_\lambda q_\lambda(y)\). If a closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{I}\) contains \(\iota_\lambda y\), its trace under \(\iota_\lambda\) is a closed halfspace containing \(y\), or all of \(\mathbb R^{I_\lambda}\) when the restricted normal vanishes. In either case its \(q_\lambda\)-mass is at least \(\tau_{q_\lambda}(y)\). Therefore
\(\tau_{q_E}(\iota_\lambda y)\ge w_\lambda\tau_{q_\lambda}(y)\).
For a singleton leaf this is the target-atom inequality, with depth 1 on the singleton.

The leaf fields are restrictions of \(h\), so (4) applies to every leaf. Summing over the disjoint leaves and then using Holder's inequality with exponents 3 and \(3/2\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(q_E)
&\ge\sum_\lambda w_\lambda^3M_2(q_\lambda)
\ge\frac1{16}\sum_\lambda w_\lambda^3 4^{-r_\lambda},\\
1=\left(\sum_\lambda w_\lambda\right)^3
&\le\left(\sum_\lambda w_\lambda^3 4^{-r_\lambda}\right)
       \left(\sum_\lambda2^{r_\lambda}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Equations (6)--(8) prove (5). The constant \(1/16\) is paid only at the terminal leaves. All transfers in (7)--(8) concern the original law on \(E\), with no multiplication of depth moments of independent factors.

Return to the original interval-order law \(p\) and its original \(Z\). Split the labels into \(B=\{i:|\ell_i|>\delta\}\) and \(G=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B\), with \(k=|B|\) and \(g=|G|\). For each actually supported assignment \(\beta\) on \(B\), define its original marginal mass
$$
\alpha_\beta=\frac1Z\sum_{\substack{x\in D_P\\x_B=\beta}}e^{\ell\cdot x}.
\tag{9}
$$
These masses are positive and sum to 1. The exact conditional support on \(G\) is a masked induced-poset support. More explicitly, start each free mask at \(\{0,1,2\}\); remove 0 from the mask at \(i\in G\) if there is a bad label \(j\) with \(\beta_j=2\) and \(j\not<_P i\); remove 2 if there is a bad label \(j\) with \(\beta_j=0\) and \(i\not<_P j\). Call the resulting masks \(A_{\beta,i}\), and put
\(E_\beta=D_{P|G}\cap\prod_{i\in G}A_{\beta,i}\).
The fixed bad assignment satisfies its own internal constraints by supportedness. The mask updates capture exactly every constraint involving one bad and one free label; all free-free constraints are exactly \(D_{P|G}\). This proves the conditional support equality, including arbitrary supported \(\beta\), rather than only endpoint or extreme assignments.

The exact conditional law and its relation to the original normalizer are
$$
Z_\beta=\sum_{y\in E_\beta}e^{\ell_G\cdot y},\qquad
q_\beta(y)=\frac{e^{\ell_G\cdot y}}{Z_\beta},\qquad
\alpha_\beta=\frac{e^{\ell_B\cdot\beta}Z_\beta}{Z},\qquad
p_{(\beta,y)}=\alpha_\beta q_\beta(y).
\tag{10}
$$
Every inherited field on \(G\) has magnitude at most \(\delta\), so (5) gives \(M_2(q_\beta)\ge4^{-g}/16\). The trace on the affine plane \(x_B=\beta\) of any original ambient halfspace containing \((\beta,y)\) is either a containing halfspace on \(G\) or all that plane. Just as in (7)--(8), this proves \(\tau_p(\beta,y)\ge\alpha_\beta\tau_{q_\beta}(y)\). The disjoint conditional strata therefore give
$$
M_2(p)\ge\sum_\beta\alpha_\beta^3M_2(q_\beta)
\ge\frac1{16}4^{-g}\sum_\beta\alpha_\beta^3.
\tag{11}
$$
This covers \(g=0\) as well, with singleton conditional laws.

It remains to prove the cubic surplus for the original large-field marginal in (9). The interval representation supplies an exact product partition. Let \(J_0=\{0,1\}^n\). For each label \(j\), define \(J_j\) by fixing \(x_j=2\), allowing \(x_i\in\{1,2\}\) when \(b_i<b_j\), allowing \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) when \(a_i>b_j\), and fixing \(x_i=1\) when \(a_i<b_j<b_i\), for all \(i\ne j\). Distinct endpoints ensure that these alternatives exhaust the other labels.

These classes are nonempty, disjoint, and cover \(D_P\). Indeed, a point with no 2 belongs to \(J_0\). Otherwise take its unique label \(j\) with maximal \(b_j\) among its 2s. If \(b_i<b_j\), then \(a_i<b_i<b_j\), so a 0 at \(i\) would contradict compatibility with the 2 at \(j\). If \(a_i>b_j\), then \(b_i>b_j\), so maximality excludes a 2 at \(i\). If \(a_i<b_j<b_i\), maximality excludes a 2 and compatibility with \(j\) excludes a 0, leaving 1. These are precisely the restrictions defining \(J_j\). Conversely, in \(J_j\) every coordinate carrying 2 has right endpoint at most \(b_j\), and every coordinate carrying 0 has left endpoint strictly greater than \(b_j\). Thus every 2 precedes every 0 in \(P\), and \(j\) is the unique 2 with maximal right endpoint. This proves the partition.

For \(\nu\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\), where nonzero labels denote the corresponding pivot class, set
\(Z_{J,\nu}=\sum_{x\in J_\nu}e^{\ell\cdot x}\) and \(w_\nu=Z_{J,\nu}/Z\). These are original class probabilities and sum to 1. Given \(J_\nu\), the original law is a product law with original field \(\ell_i\) on every binary coordinate. Let \(\mu_\nu\) be its marginal on \(B\). Then, exactly,
$$
\alpha_\beta=\sum_{\nu=0}^n w_\nu\mu_\nu(\beta).
\tag{12}
$$
Each \(\mu_\nu\) is a product of binary affine laws and fixed-coordinate laws.

We count the distinct product marginals needed in (12). For a good pivot \(j\in G\), write \(t=b_j\). Every bad label \(i\) has mask \(\{0,1\}\) when \(t<a_i\), mask \(\{1\}\) when \(a_i<t<b_i\), and mask \(\{1,2\}\) when \(b_i<t\). As \(t\) increases, this mask changes at most twice, at \(a_i\) and \(b_i\). Across all bad labels there are \(2k\) distinct endpoints, so there are at most \(2k+1\) possible joint mask patterns for good pivots. The no-2 class \(J_0\) is the same all-low pattern obtained before every bad endpoint, so it does not increase this bound. For each joint pattern the normalized marginal is identical, since its factors use only those masks and the same original fields. Each of the \(k\) bad pivots contributes at most one additional product marginal, with its pivot coordinate fixed to 2. Grouping identical realized marginals and adding their weights therefore expresses \(\alpha\) as a mixture of at most \(3k+1\) normalized product laws. For \(k=0\), this is the unique law on the empty assignment with weight 1.

On either binary support \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{1,2\}\), the cubic atom sum of the normalized affine law with field \(u\) is
$$
\frac{1+e^{3u}}{(1+e^u)^3}
=\frac{1+3\tanh^2(u/2)}4.
\tag{13}
$$
For every bad field \(|u|>\delta\), this is at least \(\rho/4\). A fixed coordinate has cubic atom sum 1, also at least \(\rho/4\), because \(1<\rho<4\). Thus every product component \(\mu\) in the grouped mixture satisfies \(\sum_\beta\mu(\beta)^3\ge(\rho/4)^k\). One grouped component has weight at least \((3k+1)^{-1}\). Keep that component in the pointwise nonnegative mixture (12), cube, and sum. This proves
$$
\sum_\beta\alpha_\beta^3
\ge\frac{\rho^k}{(3k+1)^3}4^{-k}
\ge\frac{d_*^3}{729}4^{-k}.
\tag{14}
$$
All sums may equivalently range over \(\{0,1,2\}^{B}\), assigning zero to unsupported assignments. For the last inequality, \(0<d_*<\log4<9\). The minimum on \(s\ge0\) of \(d_*s-3\log(3s+1)\) occurs at \(s=3/d_*-1/3\), and its value is
\(3-d_*/3-3\log(9/d_*)\ge3\log(d_*/9)\). Exponentiation proves the bound for every integer \(k\ge0\).

Combining (11) and (14), with \(g+k=n\), yields
\(M_2(p)\ge d_*^3 4^{-n}/(16\cdot729)=c_{\mathrm{int}}4^{-n}\).
Finally Jensen's inequality under the entire original law gives
\(M_3(p)=\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p^2)^{3/2}]\ge M_2(p)^{3/2}\), proving (1). Every conditioning step retained its exact original exponential weights, every normalizer was related explicitly to its parent normalizer, and every depth transfer used all original ambient containing halfspaces.
