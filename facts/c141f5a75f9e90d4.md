## Statement

Let \(M\in[1,\infty)\) be finite. Let \(g:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) be a log-concave probability density with \(\int_{\mathbb R}|x|g(x)\,dx<\infty\). Let \(f\) be any probability density satisfying \(f(x)\le M g(x)\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\), and put \(m_f=\int_{\mathbb R}x f(x)\,dx\). This mean is finite because of the density bound. Then
\[
\int_{m_f}^{\infty}g(x)\,dx\ \ge\ \frac1{eM},
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{m_f}g(x)\,dx\ \ge\ \frac1{eM}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here “finite mean” is understood in the usual integrable-random-variable sense. A finite real Lebesgue integral of x also implies the displayed absolute integrability. No bounded-support assumption is made. Closed and open endpoint versions of either cap have the same mass because the laws have densities.

For \(M>1\), put \(q=1/M\). There are unique finite points \(t_+,t_-\) in the interior of the support interval of \(g\) satisfying \(\int_{t_+}^{\infty}g=q\) and \(\int_{-\infty}^{t_-}g=q\). Define the probability densities \(h_+(x)=M g(x)\mathbf1_{[t_+,\infty)}(x)\) and \(h_-(x)=M g(x)\mathbf1_{(-\infty,t_-]}(x)\), with means \(m_+,m_-\). More precisely,
\[
m_-\le m_f\le m_+,
\qquad
\int_{m_+}^{\infty}g\ge\frac q e,
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{m_-}g\ge\frac q e.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(M=1\), necessarily \(f=g\) almost everywhere, and both caps in (1) are at least \(1/e\). The coefficient \(1/e\) in (1) cannot be increased uniformly, even with M fixed.

## Proof

We first prove the scalar centroid inequality used below, including unbounded supports. Let \(h\) be any log-concave probability density on \(\mathbb R\) with finite first absolute moment, let \(Y\) have density \(h\), and let \(m_h=\mathbb E Y\). Write \(T_h(x)=\int_x^{\infty}h(y)\,dy\); this notation is distinct from the locked problem's mixed-integer set \(S\).

The positive set of \(h\) is an interval of positive length. Let \(I=(a,b)\) be its interior, allowing either endpoint to be infinite. The density is positive and continuous on \(I\), since \(\log h\) is a finite concave function there. Endpoints are null sets, so \(Y\in I\) almost surely. If \(a\) is finite, \(Y-a>0\) almost surely and its expectation is positive, giving \(m_h>a\). If \(b\) is finite, the same argument for \(b-Y\) gives \(m_h<b\). Thus \(m_h\in I\) in every case.

For \(x<y\) in \(I\) and \(s\ge0\) with \(y+s\in I\), concavity gives
\[
\log h(x+s)+\log h(y)
\ \ge\ \log h(x)+\log h(y+s).
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, with \(\lambda=s/(y+s-x)\), the two points \(x+s\) and \(y\) equal \((1-\lambda)x+\lambda(y+s)\) and \(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)(y+s)\), respectively; adding the two concavity inequalities proves (3). Consequently, \(h(y+s)/h(y)\le h(x+s)/h(x)\). If \(y+s\) is beyond the upper support endpoint, the same ratio inequality holds with its left side zero. Possible values exactly at a finite support endpoint affect only one value of s and hence no integral. Integrating over \(s\in[0,\infty)\) proves
\[
\frac{T_h(y)}{h(y)}\le\frac{T_h(x)}{h(x)}.
\tag{4}
\]
These quantities are finite and strictly positive on \(I\). Thus \(h/T_h\) is nondecreasing there. Since \(T_h'=-h\) on \(I\), the differentiable function \(\log T_h\) has derivative \(-h/T_h\), which is nonincreasing. Therefore \(\log T_h\) is concave on \(I\).

The survival function is continuous, strictly decreasing on \(I\), and has endpoint limits 1 and 0. These facts hold also when an endpoint is infinite, by integrability of h. For every \(u\in(0,1)\), its unique inverse point \(x_u\in I\) satisfies \(T_h(x_u)=u\). Absence of atoms gives \(\Pr(T_h(Y)\le u)=\Pr(Y\ge x_u)=u\). Hence \(T_h(Y)\) is uniform on \((0,1)\), and
\[
\mathbb E\log T_h(Y)=\int_0^1\log u\,du=-1.
\tag{5}
\]
The supporting tangent inequality at the interior point \(m_h\) is \(\log T_h(x)\le\log T_h(m_h)+(\log T_h)'(m_h)(x-m_h)\) for every \(x\in I\). Both sides are integrable under h: (5) gives integrability of the left side, and the finite first absolute moment gives integrability of the right side. Integrating yields \(-1\le\log T_h(m_h)\), or \(\Pr(Y\ge m_h)\ge1/e\). Applying the same argument to the reflected density \(x\mapsto h(-x)\), whose mean is \(-m_h\), proves \(\Pr(Y\le m_h)\ge1/e\). This establishes both scalar centroid caps by a full proof.

Return to g and f. If \(M=1\), then \(g-f\ge0\) almost everywhere and \(\int(g-f)=0\), so \(f=g\) almost everywhere. The scalar centroid inequality just proved gives (1), including this case without an endpoint quantile at mass 1.

Suppose \(M>1\). The same continuity, strict monotonicity, and endpoint limits for the distribution and survival functions of g give unique finite \(t_+,t_-\) for \(q\in(0,1)\). This proves the quantile assertions even when the support is unbounded. The densities \(h_+,h_-\) each have total mass \(Mq=1\), are log-concave by restriction of g to a half-line and multiplication by M, and have finite first absolute moment because each is bounded by Mg. Their means satisfy \(m_+>t_+\) and \(m_-<t_-\): the respective positive distances have positive expectations, and threshold points carry zero mass.

For almost every x, \((x-t_+)(h_+(x)-f(x))\ge0\). On \(x>t_+\) this follows from \(h_+=Mg\ge f\); on \(x<t_+\) it follows from \(h_+=0\) and \(f\ge0\). Absolute first-moment integrability and equal total masses therefore give
\[
m_+-m_f
=\int_{\mathbb R}(x-t_+)(h_+(x)-f(x))\,dx
\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
Similarly, \((x-t_-)(f(x)-h_-(x))\ge0\) almost everywhere, proving \(m_f-m_-\ge0\) by the same integration. These are the two extremal-mean comparisons in (2); no assumption that f is log-concave was used.

Apply the scalar centroid inequality to \(h_+\). Because \(m_+>t_+\), its right cap agrees exactly with M times the original g cap:
\[
M\int_{m_+}^{\infty}g(x)\,dx
=\int_{m_+}^{\infty}h_+(x)\,dx
\ge\frac1e.
\tag{7}
\]
Apply the left centroid inequality to \(h_-\); because \(m_-<t_-\), it gives \(M\int_{-\infty}^{m_-}g\ge1/e\). Thus all of (2) holds. The right reference cap is nonincreasing in its threshold, and \(m_f\le m_+\); the left reference cap is nondecreasing, and \(m_f\ge m_-\). These monotonicities prove (1). Endpoint values of any density never affect these integrations, and no truncation limit or bounded-support reduction was used.

Finally, for any fixed finite \(M\ge1\), take \(g(x)=e^{-x}\mathbf1_{[0,\infty)}(x)\) and \(f(x)=M e^{-x}\mathbf1_{[\log M,\infty)}(x)\). Both are probability densities, g is log-concave, \(f\le Mg\), and direct integration gives \(m_f=\log M+1\). Therefore \(\int_{m_f}^{\infty}g=e^{-(\log M+1)}=1/(eM)\), proving sharpness of the right bound. Reflecting both densities about zero proves sharpness of the left bound. For \(M>1\) this sharp example has \(f/g=0\) on a set of positive g-mass, explicitly showing that no positive lower likelihood hypothesis is present.
