## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) be a probability vector. Use the row-array notation of accepted fact `5d0f9e78ced711d6`: suppose \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\ge0\), with both indices in \(D\), satisfies

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0,
\qquad
q_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}.
\tag{1}
\]

There is a nonnegative array \(\widetilde r\) with exactly the same column sums \(p\), row sums \(q\), and row-mean identities, satisfying simultaneously

\[
\widetilde r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}=\min\{p_{\mathbf J},q_{\mathbf J}\}
\quad(\mathbf J\in D).
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, if \(r\) minimizes \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2\) over the arrays (1) with prescribed \(p\), then \(\widetilde r\) is also a minimizer and has exactly the same positive-row indices.

For the specialization, take any member of the exact actual-body family in accepted fact `6ca311257acdfbcb`. Retain its integer dimension \(n\ge4\), actual body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), actual positive fiber weights \(w\), complete positive support \(D\), normalization \(p=w/W\), total weight \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\), and \(T=W/2^n\). Retain also \(\mathcal Q=\{0,1\}^n\), \(A_n=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,-\mathbf1\}\), and \(D=A_n+\mathcal Q\), where the latter is a set of distinct physical sites. Its multiplicities and excesses mean exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathbf I}&=|\{\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q:\mathbf I\in\mathbf J+A_n\}|,\\
\zeta_{\mathbf J}&=w_{\mathbf J}-m_{\mathbf J}
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(s_n>e\) solve \(s_n(\log s_n-1)=n-2\). The predecessor's explicit minimizing array is

\[
W r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I\in\mathbf J+A_n\}}
+\zeta_{\mathbf J}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q,\ \mathbf I\in D),
\tag{4}
\]

with every other row zero. There exists an array \(\widetilde r\) with the same \(p,q\) as (4), satisfying (2), such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0\}&=\mathcal Q,
&W\widetilde r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}&=w_{\mathbf J}
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q),\\
\mathcal R_{\log}(\widetilde r;w)&=w_{\max}=w_{\mathbf1},
&\frac{n+1}{n+s_n+3}
&\le\frac{\mathcal R_{\log}(\widetilde r;w)}{T}\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently the ratio in (5) tends to one as \(n\to\infty\). In fact the score conclusion holds for every array with those marginals and diagonals (2).

Here \(\mathcal R_{\log}\) is precisely the full-occurrence row score of accepted fact `6ca311257acdfbcb`, using the original canonical potential of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`: \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\). At a positive row \(\mathbf J\), choose \(L\ge0\) and finitely many barycentric components with nonempty supports \(E_k\subseteq D\), positive coefficients \(\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\), and masses \(x_k\ge0\). Each coefficient vector sums to one and has mean \(\mathbf J\); every positive-mass component has cost \(G_k=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\le L\). The constraints are \(\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le W\widetilde r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D\). The score is the supremum of \(\sum_kx_k/(1+L)\) over rows, cutoffs, and these schemes. Thus every occurrence charges its whole component mass, and supports and positive coefficients are unrestricted.

## Proof

First establish (2) without invoking a martingale existence theorem. For every finite-valued convex function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), apply Jensen's inequality to each row of positive mass in (1), then sum the rows. This gives \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}f(\mathbf J)\le\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}f(\mathbf I)\). Zero rows contribute zero. Define \(\nu_{\mathbf J}=\min\{p_{\mathbf J},q_{\mathbf J}\}\), \(p'_{\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}-\nu_{\mathbf I}\), and \(q'_{\mathbf J}=q_{\mathbf J}-\nu_{\mathbf J}\). These residual vectors are nonnegative and have equal total mass and equal vector mean. Subtracting the identical \(\nu\) contributions preserves every convex inequality:

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q'_{\mathbf J}f(\mathbf J)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p'_{\mathbf I}f(\mathbf I).
\tag{6}
\]

We prove that (6) supplies a nonnegative array with column sums \(p'\), row sums \(q'\), and row-mean identities. Let \(\mathscr K\) be the set of all vectors

\[
\left(
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},
\ \sum_{\mathbf I\in D}s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
\right)_{\mathbf J\in D}
\quad\text{where}\quad
s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\ge0,
\quad\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p'_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{7}
\]

The underlying set of arrays is nonempty: assign every column to any one index of the nonempty set \(D\). It is closed and bounded, since each entry lies between zero and its prescribed column sum. Hence it is compact and convex, and so is its linear image \(\mathscr K\).

Suppose the desired vector \((q'_{\mathbf J},\mathbf0)_{\mathbf J\in D}\) were outside \(\mathscr K\). There would be real scalars \(\alpha_{\mathbf J}\) and vectors \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\in\mathbb R^n\) giving strict separation in the orientation

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J}\alpha_{\mathbf J}q'_{\mathbf J}
<\min_{\substack{s\ge0\\\sum_{\mathbf J}s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p'_{\mathbf I}}}
\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\bigl(\alpha_{\mathbf J}+\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, this strict separation follows from a closest point of the compact convex set to the external vector. The difference from the external vector to that closest point defines a linear functional whose value at every point of the set is at least its value at the closest point, and whose value there exceeds its value at the external vector by the positive squared distance. The first assertion follows by differentiating squared distance along each segment in the set. Identifying the coordinates of this functional gives exactly the multipliers in (8).

Define the finite-valued convex function \(f(x)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\{-\alpha_{\mathbf J}-\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(x-\mathbf J)\}\). Each column can be assigned independently to an index attaining its least coefficient, so the right-hand side of (8) is exactly \(-\sum_{\mathbf I}p'_{\mathbf I}f(\mathbf I)\). On the other hand, \(f(\mathbf J)\ge-\alpha_{\mathbf J}\). Nonnegativity of \(q'\) and (6) yield

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J}\alpha_{\mathbf J}q'_{\mathbf J}
\ge-\sum_{\mathbf J}q'_{\mathbf J}f(\mathbf J)
\ge-\sum_{\mathbf I}p'_{\mathbf I}f(\mathbf I),
\tag{9}
\]

contradicting (8). The desired vector therefore belongs to \(\mathscr K\), proving exact finite feasibility. This argument also covers zero residual mass and lower-dimensional affine support.

Choose such a residual array \(s\), and set \(\widetilde r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}+\nu_{\mathbf J}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}\). Its columns, rows, and row means are the required ones. At each index, at least one of \(p'_{\mathbf J},q'_{\mathbf J}\) is zero. Since \(0\le s_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}\le\min\{p'_{\mathbf J},q'_{\mathbf J}\}\), that residual diagonal is zero, proving (2). No larger diagonal is possible with these marginals. The exact preservation of \(q\) preserves its positive support and its second moment, proving the minimizer assertion for the optimization in accepted fact `5d0f9e78ced711d6`.

We now verify the specialization and its scale from the exact identities of accepted fact `6ca311257acdfbcb`. Its multiplicity count is \(m_{\mathbf0}=2\), \(m_{\mathbf J}=1+\sum_iJ_i\) for nonzero \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q\), and \(m_{\mathbf I}=1\) outside \(\mathcal Q\). In particular, \(2\le m_{\mathbf J}\le n+1\) on \(\mathcal Q\). The actual table has \(w_{\mathbf I}=1\) on \(D\setminus\mathcal Q\), while its actual-volume excess in (3) satisfies \(0\le\zeta_{\mathbf J}\le s_n+1\). These are the actual weights after the predecessor's concave-roof and cube-fiber construction; \(\zeta\) is not an excess for its auxiliary exponential roof. The predecessor also establishes \(n+1\le w_{\max}=w_{\mathbf1}\le n+2\).

There are \(n+2\) distinct elements in each translate \(\mathbf J+A_n\), and those elements have sum \((n+2)\mathbf J\). Thus (4) has row mean \(\mathbf J\) and row mass \(Wq_{\mathbf J}=n+2+\zeta_{\mathbf J}\). Summing its indicator over rows gives exactly \(m_{\mathbf I}\), so the column sums are the actual weights, including every shared physical site. In particular,

\[
W(q_{\mathbf J}-p_{\mathbf J})
=(n+2+\zeta_{\mathbf J})-(m_{\mathbf J}+\zeta_{\mathbf J})
=n+2-m_{\mathbf J}\ge1
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q).
\tag{10}
\]

Every row outside \(\mathcal Q\) has zero mass. Therefore the saturated diagonal (2) assigns the entire binary column \(p_{\mathbf J}\) to its own row. Nonnegativity then forces every off-diagonal entry in a binary column to be zero. Each positive row of \(\widetilde r\) consequently has capacity \(w_{\mathbf J}\) at its own site, zero at the other binary sites, and total capacity

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\mathcal Q}W\widetilde r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=Wq_{\mathbf J}-w_{\mathbf J}
=n+2-m_{\mathbf J}\le n
\tag{11}
\]

on the sites outside \(\mathcal Q\).

The original array (4) is minimizing as stated in the predecessor; this can also be checked directly. Every integer coordinate satisfies \(j^2-j\ge0\), with equality just for zero and one. Every feasible array has the same mean as \(p\), so its second moment is at least \(\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\sum_iI_i\). The array (4), supported on binary rows, attains this lower bound. The array \(\widetilde r\) retains exactly its \(q\), hence retains both its minimum objective and all \(2^n\) positive binary rows.

At \(\mathbf J=\mathbf1\), the singleton of mass \(w_{\max}\) uses precisely the restored diagonal capacity and has logarithmic cost zero. It proves \(\mathcal R_{\log}(\widetilde r;w)\ge w_{\max}\) under the full-occurrence convention of accepted facts `488f0a583886b5fc` and `6ca311257acdfbcb`.

To prove the matching upper bound, fix any positive row \(\mathbf J\) and any permitted finite scheme at cutoff \(L\). Every positive-mass component uses only \(\{\mathbf J\}\cup(D\setminus\mathcal Q)\), by the zero capacities just proved. Components containing \(\mathbf J\) have combined mass at most \(w_{\mathbf J}\), because they all charge their entire mass to the diagonal. A component not containing \(\mathbf J\) uses only sites of weight one. Its logarithmic cost is exactly \(\log w_{\mathbf J}\), independent of its barycentric coefficients. It has at least two distinct sources: a singleton at another site cannot have mean \(\mathbf J\). Summing the full-occurrence inequalities over the outside sites and using (11) therefore bounds the combined mass of these components by \(n/2\).

If \(L<\log w_{\mathbf J}\), every positive component contains \(\mathbf J\), and the score is at most \(w_{\mathbf J}\le w_{\max}\). If \(L\ge\log w_{\mathbf J}\), use \(2\le w_{\mathbf J}\le n+2\) to obtain

\[
\frac{\sum_kx_k}{1+L}
\le\frac{w_{\mathbf J}+n/2}{1+\log w_{\mathbf J}}
\le\frac{3n/2+2}{1+\log2}
\le n+1
\le w_{\max}.
\tag{12}
\]

For the penultimate inequality, \(\log2\ge2/3\) and \(3n/2+2\le(5/3)(n+1)\) for \(n\ge2\). The logarithmic bound follows by integrating \(1/t\ge4/3-4t/9\) on \([1,2]\); their difference is \((2t-3)^2/(9t)\). Thus (12) is an exact analytic estimate, not a numerical certificate. The two cutoff cases control every support and coefficient choice, proving the score equality in (5).

Counting the translated sets gives \(\sum_Dm_{\mathbf I}=(n+2)2^n\). Since the only excesses are (3),

\[
W=(n+2)2^n+\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal Q}\zeta_{\mathbf J},
\qquad
n+2\le T\le n+s_n+3.
\tag{13}
\]

Together with \(n+1\le w_{\max}\le n+2\), this proves both inequalities in (5). The defining equation for \(s_n\) gives \(s_n\to\infty\) and \(s_n/n=(1-2/n)/(\log s_n-1)\to0\), so the lower bound tends to one. The upper bound is one, proving the stated convergence of the recoupled row score itself.

Finally, the diagonal result alone supplies only the guaranteed singleton score \(W\max_{\mathbf J}\min\{p_{\mathbf J},q_{\mathbf J}\}\), which is at most \(w_{\max}\) for an actual table. In accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`, map its integer dimension \(N\) to the present integer-dimension role, its support \(\widehat A\) to \(D\), and its maximum-atom notation \(M(w)\) to \(w_{\max}\). Its exact actual bodies \(C_{k,k^3}\) satisfy \(w_{\max}=1\) and \(W/2^N\to\infty\). At any row of any feasible array on these bodies, every singleton-only scheme has score at most the original capacity of that row's site, hence at most one. Its ratio to the target scale therefore tends to zero. Any universal good-row statement through \(\mathcal R_{\log}\) would have to use non-singleton components on that family. The diagonal construction and the specialization (5) do not settle that remaining general capacity question or the locked weak Oertel conjecture.
