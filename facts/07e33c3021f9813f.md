## Statement

Use the actual-body notation and normalization of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f: for a convex body \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\), let \(S_B:=B\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)\), let \(B_z:=\{t:(z,t)\in B\}\), and set \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_1(B_z)\) and \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z\). Define \(h_{S_B}(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_1(S_B\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over all closed ambient halfspaces, and \(h_{\max}(B):=\max_{p\in S_B}h_{S_B}(p)\). The subscripts on \(S_C\) and \(S_{C_{\downarrow}}\) only distinguish the two instances of the predecessor's \(S\).

Take \(n=2\), \(d=1\), write \(z=(z_1,z_2)\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q&:=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z_1|+|z_2|\le1\},\\
l(z)&:=|z_1|,\qquad u(z):=2-|z_2|,\\
C&:=\{(z,t):z\in Q,\ |z_1|\le t\le2-|z_2|\},\\
C_{\downarrow}&:=\{(z,t):z\in Q,\ 0\le t\le2-|z_1|-|z_2|\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are complete convex polytopes satisfying all hypotheses of the registered shaking subgoal. Their integer-fiber volume tables agree, with \(w_0=2\), \(w_{e_1}=w_{-e_1}=w_{e_2}=w_{-e_2}=1\), and all other weights zero, where \(e_1=(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\). Their optimized depths are

\[
h_{\max}(C)=h_{S_C}((0,1))=2,
\qquad
h_{\max}(C_{\downarrow})
=h_{S_{C_{\downarrow}}}((0,2/3))=\frac83.
\]

## Proof

The set \(Q\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope. The functions \(l\) and \(u\) are continuous and piecewise affine, with \(l\) convex and \(u\) concave. On all of \(Q\), their difference is \(u(z)-l(z)=2-|z_1|-|z_2|\ge1\). Thus both displayed bodies are compact, convex, and full-dimensional. They are polytopes because all their constraints are finitely many affine inequalities after expanding the absolute values. The formula for \(C_{\downarrow}\) is precisely the required segment translation by \(-l(z)\), including on the boundary of \(Q\).

The only integer points in \(Q\) are \(0,\pm e_1,\pm e_2\). The complete nonempty integer fibers are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
z&0&\pm e_1&\pm e_2\\ \hline
C_z&[0,2]&[1,2]&[0,1]\\
(C_{\downarrow})_z&[0,2]&[0,1]&[0,1].
\end{array}
\]

Consequently the claimed common volume table and \(W=6\) follow by summing interval lengths. The fibers at all four boundary lattice points of \(Q\) have positive length and are retained throughout.

For either body, every mixed point outside the central fiber has depth at most \(1\). Indeed, the closed halfspace \(z_1\ge1\) isolates exactly the fiber at \(e_1\), which has mass \(1\); the halfspaces \(z_1\le-1\), \(z_2\ge1\), and \(z_2\le-1\) do the same at the other three boundary fibers. These halfspaces contain each entire indicated fiber.

We first prove \(h_{\max}(C)=2\). Let \((0,t)\) be any central mixed point, so \(0\le t\le2\), and use \(s\) for the continuous coordinate of a variable point of a halfspace. If \(0\le t\le1\), the containing closed halfspace \(s\le t+2z_2\) takes length \(t\) from the central fiber, the entire unit fiber at \(e_2\), and zero length from each other fiber. At \(t=1\), its intersections with the fibers at \(\pm e_1\) are singletons and still have zero length. Hence \(h_{S_C}((0,t))\le t+1\le2\) for \(0\le t\le1\).

If \(1\le t\le2\), the containing closed halfspace \(s\ge t+2z_1\) takes length \(2-t\) from the central fiber, the entire unit fiber at \(-e_1\), and zero length from each other fiber. In particular, the boundary intersections at \(t=1\) on the fibers at \(\pm e_2\) are singletons. Thus \(h_{S_C}((0,t))\le3-t\le2\) for \(1\le t\le2\). Together with the boundary-fiber bound, this proves \(h_{\max}(C)\le2\).

To prove the reverse inequality, take \(p=(0,1)\). It suffices to consider halfspaces whose boundary passes through \(p\): every closed halfspace containing \(p\) contains the parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(p\). A nonvertical such halfspace is either \(s\ge1+a_1z_1+a_2z_2\) or \(s\le1+a_1z_1+a_2z_2\), for some real \(a_1,a_2\). In the first orientation it takes length \(1\) from the central fiber and the entire unit fiber at at least one of \(e_1,-e_1\), since one of its two thresholds \(1+a_1,1-a_1\) is at most \(1\). Its mass is therefore at least \(2\). In the second orientation it takes length \(1\) from the central fiber and the entire unit fiber at at least one of \(e_2,-e_2\), since one of \(1+a_2,1-a_2\) is at least \(1\). Its mass is again at least \(2\). A vertical halfspace through \(p\) contains the entire central fiber on its boundary and has mass at least \(2\). This proves \(h_{S_C}(p)=2\).

We next verify depth \(8/3\) at the shaken point \(p_{\downarrow}=(0,2/3)\). For a real number \(v\), write \([v]_0^1:=\min\{1,\max\{0,v\}\}\). We will use the elementary inequality

\[
[r+a]_0^1+[r-a]_0^1\ge\min\{1,2r\}
\qquad(0\le r\le1,\ a\in\mathbb R).
\]

The left side is unchanged when \(a\) is replaced by \(-a\), so assume \(a\ge0\). If \(a\ge r\), it equals \(\min\{1,r+a\}\), which is at least \(\min\{1,2r\}\). If \(0\le a\le r\), it equals \(2r\) when \(r+a\le1\), and equals \(1+r-a\ge1\) when \(r+a\ge1\). This proves the inequality in all cases.

As before, it suffices to consider halfspaces through \(p_{\downarrow}\). For the lower orientation \(s\le2/3+a_1z_1+a_2z_2\), the central contribution is \(2/3\). The sum of the contributions from each pair \(\{e_i,-e_i\}\) is

\[
[2/3+a_i]_0^1+[2/3-a_i]_0^1\ge1
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]

Its total mass is therefore at least \(2/3+1+1=8/3\). For the upper orientation \(s\ge2/3+a_1z_1+a_2z_2\), the central contribution is \(4/3\), and the contribution of each pair is

\[
[1/3-a_i]_0^1+[1/3+a_i]_0^1\ge2/3
\qquad(i=1,2).
\]

Its total mass is at least \(4/3+2/3+2/3=8/3\). A vertical halfspace through \(p_{\downarrow}\) contains the full central fiber, of mass \(2\), and at least one full fiber from each opposite pair. Its mass is at least \(4\). If a coefficient vanishes, both fibers in the corresponding pair are on the closed boundary and are included. These arguments cover every nonzero ambient normal and give \(h_{S_{C_{\downarrow}}}(p_{\downarrow})\ge8/3\).

Finally, this is the exact optimized shaken depth. At any central point \((0,t)\), with \(0\le t\le2\), the containing closed halfspace \(s\le t+3z_1+3z_2\) takes the two positive-coordinate unit fibers, no length from the two negative-coordinate fibers, and central length \(t\). Its mass is \(t+2\). The horizontal upper halfspace \(s\ge t\) has mass \(2-t+4\max\{1-t,0\}\). Hence

\[
h_{S_{C_{\downarrow}}}((0,t))
\le\min\{t+2,\ 2-t+4\max\{1-t,0\}\}.
\]

For \(0\le t\le2/3\), the first term is at most \(8/3\). For \(2/3\le t\le1\), the second term is \(6-5t\le8/3\); for \(1\le t\le2\), it is \(2-t\le1\). Every noncentral mixed point already has depth at most \(1\). Thus \(h_{\max}(C_{\downarrow})\le8/3\), completing both exact depth computations.

All upper bounds use actual containing closed halfspaces, and both lower bounds treat all their possible normals. Boundary fibers and vertical halfspaces carry their full mixed mass when included. No sampled direction, finite computation, or unproved geometric reduction is a premise.
