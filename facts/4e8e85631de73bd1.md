## Statement

For every integer \(N\ge2\), define
\[
S_N=\left\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^N:\sum_{i=1}^N z_i=0\right\},
\qquad
A_N=\left\{\mathbf x\in\{-1,0,1\}^{N-1}:
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}x_i\right|\le1\right\}.
\]
The notation \(S_N\) here denotes this finite balanced section, not the mixed-integer set \(S\) in the locked problem. The chart \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x,-\sum_i x_i)\) identifies \(S_N\) with the complete full-dimensional support \(A_N\subseteq\mathbb Z^{N-1}\), and identifies the lattice in the hyperplane \(\sum_i z_i=0\) with \(\mathbb Z^{N-1}\).

For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^N\), let
\[
\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)=
\sum_{\mathbf u\in S_N}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u},
\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}=
\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}
{\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)}
\quad(\mathbf z\in S_N).
\]
Adding a common constant to the coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\theta\) leaves this law unchanged. Every log-affine law on \(A_N\) occurs in this way; its intrinsic parameter is \((\theta_1-\theta_N,\ldots,\theta_{N-1}-\theta_N)\).

Use the canonical normalized actual closed-halfspace depth and cubic moment
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=
\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in S_N\cap H}p_{\mathbf u},
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in S_N}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]
Every halfspace has a nonzero normal. This depth equals the intrinsic depth in the chart. Then
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{(9/58)^N}{64(2N+1)^6(N+1)}
\ge \kappa\,8^{-(N-1)},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{(7/36)^7}{746496\,7!}>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The coefficient \(\kappa\) is independent of both \(N\) and the tilt.

## Proof

First check the intrinsic support and normalization. The displayed chart is a linear bijection from \(\mathbb R^{N-1}\) to the hyperplane \(\sum_i z_i=0\), and a bijection of their integer lattices. Thus its lattice rank is exactly \(N-1\). The set \(A_N\) contains \(\mathbf0\) and every \(\pm\mathbf e_i\), so it is full-dimensional. It is complete: the convex set
\(\{\mathbf x\in[-1,1]^{N-1}:|\sum_i x_i|\le1\}\)
contains \(A_N\), and its integer points are exactly \(A_N\). Consequently an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(A_N)\) belongs to \(A_N\), while the reverse inclusion is immediate.

An ambient normal \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^N\) restricts in the chart to the normal \((a_1-a_N,\ldots,a_{N-1}-a_N)\). If this restricted normal is zero, an ambient containing halfspace contains the whole section and has mass \(1\). Otherwise its trace is an intrinsic containing halfspace. Conversely every intrinsic normal lifts by appending a zero coordinate. Hence the intrinsic and ambient depth minima agree. This also proves the parameter map in the statement. In the application of accepted fact `ebb695e93a721952` below, its finite support \(D\) is exactly \(S_N\), its parameter \(\boldsymbol\ell\) is \(\boldsymbol\theta\), and its \(p,\tau_p,R_{\mathbf z},B_{\mathbf z}\) retain their original meanings. In particular, no product-law normalization is substituted for \(p\).

Extend the balanced-section definition to every integer \(k\ge0\), with \(S_0\) the singleton empty tuple, and put \(T_k=|S_k|\). Thus \(T_0=T_1=1\). We need the elementary count estimate
\[
T_k\ge\frac{3^k}{2k+1}.
\tag{2}
\]
To prove it, let \(a_{k,j}\) count the ternary \(k\)-tuples with coordinate sum \(j\), and extend these counts by zero outside \([-k,k]\). They are symmetric in \(j\). They are also nonincreasing for \(j\ge0\), by induction on \(k\): the recurrence
\(a_{k+1,j}=a_{k,j-1}+a_{k,j}+a_{k,j+1}\)
gives
\(a_{k+1,j}-a_{k+1,j+1}=a_{k,j-1}-a_{k,j+2}\ge0\).
For \(j=0\), symmetry makes the last difference \(a_{k,1}-a_{k,2}\); for \(j\ge1\), the induction hypothesis applies directly. The base \(k=0\) has the required property. Hence the central count \(T_k=a_{k,0}\) is the maximum of \(2k+1\) coefficients whose sum is \(3^k\), proving (2).

Fix a root \(\mathbf z\in S_N\), and put \(I=\{i:z_i=0\}\), \(J=\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus I\), and \(k=|I|\). Coordinatewise reflection shows that
\[
R_{\mathbf z}=
\left\{\mathbf u\in S_N:u_j=z_j\text{ for every }j\in J\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, at a nonzero root coordinate the requirement \(2z_j-u_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\) forces \(u_j=z_j\); at a zero root coordinate every ternary value is reflection-compatible. Since \(\sum_{j\in J}z_j=0\), the remaining balance condition is exactly \(\sum_{i\in I}u_i=0\). Thus \(R_{\mathbf z}\) is an embedded copy of \(S_k\), including the singleton cases \(k=0,1\), and \(|R_{\mathbf z}|=T_k\). The symbol \(k_{\mathbf z}\) in accepted fact `ebb695e93a721952` therefore equals \(T_k\), not \(k\).

Define the exact conditional partition function
\[
\mathcal Z_I(\boldsymbol\theta)=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{I}\\
\sum_{i\in I}u_i=0}}
\exp\left(\sum_{i\in I}\theta_i u_i\right),
\tag{4}
\]
with value \(1\) when \(I\) is empty. Equation (3) gives the exact original-mass identity
\(B_{\mathbf z}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\mathcal Z_I(\boldsymbol\theta)/\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)\).
The reflected conditional law in (4) is balanced, not a Cartesian product. Applying the general reflection bound of accepted fact `ebb695e93a721952`, with the alias map just established, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
&\ge \frac{(T_k+1)^2p_{\mathbf z}^2}{4B_{\mathbf z}}
\ge \frac{T_k^2e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}
{4\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)\mathcal Z_I(\boldsymbol\theta)},\\
p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3
&\ge\frac{T_k^6e^{4\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}
{64\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)^4\mathcal Z_I(\boldsymbol\theta)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
This accepted bound applies to every containing closed halfspace and keeps all boundary atoms.

For a common gauge \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), write \(\eta_i=\theta_i+\lambda\) and \(Z_i=1+e^{\eta_i}+e^{-\eta_i}\). Balance makes both partition functions in (4)-(5) gauge-invariant. The inclusions of their balanced supports in the corresponding full ternary products prove, with the indicated inequality direction,
\[
\mathcal Z_N(\boldsymbol\theta)\le\prod_{i=1}^N Z_i,
\qquad
\mathcal Z_I(\boldsymbol\theta)\le\prod_{i\in I}Z_i.
\tag{6}
\]
These are only upper bounds for the exact partition functions; they do not identify either conditional law with an independent product law. Combining (2), (5), and (6), and using \(k\le N\), gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64(2N+1)^6}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in S_N}
\prod_{i=1}^N v_i(z_i),
\tag{7}
\]
where the auxiliary root weights are
\[
v_i(1)=\frac{e^{4\eta_i}}{Z_i^4},
\qquad v_i(-1)=\frac{e^{-4\eta_i}}{Z_i^4},
\qquad v_i(0)=\frac{729}{Z_i^7}.
\tag{8}
\]
For example, a zero root coordinate contributes \(3^6/Z_i^7\), while a nonzero root coordinate contributes \(e^{4\eta_i z_i}/Z_i^4\); these are exactly the factors in (7). The root sum in (7) still has its balance constraint. We next bound that sum for a suitable gauge rather than factor it over all roots.

First prove the scalar margin
\[
f(\eta):=\frac{e^{4\eta}+e^{-4\eta}}
{(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^4}
+\frac{729}{(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^7}
\ge\frac16
\qquad(\eta\in\mathbb R).
\tag{9}
\]
Put \(r=e^\eta+e^{-\eta}\ge2\) and \(s=r-2\ge0\). Since \(e^{4\eta}+e^{-4\eta}=r^4-4r^2+2\), the numerator of \(6f(\eta)-1\), over the positive denominator \((r+1)^7\), is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
&6(r^4-4r^2+2)(r+1)^3+4374-(r+1)^7\\
&\quad=5s^7+81s^6+525s^5+1689s^4+2577s^3
+837s^2-2187s+2511.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
All terms of degree at least three are nonnegative. The remaining quadratic is positive for every real \(s\), as the exact identity
\[
4\cdot837\,(837s^2-2187s+2511)
=(1674s-2187)^2+3623859
\tag{11}
\]
shows. This proves (9).

We now prove that some common gauge satisfies the balanced coefficient estimate
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in S_N}\prod_{i=1}^Nv_i(z_i)
\ge\frac{(9/58)^N}{N+1}.
\tag{12}
\]
For each gauge, normalize the weights in (8) to an auxiliary probability law
\(q_i(t)=v_i(t)/f(\eta_i)\) on \(\{-1,0,1\}\). These laws are not the original law \(p\) or its reflected conditional laws. If \(\eta\ge0\), then \(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta}\le3e^\eta\), and consequently
\[
\frac{v(0)}{v(-1)}
=\frac{729e^{4\eta}}{(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^3}
\ge27e^\eta\ge27,
\qquad
\frac{v(1)}{v(-1)}=e^{8\eta}\ge1.
\tag{13}
\]
It follows that the minority endpoint has normalized mass at most \(1/29\). Reflection in \(\eta\) proves the same assertion for \(\eta\le0\). At \(\eta=0\), the three masses are exactly \((1/29,27/29,1/29)\), in the order \((-1,0,1)\).

For \(\eta>0\), retain the masses at \(0,1\) and discard the mass at \(-1\). For \(\eta<0\), retain the masses at \(-1,0\) and discard the mass at \(1\). The retained mass is at least \(28/29\). The mean of the normalized retained law is, respectively,
\[
m(\eta)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{e^{4\eta}(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^3}
{729+e^{4\eta}(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^3},&\eta>0,\\[6pt]
-m(-\eta),&\eta<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
On each side of zero this function is continuous and strictly increasing. On the positive side this follows because both factors in its positive numerator ratio \(e^{4\eta}(1+e^\eta+e^{-\eta})^3/729\) are strictly increasing; the negative side follows by reflection. Its limits at the two infinities are \(-1,1\), and its one-sided limits at zero are \(-1/28,1/28\).

At \(\eta=0\), any mean in \([-1/28,1/28]\) can instead be obtained by retaining all the center mass and a suitable fraction of one endpoint mass. Explicitly, retaining center mass \(27/29\) and fraction \(\rho/29\) of the positive endpoint, with \(0\le\rho\le1\), gives mean \(\rho/(27+\rho)\); the negative endpoint gives its negative. The retained mass is always at least \(27/29\).

There is a common gauge \(\lambda\) and a choice of these retained laws at coordinates with \(\theta_i+\lambda=0\) for which their means sum to zero. To see this, sum the monotone functions \(m(\theta_i+\lambda)\), allowing the full mean interval at their finitely many jump locations. The total has limits \(-N,N\). If it crosses zero at a continuity point, the sum there is zero. If it crosses at a jump shared by \(j\) coordinates, the other coordinates have a fixed sum, and the \(j\) allowed mean intervals add to \([-j/28,j/28]\), filling the whole jump. Hence zero can again be attained. Fix this gauge and these choices.

Write \(\rho_i(t)\le q_i(t)\) for the resulting retained subprobability masses, \(\beta_i=\sum_t\rho_i(t)\), and let independent \(Y_i\) have laws \(\rho_i/\beta_i\). Every \(\beta_i\ge27/29\), every \(Y_i\) is supported on \(\{0,1\}\), \(\{-1,0\}\), or \(\{0\}\), and \(\sum_i\mathbb EY_i=0\). We claim that
\[
\mathbb P\left(\sum_iY_i=0\right)\ge\frac1{N+1}.
\tag{15}
\]
Here is a self-contained justification. Remove deterministic zero variables. Write each remaining \(Y_i\) as either \(B_i\) or \(-B_i\), where the \(B_i\) are independent Bernoulli variables with parameters strictly between zero and one. If \(L\le N\) variables remain and \(b\) of their signs are negative, then
\(\sum_iY_i=T-b\), where \(T\) is the sum of the positive-sign \(B_i\) and the negative-sign \(1-B_i\). Thus \(T\) is a sum of \(L\) independent Bernoulli variables and \(\mathbb ET=b\), an integer. If \(L=0\), (15) is immediate.

For \(L>0\), the mass sequence of \(T\) is positive on \(\{0,\ldots,L\}\) and log-concave. This can be proved by induction, without a coefficient theorem. If a nonnegative log-concave sequence \((a_j)\) has no internal zeros, its convolution with a Bernoulli law of parameter \(u\in(0,1)\) is \(d_j=(1-u)a_j+ua_{j-1}\). Extend sequences by zero off their supports. Direct expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_j^2-d_{j-1}d_{j+1}
&=(1-u)^2(a_j^2-a_{j-1}a_{j+1})
+u^2(a_{j-1}^2-a_{j-2}a_j)\\
&\quad+u(1-u)(a_ja_{j-1}-a_{j-2}a_{j+1})\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
For the last inequality, the first two brackets are log-concavity inequalities. The third follows by multiplying those inequalities and cancelling the positive middle factors when its right-hand product is positive; otherwise it is immediate. Convolution preserves the interval of positive entries, and the singleton initial sequence starts the induction.

Let \(\pi_j=\mathbb P(T=j)\). Log-concavity makes the piecewise linear interpolation of \(\log\pi_j\) concave on \([0,L]\). Jensen's inequality at the integer mean \(b\), followed by the entropy bound, therefore gives
\[
\log\pi_b\ge\sum_{j=0}^L\pi_j\log\pi_j
\ge-\log(L+1).
\tag{17}
\]
For completeness, the second inequality is the concavity-of-log estimate
\(\sum_j\pi_j\log(1/\pi_j)\le\log(\sum_j\pi_j/\pi_j)=\log(L+1)\).
It follows that \(\pi_b\ge1/(L+1)\ge1/(N+1)\), proving (15).

The coordinatewise domination \(\rho_i\le q_i\), with the balance condition retained, now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in S_N}\prod_i v_i(z_i)
&=\left(\prod_i f(\eta_i)\right)
\sum_{\mathbf z\in S_N}\prod_iq_i(z_i)\\
&\ge\left(\prod_i f(\eta_i)\beta_i\right)
\mathbb P\left(\sum_iY_i=0\right)\\
&\ge\frac1{N+1}\left(\frac16\cdot\frac{27}{29}\right)^N
=\frac{(9/58)^N}{N+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
This proves (12). Combining (7) with (12) proves the first inequality in (1).

Finally, because \(N\ge2\), we have \(2N+1\le3N\) and \(N+1\le2N\). Hence
\[
8^{N-1}M_3(p)
\ge\frac{(36/29)^N}{512(2N+1)^6(N+1)}
\ge\frac{(36/29)^N}{746496\,N^7}.
\tag{19}
\]
The elementary integral bound \(\log(36/29)\ge7/36\), obtained by bounding \(1/t\) below by \(29/36\) on \([1,36/29]\), and the seventh term of the exponential series imply
\((36/29)^N\ge e^{7N/36}\ge(7N/36)^7/7!\).
Substitution into (19) gives \(8^{N-1}M_3(p)\ge\kappa\), with the absolute \(\kappa\) stated in (1). All singleton reflected sections, all finite tilt signs, and all equality atoms were included in the argument.
