## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be convex, with sampled set $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ and fibers $C_z$. Let $f:C\to\mathbb R$ be concave, $\varepsilon\ge0$, $\delta>0$, $G(u)>0$ for $u\ne0$, and let $\Psi$ be nondecreasing and $C^1$. For $N=(a,u)$ with unrestricted $a\in\mathbb R^n$ and $u\ne0$, define
$$
F_N(b)=\sum_z\int_{C_z}\Psi\!\left(\frac{a\cdot z+u\cdot x-b}{\delta G(u)}\right)dx,\qquad b_N(p,y)=a\cdot p+u\cdot y-\varepsilon f(p,y)G(u).
$$
Define raw depth at $(p,y)\in S$ as the infimum of $F_N(b_N(p,y))$ over these normals, optionally also using ordinary closed pure-base caps of fixed base orientations. Assume the integrals are finite and the threshold and base-normal derivatives exist by differentiation under the integral. Suppose the maximum $Q$ is attained and each deepest point has a finite attaining nonvertical normal with $-\partial_bF_N(b_N)>0$.

Distinct deepest bases have distinct coordinate parities, hence there are at most $2^n$ deepest bases. If $\varepsilon>0$ and $f$ is strictly concave on every positive-dimensional fiber, each deepest base has one deepest mark, so there are at most $2^n$ deepest marks.

## Proof

Let deepest marks $Y_0,Y_1$ have bases of the same parity. Their midpoint $Y_m$ is feasible. For fixed $N$, concavity gives $b_N(Y_m)\le[b_N(Y_0)+b_N(Y_1)]/2$, and both endpoint masses for that normal are at least $Q$. Since $F_N$ is nonincreasing, the midpoint mass is at least $Q$. For every allowed pure-base orientation, the closed tail through the midpoint contains the parallel tail through the endpoint having larger threshold in that orientation. It too has mass at least $Q$. Thus $Y_m$ is deepest.

Choose the indicated attaining normal $N$ at $Y_m$ and put $b=b_N(Y_m)$. Since $F_N(b)=Q$ and $F_N'(b)<0$, monotonicity gives $F_N(s)<Q$ for all $s>b$. Therefore $b_N(Y_i)\le b$ for both endpoints. The midpoint inequality forces equality at both endpoints, so the same normal and threshold attain their depths.

Differentiate the cap mass through $Y_i=(p_i,y_i)$ in the unrestricted $a$. The minimum gives $\int(z-p_i)\,d\nu=0$, where $d\nu$ on each fiber equals $\Psi'((a\cdot z+u\cdot x-b)/[\delta G(u)])\,dx/[\delta G(u)]$. The two endpoint equations use the same nonnegative measure of positive mass $-F_N'(b)$, so $p_0=p_1$. If the two marks in this fiber differ and the strict concavity and positive buffer-factor hypotheses hold, the threshold midpoint inequality for this normal would be strict, contradicting the equalities.
