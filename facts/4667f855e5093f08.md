## Statement

Let \(n,d,m\ge1\) be integers, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain exactly the actual-body objects of accepted facts 03f071403cdfc69f and e3bc558c3cb297be: \(C_z=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), \(T=2^{-n}W\), and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero.

Let \(M_1,\ldots,M_n\) be nonnegative integers, let \(B\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix of nonnegative real entries, and let \(f:[0,\infty)^m\to[0,\infty)\) be finite-valued and coordinatewise nonincreasing, with \(f(0)>0\). Assume joint log-concavity with the zero convention: for every \(a,b\in[0,\infty)^m\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), one has \(f((1-\lambda)a+\lambda b)\ge f(a)^{1-\lambda}f(b)^\lambda\), where a positive power of zero is zero. Suppose the entire actual integer table is
\[
w_z=
\begin{cases}
f(Bz),&z\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad 0\le z_i\le M_i\text{ for every }i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
These are exactly the table hypotheses of e3bc558c3cb297be. No continuity of \(f\), rationality of \(B\), positivity of an individual entry, or full-dimensionality of a coordinate restriction is assumed. The number \(m\) of rows is arbitrary, and no nonnegative rank factorization is assumed.

Put \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). For \(j\in D\) and finite \(t\ge0\), retain \(F_j(t)=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ w_{j+u}\ge e^{-t}w_j\}\) and the original corner maximum
\[
\operatorname{Corner}(w)=
\max_{\substack{j\in D\\t\ge0}}
\frac{w_je^{-t}|F_j(t)-F_j(t)|}{1+2t}.
\tag{2}
\]
The family \(F_j(t)\) is the set-valued family of e3bc558c3cb297be, 102f49bbb2cedb49, and 34e95d2e044ea815, and is the fixed-center \(F_t\) of d887d95075dfcc06. It is distinct from the unparameterized scalar all-pair score \(F_j\) discussed in 03f071403cdfc69f. Every coordinate inequality is componentwise, and every difference set counts each distinct difference once.

At each fixed positive center retain \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). The center dependence of \(\alpha\) is suppressed as in those accepted facts. Retain also
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
&& (L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
All displacement sums are oriented and include zero. The feature matrix \(B\) in (1) is separate from the cutoff sum \(B_j^\Delta(L)\).

Let \(I=\{i:M_i\ge2\}\), let \(r=|I|\), and list the coordinates of \(I\) in increasing order. Let \(\iota:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R^n\) insert zeros outside \(I\), and let \(B_I\) be the matrix consisting of the columns in \(I\), in that order. Define \(\bar w_y=w_{\iota(y)}\), \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^r}\bar w_y\), and
\(q=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}(B_I)\).
The scalar restriction and \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}\) have exactly their meanings in e3bc558c3cb297be; \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}\) is the \(\bar W\) of 34e95d2e044ea815. The rank \(q\) here is a new parameter; the discarded-coordinate count denoted \(q\) in that predecessor's restriction argument is \(n-r\) here. For \(r=0\), use \(\mathbb Z^0=\{0\}\), \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}=w_0\), and \(q=0\).

For positive integers \(r,q\), define the new coefficients
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)
&=\frac12\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r
3^{-q(q+5)/2}
\exp\left[-q\log3\left(\sqrt{\frac{qr}{2}}+\frac q2\right)\right],\\
\kappa_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)
&=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)}{20e(r+1)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
These are distinct from the row-count coefficients \(Q_{r,m},\kappa_{r,m}\) of e3bc558c3cb297be. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Corner}(w)
&\ge \kappa_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}
\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)T
&& (q\ge1),\\
\kappa_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)
&\ge\exp[-20(q+1)^3]
&& (r,q\ge1),\\
\kappa_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)&\ge1
&& (q\ge1,\ r\ge1000q^3),\\
\operatorname{Corner}(w)&\ge2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\ge T
&& (q=0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The maximum in (2) is attained, \(K(w)\ge\operatorname{Corner}(w)\), and there exists one \(p^\star\in S\), chosen before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(p^\star)
&\ge\frac{K(w)}{12}
\ge\frac{\operatorname{Corner}(w)}{12}
\ge\frac{\exp[-20(q+1)^3]}{12}T,\\
h_S(p^\star)&\ge\frac{T}{12}
=\frac{W}{12\,2^n}
\qquad\text{if }q=0\text{ or }(q\ge1\text{ and }r\ge1000q^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This one point works for every closed ambient halfspace containing it, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing whole fibers.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. Equation (1) makes the entire table coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative integer orthant, including zeros: if \(0\le u\le v\) and \(v\) satisfies all caps, so does \(u\), and \(Bu\le Bv\); if \(v\) violates a cap, its weight is zero. Also \(w_0=f(0)>0\), and every weight is at most \(w_0\).

For each fixed retained vector \(y\), every permitted assignment to coordinates outside \(I\) has weight at most \(w_{\iota(y)}\). There are \(\prod_{i\notin I}(M_i+1)\le2^{n-r}\) such assignments. Consequently \(W\le2^{n-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\), and hence \(2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\ge T\). This is the same scalar comparison as in e3bc558c3cb297be. Every original fiber remains in \(W\); no convex-body theorem is applied to the restriction.

Write \(\bar M_i\) for the retained caps, so each \(\bar M_i\ge2\), and put \(h_i=\lfloor\bar M_i/2\rfloor\). If \(q=0\), then \(B_I=0\), so the restricted table equals \(w_0\) throughout its capped integer box. Choose the retained center \(p=(h_i)_{i=1}^r\), interpreted as the unique empty vector when \(r=0\), and the original center \(j=\iota(p)\). Because \(2h_i\le\bar M_i\), one has \(F_j(0)=\iota(\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le u\le p\})\). Its difference set has cardinality \(\prod_i(2h_i+1)\). For every nonnegative retained cap, \(2h_i+1\ge(\bar M_i+1)/2\), so this corner value is at least \(2^{-r}w_0\prod_i(\bar M_i+1)=2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\). Empty products are one. This proves the last line of (5), including \(r=0\); its original-body consequence will follow at the end.

Assume henceforth \(q\ge1\), so \(r\ge1\). The rows \(a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(m)}\) of \(B_I\) have the same meaning as in e3bc558c3cb297be. Repeated rows, zero rows, and rows vanishing on a selected vector remain in the indexed list. The scalar formula is exactly \(\bar w_y=f(B_Iy)\) for \(0\le y_i\le\bar M_i\), and zero elsewhere.

Fix any positive threshold \(0<\eta\le w_0\) and define
\[
E=\{z\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le z_i\le\bar M_i,\ f(B_Iz)\ge\eta\},
\qquad
A_p=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le u\le p,\ p+u\in E\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(E\) is a nonempty finite downset, and \(A_p\) is exactly the forward set used for a threshold prefix in e3bc558c3cb297be. We will find \(p\in E\), with \(0\le p_i\le h_i\), such that
\[
|A_p-A_p|\ge Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)\,2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{8}
\]

Use exactly the continuous piecewise-linear interpolation \(\ell\) of 102f49bbb2cedb49 and 34e95d2e044ea815, defined by \(\ell(k)=\log(2k+1)\) at each nonnegative integer. Its slopes are positive, decreasing, and at most \(\log3\). Equation (12) of 3a39f48aba7a2790 gives \(k\log((2k+1)/(2k-1))\le\log3\) for \(k\ge1\); on each interval \((k-1,k)\), this implies \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\).

Let \(\bar z=|E|^{-1}\sum_{z\in E}z\) and let \(x_i=\min\{3\bar z_i/4,h_i\}\). Equation (10) in the proof of accepted fact 34e95d2e044ea815 has the independent scope of every nonempty subset of this capped integer box. With precisely these caps, mean, clipped vector, and interpolation, it gives
\[
\exp\left(\sum_i\ell(x_i)\right)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular this invocation does not require \(E\) to be described by two constraints. That accepted inequality includes clipped coordinates, zero means, and ties.

We first strengthen the nonnegative-form rounding observation (10) of 102f49bbb2cedb49. Given \(v\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^r\) and any finite list of nonnegative linear forms whose coefficient matrix has real rank \(h\), there exists an integer vector \(p'\) in \(\prod_i[\lfloor v_i\rfloor,\lceil v_i\rceil]\) such that every listed form at \(p'\) is at most its value at \(v\), and \(\sum_i\ell(p'_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(v_i)-h\log3\). This includes rank zero and an empty list. To prove it, maximize the affine objective \(\sum_i\ell(y_i)\) on that compact rectangle subject to the form bounds; the feasible set contains \(v\). If a maximizing point has more than \(h\) noninteger coordinates, the columns of the form matrix restricted to those coordinates have a nonzero null vector. Extend it by zero on the other coordinates. Small moves of both signs preserve every form and stay in the rectangle. Optimality forces the objective slope along this direction to be zero. Move until a coordinate reaches an integer endpoint, preserving the maximum, and repeat. Each repetition decreases the number of noninteger coordinates. The resulting maximum has at most \(h\) of them. Round those coordinates down. All listed forms remain bounded because their coefficients are nonnegative, and each coordinate loses at most \(\log3\) in the objective. This proves the rank version. An integer coordinate cap containing \(v_i\) contains its full rounding interval, so no additional cap forms are needed.

Apply this rank version to \(x\) and all the row forms \(a\cdot y\), of rank \(q\). It gives an integer \(k\), with \(0\le k_i\le h_i\), satisfying
\[
\beta_a:=a\cdot k\le a\cdot x\le\frac34a\cdot\bar z
\quad\text{for every indexed row }a,
\qquad
\sum_i\ell(k_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-q\log3.
\tag{10}
\]

For the common shrink define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_q&=\sqrt{q/2},
&s_{r,q}&=\gamma_q(\sqrt r+\gamma_q)
=\sqrt{qr/2}+q/2,\\
\theta_i&=\max_{\substack{\text{indexed rows }a\\\beta_a>0}}
\frac{a_i k_i}{\beta_a},
&\widetilde k_i&=\frac{k_i}{1+s_{r,q}\theta_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The maximum over an empty collection is zero. The quantities \(\beta_a,\theta_i,\widetilde k_i\) are exactly those of the corresponding construction in e3bc558c3cb297be, with the rank parameters \(\gamma_q,s_{r,q}\) replacing its \(\gamma_m,s_{r,m}\). The symbol \(L\) continues to denote the original curvature cutoff in (3). Nonnegativity gives \(0\le\theta_i\le1\), and a zero coordinate of \(k\) has \(\theta_i=0\).

We prove the essential bound \(\sum_i\theta_i\le q\) directly. For a vector \(a\), write \(a\odot k=(a_i k_i)_{i=1}^r\), the coordinatewise product, and put \(\mathcal L_k=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{a\odot k:\text{indexed rows }a\}\). Multiplication of all rows by the same diagonal matrix gives \(q_k:=\dim\mathcal L_k\le q\). If there are no active rows, then all \(\theta_i=0\) and the bound follows. Otherwise each active row defines \(\omega_a=(a\odot k)/\beta_a\in\mathcal L_k\), with nonnegative coordinates and \(\|\omega_a\|_1=1\); inactive rows contribute the zero vector to \(\mathcal L_k\).

The set \(\{u\in\mathcal L_k:\|u\|_1\le1\}\) is compact. Fix any linear coordinates on the \(q_k\)-dimensional space, and maximize the absolute determinant of an ordered \(q_k\)-tuple in this unit ball. A maximum exists and is positive, because a scaled basis is in the ball. Let \(\xi^{(1)},\ldots,\xi^{(q_k)}\) be a maximizing tuple. It is a basis, and each \(\|\xi^{(b)}\|_1=1\), since otherwise that nonzero vector could be scaled to norm one and increase the determinant. For any \(u\) in the unit ball, write \(u=\sum_b c_b\xi^{(b)}\). Replacing the \(b\)-th vector of the maximizing tuple by \(u\) changes its determinant by the factor \(c_b\). Maximality therefore gives \(|c_b|\le1\). Apply this to every \(\omega_a\). For each coordinate \(i\),
\[
0\le(\omega_a)_i
\le\sum_{b=1}^{q_k}|\xi_i^{(b)}|,
\qquad
\sum_i\theta_i
\le\sum_{b=1}^{q_k}\sum_i|\xi_i^{(b)}|
=q_k\le q.
\tag{12}
\]
The basis vectors \(\xi^{(b)}\) may have either sign, and the coefficients \(c_b\) may have either sign. Only their absolute-value bound was used.

For each indexed row retain the deficit and quadratic quantity of e3bc558c3cb297be, namely \(\delta_a=\beta_a-a\cdot\widetilde k\) and \(\zeta_a^2=\sum_i a_i^2 k_i\widetilde k_i\), with \(\zeta_a\ge0\). If \(\beta_a>0\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_a
&=\sum_i\frac{a_i k_i s_{r,q}\theta_i}{1+s_{r,q}\theta_i}
\ge\frac{s_{r,q}}{\beta_a}\zeta_a^2,\\
\beta_a-\delta_a
&=a\cdot\widetilde k
\le\sqrt r\left(\sum_i a_i^2\widetilde k_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt r\,\zeta_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The first inequality uses \(\theta_i\ge a_i k_i/\beta_a\); the last uses \(\widetilde k_i\le k_i\). These imply \(\delta_a\ge\gamma_q\zeta_a\). It is immediate if \(\zeta_a=0\). Otherwise, if \(\delta_a<\gamma_q\zeta_a\), the second line gives \(\beta_a<(\sqrt r+\gamma_q)\zeta_a\). The first line and \(s_{r,q}=\gamma_q(\sqrt r+\gamma_q)\) then give \(\delta_a>\gamma_q\zeta_a\), a contradiction. If \(\beta_a=0\), nonnegativity gives \(a_i k_i=0\) for every \(i\), so \(\delta_a=\zeta_a=0\) and the same reserve holds.

For the second rounding, keep \(k\) fixed and list all the nonnegative forms \(a\cdot y\) and \(\sum_i a_i^2 k_i y_i\), for all indexed rows. Their combined real rank is at most \(q+q(q+1)/2=q(q+3)/2\). To verify this count, choose a basis \(\rho^{(1)},\ldots,\rho^{(q)}\) from the rows of \(B_I\). Each row has an expression \(a=\sum_{b=1}^q c_b\rho^{(b)}\) with real, possibly signed coefficients. Coordinatewise expansion gives
\[
(a_i^2 k_i)_{i=1}^r
=\sum_{b=1}^q c_b^2((\rho_i^{(b)})^2k_i)_{i=1}^r
+2\sum_{1\le b<c\le q}c_b c_c(\rho_i^{(b)}\rho_i^{(c)}k_i)_{i=1}^r.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus all squared-row coefficient vectors lie in the span of the \(q(q+1)/2\) displayed fixed vectors. Adding the original row span costs at most another \(q\) dimensions. No sign restriction on a spanning expression is needed for a rank bound. The forms actually imposed remain nonnegative, as required for rounding down.

Apply the rank rounding lemma to \(\widetilde k\) and this complete list. The resulting integer \(p\) obeys \(0\le p_i\le k_i\le h_i\), since \(\lceil\widetilde k_i\rceil\le k_i\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot p&\le a\cdot\widetilde k,\\
\sum_i a_i^2p_i^2
&\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i p_i
\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i\widetilde k_i=\zeta_a^2,\\
\sum_i\ell(p_i)
&\ge\sum_i\ell(\widetilde k_i)-\frac{q(q+3)}2\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Combining the first two lines with the reserve proved from (13) gives, for every indexed row,
\[
a\cdot p+\gamma_q\|a\odot p\|_2
\le\beta_a\le\frac34a\cdot\bar z.
\tag{16}
\]
For an inactive row, both terms on the left are zero. If \(k=0\), the rounding intervals force \(\widetilde k=p=0\), and all of (11)--(16) remain valid.

For \(k_i>0\), also \(\widetilde k_i>0\). Integrating \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\) over the finitely many affine intervals between them and using (12) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\bigl(\ell(k_i)-\ell(\widetilde k_i)\bigr)
&\le\log3\sum_{i:k_i>0}\log\frac{k_i}{\widetilde k_i}\\
&=\log3\sum_i\log(1+s_{r,q}\theta_i)
\le q s_{r,q}\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Zero coordinates contribute zero. Equations (9), (10), (15), and (17) give the exact combined rounding loss \(q+q(q+3)/2=q(q+5)/2\), and hence
\[
\prod_i(2p_i+1)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r3^{-q(q+5)/2}
e^{-q s_{r,q}\log3}\,2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{18}
\]

Finite iteration of joint log-concavity over the positive values in \(E\) gives \(f(B_I\bar z)\ge(\prod_{z\in E}f(B_Iz))^{1/|E|}\ge\eta\). For a singleton this is equality. Thus no continuity or superlevel-set closure is required. Equation (16) implies \(B_Ip\le(3/4)B_I\bar z\le B_I\bar z\), so monotonicity of \(f\) and the caps imply \(p\in E\).

Let \(V_i\) be independent, each uniform on the integers \(-p_i,\ldots,p_i\), and put \(V_i^+=\max\{V_i,0\}\), \(V_i^-=\max\{-V_i,0\}\). For exactly this integer law, equations (13)--(14) of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790 give \(\mathbb EV_i^\pm=p_i(p_i+1)/(2(2p_i+1))\le p_i/3\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(V_i^\pm)\le2p_i^2/9\), including \(p_i=0\). For each sign define \(X_i^\pm=(V_i^\pm-\mathbb EV_i^\pm)/p_i\) when \(p_i>0\), and define \(X_i^\pm=0\) when \(p_i=0\). Each vector \(X^\pm\) is centered, its coordinates are independent, and each coordinate variance is at most \(2/9\).

Put \(\mathcal L_p=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{a\odot p:\text{indexed rows }a\}\), and let \(P_p\) be the Euclidean orthogonal projection onto this subspace. This auxiliary subspace is distinct from the original mixed-integer set \(S\). Its dimension is at most \(q\), by the same diagonal-multiplication argument as for \(\mathcal L_k\). For either sign, choosing an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal L_p\), expanding the squared projection norm, and using independence across coordinates gives
\[
\mathbb E\|P_pX^\pm\|_2^2
=\sum_{b=1}^{\dim\mathcal L_p}
\sum_{i=1}^r(e_i^{(b)})^2\operatorname{Var}(X_i^\pm)
\le\frac29\dim\mathcal L_p\le\frac{2q}{9}.
\tag{19}
\]
Here \(e^{(b)}\) denotes that orthonormal basis; if the subspace is zero, the sum and projection are zero. The second-moment inequality therefore bounds each of the two probabilities \(\mathbb P\{\|P_pX^\pm\|_2>\sqrt{8q/9}\}\) by \(1/4\). A union bound over these two signs gives probability at least \(1/2\) of the joint event
\(\|P_pX^+\|_2\le\sqrt{8q/9}\) and \(\|P_pX^-\|_2\le\sqrt{8q/9}\).
No independence between signs is used, and there is no union over rows.

On this joint event, for every indexed row and either sign, write \(\mu_a=\mathbb E(a\cdot V^\pm)\le a\cdot p/3\). Since \(a\odot p\in\mathcal L_p\), Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot V^\pm-\mu_a
&=\langle a\odot p,X^\pm\rangle
=\langle a\odot p,P_pX^\pm\rangle
\le\sqrt{8q/9}\,\|a\odot p\|_2,\\
a\cdot\bar z-a\cdot p
&\ge\frac13a\cdot p+\frac43\gamma_q\|a\odot p\|_2
\ge\mu_a+\sqrt{8q/9}\,\|a\odot p\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The second line is (16), and \((4/3)\gamma_q=\sqrt{8q/9}\). These inequalities also hold when \(a\odot p=0\), since then both signed sums are identically zero. It follows simultaneously for every row that \(B_I(p+V^+)\le B_I\bar z\) and \(B_I(p+V^-)\le B_I\bar z\).

Both integer points \(p+V^\pm\) satisfy the retained caps because \(0\le V_i^\pm\le p_i\) and \(2p_i\le\bar M_i\). Monotonicity and \(f(B_I\bar z)\ge\eta\) therefore put both points in \(E\). Consequently \(V^+,V^-\in A_p\) and \(V=V^+-V^-\in A_p-A_p\). The vector \(V\) is uniform on \(\prod_i(2p_i+1)\) distinct integer vectors, so \(|A_p-A_p|\ge\tfrac12\prod_i(2p_i+1)\). With (18), this proves (8) with exactly (4). All active-row degeneracies were included; the case of a zero projection merely makes the joint event certain.

We now select a weighted threshold without replacing any original weight. Let \(\bar D=\{y:\bar w_y>0\}\). For \(y\in\bar D\), coordinatewise floor-halving stays inside the retained capped box, and \(B_I\lfloor y/2\rfloor\le B_Iy/2\). Monotonicity and log-concavity between zero and \(B_Iy\) give \(\bar w_{\lfloor y/2\rfloor}\ge f(B_Iy/2)\ge\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\). Each integer image of floor-halving has at most \(2^r\) preimages, of the form \(2u+\varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r\). Hence \(\sum_{y\in\bar D}\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\le2^rW_{\mathrm{zero}}\).

Set \(J=\lceil2(r+1)\log2\rceil\), so \(1\le J\le2(r+1)\). The total weight of positive sites with \(\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-J}\) is at most \(e^{-J/2}\sum_y\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\le W_{\mathrm{zero}}/2\). The \(J\) disjoint bands \(\{y\in\bar D:w_0e^{-t}<\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-(t-1)}\}\), for integers \(1\le t\le J\), thus contain one of mass at least \(W_{\mathrm{zero}}/(2J)\). Their strict lower and weak upper endpoints partition all weights above the discarded threshold, including all ties. Choose its index \(t_0\), put \(\eta=w_0e^{-t_0}\), and take exactly the prefix \(E=\{y:\bar w_y\ge\eta\}\) in (7). Every weight in the chosen band is at most \(e\eta\), so \(\eta|E|\ge W_{\mathrm{zero}}/(2eJ)\).

Apply (8) to this prefix, obtaining \(p\in E\), and let \(\tau=\log(\bar w_p/\eta)\). Then \(0\le\tau\le t_0\le J\) and \(\bar w_pe^{-\tau}=\eta\). At the original center \(j=\iota(p)\), every displacement \(0\le u\le j\) vanishes outside \(I\); thus the exact original-weight identities are \(F_j(\tau)=\iota(A_p)\) and \(w_j=\bar w_p\). The linear injection preserves differences and their cardinalities. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_je^{-\tau}|F_j(\tau)-F_j(\tau)|}{1+2\tau}
&\ge\frac{Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)\,2^{-r}\eta|E|}{1+2J}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)}{2eJ(1+2J)}\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_{\mathrm{rank}}(r,q)}{20e(r+1)^2}\,2^{-r}W_{\mathrm{zero}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
