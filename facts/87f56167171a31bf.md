## Statement

Let \(n\geq2\), \(d\geq1\), and let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Define \(C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(Z:=\{z:a_z>0\}\), and \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0\). Thus \(a_z\) is the original volume \(w_z\) when the canonical discrete-index notation is used; the legacy \(A_z\) of accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884` is exactly \(a_z\). No volume or total mass is renormalized. All integer fibers, including zero-volume boundary fibers, remain in \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

For \(z\in Z\), put \(c_z:=a_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}x\,dx\), \(\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\), and \(D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z\), where
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=\left\{\frac1m\int_Kxf(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\quad \int_Kf=m\right\}.
\]
The notation \(D_z\) denotes the accepted centered half-metronoid, not the canonical complete integer support \(D\). Put \(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d\). For each base parity class \(\xi\), let \(A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}a_z\), and retain the complete strong canonical product matching of accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059`:
\[
 q^\times_{\{u,v\}}:=\frac{\gamma_da_ua_v}{A_\xi}
 \quad(u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2,\ u,v\in Z).
\]
Its other entries are zero. Write \(F^\times:=\{e:q^\times_e>0\}\), \(Q^\times:=\sum_eq^\times_e\), and, for \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F^\times\), put \(m_e:=(z+z')/2\), \(\bar c_e:=(c_z+c_{z'})/2\), \(D_e:=(D_z+D_{z'})/2\), and \(L_e:=(\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'})/2=\bar c_e+D_e\).

Choose a primal optimizer \(s_*:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) for
\[
 \Delta_C(q^\times):=
 \min_{s\text{ affine}}\sum_{e\in F^\times}
 q^\times_e\bigl(\|s(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\bigr)_+,
\]
and a gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer \(v=(v_e)\), in exactly the normalization of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`. In particular, its dual constraints are
\[
 \sum_ev_e=0,\qquad \sum_ev_em_e^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad h_{D_e}(v_e)\leq q^\times_e,
\]
and its objective is \(-\sum_eh_{L_e}(v_e)\). Define \(\omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e)\), \(\Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e\), and \(F_\omega:=\{e:\omega_e>0\}\), and assume \(\Omega>0\).

For every loaded incidence \((e,z)\), choose an actual support contact \(x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z\) in direction \(v_e\), and set \(\lambda_{e,z}:=h_{D_z}(v_e)/2\), \(u_{e,z}:=v_e/h_{D_z}(v_e)\), and \(t_z:=\sum_{e\in F_\omega:e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}\). Assume the ZERO interaction hypothesis, in the normalization of accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884`:
\[
 \mathcal I^\times
 :=\sum_{z:t_z>0}\frac1{t_z}
 \sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\subseteq F_\omega\\e\ne f,\ e\ni z,\ f\ni z}}
 \lambda_{e,z}\lambda_{f,z}
 \langle u_{e,z}-u_{f,z},x_{e,z}-x_{f,z}\rangle
 =0.
\]
Every inner sum counts an unordered pair once; \(\mathcal I^\times\) is the legacy \(\mathcal I\) of those accepted facts applied to \(q^\times\). By accepted fact `dcccecd70b052857`, there is then one actual contact \(x_z\in\mathcal K_z\) at each loaded endpoint, with \(x_{e,z}=x_z\) on every loaded incidence.

Form the strict-underload graph \(\mathscr G_<\) with edge set
\[
 F_<:=\{e\in F^\times:0<\omega_e<q^\times_e\}
\]
and vertex set consisting exactly of the endpoints of those edges. Its connected components are finite and each lies in one parity class. For each bipartite component \(\Gamma\), choose its two nonempty vertex classes \(V_\Gamma^+,V_\Gamma^-\), and put \(B_\Gamma^\pm:=\sum_{z\in V_\Gamma^\pm}a_z\). For each parity \(\xi\), let \(U_\xi\) be the union of the vertex sets of all nonbipartite components in that parity, and put
\[
 B_\xi^0:=\sum_{z\in U_\xi}a_z,\qquad
 M_\xi^0:=\max_{z\in U_\xi}a_z,
\]
with both quantities zero when \(U_\xi\) is empty. Define the original-volume completion charge
\[
 \mathcal Q_<:=
 \sum_\xi\min\left\{\frac{B_\xi^0}{4},
                         \frac{B_\xi^0-M_\xi^0}{2}\right\}
 +\frac12\sum_{\Gamma\text{ bipartite}}
                     \min\{B_\Gamma^+,B_\Gamma^-\}.
\]

Then all of the following hold.

For every \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_<\), one has the actual contact equation \((x_z+x_{z'})/2=s_*(m_e)\). On every nonbipartite component, \(x_z=s_*(z)\) at every vertex. On every bipartite component, there is a vector \(\varepsilon_\Gamma\in\mathbb R^d\) such that \(x_z-s_*(z)=\varepsilon_\Gamma\) on \(V_\Gamma^+\) and \(x_z-s_*(z)=-\varepsilon_\Gamma\) on \(V_\Gamma^-\).

Consequently there is one off-diagonal same-parity matching \(q^{\mathrm{comp}}\), with \(d_z(q^{\mathrm{comp}})\leq a_z/2\) for every physical source fiber, whose mass is exactly \(\mathcal Q_<\) and which is affinely half-metronoid realizable by the single map \(s_*\). Its allowed pairs are all distinct pairs within each \(U_\xi\), and all pairs between \(V_\Gamma^+\) and \(V_\Gamma^-\) in each bipartite component. Within these prescribed pair sets, \(\mathcal Q_<\) is the maximum matching mass under the original capacities \(a_z/2\).

In the exact capacity normalization of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, this gives
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\geq\mathcal Q_<,
 \qquad h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathcal Q_<.
\]
Here \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is the supremum of masses of same-parity matchings with degrees at most \(a_z/2\), realized by affine midpoint values of points in \(\mathcal K_z\). The depth \(h_{\max}(C)\) is the maximum, over \(p\in S\), of the infimum of the original mixed volume in a closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\). Thus if \(\mathcal Q_<\geq\lambda W\), for any \(\lambda>0\), there is one \(p\in S\) for which every such closed halfspace has original mixed volume at least \(\gamma_d\lambda2^{-n}W\).

This completion also identifies actual affine labels in the original transverse atlas of accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059`. For a label \(\sigma=(z;\{z,a\},\{z,b\})\), its unchanged actual opposite midpoint is \(X_\sigma=(x_a+x_b)/2\) at \(m_\sigma=(a+b)/2\). Whenever \(\{a,b\}\) is one of the allowed completion pairs, \(X_\sigma=s_*(m_\sigma)\). This statement retains every such label and its full original weight \(\theta_\sigma\); it neither replaces actual contacts by centroids nor adds atlas weight to the completion matching charge.

The charge above is conditional on the strict-underload graph. No lower bound for \(\mathcal Q_<\) in terms of \(W\) is asserted from near saturation \(Q^\times-\Omega=o(W)\), small unmatched mass, or the existence of odd cycles in the full loaded graph \((V(F_\omega),F_\omega)\).

## Proof

The product degree is
\[
 d_z(q^\times)
 =\gamma_da_z\frac{A_\xi-a_z}{A_\xi}
 \leq\gamma_da_z\leq\frac{a_z}{2}
 \quad(z\equiv\xi\pmod2).
\]
The last inequality follows from \((1+1/d)^d\geq2\). Thus the optimizer theorem in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` applies to the complete canonical product, and the strong matching hypotheses of accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884` also hold. The exact alias \(A_z=a_z\) identifies their original volume normalization. Its interaction formula is the one displayed above. Accepted fact `dcccecd70b052857` therefore supplies the common actual contacts \(x_z\), and also says that each nonzero direction of \(\mathcal K_z\) has a unique support contact.

Fix \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_<\). The saturation implication in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` says that \(s_*(m_e)\notin L_e\) would force \(\omega_e=q^\times_e\). Hence \(s_*(m_e)\in L_e\). The same accepted primal-dual equality, separately on each edge, gives
\[
 q^\times_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(s_*(m_e),L_e)
 =\langle v_e,s_*(m_e)\rangle-h_{L_e}(v_e).
\]
Its left side is zero on this edge, so \(s_*(m_e)\) is a support point of \(L_e\) in the nonzero direction \(v_e\).

Write \(s_*(m_e)=(p_z+p_{z'})/2\), where \(p_z\in\mathcal K_z\), \(p_{z'}\in\mathcal K_{z'}\). The support function of \(L_e\) is the average of the two endpoint support functions. The equality just established is therefore
\[
 \frac{\langle v_e,p_z\rangle+\langle v_e,p_{z'}\rangle}{2}
 =\frac{h_{\mathcal K_z}(v_e)+h_{\mathcal K_{z'}}(v_e)}{2}.
\]
Each term on the left is at most its corresponding support value. Thus equality holds at both endpoints. Uniqueness of the actual support contacts gives \(p_z=x_{e,z}=x_z\) and \(p_{z'}=x_{e,z'}=x_{z'}\). This proves \((x_z+x_{z'})/2=s_*(m_e)\), with the full vector equation, not merely equality of a scalar support score.

For a vertex of \(\mathscr G_<\), set \(\varepsilon_z:=x_z-s_*(z)\). Since \(s_*\) is affine, every edge of that graph satisfies \(\varepsilon_z+\varepsilon_{z'}=0\). Transport along a path changes the sign at each step. In a nonbipartite connected component, an odd cycle exists. Transport around that cycle gives \(\varepsilon_z=-\varepsilon_z\), so \(\varepsilon_z=0\); connectivity then gives zero at every vertex in the component. In a bipartite connected component, all paths have the parity prescribed by the two vertex classes. Choosing a root in \(V_\Gamma^+\) yields the asserted constant values \(\varepsilon_\Gamma\) and \(-\varepsilon_\Gamma\). This proves the complete actual-contact conclusions.

In particular, all vertices in \(U_\xi\), even when they came from different underload components, have \(x_z=s_*(z)\). For distinct \(u,v\in U_\xi\), affinity gives \((x_u+x_v)/2=s_*((u+v)/2)\). The same equation holds for any \(u\in V_\Gamma^+\), \(v\in V_\Gamma^-\), because their two residual vectors cancel. These are same-parity pairs and have integral midpoint bases. The points used at every endpoint are the actual contacts \(x_z\in\mathcal K_z\).

We next compute and attain the full original-volume capacity of these pair sets. Consider first one nonempty \(U_\xi\). Give vertex \(z\) supply \(a_z/2\). The total supply is \(B_\xi^0/2\), so every matching has mass at most \(B_\xi^0/4\). Choose a largest vertex. Every edge of a complete off-diagonal matching uses at least one unit of supply away from that vertex, so the mass is also at most \((B_\xi^0-M_\xi^0)/2\).

Both bounds are attained by the finite matching construction in the proof of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`; we give it here on this selected vertex set. If \(M_\xi^0>B_\xi^0/2\), match each other vertex completely to a largest vertex, giving edge weight \(a_z/2\). The total weight is \((B_\xi^0-M_\xi^0)/2\), and its used degree at the largest vertex is at most \(M_\xi^0/2\). If \(M_\xi^0\leq B_\xi^0/2\), partition a circle of circumference \(B_\xi^0/2\) into half-open arcs \(I_z\) of lengths \(a_z/2\). Rotation by half the circumference is a measure-preserving involution. Every arc has length at most half the circumference, so it has zero-measure overlap with its own rotated copy. For distinct vertices, give \(\{u,v\}\) the length of the set of points in \(I_u\) whose rotated image lies in \(I_v\). Involution makes these weights symmetric, and the degree at \(z\) is exactly \(a_z/2\). This attains mass \(B_\xi^0/4\). The equality case and empty sets cause no exception. Hence the first term of \(\mathcal Q_<\) is exactly the sum of the complete-graph capacities on the \(U_\xi\).

Now fix a bipartite component \(\Gamma\). Every allowed pair uses one unit of supply from each side, so its matching mass is at most \(\min\{B_\Gamma^+,B_\Gamma^-\}/2\). Suppose \(B_\Gamma^+\leq B_\Gamma^-\), and for \(u\in V_\Gamma^+\), \(v\in V_\Gamma^-\) set
\[
 q^{\Gamma}_{\{u,v\}}:=\frac{a_ua_v}{2B_\Gamma^-}.
\]
The degree at \(u\) is \(a_u/2\), the degree at \(v\) is \(a_vB_\Gamma^+/(2B_\Gamma^-)\leq a_v/2\), and the total mass is \(B_\Gamma^+/2\). Interchanging the sides gives the other case. Both side masses are positive, so there is no division by zero. This attains the second term of \(\mathcal Q_<\).

Combine these complete-graph and complete-bipartite matchings into \(q^{\mathrm{comp}}\). Connected components of \(\mathscr G_<\) are vertex-disjoint. Pooling the nonbipartite components within a parity does not introduce any vertex into a bipartite component or another parity. Thus each physical source fiber belongs to at most one of the prescribed matching blocks. This remains true when several underload components lie in the same full loaded component. Consequently every physical degree of the combined matching is at most \(a_z/2\), and its mass is the sum of the individual attained capacities, exactly \(\mathcal Q_<\). The same disjointness and the preceding upper bounds prove maximality within the prescribed pair sets. In particular, \(2\mathcal Q_<\leq\sum_za_z=W\).

Every positive edge of \(q^{\mathrm{comp}}\) has the previously verified endpoint witnesses \(x_u\in\mathcal K_u\), \(x_v\in\mathcal K_v\) satisfying \((x_u+x_v)/2=s_*((u+v)/2)\). Therefore this one matching is affinely half-metronoid realizable by \(s_*\). It is legal in the exact definition of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\) in accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, proving \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\geq\mathcal Q_<\). Its affine matching certificate gives \(h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathcal Q_<\), including the existence of one actual mixed-integer point whose depth uses every closed containing halfspace. If \(\mathcal Q_{<}=0\), the inequality is automatic. The asserted \(\lambda W\) consequence follows by substitution.

For completeness, that certificate charges endpoint occurrences to original fiber capacities, rather than adding independently constructed target densities. For each containing closed halfspace, each counted affine midpoint edge has at least one endpoint witness in the halfspace, including when the midpoint lies on its boundary. The total edge weight oriented toward any one physical source is at most its degree \(a_z/2\). The accepted half-metronoid depth estimate is then used once in that fiber, as in the proof of `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`. Thus coincidences of target midpoint atoms cannot duplicate a source charge. Zero-volume boundary fibers remain part of \(S\) and contribute their original zero volume to the mixed measure.

Finally, the actual atlas definition in accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059` is \(X_\sigma=(x_{e,a}+x_{f,b})/2\). ZERO contact collapse identifies these two occurrence contacts with \(x_a,x_b\), without identifying either with a fiber centroid. If the opposite pair is an allowed completion pair, the already established equation gives \(X_\sigma=s_*(m_\sigma)\). Equal pairs or target bases do not cause any label to be removed. This is an identification of actual points; no atlas weight is added to \(|q^{\mathrm{comp}}|\).

The strict-underload qualification is necessary for the argument as given. On a saturated loaded edge, the exact accepted equality remains
\[
 \langle v_e,s_*(m_e)\rangle-h_{L_e}(v_e)
 =q^\times_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(s_*(m_e),L_e).
\]
If the primal point is missed, the right side is positive and the actual midpoint equation fails. An odd cycle in the full loaded graph therefore supplies no zero-sum residual equations merely from ZERO interaction. Moreover, near saturation controls only the sum \(\sum_e(q^\times_e-\omega_e)\); it does not provide a lower bound for the original volume of vertices belonging to nonbipartite strict-underload components or for the balanced side volumes of bipartite components. The limiting saturation data \(\omega_e=q^\times_e\) have no strict-underload edges at all. This last observation is an obstruction to deriving the needed equations from the load inequalities alone, not an assertion that such data occur in an actual full hard body. An additional actual-body argument at saturated missed edges remains required for an unconditional macroscopic charge.
